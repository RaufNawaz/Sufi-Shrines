# The Auqaf Records Ask — briefing note

---

## 1. The number that reframes this

**Punjab Auqaf administers 534 shrines.**

Our entire national database — Punjab, Sindh, Balochistan, KP and Azad Kashmir, every religion — is 162 sites, of which 74 are Muslim shrines.

So a single provincial register covers **more than three times** our whole Muslim-shrine holding, and roughly **three times our entire archive**. Alongside those shrines the department also administers 534 mosques, 76,625 acres of land, 1,403 commercial and domestic properties, and 2,789 employees, and runs a 120-bed hospital at Data Darbar plus 16 dispensaries.

This is not a supplementary source. It is the single largest structured dataset on Pakistani shrines in existence, and we are one email away from the people who own it.

---

## 2. What Auqaf actually is

Formed **1 January 1960**, headquartered in Lahore. It emerged from the mid-century nationalisation of shrine endowments, which transferred major shrines from hereditary custodians to the state — Data Darbar itself was nationalised in 1960.

**The critical practical point: "Auqaf" is not one body.** Each province runs its own department under its own provincial law. Punjab Auqaf has no authority in Sindh. This matters because our archive spans five jurisdictions:

| Province | Our sites include | Separate ask needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Punjab** | Data Darbar, Bibi Pak Daman, Mian Mir, Pakpattan, Multan cluster, Uch Sharif | Punjab Auqaf — **the warm relationship** |
| **Sindh** | Bhit Shah, Sehwan, Sachal Sarmast, Luari, Pir Jo Goth | Sindh Auqaf — no relationship yet |
| **KP** | Rahman Baba, Pir Baba, Kaka Sahib, Ghamkol Sharif | KP Auqaf |
| **Balochistan** | Shah Noorani, Fateh Pur Sharif, Jhal Magsi shrines | Balochistan Auqaf |

Adil's relationship is with **Punjab**. That's where the ask should go first, and it's also where our coverage is densest. Sindh is the obvious second target — it holds our poet-saints — but it needs a separate approach and probably a separate introduction.

---

## 3. What they hold that nothing else gives us

Our sources so far are devotional and historical: *tazkira* literature, colonial gazetteers, press, Wikipedia. Auqaf holds a different **kind** of record — administrative rather than hagiographic — and it fills precisely the gaps in our schema.

| What Auqaf holds | What it fixes for us |
|---|---|
| **Notified shrine register** — the official list of administered shrines | Instantly tells us which of our 74 Muslim shrines are state-administered, and reveals hundreds we don't have at all |
| **Urs calendar in the Hijri calendar**, with budget provisions | Our single most persistent data gap. Sindh Auqaf publishes urs dates by Islamic date — Bhittai's is Safar 14. We currently record Gregorian dates that go stale within a year |
| **Custodianship records** — who administers each site, Auqaf vs family *gaddi* | Connects directly to Adil's research on religious institutions and authority |
| **Endowment, land and revenue records** | Institutional and economic detail no devotional source contains |
| **Attendance and *ʿurs* footfall estimates** | We currently carry visitor numbers only for the 9 field-surveyed sites |
| **Shrine-wise development and conservation files** | Dating of modern construction — exactly what our `year_built` field keeps getting wrong |

---

## 4. Two separate asks, two separate channels

These get conflated. They shouldn't be — they go to different people and carry different weight.

### Ask A — the records
An institutional data-sharing request. Goes to the Secretary or Director-General. Best framed as an academic collaboration, not a favour.

### Ask B — the access letter for Saifullah
A short letter on department letterhead stating that the bearer is conducting an authorised documentation survey of shrines and requesting cooperation from shrine administrators. This is operationally worth more than it sounds. At Auqaf-administered shrines it converts Saifullah from a stranger with a camera into someone with standing — which affects photography permission, access to the grave chamber, and above all whether the *khadims* and administrator will sit for a recorded interview. It costs the department nothing.

**One thing worth raising in the same conversation:** many shrines restrict photography, and Bibi Pak Daman has women-only spaces a male enumerator cannot enter at all. If a female enumerator is ever funded, she'd need her own letter.

---

## 5. Start with what's already public

Before any permission is needed: **Auqaf publishes per-shrine pages on its own website** — `auqaf.punjab.gov.pk` has dedicated pages for Data Darbar, Mian Mir, Bibi Pak Daman and others.

That is public, citable, official, and available today. It's the highest-authority source we could have for administrative facts, and it costs nothing to use. Two immediate actions:

1. **Harvest the public shrine pages now** and cross-check them against our 74 Muslim shrines. This will surface naming discrepancies and probably some dates.
2. **Use it as evidence in the ask.** "We already cite your published shrine pages; we're asking for the underlying register" is a much easier conversation than a cold request.

---

## 6. If the informal ask stalls — RTI

Punjab has a genuinely functional access-to-information law: the **Punjab Transparency and Right to Information Act 2013**.

- Apply to the department's Public Information Officer, on a form or plain paper.
- **You are not required to give a reason.** You need only describe the information adequately.
- The officer must respond within **14 working days**.
- Refusal or silence can be appealed to the **Punjab Information Commission**.
- **Get a receipt** — it's how the application is traced.

Treat this as a fallback, not an opening move. Filing an RTI against a department you're mid-collaboration with reads as adversarial. But it's worth knowing the floor exists: if the relationship cools or the department changes hands, the register is still legally obtainable. Sindh and KP have their own equivalent laws.

---

## 7. What we bring to the table

This matters — the ask is much stronger framed as reciprocity than as a request.

- **We built and handed over a working mosque map** covering 437 mosques across 24 districts. That was unpaid, and it's a functioning public asset the department now owns.
- **Cross-linking already benefits them.** Adil's point about linking every Auqaf-administered shrine on our map back to the Auqaf directory is real reciprocal visibility.
- **We are offering to publish their heritage to a global audience**, in English, in a form that no departmental website is going to produce.
- **Harvard affiliation** carries weight in this context, used lightly.

The framing that works: *we have already given you something; we would like to go further, together.*

---

## 8. Realistic expectations

- **The urs calendar is the most likely yes.** It's already semi-public, published annually, and non-sensitive.
- **The notified shrine register is a probable yes.** It's an administrative list, not confidential.
- **Revenue and land records are the least likely.** Money and land are politically sensitive, and *nazrana* income at major shrines is a live subject. Ask, but don't lead with it, and don't let it sink the rest.
- **Expect delay.** Weeks, not days, and probably a follow-up or two. Start now precisely because it's slow.
- **Get a named contact.** A department contact who has agreed to help is worth more than any single document, because it survives the first request.

## 9. Draft ask for Adil to send

> **Subject:** Documentation of Punjab's shrines — request for collaboration
>
> Dear [Secretary / Director-General],
>
> Following our presentation of the Auqaf mosque directory, I am writing about a related project that I hope may be of interest to the Department.
>
> We are building a free public archive documenting the shrines and sacred sites of Pakistan — their histories, the lives of the saints associated with them, the devotional practices they sustain, and the events they host. It currently covers over 160 sites across all four provinces and Azad Kashmir, and is published openly with no commercial use. Our aim is that a visitor anywhere in the world can find out about a shrine, its saint, and when to visit.
>
> Much of what we hold is drawn from Urdu *tazkira* literature and published histories. What such sources cannot provide is the administrative record — and this is where the Department's holdings are without equal. We would be very grateful for access to:
>
> 1. The register of shrines administered by the Department, with district and custodianship details;
> 2. The annual *urs* calendar, with dates in the Hijri calendar;
> 3. Any shrine-wise historical, development or conservation records the Department is able to share.
>
> We would of course cite the Department as the source throughout, and we would be glad to link every Auqaf-administered shrine in our archive back to the Department's own directory.
>
> Separately, we have a field researcher, Mr Saifullah Imtiaz, documenting shrines and recording oral histories with custodians. A brief letter of introduction on Departmental letterhead, requesting the cooperation of shrine administrators, would greatly assist his work — particularly in arranging interviews with *khadims* and *sajjāda nashīns*.
>
> I would welcome the chance to discuss this, and to show you the archive as it stands.
>
> With respect and thanks,
> [Name]

---

## 10. The one-line case

We handed them a mosque map for free. Their shrine register is three times the size of our entire archive. **Asking costs an email; not asking costs us the single best dataset in the country.**
