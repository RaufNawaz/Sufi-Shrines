# Place description brief: an article for every place and province, from the books

**Ruled by Rauf, 30 October 2026, in chat.** He asked that every place tag on a shrine page lead to
a page with "their own descriptions so that everything from the shrines is carried over to the
site", and chose, when asked:

1. **Cities plus provinces.** Every place in the site's place vocabulary that the books discuss,
   and pages for Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan and Azad Kashmir.
2. **The same process as the shrines.** A team version with every citation, then a public English
   version and an Urdu version, each passed by the three verifiers.
3. **Lahore first, as the pilot**, for him to judge the style; the rest run in batches after.
4. **A watcher**: if a usage limit stops the work, it picks up again when the limit resets. The
   scheduled task `Sufi-Shrines place articles (PC)` does that, from the queue below.

This brief is binding on whoever drafts, a scheduled firing or a worker it briefs. It reuses the
two shrine briefs and says only what is different for a place. Read all three:
`docs/prompts/DESCRIPTION_REWRITE_BRIEF.md` (team version), `docs/prompts/PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md`
(public English, Urdu, verifiers). CLAUDE.md RULE 2 governs every sentence.

## The material

`entries/places/sources/<slug>.sources.md`, generated by `pipeline/book_queue/build_place_sources.py`
(never edit it; rebuild). It holds, verbatim:

- `## The books`: each book's `*Source:*` line, from which its bibliography item is built.
- One `## From <Author, Title>` section per book, holding that book's
  `### Place-level material: <Name>` blocks (mapping ruling (a), 21 September 2026: a book's material
  about a place, attached to every row there) and, for a province, its
  `### Province-level material: <province>` blocks (the province sweep of 5 to 7 October). Under each
  block heading, the rows it was attached to.
- For a place a whole book is about, that book's general material (for Lahore, Kanhaiya Lal's
  *Tarikh-e Lahore* `## Cross-cutting material`: custodianship, endowments, offerings, the state and
  religious property, the urs and the mela, building and repair, the city's religious economy), under
  `## From <Author, Title>: whole-book material, "<section>"`.

That is the only source. **Not the web, not general knowledge of the city, not the shrine articles.**
If the books do not say it, the article does not say it. The site's place page already lists every
site, figure and observance the archive records there; the article is the books' account of the
place itself, which no other page carries.

## What the article is about

A place article is about **the place**: its history as the books tell it, its rulers and builders
where the books tie them to religious life, its saints and holy sites as a landscape, its religious
communities and how they lived side by side, the economy of its shrines and temples, its festivals,
its poetry, and how the books themselves describe it. It is not a list of shrines (the page lists
those) and it does not retell one shrine's article.

- **Keep every passage that speaks of the place.** "The more information we are able to capture the
  better" (Rauf, 13 October 2026) holds here too. A place's article may be long; Lahore's will be.
- **A passage the source block attaches to the place but which is about one site** belongs to that
  site's own article and is already there. Name the site in a sentence where it helps the reader see
  the place (a landmark, a quarter's shrine, a festival that fills the city), but do not rebuild that
  site's history here.
- **Material the block marks as weak** (a bare transit, "marched via", a table of contents heading
  that is only a locator, a publisher's imprint, "weigh accordingly"): leave it out, or give it as
  what it is ("the chronicle lists a section on the buildings of Lahore under Jahangir"), never as
  a fact about the place. Record which in the notes.
- **A province article** is about the province as the books' own unit: a Mughal *suba*, the
  *wilayat* of Multan, "the Punjab" of a nineteenth century author that runs from Delhi to Peshawar.
  Say whose unit it is and when, as the block does; never map a historical unit onto the modern
  province's borders. Material the sweep left `not attached` is not in the source file and stays out.
- **Places outside Pakistan** named in a block (Delhi, Jalandhar, the Deccan) appear only as the
  block uses them, in relation to the place.

## The three versions

| file | what it is | rules |
|---|---|---|
| `entries/places/<slug>.md` | **TEAM version**, every claim cited | `DESCRIPTION_REWRITE_BRIEF.md`: citations, quotation, verse, `## Where the Sources Disagree`, formatting, bibliography. Differences below. |
| `entries/places/<slug>.notes.md` | for Rauf: what was left out and why, weak material, disagreements, verse quoted, questions | as for the shrines |
| `entries/places_public/<slug>.md` | **PUBLIC English** | `PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md` in full (voice, no citations, no source narration, consensus rule, legends as legends, length 0.40 to 0.85, bibliography verbatim) |
| `entries/places_public/<slug>.ur.md` | **PUBLIC Urdu** | `PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md`, "The Urdu version", in full |
| `entries/places_public/<slug>.notes.md` | verdict stamps and choices | as for the shrines |
| `entries/places_public/verify/<slug>.<V>.r<N>.txt` | every verifier reply | as for the shrines |

**Team version, what differs from a shrine's:**

- There is no current Description to keep: the sources file is the whole material.
- Cite exactly as for a shrine: `(Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 92, folio 85)`, the title a substring of
  the `## From` heading, the page reference exactly as the block gives it. A whole-book section is
  cited like any other passage of that book.
- **Headings for a place** (use the ones the material fills, in roughly this order):
  `## Overview` · `## History` (as the books tell it) · `## A City of Saints` / `## Holy Places`
  (the landscape of shrines, temples, gurdwaras, graveyards) · `## Communities` (how traditions
  lived side by side, conversion, conflict, shared sites) · `## Shrines, Endowments and the State`
  (custodianship, *waqf*, offerings, the colonial and Pakistani state) · `## Festivals and Practice`
  · `## In Verse` · `## The Place in the Books` (how authors of different centuries saw it, each
  framed as that author's view) · `## Where the Sources Disagree` · `## Bibliography`.
- **Headings for a province**: `## Overview` · `## The Province in History` · `## Saints and
  Pilgrimage` · `## Communities` · `## Language, Poetry and Music` · `## Shrines and the State` ·
  `## The Province in the Books` · `## Where the Sources Disagree` · `## Bibliography`.
- Rename freely to fit the material; never add a heading the material cannot fill.

**Public version and Urdu**: exactly `PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md`, with "The Saint" and "The Shrine"
headings replaced by the place headings above (minus the bibliography's apparatus and the
disagreement section, as for a shrine). Urdu headings in the same spirit (`## تعارف`, `## تاریخ`,
`## اولیا کا شہر`, `## برادریاں`, `## اوقاف اور ریاست`, `## میلے اور رسوم`, `## شاعری میں`,
`## کتابوں میں`). Verifier prompts V1, V2 and V3 are used as written there, with the file paths of
this brief: V1 reads `entries/places/<slug>.md` and `entries/places_public/<slug>.md`.

## A team version too long for one writer

Lahore's sources run to about 410,000 characters, Punjab's to 306,000 and Sindh's to 241,000. Split
the work, never the article:

1. **Section drafters**, one per group of books (about 80,000 to 120,000 characters of source each),
   each writing its share of the article under the headings above into
   `entries/places/drafts/<slug>.part<N>.md`, every claim cited, with a notes part beside it.
2. **One composer** reads the parts (not the sources) and writes the single team version: merges
   the parts under each heading into one narrative, keeps every cited claim, keeps every
   disagreement in `## Where the Sources Disagree`, and builds the one bibliography. It may not add
   a claim that is in no part.
3. The coordinator runs `check_place_description.py team <slug>` (exit 0), which proves every
   citation and quotation against the sources, so a composer's slip is caught.

The parts stay in `entries/places/drafts/` as the record.

## What the Lahore pilot settled (30 October 2026, HANDOVER §9.423)

- **Prose is English only** (Rauf, 18 October 2026, `docs/prompts/URDU_OUT_OF_PROSE_BRIEF.md`). Every
  Urdu, Persian or Punjabi prose quotation is given in English; the original script appears only in
  verse paragraphs. Tell every drafter so.
- **An `[OCR?]` inside a verse line stays.** The checker allows it; removing it makes a run the sources
  do not hold.
- **Split by heading group for every stage of a long article**: section drafters by books, composers by
  headings, public writers, V1, Urdu writers, V2 and V3 by the same four heading groups. A round's
  verdict is the conjunction of its groups: it fails if any group fails, and the groups' replies are
  saved together as the round's one report.
- **One spelling per name across the groups.** The coordinator normalises before V2 and V3 confirm.
- **Never skip a confirming round.** On Lahore every round of fixes introduced at least one new fault.
- The worker prompts and helpers that ran the pilot are in
  `docs/prompts/place_description_workers_2026-10-30/` with a README giving the order.

## The short article (Rauf, 30 October 2026)

On the Lahore pilot Rauf said the public article (190,520 characters) "is too long", and ruled: the
team version stays the internal record, "far more detailed than the public one"; the site shows "an
article that is short and complete" and lets the reader "switch to the longer one if you want or toggle
which sections you need more information for"; the length **scales by place**; then, "Lahore should be around 8000 words".

So every place whose long public prose is at least 5,000 words gets a fourth and fifth file:

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `entries/places_public/<slug>.short.md` | **SHORT English**: what a reader sees first |
| `entries/places_public/<slug>.short.ur.md` | **SHORT Urdu** |
| `entries/places_public/<slug>.short.notes.md` | its verdict stamps and choices |
| `entries/places_public/verify/<slug>.short.<V>.r<N>.txt` | its verifier replies |

- **Source: the long public article only** (`<slug>.md`), which V1 has already held to the team
  version. Nothing the long article lacks. Its bibliography, verbatim.
- **Exactly the long article's `## ` headings, in the same order**, every one with prose, so the site
  can show any one section's long text in its place ("More on this") or the whole long article ("Full
  account"). The short Urdu has exactly the long Urdu's headings.
- **Complete**: each section is the essentials of its long section, told as narrative: the facts a
  visitor most needs, the established dates and names, the key saints, sites, communities, festivals
  and a verse or two where the long section's verse carries the point. Not a list of topics, not a
  teaser. A reader who reads only the short article knows the place.
- **Length scaled by place, in words**: target `44 x sqrt(long prose words)`, accepted 0.85 to 1.15
  of the target, so that Lahore (long 33,267 words) comes to about 8,000 as Rauf asked. Large places
  get longer short articles, but more slowly than their material grows (a long article of 10,000
  words gives 4,400; one of 5,000 gives 3,100).
- **Verifiers**: V1 reads the long English (as its "team version") and the short English, and checks
  fidelity, no new fact, nothing distorted by compression (a hedge, a "tradition holds", an "accounts
  differ" that the long text needs must survive), and **completeness at short length**: each section
  carries what is most important in its long section. V2 and V3 as for any Urdu. Same stopping rules.
- `python3 pipeline/book_queue/check_place_description.py short <slug>` (S1 to S5, SL, and the public
  checks P1 to P5, Q1, Q2, U1 to U9, V against the long article); the queue's steps after the long
  article is done are `draft-short-en`, `verify-short-en`, `draft-short-ur`, `verify-short-ur`;
  verdicts with `place_description_queue.py verdict <slug>.short V1 PASS 1`.
- `scripts/data/build-place-articles.mjs` adds `short` to the place's data file only when its own three
  stamps pass; `PlaceArticle` then opens on the short article, with "Full account" for the whole long
  one and "More on this" under each section for that section's long text.

## The tools

```bash
python3 pipeline/book_queue/build_place_sources.py              # sources for every place (exit 1 on an unassigned block)
python3 pipeline/book_queue/place_description_queue.py status   # every place and its step
python3 pipeline/book_queue/place_description_queue.py next 3   # what to do now
python3 pipeline/book_queue/check_place_description.py team   <slug>
python3 pipeline/book_queue/check_place_description.py public <slug> [--english-only | --no-verdicts]
python3 pipeline/book_queue/check_place_description.py short  <slug> [--english-only | --no-verdicts]
python3 pipeline/book_queue/place_description_queue.py verdict <slug> V1|V2|V3 PASS|FAIL <round>
node scripts/data/build-place-articles.mjs [--check]            # the site's data file, from passed articles only
```

The site shows a place's article only when all three verdicts are PASS on the current files
(`scripts/data/build-place-articles.mjs` refuses anything else), so a half finished article can sit
in the folders without reaching a reader.

## One firing (the scheduled task, or an interactive session)

1. Read the newest 3 or 4 `docs/HANDOVER.md` §9 sections, then
   `place_description_queue.py status` and `next 3`.
2. Run each listed place's next step, **writing every result to the PC the moment it exists**
   (each part, each draft, each verifier report and verdict). Claim the step first
   (`notes_claim.py claim place__<slug> --session <session>`), release it when the step's files are
   on the PC. A step cut off by the usage limit is rerun from the start by the next firing; a file in
   the cloud scratch folder that never reached the PC does not count.
3. Order inside a place: team (parts, compose, check), then public English and V1 until it passes,
   then the Urdu, then V2 and V3 together, then the short English, its V1, the short Urdu, its V2 and V3; the stopping rules and the late round shortcut of
   `PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md` apply unchanged.
4. A step that is `pilot-review` is not work: the rest of the queue waits until Rauf lists `lahore`
   in `data/review/places_approved.txt`. A step at `rauf` goes into the run record's "For Rauf".
5. When a place reaches `done`, run `node scripts/data/build-place-articles.mjs` so the site's data
   file carries it.
6. End with a §9 HANDOVER section (`Stage: places`): places advanced and to which step, choices made,
   anything for Rauf. Nothing is committed by a firing; Rauf commits.
