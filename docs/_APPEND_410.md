**Consensus choices** (full lists in each `.notes.md`):

- `dargah-pir-ratan-nath-jee`: the temple's saint, Pir Ratan Nath (also Baba Pir Ratan Nath), a Hindu yogi and saint of the Nath order, basis (a); his century "cannot be fixed with confidence", basis (a). The figures of the books (the Ratannath of the Nath movement, "a contemporary of Gorakhnath"; Shaikh or Hajji Baba Ratan; the Delhi *dargah mandir* of a Pir Baba Ratannath) are told in their own section, "A Name with Many Identities", which opens by saying it is not settled whether any of them is the temple's saint, basis (d); nothing from them is given to the temple's saint. V1 round 1 failed the sentence listing the team version's three readings of the Delhi endnote side by side (one of them "placed in the wrong city"), now "Whether the Delhi shrine honours the same figure as the Peshawar temple is not settled." The Hajji and the yogi "sometimes joined into one figure, but there is no consensus that they are one", basis (d). The Naths spread "from roughly the early medieval centuries", basis (a); "from the eleventh century" dropped. Peshawar's Hindus after 1947 told at the city's scale in The Temple and the province's in the Setting, not as rivals; the two reasons one author gives for the calm of 1947 at the frontier are not chosen or merged.
- `shrine-at-odero-lal-udero-lal-teerath-asthan`: Udero Lal venerated at the village of that name, basis (a) and (b); Sorley's use of the name for the Qalandar of Sehwan kept as one sentence, "a different saint at a different place". The deliverance legend in the site's telling (Mirkh Shah of Thatta, Nasarpur), basis (a), with "it is told in more than one way"; Boivin's telling and its mid tenth century date dropped. Who the saint was: "accounts of who he was differ", then each community's view as its own tradition (Jhulelal, an incarnation of Varuna; Sheikh Tahir, born a Hindu named Udero Lal; Zinda Pir, a name both communities honour). Sheikh Tahir's date: "the sixteenth or the seventeenth century", basis (d). The sharing of the sanctuary told as one picture of compatible facts (separate chapels and custodians, the lamp and the Indus shared, the one way crossing); the site's "the boundary dissolves" neither asserted nor denied. Near Tando Adam and about 50 kilometres north of Hyderabad both kept (not contradictory).
- `dargah-of-khwaja-muhammad-zaman-luari-sharif`: Khwaja Muhammad Zaman (1713 to 1775), the honorific Sultan al-Aulia as tradition, his father Shaikh Abdul Latif and Khwaja Abul Masakin as teachers, basis (a). The khanqah "traditionally dated to 1737, though accounts of when Luari became a Naqshbandi centre differ" (V1 round 1 reworded the clause, which had read as a dispute over the khanqah's own date). The master of eighteenth century Sind in *Pain and Grace* (Makhdum Muhammad Zaman Lanwari) kept in his own section, "A Master of the Same Name", which opens "may be the saint of Luari, though the identification is not certain"; inside it he is never called the saint, and his master Mian Muhammad Thattawi is never hung under Abul Masakin. Diwan Gidumal's gift: the two tellings name different invaders, so neither is named and only their shared core is told, basis (d). Damaged readings left out (the tazkira's death year, Abu'l-qasim, the Marui lament, the years 927 and 1001); Bhandari's Debal clause left out until the renderings are checked.
