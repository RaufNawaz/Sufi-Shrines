# Follow-up email to Adil

**Subject:** Shrines site — full data audit, and what I found underneath

---

Dear Adil,

I hope you are doing well. Following on from my last update, where everything was about the site itself, I have now gone through the underlying database properly, row by row, all 163 of them. I wanted to give you a full account, because what I found was not what I expected. The writing is genuinely good. The problem is the structured data around it, and a handful of errors that are visible on the live site today.

I will start with the one that concerns me most. The Allo Mahar entry lists Pir Syed Muhammad Channan Shah Nuri in its saint field, and then describes Sayyid Faiz-ul-Hassan Shah for seven hundred words. Both men are real, and both are from that same village, which is exactly why it is so easy to miss. Given the village name and a thin source, the model wrote about whichever of the two was better documented online and never checked that against the row it was filling in. The same pattern appears at the Tomb of Javindi Bibi, where the row names Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari and gives the year 1291, while the description is about Bibi Jawindi and her tomb of around 1493. I have deliberately not written a replacement biography for either. Fixing a fabrication by generating a second one would defeat the purpose, so I have cut Allo Mahar back to a short honest entry that names both men, flags it as unresolved, and lists the questions an enumerator needs to answer. It is in Sialkot district, well within Saifullah's normal range, and one visit settles it.

A few other things are live right now that I would rather you did not see for the first time in front of the department. Lal Shahbaz Qalandar's events field reads "No events scheduled right now," sitting directly beside a description of one of the largest urs gatherings in the country. Gurdwara Dera Sahib is plotted at longitude 74.0000, which puts it about twenty-five kilometres outside Lahore in farmland, and the trailing zeros look like a truncation rather than a typo, so I checked every other coordinate for the same signature and found more. Four entries have internal notes visible to visitors, including one that says "flag for a browser-enabled image pass" and several that refer to spreadsheet row numbers. None of these are hard to fix; they simply were never caught because nothing was checking.

The deeper issue is that the schema has started to strain, and the entries have begun apologising for themselves in the public text. Chandragup, which is a mud volcano, now says "It is filed here under 'Hindu Temple' for consistency with the dataset's schema." The Sain Vali Vilayat Rai darbar says "entered here under 'Hindu Temple' as the closest of the dataset's three categories." When I went through all of them, I found fourteen Sindhi Nanakpanthi and Udasi darbars that belong to neither the Hindu nor the Sikh bucket, since they install the Guru Granth Sahib alongside Hindu images, along with three Jain temples and three sites with no devotional life at all, including Qutbuddin Aibak's tomb. I have re-catalogued every row across three separate axes instead of one, so the tradition, the built form, and whether the site is active, heritage or ruined are each recorded independently. That last one turns out to be genuinely useful for visitors, since it distinguishes Katas Raj, which is festival-only, from Tilla Jogian, which is abandoned, from Prahladpuri, which was destroyed in 1992.

Two of the fields were carrying more meaning than they could hold. Founded/Opened variously contains the saint's birth, his death, the date the building went up, the date of a commemorated event, or free text like "Natural site; veneration is of unrecorded antiquity." Bahauddin Zakariya is recorded as founded in 1167, which is three years before the saint was born. Sufi Saint contains "Shiva (Mahadev)," "Jain Tirthankaras," and "Sikh women and children martyrs." I have split both into proper fields and prepared corrected values for all 162 rows I could match.

Rather than fix these by hand, I built four tools, because the same errors will otherwise come back with the next batch of entries. There is a validator that runs seventeen integrity checks and will not let a bad row through; a cleanup script for the artefacts; a script that turns the free-text bibliographies into a real source registry so we can finally tell a sourced claim from an unsourced one; and a termbase of 349 canonical terms with 1,389 variants, which is both the spelling authority for the linter and something we can paste into the prompt so the next generation run does not render silsila as "chain" or sajjada nashin as "one who sits on the prayer mat."

Running the validator against the live data was instructive. It found 165 errors, but 158 of those were a single separator artefact that one script pass removed, so the real number is eight. The most useful check turned out to be the simplest one: any entry whose named figure never appears in its own description fails. That one rule catches both Allo Mahar and Javindi Bibi, and it costs nothing.

I also found something worth knowing about the sheet itself. Google Sheets' TSV export silently strips the line breaks inside cells, while the CSV export preserves them. This matters because it means that anyone who exports as TSV, edits, and pastes back will flatten the markdown structure across every description in the archive. I nearly did exactly that. Everything is now going through CSV, and I would suggest we treat TSV export as off-limits for this sheet from here on.

On sourcing, and this is the part I think is most relevant to your earlier question about whether the model is leaving things out. The entries drawn from the Tazkirah Awliya-e-Pakistan are running to four, five, six hundred words on what you described as small pieces of information per shrine. The book seeded them and the model supplied the rest. Most of the filler is harmless, but some of it lands on silsila affiliations, teacher-student chains and dates, which are precisely the things a language model invents most fluently, and it reads exactly as authoritative as the sourced sentences beside it. The fix is not to rewrite them blindly, it is to cap the output at what the source supports, store the extracted passage alongside each entry so we can check recall later, and tag the entries honestly in the meantime.

Lastly, one thing I looked into that I think is worth pursuing while the relationship is warm. The Punjab Auqaf department administers 534 shrines. Our entire archive, across all five jurisdictions and every religion, is 163 sites, of which 74 are Muslim shrines. Their register alone is three times the size of everything we have. They publish per-shrine pages already, which we can use and cite today without asking anyone, but the underlying register, the urs calendar in the Hijri calendar, and the custodianship records would fill exactly the gaps we cannot fill from books. Separately, a short letter of introduction on their letterhead would make a real difference to Saifullah's access, particularly for arranging recorded interviews with khadims and sajjada nashins, and it costs the department nothing to provide. I have drafted both asks if you would like to look at them. It is worth noting that Auqaf is provincial rather than federal, so Sindh, where most of our poet-saints are, will need a separate approach.

Nothing here has gone into the sheet yet. The corrections are prepared and tested, and the next step is a single merge and import, which I would rather do carefully than quickly. I am happy to walk you through any of it, or to simply send you the before-and-after report once it is applied.

Please let me know if you have any questions or concerns.

Warm regards,
Rauf
