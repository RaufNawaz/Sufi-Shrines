
### 9.416, 30 October 2026 (interactive, ~02:00Z to ~02:40Z): Stage = notes. Each quoted verse now says what language it is in, and its translation what language that is in

**Rauf (in the chat):** "For the differentiation between the urdu and punjabi make it more obvious". In the Urdu article a Punjabi kafi and its Urdu translation were both Nastaliq, both centred, one above the other, and nothing said which was the Punjabi.

**What the page does now.** A caption between two short hairlines names the language above the original, and another names the translation's language above the translation: "پنجابی" then "اردو" in the Urdu article, "PUNJABI" then "ENGLISH" (small capitals) in the English. Persian reads "فارسی" / "Persian", Guru Nanak's lines "گرمکھی" / "Gurmukhi", Farid's "پنجابی، گرمکھی میں" / "Punjabi, in Gurmukhi", Sindhi and Seraiki likewise. A verse with no rendering after it still gets its caption. No caption where the original is in the article's own language (Iqbal's Urdu in the Urdu article) or where the language is not recorded. The Urdu translation is also set a step smaller (0.92em under the original's 1.06em), so it reads as the gloss and not as a second verse in the same script. Seen on a cloud dev server: Madho Lal Hussain's kafi and Data Darbar's chronogram in Urdu, the same kafi in English, Guru Nanak in Urdu, Bulleh Shah at 390 px.

**Where the languages come from.** `entries/verse_originals/languages.json`: all 67 distinct verse paragraphs the articles quote (in every row of the sheet and every Urdu article; the Urdu articles have no verse the English lacks), each with its language and the basis, which is the article naming it or the verse's grammar. 27 Persian, 14 Punjabi, 10 Urdu, 9 Gurmukhi, 1 each Punjabi in Gurmukhi, Sindhi and Seraiki, and 4 left unlabelled (the chronogram phrases پیر حق پرست, مشتاق منان, شمع جمع سیادت, and Guru Arjan's mixed line کوئی بولے رام رام). Guru Nanak's verse is labelled by script, not language, because the gurdwara article says he composed "in Hindi" and a "Punjabi" label would contradict it.

**Mechanics.** `pipeline/verse_languages.py --write` writes `src/data/verse-languages.json` (a 32 bit FNV-1a hash of each verse to its code, 1.2 KB, the same hash as `src/components/shrine/verseLanguage.ts`); `--check <sheet csv>` fails on any verse paragraph in the sheet or in `urdu-content.json` that the record lacks (0 today). Labels are UI strings (`verseLang*` in `uiStrings.ts` and `uiStrings.ur.ts`). The caption is a `div`, not a `p`, because tests and the article count paragraphs. Tests: `verseLanguage.test.tsx` (5: Punjabi and Urdu in the Urdu article, Punjabi and English in the English, none for Urdu in Urdu or an unrecorded verse, Gurmukhi by script, the map equal to the record).
