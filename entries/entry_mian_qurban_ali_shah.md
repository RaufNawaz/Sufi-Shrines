# Darbar Mian Qurban Ali Shah

## Overview

Darbar Mian Qurban Ali Shah is a Muslim shrine at Mint Stop, Lahore, dedicated to Hazrat Mian Qurban Ali Shah Sarkar. The survey records the site's affiliation as Naqshbandī Mujaddidī, within Ahl-e Sunnat; this is the only *silsila* (Sufi order, and the lineage of spiritual transmission within it) information the source gives. A mosque named after the saint stands in the same courtyard as the tomb. The site has been in the charge of the government Auqaf (religious endowments) Department since 2005.

## The figure, as the tradition relates him

The account given to the surveyor states that Hazrat Qurban Ali Shah was born in 1341 AH in Malerkotla, India, that his given name was Qurban Ali, and that people later came to call him Mian Qurban Ali Shah in recognition of his learning. His father is named as Hazrat Mian Fazal Deen, described in the survey as a great religious scholar who held thousands of disciples in a relationship of *pīrī-murīdī* (spiritual mentorship) and who taught his son the whole of his education before instructing him to devote his life to preaching. Devotees relate that virtuous people and Sufis were repeatedly born into this family, and that its members kept the call of Islam alive throughout their lives.

The survey relates that he left his ancestral home in youth at his father's command, never returned, and preached along the way until he reached the Mint Stop area of Lahore, which he made his final abode. Tradition holds that people were drawn to his camp of their own accord; that he arranged food, drink and lodging for all who came, young and old alike; that thousands took *baiʿat* (the oath of allegiance to a spiritual guide) at his hand, often with their entire families; and that neither he nor his family ever compelled anyone's conversion, people entering Islam instead out of regard for his learning and character. The account states that among those he brought into Islam were a large number of people it names as "Marzaiyas (Ahmadis/Qadianis)". Devotees relate that he served people by day and spent his nights in *dhikr* (remembrance of God), and that although his bearing was that of a simple dervish he did not disclose his spiritual station. According to the survey he passed on the *gaddi* (spiritual seat) in the last part of his life, died on 25 Rabīʿ al-Thānī, and was buried at his camp.

## Reputation and *karāmāt*

The survey reports that those seeking a cure for illness, the unemployed seeking work, and people caught in difficulty and worry would present themselves to him, that he would pray to God on their behalf, and that those prayers were granted without delay. Devotees still come to pray through his *waseela* (intercession) and say they do not return empty-handed. Those who met him in life are described as knowing his spiritual manifestations at first hand; others entered his allegiance on the testimony of their elders. His devotees hold that, having left the world while engaged in preaching, his standing is no less than a martyr's, and that martyrs remain alive.

## The site and its buildings

The survey states that his camp itself became his shrine: after his death a shrine was raised over the burial place, and a mosque was built in his name alongside it. Beneath the tomb is a basement, closed to the general public, which the survey identifies as the room where he kept his nightly *dhikr*; it has been left in its original condition and is said to remain exactly as he left it. His special disciple is buried beside him. The Auqaf Department took responsibility for the shrine and mosque in 2005, but most maintenance and decoration is still carried out by devotees themselves. The shrine was reconstructed again in 2022. No graveyard is reported at the site.

## Regular observances

The five daily prayers are offered in the courtyard mosque. On Thursday, Friday and Sunday the large door of the shrine is kept open; on the remaining days the small door is open, and worshippers and pilgrims come throughout. Every Thursday there is a public gathering with *dhikr*, *naʿt* (devotional verse in praise of the Prophet) recitation and *langar* (free communal food); the survey says a set number of people attend but does not give that number. No *qawwālī* is reported at the site.

## *ʿUrs*

The annual *ʿurs* (death-anniversary observance) is held on 24, 25 and 26 Rabīʿ al-Thānī. It includes drumming (dhol), the changing of the chadar over the grave, ritual washing (*ghusl*) and *langar*.

## Attendance and custodianship

Daily attendance is put at 500 to 600 people, and at the annual *ʿurs* at some 50,000 to 60,000 pilgrims. Residents of the neighbourhood around the shrine pay their respects there each morning before going to work. The custodian of record is the government Auqaf Department. The survey separately reports that the saint's successor in the *gaddi* was Haji Tufail, and that after Haji Tufail's death his sons hold the position of managing the site.

## Writings

The survey reports that a disciple published a discourse on the saint's life a short while ago, that it became widely popular, and that readers subsequently entered the saint's allegiance in greater numbers. No title is given and no copy was supplied to the surveyor.
