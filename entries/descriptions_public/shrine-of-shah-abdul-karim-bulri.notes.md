## Consensus choices

- **Generations between him and Shah Abdul Latif:** "Accounts differ over how many generations separated the two, but by tradition Shah Abdul Latif was his descendant in the fourth generation". Basis (a), the site's account (great-great-grandson), which Shackle and Sorley (twice) support. Basis (b) is a tie: four generations in the site's account, Shackle and Sorley; three in Alam Faqri (as the site's account reports it), Schimmel (*Pain and Grace*, *As Through a Veil*) and Boivin. The three generation count is not listed. "In the fourth generation" is Sorley's own phrase ("His ancestor in the fourth generation before him"), used to avoid the hyphenated "great-great-grandson". Schimmel's uncounted statement that Shah Habib, Shah Abdul Latif's father, was his descendant is kept, since it agrees with every count.
- **His dates:** born 1536, died 1623. Basis (a), the site's account, with Schimmel; Shackle and the index of *Mystical Dimensions of Islam* give the same death year. Sorley's floruit "circa A.D. 1600" is consistent with these dates and is dropped silently. No source gives a conflicting year, so no "accounts differ" clause.
- **The district:** Tando Muhammad Khan district (basis (a), the site's account, which the team version calls today's placing). Sorley's Hyderabad district is kept as a time anchored fact, "By 1940 ... his *dargah* at Bulri, then counted in the Hyderabad district", following the team version's own "where the site's account today places it in Tando Muhammad Khan district"; told as change over time (as in the Shah Kamal pilot), not as a variant. The *taluka* name, damaged in print, is left out.
- **The family and Matiari:** not a real conflict. Born into a Sayyid family of Matiari and spent most of his life at Bulri (site's account); the family went back to the neighbourhood of Matiari in the generations before Shah Abdul Latif's birth, how it came to Bulri and why it returned being unknown (Sorley). Both kept.
- **Who first used the folk tales:** "among the first" (basis (a), the site's account), which agrees with every source. Schimmel's "seems to be the first one" is not used; her line of three (veiled in Qadi Qadan, more obvious in Shah Abdul Karim, full bloom in the *Risalo*) is kept because it agrees with "among the first".
- **The worth of his verse:** "Judgements of the merit of the verse that survives differ, but it is most often counted pioneering, and it has been said to contain all the ingredients of later mystical poetry." Basis (b): the site's account ("pioneer"), Shackle ("pioneering"), Schimmel ("all the ingredients of later mystical poetry") and Boivin (motifs traced to him) against Sorley ("nothing of outstanding merit"). Sorley's sentence is not quoted.
- **His lines in Shah Abdul Latif's poetry:** "He also inserted some of his ancestor's lines into his own poetry" (Schimmel), with the fragment from *Pain and Grace* p. 206 told in The Poet. Common ground with Sorley, who reports the belief that a few of his verses are in the *Risalo*. Sorley's separate attribution of inserted verses to Inayat is not listed.
- **The record of his sayings:** the site's account names and dates the work (Mir Daryai Tharawi, *Bayan al-Arifin*, 1630); Shackle's undated "lengthy Persian memoir composed by a disciple" and Schimmel's "Persian malfuzat" are told as the same book, as the team version itself treats them ("Shackle, who does not name the work or date it"; "Schimmel calls the work").
- **The modern studies:** Motilal Jotwani's monograph (New Delhi, Kumar Brothers, 1970); the Karachi study of 1986 (Sindi Kitab Ghar and Indus Publications) given without its author's name, since Boivin lists the author as "Motwani, Motilal" and the team version does not identify him with Jotwani; Jotwani's work of 1996 with a complete translation. Not merged.

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: *dargah* (the shrine complex of a Sufi saint), *ʿurs* (the yearly commemoration of the saint's death), *murid* (a disciple pledged to a Sufi master), Sayyid (descendants of the Prophet Muhammad).

Plain dictionary senses, for terms the glossary lacks: *malfuzat* (the recorded sayings of a Sufi master); *madrasa* (the school of religious learning, as in the Shah Jamal pilot); *jogi* (the Hindu ascetic, as the Javindi row glosses yogi); *mero* (given by the team version itself as the Sindhi name of the fair); *gaddi nashin* (holder of the seat of spiritual authority); the Qalandariyya (the order of the Qalandars, wandering dervishes, the pilots' gloss of Qalandars); the *Risalo* (the collected poetry of Shah Abdul Latif); the *Masnavi* (the long mystical poem of Jalaluddin Rumi); OM (the sacred syllable of the Hindus); *alif* and *mim* (Arabic letters); Lower Sind (the south of the province).
