## Judgement calls for V1

1. **The opening clause of the story.** "The story is told in more than one way, but its heart is the same." It stands for every minor variant of the episode (age, sum, companion, whom he fed, what he gave, the father's answer) so that none is listed. "its heart is the same" rests on every telling sharing the money given for trade, the hungry holy men fed with it, and the bargain called true. Fallback: "The story is told in more than one way."
2. **The janamsakhi paragraph.** Dhillon's telling is kept as the fuller account "In the *janamsakhis*" (the Chakki Sahib and Kiara Sahib framing of his material), with only the details that do not contradict the site's telling: the purchase in Chuharkana from the biggest wholesale grocer, "enough to feed fifty starving" holy men (Dhillon's sadhus), vegetables, fruit and firewood, two cooks and two porters, the grove of trees, the boy's two sayings, the father calling him a failure, and Rai Bular's defence ("a whole akhara of famished sadhus" given as "a whole band of famished holy men").
