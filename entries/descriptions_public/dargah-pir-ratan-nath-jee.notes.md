## Consensus choices

- **Whether the books' Ratannath is the temple's saint:** not settled, basis (d): "it is not settled whether any of them is the saint of this temple." The figure level material (Ratannath of the Nath movement, Hajji Baba Ratan, the Delhi *dargah mandir*) is told in its own section, "A Name with Many Identities", placed after The Temple and Devotion, and told as being about those figures. Nothing from it (a date, the tales, his importance) is given to the temple's saint. Never merged (rule 4).
- **Where the figure's shrine stands:** the Jhanda Bazar of Peshawar, with his veneration reaching shrines in the wider region including Nepal, basis (a), the site's account. The Delhi *dargah mandir* of a Pir Baba Ratannath is given as a shrine of that name, and whether it honours the same figure as the Peshawar temple is given as not settled, basis (d); the three readings the team version leaves open are not listed (V1 round 1, issue 1).
- **Name and style:** Pir Ratan Nath, also Baba Pir Ratan Nath, basis (a). The figures keep their own spellings (Ratannath; Pir Baba Ratannath), which also keeps them visibly apart from the temple's saint.
- **When he lived:** the temple's saint "cannot be fixed with confidence to a particular century", basis (a). "A contemporary of Gorakhnath" stays with the Ratannath of the Nath movement and is not given to the temple's saint, since their identity is unsettled; so it is not a rival date for him.
- **When the Naths spread:** the site's "from roughly the early medieval centuries", basis (a). "From the eleventh century" is dropped as a minor variant; the rest of that sentence (from their centre at Peshawar to Central Asia and Iran, influencing *qalandars* and Sufis) is kept.
- **Who Ratan was:** the temple's saint is a Hindu yogi and saint of the Nath order, basis (a). Shaikh Baba Ratan, or Hajji Baba Ratan, is given as a separate Muslim figure, "sometimes joined into one figure" with the yogi, "but there is no consensus that they are one", basis (d); the footnote's "a very important figure of the movement" is kept as said of Ratannath in his own right. "Baba Ratan Hajji was identified with Gorakhnath" is kept as an identification ("has also been identified with Gorakhnath").
- **Gor Khatri and Gorakhtari:** minor; Gor Khatri, the site's name, kept; Gorakhtari and "McLeod rightly locates the site in Peshawar" dropped.
- **Peshawar's Hindus after 1947:** not a conflict once each is read at its own scale. The city (the greater part of its Hindus departed, a small community remained) is told in The Temple, basis (a); the province (many Hindus and Sikhs stayed on; Hindus its largest minority) is told in Peshawar and the Frontier.
- **Why the frontier was spared in 1947:** the two reasons (the Indian National Congress; Abdul Ghaffar Khan, the Frontier Gandhi) are on two pages of one book; neither is chosen and they are not merged. Kept only what both passages share: the riots in the province were less intense than in the Punjab, and many Hindus and Sikhs stayed on because of the comparatively peaceful political situation.
- **The founder of the Nath order** (an internal tension of the site's text, not a disagreement the team version lists; team notes, decision 5): the Overview's "founded in the memory of Gorakhnath" is not carried. The public text says the Naths "trace their lineage to Gorakhnath and, before him, to Matsyendranath, the founder of the sect, a figure partly of legend", as the site's second statement does.
