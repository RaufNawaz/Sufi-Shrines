## Consensus choices

- **Whose village (name):** the village takes its name from Makhdoom Burhan-ud-din, remembered locally as Hazrat Langar Makhdoom. Basis (a), the shrine (site account). The other side is not a rival claim: Chishti writes the village's name with Abdul Karim's ("Langar Makhdum Abdul Karim Sahib") and Kanhaiya Lal makes Abdul Karim its resident, but the team version says neither book says from which Makhdum the village took its name. So it is given as a fact of the village's history, not as a variant: "the house of another master, Makhdum Abdul Karim, whose name it once carried as well" (Overview) and "its name was once written with his" (A House of Teaching). No "accounts differ" clause, since no source names a different founder of the name.
- **The saint's name and title:** Makhdoom Burhan-ud-din, Hazrat Langar Makhdoom, basis (a). Bhandari's "Shah Burhan" and Chishti's "Sayyid Burhan al-Din" (p. 299) dropped: neither is tied to this place, the latter is an uncertain reading, and the former's city is a damaged reading.
- **The Hazrat Makhdum Burhan al-Din of Chishti's genealogy (p. 450):** kept, because it is the one legible passage that sets a man of the saint's name in the spiritual line of the master of Langar Makhdoom, but stated without identification: "In Mian Wadda's chain of masters, above Abdul Karim and his own master, stands a Hazrat Makhdum Burhan al-Din, who bears the saint's name. Nor is it settled whether he is the saint of the shrine." Basis (d), no source makes the identification. The chain is not merged with the shrine's saint: nothing of the tradition (1245, the mud dwelling, Changranwala) is attached to the genealogy's man, and nothing of the genealogy is attached to the saint. The intermediate link ("Muhammad and Tayyib", the second name uncertain) and the top link (partly lost) are not named; the line is kept only for its legible point, that a Burhan al-Din stands two generations above Abdul Karim (rule 5).
- **Where the tomb lies:** the shrine's tradition, basis (a): he asked to be buried in his mud dwelling, and his grave is said to lie in a burial ground near Changranwala, in the Lalian area. Bhandari's tomb of Shah Burhan "in a city on the Chenab" (the city's name damaged, Chiniot a conjecture) dropped, by rule 4 and by rule 5. The village's place on the Chenab is kept from Kanhaiya Lal (firm, 1884); Chishti's river with an illegible name dropped.
- **Death year:** "Tradition holds ... that he died in 1245." Only the shrine's tradition gives a year (no source contradicts or confirms it). The Hijri year the team version pairs with it (666 AH) is left out: see For Rauf.
- **The Sufis and conversion in the Punjab:** basis (a), the shrine's position ("the teaching and example of the Sufis, rather than the sword of conquerors, drew much of the rural population of the Punjab towards Islam"), framed "it is held", with one clause that accounts differ: "though there is no settled view of how far the saints themselves converted anyone, or of how quickly, and how consciously, the change came." Schimmel's tribes and Eaton's Jat and Rajput claims are kept as claims, which both sides accept as claims; Eaton's Islam "took root much more readily" in the Punjab and his shrines that integrated pastoral clans are kept in Legacy as general statements about the Punjab and Sind. Eaton's counter evidence (the earliest sources on Baba Farid make no mention of his converting anyone; many clans had not reached the Ajudhan area in his lifetime; the Siyal genealogies of Jhang) is not given side by side.
- **Farid and the Punjab's troubles:** minor; the verse is kept as the poet's praise, and the later annotator's reply ("not historically correct") is dropped silently.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Ismail and Mian Wadda:** the team version leaves open whether they are one man. Both stories are told under their own names, without a count ("two pupils") that would decide it, and with one sentence: "It is not settled whether Mian Wadda and Shaikh Muhammad Ismail are one man." Basis (d).
- **Time anchors:** nothing from Chishti is dated (1867 unconfirmed). Kanhaiya Lal's 1884 is firm but nothing needed a date anchor (the Chenab is given in the present, "The village stands on the river Chenab"). Rizvi's "in the fourteenth century" (the Suhrawardiyya's spread) and "the middle of the twelfth century" (Farid's ancestors) are the team version's own dates. Khalid's renovation of the haveli "a few years before his visit" is given as "More recently" (after the collapse after Partition), not dated.

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: *langar* (communal free kitchen at a shrine), *ʿurs* (yearly commemoration of a saint's death; "wedding" with God, in the team version's own wording "a joyful 'wedding' of the soul with the divine"), *pir* (spiritual guide), *murshid* (spiritual master), *wali* (friend of God), *Suhrawardiyya* (the Sufi order founded by Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi).

Team version's own: the *langar* as "the kitchen where food is cooked and served freely to all who come, regardless of rank or creed" (given as "whatever their rank or creed"); Langar Makhdoom as "the *langar* of the Makhdum"; Friday as "the day of congregational prayer".

Plain dictionary senses: *fatiha* (the opening chapter of the Qur'an, as in purana-bhalwal); Makhdoom (a title of honour for a Sufi master); gnostic (one who knows God); *khirqa* of *khilafat* (the cloak by which a master makes a disciple his deputy); *haveli* (great mansion, as in shah-jamal); *taʿwidh* (written amulets); "son of" for the team version's "b." in Sultan Husain b. Qutb al-Din; "sent to the house of nothingness, that is, put to death" (the Persian idiom's sense).

## For Rauf

- **The death year's Hijri equivalent.** The team version (from the site's account) gives "1245 of the Common Era, 666 of the Hijri calendar". These do not match: 666 AH runs from about September 1267 to September 1268 CE, and 1245 CE falls in 642 to 643 AH. The public draft gives "1245" alone (it agrees with the thirteenth century and the "some eight centuries" of the team version) and drops 666 AH rather than print a mismatched pair. Should the public article keep 1245 alone (as drafted), give 666 AH alone (which would make the year 1267 or 1268), or keep both as the tradition states them? The team version and the sheet may need the same decision.
