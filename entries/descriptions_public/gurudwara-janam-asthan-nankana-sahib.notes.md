## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Year and day of birth:** 1469, with "Accounts of the exact day differ; Sikhs celebrate his birth, the *Prakash Utsav* or *Gurpurab*, on the full moon day of the month of Kartik, in October or November." Year: basis (a) with (b) (the site, Rizvi, Dhillon and the *Walking with Nanak* back matter all give 1469). Day: basis (d), because the site's "commonly rendered as 15 April 1469" has every other source that gives a day against it (Dhillon's 14 April, the back matter's 20 October); the festival day is the site's, basis (a). Left out: 15 April, 14 April, 20 October, and Bhandari's Samvat 1526 / 880 AH in Bahlul Lodi's reign.
- **Place of birth:** Rai Bhoi di Talwandi, now Nankana Sahib, with "Accounts of his birthplace have not always agreed either, but it is generally held to be Talwandi." Basis (a) with (b) (the site, every book, the "unanimous" Janamsakhi writers and Sikh scholars). Left out: Meherban's Dera Chahal, not named.
- **Distance from Lahore:** some eighty kilometres south west, basis (a). Left out silently as a minor variant: Rizvi's "about forty miles".
- **Father's name:** Mehta Kalu, also called Kalyan Das, basis (a). Dhillon's Kalyan Chand Das Bedi and Rizvi's Kalu dropped as minor name variants. Dhillon's line (Shiv Ram, head of a Bedi family, accountant to Rai Bhoe, succeeded by his son, whom Dhillon himself calls Mehta Kalu) is kept whole from the one source.
- **How the birth came about:** only the Muslim tradition of Baba Naulakha Hazari, framed as "A Muslim tradition, which Sikhs do not accept, holds", in Muslims and the Birthplace. Basis (b): the Auqaf booklet at the shrine and the guide's statement, against one source for the alternative. Left out: Mata Tripta praying for a child at an ancient Hindu temple on a mound not far from Talwandi (cited from Dhillon's *The First Sikh Spiritual Master*), a second telling of the same episode.
- **The Guru's wife and her family:** Sulakhini, daughter of Mul Chand and Chando Rani of the town of Batala, "though accounts of the marriage differ". Basis (b) for Batala (Dhillon; Bhandari also puts the marriage at Batala). Left out: Dhillon's 24 September 1487, Rizvi's "about sixteen", Bhandari's marriage after "travelling most of the world", and the reconstruction's Sulakhni, daughter of Moolchand Khatri of Pakhoke near Gurdaspur.
- **The sons:** Sri Chand and Lakhmi Das, with Dhillon's birth dates (8 September 1494, 12 February 1497), which no source contradicts. Basis (b) for Lakhmi Das (Dhillon; Bhandari's Lakhmidas). Left out: the reconstruction's Lachman Das.
- **The journeys:** "Accounts of how many journeys he made differ, but they lasted more than twenty years". Basis (d); "more than twenty years" holds both Dhillon's twenty three (four journeys) and the travelogue's "about twenty-four years". Route: the travelogue's compass summary (Bengal, Sri Lanka, Kashmir, Nepal, Tibet, Mecca). Left out: the Puratan Janamsakhi's five journeys and the modern four, the last journey in 1523 to 1524, and Rizvi's station by station itinerary (a second telling of the same route; joining the two would merge sequences).
- **Death:** "He died on 22 September 1539 at Kartarpur ...; accounts of when he died differ." Year: basis (a) with (b) (the site, Rizvi, Dhillon). The day is Dhillon's own on the agreed year; no source gives another day. Place: the travelogue's "a small village called Kartarpur also in present day Pakistan". Left out: Bhandari's reign of Salim Shah Afghan and his age "between seventy and eighty".
- **Successor:** Lehna, Guru Angad, from Bhandari, with the travelogue's "not his son but his most loyal student"; both describe the one appointment and agree. Sri Chand not recognised as Guru (Boivin) and the Udasi sect believed founded by him (Khalid), compatible.
- **The Sacha Sauda:** the telling with the age of eighteen, the twenty rupees and Bhai Mardana, framed "It is told". Basis (b) by fullness and its own legend frame. Left out: the bare outline in which the tale begins with goods to be sold in Talwandi and ends in the forest and in Talwandi, so that two tellings are not joined.
- **Who led the jatha of 1921:** Bhai Lachhman Singh Dharowali, basis (a). The second account also has Lachman Singh at the head; its co leader, Bhai Daleep Singh, is kept only as a victim (the brick kiln). No "accounts differ" clause (see Points for V1).
- **How Lachhman Singh died:** tied to a tree and burned, "it is recounted", basis (a). "Hung upside down" dropped as a minor variant of the same death.
- **The toll:** "Scores of Sikhs were killed; accounts of the toll differ, but tradition speaks of at least eighty six martyrs." Basis (a). Left out: "some estimates run considerably higher", "about a hundred", and the gathering of "about two hundred" (left out so that the reader does not set one count against another).
- **Who the mahants were:** "*mahants* ... of the Udasi order, ... though accounts of who the *mahants* were differ". Basis (a). Left out: "Hindu priests (mahants)", and the background sentence on sectarian divisions and the British takeover, which carries that identification.
- **The mahant's guards:** "armed retainers waiting, though accounts of who they were differ". Basis (a). Left out: "Muslim Pathans from the tribal areas" and local Hindu supporters.
- **How many gurdwaras the town has:** "several gurdwaras, most of them associated with incidents from his life", in the words of *A White Trail* itself. Left out: its seven and *Walking with Nanak*'s six.
- **Size of the Sikh community:** "Within about ten years their numbers multiplied, to about two hundred households". Basis (b): *A White Trail* (thirty to forty houses ten years earlier, a sevenfold increase, about two hundred houses) and *Walking with Nanak*'s "about two hundred-odd households". Left out: Kartar's fifty against two hundred to two hundred and fifty.
- **When the Gurpurab falls and how many come:** the full moon of Kartik, basis (a). The official yearly estimate of ten to fifteen thousand, the media's three thousand in 2011 and the 2011 dates (8 to 10 November) are figures for different things and are all kept. Left out: "celebrated during the last days of November".
- **Care of the shrine after Partition:** the site's "passed into the care of the state" and today's administration through the ETPB and the PSGPC, basis (a), kept with Kartar's memory of the empty town and Giani Pratap as the first to look after the gurdwaras (custody and caretaking, not rival accounts; Giani Pratap is a named keeper, rule 7). The 1985 arrangement (no PSGPC yet; the SGPC through the Indian High Commission) kept in its own year in The Setting. See Points for V1.
- **Nadeem's earlier visit:** "returning after seven years" kept; his own "But I came a few years ago" given as "he had come before" (minor).
- **Who may enter:** not a single fact told two ways but three dated events (Lohri 2011, non Sikh visitors let in on Kartar's word; the Gurpurab 2011, Nadeem turned away) and the general statement that many Muslims devoted to Nanak visit his shrine; each kept in its place.
- **The Kaaba legend:** the Kaaba turning towards his feet. Basis (b): the travelogue's legend and Rizvi's "the legend that the Ka'ba or the mihrab of the principal mosque, moved in the direction of the Guru's feet". Left out: Dhillon's telling, in which Nanak's feet were to be turned towards "a place where God does not reside" and the *qazi* conceded, a second telling of the same episode.
- **The aarti:** one translation kept ("In the salver of the firmament ..."); the other ("The sky the salver ...") left out as a second rendering of the same lines.
- **The festival banner:** *A White Trail*'s banner of 2011 kept; *Walking with Nanak*'s banner of all ten Gurus at the yearly celebration left out as a second description.
- **Nanak and Baba Farid's line:** the visit to Pak Pattan, where he met Baba Farid's spiritual descendants and collected the saint's poetry, kept. Rizvi's visit to Shaikh Ibrahim at Ajodhan left out, so that what may be the same visit is not told twice and the two places are not identified.
- **Spellings:** Janam Asthan, Rai Bhoi di Talwandi and Lachhman Singh (the site's); Janamasthan only inside the plaque's words; Tambu Sahib for Tamboo Sahib; Naulakha Hazari for Naulakh Hazari; Sulakhini; Rai Bhoe (Dhillon's) for the father of Rai Bular; Gujranwala for Gujranawala; Khattri throughout (Rizvi's spelling) for Bhandari's Khatri. All minor.
