## Consensus choices

- **Where it stands:** "in the Naulakha Bazaar quarter of Lahore, outside Delhi Gate" (Overview), and "The gurdwara lies outside Delhi Gate, in the area known as Naulakha Bazaar" (The Gurdwara). The site's account places it in the Naulakha Bazaar quarter "of the walled city", but every other source the team version has is against that: Qaiser is emphatic that "this is not the walled city" but outside Delhi Gate, and Chishti places his Shahid Ganj outside Delhi Gate. So basis (a) yields and basis (b) applies. All three agree on Naulakha Bazaar, where a visitor finds it, so the walled city is dropped silently as a minor variant, with no "accounts differ" clause.
- **The name:** Gurdwara Shaheed Ganj Singh Singhnian, basis (a), with the team version's glosses of *Shahid Ganj* and *Singhnian*. "Also known simply as Gurdwara Shaheed Ganj" is Khalid's form, a shorter name rather than a rival one. Chishti's Shahid Ganj is used for the nineteenth century *samadh*, the quarter and the place where the scripture was read. The bibliography's "Shahid Ganj Singhnian" is not mentioned in the prose.
- **How the gurdwara began:** "Accounts of how the gurdwara began differ. In its own tradition, a gurdwara was raised here after the consolidation of Sikh power in the later eighteenth century, to enshrine the memory of the martyrs." Basis (a). Chishti's large Sikh *samadh*, raised where Sikhs had been put to death and named Shahid Ganj, agrees with that and is kept as the state of the site in the nineteenth century, not as a rival founding. Qaiser's small mosque, into which the Sikhs placed the *Guru Granth Sahib* under Ranjit Singh, is not set beside the founding as a variant. It is told in The Dispute, in his voice ("in the telling of Iqbal Qaiser"), as the ground of the quarrel, because the court cases cannot be understood without it and the contested history is to be stated as far as the team version states it. See judgement call 5.
- **Who ordered the killings:** Muin-ul-Mulk, known to the Sikhs as Mir Mannu. All the legible accounts agree: the site's account, Qaiser, Kanhaiya Lal's Mir Muin al-Mulk, and Chishti for the general order and the tomb. Chishti's "Mir Masud" in the *samadh* passage rests on a reading the team version marks uncertain (rule 5), so no real disagreement remains. The site's spellings are used throughout; quotations that would bring in Moin-ul-Mulk, Meer Manu or Mir Muin al-Mulk are not quoted.
- **What was done there:** the site's account, basis (a). The men were put to death; the women and children were imprisoned, set to grind grain, starved and brutalised; mothers were made to watch the killing of their children. Qaiser's telling is kept in his own voice as one man's testimony, not as a rival version: men, women and children slaughtered here and thrown into a well on the governor's orders, a room built to hold prisoners before their execution, and the skeletons he identified during the rebuilding. His "alike" is not carried, since it is the only word that sets his telling against the site's, and both accounts have women and children killed here (the team Overview: "tortured and killed here"). His "Thousands" is covered by "Traditional accounts speak of enormous numbers of dead". See judgement call 6.
- **When:** under Mir Mannu, governor from 1748, after the struggles of the 1740s, in the mid eighteenth century; basis (a). Khaled Ahmad's 'Shahidganj in 1746' is left out, because the passage does not say which Shahid Ganj is meant or what happened there. Chishti's placing of Mir Mannu's rule in the reign of Muhammad Shah is dropped silently as minor: it names a reign, not a rival date for the persecution.
- **The building today:** the site's "less an architectural showpiece than a site of remembrance" (as "It is austere, less an architectural showpiece than a site of remembrance") and Qaiser's account (the old building razed under British rule, the present gurdwara completed in 2004) are both kept. They do not conflict: a building completed in 2004 can be austere.
- **Dates in the courts and the rebuilding:** the team version's "Around 1957-58" and "In about 1994-95" are written "around 1957 or 1958" and "In about 1994 or 1995", so that no hyphen of our own stands between the figures. 2004 and "the late 1980s" as given.
- **Time anchors:** Chishti (1867, "the date unconfirmed") gives "In the nineteenth century" only: for the *samadh*, for the two mosques, for the scripture read and the bounty, and for Mir Mannu's tomb in the quarter. Lahori (1875, firm) gives "In the 1870s" for the other Shahid Ganj. The dates 1748, 1751 and 1758 and the court and building dates are dates of events the team version states, not dates of a description.
- **Identifications in the Setting:** Kanhaiya Lal's Mir Muin al-Mulk is written Mir Mannu (the team version's own disagreement section treats them as one man), and his Ahmad Shah Durrani is written "Ahmad Shah" after Ahmad Shah Abdali has been named, so that one governor and one Afghan run through the section. The team version uses both Abdali and Durrani without stating in so many words that they are one man. See judgement call 11.
