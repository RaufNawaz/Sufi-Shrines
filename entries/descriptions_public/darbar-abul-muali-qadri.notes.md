## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **His name:** Hazrat Syed Muhammad Khair ul Deen, *kunyat* Abul Muali, title Asadullah; one clause "Accounts of the form of his name differ". Basis (a), the survey. Left out: Alam Faqri's Shah Khairuddin Muhammad, Lahori's heading (Khair al-Din inside the name), Lahori's index (Khair al-Din as his father), Sharib's al-Maali as the name with Asad al-Din and Shah Khair al-Din as *laqabs*.
- **Birth:** 10 Zil Hajj 960 AH at Kirman, framed "Tradition holds", with one clause "accounts of the year and place of his birth differ". Basis (a) with (b) for the year (the survey and Lahori 960; Chishti's 960 is uncertain and not counted). The day is common ground (every source that gives a day gives 10 Dhu al-Hijja). Left out: Kanhaiya Lal's 992 (uncertain digits), Sharib's 910 or 920 and his Sher Garh birthplace.
- **Order:** Qadiri, stated plainly (he spread the Qadiri *silsila*; "a saint of the Qadiri order"). Basis (b): the row's name, Lahori, Chishti, Kanhaiya Lal and Lahori's editor; no source names another order. Left out: Sharib's Uwaysi tie to Jilani and the survey's answer by descent.
- **Family's coming and father's home:** the father migrated from Iran and settled at Depalpur, Okara. Basis (a). Left out: Sharib's migration of 796 AH (Faiz Allah, Mubarak Kirmani, Uch, the place near Multan, Singhra, Sher Garh). The grandfather, Sayyid Fath Allah Kirmani, kept from Lahori's pedigree, which is given whole (father and grandfather), not extended by any other source's links.
- **Training:** studied with his uncle until the age of thirty, with one clause "accounts of the length of his training differ". Basis (a). Left out: Lahori's thirty two years and Sharib's "stay with me always". Master, khalifa, khirqa of khilafat and son-in-law are common ground (survey, Lahori, the editor, Chishti, Sharib) and stated plainly.
- **Coming to Lahore:** 980 AH, at his uncle's command, from Depalpur; "Accounts of when he came to Lahore differ; the year the shrine keeps is 980 AH". Basis (a). Left out: Sharib's Delhi in 980, Sher Garh, and settling after his pir's death in 1110 (uncertain); Lahori's "deputed after khilafat" is compatible and its substance (the khirqa of khilafat) is kept in the training paragraph, with no sequence built between the two.
- **What he built on the road:** wells, mosques and *madrasas*, basis (a). Left out: Lahori's well, small garden and tank at every halt (a word uncertain) and the name Abdul Muali on them.
- **Number of followers:** "Accounts of the number of his followers differ; devotees relate that millions pledged allegiance to him". Basis (a), framed as devotees' telling. Left out: Lahori's thousands, Kanhaiya Lal's lakhs (uncertain).
- **Death:** 16 Rabi ul Awal 1024 AH, stated plainly, basis (a) and (b) (survey, Lahori, Sharib). Kanhaiya Lal's 1025 is uncertain in both places and Chishti's year is illegible, so no real rival year remains; Kanhaiya Lal's 21st is a day's difference (minor) and dropped. Age sixty five framed "it is reported" (the other ages are uncertain or illegible).
- **Reign at death:** Shah Jahan, "Accounts differ over the reign in which he died; the shrine places his death in the reign of Shah Jahan". Basis (a) with (b) (survey and Kanhaiya Lal). Left out: Chishti's Jahangir, eleven (uncertain) years after Akbar.
- **Pen name:** Gharibi (Lahori's editor). Treated as minor: Chishti's Gharib is the same name without the ending; Sharib's Gharbi, Maali and Masle are uncertain readings. Dropped silently.
- **Who built the tomb:** "Accounts of who built the tomb differ, but most hold that the saint laid its foundation in his lifetime and that it was not yet complete when he died." Basis (b): Kanhaiya Lal and Chishti both (the team version: "Both historians agree"), against the survey's "he had it constructed and repaired himself", so basis (a) gives way (every other source is against it). Then Kanhaiya Lal's own completion by his son Muhammad Baqir, in its own sentence (his account whole), and Chishti's rebuilding under the Sikhs by the people of the *sajjada*, in its own sentence (a separate, uncontested event). Left out: Chishti's "dome raised after his death" as a separate step, and the survey's "constructed and repaired himself".
- **Who lies beside him:** both kept as separate facts, not as rivals: the survey's eight sons buried beside him and Haider Ali Shah buried in the shrine; Kanhaiya Lal's four named graves on the platform (the saint, his eldest son Shah Muhammad Baqir, Shah Muhammad Raza, Haji Muhammad Fazl). Chishti's four agree. See Points for V1.
- **The ʿurs:** 15, 16 and 17 Rabi ul Awal, basis (a). Kanhaiya Lal's ʿurs is given as the 1880s practice (first night *chiraghan* and courtesans dancing, second day *qawwali*), not as a rival date; his "the crowd stays two days" is left out, so no length is set beside the three days (Mian Mir precedent for Kanhaiya Lal's fairs).
- **Where the tomb stands:** outside Mochi Gate, basis (b) (Kanhaiya Lal and Chishti), with the survey's "near Mochi Darwaza" kept where the survey tells of the seminary; Shah Abul Muali Street, Gowalmandi, given as the street's name (Lahori's editor); Tila Shahid Ganj kept only as the place of the graveyard, where Chishti gives it. Left out: Kanhaiya Lal's "north-west corner" (of what is unclear).
- **The majzub legend:** the survey's telling, basis (a). Left out silently (minor, a legend's setting): Sharib's sarai at Delhi, the journey on to Sher Garh, and the shaikh's Persian greeting "Maali, come forward".
- **The rosary and the white turban:** the survey's Dara Shikoh, basis (a), with one clause covering both: "Tellings of the next two stories differ over who received the saint's gifts, but the shrine tells both of Dara Shikoh." Left out: Sharib's rosary given to departing visitors with the *salawat* one hundred times, Sharib's Mulla Nemat Ullah, and Alam Faqri's companion of Dara Shikoh. Dara Shikoh is given no title (the survey styles him emperor; the team version flags the styling).
- **Dreams of Jilani:** the survey's "that very night"; Lahori's "on the very day of his bai'at" is the same tradition and is not set beside it.

## Points for V1

1. **Length:** 0.41 (17,734 against 43,344), inside L1. The causes are above. Please sweep for any important, certain fact I have missed.
2. **Eid al-Adha:** "born on 10 Zil Hajj, the day of Eid al-Adha" joins the survey's date to the name of the day. 10 Dhu al-Hijja is Eid al-Adha by definition, and Chishti and Kanhaiya Lal both say the birth fell on the day of Eid (their uncertain words are the weekday and the year, not the Eid). I treat it as a gloss. Fallback: delete ", the day of Eid al-Adha".
3. **The builder of the tomb:** "most hold" rests on Kanhaiya Lal and Chishti against the survey, so basis (a) gives way. Muhammad Baqir's completion is Kanhaiya Lal's own account, told whole in its own sentence; the Sikh rebuilding is Chishti's, a separate later event in its own sentence. Is this a merged sequence? Fallback: drop "Under Sikh rule it was rebuilt by the people of the *sajjada*..." or drop the Muhammad Baqir sentence.
4. **Eight sons and four graves:** the survey's eight sons "buried beside him" and Kanhaiya Lal's four graves on the platform are kept as separate facts (the sons may lie in the precinct; the platform holds four). The team version files them together under "Who lies beside him". Fallback: "He is reported to have had eight sons, and one of them is said to have been martyred..." (drop "who are buried beside him").
5. **The Arabic inscription:** Chishti's qualifier is scoped to "the last word"; I kept only "the friends of God do not die" and dropped "but pass from one place to another". Similarly the door on the north is not given (only the south door, which Kanhaiya Lal also gives). Fallback: delete the inscription clause.
6. **Scoped qualifiers:** I kept the clean names in sentences whose qualifiers are scoped to one word (Muhammad Sufi, Muhammad Shah, Shah Hasan, Hazrat Hasan Shah without Burhanpur, Sayyid Imam Shah, Sayyid Mehr Shah Sahib and Sayyid Alam Shah; the lattice without its maker; the artillery general without his name; the grandsons' graves without the number; the stones needing repair without the eight doors), following the Fariduddin and Garh Maharaja rulings that a scoped qualifier attaches to its word.
7. **Street name in the present tense:** "The street on which it stands bears the saint's name, Shah Abul Muali Street, in Gowalmandi" comes from Lahori's undated editor ("present location"). A street name is not time bound, so I used the present. Fallback: "Its address is given as ..." is source talk, so the alternative is to delete the sentence.
8. **The 1880s ʿurs:** told as that decade's practice (first night, second day), with the "two days" left out, so it does not stand as a rival length beside today's three days. Fallback: delete "and on the second day there was *qawwali*, to which the *fuqara*, the dervishes, listened" and keep the rest.
9. **Kanhaiya Lal's and Chishti's descriptions of the fabric** are joined in one paragraph (octagonal building and grand dome from one; the dome's colour, the south door's verse and the door from the other), each with its own anchor. They describe different parts, not rival accounts.
10. **Characterisations:** "He is remembered as the learned man of his time, a *faqir* ... given to *zuhd* ... and to *riyazat*" (Kanhaiya Lal's *alim* and *zuhd*/*riyazat*) and "He is counted, too, a man of *kashf* ... *fana fi al-shaikh*" (Sharib) are framed as remembrance, as in the Mian Mir row; "the learned man of his time" is not a scholar's verdict on a disputed point. Fallback: drop the Kanhaiya Lal sentence.
11. **"the shrine keeps" / "the shrine places" / "the shrine tells":** the survey is the shrine's own account (basis a), as "the shrine" is used in the Mian Mir row.
12. **The other Abul Mualis:** one sentence keeps them apart (a Shah Abul Muali of the Chishti order and one of a Gilani line). Abu al-Maali Iraqi in the promised audience is kept as a separate man, as the team version keeps him.
13. **"Gharibi":** Chishti's "Gharib" treated as a minor variant of the same pen name (basis: minor). If V1 holds it a real disagreement, the fix is "wrote under the *takhallus*, or pen name, Gharibi, though accounts of its form differ".
