## Consensus choices

- **The saint, his dates, his orders and the shrine:** the team version records no disagreement about any of them. The dates 1865 and 1928, his birth into a devout family, his training, the two lineages he united, Abul Faiz Qalandar Ali Suhrawardi as one who "is remembered as having received knowledge from him", his death and burial at Sharaqpur, and the shrine's complex, successors, five daily prayers, *naat*, *qawwali*, free kitchen and *ʿurs* all come from the site's own account, which the team version states in its own voice. The public English gives them the same way and keeps the team version's "is remembered" where it uses it. Basis (a), the shrine. **For Rauf:** this whole order frame (Qadiri and Chishti, the two lineages united, the Abul Faiz link) rests on the site's own account, which the team notes (decision 1) flag as unsourced compendium material awaiting a Sharaqpur source, against a sheet `silsila` of "Qadiri" only. The public English states nothing beyond the team version, but it inherits that open question. A verifier of the earlier, lost draft (its note 10, as the orphaned notes record it; that report is not among the V1 reports now on file) added, from general reference knowledge and not from any archive source, that Mian Sher Muhammad Sharaqpuri is often described as a Naqshbandi Mujaddidi master. The current English makes the same Qadiri and Chishti attribution, so the point applies to it. Nothing was changed on that ground (RULE 2), but the attribution is worth settling against a Sharaqpur source before import.

The public English gives "the Qadiri and Chishti traditions" in the Overview, "He united the lineages of two Sufi orders, the Qadiri ... and the Chishti" in The Saint, and the team version's own "joined the Qadiri and Chishti ways" in Legacy, so no hyphenated compound stands in the prose.

This is the team version's own statement, not a disagreement; the team notes record the same identification question as Doubt I9 ("Do not map"). His name is the form of his notice, Shaikh Muhammad Saeed Chishti Sabri Sharaqpuri; the index's shorter "Muhammad Saeed Sabri Sharaqpuri" is a minor variant, dropped silently. "Sabri" in the name and "a Chishti Sabiri" in the description are both the team version's.
