## Consensus choices

- **When Saidpur was sacked:** "traditionally dated to around 1520 or 1521" (the site's 1520-21, written without the hyphenated range). Basis (a); it holds Rizvi's 926/1520 for the third invasion and the *Babar-vani* line's gloss of 1521, so no clause is needed and no figure is built (the Rori Sahib ruling). Rizvi's year is not attached to the third invasion, so that no second dating stands beside the range. Dhillon's undated "third incursion" is kept only as the time of the janamsakhi return through Saidpur.
- **What the site was:** the site's "marks the place where a great millstone associated with the Guru was kept" and "commemorates ... his imprisonment" lead (basis (a)). The historian's words at the house ("This place was used as a prison at the time of Babur. Guru Nanak was imprisoned here and was forced to work on a stone grinder.") are kept as his words, as the only link between the house described and the story, which is how the team version holds it. I read the two as compatible (a millstone kept at the place of the imprisonment, the Guru made to work it) rather than as rival identifications, so no "accounts differ" clause is written. See Judgement calls 2 and 3.
- **How the millstone turned:** the site's self turning millstone, framed ("It is remembered", "The marvel is said"), basis (a), with which the janamsakhis agree. One clause for the variant: "Not every telling of the captivity includes the miracle." Left out: the historian's telling without a miracle as a separate account, and Bhandari's "signs of unveiling and wonders" in the Guru's early years.
- **Whether the Guru met Babur, and what passed between them:** the site's telling (Babur came to the Guru, sought his blessing and at his urging freed many prisoners), framed, basis (a), with "Accounts of their meeting differ, and it has been doubted that the two ever met." Left out: the janamsakhi telling as the team version summarises it (Babur sent for the Guru, asked a blessing to conquer India and was rebuked) and the historian's "later constructions by his devotees". Kept as reception, not as a rival telling: "The encounter has also been taken to mark the beginning of the antagonism between the Mughals and the Sikhs" and Babur, great grandfather of Jahangir, "remembered as having imprisoned Guru Nanak" (the Bhai Lalo wording V1 accepted).
- **The names of the town:** Eminabad, formerly Saidpur (the site and Khalid), basis (a) and (b). The plaque's "Eimanabad" is kept inside the plaque's words only. The renaming: "When Babur took over the city of Saidpur, he renamed it Eminabad, though accounts of how the town came by its name differ" (the Rori Sahib and Bhai Lalo wording). Left out: the legend of the woman who fed Babur roasted grain (her name rests on a reading the team version marks uncertain, and it is the rival telling); Rizvi's "Saiyidpur (Amanabad)" and index "Amnabad" (the team version says the book does not identify either with Eminabad); Bhandari's Amnabad (identified with Eminabad by spelling alone).
- **Who created the *pargana* of Amanabad:** left out with the whole Bhandari Amnabad passage (identification by spelling alone; see Left out).
- **The Guru's dates:** "most often given as 1469 to 1539", basis (b) (Khalid and Dhillon against Bhandari; Abbas's digits are lost). Left out: Dhillon's 14 April 1469 and 22 September 1539 (minor), Bhandari's Samvat 1526, 880 Hijri, Bahlul Lodi, Salim Shah Afghan and the age of seventy to eighty.
- **How many journeys:** "Accounts of how many journeys he made differ, but his *udasis* ... are most often counted as four, and they lasted more than twenty years", basis (b) (the modern accounts and Dhillon's own view against the Puratan Janamsakhi's five). "More than twenty years" holds both twenty three and about twenty four and builds no figure (the Rori Sahib and Bhai Lalo wording).
- **Whether the Guru worked wonders:** the millstone is told as tradition; the Guru's own view is given as "Nanak himself is held to have abhorred the association of miracles with religious personalities, yet the stories of his life are full of them", with the janamsakhi line "there is no such thing as a miracle" (both kept on Rori Sahib and Bhai Lalo). Left out: Bhandari's wonders in the Guru's early years (the variant), Dhillon's "clear and emphatic disavowal of the miraculous" and the other authors' verdicts (see Left out).
- **Not filed as a disagreement, kept together:** the site's "simple flat-roofed building within a brick-paved compound" with the Nishan Sahib in the courtyard, and the visitor's small house with a Khalsa flag on a pole behind its walls (the team notes judge that they differ in wording but do not contradict).
- **Minor, dropped silently:** *Babur Bani* (Khalid) for the site's *Babar-vani*, which is kept throughout (see Judgement call 6); Baisakhi in the *A White Trail* sentence written Visakhi, the site's spelling, so that a reader does not take them for two festivals (Judgement call 7); the second *aarti* rendering is kept, since both are the Guru's verse.

## Left out

- **Source apparatus:** every citation, page and folio; the Overview's second and third paragraphs on which book describes the site and which do not; "The books consulted record no builder"; "Neither passage names Chakki Sahib"; "What follows is about the town"; "What the books say of Guru Nanak himself is said of the Guru"; Khalid's book not printing the gurdwara's name (the public text keeps the link at the historian's words instead); Dhillon's retelling telling no millstone story; the `## Where the Sources Disagree` section, resolved above. The historian is not named (Iqbal Qaiser is a cited author's informant and the author of a bibliography item); the visitor of *Walking with Nanak* (visit undated) is "one visitor", the Bhai Lalo precedent; the journalist of 2017 is not named.
- **Readings the team version marks uncertain (rule 5):** the *Babar-vani* line's second half (departure "in Ninety", gloss read as 1540); the name of the woman in the renaming legend; Eminabad among the towns whose Sikh communities "Only recently" could exert themselves, go to university and own cars (the town's name misprinted there); Bhandari's emperor hearing the Guru's Hindi verses from Guru Arjan (Arjan's name uncertain; without it the line's point does not stand); Abbas's lost birth year.
- **Uncertain identification:** Bhandari's Amnabad (the two sentences on the creation of the *pargana*, Muhammad Amin, the impostor posing as Umar Shaikh Mirza punished at the halt, Shah Daula's bridge over the Deg five *kos* away); Rizvi's Saiyidpur (Amanabad) and index Amnabad.
- **The reconstruction (Khalid's own, which the team version marks as such):** Saidpur in Nanak's day (the first goal of his travels, its havelis, temples with gold tridents, idols of Laxmi and Ganesh, pilgrims' rooms and stables, Brahmin pandits, beggars, trade and courtesans); Malik Bhago's four storey haveli, the Shahi Qilla, and his office under the Rai of Saidpur; Mardana playing the *rubab* every night at Sultanpur; the boy Nanak refusing the *janeu*; the innkeepers of the herons song. Precedent: Rori Sahib, Bhai Lalo, Kartarpur, Nankana Sahib. Malik Bhago is kept only in Khalid's own voice (the court where the teaching was questioned).
- **Rival accounts dropped under rule 4:** see Consensus choices (the janamsakhi meeting and rebuke, the historian's doubts as an account, Bhandari's wonders, the Eni legend, the Guru's other dates, the five journeys).
- **Scholars' and authors' verdicts and reflections:** Khalid's "marauder"; "challenges the divinity of the Divine"; the first poem of Nanak he read and his discovery of the Guru through the historian's recitation (also in the team Legacy: the Guru "my own, being a Punjabi" and "an alien"); Nanak "repulsed by the concept of making a larger-than-life figure", "a rationalist", "His genius lies in the poetic use of words"; the historian's "That is to show that our saint is more powerful than yours", his reading of the *aarti* ("in nature", "a naturalist") and his advice on method; the paragraph on method ("The only evidence we have ..."); Dhillon's "purportedly drawn" and "clear and emphatic disavowal"; Bhandari's "clear words and plain metaphors"; McLeod's view of Punjabi Sufism (only the counter argument is kept, framed; see Judgement call 12).
- **Minor:** the journey on to Sialkot; Dhillon's day dates; "in published English translations" (kept as "in English translation"); Abbas's "an outcome of these movements" ("these movements" is not explained in the team version; kept as "He is counted among the poets of devotion", the team version's own framing); the informant's name Nadeem (kept as "one Muslim painter", the Rori Sahib wording); Rozehnal's "in certain lectures" and "religious leaders"; Kasmani's trope of the flower in milk (not explained in the team version, tied to no place; Rori Sahib and Bhai Lalo precedent); Schimmel's clause "when its literary heritage was rarely continued by Muslims"; "in the editor's variant reading, at the age of ten" (with Bhandari's wonders).

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: *langar* (the free communal kitchen). The team version's own: *chakki* (a great millstone), *Babar-vani* (the "utterances concerning Babur"), *janamsakhis* (Dhillon's "cycles of tales", with Khalid's "written by his devotees after his death"), *bani* (Abbas's glossary: devotional verses of supplication, entreaty, humility and reverence), *doha* (Abbas: an old popular Prakrit and Hindi metre in which the two lines of a couplet rhyme), *nisbat* ("Divine connection"), *panth* (sect), *kalams* (the poems). Plain dictionary senses: *gurdwara* (a Sikh house of worship), *ghumaon* (a Punjabi measure of land), Visakhi (the spring festival), *Nishan Sahib* (the Sikh flag), Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee (the committee for the management of Sikh gurdwaras), *rubab* (a stringed instrument), *kos* (an old measure of distance), *udasis* (the long journeys of his travelling years, the Rori Sahib gloss), Guru Granth Sahib (the Sikh scripture), Adi Granth (the first compilation of the Sikh scripture), *aarti* (the hymn sung as lamps are waved in worship), *janeu* (the sacred thread, the team version's own), *mahants* (the heads of religious establishments), Jhulelal (revered by the Hindus of Sindh), *Bhakti* (the path of loving devotion). The Udasis are glossed by the team version's own "counted among the devotees of Baba Nanak"; Baba Nanak "as the Guru was also called".

5. **"Not every telling of the captivity includes the miracle."** The one clause for "How the millstone turned"; it rests on the historian's telling at the house (prison and forced labour, no miracle), which the team version files against the site.

Rori Sahib and Bhai Lalo use *Babur Bani* because their team versions do in their own voice; if the coordinator prefers one name across the three Eminabad rows, *Babur Bani* can replace *Babar-vani* in four places with no other change.
7. **Visakhi for Baisakhi** in "Sikhs living abroad came for festivals such as Visakhi and the birthday of Guru Nanak": a spelling variant harmonised with the site's Visakhi earlier on the page. Fallback: restore "Baisakhi".
