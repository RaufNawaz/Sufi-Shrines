## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **The Guru's dates:** born 1563, Guru from 1581, died 1606, with "accounts of the year are not unanimous, but 1606 is the year generally given". Basis (a) with (b) (the site, both Khalid books, Dhillon, Rizvi's index). Left out: Alam's "(1563–1605)", Rizvi's damaged date string, Chishti's partly illegible year, Bhandari's twenty five years.
- **The Lahore official:** Chandu Shah, "the Lahore financier", throughout, with "Accounts differ ... over Chandu Shah's name and office". Basis (a) (the site, with one passage of *Walking with Nanak*). Left out: Chandu Lal, the Hindu Khatri, Diwan of Lahore appointed by the Mughal authorities. Chishti's episodes told of Chandu Lal (the petition, the rebuke, the refused match, the *mala*, his death) are told of the same man, as the team version treats him as one man.
- **Who was to blame:** the site's account (Jahangir's displeasure at the Guru's influence and his blessing of Khusrau; the summons; the fine; torture at Chandu Shah's instigation), with "Accounts differ over who bore the chief responsibility for his death". Basis (a). The refused match is kept, framed "Sikh tradition traces Chandu's enmity", because it explains the site's own Chandu Shah and two accounts agree on it. Left out: the Mughal sources' silence on Chandu; Jahangir's "this insignificant fellow ... commanded that he should be put to death"; Chishti's summons on Chandu Lal's report of "Arjan, the ruler of Amritsar".
- **Where and how Khusrau met the Guru:** the site's blessing, with "Accounts of where the prince and the Guru met differ"; "The emperor counted the blessing as treason" (*A White Trail*, not disputed). Basis (a). Left out: Goindwal on the Beas and the saffron mark called Qashqa; the *langar* at Amritsar and the *tilak*; Tarn Taran, three days and five hundred rupees.
- **The penalty:** the site's heavy fine, with punishment if he failed to pay, and "accounts of the penalty differ". Basis (a). Left out: the property handed to Murtaza Khan and the penal laws of Yasa.
- **Where he was tortured and for how long:** no place given, "Accounts ... of where he was held and for how long, differ; the torture is most often said to have lasted five days". Place: basis (d). Length: basis (b) (*A White Trail*'s fifth day and *Walking with Nanak*'s five days, against Chishti's month; the site's "days" agrees). Left out: outside the Alamgiri Gate; the Diwan's *haveli* by the Red Well near the Rang Mahal; Chandu Lal's *haveli* in Hira Mandi; a month.
- **The torments:** the site's heated iron plate, burning sand and scalding water, framed "Sikh tradition holds" and "it is recounted", with "Accounts of the torments ... differ". Basis (a). Left out: the cauldron of boiling water; immersion in a cauldron with boiling sand on his head.
- **His end and his body:** "Tradition holds that the Guru's end came in the River Ravi, which then flowed beside the fort, and that his body was never recovered; accounts of the manner of his death differ." Basis (a) (the site's body given to the Ravi and never recovered), worded at the common ground of the site, *A White Trail*, *Walking with Nanak* and Kanhaiya Lal (all end in the Ravi, no body). Left out: the body thrown into the river; the bath on the fifth day as a narrative step; his remains interred in Lahore; cremation by Sikh *khadims* at the spot and the marker raised there. The Sikh belief that he went into the water and passed on through a miracle stays in Legends as a belief.
- **The first memorial:** Guru Hargobind, "accounts of the first memorial differ". Basis (a) with (b) (the site and *A White Trail*). Left out: the *khadims*' marker; Kanhaiya Lal's "a small building at first" is kept only inside his own description, see Points for V1.
- **The present building:** first erected under Ranjit Singh, extended by the Sikh community in the early twentieth century. Not a conflict: one source (*A White Trail*) gives both steps in one sentence, and the site and Kanhaiya Lal agree on Ranjit Singh.
- **Its colour:** a small yellow building with a golden dome, in 2011. Basis (b): the team version shows every source that gives a colour other than the site against the site's white and gold (both Khalid books say yellow), so (a) fails. Left out: "white and gold". The site's gilded dome agrees with the golden dome and is kept.

Gurdwara Baoli Sahib, and whether it was this shrine has not been settled." The description that follows is told of "that *samadh*", in the past, under "In the 1880s".
