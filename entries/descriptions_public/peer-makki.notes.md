## Consensus choices

- **Death year:** 612 AH, 1215 CE, with one clause: "Accounts of who he was and of the year of his death have not always agreed. The shrine remembers him as Hazrat Syed Azizuddin of Baghdad, and the year it keeps for his death, 612 AH or 1215 CE, is the one most accounts give." Basis (a), the site's account and the field survey, and (b), since Lahori's text and his editor (who calls it the more plausible) also give 612 AH. Variants dropped: 796 AH (a footnote quoting *Khazinat al-Asfiya*, the reading of the year uncertain, rule 5); 1048 AH (attributed by the editor to Chishti and rejected by him as baseless; not in the Tahqiqat pages read); the illegible year Chishti gives for the saint he tentatively identifies (rule 5). The editor's "1215–16" is given as the site's 1215.
- **Who he was:** Hazrat Syed Azizuddin of Baghdad, who reached Lahore from Mecca in 575 AH. Basis (a), agreeing with (b): the survey, Alam Faqri's outline and the editor's identification all agree. The clause is shared with the death year (above). Dropped: Chishti's "his circumstances are not known" (a statement of absence) and "some say he came to Lahore in the company of Mahmud of Ghazni" (a rival account); Chishti's tentative identification with Hazrat Sa'd al-Din (rule 5: the epithet, Mecca, "holy man", the day and the year are all uncertain or illegible). The name form Sayyid Shaikh Aziz al-Din Makki (Lahori) is not given: it is a variant spelling (minor), and giving it as his name in the narrator's voice would state as settled an identification the team version calls the editor's.
- **Where the tomb stands:** the Bhati Gate quarter of Lahore's Walled City, close to Data Ganj Bakhsh, basis (a), with one clause in The Shrine: "Accounts of exactly where the tomb stands have varied over time." The Committee Bagh corner is kept as the nineteenth century setting, because it is part of the description of the tomb as it then stood and does not contradict the quarter. Dropped: "west of the *takia* he has just described" (which *takia* is not said); "Ravi Road, Pir Makki Street" as an address (the team version says it is not shown to conflict or agree with the site's account; Pir Makki Street is kept in the Legacy, where the team version names it in its own voice as part of the quarter); Kanhaiya Lal's contents line placing the tomb among the Muslim sites outside the city (it stands against "Walled City"; its page number is uncertain and the entry itself was not read).
- **Peer Makki and Data Ganj Bakhsh:** the tradition told at the shrine (Hujwiri, ordered to Lahore, objects that Peer Makki is enough for it and arrives to find his funeral under way), basis (a), with one clause: "traditions of the bond between the two saints differ", and the team version's own chronological caveat (Hujwiri's death conventionally around 1072, a century before Peer Makki's arrival; devotional tradition rather than strict chronology). Dropped: the popular claim that he tutored Data Ganj Bakhsh; Chishti's "some say" that he was Data Ganj Bakhsh's disciple; Alam Faqri's detail that on reaching Lahore he went first to the shrine of Data Ganj Bakhsh; the tentatively identified saint's visit to Hujwiri's *khanqah* (also rule 5). The devotional custom of visiting Peer Makki first, honouring him as a forerunner, is practice, not a rival account, and is kept.
- **Hujwiri's death:** around 1072, basis (a); the 1071 of the Setting's source is a year's difference, minor, dropped silently. The Setting says only that he died in Lahore.
- **The date of the *ʿurs*:** the 9th to the 11th of Rabi-ul-Awal, basis (a). Chishti's twelfth (in a printed text that garbles the month's name) is dropped, rule 5 and minor. The nineteenth century *ʿurs* is kept without month or day.
- **Ghori and Lahore:** the prophecy story is told once, as the shrine and the survey tell it (the siege of 1178, the return exactly six years later, the final conquest, Khusrau Malik taken prisoner and later killed), framed as a story ("A well loved story tells", "it is said"). The Setting gives the history without Bhandari's years (575, 580 and 582, all uncertain in print, rule 5), with one clause: "Accounts of the years of these campaigns differ", which also carries the editor's view that Lahore's historians gave even these years wrongly. Dhillon's statement that Ghori "was not able to conquer Lahore" when he took the Punjab in 1192 is dropped, basis (b): the survey, Bhandari, Kanhaiya Lal and Chishti all have Lahore pass to the Ghurids. Bhandari's "and died there" (at Ghazni) is left out so that the history does not set a second account of Khusrau Malik's death beside the story's "later killed"; "submitted and was taken to Ghazni" is kept.
- **The Auqaf's charge:** 1958, from the survey; the team version gives no rival year, so no clause. (The 1958/1960 tension in the team notes is with the Shah Jamal row, another shrine.)
- **Not listed in the team version but resolved here:** the number of the city's gates. "One of the twelve gates", basis (b) (Chishti's list and Khalid); Kanhaiya Lal's heading "the thirteenth gate" for the Zaki Gate is dropped so the two counts do not stand side by side, and his "seventh gate" for Bhati (an ordering) is dropped as minor.
- **Spellings (minor):** Khusrau Malik and Khusrau Shah (the survey's, Chishti's and Kanhaiya Lal's form) for Khusraw and Khusro; Muhammad Shahab-ud-Din Ghori (the survey's form) for the site's Sultan Muhammad Ghori and Bhandari's Shihab al-Din; Peer Makki (the site's) throughout, Pir Makki only in the names of the mosque, the street and the encyclopaedia heading; Ghaznavids for Ghaznawids.
- **Time anchors:** Chishti's material is anchored only to "the nineteenth century" (his bibliography date is unconfirmed). No material from Lahori 1875 or Kanhaiya Lal 1884 reaches the public text with a date.

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: Syed (a descendant of the Prophet Muhammad); *ʿurs* (the commemoration of the saint's death); *dhikr* (the remembrance of God); *qawwali* (the devotional music of the Sufis); *khanqah* (the Sufi lodge); *faqir* (one who has taken the Sufi path of poverty, from the glossary's "mystic/poor one" and *faqr*, spiritual poverty).

From the pilots: *dhamal* (ecstatic devotional dancing to the beat of the drum); Ahl-e-Sunnat (the Sunni way; the pilot's "the Sunni tradition", reworded to avoid "tradition" twice in one clause); Auqaf (the Punjab government's department of religious endowments, the Shah Kamal pilot's wording).

Plain dictionary senses: Makki ("of Mecca"; the team version says the title came from his years in Mecca); the Haram (the sacred mosque); the Sunna (the Prophet's way); the Hajj (the pilgrimage to Mecca); the Fatiha (the opening chapter of the Qur'an); *ghusl-khana* (a bathing room); *halwa* (a sweet); *kheer* (a rice pudding); the Ghaznavids (the house of Mahmud of Ghazni, from the team version's "the last of Mahmud of Ghazni's line" and its "end of the dynasty"). Not glossed: Hind (India is named in the next sentences), the House of God, the Junaidi lineage (explained by its tracing to Junayd), the Committee Bagh.

## Judgement calls for V1

1. **Peer Makki and Data Ganj Bakhsh.** Only the shrine's tradition (the funeral) is told, with "traditions of the bond between the two saints differ"; the tutoring claim (which the live Description carries), Chishti's "disciple" and Alam Faqri's first visit to Data Ganj Bakhsh's shrine are dropped under rule 4. If V1 holds that Faqri's visit is an event of the life rather than a rival account of the bond, the fix is to add to The Saint: "On reaching Lahore he is said to have gone first to the shrine of Data Ganj Bakhsh." It would then stand beside the funeral tradition, which it contradicts.
2. **Identity and death in one clause.** "Accounts of who he was and of the year of his death have not always agreed." followed by the shrine's position and "the one most accounts give" (site, survey, Lahori's text, the editor).
3. **Location.** "Walled City" kept from the site's account (basis (a); only Kanhaiya Lal's contents line stands against it); "Accounts of exactly where the tomb stands have varied over time" as the one clause; the Committee Bagh kept as the nineteenth century setting; Ravi Road and "outside the city" dropped.
4. **One telling of the prophecy.** The site's anecdote (the 1178 siege, the governor seeking the saint's prayers) and the survey's fuller account are told as one story ("the king and the people alike ran to the saint for his prayers"), since the team version says of the anecdote that "The fuller account ... is given above".
5. **Khusrau Malik's end.** Bhandari's "died there" is left out of the Setting so it does not stand beside the story's "taken prisoner and later killed".
6. **Shahab-ud-Din Ghori in the Setting** for Bhandari's Shihab al-Din, following the team version, which sets Bhandari's campaigns under "Ghori and Lahore". Mu'izzu'd-Din Muhammad bin Sam is named as the team version names him and not identified with Ghori.
7. **555 AH.** "AH" added to Bhandari's bare 555 (a Persian chronicle's Hijri year; precedent: shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, judgement call 17).
8. **"though fewer people came then than in earlier days"** renders "in earlier days a great fair used to be held (the word for the fair uncertain in print), and ... fewer people came in his own time" without the uncertain word.
9. **The Danishnama article** in the Legacy: "A detailed article on him, under the heading 'Pir Makki', the name by which Lahore knows him, has been written for the *Danishnama-e Shibh-e Qarra*, an encyclopaedia of the Subcontinent published in Tehran." From the editor's statement that he has written it; the editor is not named. Fallback: delete the sentence.
10. **The Zaki Gate.** Kept as a namesake warning ("Lahore's Makki Gate is named for another man ... the form Makki is said to be a corruption of Zaki; Pir Zaki is a different man from Peer Makki"), without "thirteenth" (see consensus choices). Fallback: delete the three sentences after "Bhati Gate is one of the twelve gates of Lahore's walled city."
11. **"Tradition holds that his coming was by divine instruction."** (the site's "by what tradition describes as divine instruction") is followed by the survey's account of the Prophet's command; the two stand side by side without being equated.
12. **"In Hujwiri's day Lahore was the Indian capital of the Ghaznavids, the house of Mahmud of Ghazni."** For "the capital of the Ghaznawids in India", with the gloss above. "He died in Lahore, and his shrine is still a popular place of pilgrimage there" for "died there" and "still a popular place of pilgrimage in Lahore".
13. **"the Ghurids, it is said, took the land by a thousand tricks and deceits"**: Chishti's characterisation, framed with "it is said" rather than given in the narrator's voice.
14. **The keeper.** "In that century the keeper of the tomb was a woman *faqir* ... and she had two sons, Fateh Din and Karam Din" for "The keeper was a *faqir* woman ... with two sons". "A platform marking a mosque" kept literal.
15. **"The custom of paying respects at Peer Makki's tomb before going on to Data Darbar honours him as a spiritual forerunner in the same sacred place."** For "honouring him as a spiritual forerunner in the same sacred locale".
16. **"the outward sciences of Islamic law and the inward path of the Junaidi Sufi lineage"** for "the exoteric sciences ... and the esoteric path". Junaid Baghdadi (among the family's kin) and "the classical Baghdad master Junayd" keep the team version's two spellings and are not equated.
17. **Kings and the Auqaf.** "Kings, it is said, maintained the shrine after him and added to its beauty" (survey, framed); "in 1958 the Auqaf Department took charge of it" (survey, plain, as field material). The team notes' question whether this sits against Chishti's account of a *faqir* keeper is not a conflict in the public text.
18. **Length 0.41.** Low but within the range; the cause is the apparatus listed at the top. If V1 edits push it under 0.40 with nothing important lost, the coordinator may need the L1 exception line.
