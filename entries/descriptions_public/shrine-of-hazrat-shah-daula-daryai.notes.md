## Consensus choices

- **Death year: 1675**, "He died at a great age in Gujrat, in 1675, though accounts of the year differ." Basis (a), the site's account; the rivals (Lahori's 1075 AH, the editor's 1087 AH / 1676, Bhandari's seventeenth regnal year of Alamgir, Malik's "around AH 1676") are not one source against it but several disagreeing with each other, and two of them rest on readings the team version marks uncertain. No variant named. Wording follows mithankot ("though accounts of the year differ") rather than "the year the shrine keeps", because the 1675 is the site text, not a custodians' or *ʿurs* date.
- **Birth year: around 1581**, "tradition places his birth around 1581". Basis (a); no source gives another. "Around" keeps the site's "roughly".
- **His master's name: Shah Saidan Sarmast**, with "so do the names given to his master". Basis (a). Lahori's Shaikh Sina Sarmast, Bhandari's Hazrat Mian Sayyidna (and the editor's uncertain Sayyidnam) and Malik's unnamed Suhrawardi are not listed.
- **His order:** "There is no settled view of his order. His master is remembered as a Suhrawardi, but Shah Daula is also said to have been initiated into the Qadiri order." Basis (a) and (b): the site and Malik both make his teacher a Suhrawardi; Malik's Qadiri initiation is the clause. "Most often counted a Suhrawardi" is not used, because Lahori's chain is not called Suhrawardi in the team version and Bhandari names no order, so "most often" would rest on two sources. The Chishti *faiz* is kept as a further grace, not as a rival order (Lahori gives it as received "also").

## Glosses

## Judgement calls for V1

1. Early life: only the site's telling is given, with "Accounts of his early years differ"; Bhandari's slave of a Sialkot man and Malik's turbulent childhood, treasurer, mendicant and labourer are dropped as variants (the team version lists them as a disagreement). If V1 judges them complementary rather than rival, the fallback is a sentence after "Accounts of his early years differ": "In one telling he was at first the slave of a man of Sialkot; in another, after a turbulent childhood, he kept the treasury of a local official and lived for a time as a mendicant and a labourer." That would, however, set variants side by side.
2. The chain: given only as "traced back through a long line of masters, by way of Shaikh Sadr al-Din Rajan Qattal of Uch, to Shaikh Baha al-Din Multani", not link by link (rule 4 against rule 7, as on Shah Jamal): Lahori's chain starts from Sina Sarmast, not the site's master, and its fourth link is uncertain.
3. The deathbed glance: Bhandari's transformation at his dying master's glance is kept as legend ("Tradition tells"), attached to "his master" and not to the reason he left Sialkot; Bhandari's "treasures of the unseen" is joined to it ("revealed to him") as the team version's Legends section does ("because the saint's heart was so illumined").
4. "spending, it is said, twelve years at his feet": Malik's twelve years with an unnamed Suhrawardi Sufi, joined to the site's discipleship with the site's master. If V1 holds this merges two accounts, delete the clause.
5. Bridges: the site's Nala Dek bridge ("among them, by tradition") and Bhandari's Deg bridge ("above all") are both kept, never identified or distinguished. Fallback: delete ", among them, by tradition, a bridge over the Nala Dek," if V1 holds them rival names for the great bridge.
6. "It was substantially rebuilt in 1898": the site's "traditionally 1898" without "traditionally", on the strength of Malik's plain date.
7. The Auqaf paragraph: the site's reform and Malik's "could not totally change" are given as compatible facts in two sections (The Shrine; The Chuhas of Shah Daula). "Since the middle of the twentieth century" covers Malik's 1960s.
8. 1839: "first described by an observer in January 1839 in the earliest printed notice of the *chuhas*", with "Accounts differ over when the tradition took shape" for Malik's late dating. The bibliography dates the printed book 1847; the sentence dates the observation, as the team version does ("first recorded in print in January 1839").
9. "his bridges above all the second title, *Daryai*, one affiliated to water": Malik's printed form at that point is uncertain, but the gloss is clear, the form elsewhere in his chapter is *Daryâ'î*, and the site independently explains *Daryai* by his works across streams.
10. "a crow saint seems to have quite a following in this historic city": "seems" keeps the team version's quoted "seemed to have".
11. "Colonial writers also suspected the shrine's followers of trafficking in women": kept as a colonial suspicion (the team version's "colonial authors also suspected the followers"), placed with the band of faqirs. If V1 or Rauf judges it not important enough to carry an unsubstantiated allegation on a public page, it can be deleted without loss of anything else.
12. "The exchange at the tomb has been likened to a bargain ...": the team version files Malik's analysis under the shrine's practice, so it is given there, in the past tense of the old custom, with "has been likened" for "He analyses".
13. "One scholar reads the story as showing ...": Malik's verdict on the story, unnamed; "the moral authority of the shrine" for "the moral authority emanating from the shrine"; "devotees" for "adepts".
14. "In the reign of Aurangzeb" anchors all Bhandari material (the tomb visited by every class, the town's market and crafts, the Legacy sentence).
15. "His was a practical sanctity, honoured for the tangible relief of hardship": the site's sentence without "less for miracles than", so as not to contradict Malik's "famous for his miracles", which is kept.
16. "*Chuhas* ... is the name long given to children with microcephaly who were associated with the *darbar*" and "The tradition concerns vulnerable and disabled human beings, and it has often been sensationalised, so it must be approached with care": the site's own framing, kept in its voice.
