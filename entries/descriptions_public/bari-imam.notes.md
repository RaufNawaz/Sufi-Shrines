## Consensus choices

- **Death year:** "Accounts of the year of his death differ; established tradition gives 1705." The Overview gives "(1617 to 1705)". Basis (a): 1705 is the current site text (the team version's "current account", the bibliography's "General established histories and traditions"), and Frembgen's "1705/1706" includes it. Dropped: 1706 (Frembgen's alternative; the annotator's 1117 AH / 1706 CE from a family record) and Lahori's 994 AH, which is marked [OCR?] in any case (rule 5).
- **Birth:** 1617, given as fact. The current account and Frembgen agree; no source contradicts it.
- **Name:** Sayyid Abdul Latif Kazmi / Sayyid Abdul Latif Shah, called Bari Imam and Bari Sarkar, basis (a), the current site text. Lahori's Shah Latif Bari Qadiri is kept as the name under which the 1875 notice gives him, and the Persian account's Sayyid Shah Latif Majzub is kept inside the meeting story. Frembgen's spelling Barri Imam is dropped silently as a variant spelling (Bari Imam throughout).
- **Identification of Lahori's Shah Latif with Bari Imam (a framing choice, not a listed disagreement):** the team version's Overview says the link rests on the title of a modern book the annotator cites (*Hayat-e Bari Imam*) and on the meeting in the Pothohar, not on any statement in Lahori's text; its Life section nonetheless gives Lahori's notice as about the saint. The public version keeps exactly that strength: "a Shah Latif Bari Qadiri", who "is linked with the saint of Nurpur Shahan through a later book on Shah Latif that bears the name Bari Imam in its title, and through a meeting with the saint in the Pothohar". The praise and the masters are then given as that notice's (it is said / he is said to have). The team notes ask Rauf to confirm the identification at all; V1 should confirm this framing claims no more than the team version does.
- **His masters:** "There is no settled view of his masters", then Hayat al-Mir Zinda Pir (inner blessing) and Shaikh Nasir al-Din Qurashi Multani (*faiz*), both framed "said to". Basis (d): the shrine's own account (a) names no master; only one book names any (so (b) cannot settle it), and its own annotator doubts the sobriquet Hayat al-Mir. The two masters are named by the same source, separately, as two masters; they are not joined into a chain. The annotator's argument (Shaikh Jamal Allah, Shah Abul Muali, Astarabadi's *Tazkira-e Muqimi*) is left out.
- **Tomb's place:** Nurpur Shahan, basis (a) and (b) (current account and Frembgen). Lahori's silence on the place is kept only as "though without naming the place where it stands", because it is the reason the identification is hedged.
- **Length of the ʿurs:** five days. The current account's "several days" and Frembgen's "five days" agree; the more precise figure is given. "Held in the spring" (current account) is kept; Frembgen's 1994 date (first week of May) is given in the Mama Ji paragraph where it belongs.
- **The five lamps:** kept as burning "in honour of Bari Imam", the reading that concerns this saint. The same page's "symbolising the Holy Family" is dropped as minor (rule 4, third bullet).
- **Order:** Qadiri, given as fact; the current account, Lahori's chapter and Frembgen agree.
- **Time anchors:** 1875 for Lahori's notice (firm in the bibliography: composed 1292 AH / 1875 CE). Frembgen's material is given in the present tense or with its own dates (1990, 1994); no anchor on the 1998 volume is needed.
