## Consensus choices

- **Death year:** 1210, "though accounts of the year differ" in the Death section ("in November 1210"); the Overview gives "in 1210" without the clause, as the Kot Mithan row gives its birth year. Basis (a) the site's account, and (b): Alam's note and Chishti also give 1210 (Chishti's reading flagged, so not relied on); only Schimmel's index ("d. 1206") is against it. Rizvi's "(1206-1210)" is a span of rule, not a death date; Abbas's year is unreadable; Kanhaiya Lal gives none; Bhandari's is illegible.
- **Place of death:** Lahore, with no clause. Basis (a) and (b): the site, Kanhaiya Lal and Alam. Bhandari names no place and Rizvi's index has no Lahore; both are absences, not a rival place.
- **Where Ghori left him:** basis (d), no position to choose: one clause gives both, "Accounts differ over where Aibak was first posted, whether at the head of the forces in Delhi itself or as viceroy at Kuhram, a small town seventy *kos* from Delhi". No site position; one book each (Rizvi Delhi, Bhandari Kuhram). Because the two disagree over who took Delhi, Bhandari's "took the forts of Delhi and Meerut" is given as Meerut only, and Rizvi's "Ghori had conquered Delhi" is not stated; Delhi as his seat (Bhandari, agreed by all) is kept. Bhandari's "victory at Narain" is not named, since the team version does not equate Narain with Tarain; the text says "When Ghori went back to Ghazni", which both accounts give.
- **Accession:** "Aibak then established independent rule based in Lahore, though accounts differ over whether his reign began there or at Delhi." Basis (a), the site's account, which Bhandari supports (he came out of Delhi to Lahore and took the throne there). Chishti's Delhi accession (in 602, [OCR?]) and Kanhaiya Lal's throne of Delhi handed to him are the variants, not listed. Bhandari's day and month (the eleventh of Rabiʿ al-Awwal) are kept; his year 603 is marked uncertain and dropped (rule 5). Ghori's assassination in 1206 is the site's; Chishti's "died without a son" is kept (only his year is flagged).

Bhandari's twenty years of governing Hindustan (deputyship included) and the 1206 to 1210 span (Rizvi's index, and the site's own accession and death years) measure different things, so both are kept and neither is called a variant. Chishti's "five [OCR?] years" is dropped (rule 5); Bhandari's four years of Ghazni go with the Ghazni variants.
- **His successor:** basis (a), "He was succeeded, eventually, by his son-in-law Shams al-Din Iltutmish", with Aram Shah between, as Bhandari and Chishti give him; Kanhaiya Lal's and Abbas's direct succession are absences of the interval, which "eventually" covers.
