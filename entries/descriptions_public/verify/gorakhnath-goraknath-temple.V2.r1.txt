NOTES:
1. Clarity of reference (Legends of Gorakhnath, paragraph 2). The English names Goga Pir again ("to help Goga Pir against Rai Pithora"); the Urdu replaces the name with ان and leaves the contest without a subject, so the reader has to infer whom Gorakhnath helped. The natural reading is still Goga Pir, so the meaning survives, but giving the contest to him makes ان unambiguous without repeating the name.
OLD: گوگا پیر کی کہانی کی ایک روایت کے مطابق رائے پتھورا سے مقابلے میں گورکھ ناتھ نے ان کی مدد کے لیے غیبی لشکر بھیجا۔
NEW: گوگا پیر کی کہانی کی ایک روایت کے مطابق جب رائے پتھورا سے ان کا مقابلہ ہوا تو گورکھ ناتھ نے ان کی مدد کے لیے غیبی لشکر بھیجا۔
2. Slight added exclusivity (Peshawar and the Frontier, paragraph 2). The English says only that armies and caravans "used" the Khyber road; اسی کو makes it the one road they took, which sits awkwardly beside the earlier sentence that armies did pass along the Nikshab road, with difficulty.
OLD: اور فوجیں اور قافلے اسی کو اختیار کرتے تھے۔
NEW: اور فوجیں اور قافلے اسے اختیار کرتے تھے۔
3. Style only (Guru Gorakhnath, paragraph 4). بائیں ہاتھ کے مخفی علوم is a word for word rendering of "left-handed occultism"; the sense is the tantric left hand path (وام مارگ), which the literal phrase may not convey to an Urdu reader. Faithful as it stands; a matter for V3.
4. Style only (Legends of Gorakhnath, paragraph 1). یہی خیال ... دیکھتا ہے is a little stiff; the attribution itself (اس خیال کی رو سے) correctly keeps the Mahdi parallel as a view, matching the English colon.

Checked and found faithful: all eight headings correspond in order and every paragraph has its counterpart; every figure the English has (2011 three times, 1990s, 1641, 1851, 1947, 2010) appears with the same meaning and no other figure is added; no Latin letters, no em or en dashes, asterisks balanced. The hedges are kept: تقریباً (some two thousand years, some fifteen metres, some six decades), لگ بھگ (around 1851, roughly sixty years, about a hundred), عام طور پر ... سمجھا جاتا ہے (popularly taken), معلوم ہوتا ہے (Guru Nanak seems to have stayed), روایتی طور پر ... بتایا جاتا ہے، عقیدہ ہے، بیان کیا جاتا ہے، مانا جاتا ہے، عموماً، بتایا جاتا ہے (said to have been composed), روایت ہے، اسی روایت کے مطابق، کہا جاتا ہے twice, ایک اور قصے کے مطابق، ایک روایت کے مطابق، کے بقول. "Has not" for the Hatha Yoga is rightly read as not surviving (the team version: "unlike the Hatha-Yoga, the Goraksha-Sataka has survived"). Naming Matsyendranath as the master Gorakhnath saved resolves "his master" correctly from the preceding sentence. The Bulleh Shah line, which the English gives only in translation, is rendered as one italic Urdu paragraph without the credit line, as the brief directs. Dropped glosses (yogi, khichri, dhuni, kos, Shivratri, Sufis) and removed italics on ordinary words are as the brief allows; the gloss of قلندر is kept; حضرت محمدؐ، حضورؐ، خواجہ and مہاتما are conventional honorifics.
