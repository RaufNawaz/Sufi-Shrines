VERDICT: PASS

ISSUES: none

NOTES:
1. Omission sweep. I checked the whole team version for omissions and nothing important is missing. A ratio of 0.42 is the honest length for this row. Every fact about the saint is kept: birth around 1747 / 1160 AH at Multan, death 1811 / 1226, his master, his place as third of the four chief khalifas called sahib-e qurb, his contemporary Taunsvi, the reported resistance to the Sikhs, his three languages, the Siharfi, and the three malfuzat collections with their compilers. Also kept: the tomb's site, form and paintings, the ʿurs and the visiting, the order in Multan, every verse and every passage on the setting and the Sikh conflict. What was dropped is either source apparatus or fine under the rules: the Hadiqat entry no. 72 and its second heading form "Khwaja Muhammad Jamal" (a minor name variant), the index lines, the further-reading pointers, and the material on the "Jamal al-Din Multani" of the Sufi Martyrs bibliography, which was rightly left out because that book never equates him with the saint, so naming Khuda Bakhsh as a disciple would merge two people. Also dropped: Bahauddin Zakariya's two differing death years (side detail here), the uncertain 835 AH, and Majheel[OCR?]. The one family member the team version names is the master's father, Hindal, and he comes only from an index line. Optional: "Khwaja Noor Muhammad Maharvi, son of Hindal". Leaving it out is not an important omission for this saint's page.
2. Rule 4 choices hold. 1811 and 1226 AH do not conflict, and the team version itself pairs 1226/1811, so giving both with no "accounts differ" clause is right. "Established ... firmly" (the site's own account, basis (a)), together with "The order had touched Multan long before", deals with Alam Faqri's "first" without setting variants side by side. Judgement call 1 (using the Hadiqat editor's footnote for this saint) is supported: the team version uses the footnote of him in its own voice.
3. Precision: "Under the Sikhs, the notables of the Punjab suffered, and the tombs of its shaikhs fell into ruin." The team version's phrasing ("what the notables of the Punjab suffered under the Sikhs and the ruin of the tombs of its shaikhs") does not clearly put the ruin under the Sikhs. Suggested: "The notables of the Punjab suffered under the Sikhs, and the tombs of its shaikhs fell into ruin."
4. Precision: "Around shrines in Multan, as in Sahiwal, performers of Sufi music sing to earn a living" makes a general statement out of what the team version says only of "the performers she studied". Suggested: "Some performers of Sufi music sing around shrines in Multan, as in Sahiwal, to earn a living, and in an earlier age ..."
5. Certainty: "The saints of the Punjab, among whom Hafiz Jamal is counted, are the subject of the Hadiqat al-Awliya" comes close to saying the book itself treats him, which the team version leaves unsettled ("How firmly that entry belongs to this saint is not settled"). Suggested: "The saints of the Punjab are the subject of the *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, a Persian book completed in 1875, whose modern edition names Hafiz Jamal among Noor Muhammad's *khalifas*." Alternatively, simply drop "among whom Hafiz Jamal is counted".
6. "Mecca and Medina (and for the Sindhi and Panjabi, also Multan) are inside the heart" stands in quotation marks with no speaker, so it reads as the poets' own words. It is a scholar's sentence. Suggested: "... the holy cities lie within; as one scholar of mystical poetry put it, "Mecca and Medina ... are inside the heart"." Or drop the quotation marks.
7. "Multan is also remembered as the place where Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki ... met" adds "remembered", but the team version simply names Multan as the place. Suggested: "It was also in Multan that Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki, coming from Baghdad, met Shaikh Bahauddin Zakariya."
8. Ambiguous pronoun in "his contemporary Sulaiman Taunsvi urged his followers": "his" could be taken as Hafiz Jamal's followers. Suggested: "urged his own followers". This also helps the Urdu.
9. Small points, acceptable as written: "His own age falls within ... the golden age" is a little stronger than "places its flowering in his own age", since his life runs to 1811. "A poem of Sachal speaks of" states as fact what the team version gives as Schimmel's reading. "The year of the book's completion" is a fair reading of the chronogram's 1292 AH next to "composed 1292 AH / 1875". "Bahauddin Zakariya" for Eaton's "Baha' al-Haqq Zakariya" is a spelling choice the notes record. No citations, source narration, process words or dashes remain in the prose, and the time anchors (Aurangzeb, 1875, eleventh century, Sikh period) are all firm.
