NOTES:
Rulings on the flagged choices.
(1) Devotion sentence without "after that": accept. The English has no "after that" in either place: the Mai Bhagi sentence ("He was the devoted follower of Mai Bhagi Lahori ... he would not leave her") and the Mian Mir story ("and remained a devotee of the saint and respectful of its mujawirs") both give a continuing state, not a sequence. "اور وہ میاں میر کے عقیدت مند رہے اور ان کے مجاوروں کا لحاظ کرتے رہے" carries "remained" in رہے / کرتے رہے, and "مہاراجہ مجذوبہ مائی بھاگی لاہوری کے بھی سچے معتقد تھے؛ ... پھر بھی مہاراجہ ان کا ساتھ نہ چھوڑتے" is exact. Adding اس کے بعد would have been an addition.
(2) Sada Kaur relics split into two sentences: accept. The second sentence names سدا کور again, so no reference shifts; "more than once ... and was refused" is کئی بار ... مگر سدا کور نے انکار کر دیا, and "under stronger pressure" is جب دباؤ اور بڑھا. Same facts, same order.
(3) دیگر خطے for "other lands": accept. It removes the "countries" reading of ملک and says neither more nor less than the English.
(4) Subah Shah, "کہا جاتا تھا کہ ظاہری بادشاہ تو مہاراجہ ہیں": accept. The English names no inner king, so naming one (or naming Subah Shah as it) would be an addition. The particle تو carries exactly the contrast that "outward" already implies in English, and the hedge "it was said" governs the whole remark in both languages.
(5) Jind Kaur kept as "another Muslim wife" and apart from Rani Jindan: accept. The English presents "another Muslim wife of his, Jind Kaur" and later "Rani Jindan, mother of Maharaja Duleep Singh" without identifying them; merging the two, or dropping "Muslim", would change the English.
(6) شیرِ پنجاب and "the last Maharaja" kept but lightened: accept. The English gives the epithet twice (Overview; "also known as Sher-e-Punjab" in Devotion) and "the last Maharaja of the Punjab" twice (His Death; the 2011 portraits); the Urdu now has each exactly twice, in the same places, with the 2011 instance folded into "ان کے سب سے چھوٹے بیٹے اور پنجاب کے آخری مہاراجہ دلیپ سنگھ".
(7) شیر سنگھ named in Dhian Singh's words: accept. The English "he would soon be ruler of the Punjab" is told to Sher Singh and the next sentence ("Sher Singh came to the throne") confirms the referent; naming him removes the misreading of انہیں as Dhian Singh without adding a fact.
(8) شہزادے اور شہزادیاں for "the prince and the princess": accept. The English sentence is habitual ("who would go from the fort to the city through it") and describes a class of royal users, not two named persons; the plural renders that sense naturally, where singular شہزادہ اور شہزادی read as two particular people.
(9) "ایک سیاح نے لکھا ہے کہ ..." for "as one visitor wrote": accept. "wrote" is restored and سیاح no longer reads as one of the cross border pilgrims (زائرین) of the previous sentence. Taking the framing clause "مہاراجہ جس شہر کے کبھی حکمران تھے" inside لکھا ہے کہ is a negligible shift; the quoted words are exact.
