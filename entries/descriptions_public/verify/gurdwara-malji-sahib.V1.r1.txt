Rulings on the writer's Points for V1.
1. Length: no L1 exception is needed. The four fixes make the prose about 12 characters shorter, and the ratio stays at 0.41 (about 22,539 against 54,507).
2. Placing: keep as written. The placings agree: near the station, close to Kiara Sahib, the pair furthest from the Janam Asthan, and about five hundred metres from it. None contradicts the site's account (basis a), and the team version's own narrative joins them with "likewise". These are not side by side variants. The notes say the placings are "in separate sentences", but "furthest" shares a sentence with the eastern cluster. That is fine because both statements are about the same pair.
3. Birth story: keep. Basis (a) as the notes word it is loose, because the team version gives no shrine or custodian account of the birth. The choice still stands by elimination. The only rival with a real source is the tradition that, by the team version's own account, "The Sikhs don't accept", and a Sikh shrine cannot keep that tradition. The third telling (Tripta among the sadhus and fakirs) belongs to the partly fictionalised reconstruction. The sentence is framed "Tradition tells" and has the one clause that accounts differ, so it claims no more than the source does. Record the basis in these words.
4. Sayyad Hussain: keep. The name is given inside the janamsakhi frame ("the janamsakhis go on"), so it carries no more certainty than the source, and the only rival name stands in the partly fictionalised reconstruction. Basis (c) does not apply, since no account here is devoted to this site; record the basis as above. The clause "accounts of the dervish's name differ" may stay or go, as the point is minor.
5. Day of death: keep. The year 1539 rests on basis (b). The day comes from the only source that gives one, and no source contradicts it. The clause covers Bhandari's reign variant.
6. The earliest janamsakhi (1658): keep. The team version states it plainly; it is a date, not source talk.
7. Miracles: keep. "is held" frames the view. "the stories of his life are full of them" matches "his biography is nothing but a story of his miracles" and "full of stories of miracles".
8. Partition: keep. "most often" is right, as two of the three accounts say so. "with no Sikh left" is Kartar's own statement about the same years.
9. Herons verse: keep the framing "In one modern retelling of his life".
10. Grandfather's house: leave it out. Bhandari places that house only through the town name the team version marks uncertain, and setting it beside Talwandi would make the uncertain reading say what the other sources say. Without a place it is a minor household detail, so leaving it out is not an important omission.
11. Lehna's appointment scene: leave it out. It belongs to the partly fictionalised reconstruction. The appointment itself, the title Guru Angad and the move to Khadur are all kept.
12. About 1520: keep. The team version records no disagreement here. The draft lists the four directions of travel without an order ("and west to Mecca"), so the sentence does not clash with the 1506 return after the first journey.
13. Sites kept apart: this holds everywhere except the bare "the gurdwara" in three places (Issues 2 to 4). These are all correctly kept apart: the untouched crop at Kiara Sahib, Sachkand, Rori Sahib at Eminabad, Kanganpur, Baba Farid's picture "at one of the shrines", "visited his shrine", Ranjit Singh's "many gurdwaras", the mahants, and the festivals of Nankana Sahib.
14. Janamsakhi framing: it holds sentence by sentence. Giving the marriage, the sons' births and the death as plain dates is acceptable, and the marriage and the death carry their clauses.
15. Identifications not made: confirmed. Shiv Ram is not called the grandfather, the Adi Granth and the Guru Granth Sahib are kept separate, the small village and Kartarpur are in separate sentences, and the mal and the waan tree are not joined.
16. Identifications made: accepted. Mardana has the same name and the same role in both sources. Lehna, Guru Angad, Angad Dev and "the most loyal student" belong to the same deathbed act, and the team version says the books agree on it. The mound is the team version's own "that mound". The team version's disagreement section treats Khalid's Giani Pratap and Kartar's as one man.
17. Headings: not a fidelity matter. Nine headings is above the brief's usual three to seven, so folding The Sikhs of Nankana Sahib into Nankana Sahib is optional.
18. Verse: all seven renderings are verbatim, and they are uncredited because the team version names no translator. Accepted.
19. Persons named without source names: accepted. "Kartar, a Sikh of the town" is a fair reading, since he speaks of "our Gurdwaras", his family was housed in a gurdwara, and his father served there.
20. Glosses: all are definitional. Fakir as "a poor wandering mystic" follows data/glossary.csv's faqir ("Mystic/poor one; wandering Sufi"), and Issue 1 removes the only clash with it.

L1 ruling: no exception is needed. The fixes remove almost nothing, and the ratio stays at 0.41. The sweep of the team version found no important, certain fact missing that is not a rejected variant. Every builder, ruler and family member is present: Mehta Kalu, Mata Tripta, Shiv Ram, Rai Bhoe, Rai Bular, Sulakhini and her parents, Sri Chand and Lakhmi Das.
