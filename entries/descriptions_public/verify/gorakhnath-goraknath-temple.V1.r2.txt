1. (precision) Ambiguous subject. "The disciples of Gorakhnath and other Siddhas" can be read two ways: as "the disciples of Gorakhnath, and other Siddhas", or as "the disciples of Gorakhnath and of other Siddhas". The second reading moves the belief from the Siddhas to the Siddhas' disciples. The team version has "Siddhas, such as the disciples of Gorakhnath and others are believed to have achieved the practical aspects of this philosophy". Here the Siddhas are the ones believed to have achieved it, and the disciples of Gorakhnath are one example of them. The fix follows the source's own structure, and "such as" already covers "and others". Nothing else in the sentence changes.
OLD: The disciples of Gorakhnath and other Siddhas are believed to have achieved this in the foothills of the Himalayas, and they came to be known as *jivan-mukta*, liberated while living.
NEW: Siddhas such as the disciples of Gorakhnath are believed to have achieved this in the foothills of the Himalayas, and they came to be known as *jivan-mukta*, liberated while living.
