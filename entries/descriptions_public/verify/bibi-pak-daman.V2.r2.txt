Rulings on the writer's departures from V3 (all kept):
معافی چاہتے ہوئے: keep. "Asking forgiveness" is معافی چاہنا; V3's معذرت کرتے ہوئے ("apologising") would turn the keepers' plea for pardon into a social apology.
ذکر کی ایسی مجلسیں ... جن میں قرآن کی تلاوت ہوتی تھی: keep. The English has one kind of assembly, zikr assemblies held for recitation; V3's ذکر اور تلاوتِ قرآن کی مجلسیں would make two.
ایک اہم تاریخی کتاب: keep. It renders "one of its major histories" and avoids the date/history ambiguity of a bare تاریخ.
یکے بعد دیگرے آنے والے حکمران ... ہر حکمران کے دور میں: keep. V3's بعد کے حکمران would limit the endowing rulers to those after Akbar, and ہر دور میں would lose "under every ruler".
متعدد عمارتیں ... جن میں سے بہت سی: keep. V3's کئی would weaken the English's second "many".
بی بیوں کے مرقد کی دیوار: keep. مرقد names the holy resting place, which is what "sanctum" means here; V3's احاطے would move the burial to an enclosure wall, a different structure in an article that keeps enclosures and sanctum apart, and the first draft's حرم read as women's quarters.
مفتی غلام محمد کے لیے "خورشیدِ دینِ محمد" سے تاریخ نکالی گئی: keep. The English says only that a chronogram honours him. V3's مفتی غلام محمد کی تاریخ would read as his death date, which the English does not claim (on a plain abjad count the phrase gives 1276, not his death year 1274), so the neutral کے لیے is the faithful form.
ایک ایسے مقدس جغرافیے میں: keep. The English is indefinite ("a sacred landscape") and does not name Lahore; V3's لاہور کے اس would add the place.
روحانی زندگی کے ان مراکز میں سے ہے جن کی جڑیں سب سے گہری ہیں: keep. It keeps both "one of" and "deepest", and the roots carry the fixity of "anchors"; V3's سب سے مضبوط روحانی سہاروں would change "deepest" to "strongest".
Convert prince singular in the Karbala section and plural honorific in the keepers section: acceptable. اسی نومسلم شہزادے with both his names ties the two sections to one person, and the honorific suits him as the revered founder of the line. ٹھٹھ کے ٹھٹھ: acceptable. It is an established idiom for throngs upon throngs and matches "immense crowds".
