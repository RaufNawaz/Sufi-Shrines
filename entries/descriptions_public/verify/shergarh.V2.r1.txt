1. "Daud was Abul Muali's uncle" | شاہ ابو المعالی کے لیے داؤد بندگی چچا بھی تھے: Urdu has no neutral word for "uncle", so the translator had to choose. چچا (father's brother) fits the team version's "Lahori makes Abul Muali his nephew" and the family settling at Shergarh. Accepted as it is. If the coordinator wants to be sure, check the word Lahori's Urdu edition uses (بھتیجا / برادر زادہ, or بھانجا).
2. "The shrine keeps the customary devotions: prayer, qawwali ... and langar" | یہاں حسبِ دستور نماز، قوالی اور لنگر کا اہتمام ہوتا ہے: نماز narrows the generic "prayer" to ritual prayer. It is a fair reading and not a changed fact. دعا و عبادت would be closer to the English's generality if preferred. The malang's "silent prayer" is correctly دعا.
3. The two italic captions after the chronograms ("*A chronogram on the saint's death.*", "*A second chronogram on the same death.*") have no Urdu paragraph. They state a subject, not a meaning, and the subject is already in the Urdu sentences that introduce each chronogram (جس سے ان کی تاریخِ وفات برآمد ہوتی ہے / دوسرے مادّے سے بھی یہی سال نکلتا ہے). Both phrases are readable as they stand by an Urdu reader (the brief's exception for verse already in Urdu). Not an omission. The verse lines پیر حق پرست and مشتاق منان are byte identical to the English file.
4. "Abul Muali wrote of his master in a work called the Naghmat-e Daudi" | انہوں نے اپنے مرشد کے بارے میں ...: the referent is correct, since the previous sentence's subject is Abul Muali (وہ). But the English names him, and the brief asks for ان / انہوں نے to be resolved, so ابو المعالی نے would be safer (V3's call).
5. "Two chronograms ... mark the year of his death, 982 AH" | ان کا سالِ وفات 982 ہجری ہے، اور اس کے دو مادّہ ہائے تاریخ ملتے ہیں: this has the same assertion as the English appositive, and it is kept apart from the 1575ء sentence as the notes intend. دو مادّہ ہائے تاریخ is slightly stiff; تاریخ کے دو مادّے reads more naturally (V3's call).
