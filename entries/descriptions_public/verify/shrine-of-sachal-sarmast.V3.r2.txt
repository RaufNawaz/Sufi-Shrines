The round 1 note on کیلنڈر beside تقویم is also settled (ہندو تقویم وکرم سموت).
2. Change 9, adapted: یہ مزار سچل کی یاد زندہ رکھے ہوئے ہے، جنہیں یہاں سندھی تصوف کی وجدانی آواز مانا جاتا ہے is correct Urdu. The extraposed relative is an ordinary Urdu pattern, the honorific plural جنہیں can only take سچل (not مزار or یاد), جنہیں ... مانا جاتا ہے has the right default agreement, and the memory no longer takes the form of a voice. It sits well after the sentence on the singing and before the gate inscription. Taste only: putting Sachal first (یہاں سچل کو ... مانا جاتا ہے، اور یہ مزار ان کی یاد ...) would avoid the relative altogether, but the present form is not a fault.
3. Change 8, Bedil's line: with the verb supplied, the parallel عام لوگ ... پڑھتے ہیں، اور عاشق ... پڑھتے ہیں reads as two contrasted subjects, and repeating the verb in a rendered antithesis is a device, not careless repetition. Taste only: جبکہ in place of اور would make the contrast, and so the reading of عاشق as the subject rather than as عاشقِ خدا, unmistakable.
4. Change 10: خود نمانو فقیر کے مرشد دڑازہ کے ایک بزرگ سخی قبول محمد تھے is natural; بزرگ avoids a second مرشد in the sentence and does not clash with پیروں in the next.
5. Change 11: تقویم now occurs twice in the sentence (اسلامی تقویم ... ہندو تقویم), but the two are set against each other on purpose and the Christian one is عیسوی سن, so this is parallel, not repetition.
6. Change 13: اپنی محبت اپنے ساتھ بھارت لے گئے is idiomatic; the two reflexives do different work.
7. Change 16: ان محفلوں now opens two consecutive sentences (... ان محفلوں کی گائیکی کی جان ہے۔ ان محفلوں کے ذخیرے میں ...). Across sentences, and the price of removing the ambiguity, so acceptable; taste only, یہاں کے ذخیرے would vary it.
8. Change 19: شعرا and شاعر stand in the same sentence (بہت سے سندھی صوفی شعرا ... ان میں شمالی سندھ کے شاعر). Mild; the head noun of a partitive is natural Urdu. Taste only: شمالی سندھ سے تعلق رکھنے والے would avoid the echo, as would plain شمالی سندھ کے.
9. Changes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 20, 21 read as natural, correct, educated Urdu with correct agreement, postpositions and word order, no new ambiguity and no calque, and each fits the sentences around it. In 1, کے ہاتھ سے دوسرے کے ہاتھ میں repeats ہاتھ as the idiom does. In 2, اتنی زبانوں followed by کتنی زبانوں in the next sentence is a natural follow-on.
10. Mechanics of the whole file after the changes: no Latin letters, no em or en dashes, Western digits, asterisks balanced (the checker prints ok).
