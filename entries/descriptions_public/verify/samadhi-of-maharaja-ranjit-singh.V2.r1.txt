Rulings on the flagged points.
(1) Sher Singh as Sada Kaur's نواسے: accept. The English makes Sada Kaur Ranjit Singh's mother-in-law and Sher Singh his son and her grandson, so he can only be her daughter's son; نواسا adds nothing the English does not already entail.
(2) Nau Nihal Singh as Ranjit Singh's پوتے: accept. Urdu must choose between پوتا and نواسا; the sentence pairs "his son Kharak Singh and his grandson Nau Nihal Singh", and پوتا (son's son, Kharak Singh's son) is the correct relationship. No fidelity fault.
(3) Shahid Ganj (samadhi) and Shaheed Ganj (mosque) both شہید گنج: keep both as they are. The two English spellings are transliteration variants of one Urdu name, and the English nowhere says they are different places; the head nouns (شہید گنج کی سمادھی، شہید گنج کی مسجد) already keep the two objects apart. Inventing a distinction in Urdu would itself be an addition.
(4) Subah Shah as صوبہ شاہ: accept. No spelling for him exists in urdu-dictionary.json or any urdu-i18n/content file; صوبہ شاہ is the usual Urdu form of this name.
(5) Rani Nakain as رانی نکائن: accept. No dictionary entry exists; نکائن (from the Nakai misl) is the usual Urdu form. Singular سمادھی matches the English "the samadhi of Rani Nakain and Rani Chand Kaur".
(6) Legacy naming مستان شاہ، مائی بھاگی، تاج شاہ: accept. Each English description is unique and resolved within the article itself: only Mastan Shah refused his thousands, only Mai Bhagi is a majzuba who abused him and whom he would not leave, only Taj Shah spoke of sandalwood. Naming them adds no identification the English leaves open, and the hedge "it is told" is kept (روایت کے مطابق) on the sandalwood clause. Optional: the categories majzub, majzuba, faqir are absorbed into فقیروں; that is acceptable.
(7) Dhian Singh's words with انہیں for Sher Singh: accept. "دھیان سنگھ نے شیر سنگھ سے کہا کہ خدا نے چاہا تو جلد ہی پنجاب کی حکومت انہیں ملے گی۔" is exactly as open as the English "he would soon be ruler", and the next sentence (شیر سنگھ حکمران بنے) resolves it the same way in both languages.
(8) Loudspeakers: accept. "جن کا رخ میدان کی جانب، مسجد اور شہر کے مقابل تھا" is as open as "facing the ground, opposite the mosque and the city": مقابل carries the same "across from" ambiguity as English "opposite", and میدان agrees with the article's own description of the Hazuri Bagh as "a small open ground" (کھلا میدان). No change.
