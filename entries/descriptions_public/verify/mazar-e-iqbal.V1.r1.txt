1. Point (a), birthplace: rule 4 is applied correctly and the basis is in the team version. The team narrative and its disagreement list give Sialkot, 9 November 1877, as the site's account, which rests on the bibliography's "standard biographies of Iqbal" for life and dates. The only dissent is Boivin's explanatory note ("a poet born in Lahore"), and the same book prints the same years elsewhere. That is basis (a), with (b) behind it, so "generally given" claims no more than the source allows. The variant is not named and the doubt takes one clause. "It was in Sialkot that he spent his boyhood" is supported by the team narrative (Mir Hassan near the family home, the Scotch Mission High School in Sialkot, "From Sialkot, Iqbal moved to Lahore"), and no source disputes it.
   Day of death: also correct. The team version gives 21 April 1938 as "the date given in the standard biographies and observed nationally", says the tomb's own records preserve 21 September as a variant, and the books give only the year. 21 April is the date the commemoration follows: the death anniversary on 21 April is observed nationally and marked at the mausoleum with wreaths and the parade. Every other source that gives a day gives 21 April, so under basis (a) the tomb's records do not override it. The year 1938 is common to all. The doubt takes one clause and no variant is listed.
2. Point (b): confirmed by a script comparison. All eight verse paragraphs are byte identical to the team version. Five renderings had an em dash in the team version: the prayer and the khudi couplet now use a comma, and Tulu-e-Islam, the shaheen couplet and the hand of the believer now use a colon. The other three (Tarana-e-Hindi, the ghazal, the stars couplet) are unchanged. Nothing else in any rendering differs. Leaving out a credit line is correct, since these are the team's own renderings and not a published translation. The quoted lines in Schimmel's English (Light the world..., All our glory..., For us Mustafa..., You can deny God..., If the purpose of poetry...) keep the team wording; two only gain a closing full stop.
3. Point (c): the anchor is firm. The passage is from Mystical Dimensions of Islam, whose bibliography item dates it to 1975 (the 2011 edition adds only a new foreword), and it says the maqam was erected "a few years ago". "By the 1970s" follows the house convention ("By the 1870s" for Lahori 1875, "By the 1880s" for Kanhaiya Lal 1884). Optional, for precision only: "By the middle of the 1970s a *maqam* of Muhammad Iqbal, a place bearing his name, had been raised in the garden of Rumi's tomb at Konya".
4. Point (d): sound. The text keeps the site's account (the Übermensch fascinated and repelled him, and the mard-e-momin is a figure of immense will whose power is surrendered to God) but drops "likewise". It gives Schimmel's point only as the negative her sentence states outright ("rather than from the Nietzschean superman"). The team version does not identify "these theories", so leaving the positive source unnamed is right. The mard-e-momin and the Perfect Man stay two terms and are not equated. Nothing is set side by side and no chain of influence is invented.
5. Point (e): this is one supported picture, not a merged claim. The team version's own Overview sums up the material as "his quarrel with the Sufi masters of his own day and his debt to those of the past", and the public text follows that shape. Each element is stated as the team version states it: pirism and the backwardness of so called spiritual leaders; the reaction to wahdat al-wujud poetry; Hali's verdict on mystical poetry after Hafiz; the Javid Nama couplet; following classical Sufi thought quite closely; turning from Hegelian and Ibn ʿArabi pantheism; relying heavily on the early masters; using the whole fabric of Sufi imagery; the gluttony satire in the run of poets after Bedil; and the ghazal for Nizam ad-Din Awliya' despite his reservations about institutional Sufism. The site's "decayed mysticism" stays framed as "what he saw as".
6. Optional fixes. None changes a fact, and applying any of them makes this verdict stale.
   a. Line 117: "Longing is the highest state the soul can reach, for it results in creativity, whereas union brings about silence and annihilation." This reads as the site's own doctrine, but the team version gives it as Iqbal's philosophy. Suggested: "In his thought, longing is the highest state the soul can reach, for it results in creativity, whereas union brings about silence and annihilation."
   b. Line 15: in "...Dagh Dehlvi, a connoisseur of Urdu, he is said to have marvelled...", the "he" can be read as Iqbal. Suggested: "...Dagh Dehlvi, a connoisseur of Urdu, Dagh is said to have marvelled that whoever had written them was no ordinary poet."
   c. Line 127: the team version does not give the figure "two years before". It gives 1938 and March 1940, which is less than two years from 21 April 1938. Suggested: "He died in 1938, nine years before Pakistan came into being in August 1947, and before the Muslim League presented in Lahore, in March 1940, the Lahore Resolution, drafted by Sir Zaffarullah Khan."
   d. Line 111: "for a close study of his work makes it clear that" is scholarly framing that the public text does not need. Suggested: "The attack has its counterweight, for he himself followed the Sufi thought of the classical period quite closely."
   e. Line 77: "In Iqbal's verse Muhammad is both..." narrows the team version's "Iqbal's prophetology", which is his thought about the Prophet and not only his verse. Suggested: "In Iqbal's thought Muhammad is both the political leader of his community and the scent of the mystic rose."
   f. Line 77: "calling the Prophet by his name Mustafa" treats a title as a name. Suggested: "calling the Prophet by his title Mustafa, the Chosen One".
   g. Line 27: "His collections followed one another:" is loose. The list covers single long poems such as the Asrar-e-Khudi and Musafir, and the team version calls them "the Lahore printings of the poems". Suggested: "His books of verse followed one another:".
   h. Line 155: "The neighbourhood around the tomb has stories of its own." is a connective of the kind rule 6 discourages. The paragraph reads well if it opens at "The Roshani Gate". Optional deletion.
   i. Line 125: the Shaheed Ganj sentence ("also") sits in a paragraph that opens "In the last years of his life". That hints at a date the team version does not give. Optional: move the sentence into its own short paragraph or drop "also".
   j. Line 79: "whose copies are still sold in the shops of Lahore" puts a 1975 observation in the present tense. This is harmless, and the pilots keep similar wording. Optional: "whose copies were still being sold in the shops of Lahore in the 1970s".
