NOTES: The prose is mostly fluent and often idiomatic (the ascetic passages, the Mulla Shah testing story, the Rabi al Awwal death scene read very well); the failures are a run of calques, agreement slips and ambiguous phrasings rather than wholesale problems. Taste and editorial points, not counted above: (a) "ء" appears only on 1635 and 1645 beside Hijri dates while other Common Era years are bare; defensible, but could be uniform. (b) Content repetition the editor may wish to trim: daily prayer, Thursday qawwali, langar and urs crowds appear in both the opening and the devotion section; Dara's two tazkiras are named three times; the Dharampura location is given twice; the courteous dismissal of visitors appears in two sections; the definition of "قلندر" and the introduction of Data Sahib are pitched at outsiders. (c) "اپنے پیر کی قبر" (Dara and Mian Mir) sits oddly beside "دادا پیر" and "اپنے مرشد ملا شاہ"; "اپنے دادا پیر کی قبر" would be consistent. (d) Honorific number for Dara wavers ("یہی شہزادہ ... بنا", "شہزادہ ... سپردِ خاک کیا گیا" against "داراشکوہ ... تھے"). (e) "ان کے اپنے مرشد" (Mir Inayatullah) leaves unclear whose murshid; "مرحوم محمد شریف" reads like "the late" and, if he is the "حضرت محمد شریف" of the Bibi Jamal paragraph, one form should be used. (f) "پچاس برس کی مدت میں ایک کنواں بنوایا" reads oddly (fifty years to dig a well) and may be worth checking against the source. (g) Smaller touches: "تارکِ دنیا ... ترکِ دنیا" in one sentence; "ناپسند ... ناپسندیدگی" (Sirhindi); "معروف تھے" twice in the Bala Pir sentence; "ایک سے اطمینانِ قلب" reads more easily as "ایک جیسے اطمینانِ قلب سے"; "خود شہر میں انار کلی کے صدر بازار میں انیسویں صدی میں" stacks three "میں"; "میاں میر کی یاد کی طرف کھینچ لاتی ہے"، "خونریزی کے راستے"، "متلاشی روح کی آبیاری"، "آدرش" and "دھرم پورہ ... بستی تھا" are a little literal or modern in flavour; "میاں میر روڈ کے مغربی جانب اس سے ملحق" has a redundant "اس".
