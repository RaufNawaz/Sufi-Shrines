NOTES:
1. [readability / sensitive wording] "Haydar Ali Shah, too, opposed the sectarian and messianic Ahmadi movement, but Meher Ali Shah was said to be one of those most influential in marginalising and stigmatising its latter day prophet, Ghulam Ahmad of Qadian." -> Every element is in the team version, but "the sectarian and messianic" and "latter day prophet" are Ernst and Lawrence's own characterisations, which the team version keeps inside quotation marks. The public text now says them in the site's own voice, and "latter day prophet" without the hyphen and without quotation marks could be read as endorsement or as sarcasm. Haydar Ali Shah is also not identified (the team version does not say who he is either). Optional, without adding anything: "Haydar Ali Shah, too, opposed the Ahmadi movement, but Meher Ali Shah was said to be one of those most influential in marginalising and stigmatising Ghulam Ahmad of Qadian." The sentence before it already names "Mirza Ghulam Ahmad of Qadian", so the repeated name could also become "him".
2. [precision] "In their performances they move between the Persian verses of Jami, Hafiz and Rumi, the Punjabi poetry of Meher Ali Shah and Ghulam Farid, and the classical Hindi of Amir Khusrau." -> The team version's quotation ends "among others". Without it the list reads as complete. Optional fix: "... and the classical Hindi of Amir Khusrau, among others."
3. [readability] "Pir Meher Ali Shah united the roles of *shaykh*, or spiritual master, scholar and poet." -> With the gloss in the middle of the list, it reads as four roles, or as "spiritual master, scholar" glossing *shaykh*. Optional fix: "Pir Meher Ali Shah united the roles of scholar, poet and *shaykh*, or spiritual master."
4. [readability] "In Rawalpindi, the living room wall of Mama Ji carries popular prints ..." -> Mama Ji is a private individual from an ethnography, and the visitor is not told who she or he is. The team version's own Legacy line ("his picture on the walls of devotees' homes") supports a version without the name: "In one devotee's home in Rawalpindi, the living room wall carries popular prints of the saints Abdul Qadir Jilani of Baghdad, Pir Meher Ali Shah of Golra and Baba Lal Shah." This is optional, and keeping the name is not unsupported.
5. [possible ambiguity] "Meher Ali Shah initiated him and in time appointed him his successor, or *khalifa*." together with "Among his successors at the shrine was Pir Ghulam Mohyuddin, known as Babuji" -> Both are supported, and the notes' choice of two senses of "successor" is sound: no chain is merged and neither is set against the other. A visitor may still take "his successor" to mean that Hasan Nizami succeeded at Golra. The team version also has "made him a khalifa", so an optional, fully supported softening is "Meher Ali Shah initiated him and in time made him a *khalifa*, one of his successors."
6. [precision] "Golra Sharif also holds manuscripts. Two Persian works are recorded there as unique single copies" -> This is the team version's own wording, and "Golra Sharif" names the place. Because the paragraph sits in "The Shrine", it can be read as the shrine's own holdings, which the team version says the book does not establish. That is acceptable as written. Do not strengthen it to "the shrine's library". "Persian" is a fair reading of "bibliography of Persian sources".
7. [repetition] "Today his shrine is a large and busy complex, kept by his descendants." (Overview) repeats "The shrine of Meher Ali Shah at Golra is a large and busy complex. It is kept by his descendants" (The Shrine) almost word for word. Optional: in The Shrine, open with "Its keepers are his descendants, who have continued as its *sajjada nashins* ...".
8. [style] "In a conflict over the succession at Taunsa, the succession from Shaykh Sulayman divided into two branches" uses "succession" twice. Optional: "In a conflict over the succession at Taunsa, the line from Shaykh Sulayman divided into two branches, and one of them went to Meher Ali Shah."
9. Checks that came out clean. Birth year 1859 with one "accounts differ" clause (basis (a)) is correct. The death year 1937 is agreed. The Sialvi line and the Muhammad Ali to Habib Ali Shah to Sufi Saheb line are kept separate, and South Africa is tied only to the second line. "Was said to be" keeps the source's hedge. The Chishti gloss "founded by Moinuddin Chishti" is the glossary's definition. "In the early twentieth century" is a firm anchor. Leaving out the Eaton "Rawalpindi District" pointer is correct, because the team version says the identification rests on the place name alone. There are no citations, no narration of sources and no archive or process language. The prose has no em dash or en dash; the only one is in the verbatim bibliography line. No important omission: the team version names no further family members, and what was dropped is apparatus (the books' silences, the spelling paragraph, the Gilmartin, Faruqui, Chaudhry and Einzmann pointers, and the bibliographic placement of the manuscripts), Islamabad material that the team version itself says concerns the city and not the shrine, and the specialised point about print.
