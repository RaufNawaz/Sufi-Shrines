NOTES:
1. (precision) [attribution widened] "In the nineteenth century his grave was held to be older than any other building near Lahore, and one historian of the city wrote that before him the land of Hindustan had been without a lamp." -> Both statements are one man's (Chishti's): the team version has him judge that no building near Lahore was older than this grave, and has the "without a lamp" line from him too. "was held to be" turns one historian's judgement into a general nineteenth century view, and the split makes the reader think of two sources. Give both to the one historian, naming Aibak so that "his" cannot be read as the historian's own grave.
OLD: In the nineteenth century his grave was held to be older than any other building near Lahore, and one historian of the city wrote that before him the land of Hindustan had been without a lamp.
NEW: In the nineteenth century one historian of the city held Aibak's grave to be older than any other building near Lahore, and wrote that before the Sultan the land of Hindustan had been without a lamp.

2. (precision) [claim moved outside its frame] "...as a graveyard for at least 150,000 dead; this was the origin of the present Miani Sahib graveyard." -> The team version gives the whole statement, the origin of Miani Sahib included, as one newspaper column's claim ("Aibak, according to Dawn's column, had also wanted ..., the origin of the present Miani Sahib graveyard"). After the semicolon the origin is stated in the narrator's own voice, outside "is also said to". Keep it inside the hedged sentence, as the team version does, with an appositive.
OLD: Aibak is also said to have wanted Lahore's dead buried together, setting aside land south of the village of Mozang as a graveyard for at least 150,000 dead; this was the origin of the present Miani Sahib graveyard.
NEW: Aibak is also said to have wanted Lahore's dead buried together, setting aside land south of the village of Mozang as a graveyard for at least 150,000 dead, the origin of the present Miani Sahib graveyard.

3. (precision) [ambiguous word] "as one Persian chronicler put it" -> The team version calls the book Persian ("Sujan Rai Bhandari's Persian Khulasat al-Tawarikh"), not the man, and its bibliography names him "of Batala". A general reader will take "a Persian chronicler" as a chronicler from Persia. Say he wrote in Persian.
OLD: He was playing *chaugan* when he fell from his horse; as one Persian chronicler put it, he carried the ball of his life to the field of the hereafter.
NEW: He was playing *chaugan* when he fell from his horse; as one chronicler writing in Persian put it, he carried the ball of his life to the field of the hereafter.

4. Taste: the article carries seven "accounts differ" clauses. Each answers a disagreement the team version records, but two of them (where Aibak was first posted; what followed the battle with Yildiz) are close to the "minor" end of rule 4 and could have been dropped silently. Not required.

5. Taste: "A general who sent his booty from Bihar to his master at Delhi is remembered too, in a story that..." "is remembered" is a little warmer than the team version's plain "tells of"; "There is also a story of a general who..." would be closer. Not required.

6. "twenty eight" without its hyphen looks odd; "twenty-eight" is the normal spelling of the number, not a coined compound. Neither is a fidelity point.

7. Checked and sound: the Sikh period sentence keeps "the bricks of old tombs and houses were pulled out" from a Chishti sentence the team version calls partly uncertain. The team notes put the [OCR?] marks on the words for the old mansions and the verb of the "were lost" clause, both of which the public text drops, so the bricks clause does not rest on an uncertain reading. Both verse paragraphs (the Persian couplet and the italic kafi) match the team version byte for byte. No citations, page references, narration of named authors, archive or process language survive. The time anchors are sound: "In the nineteenth century" for Chishti (date unconfirmed), "By the 1880s" for Kanhaiya Lal (1884), "In the early twenty first century" for Khalid (2013).
