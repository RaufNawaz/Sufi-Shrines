NOTES:
Rulings on the writer's doubtful choices:
1. The 1521 clause: acceptable as written. اس کا can be read as the اُداسی or as the واقعہ, which is exactly the ambiguity of the English ("one of his udasis in the early sixteenth century, often dated to around 1521"), so nothing is resolved that the English leaves open. The date carries all of the English hedge: اکثر = "often", کے لگ بھگ = "around", and the passive بتایا جاتا ہے keeps it a reported dating; the clause also stays inside the sentence that opens with روایت. No change.
2. Wali Kandhari on the summit: acceptable. The English sentence continues the story begun with "the story goes" in the previous sentence, and what it states (the pir on the summit controlling the only spring) is part of the legend, not a plain historical claim; اسی کہانی کے مطابق makes that context explicit without widening a frame over anything the English states as fact. "remembered as" is carried by کہلاتے ہیں. Setting the name gloss as its own sentence (ان کا نام ان کے قندھاری الاصل ہونے کی طرف اشارہ کرتا ہے) is fine: "recalling an origin" and اشارہ کرتا ہے are equally soft, and the gloss is the narrator's remark, so it rightly sits outside the story frame. No change.
3. Hamza Ghous, "جن کے لوگوں کو نانک نے بچا لیا": acceptable. In the English the two coordinated relatives ("who threatened ... and whose people Nanak saved") both attach grammatically to Hamza Ghous, and the Urdu جنہوں نے ... اور جن کے follows that surface reading exactly. جن کے cannot point to Sialkot (a city would take جس کے), so the Urdu is a shade less open than the English, but it adds no identification the English grammar does not already make. No change.
4. "By local media reports": correct. The figures of about 7,000 pilgrims, about 2,000 from India, the rest from Pakistan, and more than 200 at the entrance all sit inside the one sentence governed by مقامی ذرائع ابلاغ کی رپورٹوں کے مطابق, as in the English. No change.
5. Word choices. دے ماری for "hurled": correct and idiomatic. شاہی پرچم for "royal standards": correct; it keeps the English metonymy for the royal camp. ایک عرصے تک: leans towards "for a long while", slightly more than "for some time"; see precision note (c). حال ہی تک for "Until recently": correct in meaning (a limit in time, not "by"); see style. کشادہ دلی کی علامت سمجھا جاتا تھا for "taken to symbolise the openness of the religion": correct; کشادہ دلی fairly renders openness to all comers, and the past passive matches "taken to" in a 2011 narrative.
6. Ain-i-Akbari, "جسے ابوالفضل نے تیسرے مغل شہنشاہ اکبر کے عہد میں تصنیف کیا": correct. ابوالفضل is the standard Urdu form of the English "Abu-Fazl" (same person, no identification added); جسے refers to the nearest noun, آئینِ اکبری; "written" = تصنیف کیا. No change.
7. Babur Bani line in double quotes, "اٹھہتر میں آئے (1521 عیسوی)": correct. Double for single quotes is a typographic choice; "seventy-eight" as the Urdu word اٹھہتر is allowed; 1521 and C.E = عیسوی match. No change.
8. The two Mardanas: correct. Bhandari's "a singer named Mardana" is مردانہ نام کا ایک گویّا, with no بھائی and no link to the legend's companion; the legend's man is جاں نثار مسلمان مطرب بھائی مردانہ (Guru's Visit) and نانک کے مسلمان عقیدت مند بھائی مردانہ (Baisakhi), as in the English. Here نام کا ایک is right, because the English itself introduces this singer as a stranger. Nothing ties the two together.

Optional precision notes (meaning, not taste):
(a) The Gurdwara, para 1: "a sarovar ... fed by the springs, whose waters gather into pools" takes "whose" most naturally as the springs; اس کا پانی (singular) points to the tank. Suggested: "چشموں کا پانی ان تالابوں میں جمع ہوتا ہے جن میں عقیدت مند اشنان کرتے ہیں۔" in place of "اس کا پانی ان تالابوں میں جمع ہوتا ہے جن میں عقیدت مند اشنان کرتے ہیں۔"
(b) The Guru's Verse, para 1: "drew people into the snare of devotion" loses its "snare" in عقیدت کے حلقے میں (circle). Suggested: "لوگوں کو کھینچ کر عقیدت کے دام میں لے آتا تھا".
(c) The Setting, last para: "for some time" is کچھ عرصہ; ایک عرصے تک suggests a long period. Suggested: "شاہی پرچم کچھ عرصہ حسن ابدال میں ٹھہرے رہے".
(d) The Guru's Verse, para 1: "the worship of his followers centred on reading their master's verses" is stronger in Urdu, عبادت یہ تھی کہ (their worship consisted of). Suggested: "اورنگ زیب کے زمانے میں گرو نانک کے پیروکاروں کی عبادت کا محور یہ تھا کہ وہ اپنے مرشد کے اشعار پڑھتے اور انہیں سُر کے ساتھ گاتے تھے۔"
(e) The Guru's Visit, last para: "who calls his cure a miracle" becomes ایک شفا یابی, dropping the possessive. The English "his" is itself ambiguous (Karori's own cure, or the Guru's act of healing), so a neutral rendering is acceptable; no change unless the writer knows whose cure is meant.
(f) Baisakhi, para 1: "the shrine's great festival" as سب سے بڑا تہوار is a mild superlative; acceptable, since the article throughout presents Baisakhi as the main festival.
(g) The Guru's Verse: the lead in "Of washing he sang" is expanded to پانی سے ہاتھ پاؤں دھونے کے بارے میں, which anticipates the line that follows. No fact is added; "دھونے کے بارے میں گرو نانک نے گایا:" would be closer.
(h) اپنے مرشد for "their master": acceptable; مرشد carries a Sufi colour, and اپنے گرو would be the neutral word if the writer prefers.
