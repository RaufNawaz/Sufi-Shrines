The Overview: Hazarkhwani on the edge of Peshawar; the most beloved poet of Pashto and the great mystic voice of the Pashtuns; the spiritual and literary heart of the Pashto speaking world; the gathering place of poets and singers; three centuries of the *Diwan* as the book most cherished after the Qur'an; verses quoted as proverbs and sung from Peshawar to Kandahar. The life: born around 1653 near Peshawar into the Mohmand tribe; "lovingly called Rahman Baba"; the reign of Aurangzeb; little documented, no courtier, no chronicle; humble means, retiring temperament, a dervish who shunned the powerful; the Hajj "by tradition"; died around 1711 and buried at Hazarkhwani. I found no added fact, number, count, name or relationship. The evaluative words ("unmatched sweetness", "gospel", "moral compass", "hearth", "a gentler way") are all the team version's own.

3. Rule 4. I agree with all four consensus choices. (a) Death year: around 1711 is the current site text, given with "though accounts of the year differ", and 1709 is not listed. (b) Retiring life: the two accounts agree in substance. Each part keeps its own frame ("He is remembered as", "legend has it"), so no variant sits beside another and nothing is raised to plain fact. (c) Order: the current text names no order, so "he is counted a mystic of the Chishti order" contradicts nothing. "Is counted" is properly softer than a bare "was a Chishti", and "most often" is rightly avoided. (d) The 2009 attack: the current site text is basis (a), and one clause says accounts differ. The toll of "over 100" deaths is the side the team version itself records as the disagreeing account, so dropping it is a rule 4 decision, not an important omission. Taking the month from that account is not a merged account. The team version treats both accounts as the same 2009 attack ("The 2009 attack" bullet), and "March" conflicts with nothing in the current text. The writer's own claim about real reports of the bombing comes from outside the team version and correctly stays out of the text. Whether the toll should appear anywhere on the site is Rauf's question. If it is restored, it has to come back in the writer's supported form and must not sit beside the damage as a variant.

The brief's model clauses say what differs ("Accounts of the year of his death differ"). A fuller wording would have to hint at the toll, and the text deliberately does not, so the vaguer clause is defensible as it stands.
