1. [changed certainty] "The *hijras* are probably linked to the shrine by tradition, through the story of his years among the dancing girls." | غالباً خواجہ سراؤں کا اس مزار سے روایتی تعلق اس کہانی کی وجہ سے ہے جس کے مطابق بلھے شاہ نے رقاصاؤں کے درمیان برسوں گزارے تھے۔ -> The link is now in the subject, "خواجہ سراؤں کا اس مزار سے روایتی تعلق", and Urdu takes a subject like this as already given. The only thing the sentence asserts, and so the only thing غالباً hedges, is the cause, "اس کہانی کی وجہ سے ہے". The sentence therefore states as fact that the khwaja siras have a traditional link with the shrine, and hedges only why. In the English, "probably" governs the link ("are linked ... by tradition") and its route ("through the story") together, and V1 round 1 ruled that the link must not stand in the narrator's voice as fact. This is the mirror image of the round 1 fault, when the hedge covered the link but not the cause. So غالباً does not, as intended, cover both link and cause. The link has to be the main predicate under غالباً, with the story as its cause. That also meets V3's round 2 point: no long کے/کی chain before the subject, and no ایک. -> غالباً اس کہانی کے سبب، جس کے مطابق بلھے شاہ نے رقاصاؤں کے درمیان برسوں گزارے تھے، خواجہ سرا روایتی طور پر اس مزار سے وابستہ ہیں۔

The hijras sentence: fails. See issue 1.
Kushu in its own sentence, under "ایک اور روایت کے مطابق": faithful. It is still a report ("it is also told"). It keeps Kushu as his own figure, apart from Kush and from "one of the sons of Ram Chand", without saying whether they are the same person. That is what V2 round 2 ruling (a) required.
"یہ بھی مشہور ہے کہ": faithful for "is said to have founded". مشہور ہے کہ is the usual Urdu marker of a current report, the same register the brief gives for such sentences, and it does not make the founding a fact. بھی matches the additive "also" after the Luv and Kush sentence. The repetition of روایت three times in a row is gone.
