NOTES

a. Verse lines, byte for byte. Both lines have identical UTF-8 bytes in the team version (lines 39 and 368), the public English (29 and 273) and the public Urdu (29 and 273), with no stray marks and no trailing spaces. Each sits alone in its paragraph, with a blank line on both sides.

b. Insertion 1, English lead-in: supported. The team version says "Chishti dates Bahlul's coming ... with a line of verse that gives the year as nine hundred and fifty-five but names no era." The lead-in keeps each element (a line of verse, Bahlol's coming, 955, no era) and adds nothing. It does not rest on an uncertain reading. The flag "(the title uncertain in the printed text)" covers only which book Chishti follows, which the lead-in rightly leaves unnamed. Neither the line nor its use to date Bahlol's coming carries a flag. The lead-in also rightly does not add "AH". The team version's Lahori footnote gives 955 AH from *Haqiqat al-Fuqara*, but tying the two together would need the uncertain title. There is no citation, page or process language.

c. Insertion 1, rendering: accurate. سال (the year) پنجاہ و پنج و نہ صد (fifty and five and nine hundred) بود (was), so "The year was nine hundred and fifty five". It needs no credit, since it is not a published translation. Writing the number in words without a hyphen matches the article's own usage ("thirty six", "one hundred and fifty five thousand").

d. The 955 line and the surrounding text: it creates no new contradiction that the reader is not warned about. The public text gives no age at Bahlol's coming, and the line names no era, so no age can be worked out from the article. A reader who supplies AH gets about 1548: roughly ten for a birth in 1538, which fits "as a boy" and "while still a child". A reader who assumes the Christian era is warned by "without naming the era". ("Era" in the calendar sense is correct English. Its other use at line 250, "names its era, the reign of the emperor Muhammad Shah", does not clash.) One tension does exist: the Quran by heart by seven (line 23) against only six *paras* when Bahlol came (line 25). It predates the insertion, is hedged ("is said", "told as a wonder"), and the insertion neither creates nor sharpens it. It is outside this check.

e. Placement. Both insertions follow the team version's order. The 955 line follows the wonder and Bahlol's making him *imam*, and comes before "Shaykh Bahlol Daryai became his master"; the team version has it at the end of the same wonder paragraph. The further line follows the "firm mosque and firm well" couplet and comes before the sentence on the mosque belonging to the tomb, as in the team version.

f. A possible misreading for Rauf to check (not an issue under the rules). In مسجد و چاہ را انکو تعمیر, "انکو" is not a Persian word as printed. It may be a misreading, perhaps of نکو/نیکو ("finely"). The line also has no verb, so it cannot be rendered with confidence. The team version does not flag it, so rule 5 does not exclude it, and Rauf's ruling requires the original. Leaving it without a rendering is right: the team version gives none, and the "firm mosque" couplet above it also stands without one. It may be worth checking against the printed page (Chishti, p. 441) when the team version is next revised.

g. Fallback for issue 1. If the coordinator prefers to keep a hint that only one copy has the line, this wording does so without making a source the subject: English "The inscription is also given with a further line on the building of the mosque and the well:"; Urdu "یہ کتبہ ایک اور مصرع کے ساتھ بھی ملتا ہے، جو مسجد اور کنویں کی تعمیر سے متعلق ہے:". The wording in issue 1 is the cleaner of the two and is what I recommend.
