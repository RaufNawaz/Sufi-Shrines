VERDICT: PASS
ISSUES: none
NOTES:
Rulings on the flagged points.
(1) سید عبدالرحمٰن نام کے ایک بزرگ: accepted. It keeps the English's indefinite "a Sayyid Abd al-Rahman", and nothing in the paragraph links him to سید عبدالرحمٰن دہلوی or سید عبدالرحمٰن قادری of the paragraphs before. The chain has all ten links in the English order (عبدالرحمٰن، عبدالجلیل، عبدالبقا، عبدالستار، عبدالفتاح، نجم الدین برہانپوری، محمد صادق، عبدالجبار، عبدالرزاق، عبدالقادر جیلانی), and "each link received it in turn from the next" is kept as ہر کڑی نے یہ تلقین اپنی اگلی کڑی سے پائی.
(2) The three Siraiki passages in Urdu script: keep them. Earlier V2 rounds accepted the same treatment for Siraiki lines given in Latin transliteration (lal-shahbaz-qalandar, shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar), and here the words follow the transliteration word for word. Optional, for closer agreement: soṇā is سوݨا, the letter used on the Farid page for ṇ: "پیتل دا سوݨا مول نہ تھیوے". The other small departures (تے for "t", سخی for "sakha", the final nasal in کدھاں and بناں) are the usual Siraiki spellings of the same words and change no meaning. The drawn out vowels (na-a, mū-ū-l) are rightly left out, because the framing sentence says they were drawn out in the singing. Each passage has its short italic meaning paragraph, as the brief requires.
(3) مرید کی جاں کنی: accepted. In the English, "him" is the disciple, and "his death agony" most naturally refers to the disciple's death. The pir prays for the disciple at that hour, so the resolution is right.
(4) "کہ وہ دور مدینے میں دفن ہے، ھو": accepted. Urdu وہ is as open as English "it", so the ambiguity is carried over and nothing is resolved that the English leaves open.
(5) Shah Habibullah's message as direct speech: accepted. Every element is kept: the blessing is not with him, the sign of the destination, service of his own shaykh Abdul Rahman Qadiri, and holding the hem (دامن تھام لو). Direct speech also settles the pronouns cleanly.
(6) "مرنے سے پہلے موت" with the sentence reordered: accepted. The title, the fact that it is a common refrain in Sufi poetry and the fact that his poems reached English readers under it all survive. Putting the refrain first changes no claim.
(7) ناپسندیدگی کی نظر سے دیکھتے تھے for "looked askance at": accepted. It renders disapproval and suspicion faithfully.
(8) اس کی آوازوں کو کھینچ کر ادا کیا: acceptable, but loose. آوازیں is "sounds", whereas the English says "vowels". Optional, closer wording: "اور گاتے ہوئے اس کے حروفِ علت کو کھینچ کھینچ کر ادا کیا؛".

Optional precision notes (none of them a fidelity fault).
(a) The singer: the English shows that she is a woman ("the singer drew her words"). Because نے constructions carry no gender, the Urdu loses this. Suggested wording: "کسی محفل میں ایک گلوکارہ نے سلطان باہو کا سرائیکی کلام گایا" and "دوسرے کلام میں اسی گلوکارہ نے سلطان باہو کے الفاظ سے".
(b) The Hir and Ranjha grave: in the English, "are said to" governs only the burial, and "their shrine has become a place of pilgrimage" is plain fact. In "کہا جاتا ہے کہ ہیر اور رانجھا دونوں جھنگ کی ایک ہی قبر میں دفن ہیں، اور ان کا مزار زیارت گاہ بن چکا ہے۔" the hedge can be read as covering both clauses. Suggested wording: "کہا جاتا ہے کہ ہیر اور رانجھا دونوں جھنگ میں ایک ہی قبر میں دفن ہیں۔ ان کا مزار زیارت گاہ بن چکا ہے۔"
(c) "جسے سنہری الف بے بھی کہتے ہیں": the English "the Golden Alphabet" is an English reader's label, and Urdu readers do not call the siharfi by that name. The later "Golden Alphabet" is already rendered as اپنی سی حرفی, so the first one can go the same way: "سلطان باہو کی پنجابی شاعری کی ایک صنف سی حرفی ہے: ایسی نظم جس کے بند حروفِ تہجی کی ترتیب سے آگے بڑھتے ہیں۔" Keeping it is not wrong.

Checks: all eight headings correspond, and the Bibliography is correctly absent. The Punjabi line اَلف اللہ چنبے دی بُوٹی... is byte identical to the English. All twenty four works are present, in the English order. The figures match the English (1630 twice, 1691 twice, 50:16, 1870 three times; انتیس، دس، چار، آٹھ، ستر، چوبیس as words). Every legend frame and every "accounts differ" sentence is kept: the birth year, the father's name, the name Bahu, the branch name, the death year, the grave's location and his learning. There are no Latin letters, no em or en dashes and no Eastern digits. The asterisks are balanced (22). The dropped glosses (Sufis, Ramadan, khirqa as patched cloak, O He, Sikh scripture and others), the removed italics and the dropped Naveed Alam credit are deliberate under the brief.

Style: none of the points above affects fidelity. The text reads cleanly, and pronouns are kept clear by naming سلطان باہو, بی بی راستی and بایزید محمد again.
