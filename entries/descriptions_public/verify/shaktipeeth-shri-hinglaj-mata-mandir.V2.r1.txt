Rulings on the writer's flagged points:
1. Nani Mandir: the Urdu is right; keep it. The English sentence hangs the name grammatically on the goddess, but mandir means temple, and Clans, Peoples and Faiths, paragraph 4 ("the local Muslims of the region, who know it as Nani Mandir or Bibi Nani", where "it" is the shrine) uses it as the shrine's name. "اور اسے پیار سے نانی مندر بھی پکارا جاتا ہے" (اسے = مندر, the subject of the sentence) matches that and keeps the two places consistent. If the coordinator wants the English made unambiguous, that is a V1 matter, not needed for V2.
2. نعل نما سرنگ / پوری نعل کی شکل: accepted. A horseshoe is the natural Urdu image for a U curve, the نیم دائرہ of the same sentence is kept, and nothing about the shape is added.
3. شاعر کے نزدیک: accepted. The English appositive "the yogis, the true saints" sits inside a description of Latif's verse and states the poem's view, not the site's; the next paragraph's "For the poet" (شاعر کے نزدیک مشرق ...) is the same poet, so the wording is consistent. The sentence follows the Kabir comparison, but کوہِ ہنگلاج at its end ties it to Latif's verse. جوگی ہی سچے سنت ہیں stays within the English definite "the true saints".
4. بھارت کے گجرات: accepted, not an addition. The paragraph is about Indian groups, "Gujarat" is the Indian state, and گجرات alone would also read as Gujrat in Pakistani Punjab, so بھارت کے only removes a misreading the English does not have.
5. Both accepted. "1842ء تک ... تعظیم کرتے آ رہے تھے" gives a state already in place by that date and cannot be read as "until"; "1842ء تک ... بن چکا تھا" is the perfect the brief asks for (a plain موجود تھا with تک would read "until 1842").
6. سنگھاسن: accepted; it is literally the lion seat, the ordinary word for a throne, and very likely the inscription's own Hindi word.
7. "ہندو بھی اور دوسرے لوگ بھی ... اسی زمانے کا مانتے ہیں": accepted; بھی ... بھی carries "alike", the claim stays attributed (مانتے ہیں), and it is no wider than the English generic.
8. See issue 1: drop کچھ. The frame کم سے کم اندازہ ... زیادہ سے زیادہ is a fair rendering of "from ... to" under "accounts differ widely".
9. "دنیا سے رخصت ہو جانے والی برگزیدہ ہستی": accepted. برگزیدہ ہستی is the ordinary Urdu for a saint and, like "the departed saint", names no gender (ہستی is grammatically feminine for anyone).
10. "جانوروں کا چڑھاوا قبول نہیں کرتیں": accepted; same meaning as "accepts only offerings that are not animals".
11. "ناول کی کہانی 1946ء کی ہے": accepted; it says exactly that the novel is set in 1946, and the present tenses that follow keep the scene inside the novel.
