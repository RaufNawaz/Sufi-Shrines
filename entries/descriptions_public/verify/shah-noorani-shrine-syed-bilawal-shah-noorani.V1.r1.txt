2. Rule 4, the attack of 2016 (the point I was asked to check closely). The public text puts together the site's account (a suicide bombing during the evening *dhamal* that "killed and injured many worshippers", "one of the deadliest attacks on a Pakistani shrine") and Schaflechner's figure ("killing more than fifty people"). I do not judge this a merged chain or merged accounts in rule 4's sense. Both describe the same event, in the same month and at the same shrine. The team version's "Where the Sources Disagree" item records only a difference in detail and silence (Schaflechner "does not say when in the day it struck or who carried it out"); it records no opposed claim. Nothing in the combined sentences goes beyond the two together: "more than fifty people" keeps Schaflechner's "people" and does not become "worshippers", "injured many worshippers" is the site's wording, and no attacker is named. No "accounts differ" clause is needed, because nothing differs on a point that matters. "One of the deadliest attacks on a Pakistani shrine" is in the team version's narrator voice, so the public text may keep it. The writer's notes say the team notes flag it as unsupported by any book and leave the decision to Rauf; that decision should stay visible to him.

3. Rule 4, the pilgrims after 2016 and the journey, told as one story over time. Both are acceptable. "The terrible blast curbed the growing influx of visitors ... Yet ... the devotion of the pilgrims endured, and the shrine continued to draw them" is not a side by side listing of variants. A curbed growth and a continued draw are compatible, each clause carries exactly its own source's claim, and neither is hardened. The time order is also sound: the road brought a "growing influx", and the blast of November 2016 curbed it, which is what the team version's two Schaflechner passages say together. Likewise, the hard journey and "most inaccessible" (Overview) sit alongside "a newly constructed road ... has made the journey through the mountainous area easier". "Easier" is not "easy", and "The journey itself has changed" carries the team version's "a recent change in the journey itself". Optional and precision only: "Like Hinglaj, Lahut has seen a new wave of interest" could read "In the years before 2016, Lahut, like Hinglaj, saw a new wave of interest ...". That anchor is firm, since the team version says the November 2016 blast curbed this growing influx. It would make the sequence explicit for the reader. Not required.
