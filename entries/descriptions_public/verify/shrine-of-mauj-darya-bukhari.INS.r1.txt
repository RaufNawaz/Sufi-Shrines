1. [V1, rule 4: more certainty than the source. This issue alone FAILS V1]
English line 29: "a verse whose letters, read as numbers, give the year; the year 1014 of the Hijri calendar is printed twice within it:"
Problem: The team version records 1014 AH as a rival to 1604. Its Where the Sources Disagree entry on "The year and day of his death" reads: "The site's account gives 1604. The Hadiqat al-Awliya gives 1014 AH." The public notes left the verse out for exactly that reason (Left out, Verse; Points for V1, 3). The new lead-in names that rival year two sentences after "He died in 1604; accounts of the year and day of his death differ". It says the verse "gives the year" of his death, but it never says that 1014 AH is one of the differing accounts.
Most readers of the English cannot convert a Hijri year. They will take 1014 AH as the Hijri form of the 1604 they have just read, so the verse appears to confirm 1604. It does not. For context only (this is not from the team version): 1014 AH ran from about 19 May 1605 to 8 May 1606, entirely after 1604.
Is the reader misled? Yes, as the text stands. Either the page sets a rival year beside the established one without comment, which rule 4 forbids ("no listing of variants anywhere"), or, more likely, the reader infers an agreement that the team version does not record.
Rauf's ruling puts the year into the page through the verse, so it cannot be removed. The brief's tool for a disagreement that matters is one clause saying that accounts differ. The house precedent for a chronogram whose year differs is bhit-bhit-shah: "Accounts of the year of his death by the Hijri calendar differ, and this chronogram gives it as 1165". The fix is to tie the verse's year to that clause.
Fix. RECOMMENDED: replace the whole of English line 29 with the text below. It also resolves issues 2, 3 and 4.
By the 1870s his death had been set in a Persian chronogram, a verse whose letters, read as numbers, give a year. The year it gives, written twice within it, is 1014 of the Hijri, or Islamic, calendar, one of the differing accounts of the year of his death:
FALLBACK, if the coordinator keeps the book framing (see issue 2): replace the whole of English line 29 with:

"A book on the saints of the Punjab, written in 1875, closes its notice of him with a Persian chronogram"
Problem: This sentence narrates what a source does ("closes its notice of him") instead of stating the content. Rule 2 asks for the date to be anchored in time ("By the 1870s ..."), not in a source. This article already dropped the source of its other verse as book talk (notes, Left out, Minor: "the Khazinat al-Asfiya as the source of the quatrain (book talk)").
The Shah Jamal and Purana Bhalwal rows do say "In 1875 a book on the saints of the Punjab gave X a notice of his own". In those rows, though, the verse quoted is the book's own closing chronogram, so the book is part of the story, which is the brief's exception. Here the book only carries the verse.
A time anchor is still worth having. Without one, a bare "His death was set in a chronogram" could be read as a verse written in 1604. The team version does not say who wrote the chronogram or when, so the article must not say either; "By the 1870s" states only what is known.

5. [V3: calque, unclear pronoun, gloss of a familiar term, overlong sentence. FAILS V3]
Urdu line 29: "1875 میں پنجاب کے اولیا پر لکھی گئی ایک کتاب ان کا تذکرہ ان کی وفات پر ایک فارسی قطعۂ تاریخ پر ختم کرتی ہے، یعنی ایسے شعر پر جس کے حروف کے اعداد سے سال نکلتا ہے؛ اس میں سنہ 1014 ہجری دو بار درج ہے:"
Read as a native editor would read it, it has these problems:
(a) "ایک کتاب ان کا تذکرہ ... ختم کرتی ہے" makes the book the subject of ختم کرنا. This is a calque of "a book closes its notice". Idiomatic Urdu is "کتاب میں ... کا تذکرہ ... پر ختم ہوتا ہے", which is what this project's Shah Jamal and Purana Bhalwal Urdu use ("یہ کتاب ... قطعۂ تاریخ پر ختم ہوتی ہے").
(b) "ان کا تذکرہ ان کی وفات" opens a paragraph, and the last person named, two sentences earlier, is the son: "ان کے بیٹے سید صفی الدین ان کی میت لاہور لائے". The brief asks for ان to be resolved to a named person, so name میراں محمد شاہ.
(c) "وفات پر ایک فارسی قطعۂ تاریخ پر" puts two پر close together. "وفات کے ... قطعۂ تاریخ" is the natural collocation.
(d) "یعنی ایسے شعر پر جس کے حروف کے اعداد سے سال نکلتا ہے" glosses قطعۂ تاریخ, a term an Urdu reader knows. The Purana Bhalwal notes drop the same gloss as familiar, and the Data Darbar Urdu uses قطعاتِ تاریخ without one. The gloss is also inaccurate: شعر means a single couplet, but the quoted قطعہ is two couplets.
(e) The sentence has four clauses joined by ، and ؛, and the brief says to split sentences of more than three clauses. "اس میں" could mean either the book or the verse.
(f) Minor consistency point: "اولیا" here, but "اولیاء" in the Shah Jamal and Purana Bhalwal Urdu and "خلفاء" in this same article.
Fix: see issue 6.
