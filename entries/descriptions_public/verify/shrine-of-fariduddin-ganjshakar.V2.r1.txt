Rulings on the flagged points (line numbers are the same in both files; the 259 lines align paragraph by paragraph):
- Abbas qawwali lines (137, 141): acceptable. The Urdu script renderings follow Abbas's transliteration faithfully (devĩ دیویں، mangĩ منگیں، ehī ایہی، vaṇ وݨ، jhok جھوک), and since the lines are Punjabi/Saraiki the short italic meaning paragraph after each is right under the brief. Optional: "dāse" may stand for دسے (tells); داسے follows the transliteration and is not a fault. ݨ is already used in other site articles.
- Zikr syllables (87): acceptable. وُہی ہی، ہی ہی، ہِن ہی render wuhi hi, hi hi, hin hi faithfully, and italics are right for these unfamiliar terms.
- Khusrau lines (127 to 133) in Urdu script with no meaning paragraph: acceptable. The lines are Hindavi/Purbi that an Urdu reader follows (صدقہ، لاج، روپ، سووت); the English renderings exist for the English reader, and the brief's exception for verse already readable in Urdu applies. Dropping the transliterations from the lead in sentences is correct, since the line itself now stands as the verse paragraph. Translator credits correctly dropped.
- "uncles" as چچاؤں (239): acceptable. Urdu has no neutral word; the graves are of the father's line (father, grandfather; Qazi Shuaib came with three sons), so paternal uncles is the natural reading.
- Malaysian pilgrim as ایک زائر (231): acceptable. "Pilgrim" is زائر, and ایک introduces a new person naturally; not the calque the brief warns about.
