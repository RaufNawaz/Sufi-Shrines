Rule 5 keeps that reading out of the public text, and it cannot ground a public statement in either direction. The team version's other ground, that "Mian Mir" elsewhere in the book is only the cantonment, concerns other passages, not this account. The team version itself treats the same run of pages as this shrine's without any caveat: the chronogram "set over the door of the tomb" that yields the saint's death year (p. 244), the five fairs at the mazar attended by people of the Mian Mir cantonment (p. 245), and Dara Shikoh gathering stone for his pir's rauza (p. 242). 3. Ruling on point 4: supported. In the team version's own terms, Siyustani and Sistani are nisbas of the same place: it gives the birthplace as Sehwan or Sivastan, with Lahori's Sewistan and Chishti's "city of Sistan". The difference is therefore a variant spelling, which rule 4 treats as minor. Both accounts also give this master the same role: he confers the *khilafat* and permits or directs the move to Lahore. The team version words the shrine's account as "the master whom the shrine's tradition remembers as Shaikh Siyustani". Its disagreement entry "Who initiated him" concerns the first *bay'a* at the mother's hand, not two different masters. "Most often" is right: Lahori, Chishti and Kanhaiya Lal all give Khizr Sistani, against the shrine's one account. Because Siyustani and Khizr Sistani are one master, hanging Lahori's chain on Khizr Sistani does not repeat the pilot's fault.
4. Chain of masters: there is only one chain, and nothing is merged. Going up, Lahori's chain runs from Khizr Sistani through Sayyid Abdul Razzaq to Abd al-Qadir Jilani. Ernst and Lawrence's line (through 'Abd al-Qadir II and 'Abd Allah Bhiti) is reduced to its point, that Dara Shikoh set aside another lineage, and none of its upper links appear, so the two lines are not combined. Going down, line no. 152 is in the right order, Shah Bilaq (uncertain) is dropped, and "further down" marks the gap. One optional rewording, because "to Shaikh Muhsin Shah and Shah Abd al-Rashid Lahori" can read as two parallel successors: "in Lahore the Qadiri chain passed from Mullah Shah to Shaikh Muhsin Shah, and from him to Shah Abd al-Rashid Lahori, and further down to Sayyid Bhawan Shah, reaching the year 1213 AH."
