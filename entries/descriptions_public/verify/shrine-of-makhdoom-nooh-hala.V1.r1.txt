2. Glosses are definitions, not facts about the site. From `data/glossary.csv` (checked): *ʿurs*, the Suhrawardi order "founded by Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi", *dhikr*, and Sayyids as "descendants of the Prophet Muhammad". Plain dictionary senses: Sufi "or Muslim mystic"; *Makhdoom* "a title of honour meaning master"; Uwaisi "a bond with a master never met in life"; Hinglaj as a "Hindu goddess" (the bibliography title). I accept Judgement call 1: both glosses define the term and add nothing about this saint. "Glazed" in "*Kashi* pottery of the region, glazed in blue and white" is the only prose word with no basis in the team version or the glossary. It is the dictionary sense of *kashi* (glazed ware), so it is acceptable. An optional closer wording, losing nothing, would be: "with the celebrated blue and white *Kashi* pottery of the region".
3. Rule 4. (a) Century: "Accounts of the century in which he lived differ, but he is generally dated to the sixteenth century." This follows basis (a), the site's own account. It is one clause, the seventeenth century is not listed, and "generally dated" is the team version's own phrase. (e) Bhit Shah for Latif's tomb follows basis (b), Schimmel and Abbas's second passage. The Kolhi and Bhil sentence keeps Abbas's description of Hala and the work on the farms without placing Latif's shrine in Hala. I prefer the current wording to the fallback in Judgement call 9: the fallback's "come above all because this is the heart of ..." would bring back a causal link whose premise (the shrine located in Hala) has been set aside. (f) "a family of mystics who were Sayyids" joins two agreeing descriptions of one family. It is not a merged chain or account. (g) "He has been called 'the great Suhrawardi leader of Sind'" is a verbatim, contiguous quotation, cut before "in the 17th century" exactly as the team Legacy cuts it. It names no author, as rule 2 allows.
