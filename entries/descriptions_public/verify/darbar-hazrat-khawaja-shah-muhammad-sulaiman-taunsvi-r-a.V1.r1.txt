1. (precision) Judgement call 8, the Lahore khalifas. The team version's sentence ("Shaikh Haji Ramzan Lahori, and, through Haidar Shah and Khair Shah, the Lahore line of the following entry") does not make clear who the second khalifa is, or how Haidar Shah and Khair Shah stand to him. They may be his successors, as the draft reads it. They may also be the links through which a later Lahore line reaches the saint, and then one of them may be the khalifa himself. The draft picks the first reading and creates an unnamed khalifa with successors that the team version does not clearly give. The fix keeps the team version's count and the one clear name (the writer's own fallback, with the count kept).
OLD: Two of his *khalifas* are counted among the saints of Lahore: Shaikh Haji Ramzan Lahori, and another whose line in the city ran through Haidar Shah and Khair Shah.
NEW: Two of his *khalifas* are counted among the saints of Lahore, one of them Shaikh Haji Ramzan Lahori.

3. (precision) "such acceptance as no one else had in that age" is Lahori's superlative judgement. The team version itself frames it as his: "Lahori, writing in 1875, judged that he had won such acceptance as no one else in his age". Stated plainly, it becomes the narrator's own claim. The draft already frames the editor's credit to Allah Bakhsh ("has been credited") and Sharib's words on the *mazar* ("is held to be"), and the same framing fits here. The framing governs the whole sentence, including the lakh of disciples.
OLD: He won such acceptance as no one else had in that age: more than a *lakh* of people, that is, over a hundred thousand, became his disciples, and hundreds of unbelievers and sinners repented.
NEW: He is said to have won such acceptance as no one else had in that age: more than a *lakh* of people, that is, over a hundred thousand, became his disciples, and hundreds of unbelievers and sinners repented.
