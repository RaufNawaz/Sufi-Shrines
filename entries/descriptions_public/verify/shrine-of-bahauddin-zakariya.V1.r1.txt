1. [merged accounts, rule 4] The Saint, paragraph 5: "Within eighteen days, it is said, he received the *khirqa* ... and he was sent to Multan with leave to carry the order to India. It is told that the master's other disciples resented the bounty he received, and that the master answered them that they were like green wood and Zakariya like dry wood, and fire quickly catches dry wood." -> The team version records two accounts of his time with Shihab-ud-Din: Lahori's eighteen days to the *khirqa*, and Nizam al-Din's report (via Sharib) that he stayed seventy days in his master's service and received much bounty, which the other disciples resented, drawing the green wood and dry wood answer. The dry wood answer explains the speed of his progress, which is exactly the point the two accounts dispute, so the story cannot be detached from its own (rejected) timeline. Set directly after "Within eighteen days", it tells the reader that the disciples resented an elevation in eighteen days, which neither source says. Dropping the seventy (rule 5) does not cure it. -> Delete the sentence "It is told that the master's other disciples resented the bounty he received, and that the master answered them that they were like green wood and Zakariya like dry wood, and fire quickly catches dry wood." The paragraph then ends: "...and he was sent to Multan with leave to carry the order to India."

2. [important omission, the house's economy; writer's point 7] Life at Multan, paragraph 2: "He accepted endowments and gifts and managed considerable resources, though accounts differ over what chiefly sustained his house." -> The team version's four statements about what sustained the house do not contradict one another. The site says endowments and gifts. Sharib says agriculture and trade were carried on at his place on a large scale. Rizvi says gifts came from merchants and trade guilds, who traded outside India and so were independent of the government. Schimmel calls him a well to do landlord. Only Rizvi's "mainly" could set one against another. Without it the four facts stand together as a composite, which is not contradictory and not a merge (the data-darbar and Bibi Pak Daman standard). They are certain, they are not rejected variants once "mainly" goes, and they give the concrete economy of the household in an article whose main theme is his wealth. The clause "accounts differ over what chiefly sustained" also overstates the team version: no source but Rizvi claims a chief source. -> Replace the sentence with: "He accepted endowments and gifts and managed considerable resources. He was a landlord of means, agriculture and trade were carried on at his house on a large scale, and his *khanqah* prospered on gifts from merchants and trade guilds: many merchants traded outside India, were largely independent of the government, and could not be prevented from giving." In the notes file, change the consensus entry "What sustained his house" to record the composite (Rizvi's "mainly" dropped) and delete item 7 of "Rejected variants" ("the four sources of the house's wealth").
