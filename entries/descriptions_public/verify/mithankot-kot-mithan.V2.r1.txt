Rulings on the writer's questions:
(1) ڈوہڑہ جات: accepted. "Ma Dhorājāt" is the romanisation of an Urdu/Seraiki title, "with dohṛās". ڈوہڑا is the Seraiki verse form, and ڈوہڑہ جات is the normal Urdu form of the -جات plural (like رسالہ جات). A phonetic ڈھوراجات would be a meaningless word. This renders the title and adds no gloss or fact.
(2) کوریجہ: accepted. It is the usual Urdu spelling of the family name, and the English "Koreja" has neither an aspirate kh nor a retroflex ṛ. The existing short article's کھوڑیجہ looks like a mistransliteration: the same article also writes میتھن کوٹ. urdu-dictionary.json has no entry for the name. Keep کوریجہ, and consider correcting the short article if it stays in use.
(3) لدھیانہ and ستلج: accepted. Lodiana and Satlaj are the 1830s British spellings of Ludhiana and the Sutlej, and these are the same place and river. A phonetic Urdu form would be an archaism with no meaning for the reader. دریائے ستلج is right.
(4) کیفی جام پوری، کشفی ملتانی، ایم عبدالواسع: accepted.
  - Kefi Jampuri is Kaifi Jampuri, the Seraiki scholar from Jampur.
  - "Kashafi" carries an extra roman vowel in the takhallus کشفی.
  - ایم keeps the initial as the English gives it. Expanding it to محمد would be an addition.
(5) سرائیکی وسیب / خطہ / علاقہ: accepted, and applied consistently:
  - belt ×3 → وسیب
  - "across it" → اس پورے خطے
  - region → سرائیکی خطے
  - country → سرائیکی علاقے
  - "Seraiki speaking area" → سرائیکی بولنے والے علاقے
  None of these changes the extent described. وسیب is the established Urdu word and avoids the calque پٹی.
(6) Dropped glosses: all accepted under the brief. These are Sayyid, Sufi/Muslim mystics, عشقِ حقیقی/مجازی, سماع, ذکر, سلسلہ, سُر, قصبہ, مدرسہ and شریف ("noble"). The explanation that شریف is the honorific given to holy places is kept. The writer's list leaves out some glosses that were also dropped, and these are equally acceptable: kafi ("short mystical lyric", whose content still appears as مختصر صوفیانہ نظمیں in the Poetry section), wahdat al-wujud, sajjada nashin, Nawabs, murid, urs, qawwali, langar, pir and Zauq-i samaʿ.
(7) مقابیس المجالس with یعنی روحانی مجلسوں کے اسباق: accepted. The Arabic title is unfamiliar, so keeping the English's own rendering is right, and the gloss does not sit between a noun and its postposition. Transliterating the English titles (دی ٹیچنگز آف خواجہ فرید، ففٹی پوئمز آف خواجہ فرید، اے ٹریبیوٹ ٹو گریٹ مسٹکس) is right under the no-Latin rule, and each is marked as a title (انگریزی ترجمہ ... کے نام سے، کا ترجمہ، نامی ایک ریکارڈنگ). No italics on titles is fine under the brief.
