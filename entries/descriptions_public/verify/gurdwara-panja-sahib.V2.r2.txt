Rulings on the writer's doubtful choices:
1. "ساٹھ کے پیٹے کے" and "اس کا ہاتھ بھی نانک کے پنجے کے نقش میں ٹھیک بیٹھتا ہے": correct. ساٹھ کے پیٹے میں/کے is the idiom for a man in his sixties (60 to 69), so the age is no longer widened. "his hand too fitted Nanak's" means his hand matched the hand whose mark he was touching; "fits into the imprint of Nanak's palm" says exactly that, and بھی keeps "too". No change.
2. دیگر یاتری for "others were": acceptable. English "others" (no article) is open, not "all the rest"; Urdu دیگر یاتری carries no article and reads the same way, and it does not claim that every pilgrim not a Pathan Sikh was a Hindu. If the writer wants to rule out the "the rest" reading altogether, "اور کچھ دوسرے یاتری ملک کے مختلف علاقوں سے آئے ہوئے کلین شیو ہندو تھے" is the tighter form, but no change is required.
3. "جن لوگوں کی وضع قطع زیادہ مسلمانوں جیسی ہوتی": correct. "looked more Muslim" is comparative, and زیادہ ... جیسی keeps the comparison (V3's version without زیادہ would have flattened it). بھی ("even those") only spells out what the English implies after "a turban or head cover had been enough". واقعی مسلمان نکلتے = "only if they were Muslim". No change.
4. "the rest of the town was Muslim" moved into the population sentence: correct. "اور باقی آبادی مسلمان تھی" belongs with the count of Sikh families, and the 2011 frame now covers it, as in the English. One point arises from the split, though (see precision note (a)): the second sentence no longer says the families came FROM the tribal areas.
5. "سرجیت سنگھ اسی طرح اپنی سیوا کرتے تھے" for "as his way of performing sewa": meaning correct, and the habitual past fits "his way". However, اپنی سیوا کرنا, like اپنی خدمت کرنا, can be read as "to serve himself / look after himself", the opposite of voluntary service to others. Suggested: "سرجیت سنگھ اسی طرح سیوا کرتے تھے۔" or "سرجیت سنگھ کی سیوا یہی تھی۔" Not a fail, since context (sewa defined two paragraphs earlier as رضاکارانہ خدمت) steers most readers right.
6. "سکھ روایت کے مطابق" for the custom that anyone may enter: the English word is "tradition" ("By tradition anyone may enter a gurdwara, but the practice no longer existed in Pakistan"), used in the sense of custom, not legend. سکھ روایت کے مطابق is right: it reads as a rule of Sikh practice, not a story frame, and adding سکھ only names whose tradition, which "a gurdwara" makes plain. The second clause, "مگر پاکستان میں اب یہ دستور نہیں رہا تھا", renders "the practice" correctly. No change.
7. "مگر اسے بتایا گیا" in the 1922 section: correct. The English subject of "were told" is "the local sangat"; اسے points back to سنگت, the subject of the same sentence (feminine singular, so اسے is the right form), not to the prisoners. No change.

Optional precision notes (meaning, not taste):
(a) The Setting, para 2: English "who had moved there in the few years before from the tribal areas because of the rise of insurgency there". Urdu "یہ گھرانے اس سے چند برس پہلے قبائلی علاقوں میں شورش بڑھنے کے سبب یہاں آئے تھے۔" says why they came but not from where; the origin is only implied. Suggested: "یہ گھرانے اس سے چند برس پہلے قبائلی علاقوں میں شورش بڑھنے کے سبب وہاں سے یہاں آئے تھے۔"
(b) Baisakhi, entry paragraph: "A television crew that threatened to broadcast its treatment was let through." Urdu "ایک ٹیلی وژن ٹیم نے دھمکی دی کہ ... نشر کر دے گی، تو اسے اندر جانے دیا گیا۔" With تو the threat becomes the stated cause; the English only juxtaposes them in a relative clause (the cause is implied, so this is very close). If the writer wants the English shape: "ایک ٹیلی وژن ٹیم کو، جس نے اپنے ساتھ ہونے والا سلوک نشر کرنے کی دھمکی دی تھی، اندر جانے دیا گیا۔" Optional.
(c) The Gurdwara, para 1: "سہرا بھی ہری سنگھ نلوہ ہی کے سر باندھا جاتا ہے": ہی adds a slight emphasis ("Nalwa himself") that the English "who is credited" does not have; the frame and the following "accounts of who built it differ" are intact, so this is harmless. "ہری سنگھ نلوہ کے سر" would be exact.
(d) The Saka, para 1: پنجاب for "the province" (V3 fix): acceptable; the only province the article names is Punjab, and under British rule the province meant is the Punjab. No identification is added.
(e) The Gurdwara, para 2: سکھ مذہب for "the religion" (V3 fix): acceptable; the religion meant is plainly Sikhism.
