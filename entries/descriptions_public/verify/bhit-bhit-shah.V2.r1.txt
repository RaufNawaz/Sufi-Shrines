Rulings on the flagged points.
(1) Cotton spinners: not acceptable as drafted; see issue 1. A literal rendering is right in principle, but it must follow the English syntax, in which "the spinners" is the subject of "Carded", "spun" and "Accumulated". The proposed fix keeps the asyndeton before "Accumulated wealth" and leaves the main clause unsupplied, as the English does. Feminine کاتنے والیاں is fine given "O mother" and the Kapaiti context.
(2) Gender neutral wording: acceptable in all three. Karbala "میرا سر شرم سے جھکا ہے" carries "I put myself to shame" well. Malir and "One castle" drop the first person slightly ("I'd be living" becomes "there may be life"; "Wherever I look ... confronts me" becomes "wherever the gaze goes ... appears"). Not a fault, since "my corse" and the context hold the speaker, but if the writer wants the "I" back without marking gender, the subjunctive and dative do it: Malir "شاید موت میں بھی مجھے زندگی ملے،"; One castle "جدھر دیکھوں، محبوب ہی میرے سامنے ہے۔" Optional.
(3) Shah Inat's collection "شاہ لطیف کے کلام سے": acceptable. "Strikingly similar in scope" needs a comparand in Urdu, and the paragraph (he was not the first; Sindh already had such poetry) makes Shah Latif's own work the only sensible one. No new fact is introduced.
(4) Word choices: all acceptable. Falsetto as "باریک، زنانہ سی آواز" (Overview) and "زنانہ آواز کی نقل کرتے ہوئے باریک لے" (Devotion) is faithful; the Overview's "زنانہ سی" anticipates a fact the English states later and adds nothing new. سِنگی is the exact word for the Nath yogi's horn, and the feminine agreement "اس جیسی" follows it correctly. "مستند" درجہ with the quotation marks kept mirrors the English scare quotes around 'canonised'. "سندھی شاعری کی تین عظیم ہستیاں" is right; avoid تثلیث, which carries the Christian sense. "(سورۃ 89/17)" kept as the English has it is correct: Western digits, no Latin.
