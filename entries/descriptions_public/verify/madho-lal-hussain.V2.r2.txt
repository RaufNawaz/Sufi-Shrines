Rulings.
(a) "Red Fort in Lahore" as "لاہور کے شاہی قلعے کی اونچی سرخ فصیلوں": confirmed, and I withdraw my round 1 ruling (a). The English means the one Mughal fort of Lahore, and شاہی قلعہ is the Urdu name for that same fort, so it identifies nothing the English does not. Moving "red" from the fort's name to its walls adds no fact. "لاہور کے لال قلعے" would make an Urdu reader think of Delhi's Lal Qila or suspect a mistake.
(b) The milk and sugar image on the kissing: confirmed. The English attaches the simile to "kissing each other like milk and sugar", and the merging is a separate clause ("and the two friends becoming one"). V3's move of the simile to the merging would have changed the scene the spy reports. "وہ ... ایک دوسرے کو چومتے رہے" reads clearly as the pair, since the spy is singular throughout and ایک دوسرے needs two.
(c) "far and wide" on the festival crowd: confirmed. In the English, "drawn from far and wide" describes the crowds that swell to 100,000 to 125,000. The added clause "اور لوگ دور دور سے کھنچے چلے آتے ہیں" follows the swelling and refers to that crowd. V3's wording would have attached it to the daily visitors.
(d) The second charge as "کہا جاتا تھا کہ": confirmed. "Alleged" hedges only the zakat charge, and in the Urdu only that clause carries the hedge. The hajj ruling stays a plain statement, as in the English. The sense of an accusation comes from "دو الزاموں" in the lead sentence, so "کہا جاتا تھا" is enough and does not repeat الزام.
