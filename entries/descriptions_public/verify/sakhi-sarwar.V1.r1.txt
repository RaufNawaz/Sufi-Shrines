NOTES:
1. (precision) The Martyrdom, second paragraph: the father's burial place. "His father, Zain al-Abidin, lies with Sayyid Mahmud and Sayyid Sohrab in the family's burial place" comes straight after "The saint and his family were buried at the foot of the hills", so a reader takes "the family's burial place" to be the same place, and the father to lie at Nigaha. The team version says only that Chishti names "the burial place of the father's family" (by a name printed uncertainly) where Zain al-Abidin, Sayyid Mahmud and Sayyid Sohrab lie, a place of pilgrimage; it never puts it at the tomb, and Chishti's family sets out from a village of the same uncertain name. Moving the sentence to the family paragraph of The Saint removes the implied identification without naming the uncertain village.
OLD: There were two wives in his father's household, one of them the saint's mother, and the saint had a brother, Sayyid Abd al-Ghani.
NEW: There were two wives in his father's household, one of them the saint's mother, and the saint had a brother, Sayyid Abd al-Ghani. Zain al-Abidin lies with Sayyid Mahmud and Sayyid Sohrab in the burial place of his family, a place of pilgrimage for the people.
OLD: The saint and his family were buried at the foot of the hills, and the tomb became known as the tomb of the martyr. His father, Zain al-Abidin, lies with Sayyid Mahmud and Sayyid Sohrab in the family's burial place, a place of pilgrimage for the people.
NEW: The saint and his family were buried at the foot of the hills, and the tomb became known as the tomb of the martyr.

2. (precision) Judgement call 7, "soon after". The common ground of the three accounts is that the wife and son died with him (martyred among his dependants) or after him (the wife of grief at the separation, the orphaned son too; the widow who refused to remarry and was taken by the grave). None of them says "soon", a word the team version does not have; the widow's refusal to remarry, if anything, implies time passing.
OLD: His wife and young son died with him or soon after, and accounts of their deaths differ.
NEW: His wife and young son died too, with him or after him, and accounts of their deaths differ.

3. Rulings on the writer's judgement calls (accepted unless a note above says otherwise).
   - 1, "Multan and Shahkot": accepted. The site's slash names one region; "and" keeps it one region, while the writer's fallback ("or of Shahkot") would turn it into two alternatives the site does not offer.
   - 2, Pira and the headman's discipleship and mosque: accepted. Both books tell one episode (the headman of the village where the father settled gives him his daughter), and the team version itself sets Chishti's telling beside Lahori's with "likewise"; the village names that differ are both uncertain readings, and "also said" keeps Chishti's additions at the strength of a report. Not a merged chain.
   - 3, the masters: accepted. The Baghdad masters and the blessing from Bahauddin Zakariya do not exclude each other, no chain is built from either, and the order clause ("both the Chishti and the Suhrawardi Sufi orders") is the team version's own. See taste (c).
   - 4, the governor of Multan's daughter: accepted. The team version files her under the disagreement over the wife; "accounts of his marriage differ" covers her, and the legend cycle keeps the ruler of Multan offering a princess. Adding her beside Bibi Bai would set two wives side by side or join them, which the team version does not do. Not an important omission.
   - 5, the son's name, basis (d): accepted. One legible account each, no shrine position, and Bhandari's name is uncertain; giving both names in one clause is the categorical form of a range.
   - 6, "his jealous kinsmen": accepted. It covers the maternal cousins who grew jealous and "the jealous brothers" who rode after him, in the same account, without asserting either. The one sentence with "some ... others" for the killers is basis (d) with no legible tie broken by (a) to (c), and the Khokhar clause's "his brother" leaves Bhandari's uncertain name out, as the brother disagreement requires.
   - 7: raised as note 2.
   - 8, "The saint and his family were buried at the foot of the hills": accepted; Bhandari's "they" follows the deaths of the saint, his brother, his wife and his son, and "the tomb of the martyr" is the saint's.
   - 9, Chishti's martyrdom told as the devotees' tale in Legends: accepted. It is a legend kept as legend under rule 3, not a third answer in the narrative; "an old man" and "a number of the enemy" leave out the uncertain Khokhar and seventy two.
   - 10, keepers of the tomb and sweepers of the khanqah as different roles: accepted; the team version itself gives Shaikh Garha's people as sweepers of the *khanqah*, not as keepers of the tomb, and "Devotees also tell" frames Chishti's part.
   - 11, the bombing of 2011: accepted. The site's procession and Khalid's date, place and toll describe one event, as the team version's own bullet treats them, and nothing in either contradicts the other; joining compatible details of one event is synthesis, not a merged chain.
   - 12, the halt at the sarai: accepted. Only the noun for the company is marked uncertain, not that the company is Sakhi Sarwar's, not the night's halt and not the ten days, and the team version's Legacy gives "a halt for his pilgrims at a Lahore sarai" in its own voice, unflagged.
   - 13, the black blanket caravan in Devotion, "its people in black caps": accepted; it is placed where the team version places it, and both uncertain place names are gone. Its stop in Lahore rests on the team version's emendation of a printed "Laho", which the team version gives without doubt.
   - 14, the place twelve *kos* from Sialkot, unnamed and not tied to Dhaunkal: accepted; only the name is marked uncertain, and the team version expressly does not identify the two.
   - 15, 577 AH without an "accounts differ" clause: accepted. It is the only legible year; Chishti's year is an uncertain word in a damaged passage and Bhandari gives none, so no legible account differs, and no CE year is added.
   - 16, Bibi Ayesha as his mother, the index's daughter Aisha dropped: accepted. The team version treats the index line as a rival placing of the same name ("Aisha, mother or daughter"), against both notices that tell the birth; it rests on one index line and is not an important omission.
   - 17, the Lahore *chauki*: accepted; "not his tomb" and "no true place of the Hazrat's retreat here" are both the team version's, "it was said" frames the judgement of worldly desire, and the count of six owners is legible while their name is not.
   - 18, the princess and the archery contest: accepted; the uncertain answer, the two thousand banners, Kahrsi and the names of the khan and the Hazrat are all out, "a holy man" is as specific as "a Hazrat", and the gloss of *milni* is definitional.
   - 19, the lion and the *lagis*: accepted; see taste (a) on "the heap".
   - 20, the father alive in the legend: accepted; the legend cycle is framed ("the story goes on") and the narrative's "after his father's death" stays outside it.
   - 21, the region paragraph in The Shrine: accepted; it is introduced as about the hills of Baluchistan and the old *suba*, in the team version's own linking words ("the hills of Baluchistan at whose foot he places the tomb"), and says nothing about the shrine itself.
   - 22, "the emperor" Aurangzeb and Akbar: accepted as a plain description.
   - 23, "A genealogy traces the family back to Ali", without the book or the count of steps: accepted. The pedigree matches the team version name by name (23 names, Sayyid Ahmad to Ali, in order), so dropping the count loses nothing.
   - 24, Baisakhi: accepted; the paraphrase keeps the meaning, carries no quotation marks, and "In the early twenty first century" rests on the firm 2013 date.

5. Taste only (no change required).
   (a) "He killed a lion, and gave the heap away" gives "the heap" no antecedent in the public text; it is the team version's own wording, and the source does not say a heap of what, so it cannot be filled in.
   (b) *awliya* is glossed as "the friends of God" in The Saint and as "the saints" in Legends; one gloss would read more consistently.
   (c) "Accounts of his masters differ" heads two accounts that can stand together; "Accounts name different masters" would describe that more exactly.
   (d) Ali, at the head of the pedigree, goes unglossed for a reader with no background, as in the team version.
