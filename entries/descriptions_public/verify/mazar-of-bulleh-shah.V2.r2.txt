Rulings on the writer's departures from the fluency editor:
(a) کُشو kept apart from کُش: upheld. The English keeps Kush (Khalid's "Luv and Kush") and Kushu (Chishti's founder of Kashur) as two spellings from two sources. Merging them would state an identity that the English leaves to the reader.
(b) حضرت only where the English has "Hazrat": upheld. It now stands exactly twice, in the opening line and in Abida Parvin's list. شیخ before عبدالقادر جیلانی is the dictionary's own form ("Sheikh Abdul Qadir Gilani"), so it is not an added honorific. The sign ؐ after نبی is the customary formula.
(c) کڑی، راسخ العقیدہ طرز کی دینی تعلیم: upheld. It keeps "orthodox" (V3's روایتی would have weakened it to "traditional"), and طرز کی answers V3's point about collocation. Strictness now attaches to the training rather than to the orthodoxy, but the meaning is the same rigorous orthodox schooling. Optional: "کڑی اور راسخ العقیدہ طرز کی", without the comma in the English style.
(d) The kalma kept: upheld. Dropping it would be an omission (Shackle: "received the word (kalmā) of his Sufi teaching"). However, "سلوک کی تلقین پائی، اور اپنی صوفیانہ تعلیم کا کلمہ بھی" turns one thing received into two. I do not count this as an addition, because the bai'at and the khilafat already establish that he received instruction in the path from Shah Inayat. A single item is still closer to the English and keeps V3's تلقین: شاہ عنایت سے انہیں اپنی صوفیانہ تعلیم کے کلمے کی تلقین ملی۔
(e) Only Hashim Shah as the younger contemporary: upheld. The English is singular ("his own younger contemporary Hashim Shah of Amritsar and Waris Shah"), as is Schimmel in the team version. Abbas calls Waris Shah only "a contemporary". V3's plural would have added a fact.
(f) دریافت کر لیا گیا ہے: upheld. The perfect matches "an age that has rediscovered him". V3's progressive "کیا جا رہا ہے" would have turned a completed rediscovery into one still under way.
(g) صورت دیتے ہیں for "create": upheld. The myths are "of his lyrics" (Abbas: "create the female myths in their lyrics"), and the qawwals give them form in performance. V3's "زندہ کرتے ہیں" (revive) would imply that the myths had died.
(h) شام کی نماز: upheld, as in round 1. The English "the evening prayers" names no particular prayer, and مغرب would name one.
(i) خسرو کے کلام کی طرح: upheld. "Like Khusrau's" stands for Khusrau's mystical poetry, which کلام matches. گیتوں would narrow it, and V3's point (a poet compared with poetry) is met either way.
(j) جیون خان نامی ایک شخص: not upheld; see issue 1.
(k) "... میں عوامی بول چال کی زبان اپنانے کا بھی دخل ہے" for "has helped": upheld. "Has helped to ensure" names a contributing cause, and "کا دخل ہے" says exactly that. بھی only makes explicit that it is not the sole cause, which "helped" already implies. V3's "کی بدولت" would make it the cause.
(l) خواجہ سرا for hijras: upheld. The English glosses hijras as "eunuchs". خواجہ سرا is the classical Urdu word for a eunuch, and in current Pakistani usage (courts, press, the community's own organisations) it is also the standard respectful name for the hijra community. It therefore carries both senses of the English. ہیجڑا is now felt as pejorative in print.
