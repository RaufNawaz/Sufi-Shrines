Rulings on the writer's Points for V1:

1. Death year. Basis (a) applies, and 1266 is the right year to keep. The clause naming 664 AH and 1265 and "most accounts" is not allowed (issue 1). Keeping the fifth of Muharram only as the day of the ʿurs is right. So are the consistency changes: Badr al-Din Sulaiman "until 1281", "With his death began", and dropping the "fiftieth anniversary". None of them adds anything the team version does not support.

2. Kothewal for Kahtwal: accepted. The team version itself treats Kahtwal as a form of the birthplace's name (Lahori's annotation corrects Kahtwal to Kothewal, and the disagreement item lists Sharib's and Rizvi's Kahtwal under the name of his birthplace). This is a variant spelling, so it is minor.

3. Qazi Shuaib as grandfather, with the Ghaznavid appointment: accepted. The man and the appointment are the same in both accounts (both make Qazi Shuaib qazi of Kahtwal after Lahore and Kasur). Only his relation to Farid differs, and the text takes that from the site's grandfather with Sharib and the editor's sequence. This is not a merged chain.

4. Hansi: two compatible reasons, not variants. Rizvi gives fame as an obstacle to prayer. Lahori's majzub gives the crowds keeping the majzub from him. Both turn on the crowds in Delhi. Keep "Tradition adds".

5. The stay at Ajodhan without a range: accepted. The only figures are Eaton's sixteen or twenty four years and Rizvi's "from about 1236", and a span would have to be computed, which the brief forbids. "Accounts differ ... but it was his home for the rest of his life" is right.

6. Najib al-Din's nineteen visits: accepted. Rizvi's own sentence ends with Najib al-Din's death after the withheld prayer, which makes him the visitor, and the episode is framed ("it is told").

7. Khwaja Yaqub's saying: accepted as placed. The team version sets it among the sons, and the public text goes no further than the team version (it says "Khwaja Yaqub", not "his son").

8. Zakat parable: keep. The [OCR?] tag sits on the spelling of one word ("Sharia'a"), which the team version itself corrects to Shari'a, and the parallel Shari'a, Tariqa, Haqiqa fixes the sense. Nothing rests on the doubtful spelling.

9. Blame for 2001: acceptable as written. The two positions are what the parties to the event said (the authorities and the Diwan's family), not rival accounts by sources, and the sentence tells the dispute itself in one sentence.

10. The literature sentence: source talk and apparatus. Delete it (issue 2).

11. Renderings: accepted. The quotation marks and dashes are handled correctly, "[see note 6 above]" is gone, and credits appear only for Usborne, Qureshi and Abbas, whom the team version names as translators. No credit for Rizvi's doha senses, Khalid's qawwali lines or the Sher Khan verse.
