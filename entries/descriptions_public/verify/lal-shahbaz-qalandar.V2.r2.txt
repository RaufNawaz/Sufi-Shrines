Rulings on the writer's departures:
محمد شاہ تغلق and محمد بن تغلق kept apart: upheld. The English uses these two names in two places and never says they are one man. Unifying them would add an identification. For the content editor: historically they are very probably the same sultan, since Muhammad bin Tughluq died at Sonda near Thatta in 1351. If the team wants one name, the change belongs in the English first (V1), and the Urdu then follows.
The Ali clause, وہ خود حضرت علی کا اوتار نہ سہی، ان کے قائم مقام ضرور ہیں: upheld. The English speaks in its own voice here, so جن کے نزدیک would wrongly turn it into the Shiʿa view. "X نہ سہی، Y ضرور" is the idiomatic Urdu for "Y, if not X". It leaves the avatar claim open, as "if he is not" does. ضرور only marks the contrast with that open claim, and the English asserts "he stands in the place of Ali" without a hedge.
ہندوستان کے گجرات: upheld. The English spelling Gujarat is the Indian state; the Punjab city is Gujrat. Urdu writes both as گجرات, and after Rawalpindi the bare word would be read as the Punjab city. The qualifier therefore restores the English meaning and adds nothing.
مسلمان "ننگے" اور "اداسی" سادھو: upheld. The quotation marks make both words names, so ننگے does not read as "naked". سادھو governs both groups, as "sadhus" does in the English, and مسلمان has the same scope as "Muslim" there. V3's ننگے فقیر would have changed the term for one of the two groups.
The Pir of Pithoro sentence: upheld. پتھورو کے پیر کی زبان سے لعل شہباز قلندر کا نام ... آتا ہے keeps the Pir as the one who names him, as the English does.
