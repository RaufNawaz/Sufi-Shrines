NOTES:
- Naming: the article moves between "منگھو پیر" (for the shrine and the area) and "پیر منگھو" (for the saint, and in the last section also for the shrine). Both forms are in use, but keeping "پیر منگھو" for the saint and "منگھو پیر" for the place would read more consistently.
- The parenthesis "(حضرت حاجی سید سخی سلطان)" appears in full in both the introduction and the "صاحبِ مزار" section. The second time it could be shortened to "پیر منگھو کا مزار".
- "جلدی امراض" is ambiguous without a zer, since it can be read as "جلدی" (quick). Consider "جِلدی امراض" or "جلد کے امراض".
- "گجرات" can mean either the city in Punjab or the Indian state, and a Pakistani reader will default to the city. In this article context shows the state is meant.
- "پاکستان میں افریقی جڑیں رکھنے والی ثقافت" is a mild calque. The article itself later uses the more idiomatic "افریقی الاصل ثقافت", which could also be used here.
- "ہر جگہ سے شیدی اس میں شریک ہونے آتے ہیں" would read more naturally as "دور دور سے".
- "صدیوں کے دوران" (for "over the centuries") reads more naturally as "صدیوں میں".
- "ایک شخص نے صاحبِ مزار کو ... کہا" is vague and slightly informal for a reference article, but the source gives nothing more specific, so it is left as is.
- The introduction's list "ان تین چیزوں ... قرونِ وسطیٰ کے ایک صوفی بزرگ" counts a saint among "چیزیں". This is acceptable but slightly jarring.
- Hinglaj route: it is said to start at Karachi but is measured "نارائن پور سے". A reader who does not know Narayanpur is in Karachi may find this confusing. The article also switches between "سڑک" and "راستہ" for the same route.
- The Latin colon in the introduction is acceptable in Urdu typography. No Latin letters or em/en dashes were found. The asterisks are balanced (6), and the semicolon is the Urdu "؛".
