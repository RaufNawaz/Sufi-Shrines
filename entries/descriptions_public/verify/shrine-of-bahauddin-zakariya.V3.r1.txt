NOTES: Overall the prose is educated and largely idiomatic, and the hedges are carefully kept; the failures are mainly pronoun and name ambiguity and a run of calques. Taste points: "پنجاب کے روحانی نقشے کو صورت دینے والوں" reads translated ("روحانی منظر نامے کی تشکیل کرنے والوں" is smoother). "نبی کریم" appears throughout without ﷺ or صلی اللہ علیہ وسلم, which many Pakistani readers expect; a house style decision. "اساطیری شخصیت خواجہ خضر" may jar devout readers. "پاکستانی گویے" is slightly colloquial; "گلوکار" or "قوال" is more neutral. "روزانہ ہزاروں عقیدت مند اس مزار پر آتے تھے" sits in a present tense paragraph and leaves the reader unsure whether it is historical; check the source and either date it or put it in the present. Several figures appear unintroduced (نظام الدین in the qawwal story, presumably نظام الدین اولیا; شیخ ہود and "سلطان کے ہاتھوں"; the unnamed emperor in the Dara Shikoh story; "مدعی" and "مقدمے سے متعلق عورت" in the محضر story; "ایک بزرگ" on Eid night); naming them from the source would help. In "سلامتی کم کھانے میں ہے" the first member of the triad lacks the qualifier the other two have; check the source. Urdu works usually spell Hamid al-Din's nisba "سوالی", not "صوالی"; check. "کچھ ہی عرصہ بعد" is stricter as "عرصے بعد". "ہمسفر" is joined while "ہم عصر" is spaced. "یہیں" in narrative (lines on Kamal al-Din and on the return to Multan) assumes a reader standing in Multan; "اسی شہر میں" is neutral. "اس صورت میں ملتان کے اولے سو گنا بہتر ہیں" reads translated ("ایسے میں" is smoother). "دنگ رہ گئے" means astonished; if the source means scandalised, "برہم ہو گئے". "متین عبادت گزاری" is an unusual pairing. "اپنا خرقہ پہننے کو عطا کیا" is smoother as "اپنا خرقہ انہیں پہنایا". The Sadr al-Din Arif paragraph says twice that he was his father's murid and khalifa. "نعرۂ جنگ" would sit better as "نعرہ". The verse "آ رانجھے ... آؤ" mixes تو and تم address, acceptable in verse. Mixed plurals (صوفیہ and صوفیوں, شعرا and شاعروں) are acceptable. No Latin letters or dashes found; asterisks are balanced; the Persian line and the qawwal story's شیخ بہاء الدین are untouched.
