Rulings on the writer's flagged points:
1. بھتیجے: keep. The English and the team version say only "nephew", and nothing in the files settles which side of the family he was on. Any single Urdu word adds the same specificity, and بھتیجا is the usual default. No fault.
2. چنگ اور قانون: correct. چنگ is the harp and قانون the plucked zither.
3. ان کے خلاف محاذ کھول دیا: acceptable. The team version (Rizvi) makes the son's "attack" a campaign of censure that won the support of ulama, not violence, and محاذ کھولنا says exactly that. Keep.
4. اولیا کا شہر: faithful. مدینۃ الاولیاء is the established Urdu epithet for Multan, and you could use it in the Legacy sentence ("ملتان کی مدینۃ الاولیاء والی قدیم پہچان"), but that is a matter of taste.
5. عہدِ الست with "کیا میں تمہارا رب نہیں ہوں؟": correct. "it is said" governs the claim and "he said" governs the memory, as in the English.
6. اساطیری شخصیت خواجہ خضر: the meaning matches, since English "legendary" also marks a figure of legend. However, اساطیری leans towards "mythical", which some devout readers may take as calling Khizr a myth. Optional and softer: "داستانوں میں مذکور خواجہ خضر". Not a fault.
7. سائیں کے لیے تڑپتا ہے: correct and apt. سائیں is most likely the word that Shackle's "lord" renders, and it carries the sense of lord, master and beloved.
8. شیخ بہاء الدین: keep. The English deliberately uses the variant form "Shaikh Baha ad-Din" here, and the team version notes that the identification rests only on the name and on the singer coming from Multan. The Urdu mirrors this exactly: no حضرت, no زکریا and no added identification. "the Shaikh" and "the Shaikh's hospice" stay as ambiguous as in the English, which is right.
9. Hashimi gloss: keep the gloss and drop the Quraish gloss, as the writer did. One precision point: "جو خود نبی کریم کا گھرانا ہے" can read as اہلِ بیت, the Prophet's household, which is narrower than "clan" and would hint at Sayyid status. Prefer "یعنی بنو ہاشم سے، جس سے خود نبی کریم کا تعلق تھا".
10. Past tense "روزانہ ہزاروں عقیدت مند اس مزار پر آتے تھے": keep, because the English is past.
