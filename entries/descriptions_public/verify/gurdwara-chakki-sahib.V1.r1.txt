Recast as the appositive "two mutually hostile religions", it becomes the website's own timeless verdict on Islam and Hinduism: an author's characterisation moved into the narrator's voice. This is the same ruling and the same fix as Babay de Ber (V1 round 1, precision note 3). The rest of the sentence is supported and stays.

2. Rulings on the writer's judgement calls for V1. All are accepted. None needs a change beyond note 1.
   - 1, length: accepted. The four items added back are each supported (see 4 and 12 below; the second aarti rendering is verbatim). After note 1 the prose is 14,663 characters against 36,464 by the checker's own measure, 0.402, so the row stays at or above 0.40. The L1 exception does not apply, and no exception line should be written. Nothing should be added to pad it.
   - 2, prison or place of the millstone: accepted. The site's account ("marks the place where a great millstone ... was kept", "commemorates ... his imprisonment") and the historian's words ("used as a prison ... forced to work on a stone grinder") describe one place and one episode from two sides. Neither excludes the other, so rule 4 needs no clause. The historian's words stay in quotation marks as his, which keeps the link at the certainty the team version gives it ("the link to the story of the millstone rests on Qaiser's words there"). The fallback is not needed.
   - 3, the house and the gurdwara: accepted. The team version calls the house the one description of "the site itself" and puts it in its Gurdwara section. The public text never says in its own voice that the house is the gurdwara, and "the small house shown as the Guru's prison" in the Legacy keeps the identification at the historian's showing. The fallback is not needed.
   - 4, the antagonism and Jahangir: accepted. It follows "Accounts of their meeting differ", so it reads as reception, not as a second telling of the meeting. "the great grandfather of the emperor Jahangir" correctly inverts Khalid's "great grandson of Emperor Babur". "is remembered as having imprisoned" claims less than Khalid's own voice. Same as Rori Sahib point 5 and Bhai Lalo point 8.
   - 5, "Not every telling of the captivity includes the miracle.": accepted. It is the one clause for "How the millstone turned", resting on the historian's telling at the house (prison and forced labour, no miracle). The next sentence, on the Guru's own view of miracles, is his teaching rather than a rival account of the event. It is framed ("is held to") and contradicts nothing in the tradition as told.
   - 6, Babar-vani: accepted. It is the site's name, glossed, and the team version's own term in its Legacy. Harmonising with the sibling rows' Babur Bani is the coordinator's choice and not a fidelity matter.
   - 7, Visakhi for Baisakhi: accepted. It is the same festival in another spelling, minor under rule 4.
   - 8, "In the late 1990s" and "In 2017": accepted. 1998 is firm from the bibliography item, and the decade claims no more. "In 2017" is the team version's own "Visiting in 2017". The past tense for the survey's findings and the site's present for the pilgrims are both right.
   - 9, the third invasion: accepted. The team version itself files Rizvi's third invasion as one dating of the sack ("When Saidpur was sacked"), and Dhillon sets the return through Saidpur at Babur's third incursion. Told without its year, the invasion puts no second figure beside "around 1520 or 1521". The sack and the carrying off of the people are in the team version's own narrator voice. Leaving out the return from eastern India follows the Rori Sahib ruling.
   - 10, Ranjit Singh and the mahants: accepted. Placed with Gujranwala and apart from the fourteen ghumaon and the administration of 1922 to 1947, nothing ties the land grants or the mahants to Chakki Sahib, which matches the team version's "Neither passage names Chakki Sahib".
   - 11, Mardana: accepted. The team version has him as singer in the painting ("the songs of Bhai Mardana") and in Bhandari ("a singer named Mardana"). This is the Kartarpur and Bhai Lalo precedent.
   - 12, the Sufism of the Guru's Punjab: accepted. The team version introduces the passage as "the question of the Sufism the Guru knew". Rizvi answers McLeod's view of "the Panjabi sufism of Guru Nanak's period" by naming Gangohi, Jalal and the Qadiris, and that is exactly the claim that they belonged to it. "It has been argued" keeps it as an argument, and McLeod's rejected view is rightly gone. It is not obviously important, but it is supported, so keep it.
   - 13, the teaching questioned at three courts: accepted. It is framed, from Khalid's own voice outside the reconstruction, in the Bhai Lalo issue 2 wording.
   - 14, "it has been said": accepted. It keeps Khalid's judgement as a view.
   - 15, the poets of devotion: accepted. The clause after the colon is Abbas's statement and is supported. "an outcome of these movements" is rightly dropped, since the team version does not say which movements.
   - 16, Jhulelal and Bhakti: accepted. "revered by the Hindus of Sindh" identifies Jhulelal in the plain dictionary sense and says nothing about this site. "may have reached" holds Boivin's "seductive hypothesis ... could have permeated" at its own certainty.
   - 17, "the primary sources for his life": accepted. It is Khalid's description of the texts, and 1658 is from Dhillon's publisher's note.
   - 18, "To Pakistan's Sikh shrines in general": accepted. It keeps the festivals at the level of the province's shrines.
   - 19, eight headings: this is above the usual three to seven but breaks no rule, and it is not a fidelity matter.
