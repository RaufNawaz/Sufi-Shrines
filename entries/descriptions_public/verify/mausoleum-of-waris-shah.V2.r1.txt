NOTES:
Rulings on the writer's doubtful choices:
1. Baarat line in Urdu script: accepted. Latin is not allowed in the Urdu file, the English gives no meaning for the line, and none is added; the rebuild follows the romanisation word for word (Jevein جیویں، nigahein نگاہیں، raatan راتاں، thaman تھمن، dhol ڈھول، marde ماردے، rang رنگ، lawande لاوندے، ne نے). Only precision point: the romanisation has "log", which in Urdu script is لوگ; لوک is the Punjabi form of the same word. Not a change of meaning; optional: "جیویں لوگ نگاہیں تے راتاں تھمن ڈھول ماردے تے رنگ لاوندے نے" if the writer prefers to follow the English letter for letter.
2. "Yogi Balmath" as جوگی بالناتھ: not a changed name in substance. The figure is the jogi guru Ranjha goes to in the tale, whose name the Urdu tradition writes بالناتھ; "Balmath" is a variant spelling of the same name in the English rendering, and no other person is introduced. Accepted; the writer should record it in the notes (as for چوہڑکانہ, which the dictionary gives for "Chuharkana" and which renders the English "Chuhrkhana" correctly).
3. "Brother-in-law" as دیور: accepted. Urdu has no general word for brother-in-law, so any rendering must choose; دیور is the in-law of the women who speak the curse in the tale (Ranjha to his brothers' wives), so it narrows the English only in the direction the poem itself fixes, and no new relationship is created.
4. "My master" as مرشد: accepted. In a Sufi poet's own voice "master" is read as his spiritual guide, and استاد would narrow the other way; the Urdu does not name him, so the article's statement that his teacher and allegiance are disputed is untouched.
5. "Wonderful verses and designs" as عمدہ اشعار کہتے، نقش ترتیب دیتے: accepted. نقش in its musical sense is a fair rendering of "designs" here, and the habitual past (کہتے، ترتیب دیتے، گاتے تھے) fits "in Aurangzeb's reign". عمدہ is slightly milder than "wonderful"; optional: "دلکش اشعار".
6. "Erotic emotion" as شوریدہ عاشقانہ جذبہ and "erotic passion" as عشقِ مجازی: عشقِ مجازی accepted, it is exactly the Sufi concept the English means (human love as a path in spiritual life). شوریدہ adds a shade of frenzy the English does not state; optional precision: "ہیر رانجھا میں وہی پُرجوش عاشقانہ جذبہ انڈیلا" or simply "وہی عاشقانہ جذبہ انڈیلا". Not a fidelity fault.
7. "By the 1970s" and "By the early 1980s" as perfects: correct, they read as "by", not "until". See precision note b on the second clause of the 1970s sentence.
8. The fellow pupil sentence: matches. In the English "are said to" governs the whole claim (studied religion together under the same teacher), and کہا جاتا ہے کہ governs both ہم سبق تھے and ایک ہی استاد سے دینی تعلیم پائی. The preceding sentence (older contemporary, also took up Heer in Punjabi) is plain in English and plain in Urdu, so the frame is neither cut short nor widened.
9. The dropped prose paraphrase before the opening verse: not an omission. "Its opening lines set love at the foundation of the world" states nothing beyond the verse itself, and the Urdu meaning paragraph after the verse carries exactly that content (جس نے عشق کو دنیا کی اصل بنایا).
V1 round 3 changes: both rendered as the English now stands. The bait sentence gives bait as the four line stanza adopted in Punjabi Sufi poetry and Sultan Bahu using the same form in his ابیات, with no doubled words; the historian sentence has "the tale" as اس داستان کو.

Precision notes (optional, in this wording):
a. "The closing section of one of Bulleh Shah's lyrics": کافی names a genre the English leaves general (lyric). Replace "بلھے شاہ کی ایک کافی کا آخری حصہ" with "بلھے شاہ کے ایک کلام کا آخری حصہ".
b. "By the 1970s ... and a Punjabi would be deeply touched when the story was sung": the English time anchor governs both clauses, and the Urdu splits the second into its own sentence without it. The habitual past keeps it in the same period, so this is not a fault; to tie it explicitly, replace "یہ قصہ گایا جاتا تو سننے والے پنجابی کے دل پر گہرا اثر ہوتا تھا۔" with "اور جب یہ قصہ گایا جاتا تو سننے والے پنجابی کا دل بھر آتا تھا۔" joined to the previous sentence (replace the full stop after "دی تھی" with "،").
c. "Two more of his asides": برجستہ adds "witty, spontaneous". Replace "ان کے دو اور برجستہ فقرے یہ ہیں:" with "ان کے دو اور جملے یہ ہیں:".
d. "the line of the saint Makhdoom of Kasur": "the saint" is carried only by the title. Optional: "اپنی ارادت قصور کے بزرگ مخدوم کے سلسلے سے جوڑتے تھے" if the writer wants it explicit; مخدومِ قصور alone is acceptable.
e. "Poets, you should determine the worth of any poem": تم ہی adds "you alone". Optional: "شاعرو، کسی بھی نظم کی قدر تم طے کرو۔"

Style (for V3, not fidelity): "اس قبر کا احترام" in the overview comes two sentences after the poem, not the tomb; "وارث شاہ کی قبر کا احترام" would make the referent plain.
