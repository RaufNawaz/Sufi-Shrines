NOTES:
Rulings on the writer's choices:
1. "became" in the past tense (ایک ہندو مندر کے پہلو میں واقع ان کا مزار مسلم ملتان کی علامت بن گیا): keep. The English is past, and بن گیا is a plain past. V3's بن چکا ہے would turn it into a claim about the present.
2. The قلندر gloss: keep. The brief names قلندر among the terms to gloss. This is the word's first use in the article, and the gloss does not stand between a noun and its postposition.
3. نواب in the singular: keep. "The local Nawab" is one man, and نوابوں would make him several. The oblique singular pairs correctly with "دوسرے اہم عہدے داروں", which keeps the English sense of "other important officials".
4. جس نے اس کے زہر کا منتر سیکھ لیا ہو: faithful. In Urdu, a منتر for snake venom is the counter charm, so "to overcome" is carried. "اس کے" points to the serpent, and "venom" is kept, which V3's سانپ کا منتر would have dropped.
5. "بہاء الدین ملتانی" in quotation marks, with آستانے: keep. The marks show the English's variant form as the verse's own wording and add no fact. آستانہ is exactly "threshold".
6. Multan attached to the death: correct. The English reads "He died at Multan in 1267". The burial at Multan stays as its own sentence.
7. The Saadi sentence: faithful. It keeps "twice", the invitation sent to Shiraz (no suggestion that he was brought), the khanqah, the endowment of villages, and his age as the reason he could not come.
8. دعا کی درخواست for "intercession": correct. Asking a living saint to intercede for a son means asking for his prayer, while شفاعت carries the sense of the Day of Judgement. Nothing is lost.
9. Dropping the Five Pirs gloss from Poetry: nothing is lost. The Overview carries the same gloss in full ("five great saints", "believed", "together" for "collectively", "special powers"), and Poetry still says یہ پانچ پیر ہیں and names all five.
10. فرید گنج شکر: keep. Shakarganj and Ganjshakr are one epithet in inverted order. "Of Pakpattan" identifies him, and the notes record no withheld identification, unlike Surkh and Surkhposh. It is also the standard Urdu form.
11. سوالی: correct. It is the usual Urdu spelling of the nisba.
12. سخت ناگوار گزرا for "shocked": keep. "Shocked ... and they complained" means scandalised, and سخت matches the strength of the word. The recast sentence keeps every fact: one ghazal, local taverns, harp and zither, and their learning of it.
13. حضرت بہاؤالدین at the head of six paragraphs: each names the right man, since in each case the English "his" or "he" is Bahauddin. The six are ملتان میں حضرت بہاؤالدین کی خانقاہ (after the mahzar), حضرت بہاؤالدین کی دولت (after Lal Shahbaz), خود حضرت بہاؤالدین کے اقوال (after Hamid ad-Din and the ulama), حضرت بہاؤالدین کے گہرے دوست (after Nizam ad-Din), حضرت بہاؤالدین کی ایک ہی کتاب (after Naharwani) and حضرت بہاؤالدین کے سات بیٹے (after Sakhi Sarwar). The V3 additions are also right: Udero Lal's master, and the two اولاد sentences in Family.
14. "Other lands" in Shaikh Jamali's journey: faithful. The journey starts at the Haramain, runs through Egypt, Baghdad and other lands, and has Khurasan as its farthest point. دوسرے ملکوں keeps the open "other lands", which V3's wording had dropped.
15. Surkh and Surkhposh: the Urdu now merges them. See issues 1 to 3, which restore سرخ بخاری in the three mentions of the khalifa. "جلال سرخ" is unaffected. Two other names are rightly left alone: "جلال الدین کے مزار" at Uch and "اوچ کے جلال الدین مخدوم جہانیاں". V3 should not flag the restored سرخ بخاری as an inconsistent spelling, because it mirrors a distinction the English keeps on purpose.
16. V3's requests that were not applied: all three refusals are confirmed.
   a. Maulana Hussam al-Din: the English gives him no further name and no "another". Writing ایک اور would add a statement that he is a different man, and adding ترمذی would add an identification. The Urdu is exactly as open as the English, and the English spelling difference between Husam and Hussam cannot be shown in Urdu script.
   b. "Jalaludin's shrine": the English leaves it unidentified, so naming Surkhposh or Makhdum Jahaniyan would add an identification.
   c. "Umar": the English and the notes (decision 15) leave him unidentified. Making him Shihab al-Din Umar Suhrawardi, or adding حضرت, which would point readers to the caliph, would add an identification. Keep the bare عمر.

a. Referent: in "شیخ جلال الدین تبریزی نیشاپور تک ان کے ہم سفر رہے", the two pronouns just before it (ان کا قلندرانہ لباس، انہیں) point to Abd al-Quddus, so ان reads as the qalandar. The next paragraph then opens "بغداد سے ملتان آ کر انہوں نے" straight after a sentence whose subject is Tabrizi. The English has the same surface ambiguity, but the brief asks for ان to be resolved: "شیخ جلال الدین تبریزی نیشاپور تک حضرت بہاؤالدین کے ہم سفر رہے" and "بغداد سے ملتان آ کر حضرت بہاؤالدین نے اسی شہر میں مستقل سکونت اختیار کی۔" Recommended, together with the fix to issues 1 to 3.
b. The death paragraph opens "انہوں نے عزت و وقار کے ساتھ" straight after a paragraph ending on جیلانی. The year 1267 settles it, but "حضرت بہاؤالدین نے عزت و وقار کے ساتھ طویل زندگی گزار کر" removes the pause.
c. "سہروردی سلسلہ اپنے معمولات میں کم سخت تھا" comes one sentence after "شریعت کی سختی سے پابندی", so it can read as "less strict about the law". The English "less austere" means less ascetic: "سہروردی سلسلہ اپنے معمولات میں کم زاہدانہ تھا".
d. In "یہاں روزانہ نماز، قوالی اور لنگر کا وہ معمول", روزانہ can be read as governing all three, which makes the qawwali daily. In the English, "daily" goes with prayer only: "یہاں روزانہ کی نماز، قوالی اور لنگر کا وہ معمول جاری رہتا ہے".
e. "مگر آخری برسوں میں یہ معمول روزانہ کا نہ رہا" presupposes that his early fasting was daily, which "fasted much" does not say. Closer: "مگر آخری برسوں میں وہ روزانہ روزہ نہیں رکھتے تھے۔"
f. "دوسرے صوفیہ ان پر الزام تراشی کرنے لگے": الزام تراشی leans towards baseless mudslinging, a shade that the neutral "target of accusations" lacks. Closer: "حضرت بہاؤالدین کی دولت اس قدر تھی کہ وہ دوسرے صوفیہ کے الزامات کا نشانہ بننے لگے۔"
g. "سہروردی طریقے کی جڑیں برصغیر میں مضبوط کیں": "to root" means to make it take root, while مضبوط کیں presupposes roots that were already there. Closer: "سہروردی طریقے کی جڑیں برصغیر میں جما دیں".
h. "1870 کی دہائی تک ... بس چکا تھا اور مریدوں کے نذرانوں پر گزر اوقات کرتا تھا": the second verb is a plain past governed by تک, which the brief warns can read as "until". Closer: "1870 کی دہائی تک ان کا خاندان لاہور اور گاؤں ڈھولن وال میں بس چکا تھا، جہاں وہ مریدوں کے نذرانوں پر گزر اوقات کرتا تھا؛".
