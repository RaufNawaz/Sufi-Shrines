## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Birth:** "born at Uch on 15 Sha'ban 707 AH ... which fell on 9 February 1308", basis (a) (the site's 1308) with (b) (Sharib and Rizvi give 15 Sha'ban 707; Rizvi and Boivin 1308; Bhandari's night of Barat in 707 does not contradict it). Left out: Lahori's night of Saturday, 4 Shaban (a variant day, minor); Rozehnal's 1303, which belongs to a Sayyid Jalal ad-Din Bukhari the team version does not identify with this saint.
- **His father:** "Accounts of the saint's father differ, but he is most often named as this Sayyid Ahmad Kabir", basis (b) (Lahori, Sharib, Rizvi against Bhandari). Left out: Bhandari's genealogy, Shaykh Jalal son of Sayyid Mahmud son of Sayyid Jalal Bukhari, and its khalifa of "his own revered father".
- **His father's death:** left out altogether: the footnote's "after 713 AH" is a reading the team version marks uncertain (rule 5), and the appendix's "about 731 AH" is the editor's own conjecture.
- **Death year:** "died on 10 Zil-Hijja 785 AH, the day of Eid al-Adha ... which fell on 3 February 1384; accounts of the year of his death differ", basis (a) (the site's 1384) with (b) (Lahori, Rizvi, Bhandari, Boivin's 1308 to 84). No rival year is named. Left out: Sharib's 788 AH and its "after some days' illness", Boivin's other "(d. 1383)", Schimmel's 1383 and her index's 1382 to 83, Rozehnal's 1383 for Bibi Jawindi's husband, Bhandari's Wednesday.
- **Where he lies:** "Most accounts place his grave at Uch", basis (a) and (b) (the site, Lahori, Rizvi, Bhandari). Left out: the Usborne annotator's "also buried at Multan" and "of Multan", Boivin's "a saint of Multan", Rozehnal's "master of Multan" (and in the same passage his Baha ad-Din Zakaria "of Uch Sharif").
- **The meaning of his titles:** the site's "the Master of the World" (a) and "the World Traveller" (the site's and Rizvi's rendering; the site's hyphen in "World-Traveller" dropped, Rizvi's form has none). Left out as minor variants of wording: Rizvi's "Lord of the Mortals", Schimmel's "whom all the inhabitants of the world serve", the Jahan-gard of Lahori's heading.
- **How he came by his titles:** *Jahangasht* from his travels (the site, Rizvi); the conferral of *Makhdoom Jahaniyan* at tombs on a night of Eid kept as tradition in Legends. These concern two different titles and do not compete.
- **His travels:** the site's account, basis (a) ("journeyed across the Muslim world, performed the Hajj many times and visited the great centres of learning and sanctity of his age"), with Lahori's "hundreds of guiding saints", and the clause "though accounts differ over how often and how far he travelled". Left out: Rizvi's doubt ("doubtful", "mostly legendary and all of a later date"), the three journeys abroad, the *Safar Nama*, Jamali's "more than three hundred". Sharib's six years in Medina and Mecca and return in 752 AH are kept as compatible with it, and so is Rizvi's "It seems likely" that he lived abroad for most of Muhammad bin Tughluq's reign.
- **His teacher at Mecca:** Shaikh Afif al-Din Abdullah Matri, Shaikh al-Islam at Mecca, basis (c) (Lahori's notice of 1875 is the earliest account devoted to the saint; Sharib's chapter is of 1965). Left out: the city labels of Sharib's footnote ("the shaykh of Medina, Hazrat Abdullah al-Matri", "the shaykh of Mecca, Imam Abdullah Yafi'i"); Yafi'i is kept in the list of those from whom the khirqa came, without a city, and Matri is not repeated there.
- **His orders:** a Suhrawardi with a Chishti initiation at Delhi after his return, basis (b) (the site's Suhrawardi discipline; Rizvi, Sharib, Bhandari, Rozehnal, Ernst and Lawrence for the Chishti tie; Lahori's four stages are silent on Nasir al-Din, not contrary). Rizvi's initiation and Sharib's further khirqa of khilafat from Nasir al-Din "on his return" are joined in one sentence, as two descriptions of the one event (see Points for V1). Sharib's fourteen *khanwadas* kept as "is said".
- **The *Siraj al-Hidaya*:** compiled by Makhdumzada Abdullah (Rizvi), the only account that names a compiler. Left out: the editor's own manuscript "listed under the name of Taj al-Din Ahmad Barbani", an archive description whose name for the saint the team version marks uncertain.
- **The peace of Thatta:** basis (d): "Accounts of its year differ, placing it between 1362 and 1367" (Rizvi's march of 763/1362; Boivin's siege of 1367). Left out: Boivin's "a saint of Multan" for the peacemaker.
- **Bibi Jawindi:** basis (a): "who is held to be his descendant, though accounts of her relation to him differ". Left out: Rozehnal's "wife of ... Sayyid Jalal ad-Din Bukhari (1303 to 1383)", a man the team version does not identify with this saint.
- **His successor and the keepers:** his brother Raju Qattal as successor, and the *sajjada* passing to his brother and not to his son (Rizvi; Lahori's book), kept beside the site's "kept by his descendants" (a), as two compatible facts: the immediate succession and the present keeping (see Points for V1).
- ***Sama'*:** basis (d): "Whether he listened to *sama'* ... is not settled" (Sharib records both and chooses neither).
- **The relics in the romance:** his gift to Ranjha is a dagger, basis (b) (two passages of three: "Sayyid Jalal a dagger" and "the dagger given him by Makhdum Jahanian"). Left out: the lion fight's "cudgel of Jahanian" and "dagger of Sayyid Jalal Bokhari".

- **The first Qadiri at Uch:** Rozehnal's Shaykh Muhammad al-Husseini al-Jilani, founder of the first Qadiri khanqah, and his son Abd al-Qadir Jilani Thani (d. 1533). Left out: Lahori's Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Uchi Jili Gilani, "the first to come to the Punjab", by Khurasan and Multan, and the editor's 887 AH: a second telling of the same arrival or a rival "first", and the team version does not identify the two men.
- **Simnani:** Sharib's Ashraf Jahangir Simnani, who came to Uch and drew on his grace (the only one of the three that names him in full). Left out: Rizvi's "lived for a period" and Ernst and Lawrence's route after Transoxiana, so that one figure is not built from three sources.

## Time anchors
