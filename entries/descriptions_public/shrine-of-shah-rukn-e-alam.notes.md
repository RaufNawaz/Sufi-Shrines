## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Birth year:** "born in Multan in 1251 ...; accounts of the year of his birth differ". Basis (a), the site's 1251; Sharib's 649 AH falls in the same year, but no conversion is stated. Left out: the Arabic 647 AH quoted by Lahori's editor (and the editor's own Urdu figure, uncertain in print). Sharib's Friday, 9 Ramadan kept in its own "He is said to have been born" sentence, joined to no Hijri year; no source gives another day.
- **Death:** "He died in 1335." Basis (a), the site's 1335, with Boivin; Rizvi's 735 AH, given as 1334 to 1335, agrees. The Hijri year 735 is common to every source. "Accounts of the day differ; it is most often given as the sixteenth of Rajab, in the year 735 of the Hijri calendar", basis (b) (Lahori's text and Sharib). Left out: the night of Friday, 9 Jumada al-Awwal 735 AH in the Arabic quoted by Lahori's editor.
- **Father:** Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif, son of Bahauddin Zakariya, stated without a clause: every clean reading agrees (the site, Lahori, Rizvi, Sharib's narrative). Left out: Sharib's rubric giving the father as Rukn al-Din (uncertain in print, rule 5).
- **Whose disciple he was:** basis (c) after V1 round 1 (Sharib, the only account devoted to him that names his master): "He was his father's disciple, though accounts of his master differ". Left out: Chishti's statement. Superseded first draft: basis (d), "Accounts differ over whether he was the disciple of his father or of his grandfather" (Sharib: his father's *murid*; Chishti: Bahauddin Zakariya's disciple). Left out: Sharib's "chief *khalifa*" of his father (it is part of Sharib's side), Chishti's chain with a Rukn-e-Alam between Nasir al-Din and Sadr al-Din Arif (damaged reading).
- **Whose successor he was:** not a choice but a composite of compatible statements: the site's "grandson and spiritual heir" (a, with Boivin's "grandson and successor") and the succession to his father as *sajjada nashin* (Sharib, Lahori, Rizvi), joined as the editor of Lahori's book himself joins them (fifty two years on his grandfather's *sajjada*, after his father). No clause.
- **The *khirqa* of his accession:** Sharib's "*khirqa* of Shaikh Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi, which had come to him from his father". Rizvi's "Shaikh Sadru'd-Din's khirqa" says the same thing (it came from the father) and its key word carries [OCR?]; nothing of Rizvi's is used.
- **The prophecy before his birth:** one telling, Lahori's (rising against the custom of the elders; a sun at the height of sainthood and a moon of nobility), basis (c), the earliest account devoted to him. Left out: Sharib's wording (lamp of the family, candle of the lineage) and his "seven months in the womb", which belong to the other telling. (The Bahauddin article uses Sharib's wording; this team version gives both, and only one is kept.)
- **His brother's name:** Imad al-Din Ismail, basis (b) (Sharib; the *Siyar al-Auliya* in Rizvi's footnote). Left out: Rizvi's text form (uncertain in print), the footnote's long form and its remark on the title.
- **His brother's end:** "In a battle ... Imad al-Din was killed; accounts of the brother's fate differ" (Rizvi; "end" changed to "fate" after V1 round 1), basis (b): of the four accounts of the succession, three (Lahori, Jamali and Ibn Battuta as Rizvi reports them) do not make the brother the successor, so Sharib's brother as *sajjada nashin* is the minority and is left out. The battle is not identified with Kishlu Khan's revolt or with Abuhar.
- **Who succeeded him:** "After his death his seat passed to his brother's family, though accounts differ over who took it." Basis (b): Sharib (his brother), Lahori (Muhammad Ismail, of his brother's offspring) and Jamali via Rizvi (his nephew, Shaikh Isma'il), against Ibn Battuta via Rizvi (his grandson, Shaikh Hud). No successor is named, because no one name has most of the sources (the nephew has two of four, and Lahori's and Jamali's men are not identified with each other in the team version). Left out: the whole Ibn Battuta account (Hud's nomination, the nephew's challenge, Muhammad bin Tughluq's verdict, the governor of Sind's charge, the seizure of the property, Hud's ruin and execution), because it rests on a grandson that the established position does not have (see next line).
- **Whether he had descendants:** "Rukn-ud-Din himself had no children", basis (b) (Lahori, Sharib) against Ibn Battuta's "his grandson".
- **His welcome by Ala al-Din (minor):** "the sultan himself came out to receive him", the common ground of both tellings. Left out: Sharib's "at the third gate" and Rizvi's "rode back with him to the city".
- **Nizam al-Din's gift (minor):** Sharib's telling (Iqbal, bolts of silk, a hundred *ashrafis* in a fine cloth), ending "they were given instead to his brother, Imad al-Din Ismail"; who handed the gift to the brother is dropped silently. Left out: Rizvi's telling of the same gift.
- **Who received him first (one episode, two tellings):** Sharib's wording ("the best man of the city, meaning Nizam al-Din"). Left out: Rizvi's wording ("the leading figure amongst the élite", "Unhesitatingly").
- **The massacre at Multan:** the team version's own "the saint is remembered as having saved the city", told as Barani gives it (he went to the sultan soon after he learned of the intention, asked for clemency, and the sultan relented), with "Accounts of what followed differ". Basis (b): Barani, and Isami as quoted by Lahori's editor (he went to the king to intercede), against Isami as Rizvi reports him (seven days of meditation while the massacre went on). Left out: Isami's seven days, Rizvi's "apocryphal", the editor's "barefoot" (a detail of the other telling), Bhandari's version (its Shaikh Rukn al-Din is not identified with the saint).
- **The fall of the pavilion:** Rizvi's account, with "Accounts of the episode differ" for Bhandari's version (the shaikh urging the sultan by signs), which the team version does not attach to this saint with certainty. Left out: Bhandari's telling and its "some write".
- **His writings:** basis (d), "accounts differ over whether any of them survives" (Rizvi: none survives; Lahori's editor: a manuscript of his *malfuzat* exists). Left out: Shaikh Abd al-Haqq, the manuscript and its owner's library, the titles of the *khalifas'* works.
- **His *khalifas*:** the five named in Lahori's book, by its editor (Maulana Zahir al-Din, Sultan Hamid al-Din Hakim, Shaikh Fakhr al-Din Iraqi, Makhdum Jahaniyan Jahangasht Bukhari, Shaikh Sama al-Din Dehlvi), as "Among his *khalifas* ... though accounts of the list differ", with no count. Basis (c), the list printed with the earliest notice devoted to him, and the only whole and clean list (Sharib announces six, names five, and the fifth name is uncertain in print); the same list stands in the Makhdoom Jahaniyan article. Left out: Sharib's list (Shaikh Usman Sayyah, Haji Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif, Haji Chiragh Din, Shaikh Hamid al-Din called Sultan al-Tarikin) and his Shaikh Qawam al-Din. Usman Sayyah is kept as a disciple, from Rizvi's own account of him, and is not called a *khalifa*.
- **His order:** Suhrawardi, with no clause: Sharib names no order (silence, not a rival position), and the one statement of his that bears on an order is the Suhrawardi *khirqa*.
- **Nizam al-Din's illness:** Sharib's visit from Multan, with the funeral prayer, kept. Rizvi's tenth of Zu'lhijja visit "to gain from him the blessings of a hajj" left out as possibly a second telling of the same visit (Points for V1, no. 7).
- **The Sindh birth legend:** kept as a legend about a legend: in some tellings he, not his grandfather, is its Sufi master (Boivin). This describes the legend's tellings; it sets no rival beside an established fact about Rukn-e-Alam.

## Time anchors

- Lahori, 1875 (firm): "By the 1870s his tomb was a place of pilgrimage for the people"; "In 1875 a book on the saints of the Punjab gave him a notice of his own" (the Shah Jamal pilot's form, in Legacy).
- Bhandari (bibliography: composed in regnal year 40 of Aurangzeb): "In Aurangzeb's reign it was counted among the ancient cities". No decade given.
- Rizvi's dated events: "In 1327 or 1328" (his range of 1327 to 1328, written without a hyphen) and "In 1333" (Ibn Battuta at Multan).
- Dhillon and Khalid: "By the time Guru Nanak ... came to Multan" and "It was then" (their own anchor, Nanak's time).
- Sharib (preface 1965, edition 1999): "in the twentieth century it drew high and low alike" (anchor added after V1 round 1); his material is otherwise the saint's life.
- Abbas, 2002: the musicians in the family shrines of the Syeds, past tense, no anchor; "It stands next to the shrine of his grandfather" in the present, a fact of place.
- Ernst and Lawrence, 2002: "Multan was a strategic commercial centre", past tense.
- Schimmel, 1982: the poets' Multan "inside the heart", past tense.
- Chishti (1867, the date unconfirmed): only for Ghazi Beg Tughluq's rise, an event with its own time; no anchor from the book.
- The site's account (current): present tense for the tomb, its awards, the devotion, the *qawwali*, the *langar* and the *ʿurs*.
