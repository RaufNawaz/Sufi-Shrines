## Consensus choices

The seventeen disagreements of the team version, in its order:

- **Day and year of death:** Thursday, at the forenoon hour, 8 Muharram 1040 AH, 8 August 1630. Basis (a) and (b): the survey, Lahori, his editor and Kanhaiya Lal (year only) agree. The one rival, Chishti's 6 Muharram 1041, rests on two numbers marked [OCR?], so rule 5 removes it, and with it the only recorded difference; no "accounts differ" clause (judgement call 1). The weekday and the Christian date agree (8 August 1630 falls on a Thursday; checked only for consistency, not added to the text).
- **Age at death:** 54, basis (a), "the age the shrine keeps is 54, in keeping with his birth in 1576", with the clause "Accounts of his age at death differ". Lahori's fifty six not named; Chishti's figure is unreadable.
- **Where he came from:** born in Lahore, basis (a), with "though accounts of where he came from differ". Kanhaiya Lal's Sirhind dropped. Lahori's "came to Lahore at the Mujaddid's direction to guide the people" is given as "It was at the Mujaddid's direction that he set about guiding the people of Lahore", so that no second origin stands beside the shrine's.
- **Whose time he belongs to:** not a conflict on the team version's own facts. He settled at the site in the time of Jahangir (Kanhaiya Lal, p. 85) and died in the reign of Shah Jahan (Chishti, p. 188; Kanhaiya Lal, p. 292, places him in the Shah Jahan era); both fit a life of 1576 to 1630 and are given as a sequence. No clause.
- **His master, and who named him Bandagi:** Hazrat Shah Sikandar, son of Pir Kamal Kaithali, basis (a) and (b): the survey, Lahori and Adam Banuri's own account (through the editor) against Kanhaiya Lal's Shah Kamal (Kaithali [OCR?]); clause "Accounts of his master differ, but Shah Sikandar is the master most often named". The name: Shah Sikandar, basis (a); Chishti's Lal Kamal Shah of Kaithal rests on two uncertain names and is dropped, so no clause for the name. The line Pir Kamal Kaithali, Shah Sikandar, Tahir is one chain that Lahori and Adam Banuri both give; Kanhaiya Lal's Shah Kamal is not hung under it.
- **His Sufi order:** "He is counted a Qadiri" (every source) and "Most often he is counted a Naqshbandi as well, ... in its Mujaddidi line", basis (a) and (b): the shrine's name in the survey bibliography item (Qadri Naqshbandi Mujaddidi), Lahori's heading and appendix row, Kanhaiya Lal p. 85 and p. 292, the editor's *khalifa* of the Mujaddid, Chishti's *bai'at*. Chishti's three orders kept as a further statement ("He is also said to have taken *bai'at* ... in the Qadiri, Naqshbandi and Suhrawardi orders"), since it adds the Suhrawardi without denying the others; "all four" ([OCR?]) dropped. Lahori's four forms of the order not listed.
- **The name Bandagi:** minor; the index and contents forms and Kanhaiya Lal's "Zahir Bandagi" dropped silently. The text uses the row's name and gives "Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori" as another name.
- **His wives:** Mah-e-Khanam and Ismatullah, daughter of an Afghan king, basis (a). Chishti's names and parentage are all marked uncertain, so the only rival rests wholly on uncertain readings; no clause.
- **His fall and return:** the survey's account as his life (basis a), then "It is told in more than one way, and one telling makes it a prophecy fulfilled", with the compatible details of Chishti's telling framed as that telling ("it is said"): the Mujaddid's foretelling that one of those present would become a *kafir*, Sirhind, the *qashqa*, sitting in a temple having become a Hindu, the Mujaddid's prayer answered and Shaikh Tahir's repentance. Left out of that telling: the sons' protest invoking the right a teacher holds (it needs him to have taught them already, against the survey's "later"), the "moon-browed beauty" and the *zunnar* ([OCR?]), and the couplet. Precedent: Gurdwara Babay de Ber (compatible details of another telling added, framed; the rival climax dropped). Judgement call 2.
- **The promise of Paradise:** the shrine's vision during the *Taraweeh* (basis a) as the legend; Lahori's report of the saint's own words (he had asked of God that whoever was buried within the enclosure of his tomb should be of Paradise) added after it as a compatible saying, framed "He himself, it is said, used to say". Judgement call 3.
- **The raid on the books:** the survey's raid in his lifetime (basis a; Kanhaiya Lal alone is against it), with "though accounts differ over when it happened". Kanhaiya Lal's plunder after his death (thousands of Qur'ans, the attempt to sell them, the burning of the quarter) dropped. His lead in ("the quarter stayed settled until the end of Muslim rule") and his 1880s state (the quarter in ruins, the tomb standing, some old fabric of the madrasa) kept as statements about the quarter, without the plunder (judgement call 13).
- **How the graveyard began:** the survey's (it grew up around his tomb as generation after generation asked to be buried near him), basis (a), with the clause "Accounts of how the graveyard began differ" in Legacy. Kanhaiya Lal (the people of Lahore made the place a graveyard after the quarter's ruin) and Chishti (Miani was ruined and the graveyard came to be; the Sikhs looted Miani) dropped. Basis (a) is kept because the team version does not show every other source against it: those two speak of when the ground became a graveyard, not of whether it grew around his tomb, and Kanhaiya Lal's own description makes his enclosure the first and largest of the graveyard. Judgement call 4.
- **Where he lived:** at Miani (survey, Kanhaiya Lal); Chishti's walled city quarter rests on a damaged passage (rule 5); no clause.
- **Why the place is called Panj Pir Dhera:** minor; Kanhaiya Lal's five graves that stood there before the village (its point legible; his gloss word *dhera* [OCR?] not used); Chishti's five elders' coffins, brought by a Pir whose name is uncertain, and "Panj Dher" ([OCR?]) dropped silently.
- **The date of the *ʿurs*:** 7th, 8th and 9th of Muharram, basis (a). Chishti's legible "first ten days of Muharram" kept as a compatible nineteenth century fact; his date word dropped.
- **The custodians' line:** told as a sequence in time, not as alternatives. In the nineteenth century the office was disputed (Chishti) and its holders included Sayyid Mansur (Chishti) and Sayyid Shahsawar (Lahori); Shahsawar's Gilani genealogy given, basis (c) (Lahori, 1875, the earlier account), with "Accounts of the family's line differ" for the editor's correction; "Later the office was held by Pir Sher Husain Tahiri" (the editor), "Darsi" ([OCR?]) dropped; today the Gaddi Nasheen (survey). Chishti's Shah Sawar of the descendants of Abd al-Qadir Thani and the Sahsu- reading dropped ([OCR?]).
- **The chronogram word:** *gham*, grief (legible); the variant *'am* (uncertain) dropped as minor.

Time anchors: Lahori 1875 and Kanhaiya Lal 1884 are firm ("By the 1880s", "In the 1880s"); Chishti's material only "in the nineteenth century"; the editor's undated modern edition only "Later" (the custodian) or "it is said" (the rebuilding); the survey is today.

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: *ʿurs* (the yearly commemoration of his death); *murid* (the pledged disciple); the Qadiri order (founded by Abd al-Qadir Gilani); the Naqshbandi order (founded by Baha-ud-Din Naqshband); *qutb* (the axis or pole of the saints and the highest spiritual station); *silsila* (the chain of spiritual descent from master to disciple); *bai'at* (the oath of initiation into a Sufi order); *karamat* (miraculous deeds and spiritual gifts); *zikr*, the glossary's *dhikr* (the remembrance of God); *faqir* (a mystic who has embraced spiritual poverty); *khanqah* (the lodge where a Sufi's disciples gather, the Baba Shah Kamal pilot's wording); *langar* (the free communal kitchen).

Team version's own: *qashqa* (the forehead mark); *kafir* (an unbeliever); *kutub khana* (a library); *khadims*; *Gaddi Nasheen* (its present custodians); *wali-e-kamil* ("perfect saint"); *istimdad* (seeking his help); *ulama* and *fuzala*; *maulvi* (a learned and accomplished man); *miana*; *gorkans* (the gravediggers); *gham* (grief); *pakka* (fired brick, as in the Baba Shah Kamal pilot).

Plain dictionary senses: *madrasa* (religious school); *kanals* (a local measure of land); Sufi (Muslim mystic); Mujaddid Alf Sani (honoured as the renewer of the faith for Islam's second millennium); Muharram (the first month of the Islamic year); AH (in the Islamic calendar); *khalifa* (an authorised deputy); *shariah* (the religious law); *qutbiyat* (the rank of *qutb*); Mujaddidi (named after the Mujaddid); *kashf* (insight into hidden things); hadith (the sayings of the Prophet); *tafsir* (commentary on the Qur'an); *darbar* (court); *nisbat-e jazba* (the spiritual bond of rapture); *khadims* (servants); *fiqh* (Islamic law); *suluk* (the Sufi path); *chahar-diwari* (walled enclosure, from the sentence); *sajjada nashin* (the custodian who sits on the saint's prayer carpet, the Shah Jamal pilot's wording without "hereditary", since this office was disputed); *Fatiha* (the opening chapter of the Qur'an); *Mehfil-e-Naat* (gatherings for the recitation of *naat*, poems in praise of the Prophet); jinn (unseen spirits); *dhol* (a large barrel drum) and *dhamal* (ecstatic devotional dancing to the beat of the drum), the pilots' glosses; *Taraweeh* (the night prayers of Ramadan, the month of fasting); chronogram (a verse whose letters, read as numbers, give a year).

## Judgement calls for V1

1. Death date given plainly, "on a Thursday, at the forenoon hour, on 8 Muharram 1040 AH in the Islamic calendar, which is 8 August 1630", with no "accounts differ" clause, because the only rival date (Chishti's 6 Muharram 1041) rests on readings marked [OCR?]. If V1 holds that the recorded disagreement still needs a clause, the fix is "Accounts of the day and year differ; the date the shrine keeps is 8 Muharram 1040 AH ...".
2. The second telling of his fall ("It is told in more than one way, and one telling makes it a prophecy fulfilled. The Mujaddid, it is said, had foretold ... until the Mujaddid prayed, his prayer was answered and Shaikh Tahir repented."). Compatible details only; the sons' protest and its teacher's right left out. Fallback: keep only "It is told in more than one way." and delete the rest of that telling.
3. "He himself, it is said, used to say that he had asked of God that whoever was buried within the enclosure of his tomb after his death should be of Paradise." Lahori's version of the promise, added beside the shrine's vision as a compatible saying. Fallback: replace it with "The promise is told in more than one way."
4. Graveyard origin on basis (a), with the clause in Legacy. If V1 judges Kanhaiya Lal and Chishti to be "every other source against it", basis (b) would give the ruin first: "After the quarter fell to ruin, the people of Lahore made the place a graveyard", and the Overview's sentence would need the clause instead.
5. Chishti's couplet left out (see Verse).
6. "and later taught the Mujaddid's own children, among them Shaikh Muhammad Masum": the name is Chishti's (legible; his second son's name is not), set in the survey's sequence. The two accounts differ on when he taught them, not on whom. Fallback: delete "among them Shaikh Muhammad Masum".
7. "The present tomb was raised by a Gilani pir" for "Pir [illegible] Gilani": only the given name is lost. Fallback: "The tomb has been rebuilt, and it is said that when the old tomb was excavated ...", with no builder.
8. "Pir Sher Husain Tahiri" with "Darsi" ([OCR?]) dropped from the middle of the name; the *nisba* Tahiri and its source in the saint are the team version's own words.
9. Sayyid Shahsawar anchored to "the nineteenth century" (the genealogy ends with him in an 1875 book); the word "now" in that sentence is marked uncertain, so the text does not say he held the office in 1875.

11. "The Mujaddid is said to have conferred on him the *qutbiyat* of Lahore": Chishti's sentence has one illegible word, but the claim reads whole without it ("قطبیت لاہور کی … حضرت مجدد سے اونکو عطا ہوئی"). Fallback: delete the sentence.
12. "the shrine's full name, Darbar Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri Naqshbandi Mujaddidi" rests on the survey bibliography item's title (the team version: "its survey bibliography names the shrine Qadri Naqshbandi Mujaddidi").
