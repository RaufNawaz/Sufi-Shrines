## Consensus choices

- **His name and identity:** "There is no settled view of the saint's name; the shrine knows him as Shah Shams-ud-Din Sabzwari, of Sabzwar." Basis (a), the shrine's own account (the current site text). The other names are not given as variants of the saint's name but as what they are in the team version: the Shams al-Din Tabrizi counted among Multan's saints in the reign of Aurangzeb, with the team version's own caution kept ("the name and the city are the only link"); the tomb of a Shah Shams beside which Nawab Shahnawaz Khan was buried; and the Indian tradition of Shams-i Tabriz in Multan, with the closing sentence "None of these traditions identifies its Shams with Shah Shams Sabzwari", which is the team version's own statement ("None of these sources equates the figure it speaks of with Shah Shams Sabzwari"). No identification is made in either direction.
- **Shams al-Din Tabrizi placed in Rum with Maulana Rumi** (the same Persian history, elsewhere): dropped as a variant under rule 4. Listing it beside the Multan Shams al-Din would be the side by side material the brief removes, and the team version says the book does not settle whether the two are one man.
- **His death:** "He died in Multan in the thirteenth century." Basis (a), the shrine's account; no source gives a year. The flaying tradition belongs to Shams-i Tabriz and is told only as that tradition, in The Shams of Multan, never as the death of this saint.
- **His dates:** thirteenth century, basis (a); no source contradicts it. The absence of a year is not stated (an absence of record, rule 6).
- **Spelling variants dropped silently (minor):** Qarmatians / Carmathians (Carmathians kept); Sind / Sindh (Sindh kept); Ismaʿili / Ismaili (Ismaili kept, the site text's form); "mollas" rendered "mullahs" in a paraphrase, not a quotation; Maulana Rum / Maulana Rumi (Rumi kept); Shams Tabriz / Shams Tabrizi (each kept where its sentence comes from).
- **Time anchors:** the Khulasat material anchored to "the reign of the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb" (its bibliography item: composed regnal year 40 of Aurangzeb, firm). Chishti's material (bibliography date unconfirmed) anchored only to "the nineteenth century".
