Source: `entries/descriptions/bibi-pak-daman.md` (team version) only; glosses from `data/glossary.csv` or plain dictionary senses. Brief: `docs/prompts/PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md`. Why the ratio is low: about two fifths of this team version is a second identity for the six women and its retellings ("The Other Tradition", "The Long Debate", "Where the Sources Disagree", and the author by author variant tellings), which rule 4 removes, and much of the rest of the older book material rests on readings marked uncertain. The first draft (0.41) reached the old floor of 0.40 only by telling the second identity in a section of its own; V1 round 1 ruled that side by side, and the coordinator agreed that Rauf's ruling 3 comes before length. ## Consensus choices

- **Who the six women were (the identity question).** Established position: the Karbala tradition, women of the Prophet's household led by Sayyida Ruqayya bint Ali. Basis (a), with (b) in support: the shrine's traditional founding date of about 63 AH, its own devotional identity (Syed and Shia) and Muharram as its greatest season all follow it, and the current site text calls it "the older and by far the more popular tradition, the one held by most pilgrims"; in the nineteenth century it was "the common tradition on people's tongues". The text says that accounts differ (over who they were, and over when and from what country they came to Lahore) and names the second tradition once, with the current site text's own words that "the shrine holds meaning in both". The basis is not unanimous within (a): the team version says the second tradition is "advanced by the shrine's own custodial family", and the public text says so in the same sentence rather than hide it. V1 round 1 passed this framing: it claims popularity, not historical proof.
- **The second tradition (V1 round 1 ruling, rule 4 over rule 7 and L1).** The first draft gave it a section of its own, "The Saint of Chilla Bibiyan". V1 ruled that in substance the section told the rejected identity at full length beside the established one: a second parentage and a second set of marriages for the six, a line of saints descended from one of them, and a second full telling of the central miracle that differs on the century, the enemy, the keepers and who raised the graves (the team version's "Whose daughters", "Whether any married", "Who threatened the women", "The year of the Mongol sack" and "The first keepers" disagreements, laid out as two sections instead of two bullets). Rule 7 does not save the named relatives, because they are the Bibis' family only under the rejected identity, so rule 4 governs them; and length is not a reason to keep side by side material. The section is gone. What remains, in "The Shrine", is one paragraph on the neighbouring tomb, in V1's wording: who lies there (named once as the saint the second tradition makes the father of the six), his death in 602 AH, 1205 CE, and the chronogram of his title, the fate of the tomb under Ranjit Singh, the stable and the later custodial family's property, followed by the verse chronogram and its rendering, unchanged.
- **Chief of the six:** Sayyida Ruqayya bint Ali, daughter of Hazrat Ali, sister of Hazrat Abbas, wife of Muslim ibn Aqeel (basis a). She is not identified with Bibi Haj, because that identification rests on readings the team version marks uncertain. The variants that make all six (or five) daughters of Aqil (Lahori's first account, Kanhaiya Lal's *mujawirs*, Chishti's five) are not listed.
- **How many:** six (the site text, Kanhaiya Lal, Abbas, Lahori's heading and his father's entry). Lahori's two counts of five dropped as minor.
- **Names:** Bibi Haj, Taj, Noor, Hoor, Gauhar, Shahbaz, the site text's spellings; all agree, and the text states the names as common ground (V1: not a variant). "Shahnaz" dropped as a minor variant; "Milch" and "Shahr Banu" are uncertain readings.
- **Founding date:** about 63 AH, given as the shrine's traditional date (basis a). Kanhaiya Lal's "never proved" is carried by the sentence that accounts differ over when and from what country they came.
- **The journey:** the site text's telling (a year's captivity at Yazid's court, sent away by Zain al-Abideen with Basheer), basis (a). Lahori's Syria telling, Chishti's departure from Medina before Karbala and the *mujawirs'* departure from Mecca are not listed. Details from Chishti's telling that contradict nothing in the shrine's version are kept as tradition: the halt on a mound, the raja's people's alarm, the women's lament, the four *hafiz* (Abu al-Fath, Abu al-Fazl, Abu al-Makarim, Sad Allah), the convert's revelation about his marriage, the land given to his descendants. The prayer keeps the shrine's wording only ("begging God to spare them exposure to strangers"); the joined wording "to command the earth to give them refuge" was taken out on V1's note 4.
- **The courtiers sent by the raja (V1 round 1, issue 2).** The sentence "Another telling has the raja first send men of his court ..." is deleted. It was a second version of the same conversion episode, placed straight after the shrine's and introduced as a variant; the variant is minor, so the shrine's telling stays and the variant goes silently, and its one distinct point (that others converted through the women) is already carried by "many people are said to have come to Islam through the blessing of their footsteps". The Shah Jamal "Another version" precedent does not apply: there the other version was a separate and larger legend told at the shrine itself (the saint burying a whole city under his foot), not a rival sequence of the same episode. Folding the courtiers into the shrine's sequence instead would merge two accounts.
- **The raja's name:** Mahabrand Bhatti, "named in local tradition" (basis a). The other names are uncertain readings.
- **The convert:** the raja's son, Raja Kumar Bikrama Sahay, renamed Abdullah Shah Khaki or Shaikh Jamal (basis a). Chishti's raja's daughter converted by a glance is not given (her name is an uncertain reading and she is the variant in the team version's list). His refusal to return to his old faith is framed as legend ("is said to have", V1 issue 5).
- **Who travelled with them:** Basheer (basis a). Abbas's reported seven hundred male scholars and Chishti's company of 704 (uncertain) are not given: adding a large company to the shrine's small, secret party would merge two accounts.
- **The first keepers:** the *mujawirs* descend from the convert prince (basis a; Lahori's first account agrees that the convert sat as the tomb's attendant). The nineteenth century sentence no longer says the shrine's attendants told the Karbala story (V1 issue 3), since beside "advanced by the shrine's own custodial family" it set the keepers' disagreement side by side.
- **Sayyid Ahmad's tomb:** death 602 AH, 1205 CE (Lahori and his editor agree; Kanhaiya Lal's figure is uncertain); the quarter Chilla Bibiyan (site text; Chehel Bibiyan dropped as a variant). The quarter's naming from his daughters' retreats left with the section (rule 4).
- **Where the shrine lies:** today "in the heart of old Lahore" (site text); in the 1880s counted among the sites outside the city, two miles from Lahore. Told as the state at each time, not as a conflict (V1: passes). The 1974 location (Muhammad Nagar) kept (V1 note 5).
- **The plan of the graves:** today two adjoining enclosures of three Bibis each (the site text, "tradition places"); the 1880s three enclosures given as the 1880s state, which agrees with it (the outer enclosure held the maidservants), now with the third enclosure's names (Bibi Hoor, Bibi Gauhar, Bibi Shahbaz, clean in the team version; V1 note 8); the second enclosure stays unnamed because its first name is an uncertain reading. Chishti's marble platform and lower platform given without the names he is uncertain of.
- **The maidservants:** Bibi Halima, Bibi Tanoori (site text); the 1880s outer enclosure of maidservants' graves and Chishti's two unnamed maidservants kept as further facts.
- **The domed tomb:** eleventh century of the Hijra, about 1600 CE (site text); death 1016 AH (Lahori) and building 1017 AH (Kanhaiya Lal) both kept, since they do not conflict.
- **The mosque:** first built by Baba Khaki and rebuilt some years before Chishti wrote (Chishti); not merged with the 1880s domed mosque or today's mosque. Baba Khaki is not identified with Abdullah Shah Khaki, which the team version does not do.
- **The fabric:** Mahmud of Ghazni and Akbar given as nineteenth century attributions, waqf from successive rulers and the 2024 renovation from the site text. Kanhaiya Lal's absence of any endowment not given.
- **Legacy:** "However the question of who they were is answered, the meaning of the shrine is the same" (V1 note 3), replacing a sentence that set the two identities out a second time as equal options.
- **Time anchors:** Lahori (1875) and Kanhaiya Lal (1884) are firm: "in the 1880s", "by the 1880s", "in the nineteenth century". Chishti (1867, unconfirmed) only "in the nineteenth century" or "in that century". Khalid (2013) "in the early twenty first century". 1974 from the editor's table as the team version dates it.

## Left out

- **Under rule 4, with the section "The Saint of Chilla Bibiyan" (V1 round 1, issue 1):** the quarter's naming from his daughters' retreats; his Junaidi line and his spiritual descent to Zain al-Abideen; his standing as *Qutb* and *Ghaus*; his settling in Lahore in the sixth century of the Hijra; Tirmiz and Kech Makran and the sign from the unseen; the Tokhta story; his hereditary nobility and his descendants across the Punjab; the marriages of Bibi Haj to Baha al-Din son of Sultan Qutb al-Din Qureshi Hakari and of Bibi Taj to Shah Zaid; the four unmarried daughters, "the Rabia of their age"; the Suhrawardi line through Bibi Haj and Sultan Hamiduddin Hakim; and the whole Mongol telling (Jalaluddin Khwarazm Shah, the river of Sindh, the siege of two months, the massacre, the miracle, the converts who served the tomb).
- **Under rule 4, other variants:** the courtiers sent by the raja (V1 issue 2); Lahori's first account (daughters of Aqil in Syria, flight from the Umayyads, head coverings marking the graves); Chishti's departure from Medina, the raja's daughter converted by a glance, the 704 men, the seven companions sent home, the six or seven thousand converts; the *mujawirs'* account in Kanhaiya Lal (daughters of Aqil from Mecca); Abbas's seven hundred scholars; the joined prayer wording (V1 note 4).
- **Source apparatus:** every citation; "Where the Sources Disagree"; "The Long Debate" (Umm al-Banin's sons, the shrines of Ruqayya bint Ali in Damascus and Egypt, the night of Ashura, Lahore outside the Muslim world in 61 AH, Muhammad bin Qasim, the Persian names Gauhar and Shahbaz, and the defenders' replies); the editor's verdicts and his blame of named writers; Nami's role and his tract's dates; the editor's further reading list; Nasawi's silence; Lahori's own hedges on the Mongol account; Kanhaiya Lal's preference; "told by the shrine's attendants and set down in writing as well" (V1 issue 3); the description of what each book is and how many pages it gives. The shrine's keepers' closing words are kept, as the keepers' own.
- **Uncertain readings** (rule 5): Bibi Milch, Bibi Shahr Banu, Lasao, Mahabaran, Bikramajit, Abd al-Qadir, Muhammad Jamal, the word *kunwar*, Ruqayya as Bibi Haj, Gauhar and Shahbaz on Chishti's lower platform, Amat al-Fatima Taj, Bibi Nur Hi, the twelve black stones, Sayyid Nur's grave, the grant with four wells, the names of Khan Bahadur, Yaqub Khan and Mian Shaikh Raza, Bura Mianwa, Azim Shah's seal, the "eastern" enclosure, the direction of the two miles, the place west of which the four *hafiz* lie, Ghulam Muhiuddin Shah Pirzada Rata, the *Tazkira-e Hakimiya*, Takhna, Chehel Bibiyan, Chishti's damaged passages.
- **Book apparatus turned into nothing:** Kanhaiya Lal's table of contents (only the plural name "the Mazars of Bibi Pak Daman" kept), Lahori's words for the precinct, the appendix rows, the arithmetic of 258 years, the broken clause linking Ghulam Muhammad's chronogram to his burial (the chronogram and the burial are kept separately), Abbas's fieldwork note and her index under *silsilas*, Kasmani's index and his report about Murad.
- **Absences:** that the older books record no *ʿurs*, fair, offering or building date, and that Kanhaiya Lal records no custodian, endowment or income.
- **Other:** Rajab Ali Khan's garden (the team version says only that it "may be" this shrine); Abbas's general passage on women musicians' *hazri* (not about this shrine); Rizvi's dating of Jalaluddin's defeat and his Iltutmish context (they belonged only to the Mongol telling); "across more than a thousand years of retelling" (it presupposes the Karbala dating); "curious" and "precious".
