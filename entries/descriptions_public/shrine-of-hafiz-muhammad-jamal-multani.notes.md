## Consensus choices

The Hadiqat editor's 1226 AH is the same year in the Hijri reckoning, not a rival year, so it is given beside it ("Hafiz Jamal died in 1811, the Hijri year 1226") and no "accounts differ" clause is used: no source gives a different year. The 1226/1811 of the *Sufi Martyrs* bibliography belongs to an unequated "Jamal Multani" and is not used as evidence.
- **Birth:** around 1747 (1160 AH), the site's account; no rival.
- **His name:** Hafiz Muhammad Jamal Multani, short form Hafiz Jamal, basis (a), the site's account, as Ernst and Lawrence give it in their narrative. The Hadiqat heading's second form Khwaja Muhammad Jamal, the index form Muhammad Jamal Multani and the *Sufi Martyrs* bibliography's Jamal al-Din Multani and Jamal Multani are dropped: minor variants, and the last two belong to a man that book does not equate with the saint.
- **His master's name:** Khwaja Noor Muhammad Maharvi, basis (a). Nur Muhammad Maharvi and Nur Muhammad are spelling variants, dropped silently; the nisba printed Majheel[OCR?] in the Taunsvi entry is left out by rule 5.
- **The Chishti order in Multan:** "established the Chishti order firmly in Multan" (Overview, The Chishti Order in Multan, Legacy), basis (a), the site's account, agreeing with the Hadiqat editor's "flourished thanks to him" (given as "it is held"). The *Sufi Martyrs* bibliography's attribution of *Anwar-i jamaliyya* to Abd al-Aziz Parharawi (and its pairing of the title with Ghulam Husayn Multani) concerns a "Jamal al-Din Multani" whom that book does not equate with the saint; it is dropped silently, as a minor attribution (rule 4, third bullet) and to avoid merging two accounts (rule 4, fourth bullet).
- **Bahauddin Zakariya's death year:** Eaton's 1263 and Schimmel's 1267 differ; minor for this row, so neither year is given and he is simply the great Suhrawardi saint buried in the city.
- **Spellings:** Khwaja Sulaiman Taunsvi (Hadiqat editor) throughout, for Ernst and Lawrence's Sulayman Taunsawi; Bahauddin Zakariya (the site's account) throughout, for Baha' al-Haqq Zakariya, Baha'uddin Zakariya and Baha al-Din Zakariya. Minor, dropped silently.

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: *ʿurs* (the yearly commemoration of the saint's death); *silsila* (the spiritual lineage handed down from master to disciple); *qawwali* (the devotional music of the Sufis), from which *qawwal* is "a singer of *qawwali*"; the Suhrawardi order (the Sufi order founded by Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi); the Chishti order ("a Sufi order prominent in South Asia"; the glossary's founder is not named, so that a second form of his name does not stand beside Khwaja Mu'in al-Din Hasan Sijzi); shaikhs ("its spiritual masters", the glossary's master or teacher).

The team version's own: *sahib-e qurb*, "those of nearness"; the *Siharfi* as "an acrostic poem of the Punjabi and Siraiki tradition" (the team's dash written as "and"); Siraiki placed by the Siraiki belt around Multan, Bahawalpur, Mianwali and northern Sind.

Plain dictionary senses: Sufi (Muslim mystic, as in shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal); the Hijri calendar (the Islamic calendar reckoned from the Prophet's migration to Medina); *khalifas* (the deputies a Sufi master appoints to carry on his line); *malfuzat* (the collected sayings of a Sufi master); *kafis* (short sung poems); the *Siharfi*'s acrostic form (verses that open with the letters of the alphabet in turn); chronogram (a verse whose letters encode a year, as in shah-jamal); Hind (India); Nawabs (Muslim princes).

Not glossed: the title Hafiz. The glossary defines it as one who has memorised the whole Qur'an; glossing it would read as a statement about the saint that the team version does not make.
