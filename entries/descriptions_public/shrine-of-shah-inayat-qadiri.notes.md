## Consensus choices

- **Death year: around 1728**, "Accounts of the year of his death differ; the year the shrine keeps is around 1728." Basis (a): the current account (the site text) gives around 1728, and Shackle's "d. 1728" agrees, so not every other source is against it. Variants not listed: Lahori's 1141 AH (printed uncertainly, rule 5 in any case); the editor's argument from Haji Muhammad Sharif Qasuri's letter sealed 1148 AH that he died after 1150 AH / 1737 CE (figures printed uncertainly), and the same dating in the title of the editor's life of the saint (p. 307). Khaled Ahmad's "from Aurangzeb to Mohammad Shah" is kept as a view; it does not name a year.
- **His master: Shah Raza Qadri Shattari of Lahore**, "Accounts of his master differ; the one most often named is Shah Raza Qadri Shattari of Lahore". Basis (a) and (b): the field survey's Hazrat Shah Raza Qadri Shattari, Lahori's Shah Raza Qadri Lahori (of whom he was a *khalifa*), and the editor's Shah Raza Qadri Shattari Lahori (after 1111 AH; d. 1118 AH / 1707 CE). No chain is built: the editor's exoteric teachers and inner guides are kept as his teachers, in a separate sentence, and nothing is hung under Shah Raza.
- **His order:** "He combined the Qadiri and Shattari orders in his own practice, and his full name carries both, though he is most often counted simply a Qadiri". Basis (a) for the combination (the current account, with Lahori's list and the editor's full name), (b) for "most often a Qadiri" (Shackle's notes and glossary, Rafat's book three times, Sharib's nisba). - **Where he came from: Kasur**, "Accounts of his origins differ, but most tie him to Kasur". ## Glosses

The Qadiri order is glossed with its founder, but by the team version's own name for him, Sheikh Abdul Qadir of Baghdad, rather than the glossary's Abd al-Qadir Gilani, because the team version's sentence on his Hanbali creed follows and two names for one founder would confuse the reader (judgement call 13).

Plain dictionary senses: imam (the leader of the prayers); *madrasa* (a religious school); *khalifas* (deputies); the Mujaddid Alf-e Sani (the renewer of the second millennium); the titles Sheikh-ul-Mashaikh (master of masters), Shah-e-Lahore (king of Lahore), Mufakkir-e-Pakistan (thinker of Pakistan); *taḳhat* (throne); *kafi* (a short mystical lyric); the *khirqa* of succession (the robe that marks a master's successor); Mehfil-e-Milad (a gathering in celebration of the Prophet's birth); the Auqaf Department (the government department of religious endowments); qawwals (the singers of *qawwali*); the signature verse (the verse that carries the poet's name).

## Judgement calls for V1

1. **Death year** "around 1728" with the clause, placed at the head of The Shrine (the team version's Family places it there too). The editor's later dating is dropped as a rule 4 variant and for its uncertain figures.
2. **"the one most often named is Shah Raza Qadri Shattari of Lahore"**: three naming sources (field survey, Lahori, the editor) against three single others (Muhammad Ali Raza Shattari, Shah Zia Qadri, Mian Mir's seat). "most often" is a plurality, not a majority of all sources; the fallback is "the master usually named".
3. **"though he is most often counted simply a Qadiri"**: Qadiri appears in every source but Shackle's introduction; "simply" marks those that give only Qadiri.
4. **"Accounts of his origins differ, but most tie him to Kasur"**, with "born around 1643 at Kasur" from the current account. The only rival is Abida Parvin's Uch.
5. **"the ruler's ill intent towards Shah Inayat's wife"**: the team version says "the ill intent of the ruler toward his wife"; read as Shah Inayat's wife, since the tradition explains friction between the ruler and Shah Inayat. If V1 holds this unsupported, write "towards the saint's wife" or drop the clause.
6. **The referral** told as one tradition from two accounts that agree (see consensus choices). "another master" for Abd al-Hakim rests on "declined to take Bulleh Shah as his disciple".
7. **The break paragraph** joins three accounts that agree on the cause: Khaled Ahmad (the master dismayed by the rough vernacular, his advice, "Bulleh Shah did not listen"), the introduction's Aurangzeb context ("it has been argued"), and Abbas (too outspoken against the priestly class, criticism of gender, class and caste, ridicule of institutionalized religion, "afraid of the political forces of the time", exclusion from his spiritual company). The order advice, refusal, exclusion is the narrative order; no source says the exclusion followed the refusal in so many words. "After several years, master and disciple parted" is Abbas's "after several years of association ... excluded".
8. **"He himself, as one writer on Bulleh Shah put it, worked under 'the careful camouflage of sophisticated Persian metaphor'"**: the team version's subject is "the saint practised *haqiqat*", and *haqiqat* rests on 'bagigat' [OCR?]; "worked" stands in for it so that nothing rests on the flagged word.
9. **The legends of the separation:** Abbas's "He knew that Shah Inayat was fond of music" as a plain sentence; Khaled Ahmad's strand (turned out of the house, the *sarangi*, the Qadiri custom of *samaʿ* and devotional dance, taken back when the teacher saw that the heterodoxy had a divine source, "The reunion was dramatic") told continuously as "It is said ... the story continues"; Shackle's "most popular tale" (the dancing girl) after it as a separate tale, so the dance is not made the cause of the reunion and the expulsion is not tied to the dance. Khaled Ahmad's "after this" is not given an antecedent.
10. **"One of his lyrics casts the master as Ranjha of the romance of Hir"**: from Shackle's note to a lyric ("makes the master a figure from the romance of Heer", and the Ranjha comparison it quotes).
11. **"he is remembered as 'of Kasur, then of Lahore'"**: the phrase is from the title of a life of Shah Inayat Qadiri in the edition's book list (p. 295); see For Rauf.
12. **"he is also called Shah Inayat Allah Qadiri and Inayat Shah"**: Sharib's form, and the form of Rafat's poems, the editor's appendix and Abida Parvin.
13. **The Qadiri founder** named "Sheikh Abdul Qadir of Baghdad" and "the order's Hanbali founder" from Khaled Ahmad's sentence, not the glossary's Abd al-Qadir Gilani. "an openness that one writer has called ironical".
14. **"In the 1870s his *mazar*, the tomb of a saint, was spoken of as radiant"**: Lahori's "his radiant *mazar* is in Lahore", anchored by his firm 1875.
15. **The Overview's second paragraph** summarises later sections: "served as imam of a mosque near Bhati Gate, founded a school of his own" (the *madrasa* is not placed near Bhati Gate); "the young Syed Bulleh Shah" (Sharib's "the young man"); "across the lines of caste"; "in joy, in longing and in the pain of separation" (Shackle's "present source of his joy ... absent cause of his pain" and the "train of suffering caused by separation").
16. **"Of six compositions that one party of qawwals ... sang at the shrine of Bulleh Shah, five were narratives ..."**: for "the qawwals whom Abbas recorded sang six compositions".
17. **"A life of Shah Inayat was being completed in 1974, and Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi prepared a *Hayat-e Shah Inayat Qadri*"**: "prepared" for "lists ... of his own as in press"; the two are not said to be one book.
18. **The Hafiz sentence** sets the title and the memorising "by the age of five" side by side without making one the cause of the other.
19. **"His life spanned, it has been said, the Mughal rule from Aurangzeb to Mohammad Shah"** stands near "born around 1643", which falls before Aurangzeb's reign; kept as Khaled Ahmad's view, as the team version gives it.
20. **"In the *Dastur-ul-Amal*, it has been argued, he pointed to a Hindu origin ..."** (Dr. Lajwanti's claim, reported in Rafat's introduction) and "he is said to have believed in the Hindu origin of Islamic mysticism, a knowledge carried, in his view, by the Greek soldiers of Alexander the Great and borrowed from Greece by the mystics of Islam" (the note's quotation from Ramakrishna). Both framed as views; neither scholar named.
21. **"Shah Inayat is the key to his disciple's poetry."** The team version's "Shackle makes the master the key to the disciple's poetry", in the narrator's voice but followed at once by the attributed quotation ("As one translator of Bulleh Shah has put it").
22. **"The separation lives on in Bulleh Shah's songs."** A connective resting on the two readings that follow (*Teray Ishq nachaya*; the line read as symbolising the gloom of the master's departure). "a line of one famous *kafi*" is not said to be the same poem or a different one.
23. **"Another poem closes on a profession of the mystical identity between the disciple and the master standing directly before him."** Shackle's note (n. 7 on 106), set after "He is the poet's faith and his very being".
24. **"It is said that he withdrew his disciple's *wilayat*, his sainthood, and later restored it, as though it were a grant that could be revoked."** The last clause is the team version's own comment, kept; Sharib's "names no document, seal or witness" is dropped.
