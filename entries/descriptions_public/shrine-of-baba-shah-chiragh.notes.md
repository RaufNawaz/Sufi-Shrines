## Consensus choices

The nine disagreements of the team version, in its order:

- **The year of his death:** "Accounts of the date of his death differ, but he is most often said to have died on the twenty second of Dhu al-Qa'da ... in 1068 AH, the year that corresponds to 1657." Basis (a) and (b): the current site text (1657) and Lahori (22 Zi Qada 1068 AH, which the team version itself says "corresponds to the 1657 of the account above"); Kanhaiya Lal agrees on the day and month, his year illegible. Chishti's two years (1060, 1092) are both marked uncertain, so rule 5 removes them; the clause is kept because the team version records a real divergence (Chishti also gives a different day and month). The Overview gives "died in 1657" without the clause, as Mithankot gives the years in its Overview (judgement call 2).
- **The day of his death:** the twenty second of Dhu al-Qa'da, basis (b): Lahori and Kanhaiya Lal. Chishti's seventeenth of Rabi al-Awwal rests on readings the team version marks uncertain ("both readings are uncertain"); dropped.
- **His grandfather:** Sayyid Abdul Qadir Thalith, with "Accounts of his descent differ in detail, but the grandfather most often named is Sayyid Abdul Qadir Thalith." Basis (b): Lahori's chain and Chishti (p. 108) both make Abdul Qadir Thalith his grandfather. Basis (a) is set aside because every other source that names a grandfather is against the site's Hazrat Abdul Qadir Sani (Kanhaiya Lal names none). Lahori's chain is given link by link (rule 7) and keeps Abdul Qadir Sani as an ancestor four generations above the father. Chishti's p. 109 variant (Abd al-Wahhab a son of Muhammad Ghaus Bala Pir) is an uncertain reading; dropped (judgement call 1).
- **Who called him Chiragh:** his father, on the day he was born, framed "it is said", with "Accounts differ over who gave him the name." The site's version (his grandfather Hazrat Abdul Qadir Sani) cannot stand beside the genealogy chosen above without merging two accounts: in Lahori's chain, which is the one given, Abdul Qadir Sani is four generations above the father. So the naming follows the same account as the genealogy; basis (c), Lahori's notice being the earliest account that tells the naming (Kanhaiya Lal calls Shah Chiragh his title and does not say who gave it). Judgement call 1.
- **Who lies in the tomb:** "eight graves, Shah Chiragh's own and those of his family", "a family shrine across generations rather than the resting place of one man", with "Accounts differ over which of his kin lie with him". The count of eight is common to the site and Kanhaiya Lal; "his family" is the common ground of the site's "including those of his father and grandfather" and Kanhaiya Lal's "seven of his offspring" (precedent: Baba Shah Kamal, "Accounts of the family's ancestry differ in detail, but ..."). The site's father and grandfather are not named because the grandfather chosen above has a tomb of his own around Lahore (Chishti, p. 527), so the two cannot both stand. Chishti's legible *sahibzada* Zain al-Abidin is kept as one of the graves (judgement call 3).

Chishti's "Alamgir the Second" is a reading the team version marks uncertain, so rule 5 removes it and with it the only rival; no clause.
- **One man or two:** "Abdul Razzaq and Shah Chiragh are most often taken to be one man, though accounts differ, and Lahore has known other men of both names." Basis (a) and (b): the site, Lahori, Chishti and Kanhaiya Lal's account of the tomb all join the names; only Kanhaiya Lal's account of the quarters keeps a Sayyid Chiragh Shah Gilani apart from an Abd al-Razzaq. - **Who held the tomb:** told as a sequence, not as alternatives. By the 1880s the tomb was "in the hands of the Gilani Sayyids, the saint's own lineage": Kanhaiya Lal's "Sadat Gilani" (tomb account) and "the tomb-holder's descendants" (quarters account) agree in substance, and the team version itself reads the first as "the saint's own sayyid lineage". In the nineteenth century a *sajjada nashin*, and a faqir who had long held the *ʿurs*, taken its income and served as *khadim* (Chishti; both names uncertain and dropped, the roles kept). ## Judgement calls for V1

1. **Genealogy and naming follow one account.** Lahori's chain is given link by link and his grandfather Sayyid Abdul Qadir Thalith (with Chishti p. 108) as "the grandfather most often named", with "Accounts of his descent differ in detail"; the naming is his father's, at birth, "it is said", with "Accounts differ over who gave him the name". The site's "given the honorific 'Chiragh' ... by his grandfather Hazrat Abdul Qadir Sani" is dropped, because it cannot be set beside the chain without merging two accounts. If V1 holds that rule 4 (a) must keep the site's naming, the alternative is to drop Lahori's chain beyond the father and the lamp story, which loses more.
2. **The death date.** "Accounts of the date of his death differ, but he is most often said to have died on the twenty second of Dhu al-Qa'da ... in 1068 AH, the year that corresponds to 1657." The correspondence is the team version's own statement. For the checker's information only (not used in the text): by the usual conversion 1 Muharram 1068 AH falls in October 1657, so the twenty second of Dhu al-Qa'da 1068 AH falls in the summer of 1658, not 1657. If V1 prefers, the fix is to drop "the year that corresponds to 1657" from that sentence and leave 1657 to the Overview as the year the shrine keeps.
3. **The graves.** "eight graves, Shah Chiragh's own and those of his family ... Accounts differ over which of his kin lie with him; one of the graves is that of a *sahibzada*, a saint's son, named Zain al-Abidin." Common ground of the site and Kanhaiya Lal, as in the consensus choices. "a family shrine across generations rather than the resting place of one man" renders the site's "multi-generational family shrine rather than a single tomb", which both versions of the family support. "one of the graves" assumes Chishti's two named graves are inside the tomb, as the team version's paragraph on "the graves" implies.
4. **The quarter of Sayyid Chiragh Shah Gilani.** "In the 1880s this part of Lahore was also remembered as the quarter of Sayyid Chiragh Shah Gilani ... Sayyid Chiragh Shah's tomb and his *pakka* mosque ... still stood, the mosque in government possession and the tomb held by his descendants." 5. **"Mir Shams al-Arifin, the sun of those who know God."** A dictionary rendering of the title (*shams*, sun; *al-arifin*, the knowers, those who know God), not a translation of the verse. If V1 judges it an addition, end the italic at "Mir Shams al-Arifin."
