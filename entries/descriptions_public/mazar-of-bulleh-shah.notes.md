Source: `entries/descriptions/mazar-of-bulleh-shah.md` (team version) only, with glosses from `data/glossary.csv` or plain dictionary senses. Brief: `docs/prompts/PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md`. Why the ratio is low: this team version is unusually dense in apparatus. Besides the citations and the long disagreement section, most of its paragraphs quote what each scholar thinks of the poet (Rafat, Khaled Ahmad, Shackle, Schimmel), list editions, or set several tellings of one event side by side. Every fact about the man, his family, his master, the shrine, its keepers and singers, the legends and the verse is kept; what goes is the commentary and the variants. A first draft at 0.39 was brought up by restoring supported material (the second translation of the caste reply, the twice born verse and other self portraits, the *kalma*, his last years, the fabricated and Bektashi verses, the *tauhid* theme, the performance pattern outside the *ʿurs*, the Naqshbandi graves of Kasur, Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan), not by padding.

## Consensus choices

- **Death year:** "Accounts of the year of his death differ; the year his tomb keeps is 1171 of the Hijri calendar, which corresponds to 1757 or 1758. The tomb gives it in a Persian chronogram, a phrase or verse whose letters, read as numbers, yield the date." (The gloss names both forms, since the text of the chronogram is not given; V1 round 1, issue 6.) Basis (a): the tomb's own chronogram is the shrine's position. It agrees with the current site text ("around 1757"), with Sharib (1171 AH), and contains the 1758 of most of the English sources (Shackle, Khaled Ahmad, Rafat, Alam). Not listed: Lahori's author 1180 AH; his editor's 1181 AH from a seal; Schimmel's 1752 and 1754; Abbas's years (illegible); Khaled Ahmad's "1758 at the age of 78". The Overview gives no death year, so it cannot disagree with this clause.
- **Birth:** "born around 1680", basis (a) and (b): the current site text, Khaled Ahmad, Alam and Schimmel all give 1680, and "around" covers Shackle's "1680?". Place: "generally said to have been born at Uch Gilanian, ... though neither the year nor the place of his birth is known for certain", basis (b): every source that names a birthplace (Khaled Ahmad, Abbas, Rafat's note) names Uch Gilanian; the clause is Shackle's caution. The qawwals' "from Uch Sharif" stays as their own claim in their section and is not merged with Uch Gilanian.
- **Name:** given name Abdullah Shah (all sources), Bullha the familiar form and Bulleh Shah the honorific form proper to a Sayyid (Shackle; consistent with Rafat's derivation). Treated as minor: Sharib's *kunya* and *takhallus*, the editor's pronunciation "Bulha", and the spellings Bullhe, Bulle, Bulhe are dropped silently.
- **How he came to Kasur:** "Accounts of the family's moves differ, but most say that it settled in the Kasur region of the Punjab when the boy was six." Basis (a) and (b): the current site text (the family settled in the Kasur region) with Khaled Ahmad and Abbas (at six). Sharib's moves from Uch to Malkwal are not listed. Sharib's later stay on the Chenab and return to Kasur after the initiation is kept, since it does not conflict ("made his home at Kasur once more"). Iqbal Qaiser's story of flight to Kasur after his father's death is told in Legends, framed as a story, not as the life.
- **His teacher at Kasur:** "most often said to have studied under Hafiz Ghulam Murtaza", basis (b): Sharib and Khaled Ahmad against Shackle's "Rather than study with this great scholar". The fellow pupil Waris Shah is framed as tradition (the *tazkiras* as Khaled Ahmad reports them).
- **The road to Shah Inayat:** "Accounts differ over how he found his master", then Sharib's dream of Sayyid Abd al-Hakim and the bowl of milk, told as legend ("It is told that"). Basis (c): Sharib's chapter is the earliest account devoted to Bulleh Shah that tells this; Lahori's referral story stands in his entry on Khwaja Abd al-Khaliq, not in his notice of Bulleh Shah, and its place name is marked [OCR?]. The two men named Abd al-Hakim (Sharib's ancestor of the fifth generation; Lahori's living Shaikh Abd al-Hakim Qadiri) are not merged: only Sharib's is told. "When they met" leaves open, as the team version does, whether the milk scene continues the dream.
- **His order:** "Accounts of his own order differ; he is most often counted a Qadiri, a follower of the order of his master." Basis (b): Shackle's glossary, Rafat, Iqbal Qaiser and the current site text's name "Qadiri", with Lahori's double nisba also including Qadiri. Shah Inayat "of the Qadiri and Shattari line", basis (a) (current site text) with Shackle's introduction. Lahori's "Qadiri Shattari" for Bulleh Shah himself is not listed.
- **Shah Inayat's caste:** "an Arain, of a caste of gardeners and cultivators", basis (a) with Shackle and Abbas's text. Minor: "by trade" against "of descent", and Abbas's glossary gloss ("landowners"), dropped silently.
- **The break with his master:** "Accounts of the quarrel and of how long it lasted differ, but most tell that ... Bulleh Shah went away to learn music and dance and came back to win his master over. He is said to have taken lessons with a dancing girl, then reappeared in Lahore one day dressed as a dancing girl himself ..." Basis (b) for the music and dance return (Shackle, the shrine's qawwals, Abbas, Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan and Khalid all tell it). The details that differ (which teacher, how long, what dress) are minor details of one legend, so rule 4's minor branch applies: the dancing girl telling is given as the commonly told version. It is "the most popular tale", it is Abbas's own account, Nusrat's and Khalid's, and it is the persona of the shrine's own song, which is kept as the song ("At the shrine the *qawwals* sing of his reconciliation with his master ..."). Dropped silently: the qawwals' telling of almost seven years among musicians as apprentices, Abbas's "several years", the "twelve years" of Nusrat and Khalid, and the Nusrat sentence. Not listed either: Sharib's Gwalior version (the shrine of Muhammad Ghaus), Khaled Ahmad's contest of miracles, Shackle's reading of one separation as caused by the troubles of the Punjab (the lament verse is kept without a cause). V1 round 1, issue 1: the first draft gave the shrine's telling and "In another telling" the dancing girl one, which read as side by side variants.
- **The burial:** the refusal is tradition, basis (a), the current site text; Khaled Ahmad's telling as fact in 1758 is folded into the tradition and its year dropped. Kept in the Shrine section as "where by tradition he had been refused burial".
- **A martyr's death:** not mentioned. Sachal Sarmast's line has him slaughtered; the established position (Schimmel: "The traditional sources say nothing about the martyrdom") is simply that he died at Kasur.
- **Where Kasur lies:** "south of Lahore", basis (a). Rizvi's "thirty miles south east" and Abbas's "seventy miles" conflict and are dropped; Schimmel's "near Lahore" dropped.
- **The tomb's fabric:** told as change over time, not as a conflict: by the early 1980s a concrete courtyard and a dilapidated building with broken filigree (Khaled Ahmad, 1982 book); by the early 2000s a brick courtyard under a concrete canopy (Abbas, 2002 book; her fieldwork is undated, so the anchor is the book's year with "By"); today a radically modernised appearance (Shackle, 2015 and 2021).
- **His politics:** the current site text's "against every orthodoxy and every tyranny" is not kept. Basis: every other source that speaks to it (Shackle, Khaled Ahmad, Rafat) holds that his social and political critique is small and oblique, so rule 4(a)'s exception applies and (b) decides: "He never directly criticised the rulers", "His social critique, though vividly expressed, is only a small part of his poetry". "A voice of tolerance and of the ordinary human heart" is kept (uncontested).
- **Which poems are most popular:** "His most famous *kafi*" is the poem "who am I", basis (a), the current site text; "Teray Ishq nachaya" is "among the most popular of his *kafis* today", so no second superlative is set beside it (V1 round 1, issue 2). Not listed: Shackle's "probably the best known" and "most famous" for other poems.
- **Rumi:** the consensus that "Ki janan main kaun" is modelled on Rumi's ghazal is kept (Khaled Ahmad, Rafat and Shackle agree, in degrees); the minor disagreement over whether he ever names Rumi is dropped.
- **Size of the corpus:** "There is no settled count of the poems that are truly his", basis (d); the 1960 edition's 156 *kafis* is given as a fact about that edition. Not listed: Lahori's "thousands", Rafat's "156 authenticated", Shackle's 157.
- **Time anchors:** Lahori (1875) gives "By the 1870s"; Rafat with Khaled Ahmad's introduction (1982) gives "By the early 1980s" (both sentences); Abbas (2002) gives "By the early 2000s", and her undated observation of the narrow streets and *tangas* is in the past tense; Shackle (2015) gives "Today". Chishti's material (date unconfirmed) carries no time anchor (the founding legend, the Sikh damage). Kanhaiya Lal is not used.

## Left out

- **Source apparatus:** every citation, page and folio; every "Shackle writes", "Rafat counts", "Khaled Ahmad heads his section"; the `## Where the Sources Disagree` section (resolved above); the Library of Congress line; the list of editions beyond the 1889 Lahore and 1896 Kasur collections and the 1960 edition; Schimmel's undated chapbook; the sources of Lahori's editor (Qureshi's *Sawanih*, Shafi's *Auliya-e Kasur*, the editor's own works in preparation).
- **What authors say about him, as opposed to facts:** Rafat on his mysticism limiting him as a poet; the comparison with the Kannada *vacana* poets; "essentially non-poetic in terms of rhythm"; Shackle on hagiographic elaboration and the authenticity of wording; studies in English "none of which is at all comprehensive"; the *Punjab: Past and Present* complaint and the scholarly assessment still awaited; the hagiographer's "immaculate background"; the *tazkiras* being "revisionist"; "cuts across the mystical politics of his times"; his allegiance as his "only concession to form"; Khalid's reading of the years as a dancing girl's servant as a critique of caste; Shackle's hope that his volume will correct biases. Khaled Ahmad's judgements on the shrine ("mediocre", "poetasters", "execrable taste", "low taste", "bourgeois profanity") and the detail of defecation on the graves are left out; the facts they describe are kept.
- **Uncertain readings (rule 5):** Talamba [OCR?] (with the whole of Lahori's referral story, see above); Sharib's third place of the family ("perhaps Pandu"); Kanhaiya Lal's damaged sentence on *kafis* sung in *kalawant* assemblies; Abbas's illegible years; the illegible name of the son of Ram Chand (the line kept as "One of the sons of Ram Chand"); "Bhulla", which the team version gives "as the printed text is read", so the reunion pun of Bullah and Bhulla is dropped; Schimmel's 1752, which the team version says "needs checking".
- **Variants under rule 4** (see Consensus choices): Lahori's 1180 AH, the editor's 1181 AH and the seal at Ferozepur, Schimmel's 1752 and 1754; Malkwal; Lahori's referral story with Khwaja Abd al-Khaliq and Gul Sher Muhammad; Gwalior; the contest of miracles; the separation for the troubles; the twelve years; Sachal Sarmast's litany; the distances of Kasur; the counts of the corpus; the name's *kunya* and spellings.
- **Ambiguous in the team version:** Sharib's passage in which the father, after *muraqaba*, places "the elder" at the village of Sanda; which elder is meant is not clear, so it is left out rather than tied to Sayyid Abd al-Hakim or to Shah Inayat.
- **Duplicates:** Schimmel's two renderings of the *alif* lines (Shackle's kept); Rafat's rendering of the reproach ("one way ticket to hell"; his reply is kept); Ramakrishna's full translation of "Ki janan main kaun"; "Ilmon bas kareen O yar" listed among his *kafis*; the *cullha* pun; Shah Latif's oblique approach to *anā 'l-ḥaqq*; "Neh wi doongi" and Baba Farid (the team version does not say which of the two *kafis* answers to which poet).
- **Abbas's journey:** the drive from Lahore and the arrival at five o'clock (the spring evening before Ramazan is kept as the evening of the performance).
- **Absences of record** (no builder, no date of building, no *sajjada nashin*, no date of the *ʿurs* in the sources): not stated, since stating them as facts about the tomb would claim more than the team version shows. For the same reason the tomb is no longer said to bear a "single" inscription, only "a Persian chronogram" (V1 round 1, issue 3).
