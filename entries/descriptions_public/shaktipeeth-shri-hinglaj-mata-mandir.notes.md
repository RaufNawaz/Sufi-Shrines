## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Where the shrine stands:** Lasbela district, Makran coast, Hingol National Park, along the Hingol River. Basis (a), the site text. The other placings do not contradict it and are kept where they belong: about 250 kilometres from Karachi and the Sindhi speaking area of Balochistan (The Setting), seventy kos from Thatta in Aurangzeb's reign (The Pilgrimage), "west of Sindh, in the Lasbela region" (the poetry). Left out: nothing.
- **Whose shrine it is:** the goddess Hinglaj Mata. Basis (a) with (b). Left out: Schimmel's "ancient Shiva sanctuary" (also dropped from the Latif sentence), Masson's Parbatí and Diana, Bhandari's "attributed to Durga", Khalid's "incarnation of Durga", the Brahmavaivarta's "idol of Durga". Durga appears only in the priest's explanation of the second face.
- **Which part of Sati fell here:** the head, the *brahmarandhra*, with "Accounts differ over which part of her fell here, but it is most often said to be the head". Basis (a) and (b): the site, the *Pīṭhanirṇaya*, the skull stone at the image, and the team version's "prevailing" imaginary. Left out: the navel (*Caṇḍīmaṅgala*), the womb (a minority and one pilgrim).
- **Names of the Goddess and her Bhairava:** Kottari and Bhimalochana. Basis (a); the *Pīṭhanirṇaya*'s Koṭṭavī and Koṭṭarīśā dropped as spelling variants (minor). The reading of the names as "a naked woman (maybe giving birth)" left out as conjecture.
- **Origin of the name:** "There is no settled view of where the name Hinglaj comes from, though it is linked to the Sanskrit word *hiṅgula* ... vermilion". Basis (d), with the explanation the team version gives as the told story ("It is said"), on the model of the Sehwan sentence V1 passed. Left out: asafoetida.
- **Number of seats:** fifty one, with "Accounts of the number of seats differ from text to text". Basis (a). Left out: the Kulārṇava's eighteen, the Kubjikā's forty two, the lists of four.
- **Seats in Pakistan:** "Accounts of how many of the seats lie in Pakistan differ, but Hinglaj is held to be one of only two there, and it is the most frequented of them." Basis (a). Left out: Ibrahim Shah's three (the "most frequented" holds of either set).
- **First mention of the name:** "There is no consensus ...; its first mention is placed between the ninth and the fifteenth centuries." Basis (d), endpoints both from the team version. Left out: the tenth to fifteenth (inside the range) and Desai's eleventh century for Hiṅghalāja.
- **Link with the head and the claim to first place:** first in the *Pīṭhanirṇaya*, dated between 1690 and 1720, "though accounts of their age differ". Basis (b)/(c): it is the summary of the only account devoted to the shrine. Left out: the *Tantracūḍāmaṇi* and about 1571.
- **Age of the worship:** "Accounts of its age differ, but its worship is held to reach back well over a millennium." Basis (a); not every other source is against it (Kamphorst's ninth century and Tambs-Lyche's fifth to seventh agree). Left out: those datings, the cult "between the seventh and ninth centuries", the pilgrimage system "at least since the fourteenth century" and the Thatta inscriptions.
- **The second face:** "One face is the Goddess's; accounts of whose the other is differ, and no particular story had fully settled around it. In 2012 the temple's priest explained it as the face of a *vir* ... of the Goddess Durga". Basis (a), the shrine's only permanent priest, agreeing with "today ... often interpreted" (p. 229). Left out: Hingol (the legend's closing sentence "Since this day, both of their faces are found inside the cave", and the priest's explanation of 2009), Shiva or Rama (devotees), and the three answers given to television in 2010.
- **Who Hinglaj was as a woman:** only "Hinglaj herself was once a Charani ... the first of countless incarnations", with one clause "though her place among those incarnations is contested among Charans today". Basis (d). Left out: Samaur (a Charani of Thatta, daughter of Haridas; the Thatta temple as important as the main shrine), Payaka (queen of Kohala, leader of the Tumbel Charan, a virgin learned in the texts), Kamphorst (Charani Deval, eighth century). "She is their *adishakti*" is kept as the team version's own plain statement (p. 172).
- **Kapri title:** Kapri; the kāpaḍiyā and Kapadiya of Depavat and Kamphorst dropped as spellings of the same title (minor).
- **What the garbha is:** the tunnel under the image (most passages); "a small rift in the mountain wall called the garbha" dropped as the variant, the crevice kept as the oldest part of the tunnel.
- **The Goddess's form:** the site's "not as a carved idol but as a small mud-stone form daubed with sindoor", rephrased "a small form of mud and stone" to avoid the hyphen, followed by the field description of the same image. The grave-like stone of colonial visitors is told as the image before the tiling, a change over time. Basis (a), with the description of the same object.
- **Date of the renovation:** "late 1990s" and "around 1998". Basis (b), four passages. Left out: "the early 1990s" (p. 248), minor.
- **The highway:** first part complete and in use by 2001, the whole road from Liari to Gabd in 2003: two stages, not rivals.
- **The ticket system:** the wristband told without a first year; "in 2008" (one passage) and the earlier system before 2009 dropped as minor.
- **Month of the festival:** April, "accounts of the festival's month differ, but April is the month most often given". Basis (a) and (b). Left out: Khalid's October, the Brahmavaivarta's eighth of āśvin.
- **How many come:** the site's tens of thousands (lead), thousands a year early in the nineteenth century, Hotchand's family with 2,000 others in 1805 and 1806, over forty thousand in April 2010: figures for different times, not rivals. Left out: Hart's undated five to six thousand (it would sit beside Pottinger's figure), Khalid's "thousands every year".
- **Length of the journey:** "Accounts of how long the journey took differ widely, from more than fifteen days to six months." Basis (d). Left out: Briggs's twenty four days and Hotchand's two and a half months as separate reckonings.
- **Todarmal and Bharimal:** "in the sixteenth century, though accounts of the year differ". Basis (d): both years, 1508 and 1558, fall in it. Left out: both years and the arrival at Thatta.
- **Rivers of Anil Kund:** eighty four thousand, which gives the place its other name, Chaurasi. The Gujarati pilgrims' nine hundred dropped silently as a minor legend variant.
- **The great stone:** the priest's story, with "accounts of how the stone came there differ". Basis (a). Left out: Gorakhnath bringing it from Anil Kund (Gorakhnath kept only in the separate Thatta tradition, in The Pilgrimage).
- **Who counselled Rama:** "Counselled by a sage". Both tellings make the counsellor a sage and the site names none. Left out: Kumbhodar (and his refusal of Rama's food) and Vashishta.
- **Whether the Goddess takes blood:** vegetarian today, the old custom at the Kali shrine "until recently", the ban of 2004, the substitute, the continuing sacrifices, and the Devipujak-Vagri duty as one community's practice. Left out: the Sindhi Hindu authors who call the custom foreign.
- **Whether Shah Abdul Latif went there:** "is said to have wandered ... with a group of yogis, perhaps reaching Hinglaj with them, though there is no settled view of whether he did". Basis (d) in the hedged wording of most tellings (Schimmel's "perhaps", her later hedge, Boivin's "probably"). Left out: "three years", Boivin's Dwarka.
- **Which way the pilgrims travel:** not a disagreement but the poem's meaning (west on the map, "towards the East" in the poetry); kept.
- **The Muslim community:** the site's veneration by local Muslims kept, with the Zikri custodianship until 1986 and the present marginalisation. Left out: the site's "This shared reverence has helped preserve the site through difficult times", the claim the team version sets against the record.
- **Whose grave the Muslims saw:** "a saintly wife of Ali, miraculously carried to this spot, though Muslim accounts of who she was differ". Basis (b): Stein twice, and the team version's summary of the earlier reading includes it. Left out: Eve falling into a pond (Wilford), "grandmother, nanny". The Zikris' present view (the grave of one of the family of the Prophet) is kept in its own paragraph as their belief today; it does not contradict.
- **Spellings:** Chandrakup (used throughout the team version and inside its quotations) for the site's Chandragup; Lasbela throughout (the site's district name) for Las Bela (both minor).
