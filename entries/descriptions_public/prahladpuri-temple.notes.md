## Consensus choices

The team version records seven disagreements, every one between the site's account (the current site text, resting on the general items at the head of the bibliography) and Haroon Khalid's two books. Basis (a) is taken throughout, as in the Shah Jamal pilot, where the shrine's year was kept against a single dissenting book; where the point matters to a visitor, one clause says that accounts differ, without naming the variant.

- **Who built the temple:** "Local tradition holds that it was Prahlada himself who raised the temple at Multan in thanksgiving, enshrining an image of Narasimha on the spot, though accounts of its founding differ." Basis (a). Khalid's devotees who built it in Prahlad's memory are not listed. The founding matters to a visitor, so the clause is given.
- **What happened on the spot:** Narasimha slew Hiranyakashipu, basis (a), which is also the canonical myth the team version gives with the *Bhagavata Purana*. Khalid's telling, in which Prahlad himself killed his father at the spot, is a variant within the legend; it is covered by the same founding clause and not given.
- **Where the mound lay:** at the southern tip of the great fort of Multan, basis (a). Khalid's "on a mound outside the city" is dropped silently as minor: both accounts set the temple on the mound beside Bahauddin Zakariya's shrine, so a visitor finds it in the same place.
- **To whom the temple belongs:** dedicated to Narasimha and named for Prahlada, basis (a). Khalid is silent on Narasimha rather than contrary, so no clause. His names for it are kept as names: "the temple of Prahlad Bhagat" (Overview) and "the historical Prahlad Temple" (Destruction and After).
- **When worship ceased:** basis (a), a living centre of worship for centuries, renovated after 1881, its line of worship severed in 1992 (Overview and The Temple). One clause in Destruction and After: "Accounts differ over whether worship there had continued after Partition". Khalid's "abandoned ever since partition" is not given.
- **Whether the building still stands:** basis (a), destroyed in 1992, the structure reduced to rubble, surviving only as a ruin. Clause: "and over how much of the building still stands". Khalid's "a worn down building with several cracks in its walls" is not given. This matters to a visitor (what is there to see).
- **Who holds the site:** basis (a), the Evacuee Trust Property Board, with the team version's own gloss. Clause: "though accounts differ over which government body has charge of it". Parvati Devi's Awqaf department is not named, to avoid a side by side variant; her recollection of the shrine's extension and her demand are kept as hers.
- **Time anchors:** Khalid's *A White Trail* (2013) is anchored "in the early twenty first century" and "by the early 2010s"; 2006, 2008 and 2010 are dates the team version gives in the text. Nothing from Chishti (1867, the date unconfirmed) is dated: the Sikh conquest and Mulraj's rising carry no year. Bhandari is anchored "in the reign of Aurangzeb", from his bibliography item. The dates 641, 711, the eleventh century, 1175 and 1811 are dates of events the team version states, not dates of a description. The siege of 1848, the restoration around 1852, 1881 and December 1992 are the site's account as given. The Babri Masjid date (the 6th of December 1992) is the team version's, with no rival.
