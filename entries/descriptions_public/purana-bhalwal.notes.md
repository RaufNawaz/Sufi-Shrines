## Consensus choices

- **Year of death:** "Accounts of the year of his death differ; the year the shrine keeps is 1604, which falls in the Hijri year 1012." Basis (a), the shrine (site account: 1604, night of 27 Ramadan), which Lahori's editor's 27 Ramadan 1012 AH matches (the team version says 1012 AH is the Hijri year in which 1604 falls). The author's 1065 AH, which the editor calls wrong, is not listed. The night of the 27th of Ramadan kept (shrine and editor agree).
- **Life span:** "Tradition gives him a remarkably long life, from the early sixteenth century until 1604": the team version's own words and framing ("traditionally said", "precise dates should be treated as traditional"). No birth year given (the team version gives none).
- **Father:** "remembered as Shaikh Abdullah, known as Mian Mangu, or simply Mangu". The author's "Mangu" and the editor's "Shaikh Abdullah, known as Mian Mangu" do not conflict on the name, so the editor's fuller form is given with the shared name. The shrine names no father.
- **Caste:** "Accounts of the family's caste differ." Basis (d): the shrine gives no caste, and the team version's only accounts are the author (Mochi) and his editor (Qureshi, citing *Sharif al-Tawarikh* and *Manaqibat-e Naushahi*), so neither (a) nor a clear (b) or (c) settles it. Neither caste is named, so that no side by side pair appears; the team notes also leave open for Rauf whether caste should be named in the public article at all (decision 4 there). **For Rauf:** if he wants the caste named, the choice would be between basis (b) (the editor's Qureshi, backed by two works he cites) and (d) with both named.
- **The father's village:** "the village of Bhalwal". The author's reading (Bhelowal) is marked uncertain in the team version, so rule 5 removes it; the editor's correction to Bhalwal is the legible statement. The identification of the place is not stated more firmly than that ("is said to have lived").
- **Name and title:** Hazrat Shah Sulaiman Nuri and *Sakhi Badshah*, basis (a), the shrine (also Tahqiqat's "Nuri"). Lahori's heading kept as a further name, "also known as Shah Sulaiman Qadri" (it is a name he bears, not a rival fact). The index lines and the question which one leads to his entry are left out.
- **Master and order:** rule 4's own case. The shrine names his *pir* Shah Maroof Khushabi of Khushab (basis a); the only clear chain (Lahori, and Tahqiqat's pedigree) runs from Shah Maruf Chishti. So: "The shrine remembers Shah Maroof Khushabi as his *pir*; his spiritual line is most often traced from Shah Maruf Chishti ...". "Most often": both books that give a line give this one; the shrine gives none. The two names are not said to be one man or two, since the team version does not say. The site's story (the teacher who sent him to Purana Bhalwal) stays with the shrine's master, and Lahori's story (Shah Maruf marked him out as a child) stays with Shah Maruf, so neither story is moved under the other master. Order: Qadiri, basis (a) and (b) (the shrine's "Qadiri path"; Lahori heads him and his disciple "Qadri"); Shah Maruf's double Chishti and Qadiri authority kept as a fact about Shah Maruf.
- **The disciple's name:** Haji Muhammad Naushah Ganj Bakhsh, also known as Shaikh Haji Muhammad Qadri (Lahori, twice). Tahqiqat's "Haji Mahmud Shah" dropped silently as a minor variant; basis (c), Lahori's being the account devoted to this saint (Tahqiqat names him once in a pedigree, and its date is unconfirmed). The team notes record this disagreement as the team writer's own reading of the two strings, not flagged in the entry.
- **Time anchors:** "in the 1870s" (Naushahi *faqirs* counted in the thousands, "the author's own day") and "In 1875" (the book's notice and chronogram) rest on Lahori's firm 1292 AH / 1875 CE. Nothing from Tahqiqat is dated (its 1867 is unconfirmed). Bhandari's material is anchored only as "under the Mughals". Khalid's canal colonies carry no date of their own; the date "late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries" is the team version's for Bhalwal's growth.

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: *Qadiriyya* (the Sufi order founded by ʿAbd al-Qadir Gilani), *dargah* (Sufi shrine complex), *pir* (spiritual guide), *sama* (spiritual listening; Sufi music session), *bai'at* (glossary *bay'a*: initiation into a Sufi order by pledging to the *pir*; the team version's spelling kept), *urs* (yearly commemoration of the saint's death), *qawwali* (Sufi devotional music), *langar* (the free communal meal, the team version's own gloss), *faqirs* (mystics).

Team version's own: *Sakhi Badshah* ('the generous king'), Chaj Doab (the fertile tract between the Jhelum and the Chenab), *doabs* (the tracts between its rivers), *shajra* (spiritual pedigree), Purana ('old'), *chaks* (new villages), the *urs* as the soul's union with the Divine.

Plain dictionary senses: *wajd* (the ecstasy of the mystic), *khalifa* (a spiritual deputy of his master), *Fatiha* (the opening chapter of the Qur'an), *qawwals* (the singers of *qawwali*), *suba* (province), *kharif* and *rabi* (the harvests of autumn and of spring), Auqaf (the government department of religious endowments), chronogram (verses whose letters give a year).
