## Consensus choices

The shorter forms that are legible in the team version are kept as further names, "also known more simply as Shaikh Daud Kirmani, or Shaikh Daud of Shergarh" (Lahori, Sharib), as on purana-bhalwal. Kanhaiya Lal's "Khwaja Daud" is dropped silently with the title variants (next item).
- **His title in the order (Shaikh, Khwaja, Sayyid):** minor; dropped silently. He is called a Sayyid by descent and a *shaykh* of the Qadiri order, which is what the team version says of him; no variant title is listed.
- **His toponymic name (Jahniwal in Lahori's table of contents, Chuniwal in the heading of the notice):** left out. Chuniwal is marked uncertain (rule 5), and Jahniwal is a table of contents form with no other support; neither is something a visitor needs. The team version's juxtaposition with the father's move to Chunian is not carried over, since it would be an inference.
- **Year of death:** "He died in 1575", basis (a), the shrine. Lahori's 982 AH is kept as the year the two chronograms mark ("Two chronograms ... mark the year of his death, 982 AH"), because the chronograms are verse the brief requires to be kept and the team version gives 982 AH as their year. No "accounts differ" clause is written, because once Sharib's relative date is removed (it rests on a year, 1110 AH, that the team version says the printed text leaves in doubt, so rule 5 drops it) the two remaining dates do not conflict: for the coordinator and V1 only, 982 AH runs from about April 1574 to April 1575, so it contains the early months of 1575. That conversion is my arithmetic, not the team version's, so the public text does not state it and keeps the two years in separate sentences.
- **Birth:** 1513 (the shrine; no source contradicts it), with Lahori's "born four months after his father's death" kept as a further fact.
- **His descent:** the father, grandfather and the two further forebears given legibly are kept (Sayyid Fath Allah Kirmani, Sayyid Mubarak, Safi al-Din Adam Kirmani, Sayyid Taqi al-Din Ahmad). For the disagreement over Musa Mubarqa's father: "Family trees differ over the upper links of the line; the one held to be correct runs through Sayyid Muhammad Aaraji, son of Sayyid Musa Mubarqa, son of Imam Muhammad Taqi, son of Imam Ali Musa Raza." Basis (c): the earliest account devoted to the saint (Lahori's notice) records the *Shajarat al-Anwar* line and then corrects it from another genealogy "because Musa Mubarqa was no son of Imam Raza"; the team version's disagreement item says this is the genealogy Lahori follows. The shrine gives no line, and there is no majority. The *Shajarat al-Anwar*'s "son of Imam Musa Raza" is not listed. This is Lahori's own corrected line (the correction concerns only the upper links), not a merge of two independent chains; flagged for V1 below.
- **How the family reached the Punjab:** "Accounts also differ over how the family came to the Punjab. It is said that his father came from Arabia to India, settled at Haibatpur Patti, and then moved to the *qasba* ... of Chunian." Basis (c), the earliest account devoted to the saint (Lahori, after the *Safinat al-Auliya*). Dropped: the footnote in Sharib's account of Abul Muali with a migration from Kirman to Hindustan in 796 AH by way of Uch and the Multan country, ending at Shergarh after four generations (its two intermediate place names are in any case uncertain readings).
- **His order and chain:** Qadiri, all sources agree. The chain runs from Daud through Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh back to Ghaus al-Azam, as in both Lahori (his master Sayyid Hamid, by Ghaus al-Azam's inner permission) and Chishti's chain of Shah Bilawal; one chain, no merge.
- **The second line through Shah Abu Ishaq:** kept as fact ("Shah Abu Ishaq, who was Daud's *murid* and *khalifa* in the Qadiri *silsila* ... From Shah Abu Ishaq the line passed to Shaikh Shams-ud-Din and on to Shah Bilawal"). Each of the two passages behind it has an uncertain word (Kanhaiya Lal's place name; Chishti's "Daud Bandagi"), but each supplies what the other lacks legibly (Kanhaiya Lal's "Khwaja Daud", Chishti's "whose mazar is at Shergarh"), and the team version's own Overview and Legacy state the link without qualification. Flagged for V1 below.
- **The footprints:** not a disagreement in substance (one source only); the Jain comparison is kept as the historian's own view, in his words.
