## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Birth year:** 1630, "Accounts of the year of his birth differ." Basis (a), the site's year; the other sources are not all against it (Rafat 1631 and Alam 1629 are; Sharib's 1039 AH has no Common Era equivalent in the team version, and Abbas gives no year). Left out: 1631, 1629, 1039 AH, Abbas's "born in the Panjab".
- **Death year:** 1691, "accounts of the year of his death differ". Basis (a) with (b) (Rafat; Schimmel's *Mystical Dimensions*; the index of *As Through a Veil*; *Pain and Grace* once). Left out: Alam's 1690, *Pain and Grace*'s 1694, Sharib's first of Jumada al-Thani 1102 AH, the editor's 1102 AH with the colophon's third watch of the night of Friday, and Lahori's author placing him among the saints whose year of death is not established. No Hijri date is given beside 1691, since no source gives a Common Era equivalent (see Points for V1, 4).
- **Where he lies:** Garh Maharaja, in the Jhang region, basis (a), with "Accounts of where his grave has lain differ". Left out: Sharib's mazar at Shorkot; Lahori's *rauza* in the Jhang region, fifteen kos from the meeting of the Ravi and the Chenab (see Points for V1, 5). Born in the Shorkot region and buried in his homeland are the site's own words.
- **The moving of the tomb:** basis (a), the site's own words ("twice moved and rebuilt" in the Overview, "moved and rebuilt more than once" in The Shrine). Sharib's opening of the grave seventy years after the death is kept only as a framed wonder in Legends, in its own paragraph, not joined to the moves and not counted as one of them.
- **Father's name:** Bayazid Muhammad, "accounts of the father's name differ". The site names no father, and (b) is one against one, so basis (c): Sharib's notice (preface signed 1965) is the earlier of the two accounts that name him, the editor's notes being later than 1968 (his list of sources includes Zubairi's book of 1968). Left out: Bazid known as Awan (the saint's *Kalid al-Tawhid*, quoted by the editor). The father's office, learning, moves, *jagir* and death at Shorkot are Sharib's and uncontested; the site's "an official of the Mughal administration" agrees.
- **How he came by his name:** his mother named him, basis (a) (the site's tradition) with (b) (Sharib's quotation of the saint's own words), with "Accounts of how he came by the name differ." Left out: Schimmel's surname from the *hu* that ends each line of the Golden Alphabet; Sharib's word printed as *hua* (rule 5).
- **His master:** Sayyid Abdul Rahman (Qadiri) of Delhi, basis (a) with Sharib. Sharib's stages (the court of the Prophet, the mother's answer, Shah Habibullah) are told as tradition and end at the same master, so they are not a rival account. The honorific is Sayyid (the site, Sharib's narrative, the editor's chain); Shaykh, in Shah Habibullah's words, is dropped as minor, and the public text gives him no honorific in that sentence.
- **Chain of masters:** the editor's ten links given whole, from the one source that gives them; nothing from Sharib is hung on it (the Prophet giving him into Abd al-Qadir Jilani's charge stays a separate tradition, not a link).
- **Name of his order:** the Sarwari Qadiri, basis (a), with "accounts of the name of his branch differ". Left out: Sharib's Qadiriyya Masruriyya. Lahori's and Schimmel's "Qadiri" agree with "his own branch of the Qadiri order".
- **His learning:** "Accounts of his learning differ, but by the 1870s he was remembered as a great and deeply learned scholar, who united the outward and the inward sciences." Basis (c): Lahori (1875) is the earliest account devoted to him. Left out: Sharib's "he could not read or write". Sharib's twenty four titles are kept, since the number and names of his works are not in dispute.
- **Languages of his works:** a composite of compatible statements: Persian (the site), Arabic and Persian (Lahori), all in Arabic and Persian except the Punjabi *abyat* and *siharfi* (Abbas).
- **Verse form:** basis (a), the site's *siharfi* and, most famously, the *abyat*. Minor variants dropped silently: *Pain and Grace*'s "most famous ... in the traditional form of the *siharfi*", Abbas's and Rafat's *bait*.
- **The worth of the verse:** basis (a), the site's "at once fiercely devotional and gloriously musical". Left out: Schimmel's "do nothing but develop ideas well known to earlier Sufis" and Rafat's "needless and tedious mannerism".
- **The preceptor in the verse:** basis (a), the guide as one of his themes. Left out: Rafat's "no personal reference to make to an actual preceptor-beloved".
- **Music:** the shrine's *qawwali* and singing (the site) stand in The Shrine and Devotion; the saint's teaching against listening to *rag* stands in His Books and Teaching as his own teaching. Neither is set against the other (see Points for V1, 10).
- **The first verse in English:** the site's rendering, then Schimmel's whole first stanza from *Mystical Dimensions* as "another English version" (the bhit-bhit-shah precedent). The *As Through a Veil* wording ("a jasmine bud ... has planted") dropped as a minor variant.
- **His sons:** Sharib's eight kept; the son whose full name is uncertain dropped (rule 5); the seven named after Sultan Hayat Muhammad are introduced with "among the others", so the list claims no completeness and the eight against nine discrepancy is not set out.
- **His khalifas:** Hazrat Momin Shah Gilani of Sindh only; Naurang, Mulla Maani and the Khetran dropped (rule 5), and with them Sharib's count of three.
- **Hir's grave:** "The lovers Hir and Ranjha are said to be buried in a single grave at Jhang, and their shrine has become a place of pilgrimage" (Khalid). Abbas's villagers who identify Hir's tomb say the same thing and are not given separately. Jhang Siyalan (Bhandari's town) and Jhang Siyal (Abbas's territory) are kept in separate sentences, not identified.

## Time anchors

- Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya* (bibliography: composed 1292 AH / 1875 CE, firm): "By the 1870s" for his sayings and verses in Punjabi being very famous, his being counted among the saints of the Punjab as a man of ecstasy and intoxication, his complete fame, his being remembered as a great and deeply learned scholar, and the grace of his path and the birds of that wild country.
- Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh* (composed in regnal year 40 of Aurangzeb): "In Aurangzeb's reign" for Shorkot as the northern boundary of the *suba* of Multan and the Chenab joining the Bihat near Jhang Siyalan. No decade is given.
- Sharib (preface 1965, first edition 1999): the life, family, master, teaching and wonders are told as narrative or tradition, with no anchor.
- Abbas (2002): the invocation at the outset of performances, the concert in Islamabad and the sung texts are told in the past, with no decade anchor.
- Khalid (2015): Hir and Ranjha's shrine "has become a place of pilgrimage"; Damodar Das Arora "in the reign of the Mughal Emperor Akbar", as given.
- Schimmel: the Punjabi *siharfi* in his honour, "has been composed", with no date.
- The site's account: the present tense for the shrine (the round of prayer, *qawwali*, singing and *langar*, the *ʿurs* in spring, the order maintaining his memory, the shrine that "remains a great centre").
- Years kept as given: 1630, 1691.
