## Consensus choices

- **When he died:** "There he was martyred, in 577 AH by the Islamic calendar", with no "accounts differ" clause. Basis (b) in effect: 577 AH is the only legible year; the site's twelfth century agrees with it; Chishti's year is an uncertain reading in a damaged passage (rule 5) and Bhandari gives none. No conversion to CE (the team version gives none). Judgement call 15.
- **His name:** Sayyid Ahmad Sultan, called Sakhi Sarwar (site), the Sayyid Ahmad of both legible accounts, son of Sayyid Zain al-Abidin (all three books). Basis (a) and (b). The index line that makes Ahmad his father, and the index line that makes Sayyid Abd al-Latif his grandfather (the pedigree has him further back), are minor and dropped.
- **Aisha, mother or daughter:** his mother is Bibi Ayesha, daughter of the headman Pira, with no clause. Basis (b): the notice that tells his birth, and Chishti's account of the headman's elder daughter married to the father. The index line "Aisha, daughter of Sakhi Sarwar" is the variant and is dropped (judgement call 16).
- **Where the family settled:** "tradition places his origins in the region of Multan and Shahkot" (the site's "Multan/Shahkot", basis (a)), and the father "settled at a village in the Multan area" (common to all three books). The village names are uncertain readings (rule 5); Chishti's ten kos is uncertain; Bhandari's four kos south of Multan is minor and dropped (judgement call 1).
- **His masters:** "Accounts of his masters differ." The Baghdad account (the Ghaus al-Azam, Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi, Shaikh Maudud Chishti) and the blessing of *wilayat* from Bahauddin Zakariya with the family counted Chishti and Suhrawardi are both given as "said", because they do not exclude each other and no shrine position, majority or devoted account chooses between them. There is no chain in either, so nothing is hung under anything. Chishti's *Miraj al-Awliya* citation and its uncertain word for "disciple" are dropped (judgement call 3).
- **His wife:** "his wife is most often named Bibi Bai", with "though accounts of his marriage differ". Basis (b): two of the three accounts name her (Bi Bai, Bibi Bai Sahiba); the third names no wife but makes her the daughter of the governor of Multan, which is not listed and not joined to Bibi Bai (judgement call 4).
- **His son:** basis (d): "accounts differ over the boy's name, Siraj al-Din or Sayyid Raj". Bhandari's "Mian" with an uncertain name is dropped. Sayyid Raj appears again in the Legends only inside the devotees' tale where he is Bibi Bai's son, as in the team version.
- **His brother:** Sayyid Abd al-Ghani. Basis (b) (Lahori and Chishti); Bhandari's Mian Dhoda is an uncertain reading and Chishti's byname for Abd al-Ghani is unreadable, so both are dropped.
- **Who killed him:** basis (d), in one sentence: "Accounts differ over who killed him: some tell of his jealous kinsmen, who ... rode after him; others of a fight with the Khokhars that broke out by chance, in which he and his brother were martyred." No shrine position; one legible account each; the earliest (Bhandari) is not devoted to this site and does not name it, and the one that names Nigaha is later, so (c) does not settle it cleanly. Chishti's telling (the army destroyed at prayer, the old man bidden to martyr him) is told in `## Legends` as the devotees' tale (judgement calls 6 and 9).
- **How his wife and son died:** basis (d): "His wife and young son died with him or soon after, and accounts of their deaths differ." The burial "at the foot of the hills" and "the tomb of the martyr" (Bhandari) are kept; Chishti's grave legend is kept in `## Legends`.
- **Distance from Multan:** forty *kos* (minor); "more than forty" dropped.
- **Who keeps the tomb:** both kept as different roles, not as rival answers: in the reign of Aurangzeb the tomb was kept by the descendants of the three cured men; devotees tell that Shaikh Garha brought people from Kotli to sweep the *khanqah*. The names Kah o Kalang and Dholis are uncertain readings and are dropped (judgement call 10).
- **The chronograms:** two different verses, not a conflict. Lahori's is quoted whole; Chishti's (fragments only in the team version) is described by its legible names and title.
- **The bombing of 2011:** the site's "a suicide bombing struck a procession of its pilgrims" (basis (a)) with Khalid's date and toll, which do not contradict it: "On 3 April 2011 a suicide bombing at the shrine struck a procession of its pilgrims, and around fifty people were killed" (judgement call 11).
- **Time anchors:** Lahori (1875, firm) as "in the 1870s" (the spring); Bhandari as "in the reign of Aurangzeb" (his bibliography item gives regnal year 40, not converted); Chishti (1867, "the date unconfirmed") only as "in the nineteenth century"; Khalid (2013) as "in the early twenty first century".

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: Sayyid (a descendant of the Prophet Muhammad); *awliya* and *wali* (the friends of God, a friend of God); *mashaikh* (the Sufi masters, from Shaykh: master, teacher); *murid* (his pledged disciple, the pilots' wording); *khanqah* (the lodge where a Sufi's disciples gather); *faqir* (a mystic vowed to spiritual poverty, from faqir and faqr); *aqtab* (the poles of sainthood, from Qutb); *wilayat* (sainthood, from wali); Ghaus al-Azam ("a title meaning the greatest helper", from Ghawth; not identified with any person, since the team version does not identify him); *ʿurs* (the yearly commemoration of the saint's death, the pilots' wording).

Team version's own: *Lakhdata* ("the giver of hundreds of thousands"); Sakhi Sarwar ("the Generous Chief"); *nafs-e ammara* (the commanding self).

Plain dictionary senses: Hind (as India was called); Shaikh al-Shuyukh (the master of masters); *darbar* (as the shrine is called); *kos* (the old measure of road distance); *suba* (province); *jagir* (a grant of land and its revenue); *diwan* (minister of revenue); *nazr* (votive offering); Baisakhi (the spring harvest festival of the Punjab); *chauki* (described, not defined: "a building in his name"); *kotha* (house); *lagis* (the village servants who received a customary share of the harvest); *iftar* (the meal that breaks a fast); *milni* (the meeting of the two families at a wedding); *mukabbir* (who repeats aloud at prayer the call of God's greatness); chronogram (a verse whose letters, read as numbers, yield a date); AH ("by the Islamic calendar").
