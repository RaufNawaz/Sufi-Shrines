## Overview

In the old city of Multan stands the shrine of Hazrat Shah Yusuf Gardez, a Sufi, or Muslim mystic, who is counted among the earliest saints of the region and, by tradition, was a founding figure in the Islamisation of Multan. Coming from Gardez, in what is now Afghanistan, he settled in Multan when Islam was still taking root there, and founded the line of the Gardezi sayyids, a family descended from the Prophet Muhammad and long among the leading religious families of the city. His shrine, a low tomb famed for its exquisite blue tilework, is one of the most distinctive and revered monuments of Multan, the ‘City of Saints’, and it is a place of continuous pilgrimage.

## The Saint

Sayyid Yusuf, called Shah Yusuf Gardez, is remembered as having come from Gardez to Multan at a time when Islam was still taking root in the region. Tradition places his arrival in the years around 1088 and his death in the first half of the 1100s. A sayyid of the family of the Prophet, he is honoured as one of the first great saints of Multan.

He is remembered as a preacher whose presence helped make the city a centre of the faith even before the coming of the great Suhrawardi masters, the Sufis of the order founded by Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi. He founded the Gardezi sayyid family, which remained prominent in the religious and civic life of Multan for centuries. He died at Multan and was buried there.

## The Shrine

The shrine of Shah Yusuf Gardez is celebrated for its architecture as much as for its sanctity. It is a low, rectangular tomb sheathed in the brilliant blue and white glazed tilework of Multan known as *kashi*, one of the loveliest of the city's many shrines and among the oldest of its sacred monuments. It is kept by the Gardezi family.

The tomb is a place of continuous pilgrimage. Devotees come to recite the Fatiha, the opening chapter of the Quran, and to seek the blessing of one of Multan's founding saints, and his *ʿurs*, the yearly commemoration of the saint's death, gathers the faithful in the customary manner. In the reign of the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb, Multan was a city where many saints lay at rest, and Shah Yusuf Gardez was counted among the saints buried there whose tombs were places of pilgrimage.

## The Setting

In 711 the Arabs conquered Sind and the neighbouring provinces to the north as far as Multan. At one time the city was a dependency of the Fatimids of Egypt, and by the middle of the eleventh century Sufism had made its way into the country around Multan. It was into this world, tradition holds, that Shah Yusuf Gardez came. In the twelfth century many Sufi scholars made Multan their base. In 1175 Mu'izzu'd-Din Muhammad bin Sam seized the city, and in the early thirteenth century conversion gained momentum.

Multan also had its place in the region's pilgrimage and in its poetry. On reaching Multan, one sultan made the circuit of the tombs of its shaikhs, the Sufi masters of the city. For the poets of the vernacular languages, Mecca and Medina are inside the heart, and for the Sindhi and Punjabi poets, so is Multan.

## Legacy

Shah Yusuf Gardez holds an honoured place as one of the earliest saints of Multan, the founder of the Gardezi sayyids and a pioneer of Sufism in the Indus valley, whose coming, tradition holds, helped turn Multan toward the faith it would come to embody as the ‘City of Saints’. His tiled shrine remains a jewel of the old city and a living centre of devotion.
