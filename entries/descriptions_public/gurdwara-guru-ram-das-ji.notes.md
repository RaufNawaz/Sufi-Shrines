## Consensus choices

- **The bazaar:** Chuna Mandi, basis (a), the current site text. The 1880s name of the bazaar (perhaps Chowk Matti) is an uncertain reading and goes under rule 5; only "the southern lane of a bazaar" is kept from that sentence.
- **What stood on the spot:** "The Guru's childhood home is said to have survived on this spot until the era of the Sikh Empire", basis (a), framed "is said". The 1880s "small house" that stood there before the building is left out: set beside the childhood home it would read as the same house, which the team version expressly does not say. The parents' residence on this spot (Kanhaiya Lal) is kept, since it agrees with the birth and the childhood home.
- **When the gurdwara was built, and at whose wish:** "Accounts differ over when the gurdwara was built and at whose wish. Tradition relates that Maharaja Ranjit Singh ... was asked to raise a fitting shrine here by his wife, Maharani Datar Kaur, during the celebrations that attended the birth of their son Kharak Singh in 1801." Basis (a), the current site text, which the team version itself frames as "Tradition relates" and "according to the account". The one rival account (Kanhaiya Lal, 1884: in the last years of Ranjit Singh's rule, when the Guru's Sikhs turned their attention to the spot) is one source; following the Mithankot precedent (V1 round 1 accepted that one dissenting source is not "every other source"), (a) stands. Left out: "in the last years of Ranjit Singh's rule" and "the Guru's Sikhs turned their attention to it". See judgement call 1.
- **How the land was obtained:** "The Maharaja agreed and acquired the surrounding plots: by his order the houses of Mohalla Saudagran adjoining the spot were taken, the land was enlarged and the foundation laid, and the building was ready within one year." The site's "acquired the surrounding plots" (a) and the 1880s details are told as one building act, since the team version's disagreement section treats both as describing the same building (as in the Baoli Sahib row, V1 judgement call 6, accepted). "Mentions no payment" is an absence and is not stated. See judgement call 2.
- **Where the Guru grew up:** born and brought up here, basis (a) and (b) (the site text and Kanhaiya Lal against Khalid), with "Accounts of his boyhood differ, but this is revered as the place where he was both born and brought up." Left out: the village of Basarke near Amritsar, his selling boiled grain and wheat there as a child, and Amar Das first seeing him there.
- **His parents, and how he came to Amar Das:** "Orphaned young", basis (a) and (b) (the site text and Khalid). Left out: Kanhaiya Lal's parents leaving Lahore for Goindwal "by a turn of the times". Kept: "He came under the influence of Guru Amar Das" (site), Khalid's following the Guru to Khadur (the *langar*) and later to Goindwal, which nothing left in the text contradicts, and the marriage at Goindwal (Kanhaiya Lal), which no source contradicts (judgement call 5).
- **His relation to Amar Das:** son-in-law, basis (b) (Bhandari, Khalid in both books, Chishti p. 686, and the site's marriage to Amar Das's daughter). Chishti's "son (*khalaf*)" is dropped silently; that passage also rests on a name marked uncertain.
- **How many sons:** "Accounts differ on how many sons Ram Das had, but two of them, Prithi Chand and the younger Arjan, came to quarrel ...". Basis (d), no number given: Chishti gives three, Khalid speaks of "the Guru's two sons" in the dispute, which may name the two who quarrelled rather than count them all, so no range is offered either.
- **Whose land Ramdaspur stood on:** "The land is said to have been a huge tract that the Mughal emperor Akbar granted to Bibi Bhani ... when the emperor met Guru Amar Das." Treated as minor: two passages give the grant to Bibi Bhani, one calls it the Guru's *jagir*, itself a grant of land; the *jagir* wording is dropped silently. Also minor and dropped: Amar Das asking Ram Das to move to Ramdaspur, set against his founding it (the site text and two passages give the founding). "He laid the foundation of Ramdaspur, which in time became Amritsar" rests on the site text and on Khalid pp. 254, 257 and 274; "small town" is not used, because it belongs to the passage whose town name is cut off.
- **The gates of the walled city:** "most often counted as twelve", basis (b) (Chishti and *In Search of Shiva* against *A White Trail*). The Roshani Gate's position (between the Alamgiri Gate and the Royal Mosque) is kept without the count of thirteen.
- **The Guru's dates:** no disagreement (team notes): 1534 to 1581, Guru from 1574 to 1581, Bhandari's seven years agreeing.
- **Time anchors:** Kanhaiya Lal 1884 as "By the 1880s", "In the 1880s" and "At that time"; Bhandari as "In Aurangzeb's time" (regnal year 40). Chishti's bibliography date is unconfirmed, so his material is used with no time anchor at all (Har Das, the Sodhi Khatris, twelve years in the *langar*, Bibi Bhani, Prithi Chand's anger, the twelve gates, the *samadh*, Kharak Singh). The Holi assembly is "Once", since its "recently" is an uncertain reading. 1534, 1574, 1581, 1758, 1799, 1801 and 1947 as the team version gives them.
- **Spellings (minor):** Harmandir Sahib for the quoted "Harminder Sahib" (the spelling of the Baoli Sahib public row); Gobind Singh for "Gobind singh"; "guerrilla" (British) in a paraphrase of "guerilla"; "Panjab" kept inside the Shackle rendering.
