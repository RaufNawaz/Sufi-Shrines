## Consensus choices

- **The name:** Gurdwara Panjvin and Chhevin Patshahi, with the site's gloss "of the Fifth and Sixth Kingships", basis (a). The listing's "Panjvi Chati Patshahi" and the book's "Panjvin and Chevin Patshahi" are spelling variants, dropped silently. Janam Asthan is spelled as the site spells it (not Janamasthan or Janamsthan); Khusrau (not Khusro), Baba Farid (not Fareed), Shahjahan as the team version writes him.
- **Who came, and when; what happened at the spot:** basis (a), the site's account: both Gurus came to the town; Guru Hargobind visited in the month of *Har*, Samvat 1670 (1613), on his way back from Kashmir; his followers founded a *Gur Mela* where he rested; the site came in time to honour the fifth Guru too. One clause: "Accounts of the Gurus' visits differ in their details." The other telling (Arjan and Hargobind together, the horses tied to a Waan tree, the walk of about seven hundred metres to Janam Asthan) is not set beside it as a rival history; it is kept only as the tradition of the relic the gurdwara keeps, framed "It is told that" and "In this telling", and once more in the Legacy ("to which, it is told, father and son tied their horses"). Judgement call 1.
- **The buildings:** basis (a), two shrines within a common boundary wall, Guru Arjan's plain and without a dome, Guru Hargobind's domed. The features of *A White Trail* that do not conflict with the two shrines are kept as further facts: the *Nishan Sahib*, the single storey block of pilgrims' rooms, the *Thara Sahib* holding the *Guru Granth Sahib* with a fly whisk, and the small room above the shrine with the relic.
