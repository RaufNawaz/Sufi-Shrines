## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Name:** "most often named Sayyid Usman Marwandi", followers in Sehwan not agreeing on it. Basis (a) the current site text, with (b): Kasmani, Sorley and Rizvi all give Usman. The custodians' "most likely Shah Hasan" is a custodians' position (a), but the team version shows every other source against it, so (b) decides. Left out: Shah Hasan, Rizvi's "Mir Saiyid 'Usman" without Marwandi, Parsram's "Kalandar Lal Marwandi".
- **Birth year:** around 1177. Basis (a), the site text; the cataloguing heading in Kasmani's book agrees and no source gives another year. Nothing left out.
- **Birthplace:** "Accounts of his birthplace differ, but it is most often given as Marwand, in the Persian speaking lands to the west." Basis (a) and (b): the site, Kasmani (Marwand), Boivin (Persia). Left out: Rizvi's "native of Sehwan"; Kasmani's "near Tabriz" precision (the site places Marwand only in the region of Afghanistan and Iran).
- **Road to Sindh:** the site's wide travels (Iraq, the Hejaz, Central Asia) kept, basis (a), then "Accounts of his road to Sindh differ, but in the end he settled at Sehwan" (V1 round 1, issue 1: giving both as stages of one journey merged them). Left out: Kasmani's likely route (Multan in 1263, Makran, Lahut-lamakan, Panjgur, Pasni, Naing, Lake Manchhar).
- **Year of arrival at Sehwan:** "Accounts of the year of his arrival differ; the date considered more reliable is 1272." Basis (b)/(c): the only account that gives years ranks 1272 above the other. Left out: 1251.
- **Death year:** "Accounts of the year of his death differ, but it is most often given as 1274." Basis (a) and (b): the site, Kasmani and the cataloguing heading. Left out: Schimmel's index 1262.
- **Burial place:** Sehwan, basis (a) and (b). Left out: Rizvi's tomb "in Sind" only, the Waris Shah translation's "holy saint of Uch" and its annotator's "buried in Sind".
- **Why Lal and Shahbaz:** the site's explanations (the crimson robe of his order; the soaring freedom of his spirit), basis (a), with one clause "though accounts of how he came by these names differ". Left out: Rizvi's red dress and his Shahbaz as a title given by Bahauddin Zakariya; the fakir's Shahbaz from a miracle; Kasmani's lal (red, dear one) and laʿl (ruby), which V1 round 1 (issue 2) ruled a rival explanation of the name.
- **Order:** "There is no settled view of his order. The Suhrawardi and Qadiri orders both claim him, but he is remembered above all as a qalandar." Basis (d) for the orders, with (a) and (b) for qalandar (the site, Boivin's Qalandariyya, the fakirs, Sorley's kalandar, Rizvi's run of qalandars). Left out: Khalid's Malamti school as an order (Rizvi's simile "like the malamatis" kept as description); the custodians' "loose affiliation with some Qadiri strands".
- **Master:** "Accounts of his master differ too, but he is often said to have been initiated at Multan by the Suhrawardi master Bahauddin Zakariya." Basis (b), a plurality: Rizvi and the Suhrawardi tradition (as Kasmani reports it) name Bahauddin, against one telling each for Jamal al-Din Savi (many fakirs) and Shah Sadr (Boivin, "is said to have been" his master in Sindh). "Often", not "most often", because two of four is not most. No chain is given, so nothing is merged. Left out: Savi; Shah Sadr as master (his shrine at the Tando Ahmad Khan entrance is kept, without the relationship); the fourfold shave remark.
- **Four friends:** the site's wording, basis (a). Left out: Khalid's view that Bahauddin and Farid would have had serious differences with him.
- **Successor:** Sayyid Ali Sarmast, from whom Qutub Ali Shah descended on the maternal side, kept (Boivin; uncontested). Added after V1 round 1, issue 3.
- **Other names:** Raja Bharthari or Raja Vir (Boivin), Jhule Lal (Kasmani) and Jivelal (Sorley) given as names he bears, with no claim of fusion. Left out: Udero Lal (rests on one parenthesis and one index cross reference, with no ground given); the spelling Barthari.
