## Consensus choices

- **The goddess's name** (team disagreement 1): the temple's name as the site writes it, Shri Laxmi Narayan Mandir, and the goddess as Lakshmi, the site's spelling for the deity. Basis (a). Boivin's "Lakhshmi" and Khalid's "Laxmi" alone are minor spelling variants, dropped silently; the hyphenated "Lakshmi-Narayan" of the team prose becomes "Lakshmi and Narayan" (the pairing) or "Lakshmi Narayan" (the dedication), so that no hyphen of our own appears.
- **How many Hindus live in Pakistan and Sindh** (team disagreement 2): no real conflict, since the figures are for different scopes. Given together: Pakistan's Hindus "put at between 6 and 10 million, an estimate that varies widely", "over 90 per cent of them" in underprivileged tribes and subcastes; in Sindh "several million", the majority in the scheduled castes. Basis (b), with Schaflechner's own "varies widely" as the one clause; the census of 1998 (largest minority) is kept from the Setting. No figure chosen or averaged.
- **How many Hindus left Sindh at Partition** (team disagreement 3): basis (d), a range: "estimates of how many left in all differ, from around a million to 800,000 or fewer". Only one book speaks, and it gives both its own figure ("closer to 0.8 million, if not less") and "a kind of consensus" of about a million, so neither is chosen. Boivin's "exactly 0.9 million" Hindus in 1941 is left out (judgement call 7); the 21 per cent of 1941 and the 246,000 who had left by early November 1947 are kept.
- **Whether Sindh knew caste** (team disagreement 4): basis (b): "It is often claimed that Sindh had no caste system, but there were many untouchables in Sindh." Boivin states it outright and Schaflechner treats the "no caste" view as a perception tied to the Lohanas and to nationalist portrayals; only Thakur's quoted line holds the other view.
- **Whether Sindh was a land without communal strife** (team disagreement 5): basis (b), with one clause: "Accounts differ, too, over whether Sindh was free of communal strife; its communal violence never reached the level of the Punjab's, but it was not absent."
