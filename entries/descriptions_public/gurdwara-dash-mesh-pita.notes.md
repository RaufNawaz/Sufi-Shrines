## Consensus choices

- **His place in the line of Gurus** (team item 1): "the tenth Sikh Guru ... and the last of the Gurus". Basis (a), the site's account ("the tenth Sikh Guru"), and (b), Chishti, Khalid and Boivin. The annotator of Usborne's Waris Shah ("the tenth successor of Guru Nanak") is dropped as a minor variant: it is a way of counting, and no source makes him an eleventh Guru. Khalid's "the ninth incarnation of Guru Nanak" is kept, because it agrees with the tenth Guru (Nanak himself and nine after him); see judgement call 7.
- **His name** (item 2): Guru Gobind Singh, basis (a) and (b). Rafat's spelling 'Guru Govind Singh' dropped as minor. Bhandari's Guru Gobind Rai is left out entirely (see Left out and For Rauf).
- **His death** (item 3): "Guru Gobind Singh died in 1708. Accounts of his death differ; it is said that he was assassinated by a Pathan, or Pashtun, horse trader." Year: basis (b), Boivin and both Khalid books (Chishti's Samvat 1765 is marked uncertain, rule 5). Manner: basis (b) that he was killed (Khalid; Chishti's "killed in the end" on p. 686), and the Pathan horse trader is the only account that names a killer; "a Pathan horse trader" (the chronology) chosen over "Pathan horse traders" as a minor variant. Left out: Chishti's "went mad from that same grief and died" (p. 846), the partly illegible year on p. 686 and the table's date (rule 5).
- **His sons** (item 4): "He had four sons, among them Jujhar Singh and Fateh Singh, and he saw all of them die at the hands of the Mughals in his own lifetime, in 1705; accounts of their deaths differ." Basis (b): both Khalid books (all his sons; four sons). 1705 is the only year given (the *Walking with Nanak* chronology). Left out: *A White Trail*'s "martyred fighting the Mughal army" against "assassinated" (the manner), and Chishti's two sons killed, which the clause covers. The two names come from the Jhaman dome ("his sons, Jujhar Singh and Fateh Singh") and are kept with the family (rule 7).
- **When the line of Gurus was closed** (item 5): basis (d): "Accounts differ on when he made the declaration; it is placed between the founding of the Khalsa in 1699 and 1708." Two accounts by one author in two books; no shrine position, no majority, no account devoted to this site. Left out: "after he saw the death of all of his sons" (the 1708 account's reason).
- **Where he lived** (item 6): Anandpur, a city his father had founded (Khalid), with no clause. The rivals rest on readings the team version marks uncertain: Chishti's hill village (perhaps Amadpur) and Bhandari's Makhowal, which also belongs to the Gobind Rai the team version does not identify. With them removed under rule 5, nothing contradicts Anandpur.
- **His birthday** (item 7): minor (a day's difference, rule 4 third bullet). Given as "When the birthday was celebrated at Nankana Sahib in January 2011", with no day. Dropped silently: 10 against 11 January 2011, Pal Singh Purewal's calendar, Jathedar Puran Singh's previous calendar against the SGPC's new one.
- **Whether Sindh's followers of Nanak are Sikhs** (item 8): basis (d): "There is no settled view of whether Sindh's followers of Nanak are to be counted as Sikhs." No site position; the sources pull both ways. Left out: Boivin's Singh Sabha sentence ("not included in the Sikh community", "a Hindu religious sect"), Khalid's "thousands of such Sikhs" and "Nanak Panthi Sikhs" (the public text says only "Nanak Panthis" and "followers of Nanak"), and Boivin's endnote calling the Udasis of Sindh "a heterodox Sikh tradition", which would take a side. Kept, as the illustration of why there is no settled view: Schaflechner's census fact (Lohanas of Shikarpur and Hyderabad counted as Sikhs in 1881 and as Hindus in 1891); see judgement call 27.
- **Birth** (not a listed disagreement): "born at Patna in 1666, the son of Guru Tegh Bahadur". Patna is from Chishti's table, where the place is not marked uncertain; 1666 is from the modern books (Boivin, both Khalid books). The table's Samvat year, day and month, and the mother's name Gujri, are marked uncertain and left out (rule 5).
- **The Khalsa, told twice by Khalid**: one telling. The two tellings agree ("in reality, it was a goat's blood"; "In reality however, all were safe"), so they are given together. The five Ks follow *A White Trail*'s list (Kesh, Kanga, Kara, Kachera, kirpan); *Walking with Nanak*'s list has two words marked damaged.
- **The Janamasthan banner**: the two descriptions (silk gown, pearl necklaces, a tiara adorned with jewels; a turban that looks like a tiara) are given as one banner. They do not conflict.
- **Sadhu Bela**: the site's spelling is used for Schaflechner's Sadh Bela island *tirtha*; the team notes (item 6) treat Sadhu Bela, Sadh Bela and Sadh Belo as spellings of one place.
- **Time anchors**: Chishti (1867, "the date unconfirmed") only as "In the nineteenth century" (the Shahid Ganj *granth*); his attendants Bhai Mani Singh and Gulab Singh are not anchored. Bhandari's pursuit of Dara carries no year, as in the team version. Sorley's "modern Sind" (1940) is kept as "modern Sindh", with "Towards the second half of the eighteenth century" as the team version gives it. All other dates are the team version's own (1666, 1675, 1699, 1705, 1708, 1746, 1872, 1881, 1891, 1925, December 1939 to February 1940, January 2011).
