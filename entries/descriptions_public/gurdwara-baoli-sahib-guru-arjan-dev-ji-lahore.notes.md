## Consensus choices

- **How the baoli was founded:** the community's tradition (Bhai Chhajju Bhagat and a Pathan merchant lay a purse of gold *mohars* at the Guru's feet, to be spent for religious purposes; the Guru has the *baoli* sunk), basis (a), the site's account, with one clause, "Accounts of how and when the well came to be made differ." Qaiser's dispute settled before the Guru and Sheikh's 282 *mohars* are woven in, framed "it is said", because the team version itself calls Qaiser's version "the same origin story" and Sheikh's figure the size of "the disputed purse". Left out: Kanhaiya Lal's treasure story (the Sikh of Guru Arjan who sold his house to a woman for three hundred rupees, the hoard and the jewelled canopied bed, the refusal, the Qazi, the emperor sending the treasure to the Guru).
- **When it was founded:** the year of the Guru's visit, 1599, basis (a), in the Overview only, covered by the same clause. Left out: Qaiser's Samvat 1616 and Kanhaiya Lal's "in the time of the Emperor Jahangir".
- **When and how it was lost:** the site's account (the turbulence of the eighteenth century; filled in and built over during the governorship of Nawab Yahya Khan, at the instigation of Diwan Jaspat Rai), basis (a), framed "Tradition at the gurdwara holds", with "Accounts of when and how the well was lost also differ." Basis (a) holds because the team version does not show every other source against it: it calls the 1962 memoir's mosque on the site "not reconciled" with the site's account, not contrary to it. Left out: the memoir's mosque on the site between the era of the Gurus and Ranjit Singh's reign; Kanhaiya Lal's loss under Shah Jahan (Guru Hargobind's clash with the Qazi and the emperor, the confiscation, the mosque built on the site of the *langar*, the five guards killed and thrown into the well, the houses built over it).
- **How it was recovered:** "Accounts of how it came to light differ; it is most often told that the Maharaja, when he was ill, was advised by his astrologers to bathe in the baoli's water, and that the restoration followed his recovery." Basis (b) (Sheikh and the 1962 memoir against Kanhaiya Lal), and (a) in effect, since the site's account carries Sheikh's version. Left out: Kanhaiya Lal's dream (the aged Sikh to whom Guru Arjan appeared, and the variant that the Guru came in the Maharaja's own dream), and "at last the baoli appeared".
- **The date of the rebuilding:** 1834 (a new building and a *sarovar*), basis (a). Kanhaiya Lal's Bikrami 1896 (illness and dream) and Bikrami 1890 (start of work) are uncertain readings and go under rule 5. His facts that do not depend on those years are kept as part of the same Ranjit Singh rebuilding: the month's salary of the Khalsa deducted to pay for it, the houses over the well levelled, shops, sitting places and *kothras* built, the work slow and unfinished at the Maharaja's death (see judgement call 6).
- **Where the baoli stands:** Dabbi Bazaar, its precinct once adjoining the Loha, Kesera and Guru bazaars, basis (a). Kanhaiya Lal's first account agrees in substance (the iron sellers' bazaar to the east, the Kasera bazaar to the south) and is used for the 1880s description. His second placing (a house of a baoli adjoining the Sunehri Masjid in Kashmiri Bazaar) is left out with the mosque story it belongs to.
- **Guru Arjan's dates:** born 1563, Guru from 1581, "accounts of the year of his death differ, but it is most often given as 1606". Basis (b): *Walking with Nanak* and *A White Trail* (1563 to 1606) and Dhillon (Guru to 1606) against Alam's 1605. Bhandari's "twenty-five years" after succeeding is not given (it adds nothing and is not a year).
- **His place in the line:** fifth, basis (b); the single "fourth Sikh Guru" in *A White Trail* is a minor slip, dropped silently. For the same reason the team version's "in the Granth's naming, 'Arjan becomes the fourth Nanak'" is left out: it sits against "fifth Guru" with nothing to explain it, and a reader would take it as a contradiction.
- **Why he died:** the summons by Jahangir over his part in Prince Khusrau's rebellion, the *tilak* given to Khusrau and the emperor's own words, with "Accounts of why he died differ, but his death is most often tied to Khusrau's revolt." Basis (b): Khalid's narrative in both books and Jahangir's memoir as quoted. Left out: the Sikh tradition of the connivance of Chandu Lal, Diwan of Lahore, allied with Prithi Chand, and its variant of Chandu Shah, whose daughter's marriage to the Guru's son was refused; and the remark that the Mughal sources are silent on Chandu Lal (see judgement call 8).
- **His end and his body:** Sikh belief (the dip in the Ravi on the fifth day, the miracle, Mian Mir's offer refused) and the yellow *samadhi* near Lahore Fort where "it is believed that the Guru lost his life", both framed as belief, with "Accounts of his end differ." Basis (b) for the river (two of three accounts). Left out: "His body was then thrown into the river" and *A White Trail*'s "his remains interred".
- **Spellings (minor, dropped silently):** Khusrau throughout for Khusro; Mian Mir for Mian Meer; Baba Farid and Pakpattan for Baba Fareed and Pak Pattan; Shah Hussain for Shah Hussein; Hargobind for Har Gobind; Arjan for Arjun.

## Judgement calls for V1

1. **Kanhaiya Lal's 1880s description is told as the 1880s description of this gurdwara** ("By the 1880s the house of Baoli Sahib ..."; The Gurdwara; Devotion and Practice; the Legacy's tank and steps). The team version frames the account as Kanhaiya Lal's own, but gives it throughout as the account of "the house of Baoli Sahib", notes that its bazaars match the site's Loha and Kesera bazaars, and itself uses it in its Legacy for this baoli; its doubt concerns the second passage (Kashmiri Bazaar), which is left out. Fallback: open The Gurdwara with "The house of Baoli Sahib described in the 1880s ..." (no other change needed).
2. **The founding story combines the site's account with Qaiser's dispute and Sheikh's 282 mohars**, framed "it is said", on the team version's statement that Qaiser "gives the same origin story" and its phrase "the disputed purse". The sentence does not say who disputed or why. Fallback: delete "The purse, it is said, held 282 *mohars* and had been in dispute, and the dispute was settled before the Guru."
3. **Kanhaiya Lal's guard and langar are kept beside the community's founding story:** "It is told, too, that the Guru set five Sikhs to guard his well, and that he himself sometimes stayed there", and the *langar* in the building to the south. These follow his treasure story in the team version but do not depend on it, and contradict nothing in the site's account. "Five" is one named Sikh (name uncertain) and four others, and the team version's own "its five guards". Fallback: delete the paragraph's first sentence, or the whole paragraph (then the Legacy's "of a *langar* held beside it" must go too).
4. **Loss, basis (a):** the site's Yahya Khan and Jaspat Rai account, with a clause; both mosque accounts dropped. If V1 holds that the 1962 memoir and Kanhaiya Lal together show every other source against the site's account (rule 4 (a)'s exception), the alternative under (b) is that a mosque stood on the site until Ranjit Singh's reign, with no date for the loss (basis (d)).
5. **Recovery:** "it is most often told" for the astrologers rests on two accounts (Sheikh and the 1962 memoir) against one (Kanhaiya Lal's dream).
6. **The rebuilding joins the site's 1834 to Kanhaiya Lal's undated details** (the Khalsa salary, the houses levelled, the shops, sitting places and *kothras*, the slow work unfinished at the Maharaja's death). The team version lists the rebuilding date as a disagreement, but Kanhaiya Lal's two years are both uncertain readings, and once they go nothing in his account sets a date against 1834. "for five years" is left out so that the text implies no end date that no source gives; and the demolition of the mosque on the site of the *langar-khana* is left out because it belongs to his rival loss story (the shops "where it had stood" become simply "were built"). Fallback: move the Kanhaiya Lal sentences after "In 1834 ..." into a separate sentence opening "Under Ranjit Singh", or cut them.
7. **Guru Arjan's death year:** "accounts of the year of his death differ, but it is most often given as 1606" (three statements of 1606 in two books plus Dhillon's end of guruship, against Alam's 1605).
8. **Why he died:** Khusrau's revolt as the most often given reason, the Chandu Lal tradition dropped. This is a rule 4 choice, but it removes a Sikh tradition from a Sikh shrine's page; if V1 or Rauf prefers, the alternative is a framed sentence "Sikh tradition also lays a part in his death to Chandu Lal, the Diwan of Lahore" in place of the clause, which would be a second account side by side.

Fallback under strict rule 5: delete "Near Lahore Fort a small yellow structure is now called his *samadhi*, a memorial shrine, and it is believed that the Guru lost his life there."
10. **The meeting with an emperor:** the team version marks the Guru's name uncertain at its first occurrence in Bhandari ("the halting-place of Guru Arjan, the name uncertain in the printed text"), but names Guru Arjan without a flag in the following sentences (the *peshkash*, the petition), and calls him "successor of Baba Nanak". 13. **The 1880s assembly:** "whenever a gathering of any kind took place in the Hindu religion, this was the building chosen" is the team version's wording, kept although a visitor may be puzzled by "Hindu" at a gurdwara.
