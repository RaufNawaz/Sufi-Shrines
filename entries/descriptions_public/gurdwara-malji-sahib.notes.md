## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **How the miracle happened:** the site's telling (Rai Bular, riding through his fields with his attendants, sees the cobra's hood over the sleeping boy's face; the cobra withdraws as the men approach), each sentence framed, with "Accounts of how the wonder unfolded differ." Basis (a), the site text. Left out: the 1962 register's fixed shadow seen before the cobra; the cobra emerging when the sun fell on his face, with no witness; "encountered a snake".
- **Cattle or buffaloes:** cattle. Basis (a) with (b) (site, 1962 register, Dhillon). Minor; Khalid's buffaloes dropped silently.
- **Where it stands:** the site's placing (near the railway station, close to Kiara Sahib, the two forming the eastern cluster), with "about five hundred metres from Gurdwara Janam Asthan" (A White Trail, which also gives the railway station) and "furthest from the Janam Asthan" (Walking with Nanak, with Kiara Sahib) kept as compatible placings, in separate sentences. Basis (a). Left out: nothing. See Points for V1, 2.
- **Who honoured the boy:** the site's ending of the legend (Rai Bular became a devoted follower and later granted him half of his estate), framed "is said to". Basis (a): not every other source is against it (none denies it; the gift of land is only absent elsewhere). Left out: Rai Bhoe looking on Nanak "as his Guru" in 1506 and spending time with him every day (the rival honourer). Kept as not contradicting it: Rai Bular sick and failing on a later return, and Rai Bhoe as Rai Bular's father, both inside the janamsakhi frame.
- **The Guru's birth, year and place:** "Accounts of his birth differ in their details, but Guru Nanak is generally held to have been born at Talwandi in 1469." Basis (b): 1469 in Khalid and Dhillon; Talwandi in every account but Meherban's claim, the others "unanimous". Left out: 20 October and 14 April; Bhandari's Bikrami 1526, 880 Hijri and Bahlul Lodi; Dera Chahal.
- **The Guru's death:** "on 22 September 1539, though accounts of the date differ". Basis (b) for 1539 (Khalid, Dhillon, the blurb on Dhillon's book); the day from Dhillon, the only source that gives a day. Left out: Bhandari's reign of Salim Shah Afghan and his age between seventy and eighty.
- **How his birth came about:** Mata Tripta's prayers at the ancient Hindu temple on a mound near Rai Bhoi di Talwandi, framed "Tradition tells", with "accounts of how his birth came about differ". Basis (a) read as the custodians' community: this is a Sikh shrine, and the team version gives the rival as the Muslim tradition that "The Sikhs don't accept". Left out: Baba Naulakha Hazari's prayer (the Auqaf booklet, Iqbal Qaiser) and the reconstruction's Tripta among the sadhus and fakirs of the forest. See Points for V1, 3.
- **Age of his first wonders:** "in childhood". Minor; the editor's footnote "at the age of ten" dropped silently.
- **The Sufi of his youth:** Sayyad Hussain, inside the janamsakhi frame, with "accounts of the dervish's name differ". Basis (c) as the nearest: Dhillon retells the janamsakhis, the tradition of the Guru's life; the rival Sayyed Hassan stands only in a modern reconstruction that the team version calls partly fictionalised. Left out: Sayyed Hassan "who lived near his house". See Points for V1, 4.
- **His wife and marriage:** Sulakhini, daughter of Mul Chand and Chando Rani of the town of Batala, on 24 September 1487, "though accounts of the marriage differ". Basis (b): Batala in Dhillon and Bhandari; the marriage before the journeys in Dhillon and the reconstruction; Mul Chand in Dhillon and the reconstruction. Left out: Pakhoke near Gurdaspur; Bhandari's marriage at Batala after travelling through most of the world; the spelling Sulakhni (minor).
- **His younger son's name:** Lakhmi Das. Basis (b) (Dhillon; Bhandari's Lakhmidas). Lachman Das and "Sri Lachman" dropped silently as spelling variants.
- **How many journeys:** "Accounts of how many journeys he made differ, but they are most often counted as four." Basis (b): the more modern accounts and Dhillon himself against the Puratan Janamsakhi's five. The span "about twenty four years" stays inside Khalid's own sentence on where the journeys went and is not joined to the count of four. Left out: the five journeys of 1499 to 1524; Dhillon's twenty three years (minor).
