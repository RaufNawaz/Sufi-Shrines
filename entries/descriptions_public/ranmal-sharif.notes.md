## Consensus choices

- **Where he is buried:** Ranmal Sharif, with one clause: "Accounts of the year of his death differ, as do accounts of where he lies. ... he rests at Ranmal Sharif, which became the seat of his line." Basis (a), the site's account. The team version does not show every other source against it: the editor of the 1875 tazkira places the mazar at Sahnpal Sharif, district Gujrat, but the only other source, the nineteenth century passage naming Shahpur and Sahn Pal, is damaged, prints the place names uncertainly and does not make clear whose mazar each clause names (rule 5, so it cannot count either way). Sahnpal is not named as a burial place; Sahanpal appears only as the seat of the order's manuscript library and in the chronicler's name Sahnpalvi, which the team version gives as facts. See For Rauf 1.
- **Death year:** "Accounts of the year of his death differ ... The year his shrine keeps is 1654". Basis (a): the site's 1654; not every other source is against it (the editor's 12 Rabi al-Awwal 1064 AH and his list's 1653 CE stand near it; only the author's 1103 AH stands far from it). No Hijri date converted; the variants (1103 AH, 12 Rabi al-Awwal 1064 AH, 1653 CE) are not listed. The Overview's "(1552 to 1654)" carries the site's years; the clause stands where the death is told.
- **Birth year:** 1552, plainly, with no clause. Basis (a), the site's account. The only other dating is the editor's 1 Ramadan 959 AH, a Hijri date and not a rival Common Era year; giving it would require a conversion no source makes, and a clause would tell the reader of a dispute the team version does not show beyond the difference of calendar. Judgement call 3.
- **His name:** "Hazrat Syed Muhammad Noushah Ganj Bakhsh", basis (a), with "he is also known as Haji Muhammad" (the personal name in the index of the 1875 tazkira, and the name the team version's disciples' passages use for him). The tazkira's heading "Shaikh Haji Muhammad Qadiri" is not set beside "Syed"; "Syed" is not glossed, so the public text makes no claim of descent beyond the name the shrine uses. See For Rauf 2.
- **Who began the order:** he founded the Naushahia, with no clause. Basis (a) and (b): the site's account and the tazkira's own entry on him ("the Naushahia path ... began with him"). The rival passage (p. 50) says only that the line *flourished* in the Punjab through a "blessed person" whose pronoun may be Shah Maruf or Sayyid Mubarak Haqqani; it does not credit anyone else with founding it, and it rests on an ambiguous referent. Judgement call 5.
- **Where he withdrew as a young man:** "in a reed bed of the Sandal Bar or the Gondal Bar, for accounts differ over which." Basis (d): the 1875 author writes Sandal Bar and his modern editor corrects it to Gondal Bar; there is no shrine position, no majority and no earlier account devoted to the site, so neither is chosen. The third account (a vast empty wilderness, study at a village near Nowshera) is told under Legends as tradition, without a sequence that would set it before or after the reed bed (judgement call 7); its village name Hakothara is uncertain and dropped.
- **His mother's name:** Bibi Jiwani (the 1875 tazkira, given twice and indexed). The variant Mulla Bi Jiwan is an uncertain reading (rule 5) and is dropped silently.
- **His father's title:** Haji Ala al-Din (the tazkira, which also says he made the Hajj seven times); "Shaikh Ala al-Din" in the other book is a minor variant, dropped.
- **The chain through Pir Muhammad Sachyar:** the five step chain as the 1875 tazkira gives it cleanly (the saint, Pir Muhammad Sachyar, Shah Farid Lahori, Muhammad Siddiq, Rahman Shah, Fazl Shah). The other book's version (Hazrat Bajibar, Shah Farid) is printed uncertainly (rule 5) and is dropped; nothing from it is hung on the clean chain.
- **Shaikh Mittha's death and name; Shah Abdullah's, Nanu's, Hafiz Tahir's and Pak Rahman's deaths; Pak Rahman's birth:** no year given for any disciple. In each case the author and the editor disagree, the editor's figure is often itself printed uncertainly (Nanu 1094 AH, Hafiz Tahir 1097 AH, Pak Rahman 1115 AH, Mittha's spelling), and the team notes record that a human must decide which tradition the row follows before the disciples' dates enter it (team notes, decision 5). The editor's own publication list gives "Lahore, 1971" after a sixty four page study printed beside it, so the 1971 may belong to the study; not recorded as a disagreement in the team version; treated as minor, the list's entry (with its 1653 CE and its foreword) not used, so as not to merge the two notices.

## Glosses

From `data/glossary.csv`: the Qadiri order (founded by Abd al-Qadir Gilani); *faqirs* (Sufi mystics; for the Hindu party, "ascetics", its plain sense); *wali* (a friend of God); *karamat* (the miraculous deeds of a saint); *zikr*, the glossary's *dhikr* (the remembrance of God); *bai'at*, the glossary's *bay'a* (the oath by which a disciple pledges himself to a *pir*); *pir* (a spiritual guide); *majzubs* (men absorbed in divine love); *sama* (spiritual listening, the Sufi session of music, Mithankot's wording); *hal* (the state of ecstasy, the team version's own); *qawwali* (the devotional music of the Sufis); *langar* (the free communal kitchen); *ʿurs* (the commemoration of the saint's death); *qutb* (the axis of the saints in his time).

Pilots' wording: *jazb* (the rapture of a soul drawn towards God); *pakka* (of fired brick); *sajjada nashin* (the custodian who sits on the prayer carpet, here "of the saint buried there", since the Lahore tomb's saint is another man and "hereditary" is not supported).

Team version's own: *Ganj Bakhsh* ('the bestower of treasure'); *Noushah* ('the bridegroom'); *pir-e fayyaz* ('the bountiful pir'); *pahari* (the old mound of Phalia).

Plain dictionary senses: *darbar* (shrine); *sukr* (spiritual intoxication); the Hajj (the pilgrimage to Mecca); *lagan* (customary wedding dues, the sense of lāg); *khalifa* (a spiritual deputy); *naat* (poetry in praise of the Prophet); *mansabdar* (an officer holding rank); chronogram (a verse whose letters, counted as numbers, give a year); *crore* (ten million); the *dalan* and *kothri* of the Lahore tomb as "a hall" and "a small room".
