## Consensus choices

- **Who built Rohtas Fort.** Sher Shah Suri as its builder (the site's account, basis (a)), in the legend paragraph; in the fort section, the fort founded by Sher Shah "while he was still known as Sher Khan" and completed by his successor Islam Shah, and raised by the two of them on the Gakhar frontier. No "accounts differ" clause: the team version itself has Sher Khan take the name Sher Shah, so the site's builder and the founder are the same man, and completion by Islam Shah does not deny that Sher Shah built it. The three statements are compatible and are given as one sequence, not as rival accounts (judgement call 4).
- **What the gurdwara is called.** Gurdwara Choa Sahib, "the gurdwara of the exalted spring", basis (a). The unnamed "Nanak's Gurdwara in Jhelum, [near] Rohtas Fort" of the two visits is not given as a variant name; the visits are told as of this gurdwara, as the team version tells them in its own section "The Gurdwara", having identified the building by its place beside the fort (judgement call 2).
- **Which journey brought the Guru here.** The fourth *udasi*, basis (a): "The gurdwara's tradition sets the visit on the fourth of the Guru's *udasis*". The number of journeys: "Accounts of how many journeys he made differ, but they are most often counted as four", basis (b) (the modern accounts and Dhillon himself against the Puratan Janamsakhi's five), as on Rori Sahib and Babay De Ber. Their length: "more than twenty years", which holds both Khalid's about twenty four and Dhillon's twenty three and builds no figure. Left out, so that no date for the visit is built from the schemes (the Bhai Lalo precedent): Dhillon's two date ranges for the fourth journey (1519 to 1521, 1518 to 1521) and his clause that the journey closed around Babur's third incursion. The janamsakhi route of the fourth journey is kept; it names Tilla Balnath, which is not identified with Tilla Jogian, since the team version does not identify them, and nothing is said of Rohtas being absent from it.
- **The Guru's dates.** "Accounts of his dates differ, but they are most often given as 1469 to 1539", basis (b) (the site, Khalid, Dhillon against Bhandari) and (a). The Overview carries the site's "(1469 to 1539)" without the clause, which stands where his life is told (the Mithankot pattern). Dropped: Dhillon's 14 April 1469 and 22 September 1539 (minor, as on the sibling rows) and Bhandari's hearsay birth in Samvat 1526 or 880 AH under Bahlul Lodi and death under Salim Shah Afghan at seventy to eighty.
- **Ranjit Singh's accession.** "crowned ruler of the Punjab in 1801" (*A White Trail*); the other book's "became the sovereign of Punjab at the end of the eighteenth century" dropped as a minor variant of the same event. His dates 1780 to 1839 kept.
- **Bahat and Bihat.** The same river spelled two ways in the team version; Bihat used throughout (minor, a spelling).
- **Time anchors.** Bhandari (composed in regnal year 40 of Aurangzeb, a firm date) is anchored "In Aurangzeb's time", as on the sibling rows. The two visits to the gurdwara are undated in the team version; they are placed "In those years" of neglect, which the bibliography date of *Walking with Nanak* (2016) before the 2019 reopening supports, and nothing narrower is said. *A White Trail* (2013) gives "In more recent times" for the festival pilgrims. Babur's third invasion keeps Rizvi's 1520 (the Hijri 926 not given). The fair on *Kattak Suddi* is told in the past tense with no date.
