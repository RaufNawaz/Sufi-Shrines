## Consensus choices

- **His name:** Mir Mohammad, with the titles Mian Mir and Shah Mir and the epithet Bala Pir. Basis (a), the shrine's "Mir Mohammad of Sindh"; Lahori's heading "Shaikh Muhammad Mir" and the editor's "Mir Muhammad" treated as minor variants of the same name and dropped silently; Kanhaiya Lal's contents "Mian Munir" is an uncertain reading (rule 5). The other Bala Pir (Sayyid Muhammad Bala Pir Gilani) kept as a namesake not to be confused with him.
- **Birthplace:** Sehwan, also called Sivastan, in Sindh, between Thatta and Bhakkar. Basis (a). Sewistan, the city of Sistan and Sehwan Sharif treated as spellings of the same place and dropped; Kanhaiya Lal's silence is not a disagreement.
- **Birth year:** "born in the sixteenth century ... Accounts of the year differ; it is most often given as 1550." The shrine gives only the century (a). For the year, basis (b): 957 AH / 1550 (the editor, on Dara Shikoh's authority) and 1550 (Khalid) against Lahori's 960; Chishti's 957 is an uncertain reading and is not counted.
- **Father's name:** Qazi Sain Ditta, basis (b): both books give Ditta against the shrine's Datta (minor spelling).
- **Brothers:** Lahori's four names (Mian Qazi, Qazi Usman, Qazi Tahir, Qazi Muhammad). Chishti's differing name (Qazi Tawakkul) is an uncertain reading, so no real conflict remains; his other two names agree with Lahori.
- **Initiation and master:** "Accounts of his initiation differ, but his master is remembered at the shrine as Shaikh Siyustani and is most often named Shaikh Khizr Sistani." Basis (a) for the shrine's name, (b) for Khizr Sistani (Lahori, Chishti, Kanhaiya Lal). Lahori's account of a first *bay'a* at his mother's hand is the variant behind "accounts differ" and is not told side by side. The sentence treats Siyustani and Khizr Sistani as names of the same master (both are "of Sistan/Siwistan", the team version itself treating Sewistan and Sistan as spellings of Sehwan; both give him the *khilafat* and send him to Lahore); V1 round 1 ruled this supported.
- **Chain of masters:** only Lahori's chain is given, and it is hung on Shaikh Khizr Sistani, where Lahori starts it ("His Qadiri line runs from Shaikh Khizr Sistani back through Sayyid Abdul Razzaq ... to Abdul Razzaq's father, Abd al-Qadir Jilani"). Ernst and Lawrence's line (through 'Abd al-Qadir II and 'Abd Allah Bhiti to Mian Mir) is not merged with it: only its point is kept, that Dara Shikoh set aside another lineage and acknowledged the line that came down to Mian Mir and then to Mullah Shah, without the upper links (rule 4, no merged chains). Downwards, Lahori's chain no. 152 is given in order as V1 round 1 worded it: from Mullah Shah to Shaikh Muhsin Shah, "and from him to" Shah Abd al-Rashid Lahori, "and further down to" Sayyid Bhawan Shah (Shah Bilaq, an uncertain name, dropped; "further down" marks the gap).
- **Lifespan:** "By tradition Mian Mir lived some eighty eight years", basis (a). Lahori's eighty seven treated as minor (a year) and dropped; Chishti's ninety eight is an uncertain reading.
- **Death:** "He died in the reign of Shah Jahan, in 1635 (1045 AH), the year the Auqaf Department holds and most accounts give, though a few place his death in 1645." Basis (a) and (b) together (Lahori, Kanhaiya Lal's reign, Rafat, Ernst and Lawrence, Schimmel, Khalid). The hour and day (the midday prayer on 7 Rabi al-Awwal) are kept from Chishti, since only his year is illegible; after V1 round 1 (note 5) they stand in their own sentence, so his day is not joined to a year he cannot be shown to give.
- **Where he died:** in his cell, basis (a). Chishti's report that he died at the Anarkali chilla house rests on a word printed uncertainly, so it is left out (rule 5) and no disagreement remains.
- **The breath:** the shrine's telling (from the Isha prayer until dawn), basis (a). Lahori's single breath a night and Chishti's one, two and later four breaths are minor variants of the same practice and are dropped silently. Lahori's and Chishti's shared point, that he never slept at night, is kept, with Lahori's fasting, framed "He is said".
- **Where he kept his chilla:** not treated as a conflict, since the two places do not exclude each other: the retreat at Data Ganj Bakhsh's *khanqah* is kept as the shrine gives it ("He is said to have kept"), and the Anarkali house is kept as a place "known as Mian Mir's chilla", without the damaged sentence about the lamps and his retreat there (see Left out).
- **Dara Shikoh's relation to him:** "Accounts describe the bond in different ways, but the prince's own guide is most often named as Mullah Shah, who drew him into the Qadiri way and introduced him to the saint, so that Mian Mir was his *dada murshid*, his guide's guide." Basis (a) (the shrine: Mullah Shah his guide, Dara drawn through him) and (b) (Kanhaiya Lal, Chishti, Ernst and Lawrence, Schimmel's "introduced"). Rafat's "among his disciples" and Schimmel's "first mystical guide" are the variants behind the clause.
- **Jahanara's guide:** Mullah Shah, basis (a), with which Schimmel agrees ("her master Molla Shah"); Schimmel's "first mystical guide" for Mian Mir dropped as the variant.
- **The foundation stone of the Golden Temple:** kept as Sikh tradition, with one clause: "The contemporary Persian chronicles do not mention the episode, and historians differ over it; but Mian Mir's cordial and brotherly relations with the fifth Guru are well attested". The Madanjit Kaur, Sirhindi faction and rebuttal exchange is not set out. Rafat's "Guru Arjun was a follower" is not used (the stronger claim, against the shrine's "friend").
- **The offer against the Guru's oppressors:** "destroy" kept, "bury alive" dropped as the minor variant of the same story.
- **Order of Dara Shikoh's buildings:** "among the first of them a *chowk* ... and the lofty tomb of Mian Mir", without the order (minor).
- **The red stone road:** kept as Dara Shikoh's plan cut short by Aurangzeb (Kanhaiya Lal), with neither endpoint: one account's starting point is an uncertain name and the other names the fort; treated as minor.
- **What the tomb is built of:** "Accounts differ over the stone it is built of; the shrine describes a mausoleum of red sandstone." Basis (a). The nineteenth century description is kept part by part (marble stair, marble door panel in a red stone frame, marble floor inlaid with black stone, interior marble work of Dara Shikoh's time, marble grave platform, *ta'wiz* and *kalas*), but its sentence "the tomb itself is of marble" is the variant and is not repeated.
- **Whose enclosure holds the village:** Mullah Shah's, basis (b) (Kanhaiya Lal's first passage, Lahori's Mullah Shah entry, Chishti's garden); Kanhaiya Lal's other passage dropped silently.
- **The stone and the great mosque:** the shrine's tradition (a) that Aurangzeb drew on stone from the complex for the Badshahi Mosque is kept as tradition; Kanhaiya Lal's leftover stone for "a Jami Masjid" is not set beside it, since the team version does not say the two mosques are the same.
- **The Sikh period stripping:** the shrine's statement (a). Lahori's sentence about stones carried to Amritsar stands in Mullah Shah's entry and may mean Mullah Shah's tomb, so it is left out (with its "several lakh rupees").
- **Where the shrine stands:** Dharampura (Hashimpura), once the garden suburb of Baghbanpura, basis (a); Khanpura given as another name for Baghbanpura, as the shrine gives it.
- **The ʿurs:** 7 February, hundreds of thousands, basis (a). Kanhaiya Lal's five fairs are given as the nineteenth century calendar, not as a rival date; the word for the yearly fair ("urs") is an uncertain reading, so it is called only "one yearly gathering".
- **State of the building:** told as a sequence, not a conflict: the shrine "endured as a place of devotion" (a); late in the twentieth century it had fallen to pieces and was then restored and made a mosque with Friday prayer (Abbas, 2002, "almost a decade ago").
- **Burials of Dara Shikoh and Nadira Begum:** Nadira Begum near the mausoleum (the shrine and Rafat); the prince's own burial there given as the shrine's position ("the shrine holds"), since only the shrine says it. Chishti's placement on the east is left out (the name is printed uncertainly).
- **Bibi Jamal Khatun's death:** basis (d): "Accounts differ over the year of her death, which they place between 1000 and 1057 AH" (Lahori 1000; Dara Shikoh via the editor 1057; Schimmel's 1639 falls inside the range; Chishti's 1055 is an uncertain reading).
- **Bibi Jamal Khatun's burial:** "most place her grave in Lahore" (Lahori, Chishti, the editor's table against Dara Shikoh's Sehwan, basis b), "By the 1870s ... within the enclosure of her brother's *khanqah*" (the editor's 1875 column) and the 1974 column given as a later description of the place. Her son Hazrat Muhammad Sharif, the first *sajjada nashin*, kept (only the year in that register row is uncertain).
