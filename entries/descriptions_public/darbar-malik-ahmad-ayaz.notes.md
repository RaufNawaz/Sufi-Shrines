## Consensus choices

- **His name:** "Malik Ahmad Ayaz" (the shrine's name, basis a), "also known simply as Malik Ayaz" (the survey's other form and the form of the histories), and "Shaikh Ayaz Lahori" as the name under which he was honoured among the saints in the nineteenth century. These are names, not rival identities, so no clause. The survey's "Malik Ayaz Ahmad" is dropped as a minor variant; the bare "Ayaz" is used as the short form.
- **His origin:** the shrine's account (educated in Georgia in 993 AD, enslaved when his country was attacked), basis (a), with the clause "Accounts of where Ayaz came from differ." Basis (a) holds because the team version does not show every other source against it: "Turkish officer" and "the Turk" describe him rather than name a homeland; only the Kashmir story names a rival one. The Kashmir story (introduced in its source with "they say") is dropped; "the Turk" stands in a garbled sentence and is dropped; "Turkish officer" survives only inside the Legacy quotation. V1 to rule (judgement call 1).
- **Where and how Mahmud bought him:** the shrine's account (the Baghdad slave market, passed over as too weak, ten thousand dinars), basis (a). The Badakhshan merchant, the crowds at Ghazni and the "heavy price, what the merchant asked and more" are dropped. The Sultan's preference for him (intimate of the private assembly, foremost of his many thousands of slaves, the noose of love) does not conflict and is kept.
- **His looks:** minor (rule 4, third bullet). The shrine's "too weak to attract buyers" is kept; the likeness to Yusuf of Canaan and the crowds come with the dropped Ghazni purchase; the partly legible description of him is dropped under rule 5. The poets' view that he had no great outward beauty but matchless character is kept in "Ayaz in Poetry", since it agrees with the kept account.
- **His office:** not treated as a contest, since no source says the offices exclude one another. Soldier and minister (the shrine), *atalik* to Majdud (one history), *hakim* of the Punjab (another), the Sultan's intimate (a third) are told as parts of one career in their own settings; "governor of Lahore" in the Overview rests on the survey's "Governor Lahore" and "When Ayaz governed Lahore". The editor's "commander of the cantonment", offered "in their place", is dropped. V1 to rule whether this is a merged account (judgement call 4).
- **The order of their deaths:** Ayaz outlived Mahmud, basis (a) and (b): the shrine itself says that after Mahmud Ghaznavi's death he left his residence, and the history that follows him through the reigns of Masud and Maudud agrees; Mahmud's death is dated 1030. The shrine's timing of the throne story "in Ayaz's last days" is the one detail that conflicts, and it is dropped; the story itself is kept as a legend ("Mahmud is also said to have wished ..."), with "one of his two sons, Saad" as ambiguous as the team version keeps it (judgement call 3).
- **The year of his death:** "He died in 450 AH, though accounts of the year differ." Basis (c). Basis (a) fails: the team version shows every other dated source against the shrine's 8 August 1041 ("The survey's date agrees with neither year"). Basis (b) gives no majority (1041; 450 AH; 449 AH, given as 1057 CE on Ibn Athir's authority; and Firishta's undated "a few days after Maudud's death"). Basis (c): the earliest account devoted to him is the 1875 entry of his own, which gives 450 AH. The shrine's 1041, the statement that the mausoleum was built "then", the editor's 449 AH and 1057 CE, and Firishta are not listed. The building of the tomb is kept as "after the death of Malik Ayaz". No Hijri year is converted. If V1 prefers basis (d), the fix is "There is no settled year of his death" with no year, since a range would need a conversion no source gives (judgement call 2).
- **Where he is buried:** "buried inside the city of Lahore" (the 1875 entry) and the shrine's tradition that the tomb was built "at this very place", basis (a); the two do not conflict. The appendix line placing Shaikh Ayaz Lahori at Chowk Rang Mahal is dropped: a different landmark that the team version says "may be the same site" and does not tie to this tomb (rule 4, minor, and an identification it does not make). The classing of a "Tomb of Malik Ayaz" among the buildings inside the city is dropped under rule 5 (it rests on a page figure the team version marks uncertain and a contents line out of sequence).
- **The refounding of Lahore:** he restored and resettled the city, basis (a) and (b) (the shrine, the tradition that counts him its founder, the history of the gates, the Persian history's fort of baked brick), with one clause: "The tradition that he refounded Lahore is not universally accepted, and has been dismissed as 'later writers' conjecture'." The editor's replacement office is dropped (above).
- **The romance of king and slave:** the bond as love, basis (a) and (b) (the shrine and the poetry), with one clause: "though the romance itself has also been regarded as a literary growth of post-Seljuq times".
- **The old clothes:** minor. The shrine's "each night" is kept in the life; the Rūmī allegory is kept in Legends without its "every morning", as habitual "would look".
- **Ayaz's counsel at a temple:** both stories kept as separate legends in separate paragraphs (the shrine's "Onkay temple" and the magnet; Somnath and the rajas' crores), with nothing said either way about whether they are one episode, since the team version says the books do not make them one. V1 to rule whether two paragraphs count as side by side (judgement call 6).
- **The manner of his death:** the shrine's own two tellings, poisoned out of resentment or a natural death, kept in one clause ("though in some places it is said").
- **Time anchors:** the 1875 entry (firm) and the nineteenth century history of Lahore with the Somnath and Hira Singh material (dated "1867, the date unconfirmed") are both anchored only to "the nineteenth century". This row's bibliography item for *Tarikh-e Lahore* carries no date, so nothing from it is anchored in time. Mahmud's death (1030) and the death year 450 AH are given as events.

## Judgement calls for V1

1. Origin: the shrine's Georgia account kept under basis (a) with "Accounts of where Ayaz came from differ", on the reading that "Turkish officer" and "the Turk" describe him without naming a homeland, so that only the Kashmir story is a rival account. If V1 holds that every other source is against the shrine, the fallback is to drop "educated in Georgia in 993 AD" and begin "Tradition at the shrine holds that he was enslaved and taken for sale to the slave market of Baghdad".
2. Death year: "He died in 450 AH, though accounts of the year differ" (basis c, the earliest account devoted to him). The shrine's 8 August 1041 is not given anywhere, nor that the mausoleum was built "then".
3. The throne story: "Mahmud is also said to have wished to hand his own place to Ayaz; Ayaz declined, asking that it go instead to one of his two sons, Saad, and the king relinquished his power." The survey's "In Ayaz's last days" is dropped so that the legend does not contradict "Ayaz outlived his king", which the shrine itself also says ("after Mahmud Ghaznavi's death he left his residence"). The legend still sits beside the account of Masud's succession; it is framed as said, not as history.
4. Offices told as one career (soldier, minister, *atalik* to Majdud, *hakim* of the Punjab, the Sultan's intimate) rather than resolved; "Ayaz outlived his king. After Mahmud's death in 1030, Masud took the throne" joins the date (from the book on mystical poetry) to the history of the succession.
5. "in the nineteenth century he was honoured among the saints as Shaikh Ayaz Lahori": rests on the 1875 entry of his own in a book of saints, and the appendix's saint's honorific (*quddisa sirruhu*).
6. The two temple stories in separate paragraphs, with "Another story sets Ayaz's counsel at Somnath", saying nothing about whether they are one episode.
7. 8. "rajas and maharajas" for the survey's "Raja Maharaja".
9. "when his country was attacked" for "when 'the infidels attacked your country'"; "the unbelievers could never sway him" for "'he did not fall for the infidels'".
10. "It was into this resettled city that Ali Hajveri, the saint of Data Darbar, arrived": the team version's "the setting into which Ali Hajveri arrives"; Data Darbar as Darbar Ali Hajveri Ganj Bakhsh follows the team version's heading "Shah Alam Market and Data Darbar".

13. "At the court of love, it was said, king and dervish are equal, and in its presence master and slave are peers": keeps the team version's reading of "its" as love (the team notes say it could be "his").
