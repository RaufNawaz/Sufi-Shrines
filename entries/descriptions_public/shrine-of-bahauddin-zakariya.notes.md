## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Birth year and place:** "born around 1170 at Kot Karor, near Multan; accounts of the year of his birth differ". Basis (a), the site's around 1170 near Multan, with (b) behind it (Sharib's 566 AH, the editor's 566 AH / 1170 CE, Khalid's 1170); Kot Karor from Sharib, the editor (Kot Kahror) and Bhandari. Left out: Lahori's own text (578 AH, no place), Bhandari's 565 AH, Rizvi's index 1182-83. Sharib's Friday morning, 27 Ramazan is kept in a sentence of its own ("He is said to have been born"), joined to no Hijri year.
- **Death year:** "He died at Multan in 1267, full of years and honour; accounts of the year of his death differ." Basis (a), the site's 1267, with Lahori's text and Sharib (666 AH), Schimmel's *As Through a Veil* and her index, Boivin and Kasmani; not every source is against it. No rival year is named. Left out: the editor's 661 AH and his three authorities, Bhandari's 660 AH, Rizvi's 661 / 21 December 1262, Eaton's 1263, Schimmel's c. 1265 and c. 1262, the publisher's 566 to 661.
- **Day of death:** "The day of his death was the seventh of Safar", in its own sentence; every source that gives a day gives 7 Safar (Sharib, Bhandari, Rizvi). Age a hundred (Sharib, unopposed) and death on reading a sealed letter (Bhandari, unopposed) under one "He is said to have".
- **Descent:** "a Qureshi family of Arab descent" (a), then "Accounts of the family's descent within the Quraish differ, but Bahauddin is held to have been a Hashimi", the one ruling the team version records (Sharib settles it). Left out: the Mahyar bin Aswad line (first and fifth names uncertain), "only al-Qurashi al-Asadi", Asad of Quraish as Ali's maternal grandfather, Chishti's triple *nisba* (words uncertain).
- **His mother's father:** "most often named Maulana Husam al-Din Tirmizi", basis (b): Lahori and Sharib's first account, against Sharib's second (Hazrat Isa of the progeny of Ghaus al-Azam, at a place whose name is unreadable), which is left out.
- **Kunya:** Abu Muhammad, minor branch (the form Sharib gives first); Abu al-Barakat dropped silently.
- **Age at memorising the Quran:** minor branch; "As a boy he memorised the Quran". Left out: Sharib's at seven with the seven readings, Lahori's at twelve.
- **Route of his studies:** basis (a), the site's Khurasan, Bukhara, Hejaz and Medina, with Sharib's account of the same route (b), under "Accounts of his route differ". Sharib's own list of the dervishes he met (Khurasan, Bukhara, Mecca, Medina, Bait al-Muqaddas) kept. Left out: Lahori's route (Bukhara for *sarf*, *nahw*, hadith and *tafsir*, the Haramain, Bait al-Maqdis, Baghdad) and Bhandari's Iran and Turan.
- **Time with his master:** the site's "remarkable speed" (a) with Lahori's "eighteen days", under "it is said". Sharib's seventy days (Nizam al-Din's report) left out under rule 5, its number being uncertain, and with it the green wood and dry wood answer, which belongs to that report's timeline (V1 round 1, issue 1).
- **His place in the order:** he "planted the Suhrawardi order in the soil of the subcontinent" and is "the master who first gave the Suhrawardi order an Indian home" (the site), the disciple and *khalifa* of Shihab-ud-Din (Lahori, Sharib, Bhandari, Chishti). The word "founded" for the order is not used. Left out: the site's "the Suhrawardi order he founded", Rafat's "the founder of the Suhrawardiya school", Schimmel's "favoured pupil" (it comes from the rejected story of Iraqi's investiture).
- **How he became Shaikh al-Islam:** basis (d): "under Iltutmish he became *Shaikh al-Islam* ...; accounts of how the office came to him differ". The unopposed facts kept: good terms with Iltutmish, support against Qabacha. Left out: acceptance at Iltutmish's request (the editor, citing Nizami) and the removal of Najm al-Din Sughra[OCR?] for a false slander (Sharib).
- **Why he parted from Jalal al-Din Tabrizi:** basis (d): "where the two parted; accounts of why they parted differ". Left out: the rebuke over Attar (Sharib), prayer against sightseeing (Rizvi).
- **The trial at Delhi:** told once, on Sharib's fuller telling, which Rizvi repeats; Rizvi's gloss of *mahzar* used as the definition. Left out: Rizvi's "ran to receive him" and "the rivalry" wording (same content, second telling).
- **What sustained his house:** a composite of four compatible statements, as V1 round 1 (issue 2) ruled, with Rizvi's "mainly" dropped so that no one source is made the chief: "He accepted endowments and gifts and managed considerable resources (the site). He was a landlord of means (Schimmel), agriculture and trade were carried on at his house on a large scale (Sharib), and his *khanqah* prospered on gifts from merchants and trade guilds: many merchants traded outside India, were largely independent of the government, and could not be prevented from giving (Rizvi)." The "accounts differ" clause is gone. Left out: Rizvi's "mainly" and his governors "often short of funds".
- **His table and who came to him:** the site's "charity and hospitality on a grand scale" (a), with "though accounts differ over how freely his table and his company were open to all". Lahori's kings and high and low, thousands of attendants and lakhs who benefited, kept as the scale of the household (c, the earliest account devoted to him), the lakhs under "it is said". Left out: Sharib's "*langar* open to all", Rizvi's "offered to only a select few" and his fixed sums in charity, Schimmel's fixed hours for invited visitors, Rizvi's "concerned only with the élite" and his doubt about faith cures.
- **Music:** basis (d) for his attitude with the unopposed practice kept: "Accounts of his attitude to music and poetry differ, but *qawwals* ... performed in his gatherings" (Abbas's Abdullah from Turkey; the *qawwal* Abu Bakr). Left out: Sharib's "fond of *sama'*", Rizvi's "opposed to poetry and music".
- **The answer to Hamid ad-Din:** worded so as to claim neither side: "Hamid ad-Din was left unsatisfied". Left out: Rizvi's "Although the latter replied" and the letter's "did not reply, or if you did reply".
- **Iraqi's years at Multan:** "nearly twenty five years", basis (b) (Schimmel's twenty five and nearly twenty five; Rizvi's about 1239 to about 1263 is the same span). Rizvi's two years left out (his 1263 would put Iraqi's departure before the death year kept here).
- **Iraqi's investiture:** Rizvi's account (the complaint, the ruling, training declared complete, his own *khirqa*, his daughter, *khalifa* before his death), basis (b): the site's "married into his family" agrees with it. Left out: Schimmel's telling of the same episode (ten days in seclusion, the song on the eleventh, the Suhrawardi rule, "prohibited to them, but not to him", "His affair is complete", the mystic robe) and her verdict "with the spiritual insight of a true saint".
- **Why and when Iraqi left:** "Soon after his master's death Iraqi left Multan for Konya", basis (b), Schimmel's two books. Left out: Rizvi's jealousy of the other disciples and "about 1263".
- **Lal Shahbaz:** his disciple, basis (b) (Sharib's list of *khalifas*, Rizvi's adoption, Kasmani's Suhrawardi tradition of initiation at Multan), with "accounts of the relations between the two men differ" for Khalid's "would have had serious differences". Left out: Khalid's sentence; Rizvi's "and named".
- **The estate:** "On his death his estate was divided" (Lahori) and Sadr al-Din's "share" (Sharib), basis (b). The sum is left out: Sharib's seventy lakh rests on a unit marked uncertain, and Rizvi's 700,000 differs from it; Rizvi's "alone inherited" dropped as minor. Of the two reasons for the gift to the poor, the one printed as the heir's own (Sharib) is kept; Jamali's, via Rizvi, left out.

- **Punjab or Sind:** the site's Punjab framing (a); Schimmel's border of Sind and the Punjab left out. Kasmani's "southern Punjab" is used only for the shrines of the three named friends.

## Time anchors

- Lahori, 1875 (firm): "by the 1870s his family lived in Lahore and in the village of Dholanwal" (Shah Rahmatullah's descendants); "The nineteenth century mufti Ghulam Sarwar Lahori".
- Bhandari (bibliography: composed in regnal year 40 of Aurangzeb): "In Aurangzeb's reign" (the tomb inside the fort under its dome; the description of Multan); "By Aurangzeb's reign many wondrous and astonishing stories". No decade given.
- Schimmel, *Mystical Dimensions of Islam*, 1975 (firm): "In the 1970s the love poems of Fakhr-ud-Din Iraqi were still being sung".
- Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, 2016 (firm): present tense (patron saint; one of the most popular Muslim shrines beside the Prahlad temple; the posters, the banner and the mosque story at Uch).
- Khalid, *A White Trail* (no date in the bibliography): past tense only ("Thousands of devotees visited the shrine every day"; "became the symbol"; traditions "once moved freely"; Parvati Devi "said"); the 2006 and 2008 are the years of the events she names.
- Dhillon: "By the time Guru Nanak came to Multan" (his own anchor).
- The site's account (current): present tense (pilgrims come throughout the year; a working shrine and the seat of the *makhdooms*).
- Chishti (1867, the date unconfirmed): no time anchor used (the Khan-e Khanan's offering, Sakhi Sarwar).
- Kanhaiya Lal (no date): no anchor; "under Muhammad Tughlaq" is his own.
- Sharib (1965 / 1999): no anchor needed; his material is the saint's life and legends.
