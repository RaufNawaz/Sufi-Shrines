# Public description notes: `bhit-bhit-shah`

Source: `entries/descriptions/bhit-bhit-shah.md` (team version) only, with glosses from `data/glossary.csv` or plain dictionary senses. Brief: `docs/prompts/PUBLIC_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md`. English checker (`check_public_description.py bhit-bhit-shah --english-only`): ok, team 100,535 to public 43,203 characters of prose (0.43).

Why the ratio is 0.43 and not higher: the team version is long chiefly because it is a study of the scholarship on the poet. Whole paragraphs set out what Sorley, Schimmel, Shackle, Boivin and Schaflechner each think of him (rankings against the Persian masters, verdicts on his style, his learning, his sect, the worth of the legends), a 22 point disagreement list, the history of the editions, glossaries and translations, and the framing of the In Verse anthology. Rule 4 also removes several variant tellings (the itineraries, the second ending of the sect answer, the second restorer of the text). Every fact about the man, his family, his circle, his teaching, the shrine, its building, inscription, burials and relics, the music, the devotion, the legends and the setting is kept, with 31 of the team version's 33 italic verse paragraphs and the original script line. No L1 exception is needed.

## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Dates of birth and death:** 1689 and 1752, "Accounts of his dates differ slightly, but most give ...". Basis (a) the site text (1689 to 1752) and (b) Shackle, Schimmel, Boivin and Sorley's appendix. Left out: Schaflechner's 1690 to 1751 and 1689 to 1753; the Library of Congress "approximately"; Sorley's two hedges.
- **Hijri year of death:** the dome chronogram kept with its value, "Accounts of the year of his death by the Hijri calendar differ, and this chronogram gives it as 1165". Basis (c): the shrine carries both readings (the date Shackle says is recorded at the tomb; the chronogram on the dome as Schimmel gives it), so (a) does not settle it, and (b) is one against one; Schimmel's *Pain and Grace* (1976) is the earliest account devoted to the poet that gives a Hijri year (Sorley gives none). Left out: Shackle's "14 Safar a.h. 1166 (1752 CE)", including the day, which is not joined to 1165 (that would build one date from two sources). Safar is kept as the month of the ʿurs.
- **When the tomb was built:** basis (d), "Accounts of when it was raised differ, and there is no consensus: it is dated between 1754 and 1765." The site gives no date; Sorley's 1754 ("according to the tradition") and Schimmel's 1765 are one each; neither is an account devoted to the site, and Sorley's own chronology (with printed years damaged) unsettles 1754. Shackle gives no date. Builder (Ghulam Shah Kalhoro, at his expense) uncontested. Left out: the link of 1754 to Nur Muhammad Kalhoro's death.
- **Generations from Shah Abdul Karim:** basis (a), the site's "ancestor", with "Accounts differ on how many generations separated the two men". Two against two. Left out: great great grandfather (Shackle, Sorley) and great grandson (Boivin, Schimmel's *As Through a Veil*). His death in 1623 (Shackle) kept; Sorley's "flourished about A.D. 1600" dropped as minor.
- **Birthplace:** basis (b), "it is most often given as Hala" (Boivin; Schimmel's *Mystical Dimensions*; *Pain and Grace* has the family "near Hala"). Left out: "of Matiari" in Sorley's description of the family (the family is kept as his notable Sayyid house descended from Ali, without the place). Sorley's three villages of residence (Hala Haweli, Kotri, Bhit Shah) are kept as a separate fact: they name where he lived, not where he was born. See Points for V1.
- **Father's name:** Shah Habib, basis (b) (Schimmel, and Sorley once). Left out: Sayid Habib Shah and Habib Khan (Sorley), and the years 1742 and 1754 in Sorley's footnote (rule 5, digits imperfectly printed). The burial a few paces from the tomb is a clear reading and is kept.
- **Where Bhit Shah lies:** basis (a), the site's "Matiari region ... near the town of Hala", with Schimmel's "north east of Hyderabad", which stands in the same sentence as her "near Hala" and agrees with the site. Left out: Abbas's "located in Hala"; Shackle's "near Hyderabad in lower Sindh" (compatible, minor).
- **The ground at Bhit:** the site's sandhill (a) with Sorley's single detailed description (built on sandhills, unattractive as a residence, set amid striking scenery by the Kirar lake). The site's adjective "barren" and Shackle's "in the desert" are left out, since set beside Sorley's greenery they would read as a contradiction.
- **How far and how long he travelled:** the site's "wandered for years ... through the deserts and mountains of Sindh and beyond" (a), then "Accounts differ on how far and for how long he travelled with them, but he is most often said to have gone with them as far as Hinglaj" (b: Schimmel three times, Boivin "probably", Schaflechner "according to Sindhi history" and "It is said", against Sorley's doubt of Gurbuxani). The Overview gives "the pilgrimage he is said to have made to Hinglaj" at the same certainty. Left out: three years (Schimmel); three more years after Hinglaj, to Dwarka, Lakhpat, Lahoot and Jaisalmer (Schaflechner after Agha); Dwarka (Boivin); Gurbuxani's Lakhpat, Kabul and Kandahar; Sorley's "he did not travel far"; Abida Parvin's telling of a journey to Rajasthan (one telling's itinerary, which would extend the list).
- **Education:** basis (b), trained in the Islamic sciences through Arabic and Persian, with a knowledge of both far beyond the ordinary (Shackle, Sorley, Schimmel agree). Left out: the popular tradition of no regular schooling, Qani's "illiterate", Sorley's later "not learned in the narrow scholastic sense". The alif story stays in Legends, framed as a legend.
- **His answer on sect:** "between the two", basis (b) (Sorley, Shackle and Schimmel all have it). Left out: Shackle's continuation, "I am that nothing" (a second ending of the anecdote); Sorley's inference "more a Shia than a Sunni"; Schimmel's reading as *tashayyu' hasan*. The intended pilgrimage to Karbala and the Kedaro ("is said") kept from Sorley.
- **Who restored the lost text:** the Kirar lake legend, with Tamar and Hashim rewriting the whole from memory. Basis (c), the earliest telling (Gidumal, as Sorley gives it in 1940). Left out: Abbas's Mai Naimat, the maidservant from whose memory the whole *Risalo* is said to have been reconstructed (the team version lists the two as rival answers to one question; see Points for V1).
- **When the singing is held:** nightly, every evening, basis (a) the site with (b) Abbas. The weekly gathering: basis (d), "though accounts differ on its day". Left out: Thursday evening (Schimmel, in two books) and Friday (Sorley), and the "few dervishes" of 1976. The 1882 vigil is placed at "one of these gatherings", without a day.
- **Whether each Sur is named for its mode:** basis (a) the site with (b) Schimmel. Left out: Sorley's "not all of them are named after the tunes" and his clause on poems not named after a mode; his remark on modes is kept with his own "some of the Surs".
- **How many Surs, and how many for the heroines:** thirty, basis (b) (Abbas; Schimmel's Bombay edition; *Pain and Grace*'s "thirty Sindhi poems"); "at least ten" for the heroines (Abbas's main text; her endnote also says ten). Left out: the longer Hyderabad edition of 1974; Abbas's glossary's nine and the endnote's list of seven; Sorley's Muntakhab of 27.
- **Unity of being:** basis (a), the site's *wahdat al-wujud*, with Boivin's facts (he did not use the expression; his word was *wahdat*, *hekro*) and his remark on its reach among peoples. Left out: Sorley's "God and man are things apart" and his "love ... is merely the means of approaching the divine".
- **Beauty of the building:** basis (a), the site's "one of the most beautiful and most visited shrines". Left out: Schimmel's "not outstanding as a work of architecture" and "peculiar charm".
- **Edition and book dates** (the Bombay edition's year, Lilaram Watanmal's 1889 against Lalwani's 1890, Qalich Beg's 1913 against Kalich Beg's 1914): all left out as book apparatus; the glossaries are kept without names or years ("more than two decades after the first printing").
- **Trumpp's printing:** 1866, basis (b) (Schimmel's *Pain and Grace*, Boivin). Left out: *As Through a Veil*'s 1867 for his remarks.
- **The invader who received the saints' dust:** basis (d), "Accounts differ on which invader it was." Left out: Ahmad Shah Abdali (*Pain and Grace*) and Nadir Shah in 1739 (*Mystical Dimensions*).
- **The Masnavi reconciliation:** "a Kalhoro prince", not identified with Nur Muhammad Kalhoro, since the team version names no prince there.
- **Spellings unified (minor):** Sassui (Sasui, Sassi), Marui (Marvi), Sohni (Suhini), Punhun (Punnu), Uvaisi (uwaisi), Karbala (Kerbela), Hasan (Hassan), Husain (Husayn), Muharram (Moharram), *tambooro* (tambura, tambūrā), *wai* (waʾi, vāī), *Masnavi* (Masnawi, Mathnawi), Nur Muhammad Bhatti (printed "Niir"); verse keeps its own spellings.
- **Translator credits:** Christopher Shackle where the team version says "Shackle's translation" or "In Shackle's translation"; H. T. Sorley where it says "Sorley's version", "Sorley's English" or "Sorley's translation"; Annemarie Schimmel for "In her translation", "Schimmel translates", and the five In Verse passages the team version introduces as "Schimmel's translations ... in the translators' own words". No credit for the Sur Asa verse ("Schimmel quotes"), the chronogram, or the cotton spinners' lines ("Abbas quotes").

## Time anchors

- Sorley (first published 1940, firm): "in 1940" for Shah Abdul Karim's name still held in reverence, the mausoleum still drawing its crowds, the relics still preserved, and the Legacy paragraph on the spell of the poems; "By 1940" for Hala Haweli and Kotri in ruins. His description of Bhit "in the poet's time" is told in the past, as he gives it.
- Schimmel (*Pain and Grace* 1976, *Mystical Dimensions* 1975, both firm): "By the 1970s" for the building as she describes it (minaret, tiles, tulip pillars, entrance ceiling, graveyard) and for the guest house and the cultural centre being developed; "In the 1970s" for the melodies well known all over Sindh and for the cloth on the trees ("still in our day").
- Abbas (2002): no decade anchor. The caretaker of the female relatives' tombs and the train of women are told in the past; the falsetto singing every evening, the *qawwali* and the black worn by the *faqirs* stand in the present because the site's own text keeps the nightly singing in the present.
- Kasmani (2022): "Up to the late 1960s" as given.
- Boivin (2019): 23 May 1957 and April 2016 as given; the Hindus' daily visits and the local *sajjada nashin* in his present.
- Shackle (2018, reprinted 2025): the present for the Kirar depression and for the name Bhit Shah.
- Years given in the team version and kept as given: 1623, 1699 (not used), 1711, 1713, 1792, the 1850s, 1866, the middle of the nineteenth century, 1882.
- Burton: the team version gives him only as "a century and a quarter earlier" than Schimmel; not converted to a date, told in the past.
- Putu, Shaikh Ghulam Hussain, Sikander Baloch, Alan Faqir, Abdul Qadir Mangi: no anchor, told in the past.
