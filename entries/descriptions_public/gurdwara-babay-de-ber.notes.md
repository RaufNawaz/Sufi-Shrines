## Consensus choices

- **The name:** Gurdwara Babay De Ber, "the gurdwara of the elder's jujube tree", also Gurdwara Beri Sahib or Babe di Ber, basis (a). Baba Bair, "the shrine of the Berry saint", and the *mohalla* of Baba Bair are kept as the place's name in the city, not as a rival: the team version identifies Khalid's Baba Bair with this gurdwara, and Khalid derives the name from Guru Nanak. Pir Bairi is kept only as the claim of the man at the site about the grave (judgement calls 3 and 4).
- **Where it stands:** the old city of Sialkot, near the ancient fort, basis (a), in the Overview; one clause in The Gurdwara, "though accounts of exactly where in the city it lies differ". Left out: the policeman's "away from the ancient city of Sialkot" and "on top of a mound".
- **The Guru's road to Sialkot:** "coming by way of Jammu", basis (a), inside the legend. Treated as minor (a detail of the legend's route): Khalid's reconstruction (on foot from Eminabad) and Dhillon's "from Lahore" and "between Lahore and Bhatinda" dropped silently. Dhillon's placing of the faqir's story on the second journey is kept, since it names no road.
- **The faqir's grievance:** the site's broken promise and the faqir withdrawn in anger, basis (a). Added as compatible detail, framed ("some tellings", "it is said", "In the *janamsakhis*"): Khalid's Hindu man who broke his promise of a firstborn son and the forty days of prayer before the threatened destruction; Dhillon's base at the foot of an old *beri* tree, the curse pronounced in the market square, and almost the entire city seeing the Guru off. Precedent: Gurdwara Khoohi Bhai Lalo, where one compatible detail of another telling was added to the site's. See judgement call 5.
- **How the Guru met him:** the site's telling (the Guru sang the divine word beneath the tree and the sound drew Hamza Ghaus out), basis (a), with "The story is told in more than one way". Treated as a legend detail and dropped silently: Khalid's Nanak knocking on the faqir's door for days and the thunder striking the dome; Dhillon's faqir coming to the tree and the two sitting side by side in its shade.
- **The spelling of the faqir's name:** Hamza Ghaus (the site and Dhillon), basis (b); Khalid's Ghous dropped.
- **Why the tree is holy:** because the Guru sat beneath it, basis (a), throughout. The man's claim that the grave is that of Pir Bairi, whom Guru Nanak came to visit, is kept as his claim; his "Very old" is dropped because the grave was observed to be new. Left out: Khalid's "apocryphal", his explanation that a grave "of this non-existent saint was constructed" after the gurdwara was abandoned, his suggestion that the tree was sacred before Nanak came, and the reconstruction's Hindu woman praying at the tree.
- **The state of the gurdwara:** the site's position, renovated and formally reopened for worship, the Guru Granth Sahib installed in the sanctum, pilgrims paying their respects at the tree, basis (a): the only other account is Khalid's, one source against it and not every other source (the Mithankot reading of rule 4 (a)). One clause: "though accounts of its condition since then differ". Left out as the direct rival statements: "far from ready to become functional", Khalid having been told the gurdwara was functioning and finding it "quite different", "the building had been Islamised", the new tiles "rotting away", and the floor "recently completed". Kept as plain undated observations in the past tense: the board, the building, the tree, the floor tiles, the pictures of Hindu deities, the grave, the red flag, the man's Pir Bairi claim, the scribbled invocations and the new mosque. See judgement call 3.
- **Its history after 1947:** told as one sequence, since the items do not contradict one another: abandoned in 1947 at Partition (the man at the site and Khalid), damaged in the disturbances after 1992, access restored around 2012, renovated in 2012 (the board), formally reopened (the site). See judgement call 7.
- **Who founded Sialkot:** basis (d), "Accounts of its founding differ", with both: Raja Sela, uncle of the Pandavas, with Raja Satya Vachan's rebuilding (Dhillon), and, under the name Salkot, Raja Salbahan, of whom a fort of fired brick was held to be the memorial (Bhandari). Two sources each once the reconstruction's Salivahan is counted, so neither (b) nor (c) settles it. Left out: Bhandari's Raja Shal (name and the next word uncertain) and the Mahabharata dating that goes with him; the reconstruction's Salivahan as father of Puran Bhagat.
- **When Ghur took the city:** minor at city level. Both years (Dhillon's 1192, Bhandari's 580 Hijri) left out rather than set side by side; the common core told once: the city passed into the Muslim Sultanate under Shahabuddin Muhammad Ghori, who repaired its old fort and left his army there. Left out: Dhillon's city spared, Bhandari's order that it be laid waste and its people resettled at Bahlulpur, Daulat Khan and Ghazi Khan's seizure; Rizvi's year 926/1520, so that no second dating stands beside the *Babur Bani* line's 1521 (Rori Sahib precedent).
- **The Guru's family:** Sulakhini, daughter of Mul Chand and Chando Rani of the town of Batala, married on 24 September 1487, "though accounts of the marriage differ"; sons Sri Chand (8 September 1494) and Lakhmi Das (12 February 1497). Left out: Dhillon's 14 April 1469 and 22 September 1539 (minor), Bhandari's Samvat 1526, 880 Hijri, Bahlul Lodi, Salim Shah Afghan and the age of seventy to eighty.
