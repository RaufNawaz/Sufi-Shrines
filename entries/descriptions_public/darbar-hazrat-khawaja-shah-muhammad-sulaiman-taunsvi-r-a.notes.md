## Consensus choices

The variant (1260, written in words by Lahori's author) is not named. The 1267 of Lahori's chronogram rests on a figure the team version marks uncertain and is not used.
- **Day of death:** minor (rule 4, third bullet). Sharib gives 7 Safar, Lahori's author 9 Safar, a Thursday; neither is more commonly told, so the public text gives only the month both share, Safar. The day and the weekday are dropped.
- **Birth:** "He was born around 1770, in 1184 AH, in the village in the hills that was his original home, three *kuroh* ... from Taunsa." The site's c. 1770 (basis a) and the 1184 AH of Lahori's editor and Sharib are given together, with no "accounts differ" clause, because they do not contradict each other and no source gives another year. No conversion is stated. The place follows the editor, Sharib and the malfuzat quoted by the editor (a village in the hills, three *kuroh* from Taunsa), which lies within the site's "Taunsa region"; the village's name, uncertain in every printing (Gar Gojhi, Gar Kochi, Gar Goji), is left out (rule 5). See judgement call 1.
- **Name of his order:** not presented as a disagreement, since the names nest rather than conflict. He belonged to the Chishti order and its Nizami branch (the site's Chishti-Nizami, basis a; Ernst and Lawrence's derivation of the Nizami branch from Nizam al-Din Awliya, d. 1325); the wider spread of the Chishtiya Nizamiya Fakhriya order in the Punjab came about through him (Lahori's editor); a further branch, the Sulaymaniyya, is named after him, its masters called Sulaymani-Nizami-Chishti (Ernst and Lawrence). Sharib's silence is dropped.

- **The manuscript copied at Taunsa:** *Adab al-muridin*, copied around 1790, is kept; the bibliography's *Adab al-talibin*, copied "probably after" 1790, is a minor variant of the same kind and is dropped (rule 4, third bullet), with its present owner (apparatus).
