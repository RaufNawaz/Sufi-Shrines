# Shrine of Pir Lakha (Aab-e-Shifa), Jhal Magsi
shrine-of-pir-lakha-aab-e-shifa-jhal-magsi / researched 16 August 2026 / batch H

## Verified findings

- **The shrine's existence and name, confirmed by the district government itself.** The Jhal Magsi district government's official Culture & Heritage page lists it by name twice: as a page heading, "Shrine of Pir Lakha," and again in its list of the district's main shrines: "Shrines Main shrines in the district are Muhammad Ayub Shah Bukhari (Gandawah), Sain Rakhial Shah, Sain Chizal Shah (Fatahpur), Pir Lakha and Pir Chattal Shah (near Kotra)." — "Culture & Heritage," Jhal Magsi District Government of Balochistan, archived copy: http://web.archive.org/web/20250426101741/https://jhalmagsi.balochistan.gov.pk/culture-heritage/ (official body; the live URL returned a server "Database Error" at time of research, so this Wayback Machine snapshot dated 26 April 2025 was used). Accessed 16 August 2026.

## Conflicts

None found — there is nothing to conflict with, because the "Aab-e-Shifa" / hot-spring claim itself could not be independently corroborated by any citable source (see below). This is reported as a gap, not a resolved fact.

## Unverified leads

- **The central "Aab-e-Shifa" (healing-waters/hot-spring) claim in the site's own title is not corroborated by any citable source found this session.** The official Jhal Magsi district government heritage page — the one citable, fetched source that names "Pir Lakha" at all — does not mention hot springs, healing waters, or "Aab-i-Shifa"/"Aab-e-Shifa" anywhere in its text (full page text was extracted and checked). The only sources making this claim are non-citable: a Facebook video, "Pir Lakha (Aab Shifa) Jhal Magsi, Baluchistan," https://www.facebook.com/pakistantravelguide.pk/videos/pir-lakha-aab-shifa-jhal-magsi-baluchistan-qadir-ali-magsi-baloch/993701841234904/; a second Facebook post, "Hundreds of years old Masjid, Shrine of Pir Lakha and hot water springs," https://www.facebook.com/BalochistanTheLandOfMountains/posts/hundreds-of-years-old-masjid-shrine-of-pir-lakha-and-hot-water-springs-known-as-/1435043483516280/; and blog/tourism aggregator pages (Graana.com real-estate blog, pakistanalmanac.com, magsiaroundtheworld.blogspot.com). All are excluded under the README's reliability bar (social media, blogs, real-estate/travel content). None was fetchable as a citation in any case.
- A Dawn feature on nearby hot springs — "How I discovered the hidden springs of Khuzdar in Balochistan," https://www.dawn.com/news/1315708 (19 February 2017) — was fetched and checked specifically for any mention of Pir Lakha or "Aab-i-Shifa"; it contains neither. It covers different spring sites (Moola Chotook, Chota Chotook) in the same general Kirthar/Moola River area but not this shrine.
- Wikipedia's "Jhal Magsi" article was fetched and checked; it names "Peer Lakha" once, in an unsourced list of tourist attractions ("The City is also known for ancient places and tourist attractions like Peer Chattal Shah Noorani, Taj Mahal of Balochistan..., Moola River, Peer Lakha and more."), with no footnote attached to that sentence and no mention of hot springs anywhere in the article. Per the README, Wikipedia is usable only as a pointer to its own citations — since this particular sentence carries no citation to follow, it cannot be used as a source here.

## Acquisition leads

- *Imperial Gazetteer of India, Baluchistan* (1908), archive.org: https://archive.org/details/ImperialGazetteerOfIndiaBaluchistan — full-text searched this session for "Pir Lakha," "Aab-i-Shifa," "Chattal," "Gandava," and "Kachhi"; zero matches for all five. Either uses different spelling/terminology or does not cover this specific site — a manual check of the Kachhi-district sections (rather than relying on the search index) is worth doing before concluding the gazetteer is silent on it.
- No district gazetteer specific to "Kachhi" (the pre-1992 name for the area now split into Jhal Magsi and Bolan districts) was located in an archive.org catalog search this session; the Baluchistan District Gazetteer Series volumes found (Sibi, Bolan and Chagai, Las Bela, Loralai) do not include one titled for Kachhi specifically.

## Verdict

PARTIAL — only one citable source was fetched and verified (the Jhal Magsi district government's own heritage page), and it confirms only that a shrine called "Pir Lakha" exists and is officially recognized as one of the district's main shrines. It does not confirm the "Aab-e-Shifa" / hot-springs claim that is central to how the site is popularly known and that the target's own name emphasizes. That claim currently rests entirely on non-citable social-media and blog sources. This does not meet the "≥2 independent verified sources" bar for STRONG.
