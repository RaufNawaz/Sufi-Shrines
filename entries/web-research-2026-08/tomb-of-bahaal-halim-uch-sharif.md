# Tomb of Baha'al-Halim (Uch Sharif)
tomb-of-bahaal-halim-uch-sharif / researched 16 August 2026 / batch H

## Verified findings

- **UNESCO tentative-list status, official submission details.** The site is part of a five-monument nomination titled "Tomb of Bibi Jawindi, Baha'al-Halim and Ustead and the Tomb and Mosque of Jalaluddin Bukhari," Ref. 1883, "Date of Submission: 30/01/2004," "Submitted by: Department of Archaeology and Museums" (Government of Pakistan), "Coordinates: 29°14' N 71° 04' E - Uch Sharif, Punjab," "Criteria: (ii)(iv)(vi)," Category "Cultural." — UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Tentative Lists, https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1883/, accessed via Wayback Machine snapshot dated 7 December 2024 (http://web.archive.org/web/20241207021554/https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1883/) because the live UNESCO page returned an HTTP 403/browser challenge at time of research (official body). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **Builder and relative dating, per the same official UNESCO description.** "These structures were joined by a series of domed tombs; the first is said to have been built for Baha' al-Halim by his pupil, the Suharwardiya Sufi saint Jahaniyan Jahangasht (1307-1383 AD), the second for the latter's great grant daughter, Bibi Jalwindi, in c.1494 and the third for the latter's architect." This makes the Baha'al-Halim tomb the earliest of the three domed mausolea in the group, built at some point during Jahaniyan Jahangasht's lifetime (1307–1383 AD) or shortly after, and predating the Bibi Jawindi tomb (c. 1494). — UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Tentative Lists, ref. 1883 (as above, via Wayback Machine snapshot). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **Architectural description, per the same official document (applies to all three domed tombs in the group, including Baha'al-Halim's).** "They all have three tiers, an octagonal base supporting a zone of transition surmounted by a dome and are richly decorated with carved timber, cut and moulded brick, and blue and white faience mosaic tiles. The basement walls taper and are supported by 8 engaged tapering corner towers." "The eroded nature of the three clearly allows their profile, construction and decorated interiors to be seen" — i.e., official acknowledgment of significant erosion/decay across the group as of the 2004 submission. — UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Tentative Lists, ref. 1883 (as above). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **UNESCO status and general condition, per established press (independent of the UNESCO document).** "Some of the most popular shrines in Uch are those of Bibi Jawindi, Baha'al-Halim and Jalaluddin Bukhari – all of which are concentrated in a compound known as Uch Sharif and are listed as a Unesco World Heritage Site." "Most of the old shrines in Uch Sharif are in massive need of restoration and that work is currently being carried out there." — "Uch Sharif: where the shrine culture began," Dawn.com, 27 June 2011, https://www.dawn.com/news/639694/uch-sharif-where-the-shrine-culture-began (established press). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **A 2025 restoration project naming the tomb specifically, and its custodial authority.** "The Walled City of Lahore Authority (WCLA) has embarked on an ambitious conservation and restoration plan" covering ten structures including "Hazrat Bahaul Halim," at a total cost of "over Rs653 million" (with an initial "Rs120m" released), expected to take "about a year to complete the whole plan." The article notes general disrepair in the group: "The tombs of Bibi Javindi and some others are presently in a dilapidated condition as during previous years as they were affected by floods." — "Restoration of Uch Sharif tombs goes on," Dawn, 24 April 2025, https://www.dawn.com/news/1906251/restoration-of-uch-sharif-tombs-goes-on (established press). Accessed 16 August 2026. Note: this article does not describe the Baha'al-Halim tomb individually beyond including it in the list of ten monuments being restored.

## Conflicts

None found on the core facts (builder, relative dating, UNESCO status). The two Dawn articles (2011 and 2025) both describe the Uch monuments as long in need of restoration, consistent with the 2004 UNESCO submission's own note about the group's "eroded" condition — no contradiction between sources, just a fourteen-year gap during which restoration was evidently still incomplete or renewed.
