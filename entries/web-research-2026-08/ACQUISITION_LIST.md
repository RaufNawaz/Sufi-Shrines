# Book acquisition list — from the 16 August web-research pass

Every source named in a research file's own "Acquisition leads" section (see
`entries/web-research-2026-08/<slug>.md`), consolidated and deduplicated across all 40 files,
grouped by how many entries each source could help and whether it's already freely accessible.
Compiled 16 August 2026; nothing here was invented — every line traces back to a specific
research file, listed in parentheses.

## Tier 1 — free full text online, not yet fully mined

These cost nothing to pursue further and don't need Saifullah at all — someone (agent or
human) reading the right page could upgrade a PARTIAL verdict to STRONG, or find the missing
second source a "nothing reliable found" entry needs.

1. **Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board,
   1998)** — https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan — already the single
   most-used source this session, directly covering 10 of this batch's gurdwara targets with
   page numbers (Baoli Sahib p.218, Chakki Sahib p.56, Chhevin Patshahi Chitti Gatti p.228,
   Jhalian p.276 + Dhilwan p.278, Choa Sahib p.178, Khoohi Bhai Lalo pp.58–60, Malji Sahib
   pp.91–92 *— but that's the Kanganpur one, not the sheet's Nankana Sahib target*, Patshahi
   Chhevin Hadiara pp.283–284 + a separate Amar Sadhu section, Pehli Patshahi Jind Pir p.120,
   Sri Tilganji Sahib, Samadhi of Maharaja Ranjit Singh). Remaining value: a cleaner print/scan
   for Tilganji Sahib (the archive.org copy is a low-quality bilingual-edition OCR — it's the
   *only* citable source found for that entry).
2. **Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan* (Department of Archaeology,
   Government of Pakistan, 1962)** —
   https://archive.org/details/furg-sikh-shrines-in-west-pakistan-by-khan-mohammad-wal — a
   numbered register with plates, independently corroborating Baoli Sahib (entry 3, Plate 32),
   Chakki Sahib (entry 2, Plate 4), Jhalian/Dhilwan (entry 6, "Gurdwara at Dhisalwan"). Chitti
   Gatti's Plate 57 is captioned "Gurdwara at Mansehra" — plausibly this site (Chitti Gatti is
   in Hazara district near Mansehra) but not yet read closely enough to be sure it isn't the
   unrelated 1905 Sri Guru Singh Sabha a 2021 press story actually describes. Not yet checked:
   Choa Sahib specifically. **Checked 16 August for Nankana Sahib's Malji Sahib: confirmed** —
   entry 22, Plate 20, a full verbatim entry ("GURDWARA MAL JI SAHIB") distinct from and citing
   its own awareness of the separate Kanganpur tree of the same name. Now folded into
   `gurdwara-malji-sahib.md` and `data/patch_web_research.csv` — see below.
3. **Gazetteer of the Province of Sind, "B" Volume III — Sukkur District** (J. W. Smyth, 1919;
   archive.org `in.ernet.dli.2015.34603`) — **checked 16 August 2026 against all five flagged
   sites: no mentions found for any of them.** Three (Sant Baba Asudaram Darbar/Panno Aqil,
   Sant Bhagat Kanwar Ram Temple/Chak, Sant Satram Dham/Raharki) are genuine content misses —
   the places and names are absent from a book that does cover their districts. The other two
   (Bhai Waliram Darbar, Sain Vali Vilayat Rai Darbar/Kambar) are misses for a documented
   structural reason: both sites' talukas (Miro Khan, Kambar) were administratively severed
   from Sukkur/Shikarpur territory in 1901 to form the new Larkana district, 18 years before
   this 1919 volume was compiled — it was never going to cover them. **The real next target for
   those two specifically is the Larkana "B" volume gazetteer**, listed under item 8 below.
4. **Saeen Bakhsh Taj Muhammad Qaboolai, *Makhdoom Abdul Rahim Girhori (Sindhi) — A
   biography*** (2000) — https://archive.org/details/MakhdoomAbdulRahimGirhorisindhiABiography
   — a full dedicated biography (`shrine-of-makhdoom-abdul-rahim-girhori`, currently STRONG via
   press only); plus, same saint, his own recorded teachings: *Fath-ul-Fazl (Sindhi)* —
   https://archive.org/details/Fath-ul-fazlsindhi — and his collected poetry, *Kalam Girhori
   (Sindhi)* — https://archive.org/details/KalamGirhorisindhi. All in Sindhi; would need
   translation, but are free, full-text, and specifically about this one saint.
5. **Henry Cousens, *The Antiquities of Sind, with Historical Outline* (1929)** —
   https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.200876 — indexes "Gori" at p.177
   (`gori-temple-gori-jo-mandar`, currently PARTIAL); the descriptive passage itself wasn't
   located by keyword search — a manual page-177 read is the next step.
6. **Denys Bray, *Ethnographic Survey of Baluchistan*, Vol. 1** (1913) —
   https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.529529 — Vol. 2 was already used and directly
   quoted for `kalat-kali-temple` (currently PARTIAL); Vol. 1 hasn't been checked.
7. **Naseem Mughal, *Shikarpur Heritage*** (Sindh Archives Department, 2012) —
   https://archive.org/details/shikarpurheritage — already used for `khatwari-darbar-shikarpur`;
   Chak (`sant-bhagat-kanwar-ram-temple-chak`) also falls within Shikarpur district and hasn't
   been cross-checked against it.
8. **District/provincial gazetteers already located but not yet read closely enough to confirm
   a site-specific passage** (all free, archive.org):
   - *Gazetteer of the Sialkot District*, 1894-95 and 1920 — `allo-mahar` (currently nothing
     found; searched by keyword only, a manual read of the Daska tehsil section is the
     next step) — plus a separate 1920 scan, `in.gov.ignca.30718`, not yet opened.
   - *Gazetteer of the Lahore District*, 1893-94 (`in.ernet.dli.2015.105616`) — `Baoli Sahib`,
     `Jhalian/Dhilwan`.
   - *Gazetteer of the Gujranwala District*, 1883-84/1893-94 — `Chakki Sahib` (Eminabad).
   - *Gazetteer of the Hazara District*, 1883-4 and 1907 — `Chhevin Patshahi Chitti Gatti`.
   - *Gazetteer of the Jhelum District*, 1904 — `Choa Sahib`.
   - *Gazetteer of the Rawalpindi District*, 1883-4/1893-94 revised — `ram-mandir-saidpur`
     (worth a follow-up targeted search across all editions for the "Ram Kund" fair passage
     Dawn attributes to the 1893-94 edition specifically — not found in the scan searched),
     `lal-kurti-temple-balmiki-mandir-rawalpindi`, `valmiki-swamiji-mandir-gracy-lines`.
   - *Gazetteer of the Province of Sind* 2nd ed. 1876 (`wbsl.16342`) and Vol. IV 1927
     (`in.gov.ignca.30452`) — `bhai-waliram-darbar`, `swami-dharmdas-darbar-larkana`.
   - *Gazetteer of Kohat District*, Part B, 1904 — on Scribd (needs an account), not
     archive.org — `darbar-ghamkol-sharif-zinda-pir` (background only; the cult began 1948,
     after this gazetteer, so it can't describe the shrine itself).

## Tier 2 — needs Saifullah (physical, paywalled, or not locatable online)

1. **Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, *Saints, Sufis and Shrines: The Mystical Landscape of Sindh*** (EFT
   Sindh, 2022) — the single highest-value physical target: ~55 essays by the same author whose
   shorter press pieces were already the *only* source for several entries. Flagged by five
   research files as worth checking directly: `sain-vali-vilayat-rai-darbar-kambar`,
   `sant-baba-asudaram-darbar-panno-aqil`, `sant-bhagat-kanwar-ram-temple-chak`,
   `sant-satram-dham-raharki-sacho-satram-devri-sahib`, `sevapanthi-darbar-bhai-gurdas-gandava`.
   **Checked 16 August: confirmed not freely available anywhere** — EFT Sindh's own site only
   offers "view or purchase," Open Library shows 0 ebooks for this publisher, archive.org has
   no listing, and South Asia Commons' copy 403'd. Only the component essays (already used) are
   free, on Academia.edu, not the compiled 2022 book. Print/library acquisition, or a direct
   request to EFT Sindh (eft.karachi@hotmail.com), is the only route.
2. **Pnina Werbner, *Pilgrims of Love: The Anthropology of a Global Sufi Cult*** (Hurst, 2004)
   — the primary academic monograph on Ghamkol Sharif/Zindapir (`darbar-ghamkol-sharif`,
   currently STRONG via a conference paper only); also her chapter in Osella & Osella, eds.,
   *Islamic Reform in South Asia* (2013) — directly ties Ghamkol Sharif to Mohra Sharif
   (`mohra-sharif-khanqah`) as murshid/disciple, a connection between two of this batch's own
   entries not otherwise sourced.
3. **Jürgen Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*** (OUP, 2018/2020) — standard academic treatment of
   the Hinglaj pilgrimage that Chandragup is a station of (`chandragup-baba-chandragup`,
   currently STRONG via press only).
4. **Nadhra Shahbaz Khan, *The Samadhi of Maharaja Ranjit Singh in Lahore*** (2018) — the single
   most focused scholarly treatment found for `samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh` (currently
   STRONG); an excerpt could not be read this session.
5. **Tarunjit Singh Butalia (ed.), *Sikh Historical Shrines in Pakistan: Lahore District***
   (Jeevay Sanjha Punjab, 2025) — a modern guidebook with "Google Maps location coordinates …
   properties attached to each Gurdwara before partition, and colour coded maps" per its own
   description; too large to fetch via WebFetch this session. Relevant to
   `gurdwara-patshahi-chhevin-hadiara-lahore` and likely `samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh`.
6. **Multan/Suhrawardi funerary-architecture scholarship** for `shrine-of-hazrat-shah-ali-akbar`
   and the Uch tombs (`tomb-of-bahaal-halim-uch-sharif`, `tomb-of-ustad-nuriya`): Ahmad Nabi
   Khan, *Multan: History and Architecture* (1983) and *Islamic Architecture of Pakistan*
   (1990); Kamil Khan Mumtaz, *Architecture in Pakistan* (1985); Hasan Ali Khan, *Constructing
   Islam on the Indus* (2016) — none located as free scans.
7. **Mohra Sharif khanqah sourcing**, all cited by Wikipedia but not accessed: P. Lewis, *Pirs,
   Shrines and Pakistani Islam* (1985) p.22; Jürgen Wasim Frembgen, *The Friends of God* (OUP,
   2006) pp.33–36; William Birdwood, *Khaki and Gown* (1942) p.365; an untransliterated 1971
   Urdu work on the Murree valley, p.109.
8. **"Tazkira Mashaikh-e-Allo Mahar Sharif"** — title only known via a Wikipedia citation;
   thelibrarypk.com hosts it but blocked WebFetch with a 403. If this is a real book rather than
   a blog compilation, it would be the single most targeted source for `allo-mahar` — worth
   chasing through Saifullah's book channel specifically rather than the web.
9. **Alice Albinia, *Empires of the Indus: The Story of a River*** (John Murray, 2008) — the
   full book behind an excerpt already used for `gurdwara-pehli-patshahi-jind-pir-sukkur`; may
   clarify the relationship (if any) between the older Zinda Pir shrine and the Sikh gurdwara.
10. **Sir Edward MacLagan, *Lahore and some of its Historical Monuments*** and Nazir Ahmed
    Chaudhry, *Lahore Fort: A Witness to History* — for `loh-temple-lava-temple`, where
    historians are on record as unable to confirm the site's builder, date, or original use.
11. **Encyclopedia Sindhiana** (انسائيڪلوپيڊيا سنڌيانا, 13-volume Sindhi encyclopedia) — for
    `sant-baba-asudaram-darbar-panno-aqil`; only an article-title URL was tried this session, a
    proper subject search inside the set might do better.
12. ***Pakistan Tourism Directory*, Holiday Weekly (1997)** p.103 — an early print source for
    `tomb-of-bahaal-halim-uch-sharif`, not available online.
13. **World Monuments Fund project archives** on the "Uch Monument Complex" (1998/2000/2002
    Watch listings) — for `tomb-of-ustad-nuriya`.
