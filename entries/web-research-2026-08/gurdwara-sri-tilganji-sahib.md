# Gurdwara Sri Tilganji Sahib
gurdwara-sri-tilganji-sahib / researched 16 August 2026 / batch C

Note: the sheet places this gurdwara in **Quetta, Balochistan** (Masjid Road), not Lahore.
Research below follows the sheet's location, not the batch note's implied Lahore grouping.

## Verified findings

1. Location and the origin of the name "Tilganji" (Guru Nanak distributing sesame/*til* as
   *parshad*).

   > "Quetta is the capital of Baluchistan province. The Gurdwara of Jagat Guru Nanak Dev Ji
   > called Tilganii Sahib is located at Masjid road of the city. Sat Guru graced this city
   > during his third itinerary. When the Guru Ji arrived here devotees came to see him. He
   > honoured the group of devotees by distributing Til (sesame) as parshad (offering) and hence
   > this pious Gurdwara was named as Tilganji."

   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (1998), full-text scan,
   p. 134. https://archive.org/stream/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan_djvu.txt
   — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability: published book (full-text scan on archive.org) —
   citable.

2. Condition and legal status at the time of the book's writing: a government school occupies
   part of the site, and the Sikh community had gone to court over possession.

   > "Government Sandeman school is functioning inside this holy shrine. A new school building
   > has been constructed on the vacant land of the Gurdwara. The main gate of Gurdwara takes
   > after a mosque and inside there is a beautiful and strong building with dome... Sangat of
   > Quetta has filed a low suit in lower court to get back the possession of the Gurdwara."

   Citation: same as #1.

Nothing in this source dates the original construction, mentions the 1935 Quetta earthquake, or
gives a 1940 rebuild — the sheet's `year_built`/`year_built_note` (1940, circa, "Rebuilt after
the 1935 Quetta earthquake") is neither confirmed nor contradicted by anything found in this
pass; it remains unverified.
