# Gurdwara Baoli Sahib (Guru Arjan Dev Ji), Lahore

gurdwara-baoli-sahib-guru-arjan-dev-ji-lahore / researched 16 August 2026 / batch A (historical Sikh gurdwaras in Punjab)

## Verified findings

1. **Location — Dabbi Bazaar, back of the Golden (Sonehri) Mosque.**
   Quote: "This sacred shrine of fifth Guru is situated in Rang Mahal Bazaar of Lahore city at the back of the Sonehri (Golden) Mosque."
   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998), p. 218. Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

2. **Origin story — the Pathan's forgotten purse and Chajju Bhagat, Samvat 1616 (~1559 CE by the book's own reckoning, see Conflicts).**
   Quote: "The dispute came up before Guru Arjun Dev Ji, when both of them refused to accept the money, he decided to build a baoli (stepped well) in Dabbi Bazaar with this money in Samvat 1616."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998, p. 218 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026, quoting the book's own citation of "Tawarikh-e-Guru Khalsa."
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

3. **Later history — filled in Samvat 1685, restored under Ranjit Singh in Samvat 1891, now reduced to a garden.**
   Quote: "In Samvat 1685, this baoli was got filled with earth. During the reign of Ranjit Singh the baoli was restored in Samvat 1891 when a florist informed him about it. This baoli has once again been lost, only a small garden remains with shops around it where the people take rest."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998, p. 218 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

4. **Construction attributed to Guru Arjan in 1599 AD, funded by 282 gold mohars from Chajju Bhagat.**
   Quotes: "This 'baoli' was dug in 1599 by the guru himself." — Maharajah Ranjit Singh allegedly recovered from illness after drinking and bathing in the baoli's water, "leading him to order its restoration and maintenance."
   Citation: Majid Sheikh, "Harking back: The magical baolis of Arjan and Dina Nath," *Dawn*, 1 February 2015, https://www.dawn.com/news/1160776, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

5. **Damage in 1947; present remains a small opening in open ground; declining water table.**
   Quote: the structure suffered damage during the 1947 disturbances; today it exists as "a mere small opening in an open space." A few traditional healers still access water from a small hole, though underground pumping has lowered water levels.
   Citation: Majid Sheikh, *Dawn*, 1 February 2015 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

6. **1599 AD construction and the Kartarpur Pothi / Adi Granth connection.**
   Quotes: "Baoli Sahib, a water-well built by the fifth Sikh guru Arjan in 1599 AD" — "it was at the Gurdwara Baoli Sahib in Dabbi Bazaar that the original Adi Granth was kept by the Sodhi family." Under Ranjit Singh, "a grand procession from Dabbi Bazaar by Sikhs to the Lahore Fort" occurred with the manuscript; after the British takeover in 1849 the text was restored to the Sodhi family. The author notes as of 2018 that "the knocking down of the Gurdwara at Baoli Sahib also needs to be redressed" — i.e. the structure had been demolished by that date.
   Citation: Majid Sheikh, "Harking back: The connection of Dabbi Bazaar to Kartarpur 'pothi'," *Dawn*, 30 September 2018, https://www.dawn.com/news/1435814, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

7. **Official archaeological account — the baoli, its later conversion to a mosque, and 1834 re-excavation under Ranjit Singh.**
   Quote: "A Baoli with steps descending to the water level existed to the west of the Golden Mosque of Bhikari Khan. The baoli was built by Guru Arjun Dev with the money presented to him by Chhajju Bhagat. A 'langar khana' ... was also attached to it. It is said that during the time of Guru Har Gobind, the Baoli no longer in use, was filled up, the langar khana closed and a mosque built, at the site. In 1834 A.D. Maharaja Ranjit Singh fell ill and his astrologers advised him to excavate the Baoli and take bath from its water. The mosque was therefore pulled down and the Baoli re-opened. A tank was also added. The site of the Baoli is marked with the construction of a memorial gateway."
   Citation: Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan*, Memoir of the Department of Archaeology in Pakistan No. 3 (Karachi: Department of Archaeology, Ministry of Education & Information, Government of Pakistan, 1962), entry 3 "Gurdwara Baoli Sahib, Lahore," Plate No. 32. Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/furg-sikh-shrines-in-west-pakistan-by-khan-mohammad-wal, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: official body / published government report (full-text scan).

## Conflicts

- **Who filled in the baoli and built a mosque over it:** Qaiser 1998 gives only dates (Samvat 1616 built, Samvat 1685 filled, Samvat 1891 restored under Ranjit Singh) with no mention of a mosque. The 1962 Department of Archaeology memoir states explicitly that "during the time of Guru Har Gobind, the Baoli no longer in use, was filled up, the langar khana closed and a mosque built, at the site" and that Ranjit Singh in 1834 had "the mosque ... pulled down and the Baoli re-opened." Not resolved here; the two accounts differ substantially on what stood on the site between the Sikh gurus and Ranjit Singh's reign.
- **Construction date:** Qaiser 1998 (via "Tawarikh-e-Guru Khalsa") dates construction to Samvat 1616; Dawn (2015, 2018) states "1599 AD" (roughly consistent with Samvat 1616 by standard conversion, but the two sources frame it differently — one as a Vikrami-calendar figure quoted from a named older text, the other as a flat Gregorian year with no source cited in the fetched excerpt).
- **Current physical state:** Dawn (2015) describes "a mere small opening in an open space" with residual well access; Dawn (2018) separately notes "the knocking down of the Gurdwara at Baoli Sahib" as an unaddressed grievance, implying more than the baoli itself — a gurdwara structure — was demolished. Qaiser 1998 (pre-2015) already describes it as reduced to "a small garden ... with shops around it." None of the three fetched accounts is dated precisely against the others, so the sequence of loss is reported, not reconciled.

## Follow-up: *Gazetteer of the Lahore District, 1893-94* (`in.ernet.dli.2015.105616`) — attempted, unreachable — 5 September 2026

**This lead could not be pursued to a conclusion; it remains an unread source, not a checked-and-silent one.** Baoli Sahib and Dabbi Bazaar sit in the gazetteer's Chapter VI ("Towns, Municipalities and Cantonments"), Section A "Ancient Lahore" (p. 264) and Section B "Modern Lahore" (p. 281) — confirmed by fetching the book's own table of contents. Three separate scans of this same 1893-94 gazetteer were tried (`in.ernet.dli.2015.105616`, the named acquisition-list identifier; `in.gov.ignca.30664`; and `gazetteer-lahore-1893`), by two methods each:

- **Full-text download (`_djvu.txt`)**: in every one of the three scans, the fetch tool's content limit cut the text off in Chapter II ("History"), around page 25 — well short of page 264. Confirmed by literally asking the tool to report the last sentence it could see each time (e.g. one attempt ended mid-word at "the Afghans, having formed an allianco with th…"). A direct, targeted search of what *was* reachable for "Baoli", "Dabbi", "Chajju", "Sonehri", "Jhalian", and "Dhilwan" found none of these strings — but this is not evidence the gazetteer is silent on them, since the relevant chapter was never reached.
- **Archive.org's full-text search-inside API** (which returned real, literal-index results for other sources in this pass, e.g. the Qaiser and 1962-register searches above): unavailable for all three of these scans, returning `{"error": "No hOCR or Abbyy file present"}` even for the one scan (`gazetteer-lahore-1893`) whose file listing does include an `_hocr.html` derivative. This appears to be an indexing gap on archive.org's side for these particular digitizations (the "in.ernet.dli." and "in.gov.ignca." prefixed items generally, based on the same failure recurring for the Sialkot gazetteers below), not something fixable from this end.

### Revised verdict

## Re-verification against the complete text — 5 September 2026

The "attempted, unreachable" follow-up above was produced with a fetch tool that silently
truncates around page 25 of a large scan; that ceiling does not apply to
`pipeline/fetch_archive_text.py`, which retrieved the complete book: **1,126,974 bytes**
(`in.ernet.dli.2015.105616`).

A literal search of the full text for `Baoli`, `Dabbi`, `Chajju`/`Chhajju`, `Sonehri`/`Sunehri`,
and `Rang Mahal` found: one unrelated `Baoli` hit ("Baoli Laima Singh," a village/estate name in
a land-revenue table on p. ~590 of the OCR — a near-miss ruled out explicitly, not the shrine);
zero for `Dabbi` (fuzzy `Chajj`/`Dabi`/`Dubbi`/`Rangmahal` also zero); but **three real hits for
`Golden Mosque`**, which is the landmark this file's own verified finding #1 places Baoli Sahib
directly behind ("at the back of the Sonehri (Golden) Mosque"). Reading the passage around those
hits reached the actual descriptive section — Chapter VI, "Towns, Municipalities and
Cantonments," Section B "Modern Lahore," subsection "Wazir Khan's Mosque and neighbouring
buildings," printed page 292 — which the book's own contents place squarely inside the p.
264–281+ range this file's earlier follow-up correctly predicted but could not reach:

> "Behind the mosque is a bdoli or large well, with steps descending to the water's edge. The
> well is said to have been dug by Arjan, the fifth Sikh Guru; the superstructure was built by
> Ranjit Singh."

This independently corroborates the site's existence, its location directly behind the Golden
Mosque, its digging by Guru Arjan, and its superstructure's construction under Ranjit Singh —
three of the same facts already established by Qaiser (1998) and the 1962 Department of
Archaeology memoir (verified findings #1 and #7). It is a **fourth independent source**, and the
earliest one yet found (1893-94, i.e. contemporary with or predating all three already on file).

It does **not** use the proper nouns "Baoli Sahib," "Dabbi Bazaar," or "Chajju Bhagat" — the book
calls it only "a bdoli or large well" without a name, and gives no funding story — so those
specific terms genuinely do not occur in this gazetteer under any spelling tried; that absence is
now a checked negative rather than an unreached chapter, though the site itself is present and
identifiable by its description.

**This also bears on, without resolving, the file's existing Conflicts entry on the
mosque-conversion history.** The 1962 memoir describes a cycle — baoli filled in, mosque built
over the site during Guru Har Gobind's time, later demolished and the baoli re-opened by Ranjit
Singh in 1834. This 1893-94 gazetteer instead describes the Golden Mosque (built 1753 by Bikhari
Khan) and the baoli as **two separate, simultaneously standing features** — "the domes are
pretty... behind the mosque is a bdoli" — with no mention of the mosque ever having stood on the
baoli's own site or having been demolished. A third, differing account, reported rather than
adjudicated.

Citation: *Gazetteer of the Lahore District, 1893-94* (Punjab Government), Chapter VI, Section B
"Modern Lahore," "Wazir Khan's Mosque and neighbouring buildings," p. 292, full text via Internet
Archive, https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.105616, accessed 5 September 2026
(1,126,974 bytes, complete text confirmed via `pipeline/fetch_archive_text.py --list`).

### Revised verdict
**STRONG — strengthened.** A fourth independent source is now confirmed: the 1893-94 Lahore
District Gazetteer describes the baoli behind the Golden Mosque, its digging by Guru Arjan, and
Ranjit Singh's superstructure — the earliest source yet found for this site — while adding a
third, differing account of the mosque/baoli relationship to the existing, unresolved conflict.
The proper nouns "Baoli Sahib," "Dabbi Bazaar," and "Chajju Bhagat" are now confirmed absent from
this particular gazetteer (a real negative, not an unreached chapter), which does not weaken the
site's identification given the descriptive match.
