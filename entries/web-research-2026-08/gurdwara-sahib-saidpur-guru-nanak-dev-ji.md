# Gurdwara Sahib Saidpur (Guru Nanak Dev Ji)
gurdwara-sahib-saidpur-guru-nanak-dev-ji / researched 16 August 2026 / batch B

Sheet location on file: Saidpur Village, Margalla Hills, Islamabad Capital Territory, Pakistan (33.7425, 73.0671). Confirmed against the sheet's separate "Ram Mandir, Saidpur (Ram Kund Mandir)" entry, recorded at the same coordinates (33.7427, 73.0678) — i.e. the sheet's target is the Saidpur Village heritage complex in Islamabad's Margalla Hills, not the historical "Saidpur" that is an alternate name for Eminabad, Gujranwala District, referenced throughout Iqbal Qaiser's *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (e.g. "GURDWARA CHAJKKI SAHIB AT SAIDPUR (EMINABAD) DISTRICT GUJRANWALA"). Those Eminabad/Gujranwala-Saidpur passages are about a different place and are not used here.

## Verified findings

1. **Existence and likely period of construction of the gurdwara, and its attached school.** "A Gurdwara was also built by the Sikhs, probably in the 20th century." "Attached to the gurdwara is a big-sized room, which served as a school for teaching the philosophy of Guru Nanak." — The Express Tribune (APP), "Antiquity of Saidpur Village lures tourists," 28 December 2020, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2277665/antiquity-of-saidpur-village-lures-tourists, accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability class: established press (citable).

2. **The 2006 restoration and its purpose.** "In 2006, the Saidpur Village was remodelled as an Arts and Craft Village by CDA of Islamabad with an aim to preserve its centuries-old cultural heritage and promote tourism in the area." — same source as above. Reliability class: established press (citable).

3. **The adjoining Hindu temple's history and the 1960s–2006 girls'-school interlude** (context for the same complex the gurdwara sits within). "The village is home to a small 16th century temple called Rama Mandir or Ram Kund Temple, dedicated to Rama." "Hindu commander Raja Man Singh later converted Saidpur into a place of Hindu worship by constructing temples." CDA spokesperson Mazhar Ahmed: "The temple where once upper-class Hindus used to take Prasad, now serves as a dining place of Des Pardes restaurant." — same Express Tribune source as above.

4. **Independent corroboration of the gurdwara's presence and general dating from a second outlet.** "A Gurdwara was built by the Sikhs, probably in the 20th century, alongside the temple." The gurdwara has "bright yellow domes." — Arab News PK (Aamir Saeed), "In Pakistani capital, a centuries-old temple where Hindus are not allowed to pray," 7 July 2020 (updated 8 July 2020), https://www.arabnews.pk/node/1701341/pakistan, accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability class: established press (citable, though note Arab News is not among the README's named example outlets — flagged for the human reviewer to weigh).

5. **The temple's ban on Hindu worship since 1947, per the same source.** Caretaker quoted: "the area was now a 'heritage site' and worship by Hindus was not allowed." The 1960s conversion of the complex into a girls' school, and its 2006 relocation after protests, are also reported in this source.

## Conflicts

- **No fetched source ties Guru Nanak personally to this Islamabad Saidpur gurdwara.** The sheet's entry name specifies "(Guru Nanak Dev Ji)," but neither citable source found makes any claim that Guru Nanak visited or founded this site — only that the attached school taught "the philosophy of Guru Nanak" (a devotional/educational dedication, not a claimed historical visit) and that the gurdwara was "probably" built in the 20th century, many centuries after Guru Nanak's lifetime. This is reported as a gap, not resolved: the two press sources found do not substantiate the Guru-Nanak-specific dedication implied by the sheet's name.
- **Construction date is hedged, not fixed, in both sources.** Both the Express Tribune and Arab News describe the gurdwara's dating as "probably" 20th century — neither gives a specific year, builder, or sect/lineage, unlike the more detailed Eminabad-area gurdwaras covered in Qaiser's book (which is a different Saidpur — see header note above, not a source for this entry).

## Acquisition leads

- No full-text scanned book or gazetteer specifically covering Saidpur Village, Islamabad, was located in this session. The Express Tribune article mentions Jahangir's memoir *Tuzk-e-Jahangiri* as recording his stay in Saidpur (found via a search-result summary, not independently fetched/quoted) — an English translation of the Tuzk-e-Jahangiri (e.g. the Rogers/Beveridge translation) would be a natural primary source to chase for an early, dated mention of the village, though it long predates the gurdwara itself and would only speak to the site's Mughal-era garden-resort history, not the Sikh building.

## Verdict

PARTIAL — two independent, directly-quoting established-press sources (Express Tribune and Arab News) confirm the gurdwara's existence, its "probably 20th century" construction, its attached Guru-Nanak-themed school, and the 2006 CDA restoration of the complex — but neither substantiates a direct historical Guru Nanak connection to this specific site, and no source gives a precise founding date or builder.
