# Khatwari Darbar, Shikarpur
khatwari-darbar-shikarpur / researched 16 August 2026 / batch E

## Verified findings

1. **Foundation attributed to Bhai Gurdas (born Kanhiya/Kanya Lal), disciple of Guru Gobind Singh; construction dated to the second quarter of the 18th century.**
   Quote: founded by "Kanhiya Lal, alias Bhai Gurdas, a disciple of Guru Gobind Singh"; believed constructed in the "second quarter of the eighteenth century". The founding legend (per Lok Ram Dodeja's research, as cited in the article): Guru Gobind Singh found Bhai Gurdas "serving water to both the Muslim and Sikh wounded soldiers" and instructed him to preach in Shikarpur.
   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "The Magnificent Khatwari Darbar in Shikarpur", *Youlin Magazine*, 6 June 2024, https://www.youlinmagazine.com/article/the-magnificent-khatwari-darbar-in-shikarpur/MjgwNA==, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: cultural magazine (below the README's named-press list) but authored by an established academic anthropologist; same material appears in The Friday Times (item 2).

2. **Same founding account and name origin in established press.**
   Quote: "Bhai Gurdas was entrusted the responsibility of serving water to the injured Sikh soldiers"; name origin: he "used to sit on a cot while delivering lectures and sermons. Thus the darbar came to be called Khatwari Darbar." (khat = cot).
   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Gurus in Shikarpur", *The Friday Times*, 12 January 2018, https://www.thefridaytimes.com/12-Jan-2018/gurus-in-shikarpur, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (The Friday Times).

3. **Three Khatwari Darbars in Shikarpur; the main one in Thekrati Bazar.**
   Quote: "There are three Khatwari Darbars in Shikarpur. The first and main one, also known as Khatwari Dharamshala, is located in Thekrati Bazar, second is on the same street, while the third is located opposite Balakram Ji Marhi near Lakhidar."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *Youlin Magazine*, 6 June 2024 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

4. **Architecture: formerly five storeys, now two; haveli style; marble images at entrances; carved doors.**
   Quote: originally "a five-storeyed building. Now, only two storeys remain"; built in "haveli style, with a courtyard and a verandah. The marble pillars, wooden balustrades and painted wooden ceilings of the verandah and hall are beautifully made"; "Two main entrances to the Khatwari Darbar: the first entrance depicts a marble image of Durga, and on the second entrance, one finds a marble image of Ganpati flanked by his two wives"; "The central door has ten panels depicting Sikh Gurus and saints. One of the panels also shows figures, Baba Sewa Das and Baba Kashi Das."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *Youlin Magazine*, 6 June 2024 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

5. **What the darbar houses (composite Nanakpanthi devotional objects).**
   Quote: "The hall of Khatwari Darbar houses the Guru Granth Sahib, the image of Sri Chand, Guru Nanak's eldest son and Jhulelal's Jyot (light)."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *Youlin Magazine*, 6 June 2024 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

6. **Frescoes and decay (2018).**
   Quote: "Mural paintings…are now in advanced stages of decay" (ellipsis in extraction); the article also reports ornate wooden doors sold off to dealers, and walls displaying "images of ten Sikh Gurus".
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 12 January 2018 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

7. **Reduced precinct; former school now a charity hospital; post-Partition branch in Mumbai.**
   Quote: "The darbar had a vast area associated with it, which has now been reduced to a few buildings. In front of the darbar was the Vidyalaya (school), which has now been rebuilt and serves as a charity hospital." and "After Partition, a branch of Khatwari Darbar was also built in Mumbai, India."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *Youlin Magazine*, 6 June 2024 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

8. **Independent confirmation of existence and Guru Granth Sahib presence: named in 2013 desecration report.**
   Quote: "He said that the incidents occurred at Dal Darbar in Pannu Aqil; Guru Nanak Darbar in Mehar taluka of Dadu district; Samadha Ashram, Sajal Sher Jhooleylal Darbar and Khatwari Darbar in Shikarpur district."
   Citation: Staff Reporter, "Desecration of Guru Granth Sahib across Sindh cities slammed", *Dawn*, 28 August 2013, https://www.dawn.com/news/1038756, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Dawn); independent of Kalhoro.

## Conflicts

- **Founder's original name spelling:** "Kanhiya Lal" (Youlin, 2024) vs "Kanya Lal" (The Friday Times, 2018) — both by the same author. Reported, not resolved.
- **Where Bhai Gurdas first preached:** Youlin records an alternative account — "Some believed Bhai Gurdas first preached in Gandava, Balochistan, before Shikarpur. 'He was known as Khatwaro Bao (ascetic) in Sindh.'" Reported, not resolved.
- **Dedication:** Wikipedia ("Sikhism in Sindh", not citable) states "The Khat Wari Darbar in Shikarpur is a Sindhi temple dedicated to him" in a passage about Bhai Kanhaiya, the Sewapanthi founder — whereas Kalhoro names the founder Bhai Gurdas (alias Kanhiya Lal) while attaching the water-carrier story usually associated with Bhai Kanhaiya. The relationship between the two figures is not resolved by any fetched citable source; flagged for the book pass.

## Verdict

**STRONG** — two Kalhoro articles in separate publications (The Friday Times 2018; Youlin 2024) give a rich, consistent account, and an independent Dawn news report (2013) confirms the darbar's existence in Shikarpur and its housing of the Guru Granth Sahib. Note: the detailed history rests on a single scholar; founder-identity questions are flagged under Conflicts.
