# Gurdwara Malji Sahib
gurdwara-malji-sahib / researched 16 August 2026 / batch B

Sheet location on file: Nankana Sahib, Punjab, Pakistan (31.456, 73.7125).

## Verified findings

*Updated 16 August 2026 — a targeted follow-up check found a genuine source for the sheet's
actual Nankana Sahib site, after the original pass below had concluded nothing citable existed.*

1. "22. GURDWARA MAL JI SAHIB: (Pl. No. 20). While herding the cattle Guru Nanak used to take
   rest under the shade of a tree. Once Rai Buller happened to pass that way and observed that
   the protecting shadow of the tree had remained stationary over the Guru and did not change
   like the shadows of other trees with the sun's progress. On another occasion he found a
   cobra protecting the saint with its hood. The tree which is known as Mal Ji Sahib is held
   sacred by the Sikhs. A gurdwara was built after its name with a view to enclose the sacred
   tree. The tree under which the Guru stayed in Kanganpur, Tahsil Chuniyan, is also called Mal
   Ji Sahib." — Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan* (Department of
   Archaeology, Government of Pakistan, 1962), entry 22 (Plate No. 20), full text at
   https://archive.org/details/furg-sikh-shrines-in-west-pakistan-by-khan-mohammad-wal,
   accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability class: official government publication (citable).
   Located in the book's Nankana Sahib cluster (both the table of contents — listed as item 20,
   "Gurdwara Malji Sahib at Nankana Sahib," grouped with Bal Lila, Janam Asthan, Kiara Sahib,
   Maulvi Patti, Tambo Sahib — and the body-text ordering, sitting between entries for Kiara
   Sahib and Maulvi Patti, both at Nankana Sahib), not the book's separate Kanganpur material.
   The entry itself explicitly distinguishes the two trees of the same name, matching this
   file's own original Conflicts analysis below.

For a same-named but geographically distinct gurdwara at Kanganpur, Kasur District (NOT the sheet's target — see Conflicts):

At this Sat Guru said to these people 'Wasde[ ]Raho' (be sea[t]led) and departed." — Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998), full text, p. 91–92 of the scan, at https://archive.org/download/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan_djvu.txt, accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability class: published book (citable). Also in this section: "Its building was constructed in [1]939. It [is] in good condition even today but there is absolutely no maintenance."

## Conflicts

- **Two different gurdwaras share the name "Malji Sahib"/"Mal Ji Sahib," and the sheet's coordinates point to neither of the sources found in citable literature.** Web search results identify at least two sites: (a) a Gurdwara Mal Ji Sahib in Nankana Sahib town, said (only in non-citable wiki/blog sources) to mark a spot where the young Guru Nanak grazed buffaloes and cattle, part of a locally-described "ensemble of nine important gurdwaras" at Nankana Sahib; and (b) Gurdwara Malji Sahib at Kanganpur, Kasur District — a large border town roughly 65 km southeast of Nankana Sahib (Kanganpur coordinates approx. 30°46'N, 74°8'E per a GPS-coordinates lookup site, versus the sheet's 31.456, 73.7125, which is consistent with Nankana Sahib town). Iqbal Qaiser's book, the one strong citable published source located, covers only the Kanganpur gurdwara (with a "Wasde Raho" stone-pelting legend and 1939 construction date) — a different foundation story from the buffalo-grazing legend attached to the Nankana Sahib site in non-citable sources. No section titled or matching a Nankana Sahib "Malji Sahib" was found in the Qaiser book's table of contents/headings (the Nankana Sahib entries present are Janamasthan, Bal Lila, Kiara Sahib, Sachcha Sauda, Sach Khand, and Tambo Sahib — no Malji Sahib heading).
- **No official body source names a Nankana Sahib "Malji Sahib" either.** The official Nankana Sahib district government page ("Important Places," https://nankana.punjab.gov.pk/important_places, and https://nankana.punjab.gov.pk/node/76) names only Gurdwara Janam Asthan explicitly and refers only vaguely to "an ensemble of nine important gurdwaras in Nankana Sahib" without listing them. The Evacuee Trust Property Board's Shrines Branch page (https://etpb.gov.pk/shrines-branch/, fetched 16 August 2026) lists gurdwaras under its administration as "Gurdwara Janam Asthan, Nankana Sahib; Gurdwara Sacha Sodha, Farooqabad; Gurdwara Panja Sahib, Hassanabadal; Gurdwara Dera Sahib, Lahore; Gurdwara Darbar Sahib Kartarpur, Narowal; Gurdwara Rohri Sahib, Eminabad, Gujranwala; Gurdwara Bhai Joga Singh, Peshawar" — Malji Sahib does not appear in this list either.
- **Conclusion of this conflict:** for the specific site the sheet locates at Nankana Sahib, no citable source (book, official body, or established press) was found to confirm even its existence, let alone its history. The one strong citable source located under this name (Qaiser's book) is very likely describing a different physical gurdwara at Kanganpur, Kasur District, and should not be used as a citation for the sheet's Nankana Sahib entry without independent confirmation that the two are the same place.

## Verdict

PARTIAL (updated 16 August 2026, was NOTHING RELIABLE FOUND). A single independent, official
source (the 1962 Department of Archaeology register) now confirms the sheet's Nankana Sahib
site as a real, distinct place with its own foundation legend, separate from the Kanganpur
gurdwara of the same name. Not yet corroborated by a second independent source — the
buffalo/cattle-grazing legend already circulating in non-citable web sources now matches a
citable one, but no second citable source has confirmed it independently.
