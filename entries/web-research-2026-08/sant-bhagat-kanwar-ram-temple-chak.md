# Sant Bhagat Kanwar Ram Temple (Chak)

sant-bhagat-kanwar-ram-temple-chak / researched 16 August 2026 / batch F

## Verified findings

1. **The Chak temple: existence, founding after his 1939 murder, guru relationship, rebuilding in 2006, current managers.**
   Quote: "A beautiful small temple of Sant Bhagat Kanwar Ram (1885-1939)" exists in Chak; he was "a disciple of Sant Satram Das (b.1866-d.1910)" and "He was killed by extremists at Ruk Railway Station." "A temple was built in his name at Chak town after his murder in 1939," "was first managed by Ladak Mal and his family," and "The temple was rebuilt in 2006." Current managers named as Bal Chand Esrani, Tara Chand Thawani, and Prakash Lal Devani. "There are three canopies in the main hall of the temple where one can see the images of Sant Satram Das and pictures of Bhagat Kanwar Ram and his followers."
   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Historic Temples and Darbars of Chak", *The Friday Times*, 2 February 2023, https://www.thefridaytimes.com/02-Feb-2023/historic-temples-and-darbars-of-chak-in-shikarpur, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press.

2. **Location of Chak town.**
   Quote: Chak "is situated in Lakhi Ghulam Shah taluka in Shikarpur district, approximately 24 km east of Lakhi Ghulam Shah town."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 2 February 2023 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

3. **Other temples/darbars of Chak named in the same article** (for cross-reference, not this site): Mata ji Marhi; Shri Paramhans Advait Mat; Amrapur Asthan Prem Prakash; Ganga Ram Ashram and Shivalo; Guru Nanak Darbar; Radhasoami Satsang; Sacho Satram Das Dham and Baba Krishnanand Maharaj temple and a Gurdwara Sahib (all three in Juriyapur Mohallah).
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 2 February 2023 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

4. **Kanwar Ram's biography: birth, guru, murder.**
   Quote: "Kanwar Ram was born on April 13, 1885, at Tarachand's house in Jarwar village of Mirpur Mathelo tehsil." "He was one of the disciples of Satguru Satram Das of Rahrki tehsil." "He loved and used to sing poems of Sufi poets Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, Sachal Sarmast, Sami, Mira and Kabir." "On November 1, 1939, Bhagat was shot and killed in a railway carriage at Ruk Railway Station."
   Citation: Shaikh Abdul Rasheed, "Remembering Bhagat Kanwar Ram", *The News on Sunday*, 26 April 2020, https://www.thenews.com.pk/tns/detail/649256-remembering-bhagat-kanwar-ram, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press.

5. **Annual commemoration at Ruk Railway Station.**
   Quote: "Every year on November 1, a large number of people gather at Ruk Railyway Station in Lakhi tehsil of Shikarpur district to mark the death anniversary." Also, from a second, independent report: "The 77th death anniversary of Shaheed Bhagat Kanwar Ram was marked at Ruk Railway Station by his lovers and other multiple organisations here on Tuesday." "Bhagat Kanwar Ram was born on 13th April in 1885 at village Jarwar, in Sukkur district."
   Citations: Shaikh Abdul Rasheed, *The News on Sunday*, 26 April 2020 (as above); and "Bhagat Kanwar remembered", *The Nation*, 2 November 2016 (staff reporter), https://www.nation.com.pk/02-Nov-2016/bhagat-kanwar-remembered, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (two independent outlets/bylines).

6. **His memorial corner at the Institute of Sindhology, and reported death-anniversary framing by his son (a different, older commemoration site than the Chak temple).**
   Quote: the function marked his "69th death anniversary at the Institute of Sindhology"; "the vice-chancellor of the Sindh University, Dr Mazharul Haq Siddiqui, who presided over the function announced establishment of Bhagat Kanwar Ram Memorial Corner." "Bhagat Kanwar was equally popular among Hindus and Muslims of Sindh because of his devotion to Sufism," and "He was a real son of the soil who lived and died for Sindh."
   Citation: "Bhagat Kanwar died for Sindh, says son", *Dawn*, 3 November 2008, https://www.dawn.com/news/919806/bhagat-kanwar-died-for-sindh-says-son, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press. Note: this article does not mention the Chak temple, Ruk, or Raharki by name — it documents a separate, Hyderabad-based commemorative event.

## Conflicts

- **Month of murder:** *The Friday Times*/Kalhoro and *The News on Sunday* both give 1 November 1939 at Ruk Railway Station. However, a WebSearch-only summary (not independently fetched or verified) elsewhere described the killing as occurring "in November 1939" generally, and one prior search summary (not a citation used above) said "December 1939" — the discrepancy was not traced to any single fetched, citable source and is flagged here rather than resolved.
- **Birth village name:** given as "Jarwar village of Mirpur Mathelo tehsil" (The News on Sunday, 2020) versus simply "village Jarwar, in Sukkur district" (The Nation, 2016) — consistent on the village name, but the tehsil/district framing differs (administrative boundaries may have changed between the events described and today; not resolved here).

## Follow-up: Naseem Mughal, *Shikarpur Heritage* (2012) — 5 September 2026

Fetched the full text (archive.org `shikarpurheritage`, file `SHIKARPUR HERITAGE_djvu.txt`, via https://archive.org/download/shikarpurheritage/SHIKARPUR%20HERITAGE_djvu.txt, 8,303 lines) and searched it in full for "Chak," "Kanwar"/"Kunwar," "Bhagat," "Jarwar," "Lakhi," and "Ruk." None of these appear in connection with this site (the only "Lakhi" hits are "Lakhidar," an old-city gate and neighbourhood name inside Shikarpur city itself, unrelated to Lakhi Ghulam Shah taluka or Chak town).

The book's own contents and Chapter 7 ("City of Saints and Sufis," pp.49–58 — read in full) explain why: despite its subtitle ("An Illustrated Journey Through History"), the book is a heritage inventory of Shikarpur *city* specifically. Part Two is organized as an "Inventory of Old Building[s]" by the city's own Union Councils (UC-1 through UC-8), and Chapter 7 names roughly two dozen Sufi saints, every one located by city muhalla, street, or bazaar (e.g. "His shrine is located south of Mukhtiarkar Office," "opposite the Police Station Lakhidar") — none in a village, none Hindu or Nanakpanthi, none near Chak.

Quote (the book's own account of its administrative scope): "Shikarpur taluka is the headquarter of the district which is part of the Larkana region... It has four talukas - Shikarpur, Garhi Yaseen, Khanpur and Lakhi."

Citation: Naseem Mughal, *Shikarpur Heritage: An Illustrated Journey Through History* (Karachi: Sindh Archives, Information & Archives Department, Government of Sindh, 2012), full text via Internet Archive, https://archive.org/details/shikarpurheritage, accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability class: official/heritage body publication (Sindh Archives).

### Revised verdict
STRONG — unchanged. This closes the acquisition lead: the book administratively covers Lakhi taluka (where Chak sits), but its actual content — confirmed by reading Chapter 7 and searching the full text — is confined to Shikarpur city's own streets and buildings and never reaches Chak or any Hindu/Nanakpanthi site. A genuine checked-and-silent negative, for the reason given, not evidence against anything already in the file.
