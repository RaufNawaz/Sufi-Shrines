# Chandragup (Baba Chandragup)

chandragup-baba-chandragup / researched 16 August 2026 / batch D (Hindu temples/sacred sites, Balochistan/Sindh/Punjab)

## Verified findings

1. **Climbing Chandragup is one of the first rituals of the Hinglaj pilgrimage.**
   Quote: "The steep trek to the rim of Chandragup mud volcano is one of the first rituals of the Hinglaj pilgrimage."
   Citation: Gulnaz Khan (photographs by Matthieu Paley), "Hindu Pilgrimage in Pakistan," *National Geographic*, 10 January 2019, https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/muslim-majority-country-hindu-goddess-lives-pakistan-pictures, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (National Geographic).

2. **Coconut offerings into the craters.**
   Quote: "Devotees throw coconuts into the craters to make wishes and thank the gods for answering their prayers."
   Citation: Khan, *National Geographic*, 10 January 2019 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

3. **Shiva association; permission ritual before proceeding to the goddess.**
   Quote: "An image of Shiva adorns the flanks of the Chandragup volcano. Traditionally, pilgrims ask the god for his permission to visit Sati."
   Citation: Khan, *National Geographic*, 10 January 2019 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

4. **Physical difficulty of the climb.**
   Quote: "When you get close to the crater it's muddy and steep. You have the heat, you have the dust. People slip, some are fainting from exhaustion."
   Citation: Khan, *National Geographic*, 10 January 2019 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

5. **Pilgrimage scale (2019 reporting).**
   Quote: "More than 40,000 people flood the monochromatic landscape to honor the goddess Sati and cleanse their sins during Hinglaj Yatra."
   Citation: Khan, *National Geographic*, 10 January 2019 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

6. **"Most famous" of Balochistan's mud volcanoes; crater size and coordinates as reported by Dawn.**
   Quotes: "The most famous is Chandragup. The biggest crater found at 25°33'13.63\"N. 65°44'09.66\"E is about 450 feet (137.16 m) in diameter." — "In the province's Miani Hor-Hingol Valley region, the heights of mud volcanoes range between 800 to 1,550 feet (243.8 to 472.4 m)."

   Quotes: "Chandragup -- located in Hingol National Park in Balochistan -- is the largest mud volcano in Pakistan, standing at 330 feet tall and with a diameter of 49 feet." — "Chandragup is the most renowned mud volcano, where Hindu pilgrims throw flower petals because of their religious beliefs."

**Negative check:** the appellation "Baba Chandragup" in the sheet name was NOT verified by any fetched page. The National Geographic article was specifically re-checked for "Baba Chandragup" / the volcano as a deity embodiment: no such passage. The claim that the volcano is "worshipped as an embodiment of the Hindu god Shiva, and called Baba Chandragup" appears on Wikipedia ("Hingol mud volcanoes"), which cites Times Now (2024) — below the reliability bar; listed under unverified leads.

## Conflicts

- **Height/size:** *Express Tribune* (2024): "330 feet tall and with a diameter of 49 feet." *Dawn* (2014): regional mud volcano heights "range between 800 to 1,550 feet" and the biggest crater is "about 450 feet (137.16 m) in diameter." Wikipedia additionally cites *Muddy Coasts of the World* (2002) for "an almost perfect cone 58 m high" (not fetched). Not resolved here.
- **Pilgrim numbers:** *National Geographic* (2019): "More than 40,000 people"; *Dawn* (May 2025): "around 200,000 pilgrims ... across three days." Different years and possibly different counting bases; both reported as found.
