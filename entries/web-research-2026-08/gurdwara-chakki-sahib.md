# Gurdwara Chakki Sahib

gurdwara-chakki-sahib / researched 16 August 2026 / batch A (historical Sikh gurdwaras in Punjab)

Note on identification: multiple gurdwaras named "Chakki Sahib" / "Rori Sahib" exist across Punjab; the site researched here is the one at Eminabad (Saidpur), Gujranwala district, which is the site the phrase "Gurdwara Chakki Sahib" without further qualifier most commonly denotes in the sources found. No other candidate site under this exact name surfaced in the searches performed.

## Verified findings

1. **Location and relic — inside the town of Saidpur/Eminabad.**
   Quote: "GURDWARA CHAKI SAHIB (Pl. No. 4) ... The mill is venerated by the Sikhs and is placed in a gurdwara known as Chaki Sahib."
   Citation: Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan*, Memoir of the Department of Archaeology in Pakistan No. 3 (Karachi: Department of Archaeology, Ministry of Education & Information, Government of Pakistan, 1962), entry 2, Gujranwala District, Plate No. 4. Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/furg-sikh-shrines-in-west-pakistan-by-khan-mohammad-wal, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: official body / published government report (full-text scan).

2. **Babur's sack of Saidpur and Guru Nanak's captivity at the mill.**
   Quote: "During his campaign against the Lodhi Dynasty of India, Babar assaulted and destroyed Saidpur, now known as Eminabad, in the Gujranwala District. There was a general massacre of Pathan as well as Hindu inhabitants. Guru Nanak's life was spared but he was taken a prisoner and was condemned to grind corn for which purpose a hand mill was supplied to him. The mill, however, miraculously revolved by itself and Guru Nanak had only to put the corn in it. The miracle was reported to the Emperor who after a discourse with the Guru set him as well as all captives of Saidpur free."
   Citation: Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan* (1962), entry 2 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: official body / published government report (full-text scan).

3. **The Emperor Baher (Babar) captured Saidpur in Samvat 1578; grinder story; two rooms surviving from Babar's period; land endowment and Baisakhi fair.**
   Quote: "In Samvat 1578 when the armies of Emperor Baher captured Saidpur (Eminabad), they took Gur Nanak Dev Ji as prisoner along with many other people. He was put to hard labour on a stone grinder. The officials saw that the grinder of Baba Ji was moving on its own. ... A Gurdwara was built at the site where Guru Ji turned grinding wheel in captivity. Even today there are two rooms from the Babar's period. The flag (Nishan Sahib) is fluttering in the courtyard. 14 Ghumaon of land are attached to the shrine. A fair is held on Visakhi."
   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998), p. 56, "Gurdwara Chajkki Sahib at Saidpur (Eminabad) District Gujranwala." Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

4. **Encyclopaedia entry: hymn evidence Guru Nanak was present at the 1521 sack; stonemill relic preserved at Gurdwara Chakki Sahib; SGPC administration 1922–1947.**
   Quotes: "A hymn of Guru Nanak in the Guru Granth Sahib suggests that he was in Eminabad when the town was sacked by Babar in 1521. Janam Sakhis also mention that during the attack by the Mughal force the Guru was held in prison and given a stonemill to ply." — "GURDWARA CHAKKI SAHIB, inside the town, preserved as a relic a stonemill which was believed to be the one which Guru Nanak was made to ply during his brief period of captivity." — "The town claimed historical shrines which were administered by the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee since 28 February 1922 until 1947."
   Citation: "Eminabad," in *The Encyclopaedia of Sikhism*, Vol. II: E–L, ed. Harbans Singh, Editor-in-Chief, 3rd ed. (Patiala: Punjabi University, 2011). Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/encyclopaedia-of-sikhism-2-ed.-harbans-singh, accessed 16 August 2026. The entry's own bibliography lists: Tara Singh, *Sri Gur Tirath Sangrahi* (Amritsar, n.d.); Giani Thakar Singh, *Sri Gurduare Darshan* (Amritsar, 1923); Surindar Singh Kohli, ed., *Janamsakhi Bhai Bala* (Chandigarh, 1975) — not independently fetched here.
   Reliability class: academic reference work (full-text scan).

5. **Present-day fieldwork account: locked door, small plaque, site identified as the spot of the millstone episode.**
   Quotes: "Today, the Gurudwara Chakki Sahib is located at the spot where Nanak was imprisoned and performed this 'magical' deed." — "I stood at the threshold of the gurudwara. A small plaque above its wooden door declared that it was Gurudwara Chakki Sahib, Eimanabad. The door of the place of worship was locked." — "Along with other citizens, he too was imprisoned and forced to work on a stone mill."
   Citation: Haroon Khalid, "A visit to Gujranwala's Eimanabad throws new light on Babur's legacy," *Dawn*, 4 January 2017, https://www.dawn.com/news/1306164, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

## Conflicts

- **Year of Babur's sack of Saidpur/Eminabad:** Qaiser 1998 dates the capture to "Samvat 1578" (no Gregorian conversion given in the quoted text); the Encyclopaedia of Sikhism entry states the town "was sacked by Babar in 1521." The two figures are not obviously reconcilable from the text fetched and are reported as found, not resolved.
- **Guru Nanak's release:** the 1962 Department of Archaeology memoir has Babar personally holding "a discourse with the Guru" before freeing "him as well as all captives of Saidpur"; Qaiser 1998 similarly has the officials reporting the miracle up the chain but frames the release as following directly from Guru Ji's own request ("Baba Ji ordered the release of other prisoners" — per the book text extracted, though the exact clause on who initiated the order differs slightly in emphasis between the two sources).
