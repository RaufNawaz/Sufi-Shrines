# Valmiki Swamiji Mandir (Gracy Lines), Rawalpindi
valmiki-swamiji-mandir-gracy-lines-rawalpindi / researched 16 August 2026 / batch E

## Verified findings

1. **Full name, location, and age (as of 2014).**
   Quote: "the 79-year-old Maharishi Valmik Swamiji Mandir in Chaklala Cantonment" and "Valmiki Mandir or Balaknash Temple, as it is commonly known".
   Citation: Shiraz Hassan, "Historic temple's days may be numbered", *Dawn*, 28 August 2014, https://www.dawn.com/news/1128257, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Dawn).

2. **Built in 1935; hosts religious festivals; one of three active Hindu places of worship.**
   Quote: "Built in 1935, the temple hosts religious festivals" and "Being one of three active places of worship for the Hindu community".
   Citation: Hassan, *Dawn*, 28 August 2014 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

3. **2014 demolition threat.**
   Quote: "might be razed along with several Hindu residences in the area to make way for an educational and housing complex". Community representative Ashok Chand: "We have written to the authorities to resolve the issue amicably and save the historic temple; our only concern is the temple." MNA Isphanyar M. Bhandara: "This is the locality of the poor Hindus, and the temple is their only place of worship in the area." (The article also reports eviction notices of 12 August 2014 and a stay order of 21 August 2014, effective to 13 September — dates as reported by the article.)
   Citation: Hassan, *Dawn*, 28 August 2014 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

4. **Name, custodian, and 1935 date reconfirmed in 2025; serves Islamabad's Hindus.**
   Quote: "Budh Raj, 76, the custodian of the Maharishi Valmiki Swamiji Mandir in Rawalpindi"; custodian quoted: "In 1935, our temple was built."; and "Indeed, Islamabad has no functional Hindu temple, which means many of the capital city's residents travel to neighboring Rawalpindi to worship at the Valmiki Mandir."
   Citation: Uroosa Jadoon, "'No temple of their own': Islamabad's Hindus still wait for promised place of worship", *Arab News*, 29 August 2025, https://www.arabnews.com/node/2613389/pakistan, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Arab News, Pakistan bureau).

5. **Condition deteriorating (2025); pre-Partition context.**
   Quote: "worn carpet covered broken cement and chapped walls"; custodian quoted: "We had two to three mandirs in the cantonment area" and "After the Hindu population migrated, the temples were left vacant."
   Citation: Jadoon, *Arab News*, 29 August 2025 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

6. **Still functional, December 2025.**
   Quote: "Only three temples remain functional in Rawalpindi including Krishna Temple (Saddar), Valmiki Temple (Gracey Lines), and Lal Kurti Temple. All three are over a century old and in usable condition."
   Citation: Qaiser Shirazi, "Rawalpindi's Hindu heritage fades", *The Express Tribune*, 16 December 2025, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2582349/rawalpindis-hindu-heritage-fades, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Express Tribune).

7. **Described as ancient and small (2023).**
   Quote: "the ancient and small Maharishi Valmik Swamiji Mandir in Gracy Lines".
   Citation: Qaiser Shirazi, "A tale of overlooked places of worship", *The Express Tribune*, 23 January 2023, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2397318/a-tale-of-overlooked-places-of-worship, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Express Tribune).

8. **Diwali celebrated here (2024).**
   Quote: "Diwali celebrations took place at three main temples in Rawalpindi, including Krishna Temple in Saddar Cantt, Lal Kurti Temple, and the primary temple in Gracy Lines."
   Citation: "Pindi lit up as Hindus celebrate 'Festival of Lights'", *The Express Tribune*, 1 November 2024, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2506605/pindi-lit-up-as-hindus-celebrate-festival-of-lights, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Express Tribune).

## Conflicts

- **Age:** *The Express Tribune* (16 December 2025) states all three functional Rawalpindi temples, including this one, are "over a century old" — but *Dawn* (2014: "Built in 1935", "79-year-old") and *Arab News* (2025: "In 1935, our temple was built") both give 1935, which would make it ~90 years old in 2025. Reported, not resolved.
