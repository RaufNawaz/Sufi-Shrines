# Shrine of Pir Chhatal Shah Noorani
shrine-of-pir-chhatal-shah-noorani / researched 16 August 2026 / batch H

## Verified findings

- **Location: at the mouth of the Moola (Mula) Pass through the Kirthar range, near Gandava, close to Kotra.** Salman Rashid's Dawn travel piece places the shrine where the Moola River "shears the rocky Kirthar barrier to reach Gandava," describing the site a short distance north of the river's exit from the gorge: "Just five kilometres north of where the Europeans trekked into the Moola gorge, there was a right lovely little sylvan spot." — Salman Rashid, "TRAVEL: PIR CHHATAL'S MYSTICAL FISH," *Dawn*, 18 October 2020, https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1585635 (established press). Accessed 16 August 2026.
  Independently, the Jhal Magsi district government's own official page lists "Pir Chattal Shah (near Kotra)" among the district's main shrines, distinct from "Pir Lakha" and other named shrines. — "Culture & Heritage," Jhal Magsi District Government of Balochistan, archived copy: http://web.archive.org/web/20250426101741/https://jhalmagsi.balochistan.gov.pk/culture-heritage/ (official body; live URL returned a server "Database Error" at time of research, so this Wayback Machine snapshot dated 26 April 2025 was used). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **19th-century colonial-era documentation of the shrine.** Charles Masson, travelling through the area, recorded in 1831: "a conspicuous gumbaz, or domed building, the ziarat of Pir Chatta" — quoted in Salman Rashid, "TRAVEL: PIR CHHATAL'S MYSTICAL FISH," *Dawn*, 18 October 2020 (as above). Accessed 16 August 2026. (Masson's own original multi-volume *Narrative of Various Journeys in Balochistan, Afghanistan, and the Panjab* is on archive.org — see Acquisition leads — but a full-text search of several of its scanned volumes for "Chatta" during this session returned no matches, so the original passage itself could not be independently located and confirmed this session; it is reported here only as quoted within the Dawn article.)

- **The saint's identity is explicitly stated as unknown/uncertain by a shrine keeper interviewed on site.** Asked who the saint was, the keeper told the author: "Only Allah knows!" — adding that "Chhatal Shah Noorani was a granter of wishes. Hindus and Muslims alike came here and their hearts' desires were fulfilled." Salman Rashid, "TRAVEL: PIR CHHATAL'S MYSTICAL FISH," *Dawn*, 18 October 2020 (as above). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **Sacred fish and the pond.** "Back in 2003, on my first trip...I had found the pond teeming with mahashir fish, nearly 60 cm long." A local legend held that "if caught, cooked and feasted upon, the fish emerged from the sphincter the next day, alive and flopping." Salman Rashid, "TRAVEL: PIR CHHATAL'S MYSTICAL FISH," *Dawn*, 18 October 2020 (as above). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **Structure/condition (as of the author's visits).** "In 2003, I had found an ugly, roofless wart of four walls girding the elaborately draped and turbaned grave" — indicating the dome documented by Masson in 1831 had by then collapsed. Salman Rashid, "TRAVEL: PIR CHHATAL'S MYSTICAL FISH," *Dawn*, 18 October 2020 (as above). Accessed 16 August 2026.

## Conflicts

- **900-year age claim is not corroborated by a citable source.** Several non-citable sources (travel blogs, social media posts) describe the shrine as "900 years old," but this figure did not appear in either of the two citable sources fetched (Dawn travel piece; Jhal Magsi district government page). Not used as a verified finding; flagged here so it is not silently dropped.
- **Precise administrative placement varies by source.** The official Jhal Magsi district page places "Pir Chattal Shah" specifically "near Kotra." The Dawn travel account frames the location relative to Gandava and the Moola Pass/Kirthar range without mentioning Kotra. These are not necessarily contradictory (Kotra could be the nearest named settlement within the Gandava/Moola-pass area) but the two sources do not use the same reference point, so exact placement should be treated as approximate pending a map cross-check.

## Verdict

STRONG — two independent, citable sources were fetched and verified with verbatim quotes: an established-press feature (Dawn, Salman Rashid) providing an eyewitness/colonial-source account, and an official government body's own heritage listing (Jhal Magsi district government, via Wayback Machine archive) independently confirming the shrine's existence and approximate location. The saint's biographical identity remains explicitly unknown per the cited sources themselves.
