# Lal Kurti Temple (Balmiki Mandir), Rawalpindi
lal-kurti-temple-balmiki-mandir-rawalpindi / researched 16 August 2026 / batch E

## Verified findings

1. **Only remaining Hindu temple in Lal Kurti; belongs to the Balmiki community; 60+ Hindus in the locality.**
   Quote: "Before Pakistan's creation, there were two Hindu temples in the area, but now only one remains." and "It belongs to low-caste Hindus and is called the Balmiki Temple. More than 60 Hindus reside in the area."
   Citation: Aamir Yasin, "British colonial legacy lives on in Rawalpindi's Lal Kurti", *Dawn*, 12 May 2025, https://www.dawn.com/news/1910410, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Dawn).

2. **Built in 1905; still in active worship.**
   Quote: "built in 1905" … "still stands strong, with worship continuing to this day." (The sentence in the original reads as one construction: "Balmik Temple — built in 1905 — still stands strong, with worship continuing to this day"; punctuation was lost in extraction.)
   Citation: Qaiser Shirazi, "Century-old Balmik Temple stands test of time", *The Express Tribune*, 27 August 2025, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2563394/century-old-balmik-temple-stands-test-of-time, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Express Tribune).

3. **Mughal-style building, maintained; festivals celebrated.**
   Quote: "Its building reflects Mughal architectural style, and thanks to regular maintenance, it remains structurally strong and impressive even after more than a century." and "celebrates all major Hindu festivals, including Diwali, Holi, and Raksha Bandhan".
   Citation: Shirazi, *Express Tribune*, 27 August 2025 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

4. **Caretaker Om Prakash Narain, 80.**
   Quote: "At 80 years old, he is also a member of the District Peace Committee, Interfaith Harmony Committee, and serves as General Secretary of the Pakistan Hindu Balmik Welfare Society."
   Citation: Shirazi, *Express Tribune*, 27 August 2025 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

5. **Grave of Kheera Lal (post-Partition administrator) inside the temple.**
   Quote: "as per his will, Kheera Lal was buried inside a side portion of the temple. His grave still exists and is adorned with flowers by devotees."
   Citation: Shirazi, *Express Tribune*, 27 August 2025 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

6. **Hindu population context for the twin cities.**
   Quote: "Around 6,000 Hindus currently reside in the twin cities."
   Citation: Shirazi, *Express Tribune*, 27 August 2025 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

7. **One of only three functional Hindu temples in Rawalpindi (as of Dec 2025).**
   Quote: "Only three temples remain functional in Rawalpindi including Krishna Temple (Saddar), Valmiki Temple (Gracey Lines), and Lal Kurti Temple. All three are over a century old and in usable condition."
   Citation: Qaiser Shirazi, "Rawalpindi's Hindu heritage fades", *The Express Tribune*, 16 December 2025, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2582349/rawalpindis-hindu-heritage-fades, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Express Tribune).

8. **Active in 2014, known locally as Valmiki temple.**
   Quote: "There is an active temple here too, known as Valmiki temple, where Hindus celebrate their religious festivals."
   Citation: Shiraz Hassan, "Lal Kurti – an epitome of religious co-existence", *Dawn*, 21 August 2014, https://www.dawn.com/news/1126728, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Dawn).

9. **Diwali celebrated here (2024).**
   Quote: "Diwali celebrations took place at three main temples in Rawalpindi, including Krishna Temple in Saddar Cantt, Lal Kurti Temple, and the primary temple in Gracy Lines."
   Citation: "Pindi lit up as Hindus celebrate 'Festival of Lights'", *The Express Tribune*, 1 November 2024, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2506605/pindi-lit-up-as-hindus-celebrate-festival-of-lights, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Express Tribune).

**Disambiguation (verified, different site):** a *distinct* ruined Balmiki temple exists in Rawalpindi's Purana Qila — "Guru Balmik Swamiji's Mandir", built 1880: "It was a magnificent triple-story temple. Its valuable wood was stolen and shops were built here." (Qaiser Shirazi, "A tale of overlooked places of worship", *The Express Tribune*, 23 January 2023, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2397318/a-tale-of-overlooked-places-of-worship, accessed 16 August 2026.) Do not conflate with the Lal Kurti temple.
