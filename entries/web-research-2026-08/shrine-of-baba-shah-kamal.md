# Shrine of Baba Shah Kamal
shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal / researched 16 August 2026 / batch G

Note on the file_slug's instruction to "verify which city from sources — do not assume": two
independently-fetched sources this session place the shrine in Lahore (Ichhra), matching the
dataset's existing location field ("Muslim Town / Samanabad (near Ichhra), Lahore"). A third,
non-citable source claims Kasur instead — see Conflicts.

## Verified findings

- **Official government listing: Lahore.** Punjab's Auqaf & Religious Affairs Department's own
  shrine listing includes, under its Lahore Zone, an entry titled "Darbar & Jamia Masjid Hazrat
  Shah Kamal." — https://auqaf.punjab.gov.pk/shrines, accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability:
  official body (provincial government department). The page does not give further descriptive
  detail beyond the zone/title listing itself.
- **Location, custody, and description: Ichhra, Lahore.** "His tomb is located in the populated
  area of Lahore called Shah Kamal in the area of Ichhra," with the specific address given as
  "Karnalpura, Muslim Town, Combo Colony, Lahore, Punjab 54030, Pakistan." "Hazrat Shah Kamal
  was the elder brother of Sufi Saint Shah Jamal." "The whole field of the mosque and the tomb
  itself was built in the corner of a place that incorporates a large graveyard." Condition
  "Satisfactory"; "Owned By: Auqaf Department." "A congregator of devotees is held every
  Thursday night." — Chughtai (Public) Library Digital Library, "Historic Monuments of Lahore —
  Mughal Period — Shrine of Hazrat Shah Kamal,"
  https://www.chughtailibrary.com/digital_library/place_detail.php?plc_id=35, accessed 16
  August 2026. Reliability: curated heritage-library digital archive (not a government body
  itself, but an archival/heritage catalogue, not a blog or aggregator). No construction date is
  given.

## Conflicts

- **Lahore (Ichhra) vs. Kasur.** A travel-content site, beautyofpakistan.com, describes a
  "Shrine of Baba Kamal Chishti" as being in Kasur, on a hilltop on Ferozepur Road. This is
  explicitly the kind of source the README's reliability bar excludes ("Tripadvisor/travel
  aggregators... not citable"), so it is not treated as a verified conflicting citation — but it
  is different enough (different nisba, "Chishti" vs. the Suhrawardi attribution used elsewhere
  for the Ichhra saint; a hilltop shrine vs. an urban Ichhra tomb) that it may simply be a
  different, unrelated saint with a similar name, rather than a genuine rival claim about the
  same shrine. Flagged for awareness, not resolved.

## Verdict

STRONG — two independent, directly-fetched sources (Punjab's official Auqaf & Religious
Affairs Department listing, and the Chughtai Library's heritage digital archive) both place
this shrine in Lahore's Ichhra area, matching the archive's existing location data. Neither
source gives a founding date, architectural period beyond "Mughal Period" as a collection
label, or detailed biography of Shah Kamal himself.
