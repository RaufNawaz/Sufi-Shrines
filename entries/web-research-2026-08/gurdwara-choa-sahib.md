# Gurdwara Choa Sahib

gurdwara-choa-sahib / researched 16 August 2026 / batch A (historical Sikh gurdwaras in Punjab)

## Verified findings

1. **Location — outside the Rohtas Fort walls, Jhelum district.**
   Quote: "The sacred site of Gurdwara Chowa Sahib is showing its splendour outside Kabuli Darwaza (gate) of Rohtas fort on the river bank of River Ghan."
   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998), p. 178. Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan (text at https://archive.org/stream/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan_djvu.txt), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan on archive.org).

2. **Guru Nanak association and the name.**
   Quote: "Jagat Guru Nanak Dev Ji arrived here from Tila Jogian. At that time the place was unpopulated. He started the water to flow a spring using his stick. Water spring is called \"Chowa\" hence this place was got the name of Chowa Sahib."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998, p. 178 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

3. **Present building 1834, by order of Ranjit Singh; endowment and fair.**
   Quotes: "The present building was constructed in 1834 at the command of Maharaja Ranjit Singh." — "Maharaja Ranjit Singh assigned 27 ghumaon of land and Rs 260 to it. Fair is held on the 15th of Kattak Suddi."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998, p. 178 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

4. **Guru Nanak spring legend; Charat Singh's first structure; 1834 building.**
   Quotes: "Guru Nanak is said to have then struck the earth with his cane and moved a stone, thereby revealing a natural spring." — the first commemorative structure was built by Charat Singh, who "installed a sarovar pool, and area for recitation of the Guru Granth Sahib" — "The current building dates from 1834, and was commissioned by Maharaja Ranjit Singh."
   Citation: "A Complete Tour Guide To Ghurdwara Choa Sahib, Pakistan," Ministry of Religious Affairs and Interfaith Harmony, Government of Pakistan, https://www.mora.gov.pk/Detail/NGVhZjViMTYtNzEzOS00M2JjLTk3YTktYjIzZWIxMjhmNDg2, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: official body (Government of Pakistan). **Caveat:** this page's wording closely mirrors the English Wikipedia article "Gurdwara Chowa Sahib" — it should be treated as corroboration of the standard account, not as independent research.

5. **2019 restoration directive — Walled City of Lahore Authority.**
   Quote: "the WCLA delegation visited the Rohtas Fort and inspected the Gurdwara Choa Sahib and Janam Asthan Mata Sahib Kaur and directed the local administration to restore these sites."
   Citation: "WCLA to help restore Sikh sites in Jhelum," *Dawn*, 2 January 2019, https://www.dawn.com/news/1454931, accessed 16 August 2026. The inspection was led by WCLA Director General Kamran Lashari and Conservation Director Najamul Saqib.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

6. **Opened for Sikh worship in August 2019 after ETPB restoration; closed since 1947.**
   Quotes: "Seventy-two years after Partition, Pakistan on Friday opened the doors of the historic Gurdwara Choa Sahib" — "Commissioned by Maharaja Ranjit Singh, the gurdwara was completed in 1834" — "Lying closed since 1947 after Sikh community members from the area migrated". Chief guest at the opening was Dr Aamer Ahmed, chairman of the Evacuee Trust Property Board (ETPB). ETPB spokesperson: "Gurdwara Choa Sahib has been opened for worship and visit. It is also opened for foreign Sikhs...whether they come from India or any other part of the world".
   Citation: "72 years after Partition, Pak opens Gurdwara Choa Sahib for Sikh devotees," *The Tribune* (Chandigarh), 2 August 2019 (updated 3 August 2019), https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/punjab/72-years-after-partition-pak-opens-gurdwara-choa-sahib-for-sikh-devotees-811964, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (The Tribune, India).

7. **Proximity to UNESCO World Heritage property.**
   Quote: "The Gurdwara Choa Sahib, located close to Rohtas Fort -- a UNESCO world heritage site".
   Citation: *The Tribune* (Chandigarh), 2 August 2019 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

Pointer note: the English Wikipedia article "Gurdwara Chowa Sahib" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwara_Chowa_Sahib, fetched 16 August 2026) cites, among others: Iqbal Qaisar, *Pakistan vich Sikhan dian Tawarikhi Pavittar Thavan* (Punjabi History Board, 2001), p. 412; *The Sikh Courier* vols 9–12 (Sikh Cultural Society of Great Britain, 1977); Kirapala Singh & Prithipala Kapur, *Janamsakhi Tradition: An Analytical Study* (Singh Brothers, 2004), p. 174. Wikipedia also states the current renovation is by the US-based Sikh organization "Ranjit Nagara" since 2020 (its cited source, the Ranjit Nagara projects page, was not fetched).

## Conflicts

- **Which gate:** Qaiser 1998 (p. 178) places the gurdwara "outside Kabuli Darwaza (gate) of Rohtas fort"; the MORA page (mirroring Wikipedia) places it "at the northern edge of the Rohtas Fort" and "near the fort's Talaqi gate". Not resolved here.
- **Who restored it:** *The Tribune* (Aug 2019) reports the ETPB restoring and opening the gurdwara; the MORA page states "In 2020, a Sikh community organization based in USA, Ranjit Nagara, took over the rehabilitation and renovation of the Gurdwara." Possibly sequential rather than contradictory; both reported as found.
- **River name:** Qaiser names the adjacent stream "River Ghan" / "Ghan Nadi" (as printed in the OCR'd scan); no other fetched source names the river.
