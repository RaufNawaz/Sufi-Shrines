## Re-verification against the complete text — 5 September 2026

The "5 September" follow-up above was itself produced with a truncating fetch, and its own
"unreachable" conclusion is superseded here. All three scans download completely with
`pipeline/fetch_archive_text.py`, and one of the three (`in.gov.ignca.30718`) was independently
re-checked by the requester before this pass began, with the same result reported below.

- **`in.gov.ignca.30716`**, *Gazetteers of the Sialkot District, 1894-95* — **587,636 bytes**
  (matches the original August pass's own estimate of "~731 KB," within OCR-text/HTML-rendering
  variance; this is the full book, not a fragment). Searched for `Allo`, `Alo Mahar`, `Alu
  Mahar`, `Channan`, `Chanan`, `Naqshband`, `Nakshband`, `Nuri`, `Mujad`, `Amin` — **zero
  matches for every one of Allo Mahar's own proper nouns**; the only `Amin` hit is an unrelated
  "Amin Bakhsh of Chowinda." Fuzzy variants (`allu`, `aloo`, `alo m`, `chana`, `amin shah`, `shah
  nuri`) returned only unrelated noise (`alluvial`, a stray column fragment reading "Valoo").
  Crucially, this scan's OCR is **good enough to be trusted on a negative**: a full "Fairs" and
  "Temples and shrines" section was located and read in the clear, confirming the search method
  works on this book generally, not merely failing everywhere:

  > "In Diska [Daska] the Rai Jats attach particular importance to the tomb of Pir Bawar Nath in
  > Salhoke. In Marana is the shrine of Kala Mahr, which is reverenced by the Sindhas."

  This is the book's own inventory of every shrine of note in Daska tehsil and the surrounding
  district (also naming Kotli Fakir Chand, Ber Baba Nanak, the tomb of Imam Sahib at Sialkot, the
  Gulu Shah fair at Koreke, and others) — Allo Mahar and Channan Shah Nuri are absent from a list
  that names smaller, more obscure shrines than a saint with a still-observed annual urs would be.
  **Near-miss ruled out explicitly:** "Kala Mahr" (a personal/caste name, "Mahr" not "Mahar,"
  reverenced by Sindhu Jats at Marana, not Daska) is a different site from Allo Mahar Sharif and
  must not be conflated with it.

  Citation: *Gazetteer of the Sialkot District, 1894-95* (Punjab, 1895), Chapter III "The
  People," Section B, "Temples and shrines" / "Fairs," full text via Internet Archive,
  https://archive.org/details/in.gov.ignca.30716, accessed 5 September 2026 (587,636 bytes,
