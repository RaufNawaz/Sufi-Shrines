# Samadhi of Maharaja Ranjit Singh
samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh / researched 16 August 2026 / batch C

## Verified findings

1. Location and general architectural character, from a near-contemporary (1892) account.

   > "Opposite the fort entrance, to the west, is the mausoleum of Maharaja Ranjit Singh,
   > virtually the last native monarch of the Panjab. It is a mixture of Hindu and Mahomedan
   > architecture, substantially Hindu with Mahomedan details, and is built of stone in a plain,
   > unpretending style."

   Citation: Syad Muhammad Latif, *Lahore: Its History, Architectural Remains and Antiquities*
   (Lahore: New Imperial Press, 1892), p. 129. Full text:
   https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.107067/2015.107067.Lahore-Its-History-Architectural-Remains-And-Antiquities_djvu.txt
   — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability: colonial-era published book, full-text scan on
   archive.org — citable.

2. Construction history: begun under Kharak Singh, continued by Sher Singh, only finished under
   Dalip Singh.

   > "The building was commenced by Kharak Singh, but his untimely death prevented him from
   > completing it. Sher Singh executed part of the work, but it could not be finished until the
   > latter period of Dalip Singh's reign."

   Citation: same as #1.

3. Contents of the samadh — the marble lotus covering Ranjit Singh's ashes, and the smaller
   flowers for the wives/slave-girls who died on his pyre.

   > "The carved marble lotus flower in the central vault, set beneath a canopy, covers the
   > ashes of the great Maharaja, and the smaller flowers of the same description around are in
   > memory of his four wives and seven slave girls, who immolated themselves on the funeral pyre
   > of their deceased lord."

   Citation: same as #1.

4. Independent, Sikh-authored account (1998) giving the death date, cremation, and location
   relative to Badshahi Mosque.

   > "A domed building is adjacent to the southern corner of eastern wall of Badshahi Mosque
   > outside Roshanai Gate. This building is the Samadh of Sher-e-Punjab Maharaja Ranjit Singh,
   > who ruled over Punjab and Kashmir for forty years. He died of paralysis on 15th Har Samvat
   > 1896 (27th June 1839) and was cremated at this spot on the next day."

   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (1998), full-text scan,
   pp. 388–389. https://archive.org/stream/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan_djvu.txt
   — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability: published book — citable.

5. Same source, on the sati victims and present-day management.

   > "The samadh of Maharaja Ranjit Singh at its centre is surrounded by the samadhs of his
   > eleven queens who were burnt alive (sati) with the dead body of their husband. The Samadhs
   > of two pigeons burnt while flying over the flames of cremation fire are also there."
   > ... "Its control is in the hands of Evacuee Trust Board these days."

   Citation: same as #4, pp. 389–390.

6. Official recognition as a protected monument.

   The Punjab Directorate General of Archaeology's monument index lists "Samadh of Ranjit
   Singh, Kharak Singh and Nau Nihal Singh, Lahore" under "Sikh Rule (18th–19th century)" — the
   listing itself, with no further narrative text.

   Citation: Directorate General of Archaeology, Government of Punjab, "Religious Monuments,"
   https://archaeology.punjab.gov.pk/religious_monuments — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability:
   official provincial heritage body — citable.

7. Established-press coverage of ongoing scholarship on the monument (Dr Nadhra Shahbaz Naeem
   Khan's PhD dissertation on its ornamental programme; her discovery — and subsequent
   re-loss — of frescoes under whitewash).

   > "...guardian figures [discovered on the eastern facade]... they have since been covered
   > with more layers and lost again."

   Citation: Bushra Shehzad, "Rediscovering our Sikh heritage," Dawn, 24 May 2012,
   https://www.dawn.com/news/721003/rediscovering-our-sikh-heritage — accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Dawn) — citable.

## Conflicts

- **Origin of the structure.** Dunya News (15 August 2026) states: "The structure originally
  served as a baradari where Maharaja Ranjit Singh held royal gatherings before it was converted
  into a memorial Samadhi." This is not corroborated by either Latif (1892) or Qaiser (1998),
  both of whom describe the building as commissioned as a samadh/mausoleum only after Ranjit
  Singh's death, begun by his son Kharak Singh. Reported, not resolved.
- **Disposition of the ashes.** Dunya News also states: "The Samadhi houses commemorative urns
  associated with the Maharaja's ashes, while tradition holds that most of his ashes were
  immersed in the River Ganges." Latif (1892) and Qaiser (1998) instead describe the marble
  lotus in the central vault as directly covering the Maharaja's ashes on-site, with no mention
  of a Ganges immersion. Reported, not resolved.
- **Specificity of who completed construction.** Dunya News: "Construction of the memorial was
  initiated by Maharaja Kharak Singh and later completed during the reign of subsequent Sikh
  rulers" (unspecific). Latif is more precise: begun by Kharak Singh, partly executed by Sher
  Singh, only finished "in the latter period of Dalip Singh's reign." Not a direct contradiction,
  but a real difference in specificity, noted for completeness.
