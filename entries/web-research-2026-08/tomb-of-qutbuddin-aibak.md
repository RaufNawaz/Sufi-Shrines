# Tomb of Qutbuddin Aibak
tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak / researched 16 August 2026 / batch C

## Verified findings

1. Who he was and the circumstances of his death.

   > "Qutab-ud-Din Aibk, the first Sultan of India and founder of Khandan-e-Ghulaman (Dynasty of
   > Slaves) as well as successor of Shahab-ud-Din Ghori died as result of fall from horse while
   > playing Chaugan (polo) in 1210 A.D."

   Citation: Directorate General of Archaeology, Government of Punjab, "Tomb of Qutb-ud-Din
   Aibak, Anarkali Bazar Lahore," https://archaeology.punjab.gov.pk/qutbuddin_aibak — accessed
   16 August 2026. Reliability: official provincial heritage body — citable.

2. History of the grave/tomb structure, per the same official source: originally unmarked, a
   marble tomb later pulled down by Ranjit Singh, walled off under the British, then rebuilt.

   > "Originally there was only one grave without any tomb or structure." ... "According to
   > British period historical accounts there was a marble tomb which was pulled down by Ranjit
   > Singh and then in British period a specific area surrounded with a wall for protection." ...
   > "The Department of Archaeology and Museums, Pakistan built the present tomb over the grave
   > in 1990, by acquiring two houses around the grave."

   Citation: same as #1.

3. Architecture and location.

   > The tomb is "built in Central Asian style also given some touches of local architectural
   > traditions," with "a three step staircase" to enter, and stands "on Aibk road (known as
   > Anarkali Street) opposite to the Lahori/Lohari gate across the road towards New Anarkali,
   > behind Paan Bazar."

   Citation: same as #1.

4. Independent, established-press account of a 1972 reconstruction order by Prime Minister
   Z.A. Bhutto, with a named architect — contradicting the official page's "1990" date (see
   Conflicts).

   > "In 1972, after the Bangladesh War, Prime Minister Z.A. Bhutto inquired about the grave's
   > condition." Bhutto is quoted as remarking: "He was a great and kind ruler and deserved the
   > respect of the poor." The Lahore Department of Archaeology designed the mausoleum in
   > "Turkish Indian design," with Kamil Khan Mumtaz heading the architects' committee.

   Citation: Majid Sheikh, "Harking Back: The once 'mud' grave of slave Sultan Aibak," Dawn,
   24 August 2025, https://www.dawn.com/news/1936731 — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability:
   established press (Dawn), "Harking Back" column — citable.

5. Same columnist, a separate piece, on the grave's earlier history (mud room, later bricked
   under Akbar) and Aibak's broader plan for a large graveyard south of Lahore (context for the
   present Miani Sahib graveyard).

   > Aibak's grave, before Iltutmish, was left as recounted: Iltutmish "ordered that a simple
   > small room, one barely able to enclose the grave be made. Initially, it was a mud room.
   > Later on, it was bricked in the reign of Akbar." Separately: Aibak "did not like graves
   > scattered all around the walled city" and instead "wanted them at one large space," ordering
   > "a large piece of land in the south of the village of Mozang" designated as a graveyard
   > capable of housing "at least 150,000 dead."

   Citation: Majid Sheikh, "Harking back: Massive graveyard that Aibak envisaged," Dawn,
   2 June 2024, https://www.dawn.com/news/1837198 — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability:
   established press (Dawn) — citable.

6. A third independent press account, giving "the 1970s" (rather than 1990) for the present
   structure, and describing the tomb's neglected condition today.

   > "The tomb was built, in its present form, during the 1970s by the Department of Archaeology
   > and Museums, Pakistan." Before that, "the Sultan's grave, in its simple form, was enclosed
   > by residential houses." Yellow stone and a marble dome were used in the reconstruction.

   Citation: Dr Syed Talha Shah, "Qutbuddin Aibak: The forgotten tomb of a slave sultan," Daily
   Times, 23 December 2018, https://dailytimes.com.pk/277030/qutbuddin-aibak-the-forgotten-tomb-of-a-slave-sultan/
   — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability: established press (Daily Times) — citable.

## Conflicts

- **Date the present tomb structure was built.** The Punjab Directorate General of
  Archaeology's own page states flatly the department "built the present tomb over the grave in
  1990." A Dawn "Harking Back" column (24 August 2025) instead places the initiating order in
  1972 (Prime Minister Z.A. Bhutto, after the Bangladesh war, with architect Kamil Khan Mumtaz
  heading the design committee), and Daily Times (2018) independently says "the 1970s." All
  three sources are citable; the discrepancy (1990 vs. early-to-mid 1970s, roughly two decades
  apart) is reported here, not resolved. It is possible the 1972 date marks the order/design and
  1990 the actual completion, but no source found states this reconciliation explicitly.
- **Existence of a pre-Ranjit Singh marble tomb.** The Archaeology Department's page states
  there was once "a marble tomb which was pulled down by Ranjit Singh," implying a formal
  structure predating or contemporary with his 1799–1839 rule. No other source found in this
  pass corroborates or elaborates on this marble tomb's existence or date.

## Acquisition leads

- Syad Muhammad Latif, *Lahore: Its History, Architectural Remains and Antiquities* (1892) —
  full text already downloaded and searched for this batch (see the Samadhi and Jain Mandir
  files for the same source). Despite extensive coverage of Lahore's Mughal- and Sikh-era
  monuments, and of Aibak's reign as a historical figure (in the general history sections), it
  contains **no discrete passage on his tomb or grave in Anarkali**. This is a genuine gap in an
  otherwise encyclopedic 1892 source, consistent with the grave being unmarked/obscure at the
  time of Latif's writing (matching the Archaeology Department's "only one grave without any
  tomb or structure" description).
- Majid Sheikh's Dawn "Harking Back" columns are a rich, recurring vein specifically on Aibak
  and the Miani Sahib graveyard; the two used above are the most substantive found, but at least
  two further columns (listed under Unverified leads) were not checked this pass.

## Verdict

STRONG — at least three independent, citable sources verified directly (the Directorate
General of Archaeology's own page, Dawn's "Harking Back" column across two separate pieces by
Majid Sheikh, and Daily Times), with the reconstruction date (1990 vs. the 1970s) reported as a
genuine, unresolved conflict between an official source and established press.
