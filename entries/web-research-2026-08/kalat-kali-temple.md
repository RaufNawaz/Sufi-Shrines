# Kalat Kali Temple

kalat-kali-temple / researched 16 August 2026 / batch D (Hindu temples/sacred sites, Balochistan/Sindh/Punjab)

## Verified findings

1. **Oldest place of Hindu worship in Kalat; perpetual lamp historically state-supplied.**
   Quote: "Kali is worshipped mostly in Bhag, Kalat and Mastung, and the temple of Kali of Kalat is probably the oldest place of Hindu worship in the country. Here a jōt or lamp is kept burning day and night, and the oil required for it was, until recent times, supplied by the State."
   Citation: Denys Bray, *Ethnographic Survey of Baluchistan*, Vol. 2 (Bombay: The Times Press, 1913), section on "Other forms of worship of Devi." Full text via Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.103853, text fetched at https://dn760103.eu.archive.org/0/items/in.ernet.dli.2015.103853/2015.103853.Ethnographic-Survey-Of-Baluchistan-Vol2_djvu.txt, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book, full-text scan (colonial-era ethnographic survey) — the strongest and earliest source found for this site.

2. **Origin of the name "Kalat" linked to a Hindu ruler, per a local Hindu priest.**
   Quote: "The name of Kalat comes from a Hindu ruler, Kalat Seva. It was after the arrival of the Arabs and the Baloch that the Hindus' domination in the region dwindled."
   Citation: Muhammad Akbar Notezai, "Footprints: Descendants of Royalty," *Dawn*, 15 October 2017, https://www.dawn.com/news/1363933, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

3. **Contemporary temple leadership and worship, and Hindu population of Kalat city.**
   Quotes: "young Maharaj Sain Shamgir leads around 35 men in prayer at an old Kali Mata Temple." — "There are currently 200 Hindu households in Kalat city, and if -- suppose -- some 100 households had migrated, 80 of them are back in Kalat."
   Citation: Notezai, *Dawn*, 15 October 2017 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

4. **Second-largest Kali statue in Asia; three-day annual festival.**
   Quotes: "The second-largest Kali Mata statue in Asia is placed at the temple." — "The main purpose of the festival is to bind and unite the people with love and peace." — "Hindu community from across the country reached Kalat in large numbers to participate in the annual festival." — "Law enforcement agencies have made effective security arrangements at all entrance and exit points."
   Citation: "Three-day annual festival starts at Kalat's Kali Mandir," Associated Press of Pakistan, published by *The Express Tribune*, 24 January 2023, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2397505/three-day-annual-festival-starts-at-kalats-kali-mandir, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Express Tribune, wire copy).

## Conflicts

- **Dating the temple:** Bray (1913) calls it "probably the oldest place of Hindu worship" in Balochistan without giving a founding date or century. Popular sources aggregated by Wikipedia (not independently verified in this pass — see below) circulate figures such as founding "in 74 Christian era" by "the Sewa dynasty" and a "1,500-year-old" age; none of these specific figures were confirmed against a fetched primary source in this pass. Reported here as an open gap, not resolved.
- **Name origin:** the Dawn (2017) source attributes the city's name to a ruler "Kalat Seva" per a local priest's account; this is a single oral/press source, not cross-checked against Bray or another primary text.

## Follow-up: Denys Bray, *Ethnographic Survey of Baluchistan*, Vol. 1 (1913) — 5 September 2026

Fetched the full text (archive.org `in.ernet.dli.2015.529529`, file `2015.529529.ethnographic-survey_djvu.txt`, via https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.529529/2015.529529.ethnographic-survey_djvu.txt) and searched it in full for "Kalat," "Kali," and "Devi."

The title page settles the volume's actual scope, narrower than "Baluchistan" in general:

"ETHNOGRAPHIC SURVEY OF BALUCHISTAN ... Volume I. Mari Baloch, Khetran Baloch, Mandokhel Pathans, Dumar Pathans."

Citation: Denys Bray, *Ethnographic Survey of Baluchistan*, Vol. 1 (Bombay: The Times Press, 1913), title page, full text via Internet Archive, https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.529529, accessed 5 September 2026.

"Kalat" itself appears repeatedly, always as the state/city named in passing while discussing the Mari and other tribes — e.g. "pressed by the Khan of Kalat for compensation for a raid," and, in a passage on evil spirits: "Their pet haunts are the Mard-i-ghaib mosque near Kalat, the Lakri graveyard close by, and places... which have been used by Hindus as burning-grounds." That second passage is the volume's only link between Kalat and Hindu religious practice at all, and it names a Muslim shrine and a Hindu cremation ground, not a temple. A full-text search (not a spot check) confirms neither "Kali" nor "Devi" appears anywhere in the file.

Citation: Bray, *Ethnographic Survey of Baluchistan*, Vol. 1 (as above), accessed 5 September 2026.

This is a genuine checked-and-silent result, for a documented reason: Vol. 1 is a monograph on four named Baloch/Pathan tribal groups, not a description of Kalat town or its Hindu community. It is not a chronological-scope miss the way the 1919 Sukkur gazetteer was for other entries in this batch — Vol. 1 is contemporary with the temple (Bray's own Vol. 2 covers it, per the existing file) — it is simply a different subject. Vol. 2 remains the relevant volume.
