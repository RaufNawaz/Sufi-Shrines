# Jain Mandir, Lahore
jain-mandir-lahore / researched 16 August 2026 / batch C

## Verified findings

1. Construction date and builder, and use until Partition.

   > "constructed by a woman with her own resources in 1940" ... "remained active till the
   > Partition."

   Citation: Sheharyar Rizwan, "Under-reconstruction Jain Mandir in Lahore all set to reopen,"
   Dawn, 1 April 2022, https://www.dawn.com/news/1682779 — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability:
   established press (Dawn) — citable. Note: the article does not name the woman who funded the
   construction.

2. The 1992 attack, following the Babri Masjid demolition in India, and the extent of damage.

   > "attacked and damaged in 1992 — along with many other temples in Lahore" [following the
   > Babri Mosque demolition] ... "The temple's canopy was the only piece of the original
   > structure that survived."

   Citation: same as #1.

3. Two decades of neglect, then absorption into the Orange Line Metro Train project footprint.

   > "Its ruins lay neglected for the next over two decades, until in 2016 it was enclosed
   > behind a boundary wall and the remaining site given away to the Orange Line Metro Train."

   Citation: same as #1.

4. The December 2021 Supreme Court order for restoration, and ETPB's costed restoration
   progress as of the article's date.

   > "In Dec 2021, a day before the 19th anniversary of the Babri Mosque demolition, the then
   > chief justice of Pakistan Gulzar Ahmed ordered immediate restoration of the temple."
   > Deputy Secretary Faraz Abbas (ETPB) stated the project cost "approximately Rs5m," with
   > "over 70pc of the work... completed" and expected completion "in just over a month."

   Citation: same as #1.

5. Independent corroboration and detail on the 1992 attack and the Supreme Court's order,
   including the parallel Neela Gumbad (Blue Tomb) case and ETPB's stated restoration plan.

   > "After the demolition of the Babri Mosque on Dec. 6, 1992, in India, a mob two days later
   > attacked the temple and damaged the building." ... "Almost 30 Hindu temples in Pakistan were
   > damaged by mobs" [in the same period]. The Supreme Court directed an official "to examine
   > the site of the Jain and Neela Gumbad (Blue Tomb) temples and take steps for their
   > restoration and rehabilitation," with "the work... done without delay and a preliminary
   > report... filed within one month." An ETPB official: "In the first phase, the dome which is
   > lying on the ground will be picked up with the cranes and then we will move towards the
   > construction of the mandir (temple)."

   Citation: "Pakistan set to reopen Lahore's famous Jain temple," The Express Tribune,
   5 December 2021, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2332580/pakistan-set-to-reopen-lahores-famous-jain-temple
   — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability: established press (Express Tribune) — citable.

6. On the historical scale of Jain presence in Lahore, and the temple's survival as the sole
   exception.

   > "there were at least a dozen Jain temples in Lahore" [historically, of which] ...
   > "Historical records do not present accurate details about its construction, but it is safe
   > to assume it came about in late 1930s or early 1940s." And on the 1992 destruction: "it took
   > two days to bring the temple down... until help was sought with a crane. And then the dome
   > of the temple came tumbling down."

   Citation: Muhammad Asif Nawaz, "Story of the Jain Mandir," Youlin Magazine, 20 July 2022,
   https://www.youlinmagazine.com/story/story-of-the-jain-mandir/MjMwNQ== — accessed 16 August
   2026. Reliability: cultural magazine of the Pakistan-China Institute — not explicitly named
   in the README's "established press" list; treated here as citable but flagged as a borderline
   tier, corroborating rather than sole-sourcing the facts above.

7. Absence from the Punjab Archaeology Department's own monument index (checked directly,
   verified negative finding): the Jain Mandir is not listed among the department's "Religious
   Monuments," https://archaeology.punjab.gov.pk/religious_monuments — accessed 16 August 2026.

## Conflicts

- **Exact construction period.** Dawn (citing sources, presumably Iqbal Qaiser per other
  outlets' reporting — see Unverified leads) states the temple was built specifically "in 1940."
  Youlin Magazine instead hedges: "it is safe to assume it came about in late 1930s or early
  1940s." Reported, not resolved. No source verified in this pass names the woman credited with
  funding its construction.
- **Restoration completion timeline.** Dawn (1 April 2022) quotes ETPB's Faraz Abbas estimating
  completion "in just over a month" from that date (i.e., roughly May 2022) with "over 70pc"
  already done. No directly-fetched source in this pass confirms whether that timeline held;
  this is noted, not resolved.

## Verdict

STRONG — two independently fetched, established-press sources (Dawn and The Express Tribune)
directly corroborate the core narrative: c. 1940 construction by an unnamed private
individual, use until Partition, the 1992 mob attack tied to the Babri Masjid demolition,
subsequent decades of neglect and absorption into the Orange Line Metro project, the December
2021 Supreme Court restoration order, and ETPB-led restoration. A third source (Youlin Magazine)
adds detail but is of ambiguous reliability tier. Genuine uncertainty remains over the exact
construction date/builder, reported above as a conflict rather than resolved.
