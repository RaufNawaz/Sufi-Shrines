# Sant Satram Dham, Raharki (Sacho Satram / Devri Sahib)

sant-satram-dham-raharki-sacho-satram-devri-sahib / researched 16 August 2026 / batch F

## Verified findings

1. **The complex: scale, location, and adjacency to Bharchundi Sharif.**
   Quote: "a sprawling multi-acre complex sits among fields just off the main road" and "houses the Sant Satram Das temple and is just a few kilometres from the Bharchundi Sharif shrine." "At one end of the Reharki Darbar, an enormous hostel is being constructed for visiting pilgrims, while a recently completed causeway donated by the federal government provides easy access to temple sites."
   Citation: Cyril Almeida, "Hindus of upper Sindh: a bruised community carries on", *Dawn*, 2 April 2012, https://www.dawn.com/news/707485, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press.

2. **Annual urs/festival for Bhagat Kanwar Ram at the darbar, drawing tens of thousands including from abroad.**
   Quote: "In mid-April, according to caretakers at the darbar, tens of thousands of visitors will gather at the complex for a festival marking the death anniversary of Bhagat Kanwar Ram." "It's a great event and people come from all over, even outside Pakistan, from Dubai and India."
   Citation: Almeida, *Dawn*, 2 April 2012 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

3. **Satramdas's biography, death, and line of succession at Raharki.**
   Quote: Sant Satramdas was "a resident of Raharki village" who "selflessly devoted his life to serving people and spreading the message of peace, love and harmony through his devotional songs," and died in 1910. His successor was "Sant Bhagat Kanwar Ram," described as "a Sufi singer who sang devotional songs in the temples and darbars of Sindh," killed 1 November 1939 and given "a samadhi at Sant Satram Dham." Subsequent custodians: "Sant Chandiram (d. 1961), Sant Babal Bhagwandas (d. 1981), and Sant Kaniya Lal (d. 1990)"; "Sain Sadhram serves as the current custodian."
   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Shikarpur's Sant Satram Dham", *Youlin Magazine*, 14 October 2024, https://youlinmagazine.com/story/shikarpur-sant-satram-dham/Mjg5MA==, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: cultural magazine, authored by an established academic anthropologist (same convention applied to this author elsewhere in this batch — see `khatwari-darbar-shikarpur.md`, batch E).

4. **A satellite Sant Satram Dham exists in Shikarpur town itself, distinct from the Raharki original, and treats Raharki as the "principal" site.**
   Quote: the Shikarpur Sant Satram Dham, "established October 9, 2000," displays "images and pictures of Sant Satramdas and his disciples," including "a poster showing Sant Satramdas's Devri" that references the "principal Devri Sahib at Raharki village."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *Youlin Magazine*, 14 October 2024 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

5. **The Sant Satram Dham temple named directly in coverage of the September 2019 Ghotki unrest, and confirmed as the site government/civil-society delegations visited.**
   Quote: "At the Sant Satram Dham temple in Ghotki, the delegation met community leaders..." and "It appeared during the discussion with administration and civil society that the attack on the temple and riots were planned a night earlier."
   Citation: Mohammad Hussain Khan, "'Ghotki attacks on Hindu community conspiracy to foment communal unrest,' probe finds", *Dawn*, 28 September 2019, https://www.dawn.com/news/1507673, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press.

6. **The same temple, named by its acronym, visited by a national political leader after the 2019 attack.**
   Quote: "Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP) chairman Bilawal Bhutto-Zardari late on Friday evening visited the SSD Dham Mandir, a Hindu temple attacked by a mob during recent riots in Ghotki."
   Citation: "Bilawal visits ransacked temple, mosque in Ghotki", *Dawn*, 29 September 2019, https://www.dawn.com/news/1507877, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press.
