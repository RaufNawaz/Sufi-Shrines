# Ram Mandir, Saidpur (Ram Kund Mandir)

ram-mandir-saidpur-ram-kund-mandir / researched 16 August 2026 / batch D (Hindu temples/sacred sites, Balochistan/Sindh/Punjab)

## Verified findings

1. **Built by Raja Man Singh, associate of Akbar.**
   Quote: "Said to have been built by Raja Man Singh, who was close to the Mughal emperor Akbar."
   Citation: Aamir Yasin, "'Tourists, restaurants violate sanctity of Saidpur's temple'," *Dawn*, 9 October 2016, https://www.dawn.com/news/1289029, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

2. **Rama/Sita/Lakshman exile legend tied to the site.**
   Quote: "Lord Ram, his wife Sita and his brother Lakshman arrived in the Margalla Hills during their 14-year exile and stayed in this village."
   Citation: Yasin, *Dawn*, 9 October 2016 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

3. **1893-94 Rawalpindi Gazetteer records an annual fair at Ram Kund pond.**
   Quote: "According to the Rawalpindi Gazetteer 1893-94, a fair was held at Ram Kund, a pond in Saidpur, every year, visited by Hindus from the area."
   Citation: Yasin, *Dawn*, 9 October 2016 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press, reporting a colonial gazetteer's content (gazetteer itself not independently located in this pass — see Conflicts).

4. **Post-1947/1960 secular reuse as a girls' school; 2006 CDA clearance for tourism.**
   Quotes: "In 1960, after the federal capital was moved from Karachi to Islamabad, the temple building was used to house a girls' school." — "In 2006, the Capital Development Authority (CDA) had the building vacated and preserved as a tourist attraction."
   Citation: Yasin, *Dawn*, 9 October 2016 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

5. **Contemporary controversy — restaurants serving meat on-site.**
   Quote: restaurants "serving meat dishes, which is a disgrace to the sanctity of a sacred place for Hindus."
   Citation: Yasin, *Dawn*, 9 October 2016 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press.

6. **Independent 2020 corroboration: 16th-century construction, no current Hindu worship permitted.**
   Quotes: "a tiny sixteenth century temple, built as a shrine to the ancient god Ram." — "Ram who Hindus believe lived in the area with his family during 14 long years of exile." — "For centuries, Hindus traveled far and wide to worship at the Rama Mandir Temple." — "the area was now a 'heritage site' and worship by Hindus was not allowed." — restaurants and shops "violating its sanctity."
   Citation: Aamir Saeed, "In Pakistani capital, a centuries-old temple where Hindus are not allowed to pray," Arab News (Pakistan edition), 7 July 2020 (updated 8 July 2020), https://www.arabnews.pk/node/1701341/pakistan, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Arab News PK).

## Conflicts

- **Precise founding century/date:** *Dawn* (2016) does not give a century, only naming Raja Man Singh as builder; a search-engine extraction (not independently fetched and confirmed) elsewhere gives "built in the 16th century... around 1580." Arab News (2020) independently corroborates "sixteenth century" without a specific year. The exact year 1580 was not confirmed against any directly fetched page in this pass and should be treated as unverified.
- **Naming:** sources use "Ram Mandir," "Ram Kund Mandir," and "Rama Mandir Temple" somewhat interchangeably; whether "Ram Kund" properly names the temple or only the adjacent pond is not clarified by either fetched article — Dawn (2016) treats "Ram Kund" specifically as "a pond in Saidpur," distinct from the temple building itself.
