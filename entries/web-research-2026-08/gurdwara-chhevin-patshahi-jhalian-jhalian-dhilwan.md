# Gurdwara Chhevin Patshahi, Jhalian (Jhalian Dhilwan)

gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-jhalian-jhalian-dhilwan / researched 16 August 2026 / batch A (historical Sikh gurdwaras in Punjab)

Note on identification: sources describe this as a pair of linked shrines in two adjoining villages, Jhalian and Dhilwan, in P.S. Barki/Burki, Lahore district — treated together below since the sources treat them together. Do not confuse with "Gurdwara Chhevin Patshahi, Jalandhar" (India) or "Gurdwara Patshahi Chhevin, Hadiara" (a separate site near Lahore) which turned up in searches for similar names.

## Verified findings

1. **The two villages and their link.**
   Quote: "The village Jhalian is in P.S. Burki of the Lahore district. It is five kilometers east of Burki and Tongas can be hired from Burki to go to the village. These two villages of Jhalian and Dhilwan are linked with each other and that is why they are also called as Jhalian Dhilwan."
   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998), p. 276, "Gurdwara Sixth Guru at Jhalian District, Lahore." Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

2. **Jhalian shrine — Guru Hargobind association, building description, endowment.**
   Quote: "There is a sacred place in the village which was touched by the feet of Sat Gur Hargobind Ji. Gurdwara is beautiful and has been constructed like a govt bungalow with a verandah in front and with two rooms at the back. 8 ghumaon Land is endowed by the villagers to the shrine. A refugee family from Ambala is settled in it now."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998, p. 276 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

3. **Dhilwan shrine — Guru Hargobind's route from Jhalian, the Manji Sahib, and its present state as a primary school site.**
   Quote: "The village called Dhilwan is in the Barki P.S. of Lahore district. ... Manji Sahib of Guru Hargobind Ji is located towards the east outside the village. Guru Ji reached this place from Jhalian after visiting many villages. The Manji Sahib was built as the place where he sat. Once Prakash used to be held but now a primary school has been built just adjacent to it. 8 ghumaon of land has been endowed by villagers to the Gurdwara."
   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (1998), p. 278, "Gurdwara Sixth Guru at Dhilwan District Lahore." Full-text scan as above, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

4. **Independent corroboration of the Guru Hargobind route between the two villages, and a gurdwara at Dhisalwan (Dhilwan) built at the place he resided.**
   Quote: "6. GURDWARA AT DHISALWAN, POLICE STATION BARKI. Once Guru Har Gobind went to Dhisalwan from village Jhatiyan on preaching mission. A gurdwara was built at the place where he resided while at Dhisalwan."
   Citation: Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan*, Memoir of the Department of Archaeology in Pakistan No. 3 (Karachi: Department of Archaeology, Ministry of Education & Information, Government of Pakistan, 1962), entry 6, Lahore District. Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/furg-sikh-shrines-in-west-pakistan-by-khan-mohammad-wal, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: official body / published government report (full-text scan).

## Conflicts

- **Direction of travel between the two villages:** Qaiser 1998 has Guru Hargobind reaching Dhilwan "from Jhalian after visiting many villages" (p. 278), consistent with the 1962 memoir's "Guru Har Gobind went to Dhisalwan from village Jhatiyan" — the two sources agree on this point; no conflict found on direction. (Noted here only because it was checked, not because a discrepancy was found.)
- **Nature of the Jhalian shrine's present occupants:** Qaiser 1998 states "A refugee family from Ambala is settled in it now" (i.e., the Jhalian shrine building is private-residential). The 1962 memoir gives no information on current occupancy for either village's shrine. Not a contradiction, but the two sources' coverage does not overlap enough to cross-check.
