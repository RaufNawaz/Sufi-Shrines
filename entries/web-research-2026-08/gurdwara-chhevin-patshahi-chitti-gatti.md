# Gurdwara Chhevin Patshahi, Chitti Gatti

gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-chitti-gatti / researched 16 August 2026 / batch A (historical Sikh gurdwaras in Punjab)

## Verified findings

1. **Location — Chitti Gatti village, P.S. Mansehra, Hazara district.**
   Quote: "This tiny village called Chitti Gatti is in P.S Mansehra. (Hazara district) This domed shrine is visible in a garden by the road about 8 km from Mansehra."
   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998), p. 228, "Gurdwara Sixth Guru Chitti Gatti, Mansehra District Hazara." Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

2. **Construction and the shared Hindu/Sikh worship arrangement.**
   Quote: "Gurdwara is beautifully constructed. Inside the dome the genital of Shiv (Shiv Ling) used to be worshipped on the upper floor whereas the prakash of Guru Granth Sahib took place downstairs. A Shiv Ling made of stone is still there. The priests were Hindus and that is why they kept Guru Granth Sahib downstairs. A fair was held on Visakhi which was enthusiastically attended by the people living in the surrounding hills."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998, p. 228 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

3. **Official 1962 register places a "Gurdwara Chitti Chatti" in Hazara district, near Mansehra.**
   Quote (from the book's Appendix, "List of the Shrines," a tabulated register of district / place-name / shrine-type): entries read "20. Hazara ... Near Mansehra ... Gurdwara Chitti Chatti."
   Citation: Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan*, Memoir of the Department of Archaeology in Pakistan No. 3 (Karachi: Department of Archaeology, Ministry of Education & Information, Government of Pakistan, 1962), Appendix 2, "List of the Shrines." Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/furg-sikh-shrines-in-west-pakistan-by-khan-mohammad-wal, accessed 16 August 2026. Note: this OCR scan's main descriptive chapters run Attock, Bannu, D.I. Khan, Gujranwala, Gujrat, Jhelum, Jhang, Kohat, Lahore, Rawalpindi districts in sequence; no separate "Hazara District" descriptive section (only the appendix table entry above and a photograph captioned "Gurdwara at Mansehra," Plate No. 57) was found in the fetched text — either the OCR dropped a page or the shrine received only a photograph and register line in this particular book, not a prose entry. Reported as found, not resolved.
   Reliability class: official body / published government report (full-text scan; incomplete relative to its own table of contents as scanned).

## Conflicts

- **A separate, unrelated Mansehra gurdwara restoration is easy to mistake for this one.** A July 2021 Tribune India report describes the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa government handing over a Sikh gurdwara in Mansehra district to the ETPB for restoration — but that gurdwara is named "Sri Guru Singh Sabha," with foundation stone laid in 1905 by "Sardar Gopal Singh Saithi of Hazro," and had been used as a municipal library and earlier a police station (Tribune India, "Historic Gurdwara set for restoration and reopening in Pakistan," 10 July 2021, https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/world/historic-gurdwara-set-for-restoration-and-reopening-in-pakistan-280571/, accessed 16 August 2026). This is a different, much later-founded institution from the Guru-Hargobind-era shrine at Chitti Gatti described by Qaiser and the 1962 register. A related February 2021 Tribune India article ("Pakistan: KPK government to restore 19th century Sikh gurdwara," https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/world/pakistan-kpk-government-to-restore-19th-century-sikh-gurdwara-215256, accessed 16 August 2026) likewise gives only "Sikh gurdwara in Mansehra district," dates it to the reign of Hari Singh Nalwa (1791–1837), and does not name it — so it cannot be confirmed as either the 1905 Singh Sabha building or the Chitti Gatti shrine. These restoration-news leads are recorded here as a caution against misattribution, not as verified facts about Chitti Gatti itself.

## Verdict

**PARTIAL** — one strong published-book source (Qaiser 1998, full descriptive entry) plus one official register listing that only names and locates the site without prose description (1962 memoir, appendix). No second independently-reported prose account of this specific gurdwara was fetched; the press coverage located concerns a different, later-founded Mansehra gurdwara and must not be conflated with this one.
