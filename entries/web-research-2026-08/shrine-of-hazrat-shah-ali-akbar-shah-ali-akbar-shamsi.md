# Shrine of Hazrat Shah Ali Akbar (Shah Ali Akbar Shamsi)
shrine-of-hazrat-shah-ali-akbar-shah-ali-akbar-shamsi / researched 16 August 2026 / batch H

## Verified findings

- **Location: Suraj Miani (Kabuli Mohallah), Multan city's suburbs.** "Located at Suraj Miani, also known as Kabuli Mohallah, in Multan city's suburbs, the tomb of Hazrat Ali Akbar is a smaller version of the tomb of Hazrat Shah Rukn-e-Alam." — Sana Jamal / Imran Adnan, "Bids for conservation of tomb to open on November 18," *The Express Tribune*, 5 November 2019, https://tribune.com.pk/story/2093560/bids-conservation-tomb-open-november-18 (established press). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **Saint's lineage and construction date, per press.** "The saint is a descendent of Hazrat Shamsuddin Sabzwari and the mausoleum was built in 1585." — "Govt approves Rs30m to restore historic Multan site," *The Express Tribune*, 21 May 2019, https://tribune.com.pk/story/1977117/govt-approves-rs30m-restore-historic-multan-site (established press). Accessed 16 August 2026. The same article gives the descent count more precisely: "Hazrat Shah Ali Akbar was 8th in line as a descendant of Hazrat Shamsuddin Sabzwari" (Tribune, 5 Nov 2019 story above).

- **Construction date and dating, per the Punjab government's own heritage authority.** The Directorate General of Archaeology, Punjab, in its official entry for the site, dates it differently: "it may be attributed to the 17th century A.D." — "Tomb of Shah Ali Akbar & His Mother, Multan," Directorate General of Archaeology, Government of Punjab, https://archaeology.punjab.gov.pk/tomb_shahaliakbar (official body). Accessed 16 August 2026. The same page identifies the founder only as "the Ismaili Saint Sultan Ali Akbar," who "erected the tomb according to local tradition," and does not repeat the Shamsuddin Sabzwari descent claim used by the press.

- **Architecture, per the official Punjab Archaeology page.** "Built in brick, it has been embellished with panels of glazed tiles, three arched panels in each side surmounted with a series of merlons placed on bold moldings, while the lower part of the building is plastered in lime." "The interior is decorated with panels of fresco paintings." The page also confirms formal protection: the monument is registered "Under Antiquity Act 1975," with the site demarcated. — Directorate General of Archaeology, Punjab, https://archaeology.punjab.gov.pk/tomb_shahaliakbar (official body). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **Architecture and materials, per press.** "It shares the appearance and architectural features with the mausoleum of Hazrat Shah Rukn-e-Alam which included an octagonal plan, 3-storey and tapering angle buttresses crowned by turrets." "The monument is an example of fine cut and dressed work with the combination of Multani glazed kashi tiles." — *The Express Tribune*, 5 November 2019 (as cited above). Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **Condition of the interior.** "The interior of the monument has been damaged considerably by the smoke of mustard oil lamps and the candles' smoke has spoiled the fresco painting or naqashi work and the lime plaster." — *The Express Tribune*, 5 November 2019 (as cited above); the same sentence (with "Naqashi" capitalized) also appears in the 21 May 2019 Tribune piece. Accessed 16 August 2026.

- **Conservation project, cost figures (two different numbers reported across the two Tribune articles).** 21 May 2019: "The Punjab Archaeology Department has sent a Rs30 million proposal to the authorities concerned for conservation and restoration of the 5 centuries old mausoleum of Hazrat Shah Ali Akbar." 5 November 2019 (a later stage of the same or a related tender): "The Rs27.104 million project" is under bid, overseen by "The Punjab Archaeology Department." — Both *The Express Tribune*, as cited above. Accessed 16 August 2026.

## Conflicts

- **Construction century.** Press (Express Tribune, both 2019 articles) states the mausoleum "was built in 1585" (i.e., 16th century, ~993 AH). The Directorate General of Archaeology, Punjab's own official listing for the same monument instead states "it may be attributed to the 17th century A.D." Not resolved here — report both.
- **Founder's identity/lineage.** Press names the saint "Hazrat Shah Ali Akbar," "8th in line as a descendant of Hazrat Shamsuddin Sabzwari." The official Punjab Archaeology page names the founder only as "the Ismaili Saint Sultan Ali Akbar" (no Sabzwari lineage given, and "erected the tomb" is attributed to him "according to local tradition" rather than stated as established fact). Not resolved here — report both.
- **Conservation cost figure.** Rs30 million (Tribune, May 2019) vs Rs27.104 million (Tribune, November 2019) — could be the same project re-costed at tender stage, or two different phases; the articles do not clarify. Not resolved here.

## Verdict

PARTIAL — two independent, citable, established-press sources (both Express Tribune) plus one official body (Directorate General of Archaeology, Punjab) were fetched and verified with verbatim quotes, satisfying "≥2 independent verified sources." However, the two press articles may derive from the same government press-office material (near-identical phrasing across both), and they directly conflict with the official archaeology department's own dating of the monument (16th vs 17th century) and its account of the founder. Given that unresolved conflict on the central fact (construction date) between the official custodial body and the press, this is recorded as PARTIAL rather than STRONG.
