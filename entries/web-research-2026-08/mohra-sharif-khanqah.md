# Mohra Sharif (Khanqah)
mohra-sharif-khanqah / researched 16 August 2026 / batch G

## Verified findings

- **Location and age: Rawalpindi district, "200-year-old."** "German envoy visits historic
  shrine" reports the German Ambassador to Pakistan "visited the historic shrine Darbar Mohra
  Sharif, Rawalpindi" and states "Darbar Mohra Sharif, a 200-year-old Sufi centre, continues to
  promote universal values." — The Express Tribune, byline "Our Correspondent," 8 January 2026,
  https://tribune.com.pk/story/2585995/german-envoy-visits-historic-shrine. Reliability:
  established press. Accessed 16 August 2026.
- **Current custodian.** The same article states the ambassador "met with its custodian, Pir
  Mujtaba Farooq Gull." — same source as above.
- **A second press report confirms the visit but adds no history.** "German Ambassador Ina
  Lepel visited the madrassa at Mohra Sharif on Wednesday" and "spent considerable time
  interacting with the students, observing their classes, and discussing their curriculum." —
  Islamabad Post, byline "DNA," 13 January 2026,
  https://islamabadpost.com.pk/german-ambassador-visits-madrassa-in-mohra-sharif/. Reliability:
  established press (Pakistani digital outlet). Accessed 16 August 2026. No founder, order, or
  founding-date information is given in this article.
- **The khanqah's own account of its founder, order, and succession.** "Mohra Shareef... is the
  home of the Naqshbandia, Mujaddadiya. Qasimiya sufi order, is a Naqshbandi in origin." The
  founder, "Hazrat Khawaja Muhammad Qasim Sadiq (RA)," is described as born in "1263 A.H.,"
  living 120 years, and "Passed away on the 20th November of 1943." Succession: "Khawaja Pir
  Muhammad Zahid Khan," the founder's third son, chosen as successor and "first Sajjad Nasheen
  of Mohra Shareef," who "passed away December 9, 1993"; then "Hazrat Khawaja Pir Badshah
  Sahib," who "passed away on 13th August, 2008"; then "Pir Auliya Badshah Farooq Sahib," "the
  third Sajjada Nasheen (Custodian of Holy Shrine)." — "Guide to Mohra Sharif" / "History of
  Khanqah of Mohra Shareef," mohrasharif.com,
  https://www.mohrasharif.com/index.php/about/66-guide-to-mohra-sharif, accessed 16 August
  2026. Reliability: primary/self-published — this is the khanqah's own website, not
  independent scholarship or press; treat as a primary source describing itself, not as
  independent corroboration.

## Conflicts

None found between the two directly-fetched, independent sources (Express Tribune and
mohrasharif.com) — the press coverage is silent on founding details, so it neither confirms nor
contradicts the khanqah's own account. See Unverified leads for an internal inconsistency in
Wikipedia's treatment of the founder's dates, which I could not resolve because I could not
independently fetch a source that pins down either figure.

## Unverified leads

- **English Wikipedia, "Muhammad Qasim Sadiq"** — fetched directly, but per the README's rule
  Wikipedia is a pointer only, not a citation. It reports (its own, not independently verified
  by me): founding of Mohra Sharif "In 1878"; the founder's infobox giving birth 1845, death 21
  November 1942, while the article's own text says he died "November 21, 1943," and its
  category tags read "1822 births" / "1943 deaths" — i.e. three different birth years and two
  different death years appear on the same Wikipedia page. This is a genuine internal
  inconsistency worth flagging, but since it originates entirely from a non-citable source, it
  is not reported above as a verified "Conflict" — only as a lead for whoever chases the
  underlying refs. Those refs, per the Wikipedia page, include: scholars.pk (Urdu, "Hazrat
  Khawaja Muhammad Qasim Moharwi"); a PDF at
  https://www.mohrasharif.com.pk/acrobat/chapter_2.pdf (note: `.com.pk` domain, distinct from
  `.com`); nisbatrasooli.com (archived 2012); William Birdwood, *Khaki and Gown: An
  Autobiography* (Ward, Lock & Co., 1942), p. 365; Jang.com.pk (Urdu); Lat̤īf Kāshmīrī, a 1971
  Urdu work on Murree valley history, p. 109; Pnina Werbner, "Reform Sufism in South Asia," in
  *Islamic Reform in South Asia* (Cambridge UP, 2013), pp. 51–78, p. 63 — none of these were
  independently fetched this session (the one ResearchGate link found for the Werbner chapter
  turned out to deliver an unrelated PDF — see the Ghamkol Sharif file, this batch, for
  details).
- **A Brill *Encyclopaedia of Islam, THREE* entry** on Zindapir of Ghamkol Sharif (see that
  file) names his murshid as "Ḥaḍrat Muḥammad Qāthim Mohŕvī (d. 1943) of Mohŕ" — apparently the
  same founder, with a third death year (1943, matching mohrasharif.com's own "20 November
  1943" but not Wikipedia's infobox "1942"). I could not fetch the Brill page directly (403),
  so this triangulation is noted but not verified.
- English Wikipedia, "Mohra Sharif" — fetched directly; states the shrine "was established in
  the 19th century" (no specific year) and that the village is "on the Murree Hills." Its only
  two citations are the same Express Tribune and Islamabad Post articles already verified
  above directly.
- English Wikipedia, "Qasimiya" — fetched directly; explicitly flagged by Wikipedia itself as a
  stub relying on a single source (mohrasharif.com's own history page, already captured above).

## Verdict

PARTIAL — one independent press source (Express Tribune, corroborated in passing by a second,
Islamabad Post) verifies the shrine's existence, its Rawalpindi/Murree-hills location, its
approximate age ("200-year-old"), and its current custodian's name. The founder's identity,
founding date, and order name rest so far on the khanqah's own self-published website only,
which is a primary but interested source, not independent scholarship — and even that source's
death date for the founder (1943) disagrees with Wikipedia's own infobox (1942), a discrepancy
this session could not resolve because none of the underlying sources on either side were
independently fetchable.
