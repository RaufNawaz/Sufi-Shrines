# Gurdwara Patshahi Chhevin (Hadiara), Lahore
gurdwara-patshahi-chhevin-hadiara-lahore / researched 16 August 2026 / batch B

Sheet location on file: Hadiara village, Lahore District, Punjab, Pakistan (31.44469, 74.56603).

## Verified findings

1. **Basic identification and Qaiser's on-the-ground account (pre-restoration).** "GURDWARA SIXTH GURU AT HADIARA DISTRICT. LAHORE. The village by the name of Hadiara is situated on Lahore-Ghawindi road… There is a sacred shrine of Sri Guru Hargobind Ji in the village Hadiara. He visited this place in recognition of the affection of the Sikh Sangat. The Gurdwara is the tallest building of the village… At present refugees from Amritsar are settled in it." — Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998), full text, p. 283–284 of the scan, at https://archive.org/download/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan_djvu.txt, accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability class: published book (citable).

2. **Wikipedia article's description of the Hadiara gurdwara** (used here only as a pointer; the underlying facts are attributed to the article's own listed citations, which were not independently re-fetched due to time — flagged accordingly). Summary of fetched page (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdwara_Patshahi_Chhevin_(Hadiara), accessed 16 August 2026): a huge fair was held at the gurdwara every Maghi before 1947; roughly 100 acres of land were associated with it under the Sikh Empire; the site became abandoned and was occupied by migrants after Partition; it is currently defunct, containing "many frescoes albeit in poor condition"; coordinates given as 31°26'40.9"N, 74°33'57.7"E, consistent with the sheet's 31.44469, 74.56603. The article's own reference list (not yet independently verified by fetching each): Sikh Virsa Project ("Gurdwara Pathshahi Chevin - Hadiara"); Singh, Gurmail (2025), "Geo Mapping of Gurdwaras Sahib in Pakistan" (PDF), Green Tourism Pakistan; Butalia, Tarunjit Singh (April 2025), "Sikh Historical Shrines in Pakistan: Lahore District" (PDF), Jeevay Sanjha Punjab; Gurudwarapedia; Brar, Kamaldeep Singh (27 November 2023), Indian Express. Reliability class of Wikipedia itself: pointer only, not citable as a source of fact.

3. **The article does not mention any recent reopening.** Confirmed on fetch: the Wikipedia article contains no reference to a 2026 (or any) reopening ceremony — this matters for the Conflicts section below.

## Conflicts

- **A gurdwara "reopened after 79 years" in July 2026 is very likely a different site, at Amar Sidhu, not Hadiara.** The Tribune India (Charanjit Singh Teja, 11 July 2026), reporting a reopening event, states the gurdwara concerned is "Gurdwara Patshahi Chhevin at Amar Sidhu, a historic village on the Lahore-Kasur road," and that "a grand gurdwara was constructed in 1923 through the efforts of Bhai Mohan Singh Akali (Nihang), with support from Lahore's Sikh community." (Tribune India, "Historic gurdwara linked to Guru Hargobind reopens in Lahore after 79 years," https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/punjab/historic-gurdwara-linked-to-guru-hargobind-reopens-in-lahore-after-79-years/, accessed 16 August 2026 — established press, citable.) Iqbal Qaiser's book lists "GURDWARA SIXTH GURU AT AMAR SADDHU DISTRICT LAHORE" as a separate chapter/entry immediately after its Hadiara entry, treating Hadiara and Amar Sadhu/Amar Sidhu as two distinct Guru-Hargobind-associated gurdwaras in Lahore district. The Wikipedia article specifically about the Hadiara gurdwara (which matches the sheet's coordinates) makes no mention of any 2026 reopening. Taken together, the widely-reported July 2026 reopening almost certainly concerns the Amar Sidhu gurdwara, not the Hadiara one this sheet entry is about — the two should not be conflated, and any celebratory press coverage of "Gurdwara Patshahi Chhevin" reopening should be checked for which village it names before being applied to this entry.
- Other outlets covering the same reopening event (The Wire, "Gurdwara Patshahi Chhevin Reopens After 79 Years in Lahore") could not be fetched (HTTP 525/connection errors) and are listed as unverified leads rather than citations; based on the confirmed Tribune India report, this reopening coverage likely also concerns Amar Sidhu rather than Hadiara.

## Verdict

PARTIAL — one independent, directly-quoting published-book source (Qaiser) confirms the gurdwara's existence, Guru Hargobind association, and post-Partition occupation; a second citable source (Tribune India) is strong on its own topic but appears to describe a related, differently-located gurdwara (Amar Sidhu) rather than this one, so it does not independently corroborate this entry — it instead surfaces an important naming conflict that needs resolving before use.
