# Tomb of Ustad Nuriya
tomb-of-ustad-nuriya / researched 16 August 2026 / batch C

Note: the sheet places this tomb at **Uch Sharif, Bahawalpur District** (Bukhari mound), not
Lahore. Research below follows the sheet's location.

## Verified findings

1. Location and architectural grouping, from an academic paper.

   > "The shrine of Ustad Nuriya positions in the northwest corner of Uch Sharif on the sloping
   > side of a hill, the spot of an historic fortress, amid the ruins of some other
   > architecturally essential shrine, among them the shrines of Bibi Jawindi & Baha'al-Halim."

   Citation: Nusrat Bibi, "The Land of Saints Uch Sharif" [section: "Ustad Nuriya Tomb Monument
   Complex"], *International Journal of Scientific and Research Publications*, Vol. 11, Issue 10
   (October 2021), p. 282, ISSN 2250-3153, DOI: 10.29322/IJSRP.11.10.2021.p11832.
   https://www.ijsrp.org/research-paper-1021/ijsrp-p11832.pdf — accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: academic paper (open-access, DOI-registered) — citable per the reliability bar,
   though flagged: this is a Textile Design department publication (Islamia University of
   Bahawalpur), not a specialist archaeology/history journal, and its prose reads as
   non-native-English and lightly edited.

2. Official recognition as a protected monument, with a period classification that appears to
   conflict with other evidence (see Conflicts).

   The Punjab Directorate General of Archaeology's monument index lists "Tomb of Nuria, Uch
   Sharif" under the heading "Early Muslim Period (8th – 12th Century)" — the listing itself,
   with no narrative text.

   Citation: Directorate General of Archaeology, Government of Punjab, "Religious Monuments,"
   https://archaeology.punjab.gov.pk/religious_monuments — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability:
   official provincial heritage body — citable.

## Conflicts

- **Absence from a major colonial-era gazetteer's shrine list.** The 1908 *Punjab States
  Gazetteers: Bahawalpur State*, Vol. 36, gives a detailed, named list of shrines and tombs at
  Uch — Bibi Jawindi, Sayyid Safi-ud-Din Haqqani Gazruni, and eleven further named shrines/tombs
  — but includes **no entry for "Ustad Nuriya"** or any tomb attributed to an architect.

  > "(3) In addition to the above there are also at Uch the following shrines and tombs:— (1)
  > Shrine of Shah Abdul Jalil, known as Chanchal Shah Bukhari. (2) Shrine of Pir Munnan
  > Mashaikh... (11) Tomb of Faqir Salem Sudhar."

  Citation: *Punjab States Gazetteers: Bahawalpur State*, Vol. 36 (Lahore, 1908), pp. 890–891.
  Full text: https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.105641/2015.105641.Punjab-States-Gazetteers-Bahawalpur-State-Vol36_djvu.txt
  — accessed 16 August 2026. Reliability: colonial government gazetteer, full-text scan —
  citable.

  This is reported as a gap in the record, not a direct contradiction of any specific dated
  claim — it may reflect that the "Ustad Nuriya" attribution/tomb was not locally known by that
  name as of 1908, or simply that the gazetteer's list was not exhaustive. Not resolved.

- **Period/date classification.** The Punjab Archaeology Department's index places this tomb
  under "Early Muslim Period (8th–12th Century)," while the sheet's own `year_built` (1493,
  "attributed to the builder of Bibi Jawindi's tomb") and the academic paper found here both
  imply a late-15th-century date — contemporary with or shortly after Bibi Jawindi's shrine,
  which the Bahawalpur Gazetteer dates to "900 Hijra (1494 A.D.)." This may be a page-layout
  artefact on the Archaeology Department's site (grouping by a broad period heading) rather than
  a considered dating decision. Reported, not resolved.

## Verdict

PARTIAL — one academic paper and one official heritage-department listing were verified
directly, but the substantive descriptive content rests on a single paper of uneven editorial
quality; the UNESCO tentative-list text that most other coverage points back to could not be
fetched and is only an unverified lead; and a 1908 gazetteer, checked directly, notably omits
this tomb from its detailed enumerated shrine list at Uch — a genuine, unresolved gap in the
documentary record.
