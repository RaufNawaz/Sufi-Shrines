# Gori Temple (Gori jo Mandar)

gori-temple-gori-jo-mandar / researched 16 August 2026 / batch D (Hindu temples/sacred sites, Balochistan/Sindh/Punjab)

## Verified findings

1. **Location, in the village of Gori, Tharparkar district, Sindh.**
   Quote: "At Gori, about 22km northwest of Viravah, some 4 km from Marui jo Magah Malir, and deep in the desert lies an old Jain Temple."
   Citation: "Gori Temple," Heritage of Sindh (Endowment Fund Trust for Preservation of the Heritage of Sindh), https://heritage.eftsindh.com/districts/tharparkar/gori-temple.php, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: official/heritage body (EFT Sindh).

2. **Dimensions and building material.**
   Quotes: "The main building is 27.6m in length and 18.5m in width." — "It is constructed with Jodhpuri stone much like marble."
   Citation: EFT Sindh, "Gori Temple" (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: official/heritage body.

3. **Last major repairs recorded in 1715; history of damage.**
   Quotes: "The last major repairs are recorded to have been carried out in 1715." The temple "has been much defaced at various times by Sindhi troops" and "suffered from fire, and from gunpowder."
   Citation: EFT Sindh, "Gori Temple" (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: official/heritage body. **Note:** this page gives no construction date and does not mention frescoes, an earthquake, or dedication to Parshvanath — those claims come only from other sources below.

4. **Built circa 16th century (Dawn, 2016).**
   Quotes: "the 16th century Jain Gori temple near the Pakistan-India border in Nagarparkar" — "The Jain temple in the village of Gori was built around the 16th century."
   Citation: Ema Anis, "Secrets of Thar: A Jain temple, a mosque and a 'magical' well," *Dawn*, 19 February 2016, https://www.dawn.com/news/1238823, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

5. **Architecture, frescoes, dedication, and abandonment (Dawn, 2016).**

## Conflicts

- **Founding date:** Wikipedia's own "Gori Temple, Nagarparkar" article (used only as a pointer, not cited) reportedly carries a legend of construction "in 300 CE" by "Goricho," alongside an alternative "1375-1376 CE" figure flagged there as "[citation needed]," and a "more likely... middle of the 16th century" claim. Independently, both *Dawn* (2016) and *The News* (2018, unconfirmed fetch) give "16th century" without a specific year. No fetched primary source in this pass supports either the 300 CE or 1375-76 CE figures — they are reported here only as a pointer to Wikipedia's own unresolved sourcing, not verified.
- **Damage cause:** EFT Sindh attributes historical damage to "Sindhi troops," fire, and gunpowder, with repairs "carried out in 1715" — no earthquake mentioned. *Dawn* (2016) instead attributes wall destruction to "an earthquake" and to demolition "by the British." The two accounts of what damaged the temple, and when, are not reconciled here.

## Follow-up: Henry Cousens, *The Antiquities of Sind* (1929), p. 177 — 5 September 2026

Fetched the full OCR text (archive.org `in.ernet.dli.2015.200876`, file `2015.200876.An-Antiquities_djvu.txt`, via https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.200876/2015.200876.An-Antiquities_djvu.txt) and located the body-text page marker "177" directly. The earlier pass's keyword search for "Gori" only matched the index (p.176 listing), because the OCR renders the entry's own section heading as "GORlj" rather than "GORI" — an OCR misread ("I" as "l" plus a stray "j"), which is why a plain keyword search of the body text never surfaced it. The full passage, quoted verbatim as scanned:

"GORlj 14 miles north-west from Viravah, possesses an old temple about 125 by 50 feet, built of marble. The oft repeated story of the dream, the buried image, and the breakdown of the cart contaming it, at the spot where the temple stands, and the building of the latter for it, is told of this shrme. It is very similar, though superior to the one at Bhodesar, but had been much defaced at various times by the fanaticism of the Sindhi troops. It is also said to have suffered from fire, and from gunpowder which was used by Colonel Tyrwhitt, at the time of the Nagar outbreak, in order to secure a Sodha chief who was supposed to be hiding himself in its passages. There is an inscription which was put up when the temple was repaired in A.D. 1715."

Citation: Henry Cousens, *The Antiquities of Sind, with Historical Outline* (Calcutta: Government of India Central Publication Branch, 1929), p. 177, full text via Internet Archive, https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.200876, accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability class: published book, full-text scan (colonial-era archaeological survey) — the primary source the index entry pointed to.

What this changes:

- **Location/distance corroborated, in different units.** Cousens: "14 miles north-west from Viravah" (≈22.5 km) — closely matches EFT Sindh's "22km northwest of Viravah" (finding 1). Independent confirmation of the site's position.
- **Dimensions now conflict, rather than being silent.** Cousens gives "about 125 by 50 feet" (≈38.1 m × 15.2 m), against EFT Sindh's "27.6m in length and 18.5m in width" (≈90.6 ft × 60.7 ft, finding 2). These are not the same figure in different units — even the length:width ratio differs (2.5:1 vs. ~1.5:1). New conflict, not resolved here.
- **The damage narrative and the 1715 repair date trace back to this exact passage — EFT Sindh appears to be quoting Cousens, not an independent witness.** EFT Sindh's "much defaced at various times by Sindhi troops... suffered from fire, and from gunpowder" (finding 3) matches Cousens' wording ("much defaced at various times by the fanaticism of the Sindhi troops... suffered from fire, and from gunpowder") too closely to be an independent account of the same event. Likewise EFT Sindh's "last major repairs... carried out in 1715" matches Cousens' "an inscription which was put up when the temple was repaired in A.D. 1715" — Cousens attributes this to an on-site inscription and, at 1929, is the earliest source found; EFT Sindh is very likely downstream of it. This means the file's original claim of "two independently verified... sources" agreeing on damage should be read with that lineage in mind — it is one account restated, not two. *Dawn*'s (2016) conflicting earthquake/British-demolition claim remains just as unreconciled with Cousens as it was with EFT Sindh.
- **Founding date/dedication: Cousens is silent.** No construction date or century, no Parshvanath dedication, no frescoes, no earthquake — only a "dream/buried image/runaway cart" founding legend, which Cousens explicitly notes is the same type of story "told of" the neighbouring Bhodesar temple (described in the preceding paragraph). Neither the 300 CE nor the 1375-76 CE figure on Wikipedia is confirmed, contradicted, or addressed.

### Revised verdict
PARTIAL — unchanged in category. This closes the acquisition lead: the p.177 passage has been read in full. It adds a corroborated distance and a dated, inscription-sourced repair year, but replaces the appearance of two-source agreement on damage-cause with a single traceable lineage (Cousens → EFT Sindh), and introduces a new, unresolved dimensions conflict. The founding-legend dates remain wholly unverified against any fetched primary source.
