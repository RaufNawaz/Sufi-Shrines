# Sain Vali Vilayat Rai Darbar, Kambar

sain-vali-vilayat-rai-darbar-kambar / researched 16 August 2026 / batch F

## Verified findings

1. **Founder's birth: 1825, Old Hala, into a Hindu family.**
   Quote: "Vali Vilayat Rai was born in 1825 in a rich Hindu family of Old Hala."
   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Nanakpanthi Saints of Sindh", *The Friday Times*, 13 April 2018, https://www.thefridaytimes.com/13-Apr-2018/nanakpanthi-saints-of-sindh, accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press.

2. **Pre-Kambar career and conversion story: a Tapedar, imprisoned, a vision of Guru Nanak.**
   Quote: "Before moving to Kambar, Sain Vali Vilayat Rai served as a Tapedar (village accountant) under the Raj. Some irregularities were found in the accounts that he maintained. A case was registered against him and was sentenced to prison. In jail, the story goes, he got a vision or visit by Guru Nanak, who told him about his previous incarnation as a yogi."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 13 April 2018 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press. Note: this is the article's own framing as legend ("the story goes"), not the reporter's independent finding.

3. **Arrival in Kambar and choice of the Shivalo locality.**
   Quote: "When Sain Vali Vilayat Rai came to Kambar, he chose the present Shivalo locality which was not populated at that time and was a perfect rendezvous for Yogis, Sants and Sadhus."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 13 April 2018 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

4. **Annual visits to Sufi shrines, per his hagiography.**
   Quote: "According to his hagiography once in a year he used to go to visit the Sufi shrines in Rohri, Sehwan and Jhok."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 13 April 2018 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.
   Reliability: established press, but explicitly sourced by the article to hagiography rather than independent record.

5. **The darbar building, dated to 1887.**
   Quote: "The building is believed to have been erected by Sain Vali Vilayat Rai in 1887." Also: "ornately carved doors and windows reflecting colonial aesthetics" in "a three-storey building."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 13 April 2018 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

6. **Succession: Jiwat Singh, then Vishandas.**
   Quote: "Sain Jiwat Singh opened free dispensary for the poor people of Kambar...He died in 1899 in Kambar town." And: "He was succeeded by his chief disciple Vishandas, who became the caretaker of the darbar."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 13 April 2018 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

7. **Post-Partition branch in Bombay/Mumbai.**
   Quote: "A branch of the Vali Vilayat Rai Kambar Darbar was established at Kandivali, Bombay (now Mumbai) in 1960."
   Citation: Kalhoro, *The Friday Times*, 13 April 2018 (as above), accessed 16 August 2026.

## Acquisition leads

The Larkana "B" volume gazetteer is the correct next target.

- District-specific "B" volume gazetteers for the old Larkana District (Kambar/Qambar-Shahdadkot was carved out of Larkana District) are referenced in the general gazetteer's preface but were not located and checked in this pass — worth pursuing for the Saifullah book list.
- Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, *Saints, Sufis and Shrines: The Mystical Landscape of Sindh* (Endowment Fund Trust for Preservation of the Heritage of Sindh, 2022) — reviewed in Dawn (Dr Rabela Junejo, "Non-Fiction: A History of Syncretic Mysticism", *Dawn*, 14 May 2023, https://www.dawn.com/news/1753095, accessed 16 August 2026); the review's own text does **not** name Kambar or Vilayat Rai, so the book's coverage of this site is unconfirmed — but the author is the same Kalhoro who wrote the sole verified TFT piece above, and the book compiles ~55 of his shrine essays, making it a plausible source to check directly.
