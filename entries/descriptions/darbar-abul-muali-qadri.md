## Overview

Darbar Abul Muali Qadri is an active Muslim shrine in Lahore, built around the grave of Hazrat Syed Muhammad Khair ul Deen, widely known as Shah Abul Muali Qadri. The complex holds the tomb, a mosque, and a graveyard in which members of the saint's family are buried alongside ordinary and notable dead. It is looked after by the Government Auqaf Department together with the descendants of the shrine's hereditary custodians (reportedly termed *gaddī nashīn* — the *sajjāda nashīn* line). Construction work at the site is ongoing, and between 2,000 and 2,500 people come daily.

The older histories of Lahore know the tomb well. Kanhaiya Lal, in his *Tarikh-e Lahore*, calls it "یہ مشہور و معروف متبرک مقبرہ", this famous and blessed tomb, and places it outside Mochi Gate, at the north-west corner (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287). Maulvi Nur Ahmad Chishti, in his *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, opens his account of it with "یہ مکان باہر دروازہ موچی کے واقع ہے", this place lies outside the Mochi Gate (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 123). The editor of Mufti Ghulam Sarwar Lahori's *Hadiqat al-Awliya* gives its present location as Shah Abul Muali Street, Gowalmandi (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 287, folio 275), and Zahur ul-Hasan Sharib writes that his mazar in Lahore is a fountainhead of blessings, "آپ کا مزار لاہور میں فیوض و برکات کا سرچشمہ ہے" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 257, folio 253). On the saint's name, his birth, the year and reign of his death, the length of his training and the stories told of him, the accounts differ, and the differences are set out side by side below.

A reader should know that the name Abul Muali belongs to more than one man in these books. Lahori's index keeps three Abul Mualis apart, and only one of them is called Lahori: "ابوالمعالی شاہ، لاہوری" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 312, folio 300). Lahori's book has, besides its entry on this saint in the Qadiri chapter, a second and separate entry on a "شاہ ابوالمعالی چشتی" in its Chishti chapter (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 15, folio 3; p. 16, folio 4), and another of its entries concerns the son of a Shah Abul Muali of a Gilani line, not the Kirmani one (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 63, folio 51). Sharib, for his part, writes of a living pir named Hazrat Shah Abu al-Maali who lived at a town whose name is uncertain in the printed text, "اینتھہ [OCR?]", never at Lahore, and who travelled to see a disciple who died in 1131 AH; that man is not the saint of this shrine (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, pp. 326–328, folios 322–324; pp. 326–329, folios 322–325).

## The Life of the Saint

Tradition holds that Shah Abul Muali was born on 10 Zil Hajj 960 Hijri in Kirman, Iran, the son of Syed Rehmatullah Kirmani, of the Kirmani Sadaat. His *kunyat* was Abul Muali, the name by which he is known; it is also reported that his title was Asadullah and that he was Hassani Hussaini and of the Ahl al-Bayt. His father is described as a scholar and a poet in Urdu and Persian, who migrated from Iran and settled at Depalpur, Okara. Two of his father's brothers are named: Sheikh Dawood Bandgi Kirmani, whose shrine is reported to be at Shergarh, and Syed Jalal ud Deen, whose shrine is reportedly in Sindh.

The books give him a fuller name and pedigree. Lahori heads his entry "عاشق لا ابالی شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی لاہوری بن سید رحمۃ اللہ بن سید فتح اللہ کرمانی قادری": the heedless lover, Shah Khair al-Din Abul Muali Lahori, son of Sayyid Rahmat Allah, son of Sayyid Fath Allah Kirmani, Qadiri (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45). Chishti gives his title-name as "شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی قادری" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 123). Sharib calls him "زبدۃ العارفین", the *zubdat al-arifin* (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251), and writes "آپ کا نام المعالی ہے، آپ شاہ ابو المعالی کے نام سے مشہور ہیں", that his name is al-Maali and that he is famous as Shah Abu al-Maali, with "اسد الدین" and "شاہ خیر الدین" as his *laqabs* (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251).

Sharib traces the descent through twenty-eight intermediaries to Hazrat Musa al-Murqa [OCR?], son of Imam Muhammad Taqi al-Jawad, and writes "آپ کے آباؤ اجداد سادات کرمان سے تھے", that his forefathers were of the Sadat of Kirman (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251). He dates the family's coming to India: his great-great-grandfather Sayyid Faiz Allah, bringing his son Sayyid Mubarak Kirmani, came from Kirman to Hindustan in 796 AH and stayed at Uch, and leaving Uch they settled at a place near Multan whose name is uncertain in the printed text, "داؤد جال[OCR?]" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251). His father, Sayyid Rahmat Allah, moved from there to a place printed "سنگھڑہ[OCR?]", and then to Sher Garh, where he made his home (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251).

The books give his birth as follows. Lahori's text has "سال نوسو ساٹھ", the year 960 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46). Chishti, drawing on an earlier work whose title is only partly legible, writes "تاریخ دہم ماہ ذی الحجہ روز عید اضحی روز دوشنبہ[OCR?] سنہ ۹۶۰[OCR?] نہصد[OCR?] و شست[OCR?] ہجری میں تولد ہوی", that he was born on the tenth of Dhu al-Hijja, the day of Eid al-Adha, a Monday, in 960 AH, the year written out also in words (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 130). Kanhaiya Lal also has him born on the tenth of Dhu al-Hijja, the day of Eid, but in 992 AH, a year whose printed digits are uncertain (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288). Sharib places the birth at Sher Garh on 10 Dhu al-Hijja 910 AH, adding that it is also said to have been 10 Dhu al-Hijja 920 AH (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251).

It is reported that after his father's death he was taken under the care of his uncle Dawood Bandgi, who was also his father-in-law; that he studied with him until the age of thirty; and that he migrated to Lahore at his uncle's command, establishing there a mosque and seminary near Mochi Darwaza where he led congregational prayers and taught. Lahori confirms the bond: "یہ بزرگ برادر زادہ حقیقی شیخ داؤد کرمانی شیر گڈھی کے ہیں اور انہیں کے مرید تھے", he was the true brother's son of Shaikh Daud Kirmani Shergarhi and his disciple (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45). Lahori's editor adds that he was Daud's nephew, son-in-law and *khalifa*, and that he made continuous and tireless efforts for the spread of the Qadiri silsila (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46). Lahori writes that he attended on his pir for thirty-two years before reaching completion, and that after the grant of the *khirqa* of *khilafat* he was deputed to Lahore, "لاہور کو مامور ہوئے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45). Chishti, too, names Shaikh Daud at Sher Garh as his murshid and has him set out from there toward Lahore (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 479).

Sharib tells the road differently. His father taught him the exoteric sciences, which he soon mastered (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251); then "آپ کو اویسی طریقے پر غوث الاعظم محی الدین حضرت سید عبدالقادر جیلانی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ سے عشق پیدا ہوا", a love for Ghawth al-Azam Abd al-Qadir Jilani arose in him on the Uwaysi mode, and he left the city for the jungle and wandered there for several years, "کئی سال تک جنگلوں میں پھرتے رہے" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, pp. 255–256, folios 251–252). He reached Delhi in 980 AH, "آپ 980ھ میں دہلی پہنچے", and there met a *majzub* in a sarai; on that sign, "یہ اشارہ پا کر آپ دہلی سے شیر گڑھ روانہ ہوئے", he left Delhi for Sher Garh and presented himself to Shaikh Dawud (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252). Sharib makes him Shaikh Dawud's murid and pre-eminent *khalifa*, "آپ حضرت شیخ داؤد کے مرید اور ممتاز خلیفہ ہیں" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252), and records the condition the shaikh set: "ہمیشہ میرے پاس رہو، تاکہ تم کو وہ حاصل ہو، جو دوسری جگہ ساری عمر میں بھی حاصل نہ ہو", stay with me always, so that you gain what you would not gain elsewhere in a whole lifetime; he names no term of years (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252). Sharib has him come to live in Lahore and begin guiding the people after his pir's death, "اپنے پیر دستگیر کے وصال کے بعد لاہور میں آ کر رہنے لگے", in a year printed 1110 AH whose reading is uncertain (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 257, folio 253).

He is credited with having wells, mosques and *madrasas* built along his route, with writing books in Urdu and Persian, and with composing poetry, though his focus is reported to have been preaching. Lahori is more particular: at every place where he halted on the way, "چاہ و باغیچہ و تالاب بجنہ [OCR?] بنوائے", he had a well, a small garden and a tank built, which, he says, are famous to this day by the name of Abdul Muali, the book printing "عبد المعالی" there and not Abul Muali (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45; p. 58, folio 46).

It is reported that he taught and memorised the Quran, possessed both exoteric and esoteric knowledge, and was a dervish and a perfect scholar. Devotees relate that millions pledged allegiance to him, that kings came to him with requests, that he never distinguished between rich and poor, and that he kept many *chillas* (periods of seclusion) and nights of *dhikr* (remembrance of God). Reportedly, because of him millions of people entered the fold of Islam. Lahori writes that at Lahore "انہوں نے قبول عظیم حاصل کیا اور ہزاروں مرید و خادم ہوئے", he found great acceptance and had thousands of murids and attendants (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46). Kanhaiya Lal calls him the *alim* and *fazil* of his time, a God-worshipping *faqir* of *zuhd* and *riyazat*, and writes that lakhs of men were his murids, though the printed words are uncertain at that point (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288). Sharib writes "آپ صاحب کشف و کرامات تھے، حضرت غوث الاعظم کے سچے عاشق تھے، اپنے پیرو مرشد سے والہانہ عشق تھا", that he was a man of *kashf* and *karamat*, a true lover of Ghawth al-Azam, with a passionate love for his own pir, and that he stood at the degree of *fana fi al-shaikh*, "آپ فنا فی الشیخ کے درجے پر فائز تھے" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 257, folio 253).

He is said to have loved Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani and Mian Mir deeply. Lahori writes that he had a special *nisbat* with Ghaus al-Azam and a devotion to him beyond bounds (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46). Chishti makes him a contemporary of Mian Mir, "میان میر صاحب [illegible] یہ حضرت ہم عہد", and of the emperors "اکبر و جہانگیر و شاہ جہان" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 125). Sharib records two of his teachings. He did not agree that the bond of love is useless after death: "آپ کے نزدیک ہر حالت میں عشق و محبت جائز اور روا ہے", in his view love is lawful and proper in every state (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 258, folio 254). And on opposing the self, "آپ کے نزدیک نفس کی مخالفت نیک فال ہے", the self's opposition is a good omen, "جس طرح کہ کوٹھے پر زاغ کی آواز مہمان کے آنے کی بشارت ہے", as the call of a crow on the roof is good news that a guest is coming (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 258, folio 254).

Two marriages are recorded: the first to a daughter of Dawood Bandgi Kirmani, who died some time after the marriage, and the second to a daughter of Emperor Shah Jahangir, whose considerable wealth and lands the couple are said to have endowed entirely for charitable purposes. He is reported to have had eight sons, buried beside him, one of whom is said to have been martyred fighting in a war against the Sikhs.

It is reported that he died on 16 Rabi ul Awal 1024 Hijri, during the reign of Shah Jahan, at the age of sixty-five. Lahori's text has "سال ایک ہزار چوبیس", the year 1024 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46), and Sharib gives the same day, month and year: "آپ 16- ربیع الاول 1024ھ کو واصل بحق ہوئے" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 257, folio 253). Chishti has the sixteenth of Rabi al-Awwal, "سولویں ماہ ربیع الاول", the year illegible in the printed text, and places the death in Jahangir's reign, "وفات حضرت کی عہد جہانگیر بادشاہ مین وقوع مین آئی", eleven years (the reading uncertain) after the death of Akbar; the age he gives is illegible (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 130–131). Kanhaiya Lal prints the death twice on one page: once in Rabi al-Awwal 1025 AH in Shah Jahan's era, with no day, and once on 21 Rabi al-Awwal 1025 AH, the digits of the year uncertain in both places (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288).

## His Books and His Verse

It is reported that many books have been written on the saint's life and *karāmāt*, that these are easily available, and that his own books are held in the libraries of well-known universities. Lahori names his *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya*, on the wonders and *karamat* of Ghaus al-Azam, as famous (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46). Lahori's editor lists, besides it, *Naghmat-e Daudi*, on the conditions of his own murshid, which Badayuni mentions in the *Muntakhab al-Tawarikh*; *Munis-e Jan*; *Zafran Zar*; *Guldasta-e Bagh-e Iram*, in a manuscript owned by Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi; and a *Sharh-e Yazdah Bai'at* of Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani, held by the same owner (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46). The editor makes *Naghmat-e Daudi* his first-listed source on Shaikh Daud Kirmani (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 49, folio 37), and his bibliography lists an edition of the *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya* under the saint's own name, "ابوالمعالی شاہ لاہوری : تحفۃ القادریہ، سیالکوٹ ۱۳۱۰ھ", printed at Sialkot in 1310 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 296, folio 284). Sharib's list of his best-known books is "تحفۃ القادری، باغ ارم، زعفران زار، رسالہ مونس جاں" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 257, folio 253).

Later writers cited his *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya* as an authority. Lahori's editor quotes it for the statement that Shaikh Jamal Allah, of the progeny of Shaikh Abdul Razzaq son of Abdul Qadir Jilani, was in that era living in the jungles of Bastam, and uses the passage against the sobriquet Hayat al-Mir (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 45, folio 33). Chishti cites the same book, "حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی صاحب حبکا[OCR?] مقبرہ عالیہ لاہور میں ہی کتاب تحفہ القادریہ میں تحریر فرماتی[OCR?] میں", Hazrat Shah Abu al-Maali, whose exalted tomb is in Lahore, writes in his book *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya*, for the five sons of Sayyid Abd al-Wahhab, one of them Sayyid Jamal Allah "حیات المیر" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 369). Lahori's editor also credits him with prompting the commentaries of others: "فتوح الغیب اور مشکوٰۃ کی شرح انہیں ہی کے اصرار کا نتیجہ ہیں", the commentaries on the *Futuh al-Ghaib* and on the *Mishkat* are the result of his insistence (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46). An editor's note elsewhere in the same book, on Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi's commentary on Abd al-Qadir Jilani's *Futuh al-Ghaib*, says that it was completed "حضرت شاہ ابو المعالیؒ کے حکم سے ۱۰۲۳ھ میں", by Hazrat Shah Abu al-Maali's order in 1023 AH, that "مفتاح فتوح" is its chronogrammatic name, and that it was published at Lahore in 1283 AH and at Lucknow in 1290 AH; that note names no place and no silsila for the Shah Abu al-Maali who gave the order, and its link to this saint rests on the year and the subject (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 207, folio 195).

He was a poet as well. Lahori writes that a *diwan* of his verses is in the possession of his descendants, "اور دیوان اشعار ان کی اولاد کے پاس موجود ہے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46), and his editor gives his pen-name as "غریبی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46). Chishti gives it as "غریب" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 123). Sharib calls him a "خوش گو شاعر", a graceful poet, whose pen-name is printed "غربی"[OCR?], and in places "مسلے"[OCR?] and "معالی" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, pp. 257–258, folios 253–254); "آپ صاحب دیوان تھے", he had a *diwan*, from which Sharib prints four couplets of a famous ghazal and a *ruba'i* (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 258, folio 254). Lahori's editor lists among the sources on him an article by Dr Zahur al-Din Ahmad that treats him as a poet, "ظہور الدین احمد ڈاکٹر : شاہ ابوالمعالی ؒ لاہوری (شاعر) مقالہ مشمولہ نذر رحمٰن۔ لاہور ۱۹۶۶ء", published in *Nazr-e Rahman*, Lahore, 1966 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 302, folio 290).

Sharib quotes a couplet of his in which, he says, the saint acknowledges the favours of his pir, Shaikh Dawud:

بہ تخت فقر بنشینم چو حاصل گشت مقصودم
سلیمانی کنم کزجان غلام شیخ داؤدم

*Sharib gives no translation; in this couplet, he says, the saint acknowledges his pir's favours, and it names Shaikh Dawud.* (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 257, folio 253)

A verse is reported to have been spoken to him by his uncle, to the effect of: "Wander and see the whole world, blister your feet, if you find anyone like us, then say." (the story is told below).

His death, too, was put into verse. Chishti records chronograms on it sent for inclusion in his book by "حضرت سید مراد [illegible] شاہ"; they are mostly illegible in the printed text, but one hemistich reads "رحلتنش گفتا معالی خیر دین" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 130–131). Kanhaiya Lal notes that a chronogram-quatrain on his birth and death is recorded in the *Khazinat al-Asfiya*, "خزینۃ الاصفیا", printed in two couplets that begin "ابو المعالی خیر دین احمد مہدی" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288).

## The Shrine

It is reported that in 980 Hijri, when Shah Abul Muali migrated from Depalpur, Okara, to Lahore, he settled at this spot; that during his lifetime the place became a centre for receiving his spiritual blessings; and that he had it constructed and repaired himself. Devotees relate that he was buried here after his passing, and that his tomb was raised at the site of his own seminary, near Mochi Darwaza, where he had earlier built a mosque and taught.

Kanhaiya Lal tells of the quarter around it. "یہ محلہ جہانگیر بادشاہ کے وقت سید شاہ ابو المعالی قادری نے آباد کیا تہا خود بہی یہان ہی سکونت پذیر تہا": the mohalla, the "پیروں کا محلہ", was settled in Jahangir's time by Sayyid Shah Abu al-Maali Qadri, who himself lived there (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 87, folio 90); the book's contents list it as "پیرزادوں کا محلہ[OCR?]", the mohalla of the Pirzadas (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 2). When the sultanate fell to ruin and the people scattered, "شاہ ابو المعالی کی اولاد شہر کے حصار کے اندر جا رہی اور محلہ ویران ہو گیا", his offspring moved inside the city wall and the mohalla was left desolate; but "پرانی عمارات میں سے روضہ یعنی مقبرہ شاہ ابو المعالی پختہ بنا ہوا موجود ہے", of the old buildings the *rauza*, that is the tomb, of Shah Abu al-Maali still stands, built *pakka* (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 87, folio 90). The tomb, he says, has a very large graveyard and countless buildings belonging to it, "اس مقبرے کے متعلق بہت بڑا قبرستان اور بیشمار عمارتین ہین", though he limits himself to the tomb proper (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287).

Both historians agree that the saint began the tomb and did not see it finished. Kanhaiya Lal writes "بنیاد و اس مقبرہ کی شاہ ابو المعالی نے اپنی زندگی مین رکہی تہی مگر ابہی تمام نہ تہا کہ وہ خود انتقال کر گیا اور محمد باقر اسکے فرزند نے اس عمارت کو با ختتام پہنچایا": Shah Abu al-Maali laid the foundation in his lifetime, but it was not yet complete when he died, and his son Muhammad Baqir brought the building to completion (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287). Chishti has it begun "حین حیات", in his lifetime, and the dome raised after his death, and records that under the Sikhs it was rebuilt by the people of the *sajjada*, "سجادہ والے" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 124).

Kanhaiya Lal describes the fabric: on a *pakka*, high platform, an octagonal building with its door of traffic to the south, reached by three stairs (the number uncertain in the printed text); all *pakka* lime, plastered inside and out; a *qalbuti* roof under a grand dome; two-storeyed from outside and single-storeyed within, with the staircase on the west (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287). Chishti also describes an octagonal dome, "رنگ سفید منقش", white and decorated, with a door on the north; a verse over the south door; the door with a green niche and carved lattice; and the Arabic words "ان اولیآء اللہ لا یموتون بل ینتقلون[OCR?]", the friends of God do not die but pass from one place to another (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 123). In his day some of its stones had come to need repair, "کچھ[OCR?] مرمت طلب ہو گئے ہے", and he counts eight doors, the number uncertain in the printed text (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 124). He compares it to two other tombs, that of Hazrat Daud Bandagi at Shergarh (the place-name uncertain in the printed text), "مقبرہ عالیشان حضرت داود بندگی[OCR?] کا", and that of "جناب پیر دستگیر" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 123), and concludes "گویا یہ ہر سہ مقبرہ و مزار باہم مطابق ہین", as if all three tombs matched one another (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 124). The western staircase was unfinished in his day, its completion linked to "محمد باقر صاحب" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 125).

Inside, both historians count four graves on the platform. Kanhaiya Lal names them: "ایک شاہ ابو المعالی کی دوسری شاہ محمد باقر اسکے فرزند کلان کی تیسری شاہ محمد رضا چوتہی حاجی محمد فضل کی", Shah Abu al-Maali; Shah Muhammad Baqir, his eldest son; Shah Muhammad Raza; and Haji Muhammad Fazl (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287). Chishti's four are Shah Abul Maali; "حضرت شاہ محمد باقر صاحب صاحبزادہ", his son; Shah Muhammad Raza, whom he describes in a phrase whose sense is unclear in the printed text, "جو خلف شاہ محمد فاضل[OCR?] صاحب والد حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی"; and a grave linked to "حضرت حاجی شاہ محمد الفضل[OCR?] صاحب" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 124). Around the graves on all four sides stands, in Kanhaiya Lal's account, a man-high wooden lattice made by one he names "قندرا [OCR?] ایمان والے", the one of faith, whom he had already mentioned in describing that man's mosque (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287).

Custodianship is reported to have passed from the saint to his sons and then to the hereditary custodians, the first custodian being Hazrat Haider Ali Shah — also buried in the shrine — and it is said that "the kings of every era took care of this place," responsibility passing in the present era to the Auqaf Department. Chishti names, as descendants, "مولی[OCR?] شاہ", "سمن[OCR?] شاہ", "شاہ سلطان" and "شاہ محمد علی" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 124). Kanhaiya Lal names four as *safed-posh*, "چہبلی [OCR?] شاہ ۔ امام شاہ ۔ فاضل شاہ ۔ فیروز شاہ" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288).

The mosque, in Kanhaiya Lal's account, was built by the saint in his lifetime and rebuilt under the Sikhs: "یہ مسجد شاہ ابو المعالی نے اپنی زندگی مین بنوالی تہی مگر سکہون کے وقت نغوٹی [OCR?] خان جرنیل توپخانہ نے اس مسجد کو دوبارہ بنوایا", the second time by an artillery general whose name is uncertain in the printed text, and it still stood in his day (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287). Chishti adds that the well in the east-facing mosque was built by the saint himself, "مسجد شرق رویہ مین جو کنواں کے وہ بھی خود حضرت نی بنوایا", and mentions a well platform of red stone that he dates "۲۰ بیست سال سے" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 125).

Chishti walks the rest of the precinct. He records the graves of eight of the saint's grandsons, "۸[OCR?] نواسون", of a murid named "محمد صوفی", of one described as "حاکم یا شہزادہ[OCR?]", a ruler or prince, and of "محمد شاہ" and "شاہ حسن", with "حضرت حسن شاہ برہان پور[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 125). He lists *dalans* (open halls): one belonging to "میان عظیم[OCR?] شاہ"; one with "سید مراد[OCR?] … نبیرہ حضرت", a descendant of the saint; one built "سنہ ۱۲۳[OCR?] ھ", the year incomplete as printed; the old *dalan* of "سید امام شاہ"; one linked to "مسمات نوران طوایف[OCR?]"; and a room built by "سید مہر شاہ صاحب و سید عالم شاہ", with "اوکا فقیر", besides many houses of the descendants and a graveyard (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 125). The room for the ritual washing stands west of the tomb (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 128). Kanhaiya Lal likewise writes of many houses and *dalans* built for the saint's offspring, "ہر ایک اپنے اپنے مقبوضہ مکان مین بیٹہا ہے", each sitting in his own occupied house (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287).

The graveyard was once a burial ground for the city. Chishti writes that burials in "گورستان متعلقہ روضہ حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی واقع ٹیلہ شہید گنج", the graveyard of the tomb of Hazrat Shah Abu al-Maali at Tila Shahid Ganj, were banned "بحکم سرکار", by order of the government; his own mother, sister, wife and relatives were buried there in *pakka* graves (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 778). He had them exhumed and moved to the site of Dori Shah, "ششم ماہ شوال المکرم سنہ ۱۳۱۲ [OCR?] مطابق پنجم مارچ سنہ ۱۸۹۵ [OCR?] بروز یکشنبہ", on 6 Shawwal 1312, corresponding to 5 March 1895, a Sunday, the digits uncertain in the printed text (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 779); the deed repeats "از گورستان حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی مرحوم", from the graveyard of the late Hazrat Shah Abu al-Maali (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 781).

Beyond Lahore, Chishti records halting places, *jhoks*, of the saint on the road from Sher Garh: one at a place whose name is uncertain in the printed text, "مقام جہو[OCR?] کی[OCR?] حضرت شاہ ابوالمعالے[OCR?] صاحب", another at "موضع ڈولہن[OCR?] وال", "تا مقام شیر گڑھ", as far as Sher Garh (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 479); one of these is now "مقبرہ [illegible] سید مہلر[OCR?] شاہ نبیرہ حضرت شاہ ابوالمعالے[OCR?]", the tomb of a descendant of the saint (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 480).

It is further reported that the land for Dyal Singh College in Lahore, close to the shrine, was given by the saint's descendants so that people could acquire education.

## Devotion and Practice

There is a press of visitors daily. The custodians' descendants live at the shrine, and people travel from far away to receive *dum* (spiritual healing by breath and recitation) from them. The mosque inside the shrine holds the five daily prayers. A gathering is held daily at which *naʿt* (devotional poetry in praise of the Prophet) is recited, and *langar* (free food distribution) is continuous. Every Thursday, drums are beaten, gatherings are held, lamps are lit, and *niyāz* (consecrated offerings of food) is distributed. Devotees also recite Fateha at the graves.

Visitors come to pay their respects and to pray to Allah through the saint's intercession, and they are reported to say that their prayers are answered there: that whatever the worry or sorrow they find peace only from here, because he had knowledge of the unseen, and that he was a *Wali Allah* (friend of God) from whose door Allah never lets anyone go empty-handed.

The *ʿurs* — the annual commemoration of the saint's death — is held on 15, 16 and 17 Rabi ul Awal. Its programme includes Chadar Poshi (the laying of the shroud), Mela Chiraghan (the festival of lights), a *mehfil* of *naʿt* shareef, dhol and dhamal, *qawwālī*, Ghusal (the ritual washing), and *langar*. Attendance is reported as 200,000 to 250,000 people.

Kanhaiya Lal describes the festivals of his own day. "یہ وہ مکان ہے جہان دونو عیدوں کا میلہ ہوتا ہے اور شہر کے لوگ بکمال اعتقاد مقبرہ پر جاکر نذرانہ دیتے ہیں": this is the place where the fair of both Eids is held, and the people of the city go to the tomb in complete faith and give *nazrana*; the annual *ʿurs*, too, is held, with thousands, the word uncertain in the printed text (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 87, folio 90). In his account of the tomb he gives the order of the *ʿurs*: it falls each year on the date of the saint's death and the crowd stays two days; "پہلے روز رات کو چراغان ہوتا ہے اور طوائف رقاصہ جاکر ناچتی ہین دوسرے روز قوالی ہوتی ہے", on the first night there is *chiraghan* and dancing courtesans dance, and on the second day there is *qawwali*, the *fuqara* listening; and since the fair of both Eids is also held at this *khanqah*, "گویا ایک سال مین تین میلے ہوتے ہین", there are, as it were, three fairs in a year (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288). He writes that the saint's grandsons and great-grandsons are countless, that their livelihood rests on *piri-muridi* and the income of the tomb's offerings, "جسکا گزارہ پیری مریدی مقبرے کے چڑہاوے کی آمدنی پہ ہے", and that all of them live in Lahore (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288).

## Legends and Miracles

Several incidents are reported, which are said to be widely known among devotees.

**The *majzūb* at the roadside.** It is reported that, while still searching for a spiritual master, the saint set out in 980 for the house of a companion, Sheikh Hamid Karishmi, in Delhi. On the way he spoke with a *majzūb* (a spiritually absorbed person) sitting by the road, who told him that the one he was wandering in search of was in his own home, and named his uncle Dawood Bandgi Kirmani. Astonished, he returned to his uncle, who answered with a verse to the effect of: "Wander and see the whole world, blister your feet, if you find anyone like us, then say." His request for allegiance was accepted, and after some days his uncle sent him back to Lahore with the instruction to spend his life in the path of Allah.

Sharib sets the meeting in a sarai at Delhi, where the *majzub* smiled and spoke a sentence in Persian; "یہ الفاظ آپ کے لئے کافی تھے", these words were enough for him (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252). When he reached Sher Garh, Shaikh Dawud addressed him at sight in Persian, "معالی! پیش بیا۔ بارے بگو آں مجذوب راچوں دیدی چہ گفتی وچہ شنیدی", which Sharib glosses in Urdu: Maali, come forward, and tell me, when you saw that *majzub*, what you said and what you heard (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252).

**The promised audience.** Sharib relates that Shaikh Dawud learnt "بذریعہ کشف", by *kashf*, of his longing, and told him: "معالی! امروز یا فردا ترا بجانب جناب مقدس معلی حاضری می کنم۔ منتظر ساعد[OCR?] و وقت باش", glossed "آج یا کل میں تجھ کو مقدس معلی کی جانب حاضر کرتا ہوں، اس گھڑی کا انتظار کرو", today or tomorrow I will bring you into the presence of the holy and exalted one; wait for that hour (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252). One night, between sleep and waking, his pir took him by the hand into the assembly of Ghawth al-Azam, where Abu al-Maali Iraqi sat on the right and his pir on the left. The moment the thought crossed his heart that the man on the right might outrank his pir, Ghawth al-Azam looked at him and spoke in Arabic, which Sharib gives only in Urdu: "اے ابو المعالی! تیرا شیخ میرا دل ہے اور دل بجانب چپ ہی ہوتا ہے", O Abu al-Maali, your shaikh is my heart, and the heart is on the left side (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 257, folio 253).

**Dreams of Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani.** It is said that whoever pledged allegiance at his hands would see Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani in a dream that very night, and that this was due to his *karāmāt* (miraculous graces). Lahori tells the same of him: "ادنیٰ کرامت ان کی یہ تھی کہ مریدان کا بیعت کے روز ہی حضرت غوث الاعظم کی زیارت سے مستفید ہو جاتا تھا", his least *karamat* was that a murid of his would be granted a vision of Ghaus al-Azam on the very day of his *bai'at* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46).

**The rosary.** It is reported that the Mughal emperor Dara Shikoh — so styled in this telling — wrote that he once accompanied Shah Abul Muali to the court of his teacher Sheikh Imam Niyamat Ali, where someone presented the saint with a rosary. Dara Shikoh relates that he silently wished it might be given to him; on their return to the seminary the saint handed him that same rosary, telling him to recite Darood on it daily so that the giver, the recipient and the saint would all share in the reward. He is said to have been amazed that his unspoken thought was known.

Sharib tells it as a wish read and a rosary granted, without naming Dara Shikoh: a man came "اور آپ کو تسبیح نذر کی", and offered him a rosary as *nazr* (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 258, folio 254); when his visitors took their leave, "آپ نے وہ تسبیح ان کو دی اور تاکید فرمائی کہ ہر روز (100) سو بار صلوٰۃ پڑھا کریں", he gave them the rosary and enjoined them to recite the *salawat* one hundred times every day (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 259, folio 255).

**The white turban.** In a second incident attributed to Dara Shikoh, he had never disclosed his love for Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani. One night, it is reported, the Sheikh came to him in a dream and gave him a white turban. In the morning, presenting himself before Shah Abul Muali, he was given a white turban by the saint, who said that Sheikh Abdul Qadir Jilani had asked that it be given to him.

In Sharib's telling the dreamer is Mulla Nemat Ullah. He dreamt himself bare-headed and helpless; "حضرت غوث[OCR?] الاعظم وہاں تشریف لائے، اور سفید دستار ان کو مرحمت فرمائی", Ghawth al-Azam came there and bestowed a white turban on him, saying "ملانعمت! مادر چنیں جاہا از شما خبرداریم", which Sharib glosses "ملانعمت! ہم ایسی جگہ (حالت میں) تمہارے ہال[OCR?] سے خبردار ہیں", Mulla Nemat, in such a place, in such a state, we are aware of you (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 259, folio 255). "دوسرے دن آپ نے ان کو بلایا… آپ نے ان کو سفید دستار عطا کی اور فرمایا کہ یہ وہی دستار ہے": the next day the saint called him, gave him a white turban and said, this is that same turban (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 259, folio 255).

**The rain at the Eidgah.** It is reported that during a drought at Lahore, when Hakim Afzal Agha was ruling, the Hakim gathered a very large crowd at the Eidgah outside the city for congregational prayer for rain, and came to invite the saint. Shah Abul Muali excused himself, saying he could not walk. The Hakim pressed him, saying that if the saint would not come he too would remain sitting there. At last the saint told him to go, and that it would certainly rain after two days. No rain fell for two days; on the third it rained heavily. When the Hakim returned to ask how he had known, the saint is said to have answered that had it rained on the day of the gathering, the arrogant among the worshippers might have taken it for their own wisdom and it would later have become a cause of sin — so Allah delayed it.

**Blessed halting places.** Chishti relates that at each halt on his way toward Lahore "آپ کے قدم سکے[OCR?] برکت سے مکاں متبرک آباد ہو گیا", by the blessing of his steps the place became blessed and settled; that he stayed awake all night; and he exclaims "سبحان اللہ کیا ادب آپنی اپنے پیر دستگیر کا", glory be to God, what reverence he had for his pir, for which he was raised to high rank (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 479–480). Of his miracles, Chishti says they are "صد ہا مشہور", famous by the hundreds, citing "صاحب سفینہ" and the "تذکرة العارفین" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 131).

## Setting

The shrine lies outside one of the gates of the walled city. Chishti lists Mochi Gate, "موچی دروازہ", among the twelve gates of Lahore (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 498). In the histories, the tomb serves as a landmark for its quarter. Chishti speaks of "وہ راہ ہی جو مزار حضرت شاہ ابوالمعالی صاحب کو جاتا ہی", the road that goes to the mazar of Hazrat Shah Abul Maali (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 346); places the tomb of a Shah Kamaita in relation to "مقبرہ شاہ ابوالمعالی صاحب کو گوشہ شمالی و غربی میں", at the north-west corner of the tomb (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 348); writes of the Takia Luharan that it belongs to the tomb, "ہمہ[OCR?] متعلقہ مقبرہ شاہ ابوالمعالی[OCR?] ہے" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 352); sets the Khanqah of Abdi Shah west of the "قبرستان حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی صاحب", the graveyard of Hazrat Shah Abu al-Maali (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 773); and places the tomb of Faqir Taj Shah by reference to it (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 134). Kanhaiya Lal places the tomb of Shah Kanhh [OCR?] in this way: "شاہ ابو المعالی کے مقبرے کے شمال کی سمت موچی دروازے کے باہر کے علاقے میں", north of Shah Abu al-Maali's tomb in the area outside Mochi Gate (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 304, folio 307). His book treats the tomb of Shah Abu Ishaq Qadri, a different saint, in the entry immediately before this one (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 4, folio 3).

## Where the Sources Disagree

- His name. The survey names him Hazrat Syed Muhammad Khair ul Deen, with Abul Muali as his *kunyat* and Asadullah as his title. Alam Faqri's compendium gives his real name as Shah Khairuddin Muhammad. Lahori's entry heading and appendix make Khair al-Din part of his own name, "شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی لاہوری بن سید رحمۃ اللہ" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45; p. 287, folio 275), but the book's index makes Khair al-Din his father, "خیر الدین (والد شاہ ابوالمعالی) | ۴۵", a gloss the same index uses for the father of a different Abul Muali (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 317, folio 305; p. 327, folio 315). Sharib makes al-Maali his name and Shah Khair al-Din a *laqab* beside Asad al-Din (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251). Kanhaiya Lal calls him Sayyid Shah Abu al-Maali Qadri (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 87, folio 90).
- His order. The row's name, Lahori, Chishti and Kanhaiya Lal call him Qadri (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45; Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 123; Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 87, folio 90). The survey answers the question of his order with descent and personal affiliation to Dawood Bandgi Kirmani. Sharib never uses the word Qadri of him; his tie to Abd al-Qadir Jilani is "اویسی", and his *bai'at* is to Shaikh Dawud (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251; p. 256, folio 252).
- His birth. The survey gives 10 Zil Hajj 960 AH at Kirman, Iran. Lahori gives 960 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46); Chishti 10 Dhu al-Hijja, Eid al-Adha, a Monday, 960 [OCR?] (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 130); Kanhaiya Lal 10 Dhu al-Hijja, the day of Eid, 992 [OCR?] (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288); Sharib 10 Dhu al-Hijja 910 AH at Sher Garh, or, it is also said, 920 AH (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251).
- His death. The survey, Lahori and Sharib give 1024 AH, the survey and Sharib on 16 Rabi ul Awal (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46; Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 257, folio 253). Chishti gives the sixteenth of Rabi al-Awwal with the year illegible (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 130). Kanhaiya Lal gives Rabi al-Awwal 1025 [OCR?] in one place and 21 Rabi al-Awwal 1025 [OCR?] in another on the same page (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288).
- The reign in which he died. The survey and Kanhaiya Lal say in the reign of Shah Jahan (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288); Chishti says in Jahangir's reign, eleven [OCR?] years after the death of Akbar (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 131).
- His age at death. The survey gives sixty-five; Kanhaiya Lal prints an age that is uncertain in the printed text, and Chishti's is illegible (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288; Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 131).
- His family's coming to India. The survey has his father migrate from Iran and settle at Depalpur, Okara, and the saint born in Kirman. Sharib has the family come from Kirman in 796 AH, a great-great-grandfather's generation earlier, settle at Uch and then near Multan, and the father move on to Sher Garh, where the saint was born (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 255, folio 251).
- His years with his master. The survey says he studied with his uncle until the age of thirty; Lahori says he attended on his pir for thirty-two years (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45); Sharib says the shaikh bade him stay with him always and gives no term (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252).
- His coming to Lahore. The survey says he migrated from Depalpur to Lahore in 980 AH, at his uncle's command. Lahori says he was deputed to Lahore after receiving *khilafat* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45). Sharib has him reach Delhi in 980 AH and settle in Lahore after his pir's death, in a year printed 1110 [OCR?], which as printed falls after the death year the same book gives (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252; p. 257, folio 253).
- The meeting with the *majzub*. The survey has it on the road while he was travelling to Delhi, after which he returned to his uncle; Sharib has it in a sarai at Delhi, after which he went on to Sher Garh (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 256, folio 252).
- The rosary. The survey has Dara Shikoh receive it, after a visit to the court of Sheikh Imam Niyamat Ali, with the charge to recite Darood daily. Sharib names no Dara Shikoh: a man offered the saint a rosary as *nazr*, and he gave it to his departing visitors with the charge to recite the *salawat* one hundred times a day (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 258, folio 254; p. 259, folio 255). Alam Faqri's compendium makes the saint the *murshid* of a companion of Dara Shikoh.
- The white turban. The survey gives the dream and the turban to Dara Shikoh; Sharib gives them to Mulla Nemat Ullah (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, p. 259, folio 255).
- The number of his followers. The survey says millions pledged allegiance to him; Lahori says thousands (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46); Kanhaiya Lal says lakhs, the word uncertain in the printed text (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288).
- What he built on the road. The survey credits him with wells, mosques and *madrasas* along his route; Lahori with a well, a small garden and a tank at every halt (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 57, folio 45).
- His pen-name. Lahori's editor gives "غریبی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46); Chishti "غریب" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 123); Sharib "غربی"[OCR?], and in places "مسلے"[OCR?] and "معالی" (Sharib, *Tazkirah Awliya-e Pak o Hind*, pp. 257–258, folios 253–254).
- Who built the tomb. The survey says he had the place constructed and repaired himself. Kanhaiya Lal says he laid the foundation and his son Muhammad Baqir completed it (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287); Chishti that it was begun in his lifetime, the dome raised after his death, and the whole rebuilt under the Sikhs by the people of the *sajjada* (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 124).
- Who lies beside him. The survey gives him eight sons buried beside him and names Haider Ali Shah, the first custodian, as also buried in the shrine. Kanhaiya Lal names the four graves on the platform as the saint's, his eldest son Shah Muhammad Baqir's, Shah Muhammad Raza's and Haji Muhammad Fazl's (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287). Chishti names the same four, with Shah Muhammad Raza described in a phrase of unclear sense, and records the graves of eight [OCR?] grandsons (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 124; p. 125).
- The *ʿurs*. The survey gives three days, 15 to 17 Rabi ul Awal; Kanhaiya Lal gives two days, held on the date of his death (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288).
- Where the tomb stands. The survey places it near Mochi Darwaza; Kanhaiya Lal and Chishti outside Mochi Gate, Kanhaiya Lal at the north-west corner (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 284, folio 287; Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 123); Chishti places its graveyard at Tila Shahid Ganj (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 778); Lahori's editor gives Shah Abul Muali Street, Gowalmandi (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 287, folio 275).
- The *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya* on Sayyid Jamal Allah. Lahori's editor uses the saint's book against the sobriquet Hayat al-Mir (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 45, folio 33); Chishti, citing the same book, names Sayyid Jamal Allah with "حیات المیر" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 369).

## Legacy

It is reported that because of him millions of people entered the fold of Islam, and that visitors still come in their thousands each day. The older books measure his standing by his learning and by his descendants. Lahori writes "اولاد ان کی لاہور میں سکونت پذیر ہے مگر افسوس کہ ایسے عالم و فاضل و ولی کی اولاد علم سے بے بہرہ ہے", his descendants live in Lahore, but alas, the offspring of so learned and saintly a man are without learning (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 58, folio 46), and Kanhaiya Lal, of the four *safed-posh* descendants he names, "مگر علم سے بہرہ نہین رکہتے", that they have no share of learning (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288). Yet it is reported that the saint's descendants gave the land for Dyal Singh College so that people could acquire education. Kanhaiya Lal writes that many books of his composition survive, "بہت سی کتابین اسکی تصنیف کی ہوئی ابتک موجود ہین" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 285, folio 288), and a modern article on him as a poet appeared in Lahore in 1966 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 302, folio 290). Of the old buildings of his quarter, Kanhaiya Lal names his tomb as the one still standing, with its two Eid fairs and its annual *ʿurs* (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 87, folio 90).

A general compendium of Pakistani Sufi saints, consulted independently of the field survey, gives this figure's real name as Shah Khairuddin Muhammad — consistent with the survey's own "Hazrat Syed Muhammad Khair ul Deen" — with Abul Ma'ali recorded as his *kunyat* (byname), matching the survey exactly. It adds that he served as spiritual guide (*murshid*) to a companion of the Mughal prince Dara Shikoh — a somewhat different framing than the field survey's own account, which has Dara Shikoh himself directly receiving a rosary and a turban from the saint's hand. Both descriptions agree the two were connected; which is the more precise account is not resolvable from these two sources alone.
