## Overview

In Lahore's Miani Sahib Graveyard, by local report the largest graveyard in Asia, the oldest seat belongs not to the dead in general but to one man in particular: Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri, a scholar and Qur'an-teacher who built a mosque, a madrasa and a library on this ground while he was still alive, and who was buried beside his own mosque when he died in 1630. It is reported that the graveyard grew up around his tomb over the following centuries, as generation after generation asked to be laid to rest near him, and not the other way around. The graveyard is reported to cover some 475 kanals.

Three older books on the saints and monuments of Lahore know the tomb and set it in the same graveyard. Mufti Ghulam Sarwar Lahori's *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, written in 1875, gives him a full entry as "شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری قادری و نقشبندی", Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori, Qadiri and Naqshbandi (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 59–60, folios 47–48), and its appendix lists his tomb in the Miani graveyard, "قبرستان میانی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 288, folio 276). Maulvi Nur Ahmad Chishti's *Tahqiqat-e Chishti* opens its chapter on the Miani graveyard with "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت شیخ طاہر بندگی", the section on the tomb of Hazrat Shaikh Tahir Bandagi (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 186). And Kanhaiya Lal's *Tarikh-e Lahore* calls his the first and largest enclosure of the Miani graveyard, a walled precinct holding thousands of graves, old and new (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295). The three books spell his name in several ways, name his Sufi order in several ways, and differ from what is reported at the shrine and from one another on the day and year of his death, on who gave him the name Bandagi, and on how the graveyard came to be; those differences are set out side by side below.

## The Life of Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri

He was born in Lahore in 1576 CE. His father, Fazlullah, gave him his early education, and he went on to memorize the Qur'an. For advanced learning he became a disciple (*mureed*) of Hazrat Shah Sikandar, who is credited with giving him the name "Tahir Bandagi" and is reported to have told him, "you are meant for the spiritual reformation of people; simply remain devoted to the service of humanity at all times."

Lahori names the same master more fully: Shah Sikandar, son of Pir Kamal Kaithali, whose *murid* and *khalifa* Shaikh Muhammad Tahir was, and under whom he first completed his training (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47). Chishti credits the title to another man. He writes that it came from "حضرت لال کمال شاہ[OCR?] ساکن کیتھل[OCR?]", a Hazrat Lal Kamal Shah of Kaithal (the reading of both names uncertain in the printed text), and that "سو اب تک طاہر بندگی مشہور ہیں", he is known as Tahir Bandagi to this day (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 189). Kanhaiya Lal makes him the *murid* of "شاہ کمال کیتہلی [OCR?]", Shah Kamal Kaithali, in the Qadiri family (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).

It is also reported that there was a real trial in his life: a period in which he became enamored of a Hindu woman and stepped back from his religious work. His well-wishers are said to have appealed to Hazrat Mujaddid Alf Sani on his behalf, after which he returned to his path and formally became Mujaddid Alf Sani's disciple as well, later teaching Mujaddid Alf Sani's own children. The episode is told plainly, as part of his story rather than something to smooth over. Chishti tells the episode at length, and it is given under Legends and Miracles below.

The books record the bond with the Mujaddid in several forms. Lahori writes that after his training under Shah Sikandar he attended "امام ربانی مجدد الف ثانی پیر احمد فاروقی سرہندی", the Imam-e Rabbani, Mujaddid Alf Sani, Pir Ahmad Faruqi Sirhindi, gained great benefits from him, and came to Lahore at the Mujaddid's direction to guide the people, where thousands reached high stations through him (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47). The edition's modern editor separately names him a *khalifa* of the Mujaddid, extremely strict in the *shariah*, who died in 1040 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1). Chishti calls him a *murid* of "حضرت شیخ احمد سرہندی", writes that he first taught the Mujaddid's sons "حضرت شیخ محمد معصوم اور شیخ محمد سعید [OCR?]", Hazrat Shaikh Muhammad Masum and Shaikh Muhammad Said, and says that the *qutbiyat* of Lahore was conferred on him by the Mujaddid, "قطبیت لاہور کی [illegible] حضرت مجدد سے اونکو عطا ہوئی" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187). Kanhaiya Lal calls him a *khadim* of Shaikh Ahmad in the Mujaddidi silsila (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).

Kanhaiya Lal writes that he came "شہر سرہند سے", from the city of Sirhind, and settled at the site in the time of "شاہ جہانگیر بادشاہ" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88); elsewhere he places him in the Shah Jahan era, "خاندان قادریہ مین ایک خدا پرست صاحب کشف و کرامت شاہجہانی عہد مین تہا", a God-worshipping man of *kashf* and *karamat* of the Qadiri family in the time of Shah Jahan, and adds that he was accomplished in the outward sciences too (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295). Chishti, in a passage much damaged in print, says he lived inside the walled city, in a quarter printed "محلہ شیخ اسحاق[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 191).

Lahori calls him the *qutb* of his age in the Qadiri silsila, "اپنے عہد کے قطب وقت", and says of him "کوئی سائل دین و دنیا کا جو ان کے دروازے پر آیا خالی نہ گیا": no seeker of faith or of the world who came to his door went away empty (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47). He lived, Lahori writes, by his pen: "کتب احادیث و تفاسیر کی کتابت حضرت کرتے اور ہدیہ کر کے گزارہ اوقات کرتے", he copied out books of hadith and *tafsir* and subsisted by presenting them, and his nights passed in instructing his attendants and in worship (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47). Chishti, too, has him live on copying hadith and *tafsir* and accept no "نذر و نیاز [OCR?]", no votive offerings (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187). Lahori records a refusal of patronage: "یہ حضرت تمام عمر کسی دولت مند کے پاس نہ گئے اور نہ ان کو اپنے دربار میں بار دیا", all his life he went to no rich man, nor admitted the rich to his own *darbar* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47).

It is reported that in his later years he married twice, to Mah-e-Khanam and to Ismatullah, the latter the daughter of an Afghan king, and that he spent his nights absorbed in remembrance (*zikr*) rather than in the comforts that came with his following. He is described as periodically fasting and practicing self-denial for stretches of fifteen to twenty days, and as never allowing the crowds who gathered around him to produce pride. Chishti records the graves of two wives under other names and parentage: "ماہ خاتم[OCR?] دختر مرزا امان اللہ[OCR?]", Mah Khatam, daughter of Mirza Amanullah, and "عصمت النسا[OCR?] دختر سید شہ[OCR?]", Ismat al-Nisa, daughter of a Sayyid whose name is cut short in print (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 189). He adds "اولاد حضرت کی باقی نہیں", none of his descendants remain (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 189).

Of the orders in which he initiated others, Chishti writes "حضرت شیخ طاہر ہر چار[OCR?] سلسلہ یعنی قادریہ ونقشبندیہ وسہروردیہ میں بیعت لیتے تھی": that he took *bai'at* in all four (the numeral uncertain in print) silsilas, though only three are then named, the Qadiri, Naqshbandi and Suhrawardi (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 215).

He died on 8 Muharram 1040 AH, reported as 8 August 1630 CE, the two calendars agreeing exactly, at the age of 54, consistent with the reported birth year of 1576. Lahori gives the same day and adds the hour: "بروز پنجشنبہ وقت چاشت آٹھویں محرم سال ایک ہزار چالیس ہجری", on Thursday, at the forenoon hour, 8 Muharram 1040 AH; but he gives his age as fifty-six, "اور عمر چھپن برس کی پائی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47). The edition's editor repeats the year, as "حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہوری (ف ۱۰۴۰ھ)" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 129, folio 117, حاشیہ 1). Chishti has a different day and year, "بروزِ پنجشنبہ ششم [OCR?] ماہ محرم سنہ ۱۰۴۱ [OCR?]", Thursday the sixth of Muharram, 1041, both numbers uncertain in print, and writes that he was buried at Miani, "بمقام میانی مدفون ہوئی" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187); he places the death in the reign of Shah Jahan, the age and regnal year given in words that cannot now be read with confidence (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 188). Kanhaiya Lal gives the year only, 1040[OCR?] AH, without month or day (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88).

## His Khalifas and His Circle

Lahori names four *khalifas*: "شیخ طاہر کے چار خلیفہ تھے", Shaikh Tahir had four *khalifas*, Abu Muhammad Qadiri, Sayyid Sufi, Shaikh Adam Banuri and Shah Ghulan Sarmast, the last with his own *mazar* in Lahore, "موری دروازے کے باہر سرکاری باغ کے اندر", outside the Mori Gate, inside the government garden (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 279, folio 267).

Shaikh Adam Banuri, Lahori writes, came to him on foot, "پیادہ بنور سے لاہور میں آیا", walking from Banur to Lahore (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47). The edition's editor quotes Adam Banuri's own Persian account, from the second volume of the *Khulasat al-Maarif*, of receiving the *nisbat-e jazba* from Shaikh Tahir Lahori, "کہ خلفائی کبار حضرت پیر است", who is among the great *khalifas* of Hazrat Pir, and of that *nisbat* having reached Tahir through Shah Iskandar son of Pira Shah Kamal Kaithali (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47, حاشیہ 1).

Chishti records the grave of Hazrat Abu Muhammad Qadiri Lahori within Shaikh Tahir's enclosure (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 190), and writes that Abu Muhammad's family were *khadims* of Shaikh Tahir (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 191). Chishti records that Abu Muhammad Lahori was "سلسلہ قادریہ میں مرید شیخ طاہر صاحب", a *murid* of Shaikh Tahir in the Qadiri silsila (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 215). Lahori traces a three-generation chain out of his circle: Shaikh Muhammad Fazil Qadri Batalvi was *murid* and *khalifa* of Shaikh Muhammad Afzal Kalanauri, who was *murid* of Shaikh Abu Muhammad Lahori, who was *murid* of Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 76, folio 74).

In a footnote, Lahori's editor records Shaikh Tahir's wish to visit the *mazar* of Shah Husain to seek his help, *istimdad* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1).

A book has also been ascribed to him. Lahori's editor writes "تذکرہ مجددیہ کے مصنف کا نام معلوم نہیں ہے", the author of the *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya* is not known, and then reports that Muhammad Din Fauq stated it to be the work of "حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہوری (ف ۱۰۴۰ھ)" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 129, folio 117, حاشیہ 1). The attribution is Fauq's, given by an editor who has just said the author is unknown. Chishti, for his part, draws on a second source whose title is printed "کتاب تذکرہ محدودیہ[OCR?]" and copies from it a Persian petition, an "عرضی", from one "احقر العبد مرتب[OCR?] محمد طاہر[OCR?]" who writes "متوجہ لاہور شدم", I set out for Lahore, and names the Qadiri and Chishti orders; nearly every word of it is uncertain in print (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 188). Chishti's first source for the chapter is "کتاب تذکرۃ الاحبا [OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 186).

## The Mosque, Madrasa, and Library

The land that is now Miani Sahib Graveyard was, in Hazrat Tahir Bandagi's lifetime, gifted to him so that he could teach. On it he built a mosque, a madrasa, and a library (*kutub khana*), and taught the Qur'an there himself. One specific episode is reported from this period: Sikh raiders, believing gold was hidden inside boxes on the premises, broke in and found only books, and, on discovering this, threw the books back into the locality and left. When Hazrat Tahir Bandagi died, his resting place beside this mosque became his shrine, and rulers and disciples of the period are said to have kept it under careful administrative care from the start.

Kanhaiya Lal describes the madrasa in his own terms: "شیخ محمد طاہر نے بہت برست [OCR?] تک اس جگہ پر مدرسہ جاری رکہا اور علوم فقہ و حدیث و تفسیر طلبا کو مفت پڑہانا رہا مرید بہی بہت کئے اور سلوک کی تلقین کی", Shaikh Muhammad Tahir kept a madrasa running at this place for many years (the word uncertain in print), taught *fiqh*, hadith and *tafsir* to students free of charge, made many *murids* and gave instruction in *suluk* (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88). "چونکہ محمد طاہر عالم و فاضل و فقیر کامل تہا چند سال میں ہزاروں لوگ اُسکے مرید و شاگرد ہو گئے": because he was learned, accomplished and a perfect *faqir*, within a few years thousands became his *murids* and pupils, and a magnificent settlement grew up around him (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88). Chishti likewise writes that "ہزار ہا", thousands, came to him in Lahore (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187).

Kanhaiya Lal tells of a plundering, too, though not as the survey does. After the saint's death, he writes, the quarter stayed settled "اخیر سلطنت اسلام تک", until the end of Muslim rule; then plunderers looted "ہزاروں قرآن و کتابیں", thousands of Qur'ans and books, tried to sell them, and "باہر گاؤں پہینک کر چلے گئے", threw them outside the village and went away, burning the quarter to ashes in their anger (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 85–86, folios 88–89). Of the ruined quarter, "ایک تو مزار شیخ محمد طاہر موجود ہے اور دوسری کسی قدر عمارت کہنہ مدرسہ شیخ محمد طاہر کا ابتک موجود": the *mazar* of Shaikh Muhammad Tahir still stands, and some of the old fabric of his madrasa still stood when Kanhaiya Lal wrote (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 86, folio 89).

## The Shrine

All three books place the tomb in the Miani graveyard. In Lahori's book, Shah Muhammad Muqim, travelling to Lahore, halts "جب گورستان میانی میں بجوار مزار شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری پہنچے", when he reached the Miani graveyard, in the vicinity of the *mazar* of Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 64, folio 52); the editor calls the place "میانی شریف" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 64, حاشیہ 1), and fixes another grave by the shrine: "حاجی شیخ رمضان لاہوری کا مزار دربار حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہور میانی صاحب کے قریب ہے", the *mazar* of Haji Shaikh Ramzan Lahori is near the *darbar* of Hazrat Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Bandagi, Lahore, Miani Sahib (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 125, folio 113). Kanhaiya Lal gives the place as lying from mauza Mazang in a direction whose printed word is uncertain, "موضع مزنگ سے بسمت نیبرت [OCR?]" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88), and lists the tomb among the buildings outside the city (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 4, folio 3). Lahori's own text calls the site a "مزار", his editor a "درگاہ" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 274, folio 262).

Kanhaiya Lal describes the fabric: "اول احاطہ مزار شیخ ظاہر بندگی [OCR?] یہ احاطہ سب سے بڑا ہے اور وسیع چار دیواری پختہ خشتی بنی ہوئی ہے جسکے اندر ہزارون قبرین قدیمہ جدیدہ ہین", first, the enclosure of the *mazar* of Shaikh Tahir Bandagi, which is the largest of all, a spacious *pakka* wall of brick, inside which are thousands of graves, old and new (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295). The tomb itself stands on a platform within it: "اس کلان چار دیواری کے اندر ایک اور بڑا پختہ چبوترہ ہے جسپر پختہ چونہ گچ مزار اس بزرگ کا بنا ہے", within this great wall is another large *pakka* platform, on which this elder's *pakka* lime-plastered *mazar* is built (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).

The tomb has been rebuilt. Lahori's editor writes "موجودہ مزار جناب پیر [illegible] گیلانی مدظلہ کا تعمیر کردہ ہے", the present *mazar* was built by Pir [illegible] Gilani, the name lost in print, who reports that when the old tomb was excavated for the new construction, "قدیم مزار بالکل مزار حضرت مجدد الف ثانی رح کی طرز پر بنا ہوا تھا", the old *mazar* had been built exactly in the style of the *mazar* of Hazrat Mujaddid Alf Sani (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 59–60, folios 47–48, حاشیہ 2).

His walled enclosure, the *chahar-diwari*, served others as a burial ground and as a landmark. Lahori writes of Sayyid Munawwar Ali Shah Naqshbandi Suhrawardi Lahori that he died in 1264 AH and that "مرقد چار دیواری شیخ طاہر لاہوری کے اندر ہے", his tomb is inside the *chahar-diwari* of Shaikh Tahir Lahori (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 230–231, folios 218–219); that passage names no place, but the book's appendix lists the same man's tomb in the Miani graveyard, "قبرستان میانی نزد مقبرہ حضرت طاہر بندگی قادری", near the tomb of Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 290, folio 278). The appendix places a Chishti beside him as well, Shaikh Haji Ramzan Lahori Chishti, "نزد مقبرہ شیخ طاہر بندگی میانی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 288, folio 276). Chishti uses the wall to locate other graves: "یہ رویہ چار دیواری مقبرہ حضرت شیخ طاہر متصل ہے" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 195); "گوشہ شرقی چار دیواری حضرت شیخ طاہر میں", in the eastern corner of Hazrat Shaikh Tahir's wall (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 198); and "چار دیواری شیخ طاہر بندگی[OCR?] کے شمال", to the north of it (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 209). Kanhaiya Lal, too, sets a neighbouring enclosure by "چار دیواری شیخ طاہر" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 294, folio 297).

## Custodianship

The shrine is overseen today by its Gaddi Nasheen (current custodians), with several people formally employed as staff who reportedly consider caring for the shrine year-round to be a privilege rather than a job.

The older books name custodians of their own day, and on their line they disagree. Lahori, giving the genealogy of a Gilani family, ends it with "سید شہسوار جو اب [OCR?] سجادہ نشین مزار شیخ طاہر بندگی کا ہے", Sayyid Shahsawar, who is now (the word uncertain in print) *sajjada nashin* of the *mazar* of Shaikh Tahir Bandagi (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 274, folio 262). His line runs back to Sayyid Abd al-Qadir Lahori, "سید عبدالقادر لاہوری", son of Sayyid Jalal al-Din Baghdadi, who settled at Lahore in Akbar's reign and married a daughter of "نواب میر کفایت خاں"; and it records one member's trade, "بہادر بن چراغ شاہ جو مہر کنی کا کام کرتا ہے", Bahadur son of Chiragh Shah, a seal-engraver (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 274, folio 262). The edition's editor corrects him: "یہاں شجرہ نسب درست نہیں ہے۔ بلکہ یوں ہونا چاہیے", the genealogy here is not correct, it should run thus, and gives a line that ends in "پیر شیر حسین درسی [OCR?] طاہری مد ظلہ اس وقت سجادہ نشین درگاہ حضرت شیخ طاہر بندگی ہیں", Pir Sher Husain Darsi (the reading uncertain) Tahiri, may his shadow endure, who is at this time *sajjada nashin* of the *dargah* of Hazrat Shaikh Tahir Bandagi (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 274, folio 262, حاشیہ ۲). The *nisba* Tahiri is taken from the saint himself.

Chishti names a *sajjada nashin* as one of his informants: "زبانی سید منصور سجادہ نشین خانقاہ حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر صاحب قدس سرہ", from the mouth of Sayyid Mansur, *sajjada nashin* of the *khanqah* of Hazrat Shaikh Muhammad Tahir (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 209). Elsewhere he names a "سید شاہ سوارا[OCR?]", of the descendants of Abd al-Qadir Thani, as "سجادہ نشین خانقاہ شیخ طاہر بندگی[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 649), and in further passages, badly damaged in print, a Sayyid whose name begins "سید سہسو[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 204) and a "شہسوار" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 650). He records, too, that the office itself, "عہدہ سجادہ نشینی شیخ طاہر صاحب", was disputed (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 215–216).

## Devotion and Practice

A mosque stands beside the shrine where the five daily prayers are held, and visitors come both for prayer and to recite the Fatiha and make personal supplications. It is reported that some families have visited for generations, following parents and grandparents before them. The main recurring gatherings are Mehfil-e-Naat and langar, held weekly on Thursday nights and Fridays.

Daily attendance is given as 1,200 to 1,600 visitors.

It is reported that the shrine owes its continuing importance to answered prayer across generations: families who saw their petitions answered here return, and bring their children to do the same. Hazrat Tahir Bandagi is described by visitors as a "perfect saint" (*wali-e-kamil*) who was granted visions of the Prophet and of other venerable elders on multiple occasions, and the presence of Jinnat and of other noble burials nearby is offered by devotees as further evidence, in their eyes, of his spiritual standing.

The Urs falls every year on the 7th, 8th, and 9th of Muharram, drawing an estimated 250,000 to 300,000 pilgrims across the three days. The current custodians (*Gaddi Nasheen*) describe Hazrat Tahir Bandagi as someone who did not care for drumming (*dhol*) or ecstatic dance (*dhamal*), and say the Urs is kept accordingly restrained, centered on Mehfil-e-Naat and langar rather than music or dancing.

Chishti records the *urs* of his own day, "عرس یعنی فاتحہ سالیہ[OCR?] حضرت شیخ طاہر", the *urs*, that is the annual *fatiha*, of Hazrat Shaikh Tahir, kept by the *sajjada nashin* and the gravediggers in the first ten days of Muharram, on a date printed "تاریخ تیریں[OCR?] ماہ محرم", whose reading is uncertain (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 216). Kanhaiya Lal, in his first account of the saint, names no *urs*, fair, *langar*, offering, custodian or endowment (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88).

## Legends and Miracles

Several traditions are attached to the shrine, and it is reported that not all of them are agreed on even by today's visitors.

One account holds that Hazrat Tahir Bandagi, while leading Taraweeh prayers, was granted a vision of the Prophet Muhammad, who told him that whoever looked upon him, learned from him, or was buried near him would enter Paradise, offered as the reason so many notable figures chose burial around his tomb. Lahori records a promise in the saint's own words: "حضرت فرماتے تھے کہ میری وفات کے بعد جو شخص میرے احاطہ مزار میں مدفون ہو گا۔ میں نے خدا سے مانگا ہے کہ وہ جنتی ہو گا", the Hazrat used to say that whoever is buried within the enclosure of his *mazar* after his death, he has asked of God that that person shall be of Paradise (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47).

A second, more contested tradition concerns jinn. It is reported that graves near the shrine are popularly said to belong to jinn who watch over the site, and that some visitors report having seen figures in human form vanish before their eyes. But this is reportedly not universally accepted even among visitors: others say that although the graves exist, they have never personally witnessed any jinn or wild animal there, and regard the jinn stories as ignorance, pointing out, in their own study of Hazrat Tahir Bandagi's life, that he himself never taught or endorsed such beliefs. Both positions are reported as live, current opinions, not settled fact.

Chishti tells the story of his fall and return as a prophecy fulfilled. The Mujaddid, he relates, foretold that one of those present would become a *kafir*. Then, "بمقام سرہند", at Sirhind, Shaikh Tahir fell in love with a "ماہ پیشانی [OCR?]", a moon-browed beauty; he put on the "زنار [OCR?]", the sacred thread, drew the "قشقہ", the forehead mark, "قشقہ کھینچ کر", and "بتخانہ میں جا بیٹھے", went and sat in the temple, "ہندو ہو کر", having become a Hindu (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187). At this point the story gives a Persian couplet:

کافرِ عشقم مسلمانی مرا در کار نیست
ہر رگِ من تار گشتہ حاجتِ زنار [OCR?] نیست

Chishti gives no translation; the couplet turns on the words کافر, the unbeliever, and زنار, the thread he had put on (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187). The Mujaddid's sons, whom he had taught, protested, "ہمارا اوستاد کافر ہو گیا", our teacher has become a *kafir*, and invoked the "حقِ اوستادی", the right a teacher holds; the Mujaddid prayed, his prayer was answered, and Shaikh Tahir repented (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187).

Kanhaiya Lal writes that "خزینۃ الاصفیا و حدیقۃ الاولیا و تذکرہ مجددیہ وغیرہ کتب مین اس بزرگ کی کرامتین بیشمار تحریر ہین", countless *karamat* of this elder are written in the *Khazinat al-Asfiya*, the *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, the *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya* and other books, but he narrates none of them himself (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).

## In Verse

Lahori closes his entry with a chronogram quatrain on the saint, which opens:

شیخ طاہر پاک باز و پاک دین
اوج عرفان پر تھے مثل آفتاب

The quatrain is on Shaikh Tahir himself, naming him in its first line; no translation of it is given, and the year it encodes is not stated with these opening lines (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 60, folio 48).

Chishti records a *qita* on his death by "مفتی [OCR?] غلام سرور", nearly every word of which is uncertain in print (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187). Kanhaiya Lal says that the year of his death comes out of a single word, though he prints that word in two ways in two places: "سنہ ۱۰۴۰ [OCR?] ہجری میں وہ مر گیا جسکی تاریخ غم کے لفظ سے نکلی", he died in 1040 AH, whose date comes out of the word *gham*, grief (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88); and "عم [OCR?] کے لفظ سے اسکی تاریخ وفات نکلی ہے", the date of his death comes from the word printed عم, the reading uncertain (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295). No fuller chronogram phrase is printed in either place.

## Setting: The Miani Graveyard

The books' accounts of the Miani graveyard speak of the graveyard as a whole, not of this shrine alone. Kanhaiya Lal writes "یہ خطہ جسکو گورستان میانی کہتے ہین لاہور سے بجانب جنوب بفاصلہ دو میل واقع ہے", the tract called the Miani graveyard lies two miles south of Lahore; it was formerly a village of the Miani people, that is of *ulama* and *fuzala*, called Panj Pir Dhera because five graves stood there before the village, "کیونکہ ڈہیرہ [OCR?] پنجابی زبان مین قبر کو کہتے ہین", since in Punjabi *dhera* means a grave (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 291–292, folios 294–295). He explains the name Miani from the learned men who lived there: "چونکہ میانا پنجابی زبان میں مولوی عالم فاضل کو کہتے ہیں اور وہی لوگ اسمیں رہتے اسواسطے اس بستی کا نام میانی مشہور ہو گیا", because in Punjabi a *maulvi*, a learned and accomplished man, is called *miana*, and such people lived there, the settlement came to be known as Miani (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88). Within the graveyard he names, besides the saint's enclosure, only the unfinished enclosure of Rani Gul Begum, "یہ عورت قوم طوایف", a woman of the courtesan caste (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).

Kanhaiya Lal describes how the ground became a burial place after the ruin of the quarter: "بعد ویرانی اس محلہ کے شہر لاہور کے لوگوں نے اس جگہ کو قبرستان بنا لیا", after the ruin of this quarter the people of Lahore made the place a graveyard, where thousands of graves have been made, burials continue, and many *pakka* enclosures have been built (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 86, folio 89). In his later account he adds that for the last ten or twelve years general burial had been stopped, and that only those who had *pakka* enclosures still buried their dead there (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).

Chishti's chapter carries several accounts of the place, kept side by side. In Akbar's reign, he writes, it was a village where only learned men lived, and because Punjabis call *ulama* "میاں", it was called "میانی"; its chief was "حافظ جان محمد صاحب والد حضرت ابو محمد قادری", Hafiz Jan Muhammad, father of Hazrat Abu Muhammad Qadiri, after whose death Abu Muhammad was made its head; a dispute over a point of law then emptied the village, until a Hazrat Mir Ali, the name uncertain in print, was appointed by the king and settled there, and it was peopled again (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 191). He explains the second name differently from Kanhaiya Lal: a "پیر علیمہ[OCR?] صاحب" had the coffins of five elders brought and buried here, and since a grave mound is a *dher* in Punjabi, the place was called "میانی پنج ڈھیر[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 191). In his account of the gravediggers, heavily damaged in print, the account passes from the reign of Shah Jahan to that of Alamgir, has the graveyard fall desolate after Muhammad Shah, and has it grow again when burials resume under Maharaja Ranjit Singh, the sequence only approximately legible (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 204); elsewhere he writes that the Sikhs looted mauza Miani (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 212), and "میانی[OCR?] ویران ہوئی یہ قبرستان بنا", Miani was ruined and this graveyard came to be (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 213). A passage in the same section speaks of Shaikh Tahir's grave and of very many graves, but its words are too damaged to say what it tells of how the graveyard grew (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 212).

Chishti also records the gravediggers, the *gorkans*, who served the graveyard and, with the *sajjada nashin*, kept the saint's *urs*. He lists the *gorkans* of his day as Sayyid Mansur, the *sajjada nashin*, gave them to him (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 209), and gives two statements of how their income was shared: one in which part of it reaches the *sajjada nashin* (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 209), and one in which "جو مردہ آتا ہی تو مالک میت حق گورکنی انکو دیتا ہے اور علاوہ اسکے سجادہ نشین کو بھی کچھ حتی المقدور دیتا ہی", the family of the dead pays the gravediggers their due and gives the *sajjada nashin* something besides, as they can, the *sajjada nashin* having no share in the gravediggers' income (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 213).

## Where the Sources Disagree

- **The day and year of his death.** The survey gives 8 Muharram 1040 AH, 8 August 1630 CE. Lahori gives Thursday, at the forenoon hour, 8 Muharram 1040 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47), and his editor 1040 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1). Chishti gives Thursday the sixth[OCR?] of Muharram, 1041[OCR?] (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187), and returns to the date later in words largely illegible in print (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 216). Kanhaiya Lal gives 1040[OCR?] AH, with no month or day (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88; p. 292, folio 295).
- **His age at death.** The survey gives 54, consistent with its birth year of 1576. Lahori gives fifty-six (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47). Chishti's figure cannot be read with confidence in print (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 188).
- **Where he came from.** The survey has him born in Lahore. Kanhaiya Lal has him come from the city of Sirhind (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88); Lahori has him come to Lahore at the Mujaddid's direction (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47).
- **Whose time he belongs to.** Kanhaiya Lal has him settle at the site in the time of Jahangir (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88) and elsewhere places him in the Shah Jahan era (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295); Chishti places his death in Shah Jahan's reign (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 188).
- **His master, and who named him Bandagi.** The survey names Hazrat Shah Sikandar as his master and as the giver of the name Tahir Bandagi. Lahori makes him *murid* and *khalifa* of Shah Sikandar, son of Pir Kamal Kaithali (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47). Chishti credits the title to Hazrat Lal Kamal Shah[OCR?] of Kaithal (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 189). Kanhaiya Lal makes him *murid* of Shah Kamal Kaithali[OCR?] (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).
- **His Sufi order.** The site names him Qadri, and its survey bibliography names the shrine Qadri Naqshbandi Mujaddidi. Lahori names him four ways: Qadiri in the contents and in a later entry (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 15, folio 3; p. 279, folio 267); "قادری و نقشبندی" in the heading of his own entry (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47); "شیخ محمد طاہر قادری نقشبندی" in one appendix row (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 288, folio 276); and "حضرت طاہر بندگی قادری" in another (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 290, folio 278); his editor makes him a *khalifa* of the Mujaddid (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1). Kanhaiya Lal names him "شیخ محمد طاہر قادری و نقشبندی" in one place (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88), and in another a Qadiri *murid* of Shah Kamal and a Mujaddidi *khadim* of Shaikh Ahmad (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295). Chishti has him take *bai'at* in the Qadiri, Naqshbandi and Suhrawardi orders (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 215).
- **The name Bandagi.** The survey, Chishti and Lahori's editor use it (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 186; Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 129, folio 117, حاشیہ 1). Lahori's own entry heading and contents omit it (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 15, folio 3; p. 59, folio 47); the book's persons index gives the full form "محمد طاہر بندگی لاہوری" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 328, folio 316), while another reading of the same index lists four Tahirs, none of them Bandagi (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 321). In Kanhaiya Lal's first account the name appears only in the contents (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88; p. 4, folio 3); in his second it appears in the text, printed "شیخ ظاہر بندگی [OCR?]" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).
- **His wives.** The survey names Mah-e-Khanam and Ismatullah, the latter the daughter of an Afghan king. Chishti records the graves of "ماہ خاتم[OCR?] دختر مرزا امان اللہ[OCR?]" and "عصمت النسا[OCR?] دختر سید شہ[OCR?]", Mah Khatam, daughter of Mirza Amanullah, and Ismat al-Nisa, daughter of a Sayyid (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 189).
- **His fall and return.** The survey says he became enamored of a Hindu woman and stepped back from his religious work, that his well-wishers appealed to the Mujaddid, and that he then became the Mujaddid's disciple and later taught his children. Chishti sets the episode at Sirhind, has him put on the sacred thread and sit in a temple as a Hindu after the Mujaddid had foretold it, has the Mujaddid's sons, already his pupils, protest, and has the Mujaddid's prayer bring him back (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 187).
- **The promise of Paradise.** The survey has the promise made to him by the Prophet in a vision during Taraweeh prayers, to whoever looked upon him, learned from him or was buried near him. Lahori has the saint himself say that he had asked of God that whoever was buried within the enclosure of his *mazar* should be of Paradise (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 59, folio 47).
- **The raid on the books.** The survey places it in his lifetime: Sikh raiders, looking for gold, found only books, threw them back into the locality and left. Kanhaiya Lal places it after his death, at the end of Muslim rule: plunderers looted thousands of Qur'ans and books, tried to sell them, threw them outside the village and burned the quarter (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 85–86, folios 88–89).
- **How the graveyard began.** The survey says the graveyard grew up around his tomb as generation after generation asked to be buried near him. Kanhaiya Lal says the people of Lahore made the place a graveyard after the quarter was ruined (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 86, folio 89). Chishti writes that Miani was ruined and the graveyard came to be (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 213).
- **Where he lived.** The survey and Kanhaiya Lal set his mosque, madrasa and settlement on the ground of the graveyard (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88). Chishti, in a damaged passage, places his residence inside the walled city (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 191).
- **Why the place is called Miani Panj Pir.** Kanhaiya Lal derives Panj Pir Dhera from five graves that stood there before the village (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 291–292, folios 294–295); Chishti from the coffins of five elders brought there by a Pir and buried (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 191).
- **The date of the urs.** The survey gives the 7th, 8th and 9th of Muharram. Chishti places the *urs* within the first ten days of Muharram, on a date whose printed word is uncertain (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 216).
- **The custodians' line.** Lahori names Sayyid Shahsawar as *sajjada nashin* at the end of a Gilani genealogy (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 274, folio 262); his editor declares that genealogy incorrect, gives another, and names Pir Sher Husain Darsi[OCR?] Tahiri as *sajjada nashin* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 274, folio 262, حاشیہ ۲). Chishti names Sayyid Mansur (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 209) and a Sayyid Shah Sawar[OCR?] (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 649), and records that the office was disputed (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 215–216).
- **The chronogram word.** Kanhaiya Lal derives the year of death from the word غم in one place (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 85, folio 88) and from the word printed عم[OCR?] in another (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).

## Legacy

Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri's tomb is the oldest seat of the Miani Sahib Graveyard, and the books show it serving as the point by which other graves in the graveyard were found. Kanhaiya Lal writes "اس بزرگ کی بزرگی کے سب مسلمان قائل ہین اور بڑا خاندان اسکے سلسلہ کا قصبہ بٹالہ مین ہے جنکے ہزارون مرید پنجاب کے علاقہ مین ہین", all Muslims acknowledge this elder's greatness, and the great family of his silsila is at the town of Batala, with thousands of *murids* across the Punjab (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295). A Chishti and a Naqshbandi-Suhrawardi saint lie beside him (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 288, folio 276; p. 290, folio 278).

Lahori's entry on him closes with a list of further sources, among them the *Zubdat al-Maqamat*, the *Hazarat al-Quds*, the *Rauzat al-Qayyumiya* and the *Tahqiqat-e Chishti* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 60, folio 48), and Kanhaiya Lal points his readers to the *Khazinat al-Asfiya*, the *Hadiqat al-Awliya* and the *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya* for the many miracles told of him (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295).
