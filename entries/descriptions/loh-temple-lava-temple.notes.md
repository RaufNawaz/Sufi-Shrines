## The short version

This is a thin row. Only one book names the temple: Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 71 (l. 1159–1168). Everything else is about the founder figure (Lo / Lohu / Luv / Lava) or about Lahore and its Fort. The Overview says so in its third paragraph, and the Setting section opens by saying its material is about the place, not the temple. The length comes mainly from the founding legend in five books and from framed Fort and Alamgiri Gate context.

## Current Description: kept

Every claim in the current text is kept. Most of it stays almost word for word, spread across Overview, Lava, The Temple and Legacy. That covers the location, the Lavapuri and Kusha/Kasur tradition, MacLagan's early-19th-century date and the 1799 link, the hexagonal plan, the lost dome, the absence of carvings and frescoes, 20th-century neglect, the 2024–26 restoration (WCLA, Aga Khan Cultural Service–Pakistan, US Ambassadors Fund), the reopening on 27 January 2026, the absence of regular congregational worship, the Dawn 2018 visit ("we can only speculate"), WCLA's "the temple of Raja Loh," and the WCLA framing of cross-cultural heritage. ## Disagreements listed (4)

1. The founder's name: Lava/Luv/Loh (current and WCLA), Luv (Khalid), Lava (Schaflechner), Lo (Kanhaiya Lal, Bhandari, Chishti p. 661), Lohu (Chishti p. 795).
2. The number of sons and who founded Kasur: twins (current, Khalid); Lo and Kasho (Kanhaiya Lal); three sons (Chishti p. 661) against Kushu and Lohu (Chishti p. 795).
3. The city's first name: Lavapuri (current); Lopur, the rival لوہار پور and the Lohar Chand account (Kanhaiya Lal); the Lahur and Lahawar spellings (Bhandari).
4. The building's age: early 19th century (current, after MacLagan); Mughal era, with the original built by Luv (Khalid, as a belief); unknown (Dawn 2018).

## English I supplied for unglossed non-English prose (please check)

- "صدہا بلکہ ہزار ہا سال": "hundreds and indeed thousands of years".
- "اُس نے پنجاب کا علاقہ … لوہار چند کی جاگیر میں دیا" and the following clause: "giving the region of the Punjab in jagir to a brother, Lohar Chand …, who, once his hold on the Punjab was complete, made this city his capital". Whose hold (اُس) is meant is ambiguous in the source.
- "تحفۃ الواصلین ایک وضعی تذکرہ": "a spurious *tazkira*".
- "کہتے ہیں": "they say". "واللہ اعلم": "God knows best". "قصور آباد کیا": "founded Kasur". "لوہو نی شہر لاہور آباد کیا": "Lohu founded the city of Lahore".
- "دربعضی تواریخ لهور و لهاور نیز مینویسند": "in some histories it is also written Lahur and Lahawar".
- "قلعه متین شهر پناه و دولتخانه": "a strong fort, a city wall and a palace". "عمارت عالیه که بالفعل موجود است": "the lofty building still standing in Bhandari's day".
- "دار الحکومت این ولایت شهر سیالکوت گردید": "the seat of government of the region passed to Sialkot".
- "در وقت عبور از دریاے راوی پایان قلعہ لاہور": "at the crossing of the Ravi below the Fort of Lahore". The entry paraphrases this without glossing it.
- "خسرو ملک تاب نیاورده درقلعه لاهور متحصن گردید": "Khusraw Malik shut himself up in the Fort of Lahore".
- "بعد محاصرہ قلعہ لاہور و معرکہ عظیم": "after the siege of the Fort of Lahore and a great battle".
- "روبروی تخت واقع قلعہ لاہور جنوب رویہ": "facing the throne on the south side of the Fort".
- "لاہور مین آکر قابض ہو": "to come to Lahore and take possession of it".
- "سلطنت کا خزانہ": "the treasury of the state". "علیحدہ مکان میں": "in a separate building".
- "شاهزاده هر چند سعی کرد قلعه لاهور بدست نیامد" and "راجه بهگونت داس صوبه دار لاهور": quoted, and described in the entry's own words ("held it firm" is the entry's paraphrase).
