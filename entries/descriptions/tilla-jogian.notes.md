## Disagreements listed (Where the Sources Disagree)

1. Who initiated Ranjha: the current text and Usborne say Bal Nath (line 121); Shackle's glossary says
   Gorakhnath (line 48); Bulleh Shah's lyric says "the guru" (line 52).
2. Gorakhnath's date: the current text gives tradition's two thousand years and scholarship's
   c. 11th–12th century; Dhillon gives the eleventh century, and the Puratan Janamsakhis set Gorakhnath
   in conversation with Nanak (line 626).
3. Gorakhnath's role: the current text says "organiser"; Boivin says "founded" and "avatar of Shiva"
   (lines 394, 398); Shackle says "head of the Kanphat yogis" (line 47).
4. The name and the location: the current text has Tilla Jogian on the highest peak of the eastern Salt
   Range; Shackle has "high up in the hills of District Jhelum" (line 42); Usborne's annotator has 'Tilla
   Guruan' "on the banks of the river Jhelum" (lines 120–122); Bhandari has تله[OCR?], variant بله[OCR?],
   on the salt mountain seven kos from Rohtas (line 726).
5. The ear-ring: the current text has "great rings"; Shackle has "a wooden ring" (line 44).
6. The hill since 1947: the current text says abandoned and ruined; Usborne's annotator writes, in the
   present tense, that the place "is still preserved" and the Jog Mat "still practised" (lines 123–126).
   The annotator's note carries no date in the entry, so I did not give one.
