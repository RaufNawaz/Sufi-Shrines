## The shape of this row

Only one book has row-level material: Boivin's Jinda Pir passage (lines 60–92), which its own Doubt
(87–92) calls the weakest identification in its batch (for: name + city + riverbank; against: spelling
Jinda/Jind, never called a gurdwara, Guru Nanak never named). Everything else is person-level (Guru
Nanak), place-level (Sukkur, Rohri) or province-level (sindh). So the article:

- says in the second Overview paragraph that no book names a Gurdwara Pehli Patshahi or ties Nanak to a
  Jind Pir shrine;
- gives Boivin's darbar as a **hedged pointer** in its own section "A Darbar Called Jinda Pir", stating
  that the book never calls it a gurdwara or names Nanak, and that the identification is only a
  possibility. **Decision for Rauf:** keep it as a pointer (as drafted), or drop it to the notes only.
  It is notable that Boivin independently matches the current text's gazetteer account (late 19th c.,
  Hindu shrine to Jinda Pir on the Sukkur bank), so I judged it worth a reader's knowing.
- frames all Nanak, Sukkur, Rohri and Sindh material as about the figure/place (explicit framing
  sentences open "The Life of Guru Nanak", "Legends of the Guru", "In Verse", both Setting sections).

## Disagreements listed (11)

1. What Jind/Jinda Pir is: Sikh gurdwara (site) vs darbar of a Hindu Khwaja Khizr cult (Boivin).
2. When it began: Nanak's journey (site) vs last decade of 19th c. (Boivin; and the current text's own
   gazetteer account).
3. Why Hindus left the older shrine: "abandoned their claim" (1919 Gazetteer via current text) vs
   "expelled" (Boivin).
4. The name: Jind Pir / Zinda Pir / Jinda Pir; Boivin's two index headwords; Zinda Pir = Khizr (p. 54)
   vs caption name for Jhulelal (p. 156).
5. Which udasi: third (site) vs fourth (Dhillon) vs late, from Kartarpur (Khalid).
6. Number/length of journeys: five (Puratan) / four (modern; Dhillon) ; 23 vs 24 years.
7. Are Sindh's Nanakpanthis Sikhs: current text vs Boivin vs Khalid vs Schaflechner census.
8. Guru's dates: Dhillon / Khalid / Bhandari (Vikrami and Hijri; Salim Shah's reign). No conversion made.
9. Wife and sons: Dhillon (Batala; Lakhmi Das) / Khalid reconstruction (Pakhoke; Lachman Das) /
   Bhandari (one son, Lakhmidas).
10. Miracles: Bhandari vs Dhillon vs Khalid.
11. Mecca story: Dhillon (words) vs legend Khalid reports (Kaaba turned).

## Worker-written English for unglossed or partly glossed Persian (Bhandari)

All glosses after these Persian quotations are mine; the entry gives only a summary or none:
- p. 519 "خلاصه عبادت اینطایفه…" → "the sum of this group's worship is the reading of their master's
  verses, which they recite in song and melody".
- p. 519 "عبادت عظمی" → "the greatest worship".
- p. 519 "از مریدان و معتقدان…مستجاب الدعوات می باشند" → "of the disciples and devotees … whose prayers
  are answered".
- p. 519 "با دوستان یکرنگ و با دشمنان بی جنگ" → "of one colour with friends and without war with enemies".
- p. 519 "اعتقادی که این فریق…" → "the faith this group has in its leader is seldom found among other
  peoples".
- p. 520 birth sentence → "in the year one thousand five hundred and twenty-six of Bikramajit,
  corresponding to eight hundred and eighty of the Hijra, in the time of Sultan Bahlul Lodi".
- p. 520 "علامات کشف و کرامات و ایات خارق عادات" → "the signs of unveiling and of miracles and wonders
  beyond the ordinary"; "درخورد سالگی" rendered "in his early years".
- p. 520 "دردهی از دیهات بتاله برآب دریاے راوی" → "in one of the villages of Batala on the water of the Ravi".
- p. 520 "دوازده کروهے بتاله…" → "twelve kos from Batala, on the bank of the Ravi, is the place of Baba
  Nanak, where his descendants live to this day".
- p. 520 "عالم عالم خلایق…" → "people came in their multitudes from every land and became his disciples";
  "در دام عقیدت" → "into the snare of devotion".
- p. 520 "بعبارات واضحه و استعارات لایحه" → "in plain words and shining metaphors".
- p. 520 death sentence → "in the reign of Salim Shah Afghan, at an age between seventy and eighty".
- p. 520 "لکهمیداس نام خلف رشید" glossed only as "the son passed over".
- p. 567/566 "چهارمین صنف اوداسی از معتقدان بابا نانک" → "the fourth class, the Udasis, of the devotees of
  Baba Nanak". Cited p. 567 (the entry puts the section at p. 567 and the sentence-run at p. 566;
  **Rauf:** check which page carries this exact sentence).
- p. 164 "اشعار هندی تصنیف بابا نانک که در معرفت حق گفته" → "Hindi verses composed by Baba Nanak on the
