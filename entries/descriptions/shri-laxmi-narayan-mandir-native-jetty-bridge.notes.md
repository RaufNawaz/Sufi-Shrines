## The shape of the evidence

**One book, one sentence, and a hedged identification.** Boivin p. 192, folio 179 (lines 36–53):
"In Karachi, for example, a painting of Ramdeo Pir stands in the temple of Lakhshmi Narayan." The
entry's own doubt (49–53) says the mapping rests on deity name plus city alone ("If the book means
some other Karachi temple of that name, the material moves to 'Figures and sites not in the
archive'"). The Overview says this in plain words, and the sentence is used in Overview, "The
Painting of Ramdeo Pir", and Legacy, each time hedged in the Overview's framing. Everything else is
person-level (Laxmi: Khalid x2, 522–524, 555–558) or place/province-level, framed as context.

The current Description is kept in full and verbatim (its five sections, dashes included). I added
one paragraph after "The Temple by the Creek" and one after "Under Threat" saying what the books do
and do not record. Its bibliography is kept verbatim and first.

## DECISION FOR RAUF (most important)

- **Khalid's Port Grand temple** (*Walking with Nanak*, pp. 224–225, folios 198–199; line 534). Khalid
  describes a temple inside the Port Grand complex with stairs into polluted water, fishermen's boats,
  rooms for different deities, a Jhule Lal mural, and a new mosque built on top of it. The entry says
  "None of the temples below is identified with an archive row" (530), and the book does not name its
  deity, so the draft uses it only under Setting ("Temples of Karachi"), says the book identifies none
  of them with this one, and adds "The book does not name the deity of that temple". If you know from
  outside the books that this is the Laxmi Narayan Mandir (the draft cannot say so), this is the
  richest description any book gives of the site, and the material could move up into the article.
- Should the Boivin sentence be presented as firmly as it is (it is the only site evidence)? I kept
  the hedge; you may prefer it firmer or softer.
