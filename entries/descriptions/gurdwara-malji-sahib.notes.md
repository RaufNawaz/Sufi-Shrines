## The shape of this row

Two books name the site itself, both by Haroon Khalid:
- *Walking with Nanak* (row section, lines 289–298): the cobra legend and its place with Kiari Sahib
  "the furthest from Gurdwara Janamsthan" (p. 91, folio 73); the Doubt (line 298) that the plate of
  "Gurdwara Malji Sahib at Kanganpur" (p. 169) is a different site.
- *A White Trail* (lines 457–464): "Mal Ji Sahib", about 500 m from Janamasthan, near the railway
  station (p. 191); "where he encountered a snake" (p. 222). The Doubt (line 464) on the spelling join
  "Mal Ji Sahib" = this row is accepted as the entry gives it.

Everything else is Guru Nanak person-level, Nankana Sahib / Talwandi place-level, or province context. The
Overview says early that only Khalid's two books (plus the 1962 register from the current text) name the
gurdwara, and every passage from the other books is framed as being about the Guru or the town
("The Boyhood of Guru Nanak at Talwandi", "Stories of His Birth", "The Life of the Guru", "Setting:
Nankana Sahib", "The Sikhs of Nankana Sahib", "Guru Nanak in the Wider Record").

## Current Description

Every claim is kept (lines 5, 7): the name "Gurdwara Sri Mal Ji Sahib"; location near the railway station
and Kiara Sahib; the *mal* tree also called *van* or *jal*; the *sakhi* with Rai Bular, his attendants,
the cobra withdrawing; the link with Kiara Sahib's untouched crop; Rai Bular's devotion and the gift of
half his estate; Diwan Kaura Mall's building and the Ranjit Singh renovation; dome, kiosks, porches,
4-inch cobra tiles; maintenance since Partition and pilgrims at the great anniversaries; the "eastern
cluster" with Kiara Sahib; the 1962 register's distinction from Kanganpur and its fixed-shadow version.
The register paragraph is rewritten (its phrase "the tile-and-architecture description above" referred to
the old layout). No current sentence carries an inline citation, as before. The one bibliography item is
kept verbatim and first.

None of the books confirms Diwan Kaura Mall, the Ranjit Singh renovation of this gurdwara, the tiles, the
fixed shadow, the Kiara Sahib link or the gift of half the estate; these stand as the site's account.

## Worker-written English for unglossed Persian prose (Bhandari, all p. 520 unless noted)

- "علامات کشف و کرامات و ایات خارق عادات از و بمنصه ظهور رسید" → "signs of inner vision and of wonders
  beyond the ordinary appeared in him"; "درخورد سالگی" → "in childhood"; "ده سالگی" → "at the age of ten".
- "قافله سالار مسالک حقیقت و مشعله دار طریق طریقت" → "the leader of the caravan on the roads of truth and
  the torch-bearer of the mystic way".
- "بعبارات واضحه و استعارات لایحه" → "in clear words and shining metaphors"; "اشعار هندی" → "verses in the
  Hindi tongue".
- "عالم عالم خلایق از اطراف ممالک آمده مرید شدند" → "crowds upon crowds came from every land to become his
  disciples"; "در دام عقیدت" → "into the snare of devotion".
- "دوازده کروهے بتاله … سکونت دارد" → "twelve kos from Batala on the bank of the Ravi … where his
  descendants live to this day".
- "چون دولت معنوی نصیب او نبود" → "since spiritual fortune was not his lot"; "دروقت نزع" → "at the hour of
  death"; "دردهی از دیهات بتاله برآب دریاے راوی" → "in one of the villages of Batala on the Ravi".
- Birth date line → "in the Bikrami year 1526, corresponding to 880 Hijri, in the time of Sultan Bahlul
  Lodi"; death line → "in the reign of Salim Shah Afghan, at an age between seventy and eighty".
- p. 519: "از مریدان و معتقدان … می باشند" → "many of his disciples and devotees … are men of spiritual
  states and austerities whose prayers are answered"; "خلاصه عبادت …" → "the sum of their worship is their
  master's verses, which they sing"; "با دوستان یکرنگ و با دشمنان بی جنگ" → "of one colour with their
  friends and without war with their enemies"; "اعتقادی که …" → "the faith these people have in their leader
  is seldom found among others"; "عبادت عظمی" → "the greatest worship".
- p. 76: "سجاده نشین بابا نانک" → "the holder of Baba Nanak's seat"; p. 164: "اشعار هندی تصنیف بابا نانک که
  در معرفت حق گفته" → "Hindi verses Baba Nanak composed on the knowledge of God".
