## The shape of this row

No book names Gurdwara Choa Sahib. One book has row material: Khalid, *Walking with Nanak* (lines 285–296),
whose gurdwara "in Jhelum, [near] Rohtas Fort" is never named and is mapped on place (Doubt, line 296).
Bhandari's *Khulasat al-Tawarikh* (lines 546–566) has Rohtas Fort as a place-level toponym only; the entry
says the book never names Nanak, a gurdwara or a spring at Rohtas (line 550). Rizvi names no Rohtas, Dina
or Jhelum district (lines 19–20). Everything else is Guru Nanak person-level, Jhelum place-level or Punjab
province-level. The Overview says early that no book names the site and that only Khalid describes a
gurdwara at Rohtas; the Rohtas Fort, Setting and Wider Record sections each open by saying they are about
the fort, the place or the Guru, not the gurdwara.

## Disagreements listed (4)

1. Who built Rohtas Fort: the site (Sher Shah Suri) against Bhandari (Sher Khan founded, Islam Shah completed,
   p. 268; both raised it, p. 239).
2. What the gurdwara is called: the site (Choa Sahib) against Khalid (unnamed, "Nanak's Gurdwara in Jhelum,
   [near] Rohtas Fort", p. 248).
3. Which journey: the site (fourth *udasi*) against Dhillon's two schemes (fourth in 1519–1521 or
   1518–1521; return route names Tilla Balnath and Katasraj, not Rohtas); Khalid's 24 years against
   Dhillon's 23.
4. The Guru's dates: site/Khalid/Dhillon 1469–1539 against Bhandari (Samvat 1526 / 880 AH; death under
   Salim Shah, aged 70–80).

Not listed as disagreements, because they do not contradict: Khalid's undated visit finding "an abandoned
structure" against the 2019 reopening (the draft says Khalid gives no year); Khalid's "there was no Rohtas
fort" when Nanak stayed, which agrees with the legend that the spring came before Sher Shah's fort.
