## Overview

In the Muslim Town locality near Ichhra, within a graveyard that still bears his name and lends it to a surrounding housing scheme, lies the tomb of Hazrat Baba Shah Kamal, remembered chiefly as the elder brother of the celebrated Sufi Baba Shah Jamal, whose shrine stands nearby. Far less is independently documented about Shah Kamal than about his more famous sibling. The shrine sits within the historic Shah Kamal graveyard, still in active use as a burial ground, and continues to draw local devotees to weekly Thursday evening gatherings and to an annual *ʿurs*.

Three of the older books on Lahore and its saints speak of him, each in its own way. Maulvi Nur Ahmad Chishti's *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, written as an account of the buildings, mazars, tombs and mosques of Lahore's environs (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 28), gives a section to the tomb of Shah Kamal, places it "متصل خانقاہ شاہ جمال صاحب", adjoining the khanqah of Shah Jamal, describes its fabric as he found it, and names the man who swept it and lit its lamps (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661). Kanhaiya Lal's history of Lahore describes a tomb of a Suhrawardi saint named Shah Kamal near Ichhra, three miles south of the city, and gives a year for his death; the book's account is matched to this shrine on its location, and is given below as that book's account (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). Mufti Ghulam Sarwar Lahori's *Hadiqat al-Awliya* knows a Shah Kamal as the brother of Shah Jamal of Lahore, but gives him no grave, no date and no place (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 185, folio 183 as printed); whether that brother is the saint of this tomb the book does not say.

These accounts differ on the saint's order, on his dates, on the distance of the tomb from the city and on the state of its fabric. Each is set out below as its source gives it, and the differences are gathered under "Where the Sources Disagree".

## The Life of the Saint

According to the account the shrine keeps, the father of Shah Kamal and Shah Jamal was Syed Ahmad Shah, a descendant of Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari of Uch Sharif, who personally instructed both sons in Islamic theology before the brothers settled together in the Ichhra area of Lahore. Local devotional tradition attaches to Shah Kamal the composite title "Qadiri Suhrawardi," suggesting an affiliation with the same overlapping Sufi orders associated with his brother's shrine nearby, and his likely lifetime is generally placed in the same late-sixteenth to seventeenth-century Mughal-era window as Shah Jamal's own.

The books know the two brothers together. Chishti names them as a pair, "ایک شاہ جمال دوسرے شاہ کمال", the one Shah Jamal and the other Shah Kamal (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 690). Lahori speaks of Shah Kamal as Shah Jamal's second brother: devout, a man of *jazb*, a Sayyid Husaini of a Suhrawardi family from Baha al-Din Zakariya. He gives Shah Kamal no grave, no date, no location and no spiritual chain (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 185, folio 183 as printed). The book's index lists him as "شاہ کمال (برادر شاہ جمال لاہوری)", Shah Kamal, brother of Shah Jamal Lahori; that index line is a gloss, and no text is quoted with it (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 326, folio 314). The *Hadiqat al-Awliya* is cited here for its own claim about Shah Jamal's brother; nothing in it ties that brother to this tomb.

Kanhaiya Lal's account of the Shah Kamal buried near Ichhra makes him a Sayyid, famous in the Suhrawardi family as a possessor of *karamat* and *khawariq*, marvels: "یہ بزرگ سید تہا اور سہروردی خاندان مین صاحب کرامت و خوارق مشہور تہا" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). It names no order beyond the Suhrawardi family, and no pir, murshid or genealogy (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). For his death it gives a year and a reign in a single clause: "سنہ ۱۰۸۰[OCR?] ہجری عہد شاہ جہانی مین فوت ہو کر یہان دفنایا گیا", he died in 1080[OCR?] AH, in the era of Shah Jahan, and was buried here. The reading of the year is uncertain in the printed text, and the book prints the year and the reign together without reconciling them (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316).

Chishti's book carries two further passages that name a Shah Kamal without making clear who is meant. In one, a mother is said to be the daughter of a "حضرت سید آنگ[OCR?] شاہ کمال", in a passage too damaged in the printed text to say whom it concerns (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 368). In the spiritual chain of Badhu Shah the name appears bare: "...شاہ سکندر خادم حضرت شاہ کمال شاہ کمال خادم حضرت فضیل شاہ..." (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 775). Neither passage says which Shah Kamal it means.

## Other Saints of the Name

The books know several men called Kamal, and keep them apart from the brother of Shah Jamal. Lahori's *Hadiqat al-Awliya* has a Shah Kamal of Kaithal, "شاہ کمال کیتھلی", in the Qadiri chain of the Mujaddid, with no grave, no date and no connection to Lahore (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 129, folio 117); its index lists him on a separate line from the brother of Shah Jamal, and lists separately a Kamal Bukhari called Pir Jahaniyan and a Sayyid Kamal (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 326, folio 314). The same book's appendix names a "سید کامل شاہ لاہوری" at mauza Babu Sabu, a different name, Kamil rather than Kamal (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 287, folio 275).

Kanhaiya Lal names a "شاہ کمال کیتہلی [OCR?]" only as the Qadiri pir of Shaikh Tahir; whether he is the same man as the Suhrawardi Shah Kamal buried near Ichhra the book does not settle (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295). The contents of the same book list a "مزار سید کمال[OCR?]", the tomb of a *Sayyid* Kamal, with no quarter, order or date (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 4, folio 3). Chishti records a "سید کمال شاہ چشتی" outside the Kashmiri Gate, with a year of death printed as "سمنت ۱۹۰۰[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 339). None of these is the saint of this tomb in the books that name them.

## The Shrine

The shrine sits within the historic Shah Kamal graveyard, still in active use as a burial ground. The site is formally catalogued as a monument of the historic period by the Punjab Department of Archaeology and Museums. Its location is independently confirmed by two directly consulted sources: the Punjab Auqaf & Religious Affairs Department's own shrine listing includes, under its Lahore Zone, an entry titled "Darbar & Jamia Masjid Hazrat Shah Kamal," while the Chughtai Library's digital catalogue of Lahore's historic Mughal-period monuments gives the shrine's precise address as Karnalpura, Muslim Town, Combo Colony, Lahore, and records it as owned by the Auqaf Department, its condition rated "Satisfactory," within a mosque-and-graveyard complex.

Chishti's section on the tomb, headed "حال [illegible] شاہ کمال رحمۃ اللہ علیہ", places it "جنوب روبہ[OCR?] موضع راوان", by mauza Rawan, "لاہور سی دو کوس[OCR?]", two *kos* from Lahore, the reading of the distance uncertain in the printed text, and "متصل خانقاہ شاہ جمال صاحب", adjoining the khanqah of Shah Jamal (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661). He describes a high enclosure wall, "چار دیواری بلند", partly fallen; an eight-sided platform, "چبوترہ ہشت پہلو", of a height he gives as "ڈیڑہ[OCR?] گز", a *gaz* and a half, the reading uncertain in the printed text; and an octagonal dome (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661). Of the doors he writes: "چار دروازہ تہی[OCR?] اب تین بند ایک جنوبی کشادہ", there were four doors, now three are closed and one, the southern, is open (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661). The grave itself, "مزار حضرت شاہ کمال صاحب", was broken in places and finished in "چونہ گچ", lime plaster; a *kikar* stood to the north, and to the west and south what the printed text gives as "گوندی[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661).

Kanhaiya Lal places the tomb of his Shah Kamal "موضع اچہرا اور رانوان کے قریب لاہور بفاصلہ تین میل بجانب جنوب", near mauza Ichhra and Ranwan, three miles south of Lahore (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). He records who built it: "مقبرہ اسکے خاندان کے مریدون نے بنوایا", the tomb was built by the *murids* of his family, not by a ruler or a state (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). He describes a grand *pakka* brick tomb standing on an octagonal *pakka* platform; in its middle the dome, also octagonal, with an arched opening, "محرابی دہین[OCR?]", in each side; and inside, a second *pakka* brick platform on which Shah Kamal's grave is built *pakka* (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). Like Chishti he counts four doors on four sides, "جنہین سے اب تین بند کر دئے گئے میں اور ایک جنوبی دروازہ کہلا ہے", of which three have now been closed and one, the southern door, is open (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316).

Of the tomb's condition Kanhaiya Lal writes: "پہلے یہ مکان بہت آباد و تہا و چار دیواری بہت بلند و پختہ بنی ہوئی تہی مگر اب گر گئی ہی", the place was formerly much frequented, and its enclosure wall was very high and *pakka*, but it has now fallen; the tomb itself "ابتک بہیج[OCR?] و سالم ہو اگر چہ مرمت طلب ہی", still sound and whole though in need of repair, the first word uncertain in the printed text (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). He gives a reason for its survival: "اگر شہر کی عمارت کا مقبرہ ہوتا تو گرا لیا جانا", had it been a tomb among the city's buildings, it would have been pulled down (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). In a history that records demolition after demolition in the city, the remark makes the tomb's distance from Lahore the cause of its standing.

The keepers of the tomb appear only in Chishti. He records that for a long time a man named Nihal, known as Nihal Shah, had swept the tomb and lit its lamps: "عرصہ دراز سی مسمی نہال المعروف نہال شاہ [illegible] جاروب کشی اور چراغ روشن کرتا ہی" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661). Kanhaiya Lal records no *ʿurs*, fair, offering, keeper, endowment or land at the tomb (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). Today, as the Chughtai Library's catalogue records, the shrine is owned by the Auqaf Department.

## Devotion and Practice

The shrine continues to attract local devotees for weekly Thursday evening gatherings involving dhol drumming and *qawwali*, echoing in more modest form the famous *dhamal* tradition at his brother's shrine. An annual *ʿurs* is held in his honour, drawing pilgrims from the surrounding neighbourhoods, though the precise calendar date is not widely documented, and none of the books consulted here records it.

Of the saint's wonders the books say little. Kanhaiya Lal calls him famous as "صاحب کرامت و خوارق", a possessor of marvels, but narrates no incident and names no witness (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316).

## A Legend of Lahore's Founding

Chishti's section on the tomb of Shah Kamal opens with a legend of the founding of the cities round about, introduced with "کہتی[OCR?] ہین", it is said. Raja Ram Chand had three sons: "ایک لو[OCR?] نے [illegible] لاہور بنایا", one, Lo[OCR?], built Lahore; a second, whose name is lost in the printed text, "قصور[OCR?] آباد کیا", settled Qasur[OCR?]; and the third, "مسمی راو[OCR?]", founded mauza Rawan, the village by which the tomb stands. Chishti closes the story with "واللہ اعلم", God knows best (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661). The legend is about the place, not about the saint. In his dynastic history Chishti tells it another way: Raja Ramchandar had two sons, Kushu and Lohu, and "لوہو نی شہر لاہور آباد کیا", Lohu settled the city of Lahore (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 795).

## Setting: Ichhra

The books' passages on Ichhra speak of the village and its neighbourhood, not of this shrine. Chishti relates that Mian Natha told Mian Mir he had once sat in seclusion in "موضع اچہرہ[OCR?]", and left because all the date palms there, "تمام درختان خرما[OCR?]", recited the *tasbih* "سبحان اللہ و الحمد للہ", which disturbed his concentration (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 287).

Sujan Rai Bhandari's *Khulasat al-Tawarikh* knows Ichhra as a camping ground by Lahore. Khalilullah Khan and Jai Singh, ordered not to enter Lahore, marched on 11 Dhu al-Hijja and halted "اچهره[OCR?] ان طرف لاهور", resting a day to put the army in order (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 67). On a return the emperor camped "بست و چهارم محرم در ساحت اچهره[OCR?] نزدیکی لاهور", on the twenty-fourth of Muharram on the ground of Ichhra near Lahore, received there the amirs who had been in Lahore, and on the twenty-fifth rode from Ichhra toward the city (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 65).

Of the brother's tradition in the present-day city, Scott Kugle writes that in Lahore today the Qalandars are still associated with the great Qadiri saints of the city, among them Shah Hussayn, Shah Jamal and Miyan Mir (Kugle, *Sufis and Saints' Bodies*, p. 326, folio 305, ch. 4 n. 7). He says nothing of Shah Kamal.

## Where the Sources Disagree

- **His order.** Local devotional tradition, as the site's account gives it, calls him "Qadiri Suhrawardi". Lahori describes Shah Jamal's brother as of a Suhrawardi family (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 185, folio 183 as printed), and Kanhaiya Lal places the Shah Kamal buried near Ichhra in the Suhrawardi family, "سہروردی خاندان" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). The Shah Kamal whom both books call Qadiri is the Shah Kamal of Kaithal (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 129, folio 117; Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 292, folio 295), whom Lahori's index keeps apart from the brother of Shah Jamal (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 326, folio 314).
- **His dates.** The site's account places his lifetime in the late sixteenth to seventeenth century, the same Mughal-era window as Shah Jamal's. Kanhaiya Lal gives his death as 1080[OCR?] AH, in the era of Shah Jahan, printing the year and the reign together without reconciling them (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). Lahori gives no date for Shah Jamal's brother (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 185, folio 183 as printed).
- **Which brother.** The site's account remembers Shah Kamal as the elder brother of Shah Jamal. Lahori speaks of him as Shah Jamal's second brother (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 185, folio 183 as printed); Chishti names Shah Jamal first and Shah Kamal second, "ایک شاہ جمال دوسرے شاہ کمال" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 690).
- **His descent.** The site's account makes his father Syed Ahmad Shah, a descendant of Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari of Uch Sharif. Lahori calls Shah Jamal's brother a Sayyid Husaini of a Suhrawardi family from Baha al-Din Zakariya (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 185, folio 183 as printed); Kanhaiya Lal calls his Shah Kamal a Sayyid and names no genealogy (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316).
- **The distance from Lahore.** Chishti puts the tomb two *kos* from Lahore, the reading uncertain in the printed text (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661); Kanhaiya Lal puts it three miles south of the city (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316).
- **The neighbouring village.** Chishti names it "موضع راوان", mauza Rawan (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661); Kanhaiya Lal names Ichhra and "رانوان", Ranwan (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). The site's account places the tomb in Muslim Town near Ichhra, and the Chughtai Library's catalogue at Karnalpura, Muslim Town, Combo Colony.
- **The state of the tomb.** Chishti found the grave broken in places and the enclosure wall partly fallen (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661). Kanhaiya Lal found the enclosure wall fallen but the tomb itself sound and whole, though in need of repair (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 313, folio 316). The Chughtai Library's catalogue rates its condition "Satisfactory".
- **The founding of Lahore.** In his section on Shah Kamal's tomb Chishti gives Raja Ram Chand three sons, one of whom built Lahore and another of whom founded mauza Rawan (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 661); in his dynastic history he gives Raja Ramchandar two sons, Kushu and Lohu, and has Lohu settle Lahore (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 795).

## Legacy

Together with the Shah Jamal shrine, the tomb of Shah Kamal helps anchor the religious identity of this part of Lahore as a historic centre of Sufi devotion, reflecting the broader pattern by which entire Lahore neighbourhoods have taken their names and characters from the saints buried within them; the graveyard that holds his tomb still bears his name and lends it to the housing scheme around it. Chishti and Kanhaiya Lal found its enclosure fallen and its southern door alone left open, and Chishti found one keeper sweeping it and lighting its lamps; today it stands as a mosque-and-graveyard complex in the care of the Auqaf Department, still visited on Thursday evenings and at its annual *ʿurs*.
