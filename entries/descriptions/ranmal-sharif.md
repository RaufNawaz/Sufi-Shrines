## Overview

At Ranmal Sharif, near Phalia in the Mandi Bahauddin region of the Punjab, stands the shrine of Hazrat Syed Muhammad Noushah Ganj Bakhsh (1552–1654) — the founder of the Naushahia branch of the Qadiri Sufi order. A saint of long life and wide influence, Noushah Qadiri established a spiritual line that spread across the Punjab and beyond and endures to this day, and his darbar at Ranmal Sharif remains an important centre of pilgrimage.

None of the books consulted here names Ranmal Sharif or Phalia as the place of the saint's tomb. What they offer is the saint himself, his order and his disciples. Mufti Ghulam Sarwar Lahori's *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, a tazkira of the saints of the Punjab composed in 1875 and read here in a modern annotated Urdu edition, gives him a full entry headed "شیخ حاجی محمد قادری المشہور بحضرت نوشہ گنج بخش" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 70–72, folios 58–60), with his parents, his training, his pir, a Persian chronogram on his death and a circle of disciples; and the book's modern editor states that "آپ کا مزار شریف اس وقت ساہنپال شریف ضلع گجرات پنجاب میں ہے", that his holy mazar is at present at Sahnpal Sharif, district Gujrat, Punjab (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ). Maulvi Nur Ahmad Chishti's *Tahqiqat-e Chishti* carries a chapter headed "حال حضرت نوشہ صاحب رحمتہ اللہ علیہ" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 483), whose damaged passage on his burial names district Shahpur and Sahn Pal in district Gujrat (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 483–484). Kanhaiya Lal's history of Lahore names "نو شاہ گنج بخش" only at the head of a Naushahi spiritual pedigree, and says nothing of the site (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 304, folio 307). Everything below drawn from the books is therefore said of the saint, his order or the town, not of the shrine at Ranmal Sharif.

## The Life of the Saint

Muhammad Noushah — honoured as *Ganj Bakhsh*, “the bestower of treasure,” and *Noushah*, “the bridegroom” — was born in 1552. He took the Sufi path in the Qadiri order and, attaining a high spiritual station, founded his own branch, the *Naushahia*, which he propagated with great energy across the Punjab. He lived through a full century of the Mughal age, guiding a vast body of disciples and establishing a network of successors, and he is remembered both as a saint of powerful blessing and as an organiser of an enduring order. He died in 1654, full of years, and was buried at Ranmal Sharif, which became the seat of his line.

Lahori's book lists him in its contents as "حضرت نوشہ گنج بخش", entry no. 30 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 15, folio 3), and its persons index gives his personal name as Haji Muhammad: "نوشاہ گنج بخش (حاجی محمد)" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 332, folio 320). Elsewhere the book names him in full honorific as "حضرت حاجی محمد نوشہ گنج بخش" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 272, folio 260). Lahori calls him a wali from birth and the khalifa of Shah Sulaiman Qadri; states of *jazb* and *sukr*, of love and longing, dominated him, and he was unequalled in asceticism, piety and *karamat* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 70, folio 58).

**His parents.** Lahori names both on one page. His father, Haji Ala al-Din, was himself a great elder who performed the Hajj seven times and was honoured with a vision of the Prophet; his mother, Bibi Jiwani, was also a great worshipper and ascetic (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 70, folio 58). The book's indexes list them as "جیونی بی بی (والدہ نوشاہ گنج بخش)" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 316, folio 304) and "علاؤ الدین (والد نوشاہ گنج بخش)" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 323, folio 311). Chishti, citing a work he calls "مناقب نوشاہیہ", gives the family's old home as "موضع کہلیانوالی[OCR?]", the father as "شیخ علاء الدین" and the mother as "ملا بی جیون[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 486).

**His birth and boyhood, as Chishti tells them.** The father left three months into the pregnancy, saying "پہلوان دین ہوگا" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 486), and returned "سفر مکہ سے", from the journey to Mecca, when the child was four (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 487). "حضرت شاہ سلیمان صاحب", the "قطب وقت", foretold the birth (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 486–487) and advised the family (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 487–488). Chishti names a brother, "شیخ اسمعیل" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 488). At twelve the boy was taken for "بائیس[OCR?] برس" in age, so strong was he, and he fought in "اکثر لڑائیون" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 488–489). He then withdrew into a "لق و دق" wilderness, "دائم الصوم", and was fed with milk each night (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 489–490); he studied at "موضع حاکوثارہ[OCR?]" near "نوشہرہ" and memorised the Quran in a single night, where the printed text breaks off (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 490).

**His training and initiation, as Lahori tells them.** Up to the age of seventeen he completed the exoteric sciences; then he renounced the world and stayed in zikr in the reed-bed of "ساندل بار", Sandal Bar, "مگر والدین نے ان کو وہاں نہ چھوڑا". Coming away, he was brought by Mulla Karim al-Din to Shah Sulaiman, took *bai'at*, and in a few years reached completion (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 70–71, folios 58–59). The book's editor corrects the place: "ساندل بار کی بجائے گوندل بار ہونا چاہیے", instead of Sandal Bar it should be Gondal Bar (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 70, folio 58, حاشیہ 2).

**The founding of the order.** Lahori states it plainly: "طریقہ نوشاہیہ جس کے فقیر پنجاب میں ہزاروں ہیں۔ ان سے شروع ہوا", the Naushahia path, whose faqirs in the Punjab number in the thousands, began with him (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 70, folio 58). The same book names the order "نوشاہیہ قادریہ خاندان" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 272, folio 260). In another entry, however, it ascribes the order's rise in the Punjab to another figure (see below, under the disagreements).

**His death.** The current account gives 1654. Lahori himself gives 1103 AH, with the chronogram "پیر فیاض" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59). His modern editor rejects that year in a footnote: "حضرت نوشہ رح کا سال ولادت ۹۵۹ھ بمطابق یکم رمضان ہے" — his birth fell on 1 Ramadan 959 AH — and "آپ کا صحیح سال وفات ۱۲ ربیع الاول ۱۰۶۴ھ ہے۔ ۱۱۰۳ھ غلط ہے۔ سید شرافت نوشاہی نے تحقیق سے ثابت کیا ہے": his correct year of death is 12 Rabi al-Awwal 1064 AH, 1103 AH is wrong, as Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi has proved by research in his *Sharif al-Tawarikh* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 2). The same editor's own list of his publications gives yet another date, describing the collected verse as that of "حضرت حاجی محمد نوشہ گنج بخش قادری ف ۱۶۵۳ء", who died in 1653 CE (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 307, folio 295). Lahori also records a *bai'at* the saint accepted when he was ill and one day remained before his death, without giving the year (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 241, folio 229).

**A family wedding.** Chishti tells the story of the marriage of Hazrat Naushah's daughter, in which the bride's side demanded "ایک سو روپیہ لاگون کا", a hundred rupees of *lagan*, "حسب دستور مسلمانان پنجاب", after the custom of the Muslims of the Punjab (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 484).

## The Shrine and the Naushahia Order

The shrine of Noushah Ganj Bakhsh at Ranmal Sharif is the mother-shrine of the Naushahia Qadiri order, kept by his descendants and successors, and a busy centre of pilgrimage. It maintains the customary devotions — prayer, qawwali and *naat*, and the free kitchen — and its annual urs draws great crowds of the order’s followers and of the wider devout. The Naushahia order he founded remains active across the Punjab and among the Punjabi diaspora, so that his shrine is the head of a living and widespread spiritual family.

The books consulted record nothing of the village of Ranmal, its urs, its custodians or its fabric. The place they give for the saint's tomb is another. Lahori's modern editor writes "آپ کا مزار شریف اس وقت ساہنپال شریف ضلع گجرات پنجاب میں ہے", that the mazar is at present at Sahnpal Sharif in district Gujrat (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ); the same editor describes the author of the order's chronicle *Tazkira-e Naushahi* as Hafiz Sayyid Muhammad Hayat Rabbani Naushahi Sahnpalvi (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 1). Chishti's passage on the burial is damaged in the printed text: "مزار مقام [illegible] ضلع شاہ پور مین اور انکے مزار بمقام سہن پال[OCR?] بہ [illegible] شیر شاہ[OCR?] بہ رسول نگر[OCR?] [illegible] ضلع گجرات مین". It names a mazar in district Shahpur and a mazar at Sahn Pal in district Gujrat, near names read as Sher Shah and Rasul Nagar, and whose mazar each clause names is not clear (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 483–484).

Lahori's index names a holder of the order's *sajjada*: "پیر محمد سجادہ نوشاہی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 315, folio 303). In the editor's time the manuscripts of the order were mostly held in a private Naushahi library at Sahanpal, Gujrat (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 292–295, folios 280–283; Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 243, folio 231).

## Devotion and Practice

The current account records prayer, qawwali and *naat*, the free kitchen and the annual urs at Ranmal Sharif. The books speak of the order's devotions, not of this site's.

**The *sama* of the order.** Writing in his own present, in the 1870s, Lahori describes the ecstatic *sama* of the order under the entry for Abdul Rahman Pak Naushahi, whom he calls "بڑے خلیفہ حضرت حاجی محمد نوشہ گنج بخش", the great khalifa of Noushah Ganj Bakhsh (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 80, folio 68 [marker 78]):

"پوشیدہ نہ رہے کہ فرقہ نوشاہیہ عالیہ میں سب لوگ صاحب وجد و سماع و شوق و ذوق و مستی ہیں۔ مگر فقرائے سلسلہ پاک رحمان کے سماع کے وقت سب سے زیادہ مست ہو جاتے ہیں۔ جب تک ان کے پاؤں میں رسہ ڈال کر الٹا نہ لٹکائیں اور وہ ساعت دو ساعت اسی حالت میں رقص نہ کریں سرد نہیں ہوتے اور اگر اس عمل سے ہوش میں نہ آئیں تو اسی حالت میں ان کو زمین پر کھینچتے ہیں۔ جب تک وہ ہوش میں آ جائیں رسن ان کے پاؤں سے کھولا نہیں جاتا" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 81, folio 69 [marker 79]).

All in the order, he says, are possessed of ecstasy and *sama*, but the faqirs of the Pak Rahman line become the most intoxicated at *sama*: they do not cool until a rope is put round their feet and they are hung upside down and dance so for an hour or two; failing that they are dragged along the ground, and the rope is not untied until they come to themselves (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 81, folio 69 [marker 79]). Chishti, too, speaks of the Naushahi followers, "نوشاہی" and "نوشاہیان", and of their qawwali and their "حال" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 466).

**Grain at his door.** A party of "فقرائے ہنود", Hindu faqirs, among whom the apostate Hafiz Tahir had joined, passed his house: "ایک روز ان کا گذر حضرت نوشاہ کے دولت خانے پر ہوا۔ حضرت نے حکم دیا کہ ہر ایک شخص کو غلہ گندم دے دو۔ چنانچہ سب کو مل گیا۔ مگر حافظ طاہر کو کچھ نہ ملا کہ غلہ ختم ہو چکا تھا". The saint ordered wheat to be given to every one of them; all received it except Hafiz Tahir, for the grain had run out (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, folio 230).

**A deathbed initiation and a teaching.** Of Shah Abdullah, whom the book describes as "منصبدار ہفت [OCR?] ہزاری دربار عالمگیری", Lahori writes: "وہ خدمت شیخ حاجی محمد نوشاہ کے حاضر ہوا۔ اس وقت حاجی محمد بیمار تھے اور ایک روز ان کی وفات میں باقی تھا حضرت نے اس کی بیعت قبول کی اور ذکر و شغل کی اجازت دی" — he came when Haji Muhammad was ill, with one day left before his death, and the saint accepted his *bai'at* and gave him leave for *zikr* and spiritual practice (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 241, folio 229). When Shah Abdullah insisted on becoming a *majzub*, the saint answered in words the book preserves: "تعلق میں بے تعلق رہنا مردوں کا کام ہے۔ مجذوب ہونا آسان ہے" — to remain detached amid attachments is the work of men; becoming a *majzub* is easy. He gave way only when Shah Abdullah "خدا کا واسطہ درمیان لایا", invoked God between them (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 241, folio 229).

## The Saint's Circle

**His khalifas.** Chishti names "دو خلیفہ نامی", two renowned khalifas: "فقیر عبد الرحمن", called "پاک رحمن", and "پیر محمد سچیار[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 484). Lahori says of Abdul Rahman Pak Naushahi that his pir's favour to him "کسی پر نہ تھی", was shown to no one else (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 80, folio 68 [marker 78]), and that his "مزار پر انوار موضع بھڑی [OCR?] زیارت گاہ خلق ہے", his radiant mazar at the village of Bharri is a place of pilgrimage. The author gives his death as 1153 AH; the editor, from three family manuscripts, *Lataif-e Gul Shahi*, *Bayaz-e Qadri* and *Kitab al-Fawaid*, gives 4 Muharram ۱۱۱۵ھ [OCR?], "جو معتبر ہے", and his birth as 995 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 81, folio 69 [marker 79], حاشیہ 1).

**A chain of succession.** Under entry ۲۳۱ Lahori sets out a five-step Naushahi Qadiri chain that ends in the saint: "نوشاہیہ قادریہ خاندان میں بیعت اس بزرگ کی بخدمت رحمان شاہ کے اور اس کی بخدمت محمد صدیق کے اور اس کی بخدمت شاہ فرید لاہوری کے اور اس کی بخدمت پیر محمد سچیار کے اور اس کی بخدمت حضرت حاجی محمد نوشاہ گنج بخش کے تھی" — Fazl Shah, from Rahman Shah, from Muhammad Siddiq, from Shah Farid Lahori, from Pir Muhammad Sachyar, from Haji Muhammad Naushah Ganj Bakhsh (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 272, folio 260). Beside it stand "حضرت شاہ کنٹھہ [OCR?] نوشاہی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 272–273) and "حضرت شاہ فرید نوشاہی", a murid of the same "شیخ پیر محمد سچیار" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 274, folio 262). Of this line Lahori says "اس خاندان کے مرید بھی اکثر لوگ شہر میں ہیں", that most of the family's murids are in the city of Lahore, and that it was materially supported by the Sikh state through Raja Dina Nath (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 273, folio 261). Chishti gives the same Fazl Shah line — Rahman Shah, Muhammad Siddiq, "شہ[OCR?] فرید[OCR?]", "حضرت بجیبار[OCR?]" — ending "اور اونکی پیر روشن ضمیر حضرت نوشہ گنج بخش" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 337). He also closes Shah Kamaita's spiritual pedigree with "… اور یہ حضرت نوشہ صاحب کا فقط" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 349), and sets Bhage Shah's second pedigree directly below Shah Sulaiman Nuri (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 467).

**Four God-absorbed disciples.** The sixth section of Lahori's book, "چھٹا چمن / مجانین و مجاذیب کے ذکر میں", on the madmen and the God-absorbed, opens at p. 236 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 236, folio 224), and four of its *majzubs* were Noushah Ganj Bakhsh's disciples, each carrying the *nisba* "نوشاہی". The book introduces them in turn as "شیخ مٹھا مجذوب نوشاہی … یہ بزرگ حضرت حاجی محمد نوشاہ گنج بخش کا مرید تھا" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 240, folio 228); "سید شاہ عبد اللہ مجذوب نوشاہی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 241, folio 229); "نانو مجذوب نوشاہی … حاجی محمد نوشاہ گنج بخش کا مرید تھا" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, folio 230); and "حافظ طاہر مجذوب نوشاہی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, folio 230).

Of Shaikh Mittha the book says: "حالت جذب و استغراق اس پر ایسی غالب ہوئی کہ ہمیشہ ویرانے جنگل میں پھرا کرتا اور وحش و طیور سے ہم کلام ہوتا اور جس شخص پر توجہ کرتا۔ وہ بھی مجذوب ہو جاتا" — absorption so mastered him that he wandered the wilderness, spoke with beasts and birds, and whoever he turned his attention upon became a *majzub* too (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 240, folio 228). Of Nanu, whose real name, the editor says, was Namak — "نانو مجذوب کا اصل نام نامک تھا۔ عوام میں نانو معروف ہو گئے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, حاشیہ 1) — the book tells that, hearing that on the Day of Judgement all men save the Prophet would be beardless, "اس نے موچنا خریدا اور تمام ریش و بروت کے بال اکھاڑ ڈالے", he bought tweezers and plucked out every hair of beard and moustache (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, folio 230). After his pir's death he left for the wilderness: "بعد وفات پیر روشن ضمیر کے یہ شخص جنگل کو نکل گیا اور آبادی کا رہنا ترک کیا۔ وہاں اس شخص کو قطاع الطریقون نے سال ایک ہزار ایک سو پینتیس میں شہید کیا", and there highway robbers killed him in 1135 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, folio 230). The editor gives his grave: "شیخ نانو مجذوب کا مدفن موضع کلاسکے [OCR?] چیمہ ضلع گجرات پاکستان ہے", the name of the village uncertain in the printed text (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, folio 230, حاشیہ 2).

## Legends and Miracles

Lahori says his *karamat* "کا انتہا نہیں ہے", have no end, and that they are set out in detail in the *Tazkira-e Naushahi* of Muhammad Hayat (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59). He relates two of them through his disciples.

**The worn shirt.** When Shah Abdullah would not be dissuaded from the way of the *majzub*, "ناچار حضرت نے ایک کرتہ جس کو آپ چند روز پہن چکے تھے۔ اس کو دیا اور کہا کہ اس کو پہن لے۔ بمجرد پہننے کے عالم ملکوت اس پر منکشف ہوگیا" — the saint, left no choice, gave him a shirt he had worn for some days and told him to put it on, and as soon as he wore it the angelic realm was disclosed to him (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 241, folio 229).

**Hafiz Tahir at the door.** When the party of Hindu faqirs came for grain, the saint called the stranger by name, "حافظ طاہر ہمارے پاس آ۔ کہ حصہ تیرا ہمارے پاس ہے", Hafiz Tahir, come to us, your share is with us; he ordered his servant "اس کا کرتہ اتار کر اس کے گلے میں جو زنار ہے توڑ دے", to take off his shirt and break the sacred thread at his neck; "من بعد مجدداً مسلمان ہوا اور ایک نظر فیض اثر سے اس کو واصل بحق کیا" — after which Hafiz Tahir became a Muslim anew, and with a single look the saint brought him to God (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 243, folio 231).

## In Verse

Lahori closes his entry with a Persian chronogram on the saint's passing:

حضرت حاجی محمد پیر نوشہ گنج بخش
آنکہ شد مشہور در اہل جہاں اجلال او
کرد چوں آخر عروج از فرش بر عرش بریں
پیر فیاض است ہم اہل سخاوت سال او

The book gives no translation of the chronogram; its final line carries "پیر فیاض", the phrase in which the author reads the year 1103 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 72, folio 60; Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59).

Lahori's editor records that his Urdu and Punjabi verse was selected and edited by Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi as "گنج شریف" and published by Dar al-Mu'arrikhin, Lahore, in 1975 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59). The editor's own list of publications gives it as "گنج شریف (کلیات نظم اردو حضرت حاجی محمد نوشہ گنج بخش قادری ف ۱۶۵۳ء) جامع سید شرافت نوشاہی۔ تقدیم محمد اقبال مجددی", with his foreword, beside a 64-page "احوال و آثار سید شرافت نوشاہی۔ لاہور ۱۹۷۱ء" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 307, folio 295). The books consulted quote none of the saint's own lines.

## The Order's Own Record

The Naushahi family's manuscripts were the order's archive, and Lahori's editor draws on them throughout. He lists nine sources for the Naushahia line (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59): Razi al-Din Qazi Ganjahi, *Risala Tuhfa-e Razwiya*, within Mirza Ahmad Beg Lahori's *Risala al-Ijaz*; Ahmad Beg Mirza Lahori, *Risala al-Ijaz*, 1107 AH; Sadaqat Muhammad Mah Ganjahi, *Thawaqib al-Manaqib*, 1126 AH; Hafiz Muhammad Hayat, *Tazkira-e Naushahi*, 1146 AH; Pir Kamal b. Shaikh Usman Lahori, *Sahaif-e Qudsiya*, 1186 AH; Imam Bakhsh Barqandazi Jalandhari, *Mirat al-Ghafuri*, 1186 AH; Muhammad Ashraf Manjri, *Kanz al-Rahmat*, 1220 AH, printed at Lahore in 1330 AH; Ghulam Muhi al-Din Faqir Lahori, *Tashrif al-Fuqara*, about 1248 AH [OCR?]; and Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi's own *Sharif al-Tawarikh*, 1355–1394, "سہ جلد سات ہزار صفحات", three volumes of seven thousand pages, in manuscript in its author's possession. All but the last two, the editor says, are owned by Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi.

The *Tazkira-e Naushahi*, by Hafiz Sayyid Muhammad Hayat Rabbani Naushahi Sahnpalvi (d. 1173 AH), was composed in 1146 AH and "اصل میں رسالہ الاعجاز مولفہ مرزا احمد بیگ لاہوری کی مشروح نقل ہے", an annotated copy of the *Risala al-Ijaz* with additions, holding only the affairs of the Naushahia elders; four manuscripts are in Sharafat Naushahi's library and two in the Punjab University Library (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 1). The author himself cites it, "کتاب تذکرہ نوشاہی میں لکھا ہے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, folio 230), and the editor cites a copy as "محمد حیات نوشاہی : تذکرہ نوشاہی قلمی ملکہ سید شرافت نوشاہی گجرات" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 243, folio 231). The edition's bibliography adds the *Risala al-Ijaz*, "(حالات مشائخ سلسلہ نوشاہیہ) ۱۱۰۷ھ مملوکہ مولانا سید شرافت نوشاہی، ساہن پال گجرات" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 292, folio 280), and Naushahi works such as "عمر بخش رسول نگری : مناقبات نوشاہی", by Umar Bakhsh Rasulnagri, "غلام قادر نوشاہی : بیاض قادری", by Ghulam Qadir Naushahi, and "گل محمد نوشاہی : لطائف گل شاہی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 294, folio 282). Which of these treat Noushah Ganj Bakhsh himself the book does not say.

## Setting: Phalia

Haroon Khalid, writing of the town and not of the shrine, records the story he had heard that Phalia "was named after Alexander's legendary horse Bucephalus— Phalus became Phalia", and that the horse's grave was there (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 12, ch. 6, folio not stated). His companion Salman Rashid disputed it: "The horse is not buried here ... He was buried near Jalalpur on the other side of the river's bank where his grave was swept by the flood only a few months after"; "Coins dating back to the Indo-Greek era were discovered from the pahari of the city of Phalia, giving birth to the rumour"; and "This version became popular during the colonial era when British officers roamed all over the country collecting oral history" (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 12, ch. 6, folio not stated). The old mound, Rashid said, now has "a government office there along with a school", and Iqbal Qaiser relayed an old shopkeeper's report: "That pahari has been sold by the department for nine crore rupees. Now there will be a shiny, new shopping plaza there" (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 12, ch. 6, folio not stated).

## Where the Sources Disagree

- **Where the saint is buried.** The current account places his shrine at Ranmal Sharif, near Phalia. Lahori's editor writes that his mazar is at present at Sahnpal Sharif, district Gujrat (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ). Chishti's damaged passage names a mazar in district Shahpur and a mazar at "سہن پال[OCR?]" in district Gujrat, without making clear whose each is (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 483–484).
- **The year of his death.** The current account gives 1654. Lahori gives 1103 AH, with the chronogram "پیر فیاض" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59). His editor's footnote gives 12 Rabi al-Awwal 1064 AH and calls 1103 AH wrong (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 2). The editor's own list of publications gives 1653 CE (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 307, folio 295).
- **His birth.** The current account gives 1552. Lahori's editor gives 1 Ramadan 959 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 2).
- **His name.** The current account calls him Syed Muhammad Noushah Ganj Bakhsh. Lahori heads his entry "شیخ حاجی محمد قادری المشہور بحضرت نوشہ گنج بخش" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 70–72, folios 58–60), and the index gives his personal name as Haji Muhammad (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 332, folio 320).
- **Who began the order.** The current account and Lahori's entry on him make him its founder: the Naushahia path "ان سے شروع ہوا" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 70, folio 58). In his entry on Shah Maruf Chishti wa Qadri, Lahori writes "انہیں کی ذات بابرکات سے پنجاب میں سلسلہ نوشاہیہ نے فروغ پایا", that through his blessed person the Naushahia line flourished in the Punjab; the pronoun may refer to Shah Maruf or to Sayyid Mubarak Haqqani, named just before (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 50, folio 38).
- **Where he withdrew as a young man.** Lahori writes Sandal Bar; his editor says it should be Gondal Bar (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 70, folio 58, حاشیہ 2). Chishti speaks of a "لق و دق" wilderness and of study at "موضع حاکوثارہ[OCR?]" near "نوشہرہ" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 489–490; Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 490).
- **His mother's name.** Lahori gives Bibi Jiwani, "جیونی بی بی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 316, folio 304); Chishti "ملا بی جیون[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 486).
- **The chain through Pir Muhammad Sachyar.** Lahori names the link above the saint "پیر محمد سچیار" and the link below him "شاہ فرید لاہوری" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 272, folio 260); Chishti's version of the same chain reads "حضرت بجیبار[OCR?]" and "شہ[OCR?] فرید[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 337), while his list of khalifas has "پیر محمد سچیار[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 484).
- **Shaikh Mittha's death and name.** The author gives 1115 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 240–241). The editor writes "شیخ مطا [OCR?] مجذوب نوشاہی کا سالِ وفات ۱۱۱۵ ھ غلط ہے۔ بلکہ ۱۰۷۶ ھ خاندانی روایات کے مطابق صحیح ہے", that 1115 is wrong and 1076 is right by the family traditions, spelling the name differently from the author's "شیخ مٹھا" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 241, folio 229, حاشیہ 1).
- **Shah Abdullah's death.** The author gives "۱۱۳۱ ھ ایک ہزار ایک سو اکتیس ہجری" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 241). The editor: "خاندانی مآخذ کے مطابق ۱۰۹۲ ھ درست ہے۔ اس لیے مفتی صاحب کا مندرجہ سال وفات ۱۱۳۱ ھ غلط ہے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 241, folio 229, حاشیہ 2).
- **Nanu's death.** The author gives 1135 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242). The editor notes that the *Khazinat al-Asfiya* gives 1132, calls both years wrong, and gives the year of his martyrdom in the family sources as "۱۰۹۴ ھ [OCR?]" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 242, folio 230, حاشیہ 2).
- **Hafiz Tahir's death.** The author gives 1136 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 243). The editor: "حافظ طاہر نوشاہی کا سال وفات بھی ۱۱۳۶ ھ غلط ہے۔ جناب سید شرافت نوشاہی کی تحقیق کے مطابق صحیح سال وفات ۱۰۹۷ ھ [OCR?] ہونا چاہیے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 243, folio 231, حاشیہ 1).
- **Pak Rahman's death.** The author gives 1153 AH; the editor gives 4 Muharram ۱۱۱۵ھ [OCR?] from three family manuscripts (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 81, folio 69 [marker 79], حاشیہ 1).

## Legacy

Syed Muhammad Noushah Ganj Bakhsh is remembered as the founder of the Naushahia Qadiri order — one of the enduring Sufi lines of the Punjab — a saint whose long life and organising genius planted a spiritual family that has lasted nearly four centuries. His shrine at Ranmal Sharif remains its living heart and a major centre of Qadiri devotion in the region.

Already in the 1870s Lahori counted the faqirs of the Naushahia path in the Punjab in the thousands (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 70, folio 58), and he found the order's murids numerous in Lahore (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 273, folio 261). Kanhaiya Lal's history of Lahore, which speaks of a Lahore tomb and not of this site, carries the order's spiritual pedigree up to the saint through the custodian of Shah Kanhh[OCR?]'s tomb outside Mochi Gate: "جواہر شاہ خادم آلہی شاہ او وہ گامی شاہ کا او وہ محمد زبان[OCR?] کا او وہ شاہ عصمت المند[OCR?] کا او وہ شیخ عبد الرحمن المعروف پاک رحمان کا او وہ نو شاہ گنج بخش کا" — Jawahar Shah, *khadim* of Alahi Shah, he of Gami Shah, he of Muhammad Zaban[OCR?], he of Shah Ismat al-Mand[OCR?], he of Shaikh Abdul Rahman known as Pak Rahman, he of Nau Shah Ganj Bakhsh (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 304, folio 307). At that tomb, Kanhaiya Lal records, Jawahar Shah "سنہ ۱۲۹۴[OCR?] ہجری مین" rebuilt the tomb anew and made the mosque *pakka*; "جواہر شاہ سجادہ نشین بنا ہے", Jawahar Shah has become *sajjada nashin*; and a dalan, a kothri and a well "فقرا کے آرام کے لئے", for the faqirs' comfort, stand within the enclosure (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 304, folio 307).
