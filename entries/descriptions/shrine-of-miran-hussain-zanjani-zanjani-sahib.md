## Overview

In the old riverside locality of Chah Miran, named directly in his honour, stands the tomb of Syed Miran Hussain Zanjani, one of the earliest Muslim missionary figures associated with Lahore. The tomb, unusually for a site of such antiquity, rests on a simple raised platform without a dome, standing within what was once a dedicated garden named for the saint. It is formally recognised and maintained by the Punjab Auqaf and Religious Affairs Department, continues to host an annual *urs* drawing local devotees, and serves as the namesake and spiritual anchor of the surrounding Chah Miran neighbourhood on the historic bank of the River Ravi near the old walled city.

The books bear witness to the grave from several sides. Mufti Ghulam Sarwar Lahori's *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, in its modern annotated edition, places the burial of Shaikh Husain Zanjani at Chah Miran, "مدفون چاہ میراں", and its appendix gives the present location as Miran Husain Park, Chah Miran (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 196; p. 289, folio 277). Saiyid Athar Abbas Rizvi writes that "Zinjani was buried in Chah Miran, which is now a suburb of Lahore" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112). Two nineteenth-century surveys of Lahore's shrines, Nur Ahmad Chishti's *Tahqiqat-e Chishti* and Kanhaiya Lal's *Tarikh-e Lahore*, describe the enclosure, the well and the yearly gathering at the grave, and set it in the village they call Khoi Miran (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 403; p. 705; Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278).

The same books disagree, sharply, about who lies there and when he lived. The site's account gives his death as 1042 CE; Lahori gives 600 AH; Kanhaiya Lal prints a year 604 with no era; Rizvi dates his death only by the arrival of Ali Hujwiri at Lahore, about 1035. Lahori's modern editor goes further and argues that two different men of the same name have been run together. These accounts are set out below, each in its own words, and gathered side by side under *Where the Sources Disagree*.

## The Life of the Saint

According to the site's account he was a Hussaini Syed, born in 958 CE in Zanjan, in present-day Iran. He travelled to Lahore around 998 CE on the instruction of his spiritual master, Sheikh Abu al-Fazal Khatil, and is remembered to have preached in the city for some thirty-six years, converting large numbers of its inhabitants to Islam well before the Ghaznavid and later Mughal periods reshaped the region's religious landscape. He died in 1042 CE and was buried alongside his brother, Hazrat Musa Hussain Zanjani, whose own shrine lies nearby.

A substantial chapter devoted to the saint in Alam Faqri's *Tazkirah Awliya-e-Pakistan* traces his family's descent from Imam Musa Kazim through the Zanjan line. The tazkira casts Zanjani as the elder of the two missionary saints credited with Lahore's early Islamization, his death preceding the arrival of the more widely celebrated Data Ganj Bakhsh.

Rizvi, who calls him "Shaikh Husain Zinjani", "Shaikh Husain Zanjani", and, in his report of Abu'l-Fazl, "Shaikh Husaini Zanjani", prints neither "Syed" nor "Miran" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112; p. 139, folio 121). He sets the saint's coming in the wake of the Ghaznavid conquest: "the annexation of the Panjab by Mahmud of Ghazna and its incorporation into his empire prompted many sufis to settle in the area", and Khattali "ordered his disciple, Shaikh Husain Zinjani to move to the Panjab", later sending Hujwiri after him (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112).

Lahori's own entry on him, headed "سید حسین زنجانی لاہوری", is four sentences long (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, folio 186). It counts him among the ancient elders, a man of guidance, austerity, piety, nobility and sayyid descent. His chain, it says, meets Junaid of Baghdad: "شجرہ ان کا حضرت جنیدؒ بغدادی کے ساتھ ملتا ہے". He came to Lahore together with Sayyid Yaqub Zanjani, "سید یعقوب زنجانی کے ساتھ یہ لاہور میں آئے", spent his whole life guiding the people, and died in the year 600 AH: "آخر سال چھ سو ہجری میں وفات کی" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198).

Kanhaiya Lal tells the saint's coming differently. In the time of the Ghurid sultans, he writes, the saint came from the city of Zanjan to travel in Hind, toured the whole realm, and on his way back came to Lahore, settled, and died there: "سلاطین غوریہ کیوقت یہ شہر زنجان سے ہند کی سیر کو آیا اور تمام کشور ہند کی سیر کی واپسی کیوقت لاہور آ کر قیام پذیر ہوا اور یہان ہی فوت ہوا" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). He names no spiritual chain. His year of death is printed as "سال وفات اسکا سنہ ۶۰۴ [OCR?] ہے", the reading of the numeral uncertain in the printed text and no era given with it (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278).

Chishti, citing Dara Shikoh's *Safinat al-Awliya*, has a party of saints come "ولایت سے ملک ہند مین", the first of them "شیخ المشایخ سید حسین زنجانی جنکی مزار [illegible] ہے کہ وہی [illegible] نواح لاہور میں موجود ہے", that is, the master of masters whose grave is in the environs of Lahore; he promises a separate account of him, "اوسکا[OCR?] حال انکا علاحدہ تحریر یہ ہوگا" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 269). All of them, he says, reached Lahore "پانچویں[OCR?] صدی[OCR?] ہجری میں", in the fifth century of the hijra, the reading uncertain in the printed text (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 270). In the separate account he has the saint come to Lahore with "حضرت صدر دیوان صاحب", in a year whose numerals cannot be read; of his death he can say only "تاریخ وفات انکی سنہ [illegible: year in numerals] ہجری", the year again unreadable, and that further particulars are "نامعلوم", unknown (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705).

## The Pir-Brother of Data Ganj Bakhsh

The best-known story about the saint ties him to Ali Hujwiri, Data Ganj Bakhsh. In the form the *Tazkirah Awliya-e-Pakistan* preserves it, as one of Lahore's best-known early Sufi foundation stories, Ali Hujwiri was instructed by his own pir to travel to Lahore and preach, and replied that his senior "pir-brother," Hazrat Shaikh Hussain Zanjani, was already established in the city — only to arrive and find Zanjani's funeral procession already under way.

Chishti tells it the same way: Zanjani was Data Ganj Bakhsh's "پیر بہا ہی", already in Lahore, and Data joined his "جنازہ" and saw to "تکفین و تدفین", the shrouding and the burial (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 163). Rizvi gives the morning of the funeral: "In the morning Shaikh Husain Zinjani's coffin was carried out for burial—he had died during the night. Zinjani was buried in Chah Miran, which is now a suburb of Lahore" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112). Rizvi dates the saint's death by this arrival alone, Hujwiri reaching Lahore "in c. 1035, while Sultan Mas'ud I (1031-41) reigned in Ghazna" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112).

Lahori's modern editor reports the Mufti's telling of the same story in his other book, *Khazinat al-Asfiya*, on the authority of *Fawaid al-Fuad*: ordered to Lahore, Data objected, "وہاں تو میرے برادر طریقت حسین زنجانی موجود ہیں۔ میری کیا ضرورت ہے", that his brother in the path was already there; his murshid repeated the order; and on reaching Lahore Data saw Husain Zanjani's bier going past and understood his murshid's far-seeing eye (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1).

## Two Men of One Name

It is that story which leads Lahori's editor to divide the saint in two. The Mufti, he writes, appears quite tangled over him: "شیخ حسین زنجانی لاہوریؒ کے حالات کے سلسلہ میں مفتی صاحب خاصے الجھے ہوئے معلوم ہوتے ہیں" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1). Hujwiri's death falls after 400 AH, the editor observes, so that on the bier story Husain Zanjani had died long before: "حضرت علی ہجویری کا سال وفات با ختلاف روایت بعد ۴۰۰ھ ہے۔ گویا اس مندرجہ روایت کے مطابق شیخ حسین زنجانی کا انتقال ۴۰۰ھ سے بہت پہلے ہو چکا تھا". Yet the Mufti himself, in *Khazinat al-Asfiya* and in this very book, gives 600 AH without any reference at all: "اب مفتی صاحب خود ہی خزینۃ الاصفیا (۲۵۰/۲) اور حدیقۃ الاولیاء میں شیخ حسین زنجانی کا سال وفات بغیر کسی حوالے کے ۶۰۰ھ لکھ دیتے ہیں" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1).

The editor's resolution is that the man of this entry is a different person from the Shaikh Husain Zanjani of *Fawaid al-Fuad*: "حقیقت یہ ہے کہ زیر بحث شخصیت فوائد الفواد میں مذکور شیخ حسین زنجانی سے مختلف ہے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1). In his note on Data Ganj Bakhsh he names the later man as the one buried at this site: "معروف شیخ حسین زنجانی ف ۶۰۰ [OCR?] ہجری (مدفون چاہ میراں)", the well-known Shaikh Husain Zanjani, died 600 AH, the reading of the year uncertain in the printed text, buried at Chah Miran (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 196). The editor thus separates the two as different persons (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 196–197, folios 184–185).

He supports the later date with two arguments. The first is a meeting. When Khwaja Muin al-Din Chishti came to Lahore around 585 AH, he writes, he had meeting and company with Shaikh Husain Zanjani: "حضرت خواجہ معین الدین چشتی اجمیریؒ جب قریباً ۵۸۵ھ (تاریخ السلف ۷، ۹) میں لاہور تشریف لائے تو شیخ حسین زنجانی سے ملاقات و صحبت رہی"; for the meeting he cites the *Ain-e Akbari*, Jamali's *Siyar al-Arifin*, Ghausi's *Gulzar-e Abrar*, Muhammad Salih Kanbo's *Amal-e Salih* and Dara Shikoh's *Safinat al-Awliya* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1).

The second is a dated document. From *Waqai-e Sialkot*, written in 1071 AH by Muhammad Muqim, the editor quotes a Persian passage that mentions, in 758 AH, Khizr Khan, a grandson (نواسہ) of Sayyid Husain, with the words "برادر … سید یعقوب صدر شاہ زنجانی کہ مرقد مقدس ایشاں در لاہور گذر سنجا [OCR?] زیارت گاہ خاص و عام است", and places Khizr Khan "در مقربان عالی شان و امیران … فیروز شاہی", among the high intimates and amirs of Firoz Shah (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 199, بقیہ حاشیہ 1). His conclusion: in 758 AH the grandson was alive, and therefore the stated 600 AH is close to correct, "اس اقتباس سے تصریح ہوتا ہے کہ ۷۵۸ھ میں شیخ حسین زنجانی کے نواسے بقید حیات تھے۔ اس لیے شیخ حسین زنجانی کا مذکورہ سال وفات ۶۰۰ھ قرین صحت ہے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 199, بقیہ حاشیہ 1). The place-name in the passage, گذر سنجا, is uncertain in the printed text, and since the words about the holy grave follow Sayyid Yaqub's name, it is not clear whose grave the passage locates there (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 199).

## Khwaja Muin al-Din at the Tomb

Whether the founder of the Chishtiyya in India met Zanjani alive, or prayed at his grave, is itself an old question. Rizvi writes that Khwaja Muʿinuʾd-Din "stayed near the tombs of Shaikh Husain Zanjani and Shaikh ‘Ali Hujwiri" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 139, folio 121). In a footnote on the same page he sets out the earlier authorities. Jamali says that Hujwiri died in the year the Khwaja reached Lahore "but that Shaikh Husain Zanjani was still alive"; the *Fawaʿidu'l-Fuʾad* says that "Hujwiri reached Lahore in the year Shaikh Husain Zanjani died"; "According to Abu'l-Fazl, Khwaja Mu'inu'd-Din lived with Shaikh Husaini Zanjani"; and Firishta "simply mentions that after leaving Ghazna, the Khwaja stayed at Lahore" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 139, folio 121, footnote 3). Rizvi's own judgement: "Fawaidu'l Fu‘ad's statement appears to be correct and it is likely that the Khwaja stayed near the two tombs" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 139, folio 121, footnote 3).

Chishti, following Dara Shikoh, says that Muin al-Din "اوران حضرات سے ملاقاتین کین", met with these saints, after they had reached Lahore (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 270). Lahori's editor, as above, gives the meeting and company around 585 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1).

## The Shrine

The site's account describes a modest architectural footprint that belies its historical significance as one of Lahore's oldest surviving Islamic devotional sites, predating by centuries the grander Mughal-era shrines for which the city is more widely known: a simple raised platform without a dome, within what was once a garden named for the saint.

Lahori's entry on the saint gives no grave at all; the burial is stated in the editor's note on Data Ganj Bakhsh, "مدفون چاہ میراں" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 196). The edition's appendix lists him as "سید حسین زنجانی قدس سرہ", with no address from the Mufti's text and a present location of "میراں حسین پارک چاہ میراں", Miran Husain Park, Chah Miran (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 289, folio 277).

Kanhaiya Lal, writing of the shrines outside the walled city, places it in a village east of the city: "یہ متبرک مزار موضع کہوئی میران بیرون شہر لاہور بجانب شرق بفاصلہ ایک میل کے واقع ہے", this blessed mazar lies in mauza Khoi Miran, outside the city of Lahore to the east, at a distance of one mile (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). The mazar stands inside a brick enclosure a man's height, "قد آدم بلند چار دیواری خشتی"; the grave itself is of brick; pakka houses have been built for faqirs to live in, "مکانات پختہ فقرا کی سکونت کے لئے"; and a Persian-wheel well is running, "چاہ چرخی دار جاری" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). His verdict on the place: "اگرچہ عمارت مختصر ہے مگر مکان قدیم ہے اور صاحب مزار قدما ئے بزرگان شہر لاہور سے ہی", though the building is small the place is ancient, and the one buried there is among the earliest elders of the city of Lahore (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). In his table of contents the shrine stands under the name "شاہ حسین زنجانی", Shah Husain Zanjani, with no place or date given there (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 4, folio 3).

Chishti describes the shrine twice. In the first account, headed in his contents "حال مزار شاہ حسین زنجانی[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 6), the "خانقاہ حضرت سید [OCR?] حسین زنجانی" lies "جنوب رویہ", on the south side, of Khoi Miran. It has a *chardiwari* with a door on the east "معہ طاق [OCR?] پختہ [OCR?]"; the mazar stands on a *chabutra* with a lamp-stand of brick, "خشتی [OCR?]"; north of the door is "ایک سہ درہ [OCR?]"; and there are a *dalan*, a well and a *charkhi*. But the place, he adds, is now forsaken: "مگر اب یہہ مکان آوارہ [OCR?] … کوئی [OCR?] فقیر مکاندار [OCR?] یہان نہین بیٹہتا", no resident faqir sits here (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 403).

In the second, fuller account he again sets it to the south of the village, "جنوب رویہ موضع میران دی کہوئی[OCR?] واقع ہی": a brick enclosure taller than a man with its entrance on the north, a niche with wooden shutters, a second enclosure with a "چراغدان", a three-arched *dalan*, a "چاہ چرخی دار", a few graves and a *kotha* (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705).

The site's account says the shrine is now recognised and maintained by the Punjab Auqaf and Religious Affairs Department. Chishti, in his day, names its holder: "مگر نامہم[OCR?] اوس مزار پر قبضہ کرم علی شاہ گدی نشین صدر دیوان صاحب کا ہی", the mazar being in the possession of Karam Ali Shah, *gaddi nashin* of Sadr Diwan Sahib; the faqirs there are few, and he mentions its income, "آمدنی[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705). Kanhaiya Lal records no offerings, custodian or endowment (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278).

## Devotion and Practice

The site's account records an annual *urs* that still draws local devotees. Chishti, too, writes that an *urs* is held once every year, "ہر سال ہمیشہ[OCR?] ایک دفعہ عرس ہوتا ہی" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705). In his first account he says that the *sajjada nashin* of Sadr Diwan has the *fatiha* said there year by year, "سجادہ نشین صدر دیوان [OCR?] سال بسال [OCR?] فاتحہ کرا دیتا ہی", in a sentence that opens on the well, "جب [OCR?] چاہ [OCR?] یہان کا کم ہو گیا", its sense uncertain in the printed text (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 403).

Kanhaiya Lal remembers a larger gathering in decline: "سکہون کیوقت اس مزار پہ بڑا میلہ سالانہ ہوتا تہا مگر اب رفتہ رفتہ رونق میلے کی جاتی رہی ہی", in the Sikhs' time a great annual *mela* was held at this mazar, but now the mela's liveliness has gradually gone (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). He gives no date for the *urs* (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). On the same run of pages he notes the same fall in attendance after the Sikh state at the mazar of Fateh Shah Mast (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 274, folio 277).

## Legends and Miracles

Chishti writes that the saint's miracles are famous by the thousand, "کرامات انکی ہزار ہا مشہور ہین", but he relates none of them (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705). The one story the books carry is that of Data Ganj Bakhsh and the bier, told above; in the Mufti's telling as his editor reports it, Data understood on seeing the bier his murshid's far-seeing eye (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1).

## Companions and Namesakes

The site's account names his brother, Hazrat Musa Hussain Zanjani, buried nearby. The books name another Zanjani as his companion. Lahori has the saint come to Lahore with Sayyid Yaqub Zanjani (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198), and the edition's appendix lists that saint on a separate line, "سید یعقوب المخاطب صدر دیوان زنجانی لاہوری قدس سرہ", at Hospital Road, Lahore, "ہسپتال روڈ لاہور" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 289, folio 277). Chishti has him come with "حضرت صدر دیوان صاحب" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705), and it is the line of Sadr Diwan whose *sajjada nashin* held the *fatiha* and the possession of the mazar in his day (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 403; p. 705). The *Waqai-e Sialkot* passage quoted by Lahori's editor names "سید یعقوب صدر شاہ زنجانی" beside Sayyid Husain with the word "برادر" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 199, بقیہ حاشیہ 1).

Kanhaiya Lal places Shah Yaqub Zanjani, known as Sadr Diwan, outside Shah Alami Gate, and says he came from Zanjan in "سنہ ۵۳۵[OCR?] ہجری" and died in "سنہ ۵۵۶[OCR?] ہجری", the readings of both years uncertain in the printed text; the clause in which he names "سید حسین زنجانی" is damaged, "یہ سید حسین زنجانی کے لاہور مین آ کر سکونت پذیر ہوا", and does not show what relation, if any, he meant between the two (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 301, folio 304). The same book names a third Zanjani, "شیخ احمد زنجانی", author of the treatise *Tuhfat al-Wasilin*, whom it does not connect with the saint (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 14, folio 9).

## Setting

The books' wider remarks on Lahore and the Punjab speak of the city and the province, not of this grave. Rizvi ties the coming of Sufis to the Punjab to Mahmud of Ghazna's annexation, which "prompted many sufis to settle in the area" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112), and of eleventh-century Lahore he writes that "a number of mystics and scholars who had settled in Lahore were strongly hostile to the views of its author" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 131, folio 113).

Lahori's text gives the title of first caller to Islam in Lahore to another man, Shaikh Muhammad Ismail Muhaddith Lahori, a Bukhari sayyid who taught hadith and *tafsir* there and through whom, it says, "اور دین اسلام ان کے طفیل سے پنجاب میں شائع ہوا", the faith spread in the Punjab; it gives his death as 448 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 191, folio 179). Its modern editor rejects the source of that account and the date: "شیخ اسمٰعیل محدث لاہوری کا صحیح سال وفات ۹۸۰ ھ ہے نہ کہ ۴۴۸ ھ" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, pp. 191–192, folios 179–180).

Of the neighbourhood, Kanhaiya Lal records a second shrine in the same village, the mazar of Faqir Sain Qutb Shah Qadri, lying "موضع کہوئی میران کے مشرق کی سمت", to the east of mauza Khoi Miran (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 271, folio 274). The arrangement he describes at the grave, faqirs' pakka quarters and a working Persian-wheel well, he records a page earlier at the mazar of Shah Gada Lahori (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 274, folio 277).

## Where the Sources Disagree

- **The year of his death.** The site's account gives 1042 CE. Lahori's text gives 600 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198), and its editor, in a note, "ف ۶۰۰ [OCR?] ہجری", the reading uncertain in the printed text (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 196). Kanhaiya Lal prints "سال وفات اسکا سنہ ۶۰۴ [OCR?] ہے", with no era (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). Rizvi dates the death only by Hujwiri's arrival at Lahore, "in c. 1035" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112). Chishti's year cannot be read, and he calls the particulars "نامعلوم" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705).
- **When he came to Lahore.** The site's account has him born in 958 CE and arriving around 998 CE, on the instruction of Sheikh Abu al-Fazal Khatil. Rizvi has Khattali order him to the Punjab after Mahmud of Ghazna's annexation, before Hujwiri (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112). Kanhaiya Lal has him come from Zanjan "سلاطین غوریہ کیوقت", in the time of the Ghurid sultans (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). Chishti, following Dara Shikoh, has him reach Lahore in the fifth century of the hijra, the reading uncertain in the printed text (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 270). Lahori's editor argues that this saint lived around 585–600 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1).
- **One man or two.** The site's account, after the *Tazkirah Awliya-e-Pakistan*, makes the saint of this tomb the senior pir-brother whose funeral Data Ganj Bakhsh met; Chishti agrees (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 163), and Rizvi has the man of the funeral buried in Chah Miran (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112). Lahori's editor holds that the Husain Zanjani buried at Chah Miran, died 600 AH, is a different person from the Husain Zanjani of *Fawaid al-Fuad* whose bier Data met (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 196; p. 198, حاشیہ 1).
- **The Mufti against himself.** As his editor reports, the Mufti tells the bier story in *Khazinat al-Asfiya*, which would put the death well before 400 AH, and gives the death as 600 AH both there and in *Hadiqat al-Awliya* (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1).
- **Whether Khwaja Muin al-Din found him alive.** Jamali says he was "still alive" when the Khwaja reached Lahore; Abu'l-Fazl that the Khwaja "lived with" him; the *Fawaʿidu'l-Fuʾad* that he died in the year Hujwiri arrived; Firishta mentions only the Khwaja's stay at Lahore (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 139, folio 121, footnote 3). Lahori's editor gives a meeting and company around 585 AH (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, حاشیہ 1), and Chishti, after Dara Shikoh, says Muin al-Din met these saints (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 270). Rizvi judges that the Khwaja "stayed near the two tombs" (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 139, folio 121, footnote 3).
- **His name.** The site's account calls him Syed Miran Hussain Zanjani, and the *Tazkirah Awliya-e-Pakistan* Hazrat Shaikh Hussain Zanjani. Lahori heads his entry "سید حسین زنجانی لاہوری" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198, folio 186). Kanhaiya Lal's contents and Chishti's contents print Shah Husain Zanjani (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 4, folio 3; Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 6). Rizvi prints Shaikh Husain Zinjani, Shaikh Husain Zanjani and Shaikh Husaini Zanjani, and neither Syed nor Miran (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112; p. 139, folio 121). The site's account names his master Sheikh Abu al-Fazal Khatil; Rizvi calls him Khattali (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112).
- **His spiritual chain.** Lahori says his chain meets Junaid of Baghdad (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198); Rizvi makes him a disciple of Khattali (Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112); Kanhaiya Lal names no chain (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278).
- **His companion.** The site's account has him buried alongside his brother, Hazrat Musa Hussain Zanjani. Lahori has him come to Lahore with Sayyid Yaqub Zanjani (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 198), whose own grave the edition's appendix places on Hospital Road (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 289, folio 277); Chishti has him come with Hazrat Sadr Diwan Sahib (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705).
- **Where the grave stands.** The site's account places it in the riverside locality of Chah Miran, on the historic bank of the Ravi near the old walled city. Lahori's editor and Rizvi name Chah Miran (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 196; Rizvi, *A History of Sufism in India*, p. 130, folio 112). Kanhaiya Lal puts it in mauza Khoi Miran, a mile east of the city (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278). Chishti sets it to the south of Khoi Miran (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 403; p. 705).
- **The doorway, in Chishti's two accounts.** One gives the enclosure a door on the east (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 403); the other gives its entrance on the north (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705).
- **Who keeps the shrine.** The site's account says it is maintained by the Punjab Auqaf and Religious Affairs Department. Chishti, in his day, has it in the possession of Karam Ali Shah, *gaddi nashin* of Sadr Diwan Sahib (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705); in his other account no faqir sits there (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 403), and in this one the faqirs are few (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705).
- **The yearly gathering.** The site's account and Chishti record an annual *urs* (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 705). Kanhaiya Lal records a great annual *mela* in the Sikh period whose liveliness has since gone (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278).

## Legacy

As one of the earliest attested Sufi graves in the Lahore region, the shrine offers valuable testimony to the gradual, missionary-led spread of Islam in Punjab in the centuries before the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal empire. The nineteenth-century surveys already counted it among the city's oldest: Kanhaiya Lal calls the place ancient and its occupant one of the earliest elders of Lahore (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 275, folio 278), and Chishti, after Dara Shikoh, names him first among the saints who came from abroad to Lahore, "شیخ المشایخ" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 269).

His name has also travelled with the story of Data Ganj Bakhsh, whose arrival in Lahore the books measure by Zanjani's funeral, and with that of Khwaja Muin al-Din Chishti, who is said to have kept company with him or to have stayed near his tomb. Whether one man or two lie behind that name, the locality of Chah Miran around the grave is named in his honour.
