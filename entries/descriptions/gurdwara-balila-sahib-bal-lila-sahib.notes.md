## The shape of this row

Two books name the gurdwara, both by Haroon Khalid:
- *Walking with Nanak*, lines 314–319: "Gurdwara Bal Lila", "A few feet away from Gurdwara Patti Sahib",
  the open field where Nanak played (p. 90, folio 72; p. 91, folio 73), and the Lohri sports for children
  (p. 91, folio 73).
- *A White Trail*, lines 478–483: "Gurdwara Balila", near Janamasthan, across the road from Patti Sahib, built
  to mark where Nanak played (p. 177; p. 222), and Surjeet's "small event" last year, with none this year
  because of the renovation (p. 177).

The only Doubt on the row (line 487) is the spelling join "Balila" = this row. The draft gives the book's
spelling at the point where it cites it.

All the other material is person-level (Guru Nanak), place-level (Talwandi / Nankana Sahib) or
province-level. The Overview says early that the other books do not mention the gurdwara. The childhood,
setting and wider-record passages are framed as being about the Guru or the town. The row-specific facts
are short, so the article's length comes from the Talwandi childhood material, which bears directly on
what this shrine commemorates.

## Disagreements listed (15)

1. Where the gurdwara stands. The current text says both "three hundred metres south-east" and "a few steps
   away" from Janam Asthan. *Walking with Nanak* says a few feet from Patti Sahib. *A White Trail* says
   near Janamasthan and across the road from Patti Sahib.
2. The children's games. *Walking with Nanak* says every year at Lohri. *A White Trail* has Surjeet's
   "Last year" with none this year, and Kartar says Lohri only began in the last couple of years.
3. Six gurdwaras at Nankana (*Walking with Nanak* p. 90) against seven (*A White Trail* p. 192).
4. The birth date: 1469 (current text); 20 October 1469 (Khalid); 14 April 1469 (Dhillon); Samvat 1526 /
   880 AH under Bahlul Lodi (Bhandari).
5. The death: 1539 (current text, Khalid); 22 September 1539 (Dhillon); under Salim Shah, aged 70–80
   (Bhandari).
6. The birthplace: Meherban's Dera Chahal against the others' Talwandi (*Walking with Nanak* p. 43).
7. How the birth was granted: Tripta's prayers at the mound temple against the blessing of Naulakha Hazari.
8. Who held Talwandi: Rai Bular (current text, Khalid) against Rai Bhoe in Dhillon.
9. The boy's Muslim teacher: Sayyad Hussain (Dhillon) against Sayyed Hassan (Khalid).
10. The wife's family: Batala (Dhillon) against Pakhoke near Gurdaspur (Khalid). Bhandari has the Guru
    marry after coming to Batala.
11. The Punjab in the fifteenth century: peaceful (Rizvi p. 408) against constant conflict (Dhillon p. 16).
12. The number of journeys: five or four (Dhillon), about 24 years (Khalid) against 23 years (Dhillon).
13. The size of the Sikh community at Nankana: three sets of figures.
14. The birthday festival: "last days of November" and 10–15k every year against 8–10 November and 3,000
    in 2011.
15. When Ranjit Singh's rule began: end of the eighteenth century (*Walking with Nanak* p. 34) against
    crowned 1801 (*A White Trail* p. 207). The current text dates his renovation to the early nineteenth
    century.
