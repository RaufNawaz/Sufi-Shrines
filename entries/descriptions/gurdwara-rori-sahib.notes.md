## The shape of this row

The row has two principal figures, Guru Nanak and Bhai Mardana. Only one book describes the gurdwara:
Khalid's *Walking with Nanak* (row section, lines 317–341: travelogue pp. 116–118, Iqbal Qaiser's
explanation of the name, the Bhai Ram Singh gateway, the caretakers, the former buffalo market).
Rizvi (lines 39–52) puts Nanak and Mardana at "Saiyidpur (Amanabad)" during the massacre and capture, but
names no site. Dhillon (lines 426–438) is Mardana-only person-level material, some of it set at Saidpur.
*A White Trail* (lines 494–501) and Bhandari (lines 619–622) are Mardana person-level material. Everything
else is Guru Nanak person-level, Eminabad/Saidpur place-level (Khalid lines 380–395; Bhandari lines
659–668), or province context. The Overview says early that only Khalid describes the gurdwara. The
person-level and place-level sections are framed as being about the figure or the town.

The overview also says that the gurdwara Khalid enters at pp. 116–117 is identified as Rori Sahib only
at p. 118, from context. This is the first Doubt (line 339), given in plain words.

## Disagreements listed (9)

1. When Saidpur was sacked: current "around 1520–21"; Rizvi 926/1520 for the third invasion (line 68);
   the *Babur Bani* gloss 1521 (line 360); Dhillon no year (line 488).
2. Why it is called Rori Sahib: current, rest/meditation on pebbles; Qaiser, the place of the arrest
   (line 333).
3. Capture as history (Rizvi, line 43) or legend (Dhillon, line 438); Khalid's Janamsakhi meeting with
   Babur (line 367).
4. When the gurdwara was built: current, under Ranjit Singh, multi-storey; Qaiser, "around 1922-23",
   before that a small shrine, only the gateway by Bhai Ram Singh; Khalid, the main shrine "an ordinary
   room" (lines 328–329). **Qaiser's "It" may mean only the gateway. The draft quotes his words and does
   not resolve this.**
5. State after 1947: current, fire damage and neglect; Qaiser, standing but unprotected, buffalo market,
   now restored (line 331).
6. Town names: Eminabad/Saidpur (current, Khalid) against "Saiyidpur (Amanabad)" (Rizvi) against
   "امن آباد" (Bhandari). Two accounts of the renaming in Khalid: Babur (p. 115) and Eni (p. 116).
7. Where Mardana died: Baghdad (Khalid p. 288); Baghdad or Kartarpur (Khalid p. 304); before Nanak
   (Dhillon p. 257); nothing (Rizvi).
8. The Guru's dates: 1469–1539 (current, Khalid, Dhillon) against Bhandari's Samvat 1526 / 880 AH and death
   under Salim Shah at seventy to eighty.
9. Number of journeys: five or four (Dhillon), twenty-four years (Khalid) against twenty-three (Dhillon).
