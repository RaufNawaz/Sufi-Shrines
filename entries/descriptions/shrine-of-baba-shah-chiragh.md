## Overview

Set beside the Lahore High Court on The Mall, in the shadow of the colonial-era Aiwan-e-Auqaf building that now bears its name, the shrine of Baba Shah Chiragh commemorates Syed Abdul Razzaq, a Sufi of the Qadiri lineage who traced his descent to Sheikh Abdul Qadir Gilani of Baghdad and whose family originated from Uch in what is now Bahawalpur. Given the honorific "Chiragh," meaning lamp, by his grandfather Hazrat Abdul Qadir Sani, he flourished during the reign of the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan and died in 1657 CE. His mausoleum was constructed by imperial order of Aurangzeb, and an adjoining mosque was added later, in 1716, under the patronage of Lahore's Mughal viceroy Zakariya Khan during the reign of Muhammad Shah Rangeela.

The mausoleum, built of limestone on a square plan in restrained Mughal style, contains eight graves in total, including those of Shah Chiragh's father and grandfather, making it a multi-generational family shrine rather than a single tomb. Its façades feature cusped-arch niches and panelled cartouches, while the interior retains traces of floral fresco decoration. In the late nineteenth century the surrounding precinct was absorbed into the grand Indo-Saracenic Aiwan-e-Auqaf complex, built by the British administration between 1875 and 1880, which today houses the offices and library of the Auqaf Department, the same body that has administered the shrine since 1973.

Three nineteenth-century Lahore books describe the tomb. Mufti Ghulam Sarwar Lahori's *Hadiqat al-Awliya* gives the saint his own notice, as "سید عبد الرزاق المشہور شاہ چراغ قادری لاہوری", and its appendix places his grave in Lahore "متصل ہائی کورٹ", adjoining the High Court (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 287, folio 275). Kanhaiya Lal's *Tarikh-e Lahore* names him Sayyid Abdul Razzaq bin Sayyid Abdul Wahhab Gilani, with Shah Chiragh as his title, and describes the tomb, its eight graves and its mosque (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Maulvi Nur Ahmad Chishti's *Tahqiqat-e Chishti* describes the tomb as its author saw it on a visit of his own (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 110).

The same books also name other Lahore men called Chiragh or Abd al-Razzaq, and none of them settles how these men relate to one another. Kanhaiya Lal himself gives two accounts: in one he treats Shah Chiragh and Abd al-Razzaq as a single man; in another he keeps a Sayyid Chiragh Shah Gilani, whose quarter stood where the Chief Court building was going up, apart from a Sayyid Abd al-Razzaq who was a khalifa of Mauj Darya Bukhari (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 79–80, folios 82–83). What follows gives each book's account as that book's own, and sets the other men of the name in a section of their own.

## The Life of the Saint

**Name and descent.** Lahori opens his notice, no. 28 in his book, with "سید عبد الرزاق المشہور شاہ چراغ قادری لاہوری" and calls him one of the shaikhs of the Qadiriyya: "مشائخ قادریہ میں سے یہ بزرگ صاحب عبادت و ریاضت و زہد و تقویٰ جامع علوم ظاہری و…" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 66, folio 54). His father, Lahori writes, was Sayyid Abdul Wahab, son of Sayyid Abdul Qadir Thalith, son of Muhammad Ghaus Bala Pir, son of Zain al-Abidin, son of Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani, son of Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Uchi Halabi Gilani. That makes him the fifth generation of the Uch Gilani family whose earlier members fill the notices before his own (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56). Chishti gives the chain as "عبدالرزاق المشہور شاہ چراغ بن سید عبدالوہاب بن سید عبدالقادر ثالث" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108). On the next page he names "سید عبدالقادر ثانی [OCR?]" and makes "عبدالوہاب" a son of "جناب محمد غوث بالا پیر [OCR?]", with "سید عبدالرزاق المشہور شاہ چراغ" as Abd al-Wahhab's son (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109). Chishti learned his account, he says, from Hazrat Pir Nizam al-Din Shah Gilani: "[illegible] نام حضرت پیر نظام الدین شاہ گیلانی سے معلوم ہوا" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108).

Elsewhere Chishti places the saint in the same family line: "حضرت شاہ چراغ صاحب و عبد القادر ثالث کہ جنکو مزارات نواح لاہور میں زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہیں انکی اولاد میں سے ہیں". He adds that both are descended from Abd al-Qadir Sani, a son of Hazrat Muhammad Ghaus, and that their lives are told at length elsewhere in his book: "حال اونکا بتفصیل مختلفہ درج کتاب ہذا ہے" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 527).

Kanhaiya Lal writes: "اصلی نام اس بزرگ کا سید عبد الرزاق بن سید عبد الوہاب گیلانی تہا اور شاہ چراغ خطاب تہا". His real name, that is, was Sayyid Abdul Razzaq bin Sayyid Abdul Wahhab Gilani, and Shah Chiragh was his title. Because the family's correct genealogy joins that of Piran-e-Pir Muhi-ud-Din, he adds, people hold them in great respect: "چونکہ نسب نامہ صحیح اس خاندان کا پیران پیر محی الدین کے ساتہ ملتا ہے لوگ اُنکا بہت ادب کرتے ہین" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284).

**The lamp of the house.** Lahori tells how the name came about. On the day the child was born, his father said that a lamp had been born into their house, by which the whole family would be lit, and so he was named Shah Chiragh:

"جس روز یہ پیدا ہوئے ان کے والد بزرگوار نے فرمایا کہ یہ فرزند ہمارے گھر کا ایک چراغ پیدا ہوا ہے جس سے تمام خاندان روشن ہو گا۔ ان کا نام شاہ چراغ مقرر ہوا" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56).

Another of Lahori's notices calls him "حضرت شاہ چراغ گیلانی لاہوری", pairing the family nisba Gilani with Lahori (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 267, folio 255). Whether that notice means this man is discussed below.

**From Uch to Lahore.** Kanhaiya Lal makes the move from Uch in two stages. The saint's elders came from qasba Uch, in the territory of Bahawalpur State, to qasba Satghara [OCR?] in the Montgomery district, and from there came to live in Lahore: "بزرگ اسکے قصبہ اُوچ علاقہ ریاست بہاولپور سے آکر قصبہ ستگہرہ [OCR?] ضلع منٹگمری مین اور یہان سے لاہور مین آکر سکونت پذیر ہوئے" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti says that Muhammad Ghaus Bala Pir settled "ایک محلہ رسول آباد [OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).

**Travel and pilgrimage.** Lahori says he loved travel and journeyed through many countries "بطور تجربہ", by way of experience, and that he also went to the Haramain Sharifain, the two holy cities (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56).

**A marriage refused.** Lahori records that Shah Jahan was wholly devoted to him and tried more than once to give one of his daughters in marriage to him or to his son, Sayyid Mustafa. The offer was not accepted:

"شاہ جہان بادشاہ ان کا کامل معتقد تھا۔ اس نے ہر چند چاہا کہ ایک اپنی لڑکی ان کے یا ان کے فرزند سید مصطفٰی کے ساتھ منسوب کرے۔ قبول نہ کیا" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56).

The book's index lists the son as "مصطفیٰ ابن شاہ چراغ لاہوری | ۵۶" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 330, folio 318). Chishti also tells of a promised marriage that he declined: "مگر آپ نے قبول [OCR?] نہ [OCR?] فرمایا" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108).

**A pir with a disciple.** Lahori's notice of Shah Dargahi Qadri calls him a murid of "حضرت شاہ چراغ گیلانی لاہوری", a man given to visions and miracles, sincerity and purity, abstinence and discipline: "یہ بزرگ حضرت شاہ چراغ گیلانی لاہوری کے مرید صاحب کشف و کرامات و صدق و صفا و زہد و ریاضت تھے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 267, folio 255). Lahori places that disciple's grave beside Shah Ismail Muhaddith's, with a well whose cure became a standing Lahore ritual (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 267, folio 255). The book does not say in that notice when the pir died or where he is buried. Kanhaiya Lal also makes Dargahi Shah the murid of a "سید شاہ چراغ", already mentioned earlier in his book (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 299, folio 302). Neither book settles that this pir is the Shah Chiragh of the Mall.

**Death.** Lahori gives the date in words: "بائیسویں ذی قعدہ سن ایک ہزار اڑسٹھ ہجری", the twenty-second of Zi Qada, 1068 AH, the year that corresponds to the 1657 of the account above. He adds, "اور روضہ حضرت کا لاہور میں ہے", and his tomb is in Lahore (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56). Kanhaiya Lal gives the same day and month, but the year cannot be read in the printed text: "وفعات اسکی ۲۲ ماہ ذی قعدہ [illegible] ہجری مین واقع ہوئی" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti gives two dates. Beside a sentence whose year is illegible, "سنہ [OCR?] ایکہزار [OCR?] [illegible] میں وفات [OCR?] انکی واقع ہوئی", the margin carries "۱۰۶۰ [OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108). On the next page he gives the day of death as the seventeenth of Rabi al-Awwal and the year as 1092: "روز فوت انکا ۱۷ [OCR?] ربیع الاول ، سالِ وفات [illegible] سنہ [margin] ۱۰۹۲ [OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).

**A chronogram.** With that second date Chishti copies a chronogram on the saint's death. He attributes it to "مفتی غلام سرور مصنفِ تذکرہ", Mufti Ghulam Sarwar, the author of a tazkira. It opens "از فیض یافتہ [illegible]" and ends with the line "گفت سرور میر شمس العارفین", and Chishti gives its year again as "سنہ [margin] ۱۰۹۲ [OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109). Most of the verse cannot be read in the printed text, and the book gives no translation of it.

## The Shrine

**Where it stands.** Lahori's appendix lists the tomb as "سید عبدالرزاق المشہور شاہ چراغ قادری لاہوری | لاہور | متصل ہائی کورٹ": Lahore, adjoining the High Court. The appendix gives no date for him (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 287, folio 275). Kanhaiya Lal explains why the tomb stands where it does. The spot was the quarter of Langar Khan Baloch, who was a murid of the family, and that is why the tomb was built there: "اس موقع پر جہان اب یہ مقبرہ ہے محلہ لنگر خان بلوچ کا تہا اور وہ انکا مرید تہا اس سبب سے یہ مقبرہ اسی موقع پر بنا" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti names the area "لنگر جاں [OCR?]" on one page (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108) and "محلہ لنگر خاں" on the next (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109). He sets the tomb by a road and by the kothi of a McLeod, with the Accounts office to the west: "یہ مقبرہ [illegible] سڑک [OCR?] [illegible] رویہ لاہور و [illegible] رویہ کوٹھی جناب مکلوڈ [OCR?] صاحب بہادر کے واقع", west "دفتر اکونٹ [OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108).

Kanhaiya Lal's contents list the tomb at printed page ۲۸۴ among the Islamic buildings outside the city (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 4, folio 3), and list a "محلہ شاہ چراغ" at printed page ۸۲ in the book's second part (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 2).

**The founding.** Kanhaiya Lal writes that the rauza was built by order of Alamgir Aurangzeb: "یہ روضہ عالمگیر اورنگ زیب کے حکم سے تعمیر ہوا" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti dates the building to the time of "عالمگیر بادشاہ", and in the same passage writes "حسب الحکم عالمگیر ثانی [OCR?]", by order of Alamgir the Second (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).

**The fabric.** In Kanhaiya Lal's description the tomb is a square, pakka building of lime, its door facing south, with a roof of brick and a grand dome above (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti notes a wooden door with a lattice (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108). He also saw an inscription, "لا الہ الا اللہ محمد رسول اللہ سنہ ۱۲۶۸ [OCR?]", the profession of faith with a year (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 110).

**The graves.** Kanhaiya Lal counts eight graves inside, one of Shah Chiragh and seven of his offspring: "مقبرے کے اندر آٹہہ قبرین ہین ایک تو شاہ چراغ کی اور سات انکی اولاد کی" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti names two graves, Shah Chiragh's and that of "زین العابدین صاحبزادہ" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109). He also describes a walled enclosure, a chardiwari, holding "۳۴ [OCR?] قبریں", thirty-four graves, with a well, trees and a broken floor, and adds of the ground, "بطور باغیچہ ہوگا" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).

**The mosque and a woman's bequest.** Kanhaiya Lal writes that the mosque was built by order of Khan Bahadur Zakariya Khan, because his mother was a murida of this family. She had willed that her grave should be here and that a mosque should be built from her jewellery: "خان بہادر زکریا خان کے حکم سے … کیونکہ اُسکی والدہ اس خاندان کی مریدہ تہی اور اُس نے وصیت کی تہی کہ میری قبر اس جگہ ہو اور میرے زیور سے مسجد تعمیر کروا دی [OCR?] جائے" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti describes a mosque "جسکے پانچ گنبد چار حجرہ", with five domes and four cells, built by "نواب خان بہادر [illegible]", the rest of the name unreadable. He saw the grave of the "والدہ خان بہادر نواب", the Nawab's mother, under a tree (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109). Neither book gives the year of the mosque.

**Arsenal, kothi and office.** Both books record what became of the mosque after Mughal rule. Kanhaiya Lal writes that before English rule the Sikhs had put something into it, the word illegible: "قبل عملداری انگریزی کے اس مسجد مین سکہون نے [illegible] ڈالا ہوا تہا". When the English took over it was made a kothi. A Major [OCR?], a Dwyderburn [OCR?] Sahib, a Summ [OCR?] Sahib Bahadur and Deputy Commissioners lived in it, and then the Accountant's office was set up there: "جب انگریزون کی عملداری ہوئی تو اسکو کوٹہی بنا لیا گیا اور مسجد میجر [OCR?] صاحب ڈویڈربن [OCR?] صاحب و سمم [OCR?] صاحب بہادر و ڈپٹی کمشنران اسمین رہتے رہے پہر اکونٹنٹ کا دفتر اُس مین مقرر ہو گیا" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 282, folio 285). On the page before, he says it now houses the government office of the Accountant and the Civil Auditor: "اب سرکاری دفتر صاحب اکونٹنٹ و سول ابیڈیٹر [OCR?] کا رہتا ہے" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti writes that a mosque "اب دفتر اکونٹس [OCR?] میں آگئی ہے" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109), and has been so "کئی [OCR?] سالہ سال سے", for many years (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108).

**Two enclosures.** By Kanhaiya Lal's day the kothi and mosque had their own enclosure, separate from the tomb's, and the tomb was in the possession of the Sadat Gilani: "اب کوٹہی [OCR?] مسجد کا احاطہ علیحدہ ہے اور مقبرہ کا علیحدہ اور مقبرے پر قبضہ سادات گیلانی کا ہے" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 282, folio 285). He reads the complex as one split in two by colonial occupation, the mosque taken into government use as an office and the tomb left in the hands of the saint's own sayyid lineage (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 281–282, folios 284–285).

**Keepers and lands.** Chishti names a living sajjada nashin, "حضرت ثنا [OCR?] شاہ". A faqir, "موصی [OCR?] شاہ", had long held the *ʿurs*, taken the income and served as khadim (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 110). There was a broken enclosure, "مسکونہ فقیر", where a faqir lived. Before British rule, in the time of the Sikhs, the mosque and the rest had land, the passage partly illegible: "قبل از عملداری صاحبان عالیشان بعہد سکھان". Little of that land, Chishti says, was left in his day (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).

**Another reading of the site.** In his account of Lahore's quarters, Kanhaiya Lal tells of a Sayyid Chiragh Shah Gilani whose quarter lay east of Mauj Darya Bukhari's. Gilani sayyids lived there, "سادات گیلانی اسمیں سکونت رکھتی تھی". It was settled in the time of "شاہ جہاں شاہ [OCR?]" and, in his words, "جاگیر آباد ہوا"; it was later laid waste by plunderers (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 79, folio 82). He places that quarter "یہ محلا اُس موقع آباد تھا جس جگہ اب محکمہ چیف کورٹ کی عمارت بنی شروع ہوئی ہے", where the Chief Court building had begun to go up. Of its buildings he writes "سید چراغ شاہ کا مقبرہ و مسجد پختہ اب تک موجود ہے", Sayyid Chiragh Shah's tomb and pakka mosque stand to this day. The mosque, "مسجد تو سرکاری قبضہ میں ہو", is in government possession, and the tomb is held by the tomb-holder's descendants, "مقبرہ یہ صاحب مقبرہ کی اولاد کا قبضہ ہو" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 79, folio 82). The place answers to the shrine's own location near the High Court, but in this account Kanhaiya Lal does not call the man Abd al-Razzaq, and he gives no death date (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 79–80, folios 82–83).

## Devotion and Practice

**Royal offerings.** Kanhaiya Lal writes that under Muslim rule the family stood very high and the sultans of the time offered lakhs of rupees at their shrine: "مسلمانی عملداری کیوقت اُنکا بڑا عروج تہا سلاطین وقت لاکہون روپیہ انکی نذر پر چڑہاتے [OCR?] تہے" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284).

**The yearly fair.** Kanhaiya Lal records a mela held here every year on the seventeenth of Rabi-us-Sani, when great crowds gather: "ہر سال یہان میلہ ربیع الثانی کی ستروین تاریخ کو ہوتا ہے اور خلقت بہت جمع ہوتی ہے" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 282, folio 285). Chishti dates the *ʿurs* to Rabi al-Thani, but the day cannot be read: "عرس انکا [illegible] ربیع الثانی [OCR?] کو" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108).

**Thursdays.** Chishti writes that every Thursday something sounds out at the tomb of Hazrat Shah Chiragh: "مقبرہ حضرت شاہ چراغ پر ہر روز پنجشنبہ خوب [illegible] بجتی ہے". The word naming what was played is illegible in the printed text (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).

**An author's visit.** Chishti went to the tomb himself: "کمترین بھی خود ہی اس مقبرہ پر حاضر ہو کر" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 110).

## Other Men of the Name

Lahore's books know several men called Chiragh, and several called Abd al-Razzaq. Some lived in the same part of the city as the Shah Chiragh of the Mall, and the books do not cross-refer them. They are set out here as the books give them. None of them is identified with this shrine's saint unless a book says so.

**Kanhaiya Lal's second Abd al-Razzaq.** In the same account of the quarters, Kanhaiya Lal describes a Sayyid Abd al-Razzaq of Mohalla Dola Wari. He was a khalifa of Mauj Darya Bukhari and was the first to settle there, after which the quarter flourished: "سید عبد الرزاق کی جو ایک بزرگ سید موج دریا بخاری کے خلیفوں میں سے تھا سب سے اول یہان آکر سکونت پذیر ہوا اور رونق آبادی کی بڑھ گئی" (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 80, folio 83). The quarter kept its standing to the end of the Chughtai sultanate, because some holy man always lived in its khanqah: "کوئی نہ کوئی بزرگ ایسا قیام پذیر رہتا جسکے سے تمام مزارہ [OCR?] کو فیض پہنچتا تھا". His pakka rauza stands, called the Tibela [OCR?] Gumbad, "ایک تو روضہ پختہ سید عبد الرزاق کی کا موجود ہے جسکو بازار اُرار [OCR?] گلی میں ٹبیلہ [OCR?] گنبد کہتے ہین", with a pakka mosque inside its enclosure (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 80, folio 83). Under Ranjit Singh both tomb and mosque were kept full of gunpowder, and iron shot was made in the mosque courtyard: "مقبرہ اور مسجد دونو میں بارود [OCR?] بہری رہتی تھی اور مسجد کے صحن میں لوہا آہنی گولی بنانے تھے". The English emptied both. When the cantonment left Anarkali, the meat contractor Munshi Najm al-Din took the mosque and tomb from the government, "مسجد و مقبرہ منشی نجم الدین ٹہیکہ دار گوشت نے سرکار سے لے لیا". He repaired the mosque and opened it again, and a grave was made in the tomb (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 80, folio 83). Kanhaiya Lal gives this man no silsila and no death date, and in this account he never calls him Chiragh (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 80, folio 83).

**Chishti's Abd al-Razzaq near "کوٹھی شاہ[OCR?] چراغ".** Chishti gives a chapter of its own to "فصل در بیان مقبرہ حضرت عبد الرزاق" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 154). Its subject is "نام انکا عبد الرزاق [illegible] سید سہروردی[OCR?]", known by another name: "نام انکا اگرچہ سیّد عبد الرزاق ہی مگر سیّد لگی[OCR?] کرکے مشہور میں". He first gave his allegiance to Hazrat Mauj Darya, "اول ارادت انکی سجادت[OCR?] حضرت موج دریا". After Mauj Darya's death he worshipped by night at his tomb and rested by day where his own tomb now stands, and he was buried in that hujra "حسب وصیت", as he had willed. He died "بروز پنجشنبہ", on a Thursday, "سنہ ایک ہزار چوراسی[OCR?] ہجری[OCR?]" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 157). His tomb stood by "کوٹھی شاہ[OCR?] چراغ", its walls new (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 154). In Chishti's telling the tomb was built through "چندہ", a subscription, with a large mosque beside it from what was left over (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 155). Later an order said the building would be seized unless it was put in order within two months, and the "سوداگران بلی[OCR?]" raised "زر چندہ" for it. He also tells of a dream in which Abd al-Razzaq appeared to the manager of the building, but the text breaks off (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, pp. 157–158). Chishti also places the enclosure of Haji Muhammad Saeed "بطرف مغرب مقبرہ[OCR?] شاہ چراغ صاحب رحمۃ اللہ علیہ", to the west of Shah Chiragh's tomb (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 158).

**Lahori's Shah Abd al-Razzaq Makki.** Lahori has a notice of a "شاہ عبدالرزاق مکی", a murid of Mauj Darya, whose tomb is in Anarkali bazar and who died in "ایک ہزار چوراسی". He bears the saint's personal name and lived in the saint's quarter of Lahore, but he had a different pir and died in a different year (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 269, folio 257). Lahori's index carries "عبد الرزاق شاہ مکی لاہوری | ۲۰ ، ۲۵۷" among four separate Sayyid Abd al-Razzaqs (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 322, folio 310). Kanhaiya Lal's contents list a separate "مقبرہ عبدالرزاق مکی[OCR?]" at printed page ۲۸۹ (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 4, folio 3).

**Other Chiraghs.** Lahori's notice no. 238 is of a "مزار چراغ شاہ" outside the Roshnai Gate, the successor of a Sayyid Ali Gilani who died in "ایک ہزار دو سو ستائیس" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 275, folio 263). His index files "شاہ چراغ لاہوری (عبدالرزاق) | ۵۵" and "چراغ شاہ لاہوری | ۲۶۲" as separate lines (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 316, folio 304). It also names a grandfather of a Shah Chiragh of Delhi, "عبد اللطیف (جد شاہ چراغ دہلی) | ۸۵" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 322, folio 310). Chishti tells of a "حضرت شاہ چراغ صاحب قادری سلطان پوری" who looked on Nur Husain with great kindness, "بدرجہ غایت نظرِ مہربانی" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 151). Kanhaiya Lal names a living "سید چراغ شاہ حکیم" of the tenth enclosure in his account, a man with murids in the Chishtiya and the Qadiriya (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 296, folio 299). He also names a "شیخ عبد الرزاق مکی" of the Nila Gumbad, who came from Mecca [OCR?] in Humayun's time and died in 1085 [OCR?] AH (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 286, folio 289).

**A son of the Ghaus.** Chishti's chain of Mian Mir names a "سید عبد الرزاق خلف الصدق حضرت غوث الاعظم", a murid of his father, with a Shaikh Qarshi [OCR?] as his own murid (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 299). The passage concerns the chain and does not speak of a tomb on the Mall.

## Setting

The books speak of the family's origins, of the patron of the mosque and of the colonial city around the tomb. Their words concern those people and places, not the shrine itself.

**The Gilanis of Uch.** Lahori writes that the first of the Qadiri line to come to the Punjab was Hazrat Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Uchi Jili Gilani: "حضرت کے سلسلہ میں سے حضرت سید محمد غوث اوچی جیلی گیلانی اول پنجاب میں آئے". His ancestors had left Baghdad in the time of Hulagu Khan. He came "خراسان کے راستے ملتان میں", by way of Khurasan to Multan, and settled "اوچ کے مقام پر", at Uch. A modern footnote by the editor dates his arrival at Uch, citing Ali Asghar Gilani: "بقول علی اصغر گیلانی آپ ۸۸۷ھ میں اوچ میں داخل ہوئے" (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 40, folio 28). Shah Chiragh's descent, in Lahori's notice, runs back to this man.

**Zakariya Khan.** Kanhaiya Lal names the governors "نواب ذکریا خان بہادر و شہنواز خان وغیرہ حکام پنجاب" as living in Mughalpura (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 90, folio 93). He also copied a tile inscription on the north mihrab of another mosque Zakariya Khan built, west of Madho Lal Hussain's mazar. The inscription dates the building "در دور شاہ محمد شاہ", in the reign of Muhammad Shah, and its verse names the builder:

زبدۂ بارگاہ او نواب
زکریا خان صوبہ پنجاب

*The verse names its builder, Nawab Zakariya Khan, governor of the Punjab* (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 262–263, folios 265–266).

**The Chief Court and the Lieutenant-Governors.** Kanhaiya Lal describes the Chief Court of his day as the court that gave the final decision in civil and criminal cases. Appeals from the commissionerships of the Punjab came before it, "کمشنریان صوبہ پنجاب کا اپیل اسی محکمہ میں ہوتا ہی", and capital sentences needed its sanction (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 60, folio 63). He names a "مکلوڈ [OCR?] صاحب بہادر لفٹننٹ گورنر سابق پنجاب", a McLeod who had been Lieutenant-Governor of the Punjab (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 213, folio 216). This is the same name Chishti gives to the kothi beside the tomb.

**The Mall today.** Haroon Khalid, writing of a Sunday drive in Lahore, calls Mall Road "one of the busiest boulevards in the city" and writes that "The Lahore High Court, combining European and Eastern architectural techniques, stands close to the Panorama Market" (Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 100, folio not stated). Khalid does not mention the shrine.

## Where the Sources Disagree

- **The year of his death.** The current account gives 1657 CE. Lahori gives 22 Zi Qada 1068 AH, the year that corresponds to 1657 (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56). Kanhaiya Lal gives 22 Dhu al-Qa'da with the year illegible (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti carries 1060 [OCR?] in the margin beside a sentence whose year is illegible (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108), and gives 1092 [OCR?] on the next page, with the chronogram (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).
- **The day of his death.** Lahori and Kanhaiya Lal give the twenty-second of Dhu al-Qa'da (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56; Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti gives the seventeenth of Rabi al-Awwal (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).
- **Who called him Chiragh.** The current account says the honorific was given by his grandfather, Hazrat Abdul Qadir Sani. Lahori says his father gave him the name on the day he was born (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56). Kanhaiya Lal calls Shah Chiragh his *khitab*, his title, and does not say who gave it (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284).
- **His grandfather.** The current account makes Hazrat Abdul Qadir Sani his grandfather. Lahori makes his grandfather Sayyid Abdul Qadir Thalith and places Abdul Qadir Sani four generations above his father (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 68, folio 56). Chishti also names Abd al-Qadir Thalith as his grandfather (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108). On his next page, though, Abd al-Wahhab is a son of Muhammad Ghaus Bala Pir (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).
- **Who lies in the tomb.** The current account counts eight graves, including those of his father and grandfather. Kanhaiya Lal counts eight: his own and seven of his offspring (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti names two, his own and that of a sahibzada, Zain al-Abidin (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).
- **Which Alamgir built it.** The current account and Kanhaiya Lal credit Aurangzeb (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). Chishti dates it to "عالمگیر بادشاہ" and writes "حسب الحکم عالمگیر ثانی [OCR?]", by order of Alamgir the Second (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 109).
- **One man or two.** Kanhaiya Lal's account of the tomb makes Sayyid Abdul Razzaq and Shah Chiragh one man (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 281, folio 284). His account of the quarters keeps a Sayyid Chiragh Shah Gilani, at the Chief Court site, apart from a Sayyid Abd al-Razzaq of Mohalla Dola Wari, a khalifa of Mauj Darya Bukhari (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, pp. 79–80, folios 82–83). Lahori and Chishti both call the saint Abd al-Razzaq known as Shah Chiragh (Lahori, *Hadiqat al-Awliya*, p. 66, folio 54; Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 108).
- **Who held the tomb.** Kanhaiya Lal's account of the tomb gives possession to the Sadat Gilani (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 282, folio 285). His account of the quarters gives it to the tomb-holder's own descendants (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 79, folio 82). Chishti names a sajjada nashin, Sana [OCR?] Shah, and a faqir, Musi [OCR?] Shah, who held the *ʿurs* and took the income (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 110). The current account says the Auqaf Department has administered it since 1973.
- **What became of a mosque.** Kanhaiya Lal tells two histories of a mosque attached to the name. In one, the Sikhs used it and the English made it a kothi for officers and then the Accountant's office (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 282, folio 285). In the other, belonging to the Abd al-Razzaq of Dola Wari, it was a gunpowder store under Ranjit Singh and was later taken from the government by a meat contractor (Kanhaiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore*, p. 80, folio 83).

## Legacy

Though it lacks the international renown of some of Lahore's other Sufi sites, the shrine is formally catalogued as a protected heritage monument by the Punjab Directorate General of Archaeology. Its distinctive Mughal fabric, set incongruously amid the bureaucratic buildings of modern Lahore, makes it a quietly significant survival from the city's seventeenth-century religious landscape.

An independent English-language academic study of Sufis and pre-colonial Muslim rulers in India, consulted separately from Alam Faqri's tazkira, states in passing that "Aurangzeb gave importance to some other Shari minded Qadri sufi, like Shah Chiragh whose tomb was constructed by Aurangzeb". This corroborates, from an independent scholarly source, the claim that Aurangzeb ordered the mausoleum built. The study's own full citation details have not been recovered, and they should be verified against the original before the citation is treated as fully resolved. A general compendium of Pakistani Sufi saints notes, in its entry on the nearby shrine of Mauj Darya Bukhari, that Mauj Darya Bukhari's own grave lies inside Shah Chiragh's mausoleum complex. Chishti, in his own account of Mauj Darya, also refers to the inside of Shah Chiragh's tomb, in a sentence too damaged to read in full: "اندرون [OCR?] مقبرہ حضرت شاہ چراغ [OCR?] کرہی [OCR?] تھی" (Chishti, *Tahqiqat-e Chishti*, p. 104).
