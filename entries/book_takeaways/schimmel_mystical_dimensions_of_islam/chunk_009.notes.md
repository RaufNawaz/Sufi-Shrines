# schimmel_mystical_dimensions_of_islam — chunk 009 — pp. 161–178

**What this chunk is.** Eighteen consecutive pages of continuous doctrinal exposition inside
chapter 3, whose running header throughout is `THE PATH` (printed with letter-spacing that varies
page to page: `THE P A T H`, `THE PATH`, and once `THE pATH`). The chunk opens mid-sentence
(`moment in which a certain mystical state is granted to the Sufi.`, p. 161) and closes
mid-sentence (`Sarraj, though rather close to the beginnings of Sufi speculation,`, p. 178), so
both ends run into the neighbouring chunks. The section heading `LOVE AND ANNIHILATION` is printed
**part-way down p. 161**, below the end of the preceding section, not at the head of a fresh page.
The material runs from *maḥabba* and *maʿrifa*, through the stages of love (*uns*, *qurb*, *shauq*),
suffering and the martyr-lover, the *ʿishq* controversy and *ḥubb ʿudhrī*, into *fanāʾ* and *baqāʾ*,
*shaṭḥiyāt*, and *tauḥīd*. No plate, no blank page, no chapter opening.

**Archive yield is low, as forecast, but it is not zero.** Two archive ids are carried, both on the
person rule and both on named, quoted material: **`data-darbar`** (Hujwīrī, cited twelve times and
named as an authority in his own voice on four of them) and **`mazar-e-iqbal`** (Muhammad Iqbal,
quoted for a doctrine, p. 170). **No toponym fires ruling (a) in this chunk.** Both `Lahore` hits
and both `Hyderabad` hits are publisher's imprint cities inside footnotes and are **declined** under
brief §6(ii) — the 35 Lahore ids and the 1 Hyderabad id are listed under `## Doubts`, on one line
each, so a reversal is one step.

**Folio regime — all eighteen pages print a header folio, and parity passes 18/18.** Every page of
pp. 161–178 carries the folio inside the running-header line; not one is a chapter- or
section-opening page, so the brief's foot-of-page case does not arise anywhere here and no page
takes `folio not stated`. Leading (verso) folios 130, 132, 134, 136, 138, 140, 142, 144, 146 are all
**even**; trailing (recto) folios 131, 133, 135, 137, 139, 141, 143, 145, 147 are all **odd**.
`folio = PDF − 31` holds on all eighteen without exception. One header only is digit-damaged:
p. 163 prints `1J2` [text-layer?: 132], read here against the folio rule and the leading-even parity
rather than guessed — and note that `J` for `3` is **not** in the brief's measured substitution
table (the table gives `J` only as a substitute for `1`). The headers are apparatus and are not
quoted below as content.

    [text-layer?: maʿrifa] (p. 161, folio 130). `H 267` is a page of the *Kashf al-Maḥjūb* in

  - His own classification of lovers, which Schimmel says Ghazzālī took over: `Abu Talib al-Makki
    knew nine aspects of love, and Hujwiri gives a classification of the lovers, which Ghazzali
    adopted as well, in slightly elaborated form: "Those who regard the favor and beneficence of God
    toward them and are led by that regard to love the Benefactor, and those who are so enraptured
    by love that they reckon all favors as a veil and by regarding the Benefactor are led to
    [consciousness of] His favors"` (cited by Schimmel as `H 308`) (p. 163, folio 132). The square

  - His own threefold *tauḥīd*, introduced in his own voice: `Hujwirl` [text-layer?: Hujwīrī]

    the saying `"I did not see anything without seeing God before it and after it and with it and in

    representing the four different approaches of one who confesses tauhld` [text-layer?: tauḥīd],

    [text-layer?: Wāsiʿ] `said: "I never saw anything without seeing God therein,"` (p. 178,

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)


- **Sarī as-Saqaṭī** — `His younger contemporary, Sari as-Saqati in Baghdad, who had first
  formulated the idea of mutual love between man and God` (p. 162, folio 131). His prayer, which
  Schimmel says Hujwīrī preserved: `It is the custom of God to let the hearts of those who love Him
  have vision of Him always, in order that the delight thereof may enable them to endure every
  tribulation; and they say in their orisons: We deem all torments more desirable than to be veiled
  from Thee. When Thy beauty is revealed to our heart, we take no thought of affliction.` (cited by
  Schimmel as `H 111; cf. A 10:120`) (p. 162, folio 131). **Reported by Hujwīrī, not spoken by him.**
- **Junayd** — `It was apparently Sari's nephew Junayd who discovered the truth: "Love between two
  is not right until the one addresses the other, 'O Thou I'"` (`T 2:29`) (p. 162, folio 131); his
  definition of *maʿrifa*, `the most poignant formulation`: `"Gnosis... is the hovering of the heart
  between declaring God too great to be comprehended, and declaring Him too mighty to be perceived.
  It consists in knowing that, whatever may be imagined in thy heart God is the opposite of it"`
  (`K 133`) (p. 161, folio 130); `"Love is the annihilation of the lover in His attributes and the
  confirmation of the Beloved in His essence"` (`H 40`) (p. 165, folio 134); and his long definition
  of unification beginning `Unification is this, that one should be a figure in the hands of God`
  and ending `that he should be as he was before he existed.` (`H 282-83`) (p. 177, folio 146). The
  two `(H …)` items are **reported by Hujwīrī, not his own**.
- **Sumnūn** — `no one could put this truth better than Sumnun, surnamed "the Lover": "A thing can
  be explained only by something that is subtler than itself. There is nothing subtler than love—by
  what, then, shall love be explained?"` (`H 173`) (p. 171, folio 140). **Reported by Hujwīrī.**
- **Muḥammad ibn Wāsiʿ** (`Muhammad ibn Wasic`) — `"I never saw anything without seeing God
  therein," which indicates an advanced stage of contemplation in which the mystic sees only the
  agent.` (p. 178, folio 147).

- **Ḥallāj** — the central martyr-figure of the chunk. `"outward" death was also an aim in itself,

  `anffl-Haqq` [text-layer?: anāʾl-Ḥaqq] among the `shathiyat` (pp. 176, folio 145). No date of his

- **Bāyezīd** (`Bayezid`) — the fourfold nature of love (`A 10:242`) (p. 163, folio 132); the boast

## Legends and miracles (karamat)


These are told by Schimmel as illustrations of doctrine rather than as shrine miracles, and are
attributed here exactly as she attributes them.

- The Sufi left in a corner and forgotten for a week, who on his master's apology answered `"Do not
  worry, God has taken away the fear of loneliness from His friend"` — told without a named subject,
  cited `N 94` (p. 163, folio 132).
- Ghazzālī's story of Abraham refusing the angel of death, until addressed `"Have you ever seen a
  lover who refuses to go to his beloved?"`, whereupon `he gladly submitted his soul to the angel` —
  `Ghazzali relates a touching story`, cited `G 4:253` (p. 166, folio 135).
- `that is why Hallaj danced in fetters when he was led to execution` (p. 167, folio 136).
- The *ḥadīth qudsī* `revealed to Shibli when he asked God about the meaning of Hallaj's martyrdom:
  "Whom My love kills, for him shall I be blood money."` — Schimmel calls it `a wonderful hadith
  qudsl` known to the Sufis and gives it no siglum (p. 167, folio 136).
- Ḥallāj's allegory of the moth and the candle, `symbolized, in Hallaj's Kitab at-tawdsin, as the
  way of the moth, which experiences cilm al-yaqm when it sees the light of the candle, cayn
  al-yaqin when it draws near and feels its heat, and haqq al-yaqin when it is, finally, burned and
  consumed by the flame.` (p. 173, folio 142).
- The Shiraz preacher and the lost donkey, `probably told first by cAttar (U 129) and retold by
  Iraqi`, ending `"Ho, thou that hadst an ass, lo, I have found thine ass. The cropper, quick!"`
  (p. 172, folio 141).
- The green water of life `which, according to the legends, is hidden in the deepest darkness` —
  Schimmel's own words, marking it as legend (p. 175, folio 144).
- The divine essence as `a salt mine, into which a dog, or a donkey, falls; thus, losing its low
  qualities, the animal is transformed into this element and preserved in it.` — used by `mystics
  from cAttar to the eighteenth-century Indian Mir Dard` (p. 176, folio 145).



## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)


- Schimmel's framing decision for the section: `We shall dwell here mainly upon those mystical
  currents in which love was praised as the highest possible state` (p. 161, folio 130).
- On the early controversy: `In early Sufism the problem of love was the central point of
  divergence. Orthodoxy accepted mahabba only as "obedience,"` (p. 162, folio 131), and
  `Before the tenth century, however, discussions about mystical love and longing were conducted
  mainly on the theoretical level. The question was one of semantics.` (p. 168, folio 137).
- She attributes the introduction of `cishq` to Nūrī (`who probably introduced the use of the word`)
  and its full doctrinal use to Ḥallāj, adopting Massignon's `"le desir essentiel"` (p. 168,
  folio 137).
- A first-person ethnographic aside, rare in this chunk: `until Fakhruddin clraqi poetically changed
  the words of the profession of faith into la ildha illffl-^ishq` [text-layer?: lā ilāha
  illāʾl-ʿishq]`, "there is no deity save Love," a formulation that I often heard from Turkish
  Sufis.` (p. 168, folio 137).
- She rejects the Manichaean reading for early love mysticism but concedes a later drift: `This
  interpretation is incorrect for the early stages of mystical love, but it points to a development
  that was inherent in Sufi love theories` (p. 169, folio 138).
- She rejects the *nirvāṇa* equation outright: `Some scholars have tried to equate fand with the
  Hindu or Buddhist concept of nirvana, but this is incorrect. It is not the experience of being
  freed from a painful circle of existence, since Islam lacks the idea of karma and accepts the
  reality of the individual soul.` (p. 173, folio 142).
- She prefers a German term to the English ones: `The German term Entwerden, as used by the medieval
  mystics, is closer to its meaning than words like "annihilation," "being naughted," or "passing
  away," since it is the opposite of "becoming," werden.` (p. 173, folio 142).
- She names a best reading: `The best interpretation of fand and the following stage, baqa, has been
  given by the Japanese scholar Toshihiko Izutsu` (p. 174, folio 143).
- On Koranic terminology: `These terms are taken from Sura 102 and Sura 56:95, where they have,
  however, no mystical connotations at all.` (p. 173, folio 142).
- On metaphor as method: `The metaphors of Islamic mysticism and philosophy are not merely poetic
  ornaments but are indicative of a peculiar way of thinking.` (p. 175, folio 144).
- On Western misreading — the closing argument of the chunk: `It is from these enthusiastic
  expressions of unity, found in most of the Persian mystics in more or less outspoken form, that
  Western scholars have gained the impression that Sufism is nothing but measureless pantheism.`
  (p. 178, folio 147).
- She notes that the suffering-imagery `has even caused misunderstanding among modern Islamic
  thinkers` (p. 168, folio 137) — the thinkers are not named, `(not stated)`.
- A tomb-adjacent note of Schimmel's own, thin but recorded because it is the only tomb reference in
  the chunk: footnote 34 opens `The same expression has also been used for the tomb.` — the
  expression being `ba-namak, "saltish"`, which `also means "cute, charming"` (p. 176, folio 145).
  This is imagery, not a practice.

## Doubts


**Archive mapping.**

- **`data-darbar` rests on Hujwīrī as a cited authority, not on shrine material.** This chunk names
  him, quotes his book twelve times, and gives four statements in his own voice — but it gives no
  city, no death year, no shrine and no practice. The mapping therefore stands on the 18 September
  person rule plus brief §3, and a consolidator should weigh it as author-level evidence. The
  chunk's evidence does **not** duplicate chunk 006's biographical passage; it adds doctrine only.
- **The `(H …)` citations are Hujwīrī's book, not always Hujwīrī's voice, and in this chunk the
  split is 4 / 5 / 3.** His own voice, carried as `data-darbar` material: `H 267` (p. 161), `H 308`
  (p. 163), `H 278` (p. 177), `H 91, 330` (p. 178). Reported by him and therefore **not** carried as
  `data-darbar` material: `H 111` (Sarī as-Saqaṭī, p. 162), `H 40` (Junayd, p. 165), `H 173`
  (Sumnūn, p. 171), `H 158` (an unnamed `earlier Sufi`, p. 177), `H 282-83` (Junayd, p. 177).
  **Three carry no named speaker at all** and Schimmel writes them in her own summarising voice —
  `H 148` on *qurb* (p. 164), `H 404` on love not diminishing by cruelty (p. 166), `H 398` on love
  as `a gift, not an acquisition` (p. 170). Those three are left unattached to any figure rather
  than assigned to Hujwīrī; a human should confirm that refusal.
- **`mazar-e-iqbal` is a one-sentence, author-only mapping.** The evidence is `He is, as Muhammad
  Iqbal has said in our day, "like us, a prisoner of Desire."` plus footnote 28 (p. 170, folio 139).
  The protocol names Iqbal explicitly as the model case for the 18 September rule, so it is mapped;
  but it is thin, and the row's `figure_died` of `1938` is **not** confirmed by anything in this
