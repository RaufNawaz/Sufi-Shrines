**Two-column interleaving — how it was handled.** The index is set in two columns and the text layer
interleaves them: one output line carries the tail or head of a left-column entry followed by the
tail or head of a right-column one. The brief's worked example is verbatim in this chunk at
p. 352 (folio 331), where one line reads `Earth and terrestrial realms (ard), 44-` followed on the
same line by `Ethics, 9, 21, 27, 50,101,161, 221-24, 230` — two different headwords, two different
page lists. Every entry recorded below was reconstructed by column, using the constant indents (left
column ≈ col. 78, right column ≈ col. 155, with the first line of each page's left column set flush
left), and each reconstruction was verified against alphabetical order. A further trap was found and
handled: **the top of each page's right column is the continuation of the bottom of that page's left
column**, so the extraction places a left-column entry's tail *above* its own headword in the file.
`Space` on p. 364 (folio 343) is the clearest case — the headword is the last left-column line on the
page and its remaining locators sit at the very top of the page's text block. No page list below
merges two columns. Where a run could not be assigned it is left under Doubts, unassigned.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

An index is apparatus, but **how it is organised is evidence of the argument**, and that is what is
recorded here. Nothing below is inferred from outside the seventeen pages.

- **`Body` is the book's largest entry and it is organised by verb, not by part.** `Body:` runs with
  eleven sub-entries — `burial of`, `death of`, `denigration of`, `discipline of`, `dualism with
  mind`, `engagement with`, `holistic view of`, `human`, `Islamic discourse about`, `movement of`,
  `regeneration of`, `release into`, `restraint of`, `techniques of`, closing `See also Dance;
  Embodiment; Rapture; Resurrection` (p. 350, folio 329). The entry occupies most of one column.
- **The index distinguishes three terms the book evidently does not treat as synonyms**:
  `Corporal dimension of body, 17-20, 89, 95-96, 120`; `Corporeal dimension of body, 17-20, 95, 127`;
  `Corporeality, 17-20, 51, 107, 265, 289` — all three on p. 351 (folio 330), all three sharing
  **17–20**. Whatever that distinction is, it is made on printed pp. 17–20.
- **Body parts are indexed individually and heavily**: `Anus`, `Belly`, `Bones` (with sub-entry
  `marrow, 9, 61`), `Brain`, `Breast milk and lactation`, `Breath`, `Chest` (with `rib of, 90-91`),
  `Ear`, `Eye`, `Face`, `Flesh and muscle`, `Foot and ankle`, `Genitalia` (with
  `circumcision, 70`), `Hair` (with `shaving, 84, 183, 199, 208-12`), `Hand and wrist` (with
  `of God, 30, 235, 314 (n. 48)`), `Head and skull`, `Heart` (with `inner moral core (fuad]`),
  `Knee`, `Lips`, `Liver and gut`, `Mouth`, `Navel`, `Organs, bodily`, `Pulse`, `Saliva`, `Semen`,
  `Skin`, `Spine`. This is the book's method visible in its apparatus.
- **The index's own headnote states the author's transliteration and gender policy**, and it is the
  only continuous prose in the chunk (p. 348, folio not stated) — quoted below.
- **Cross-references show three organising decisions worth recording**: `Pakistan. See South Asia`
  and `India. See South Asia` (pp. 361, 356) collapse both modern states into one regional
  category; `Awliya … See Saints, Muslim` (p. 349, folio 328) makes the English term primary and
  the Arabic secondary — as do `Death anniversary of saint` for ʿurs, `Sufi gathering place` for
  zawiya/khanqah, and `Masters, spiritual: chain or lineage of (silsila)` for silsila; and
  `Monism. See Being, oneness of` (p. 359, folio 338) refuses the Western term for
  `wahdat al-wujud`.
- **The reform chapter's locators cluster tightly**: `Ibn Abd al-Wahhab, Muhammad al-Najdi, 271-88`,
  `Kitab al-Tawhld … 273-88`, `Idols … 271, 273-76, 281-86, 290-91`, `Mushrik (one associating

  folio 339), and `Shrine … 271, 278, 280, 283, 291` all share printed pp. 271–291. An entry on any

## Doubts

**Headline caution, and it governs everything above.** An index is almost entirely bare numerals,
and a numeral in a page list has no surrounding words to constrain it. `2` at a line end may be a
truncated run; `161` may be `16l`; `221-2` may be `221-22`; `187-99` may be `187-199`. **No page
list from this index may be relied on without checking the printed book.** Nothing above has been
silently normalised: `59-6o`, `loo-no`, `i59-6i`, `24O-43`, `217-21`, `187-99` and `221-24` are
reproduced as printed, and no truncated list has been reconstructed.

**Mapping doubts.**

- **`Jamal, Shah, 186, 305 (n. 7)` (p. 357, folio 336) is mapped to `shah-jamal` on inference, not on
  what the index states.** The headword carries **no toponym, no dates, no order and no gloss**.
  The competing archive row is **`shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani`** (Hafiz Muhammad Jamal
  Multani, Multan), and the archive holds no other row whose principal figure is named "Shah Jamal".
  The evidence used is internal to the index — the locator **186** and the note **305 (n. 7)** both
  recur in the `Lahore` entry (p. 358, folio 337), and `305 (n. 7)` is shared with `Qadirl Sufi way`
  and `Qalandar or antinomian Sufi` — plus the standing fact that earlier chunks of this book
  already map `shah-jamal` from its Lahore chapter. **If a reviewer wants the index alone to carry
  the mapping, it cannot: move the bullet to "Other saints, sites and events" and the page list is
  unaffected.**
- **`Krishna, 189, 205-7, 210; worship of by Vallabhacharya sect, 206-7` (p. 358, folio 337) is left
  unmapped.** `Krishna` is the principal figure of **two** archive rows —
  **`krishna-mandir-kabari-bazar`** (Kabari Bazar, Rawalpindi) and **`krishna-mandir-ravi-road`**
  (Ravi Road, Lahore) — and the index attaches no site, temple or city to the headword. Refused, per
  the rule, rather than picked.
- **`Lahore` (p. 358, folio 337) takes no id under the interim bare-toponym convention.** If that
  ruling reverses, the rows it would bear on are, at minimum, the three this book already informs —
  **`data-darbar`**, **`madho-lal-hussain`**, **`shah-jamal`** — since its page list **181-83,
  185-86, 191, 194, 197-98, 211, 220** contains every locator those three entries give. The archive
  holds roughly two dozen further Lahore rows (`bibi-pak-daman`, `gurdwara-dera-sahib`,
  `shrine-of-mian-mir`, `shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari`, `shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh`,
  `krishna-mandir-ravi-road`, `mazar-e-iqbal`, `tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak` and others), **none of
  which the index's `Lahore` entry distinguishes**; a reversal of the ruling would therefore make
  this headword indeterminate across all of them, not usefully assignable to any.
- `Punjab` (p. 361, folio 340) and `Sindh` (p. 364, folio 343) likewise take no id; both are bare
  regions, and a reversal would make them indeterminate across dozens of rows apiece.
- **`Haydarabad (Hyderabad), xi, 312 (n. 64)` (p. 356, folio 335): the index does not say which
  Hyderabad.** The archive's Hyderabad row is `guru-gurpat-mandir-db-80-sirey-ghat` (Hyderabad,
  Sindh); the book's other Deccan material (`Awrangabad`, `Gulbarga`) suggests the Deccan city, but
  **the index states nothing** and no id is taken. Left unmapped, flagged here.
- **`Malik All, Kotwal (police chief of Lahore)` (p. 358, folio 337) is NOT the archive's
  `darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz`** (Malik Ahmad Ayaz, Shah Alam Market, Lahore). Different person,
  different gloss, no tomb or shrine attached. Recorded under "Other" and the refusal noted so the
  next reader does not assume the check was skipped. Likewise **`Musa Sada Sohag, 209`** (p. 360,
  folio 339) is **not** `shrine-of-syed-musa-pak` (Syed Musa Pak Shaheed, Multan) — the names
  differ and the index gives no location, dates or order; refused.
- **`Ranjha and Hlr, 207, 308 (n. 50)` (p. 362, folio 341) takes no id.** The archive row is
  `mausoleum-of-waris-shah`, whose principal figure is **Waris Shah**, and **Waris Shah is not a
  headword in this index's A–Sufism range**: **(not stated)**. The 18 Sep 2026 author-only ruling
  cannot be applied, because the index names the poem's characters, not the poet. If a reviewer
  judges that the characters stand in for the poet, the row it would take is
  **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`** and the page list is `207, 308 (n. 50)`.
- **`Shaktipeeth Shri Hinglaj Mata Mandir` does not arise.** No `Hinglaj` headword appears in range,
  so the archive's empty id cell for that row is not touched and **no id has been invented**.
  Recorded here only so the next reader knows the check was made.

**Other doubts.**

- **A printed ordering anomaly, not a reading error.** On p. 357 (folio 336) the left column runs
  `Insight`, `Inspiration (ilham)`, `Intention (niyyd)`, `Intercession (shafaa) and mediation`,
  `Intimacy`, **`Initiation (bay a)`**, `Iran` — `Initiation` is out of alphabetical order, sitting
  after `Intimacy` instead of before `Insight`. The lines are sequential in a single column, so this
  is not a column-interleave artifact of mine; it is either the printed index's own error or a
  reflow in the text layer. **A human should check p. 357 against the book.**
- **The brief circulated an illustrative form of the Shah Hussayn entry, `Hussayn, Shah, 181-220,
  236`. That is not what the page prints.** The printed entry is `181-214, 217-21` with nine note
  clusters and two sub-entries (p. 356, folio 335). The illustrative figure should not be carried
  into Pass 2.
- **`217-21` and `187-99` cannot be distinguished from truncations of `217-221` and `187-199` on the
  text layer alone**, and `Madho`'s `187-99` overlaps `Hussayn, Shah`'s `181-214` either way. Not
  normalised; flagged.

  `…, 296 (nn. 30, 40), 298 (nn. 24, 29), 303 (n. 41); Neo-Sufism, 268-69` (p. 364, folio 343) and

- **Roman-numeral locators appear in several entries** (`xi`, `xii`, `xiii`, `ix`, `x`) and refer to

  p. 357 (folio 336) the run `2IO, 212, 2I4-2O` inside `Jurists and judges, Islamic` — both are

  settled from the text layer.
