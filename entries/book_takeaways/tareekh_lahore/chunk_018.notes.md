# tareekh_lahore — chunk 018 — pp. 251–264

**A run of eleven buildings east and north-east of the walled city, described one by one: four mosques,
six tombs and one khanqah.** The chunk's centre of gravity is not hagiography but *property* — who built,
who seized, who auctioned, who repaired. Three archive rows appear, two of them only as landmarks. The
richest single seam is British-era disposal of Mughal funerary buildings: two tombs auctioned out of the
government "نزدول [OCR?]" register, one converted into a bungalow with its graves demolished, and three
tombs repaired by the government **through the agency of the author of this book himself**.

**Folio markers.** Every page in this chunk prints one, and in this chunk the relation is folio = PDF index
+ 3 throughout: p. 251 → folio 254 through p. 264 → folio 267, each marker reading exactly that. Only the
printed folios are used below.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **bibi-pak-daman** (بی بی پاک دامنان کی خانقاہ, "the khanqah of Bibi Pak Damanan"): named **only as a
  compass point**, to fix the position of another site. The mazar-house of Shah Rahmatullah Qureshi "بی بی
  پاک دامنان کی خانقاہ کے شمال کی سمت کو" — to the north of the khanqah of Bibi Pak Damanan — and to the
  east of the mosque of Najib Ali[OCR?] (p. 251, folio 254). The chunk says nothing else about it: no
  figure, no date, no practice, no custodian. **Note the word the book uses for it is خانقاہ, not mazar or
  darbar** (p. 251, folio 254).

- **madho-lal-hussain** (مزار مادہو لال حسین / مقبرہ مادہو لال حسین, and in the inscription simply "لال
  حسین"): the one archive row this chunk gives substantive material on, all of it about an adjoining mosque.
  - **A provincial governor's mosque built to the west of the mazar.** "غرب کی سمت مزار مادہو لال حسین کے
    یہ مسجد مبنیہ نواب زکریا خان … المعروف خان بہادر صوبہ لاہور موجود ہے" — to the west of the mazar of
    Madho Lal Husain stands this mosque built by Nawab Zakariya Khan, known as Khan Bahadur, Suba of Lahore
    (p. 262, folio 265). The chunk prints "زکریا خان [OCR?]" a second time as a **marginal note** at this
    line.
  - **Fabric.** To the east of the mosque a large well with a wheel (چاہ کلان چرخی دار) and two
    bathhouses; the courtyard floor pukka "مگر کہنہ و لوسیدہ [OCR?]", old and worn; a wall of man's height
    (دیوار قد آدم) round the courtyard; three mihrabs; on the central mihrab, in thuluth script in
    kansi-work with a sky-blue[OCR?] ground, the Bismillah and the Kalima Sharif (p. 262, folio 265).
  - **The north mihrab's inscription names the reigning emperor and the builder's office**: "خوہست [OCR?]
    در دور شاہ محمد شاہ" — in the era of Shah Muhammad Shah — and "زبدۂ بارگاہ او نواب ۔ زکریا خان صوبہ
    پنجاب", the choicest of his court, Nawab Zakariya Khan, **Suba of Punjab** (pp. 262–263, folios
    265–266). Note the two styles the chunk gives him: **Suba of Lahore** in the prose, **Suba of Punjab**
    in the verse.
  - **The inscription states the endowment's motive.** "چاہ و مسجد ز خود بنا کہ منہ [OCR?] ۔ عالی و خوب و
    خوشنما یکند [OCR?]" — he built well and mosque of himself, lofty, fine and handsome — and "محض بہر خدا
    کند این کار ۔ تا نمازی شود گماز [OCR?] گزار", he does this work purely for God's sake, so that the
    worshipper may offer prayer (p. 263, folio 266).
  - **The south mihrab's inscription places the mosque by the saint's dargah in so many words**: "نزد
    درگاہ صاحب عرفان ۔ واقف سر حضرت رحمان ۔ آنکہ معروف شدہ بہ لال حسین ۔ خاک نعلین اوست سرمہ عین" — near
    the dargah of the master of gnosis, knower of the secret of the Merciful, he who has become known as
    **Lal Husain**, the dust of whose sandals is collyrium for the eye (p. 263, folio 266). It also records
    "کرد احداث مسجد محکم ۔ نیز خویش دو در چاہ مستحکم", that he newly raised a firm mosque and a firm well
    (p. 263, folio 266).
  - **A building date, from the chronogram.** "ز تاریخ او ہر کہ دو بید [OCR?] شمار ۔ بداند ہزار و صدہ و
    چہل و چار" — one thousand one hundred and forty-four — with the figure printed beneath as "۱۱۴۴
    [OCR?]" (p. 263, folio 266).
  - **Custodianship, stated flatly.** "اب یہ مسجد متعلق مقبرہ مادہو لال حسین کے ہے اور سجادہ نشین کا قبضہ
    ہے" — now this mosque belongs to the tomb of Madho Lal Husain, and is in the **possession (قبضہ) of the
    sajjada nashin** (p. 263, folio 266). No name for the sajjada nashin, no date of the transfer, no
    income (not stated).

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (میان میر): named three times, always as a landmark or as a master, never as the
  subject of an entry.
  - **His cantonment as a place-name.** The tomb of Bahadur Khan was converted by the English "وقت مقرر پہ
    ہونے چہاونی [OCR?] میان میر کے", at the appointed time of the making of the **Mian Mir cantonment**
    (p. 257, folio 260); and the tomb of Miskin Shah Amri lies "چہاونی میان میر کی غربی سمت کو", to the
    west of the Mian Mir cantonment (p. 263, folio 266).
  - **His own tomb as the survey's reference point.** The Miskin Shah tomb is reached "میان میر بالا
    اسودتی [OCR?] کے مقبرے سے آگے بڑہ کے", going on past the tomb of Mian Mir Bala Asudati[OCR?] (p. 263,
    folio 266). The second word of that style is OCR-flagged; the chunk gives no location for Mian Mir's own
    tomb beyond this and no date for him (not stated).
  - **A servant of his is the occupant of a neighbouring tomb, and Mian Mir names him.** "صاحب مقبرہ کا نام
    میر عنایت اللہ تہا جو میان میر کا خادم تہا" — the occupant's name was **Mir Inayatullah**, who was the
    **khadim** of Mian Mir, and lived at the very spot where his tomb now stands (p. 264, folio 267). The
    title-giving is under Legends.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **An unnamed mosque, repaired by Nawab Ali Raza Khan** — the entry the chunk opens in the middle of.
  "مین مسجد خالی ہوگئی تو نواب علی رضا خان نے اسکی مرمت کرائی" — when the mosque fell empty, Nawab Ali
  Raza Khan had it repaired; pukka inside and out, three mihrabs and three grand domes; the courtyard floor
  now broken by long passage of time; the traces of the earlier building show "کہ سابق یہان باغ ہوگا", that
  formerly there was a garden here; an old tank (حوض کہنہ) survives beside the well. At the time of the
  city's outer settlement the muhalla of Haji Sawari[OCR?] stood on this spot, and the mosque was built in
  his time of office by the nephew (برادر زادہ) of Shaikh Muhammad Salih Haji Hanoz[OCR?], "امیر کبیر", a
  great amir (p. 251, folio 254). **The site's own name is lost with the chunk boundary — see Doubts.**
- **Shah Rahmatullah Qureshi** (مکان مزار شاہ رحمت اللہ قریشی, pp. 251, folio 254): "یہ بزرگ عہد عالمگیر
  مین ایک عالم فاضل و صاحب تصرف آدمی تہا" — in the era of Alamgir a learned and accomplished man and a
  **sahib-e tasarruf**, with murids beyond number. His grave sits on a chabutra inside a pukka lime-plastered
  enclosure wall whose gate is to the south; "چبوترہ کے اوپر اور زیر بہی قبرین بہت ہین", many graves both on
  and below the chabutra; the place is leafy, "درخت بہت لگائے ہوئے ہین". Death year (not stated). Family
  and custodianship under Practices.
- **Masjid Naqiban** (مکان مسجد نقیبان, pp. 251–252, folios 254–255): an old mosque "بادشاہی وقت کی تعمیر
  کی", built in the royal period, at the north-west corner of **Qila Gujar Singh** (قلعہ گوجر سنگھ), still
  standing firm "باوجود گزرنے صد ہا سال کے", despite hundreds of years. Three grand mihrabs, three domes,
  all three mihrabs qalbuti and figured, the ceiling likewise, and above the middle mihrab an inscription
  carrying the **Ayat al-Kursi** (pp. 251–252, folios 254–255). Outside, a pukka brick courtyard floor, a
  pukka tank still surviving, and a pukka well at the tank's south-east corner (p. 252, folio 255). Built
  at his own expense in the era of **Bahadur Shah Badshah** by an amir named Muhammad Wasl[OCR?] (محمد وصل
  [OCR?]), a native of Saharanpur who came from Delhi on royal service and settled in Lahore; afterwards
  his descendants held the service of **niqabat** before the Suba of Lahore, "اس سبب سے وہ نقیب کہلانے
  لگے", for which reason they came to be called Naqib, and the mosque became known as the Naqibs' mosque
  (p. 252, folio 255).
- **Shaikh Hamid Qari** (خانقاہ حامد قاری, pp. 252–253, folios 255–256): a khanqah two miles east of
  Lahore, east of Padaoh Badhu[OCR?] (پڑاوہ بدہو [OCR?]). "چونکہ قرآن پڑہنے مین یہ استاد تہا اسلئے اسکو
  قاری خطاب ہی ملا تہا" — because he was a master of Quran recitation he received the title **Qari**. In the
  time of **Muhammad Shah Badshah** he was in Lahore "عالم و فاضل صاحب فتوحی [OCR?]"; he taught his dars in
  this very mosque; "اس وقت یہ مکان بیرونی آبادی شہر لاہور کے اندر تہا", at that time the place lay within
  Lahore's outer settlement. Died "سنہ ۱۰۶۴ [OCR?] ہجری" and was buried here (p. 253, folio 256) — see
  Doubts, this cannot stand beside the reign and the mosque's own date. His bai'at[OCR?] was "خاندان
  سہروردیہ مین بخدمت مولوی تیمور کے", in the **Suhrawardi** family at the hand of Maulvi Taimur, himself "ایک
  عالم متبحر و یگانہ عصر" of Lahore (p. 253, folio 256).
- **Ali Mardan Khan, Shahjahani counsellor** (مقبرہ علی مردان خان مشیر شاہجہانی, pp. 253–255, folios
  256–258): son of Ganj Ali Khan, amir al-umara and **subahdar of the province of Qandahar under the Safavid
  Persian sultanate**; displeased with the Shah of Iran, he turned to the "سلطنت جہانیہ [OCR?]". The chunk
  frames the defection as a bargain: Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan all three wished "کہ کابل کی طرح
  قندہار کا صوبہ بہی انکی قلمرو کے شامل ہو", that like Kabul the province of Qandahar too should enter their
  realm, and he wrote a petition (عریضہ) to Shah Jahan that "اگر سرکار ہند مین میری عزت و توقیر ہو تو قلعہ
  قندہار مین نذر کر پکڑ تا ہون [OCR?]" — if I have honour and esteem in the Sarkar of Hind, I make over the
  fort of Qandahar. The emperor, delighted, sent his own subahdar with "سپہ [OCR?] فوج جرار", a strong army,
  ordering Ali Mardan Khan to hand possession to him and himself attend court; he did so and "سلک امرائے ذی
  الاقتدار مین منسلک ہوا", was enrolled among the powerful amirs, rising to the administration of the
  provinces of **Lahore, Delhi and Kashmir** (p. 253, folio 256). His works are under Practices. Died "سنہ
  ۱۰۶۹ [OCR?] ہجری" and was buried at the spot where the tomb now stands (p. 254, folio 257).
- **Shah Shams ud-Din Qadri Lahori** (مقبرہ شاہ شمس الدین قادری لاہوری, pp. 255–256, folios 258–259): "یہ
  بزرگ خاندان قادریہ مین شاہ ابو اسحاق قادری کا مرید تہا ۔ اور شاہ بلاول قادری کا پیر" — in the **Qadiri**
  family the murid of **Shah Abu Ishaq Qadri** and the pir of **Shah Bilawal Qadri** (p. 256, folio 259).
  "آزاد طبع و مجرد و لمکر [OCR?] تہا" — of free temper and celibate. Two nastaliq couplets over the inner
  face of the north door carry his date: "چو شمس الملل زین جہان رخت بست ۔ بیا راست ایزد بر اشیش [OCR?]
  بہشت … بجستم زپیر خضر [OCR?] سال او ۔ بگفت از سر لطف جایش بہشت", with "۱۰۲۴ [OCR?]" printed between them
  (p. 256, folio 259). His tomb is square, four-doored, its plinth barely raised, its four corner minarets
  "اب مسمار ہو گئے", now demolished, and it stands a short way south-east of the **Lieutenant Governor's
  kothi** (pp. 255–256, folios 258–259). Shah Jahan's patronage is under Practices and his prophecy under
  Legends. **Not the archive's Shah Shams — see Doubts.**
- **Shaikh Muhtaram Naqshbandi** (روضہ حضرت شیخ محترم نقشبندی, pp. 256–257, folios 259–260): "نقشبندیہ
  سلسلہ مین اس کی ارادت تہی" — his devotion was in the **Naqshbandi** silsila; "عابد و زاہد متقی خدا پرست",
  a worshipper, ascetic and god-fearing man of the old times, long resident in Lahore. "اکبر بادشاہ کے عہد
  مین آیا اور عالمگیر کے عہد تک زندہ رہا ایک ہزار ایک سو دو [OCR?] ہجری مین فوت ہوا" — he came in Akbar's
  era, lived to Alamgir's, and died in **1102 AH**. The tomb stood north of the **Shalamar Bagh road**, west
  of a padaoh, near kothis belonging to the Railway Station; square, pukka and lime-plastered, four small
  square domes at the corners, four qalbuti arched doors, a grand central dome, and inside "تین قبرین پختہ
  ایک شیخ محترم اور دو نامعلوم الاسم", three pukka graves — one Shaikh Muhtaram and **two of unknown name**,
  said (with an OCR-flagged word) to be his sons (pp. 256–257, folios 259–260). Its walls carried "بخط
  عربی و فارسی نہایت سے آیات و اشعار", many verses in Arabic and Persian, "جن مین سے کچہ پڑہے جاتے مین اور
  کچہ نہین پڑہے جاتے" — some legible, some not; the legible part is copied out, ending "قدس اللہ سرہ
  الاشرف" (p. 257, folio 260). The abjad arithmetic and the tomb's auction are under Practices.
- **Bahadur Khan** (مقبرہ بہادر خان, pp. 257–258, folios 260–261): "صاحب مقبرہ دربار اکبر بادشاہ مین امیر
  الامراؤ و و دیر [OCR?] تہا اور شاہجہان [OCR?] ۱۰۱۰ [OCR?] مین فوت ہو کر اس جگہ مدفون ہوا" — amir
  al-umara and (word flagged) in the court of **Akbar Badshah**, who died in Shahjahan[OCR?] 1010[OCR?] and
  was buried here. "یہ مقبرہ لاہور کے قدیمی مقبرون مین سے ایک عالیشان مقبرہ ہے" — one of Lahore's ancient
  and grand tombs, by the Amritsar iron road (سڑک آہنی امرتسر) at the iron bridge. Fabric, measurements and
  fate under Practices. See Doubts on the date.
- **Mian Jan Muhammad** (pp. 259, folio 262): maulvi and mudarris of the Qasab Khana muhalla, who taught in
  its mosque when the quarter was settled, "اور وہ میان وڈا کا شاگرد و مرید تہا", and was the pupil and
  murid of Mian Wadda. "یہ جان محمد پورے [OCR?] فضلا اور بزرگون مین مشہور و معروف" — famous among the
  learned and the elders, "جسکا تذکرہ اکثر کتب مین و دیکہا جاتا ہے کہ علم و فضل و عمل و ولایت اسکی ذات مین
  جمع تہی", his tazkira seen in many books, knowledge, excellence, practice and **wilayat** gathered in his
  person. Died "سنہ ۱۲۳۸ [OCR?] ہجری" and was buried on the spot; his grave lies on the left[OCR?] side of
  the mosque inside an enclosure wall (p. 259, folio 262).
- **Makhduma Begum[OCR?], wife of Nawab Abul Hasan Khan** (مقبرہ مخدومہ بیگم [OCR?] زوجہ نواب ابو الحسن
  خان, pp. 259–260, folios 262–263): her tomb stands two and a half miles east of Lahore. The husband is
  given as "ابو الحسن بیدف [OCR?] خان بن اعتماد الدولہ نور الحسن [OCR?] خان طہرانی", a real[OCR?] sibling

- **Shaikh Mahmud Shah Naqshbandi** (مقبرہ شیخ محمود شاہ نقشبندی, pp. 260–261, folios 263–264): "یہ محمود

  المشہور جہولن شاہ [OCR?]), "صرف رستہ اسکے درمیان حد فاصل ہے", only a road dividing them (p. 260, folio

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A hereditary piri-muridi line still functioning, and doubling as the site's custodian.** Of Shah
  Rahmatullah Qureshi: "اب بہی اسکے خاندان مین پیری و مریدی کا سلسلہ چلا آتا ہے اور اسکی اولاد موضع
  ڈہولنوال و شہر لاہور مین موجود ہے اور اس مکان کے سرپرست وہی لوگ ہین" — even now the line of piri-muridi
  continues in his family; his descendants are in **mauza Dhulnawal** and in the city of Lahore, and **those
  same people are the guardians (سرپرست) of this place** (p. 251, folio 254). Descent, discipleship and
  custody in one sentence, with the family seated in a named village outside the city.
- **A mela with a fixed date that is also the death anniversary, at a khanqah held by another shrine's
  sajjada nashin.** "اب حفاظت اس مکان کی متعلق میان احمد الدین سجادہ نشین خانقاہ میان وڈا کے ہے ۔ ہر سال اس
  جگہ میلہ ہوتا ہے اور ۱۴،۱۳ [OCR?] ماہ جمادی الثانی میلے کی تاریخ مقرر ہے اور اسی تاریخ یہ بزرگ مرا تہا" —
  the keeping of Hamid Qari's khanqah belongs to **Mian Ahmad ud-Din, sajjada nashin of the khanqah of Mian
  Wadda**; every year a **mela** is held here; the 13th–14th[OCR?] of **Jumada al-Thani** is its appointed
  date, and on that date the elder died (p. 253, folio 256). One establishment administering a second,
  unrelated saint's site.
- **The same sajjada nashin takes possession of an empty mosque, on a claim of pedagogical descent.** Of the
  Qasab Khana mosque: "جب سکہی سلطنت جاتی رہی اور یہ مسجد خالی ہوگئی تو میان احمد الدین سجادہ نشین درس میان
  وڈا نے اس پر قبضہ کر لیا اس دعوی پر کہ بوقت آبادی اس محلے کے میان جان محمد مولوی و مدرس اس مسجد مین درس
  پڑہاتا تہا اور وہ میان وڈا کا شاگرد و مرید تہا" — when the Sikh sultanate passed and the mosque fell
  empty, **Mian Ahmad ud-Din, sajjada nashin of the dars of Mian Wadda, took possession (قبضہ) of it**, on
  the claim that when the quarter was settled Mian Jan Muhammad, maulvi and mudarris, taught in this mosque
  and was Mian Wadda's pupil and murid; and Jan Muhammad's grave is there, inside an enclosure wall
  (p. 259, folio 262). **The claim is a title deed made of teaching lineage** — the clearest institutional
  mechanism in the chunk.
- **A second, secular claim on an emptied mosque, by an office-holder.** Of Masjid Naqiban: in the Sikhs'
  administration the mosque "مین بارونق بہر ہی رہتی تہی"; when under the English government's
  administration "بارونق تلف ہو کر یہ مسجد خالی ہوگئی تو صوبہ شاہ بشاہ [OCR?] نقیب نے دعوی کرکے یہ مسجد
  واگزار کرالی" — the بارونق was got rid of and the mosque fell empty, whereupon a **Naqib** (name heavily
  flagged: "صوبہ شاہ بشاہ [OCR?] نقیب") made a claim and had the mosque **released (واگزار)** (p. 252, folio
  255). See Doubts on the word بارونق.
- **Government escheat as the engine of destruction: three cases, one chunk.**
  - **Shaikh Muhtaram's rauza auctioned to an Englishman and made into a bungalow.** "چونکہ یہ گنبد ہی
    مقبرہ و بسبب و اوث [OCR?] ہونے کے سرکاری نزدول [OCR?] کے رجسٹر مین درج تہا ۔ ایک انگریز کی درخواست پہ
    نیلام ہو گیا اور انگریز نے اسکو خرید کر … چارون طرف برامدہ بنا کر کوٹہی کی [OCR?] صورت بنالی ہے قبرین
    گرا دی مین" — because this domed tomb was, on account of being [flagged word], entered in the register
    of the government **نزدول [OCR?]**, it was **auctioned on the application of an Englishman**, who bought
    it, threw a verandah round all four sides, made it into the form of a **kothi**, and **demolished the
    graves** (p. 257, folio 260).
  - **Abul Hasan Khan's ten-lakh tomb auctioned under the same head and quarried away.** "اسکو بصیغہ نزدول
    [OCR?] صاحبان عالیشان نے نیلام کر دیا اور خشت فروشون سنے [OCR?] اسکی بنیادون کو ایسا کہود وا کہ نشان
    باقی نہ چہوڑا" — the grand gentlemen auctioned it **under the head of nazul[OCR?]**, and the
    brick-sellers dug out its foundations so that no trace remained (p. 260, folio 263).
  - **Nusrat Khan's tomb passes to nazul and then is sold.** After the Sikhs' possession lapsed, "پہر خالی
    ہو گیا اب متعلق نزدول [OCR?] ہے" — it fell empty and now belongs to nazul[OCR?]; and "اب کوٹہی کورٹ
    صاحب کی تو سرکار نے فروخت کر لی [OCR?] ہے اور اسمین [OCR?] اسکی لالہ میلا رام شہیکہ [OCR?] وارثے [OCR?]
    گیا" — the government has now **sold** Court Sahib's kothi, and it went to **Lala Mela Ram** (name of
    buyer clear, surrounding words flagged) (pp. 261, 262; folios 264, 265).
- **Three tombs repaired by the colonial government through the author of this book.** Of Ali Mardan Khan's
  tomb: "اب سرکار انگریزی سے براہ قدردانی اس مقبرہ کو معرفت مولف کتاب کے مرمت کرایا ہے ۔ تاکہ [OCR?] کسی ہو
  نشیال [OCR?] کے صدمہ سے گر نہ جاوے", and its basement also cleaned (p. 255, folio 258). Of Bahadur Khan's
  dome: "اس گنبد کی مرمت بہی اب سرکار نے مولف کتاب کی معرفت کرائی ہے" (p. 258, folio 261). Of Nusrat Khan's
  tomb: "اس مقبرہ کی مرمت بہی سرکار نے مولف کتاب کی معرفت کرائی ہے جس سے استحکام عمارت کا ہو گیا ہے"
  (p. 262, folio 265). **The book's author is a party to the conservation he reports** — a standing caution
  for every judgement he passes on ruin and repair in this stretch.
- **Systematic quarrying of tomb fabric, agents named.** The enclosure bricks of Ali Mardan Khan's complex
  were carried off by "لاہور کے کشمیری خشت فروش", the **Kashmiri brick-sellers of Lahore**, and the
  remaining buildings pulled down by **Sardar Gulab Singh** for cantonment bricks — "جس چہاونی کا بہی اب نام
  و نشان نہین رہا", of which cantonment no trace now remains either (p. 254, folio 257). Red sandstone and
  marble slabs (سنگ سرخ و سنگ ابری) from the tomb's first storey "سکہون نے براہ سنگدلی اکہاڑ لی تہین", the
  Sikhs hard-heartedly pulled out (p. 255, folio 258). The Qasab Khana mosque's tank bricks were pulled out
  by **Colonel Gulab Singh Kabh Kabh[OCR?]**, "جو کورٹ والی پلٹنون کا افسر تہا", an officer of **Court
  Sahib's platoons**, to build himself a house north of the mosque (p. 259, folio 262). The circular well
  north of Nusrat Khan's dome lost its bricks too (p. 262, folio 265). Of Bahadur Khan's grave: "مسن [OCR?]
  لوگ بیان کرتے مین کہ یہ قبر سنگ مرمر کی تہی مگر … مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ نے اتر والیا [OCR?] تہا" — aged
  people relate it was of marble but **Maharaja Ranjit Singh** had it taken away (p. 258, folio 261).
  Makhduma Begum's marble grave: "پہلے قبر سنگ مرمر کی تہی وہ سکہہون نے گرا لی" (p. 260, folio 263).
- **Tombs as barracks, residences and offices.** Ali Mardan Khan's deorhi was made his dwelling in the Sikh
  period by a colonel of platoon, cavalry and artillery ("گورد ت سنگھ [OCR?] کرنیل افسر پلٹن رسالہ و
  توپخانہ", p. 254, folio 257), and a man of Gulab Singh's force lodged in the tomb itself under Ranjit
  Singh (p. 255, folio 258). Bahadur Khan's tomb was converted by the English when the Mian Mir cantonment
  was made, and "اب بہی محکمہ ریل کے لازم اسمین قیام … مین قبر پر [OCR?] گرا دی گئی ہے جس کا نشان بہی باقی
  نہین" — Railway department men still reside in it, and the grave has been demolished without trace
  (p. 257, folio 260). Nusrat Khan's tomb was made his residence by **General Court Sahib**, who built a
  grand kothi against it, "بہت سی جدید عمارت بنا کر صورت تبدیل کر دی تہی", changed its form with much new
  building, absorbed its **ancient attached mosque into the kothi**, and "اس قبر کو گروا کر پختہ فرش بنوا
  دیا تہا", had the grave pulled down and a pukka floor laid over it (pp. 261–262, folios 264–265). A grand
  pukka mosque with a hujra still stands to its south (p. 262, folio 265). Makhduma Begum's husband's tomb
  was occupied under Ranjit Singh by "جنرنیل اویطولیہ [OCR?] صاحب فرنسیسی", a **French** general (name
  flagged), who put [flagged word] in it (p. 259, folio 262).
- **A saint's tomb built by an emperor, with a garden attached — and the garden gone.** Of Shah Shams ud-Din
  Qadri: "جب فوت ہوا تو شاہجہان نے یہ مقبرہ … بہت خرچ کرکے بنوایا اور مقبرہ کے متعلق ایک باغیچہ لگوایا اب
  صرف مقبرہ موجود ہے باغیچہ کا نشان ابتک [OCR?] نہین ہے" — when he died **Shah Jahan** had this tomb built
  at great expense and laid out a **garden attached to the tomb**; now only the tomb survives, and of the
  garden no trace (p. 256, folio 259).
- **A tomb built by murids in the saint's own lifetime, with an annual urs.** Of Shaikh Mahmud Shah
  Naqshbandi: "یہ مقبرہ اسکے مریدون نے اسکی زندگی مین بنوار کہا تہا" — his murids had this tomb built while
  he lived; a **basement (تہہ خانہ)** was made beneath it first, and "جب وہ فوت ہوا تو صندوق اسکا تہہ خانے
  مین رکہہ کر دروازہ تہہ خانے کا بند کر دیا گیا", when he died his **coffin (صندوق)** was placed in the
  basement and the basement door shut; "ایسا بہی اس مقبرہ پر سالانہ عرس ہوتا ہے" — an **annual urs** is
  held at this tomb (pp. 260–261, folios 263–264). A dated 19th-century foundation (1283 AH[OCR?]) with an
  urs already established, and an unusual burial arrangement.
- **A woman builds her own tomb, and deliberately builds it smaller than her husband's.** Of Makhduma
  Begum: "اس نے اپنا مقبرہ اپنی حین حیات بنوار کہا تہا اور ادب کے سبب سے شو ہر کے مقبرے سے چہوٹا بنوایا جو
  ابتک اسکا یادگار … موجود ہے" — she had her tomb built in her own lifetime and, **out of deference (ادب),
  had it made smaller than her husband's** (p. 260, folio 263). Her husband's tomb "جو دس لاکہہ روپیے خرچ
  کرکے بنایا گیا" cost **ten lakh rupees** (p. 259, folio 262) — the only building cost given in the chunk.
- **A second woman's endowment, with a deposited Quran and sword and a testamentary condition.** Sharaf
  un-Nisa Begum "نے اپنی زندگی مین یہ مقبرہ بنوا بایا [OCR?] تہا اور ہر روز ایک گہنٹہ گہنٹہ بعد نماز ظہر
  کے یہان آتی اور قرآن پڑہتی تہی" — built the tomb in her lifetime and came here daily for an hour after
  the **Zuhr** prayer to read the Quran; "بعد تلاوت کے خود تو محلون مین چلی جاتی مگر قرآن شریف اور ایک تلوار
  یہان چہوڑ جاتی", after the recitation she returned to the palaces but left the Quran Sharif and a
  **sword** behind. Because the dome was very high "بے زینہ", **without a staircase**, "بیگم ہر روز چوبی
  زینہ رکہکر اوپر جایا کرتی تہی", she went up daily on a wooden ladder. "جب مرنے لگی تو وصیت کی کہ میری قبر
  اسی مقبرہ مین ہو اور میرا قرآن اور تلوار اسی مقبرہ مین رکہی رہے" — dying, she **willed (وصیت)** that her
  grave be in this tomb and her Quran and sword remain kept in it; so it was, "اور مقبرہ اسی طرح بے زینہ
  رہا", and the tomb stayed stairless (p. 264, folio 267). The chunk breaks mid-sentence at "آخر جب سلطنت
  مغلیہ جاتی رہی اور".
- **Canals and waterworks credited to one official.** Of Ali Mardan Khan: "خصوصاً عمارت کے کام مین یہ ایسا
  اوستاد تہا ۔ کہ کروڑون روپیہ اس کے ہاتہ سے صرف ہوے" — especially in building work such a master that
  **crores of rupees** were spent through his hand; the **Nau Lakha** garden in Lahore was his memorial, of
  which only the deorhi now survives; "دہلی کی نہر جو مین شہر اور قلعہ مین پہنچتی ہے یہی شخص کہود وا کر لایا
  تہا" — the **Delhi canal** that reaches into the city and the fort he had dug and brought; a further canal
  (names flagged: "نہر شہنسلی [OCR?] ہاہو [OCR?] ہولو پور [OCR?]") he dug and brought to Lahore "اور … شالامار
  کو سیراب کیا", watering the **Shalamar**; and a great canal toward **Hansi Hisar** (the intervening
  place-names, "فیروپور [OCR?] و زیرو چو دہلی [OCR?]", are broken) (p. 254, folio 257).
- **A well that could carry twelve Persian wheels, irrigating a tomb garden.** In the shared enclosure of
  the Abul Hasan and Makhduma Begum tombs, "جو چاہ کلان … تہا اور اس سے باغ کو پانی دیا جاتا تہا ابتک موجود
  ہے وہ اتنا بڑا چاہ ہے کہ بارہ ریہٹ [OCR?] اس پر بآسانی چل سکتے مین" — the large well from which the garden
  was watered still survives, so big that **twelve rehats (Persian wheels)[OCR?]** could work on it easily;
  "اس چاہ کے اندر ایک محرابی دریچہ بہی نظر آتا ہے", an arched opening is visible inside it (p. 260, folio
  263). Water furniture recorded at Hamid Qari's khanqah too: a wheeled well, **two bathhouses
  (غسلخانے)**, a tank, and "ایک کوٹہد [OCR?] پختہ فقیر کے رہنے کے لئے", a pukka cell built for a faqir to
  live in (p. 252, folio 255); and again at the Zakariya Khan mosque, a wheeled well and two bathhouses
  (p. 262, folio 265).
- **Building dates carried by inscription, with the chunk's own arithmetic shown once.** The Hamid Qari
  mosque's qatʿa-e tarikh over two small mihrabs — "چو خوش مسجد از دوست مسکین بہاد [OCR?] ۔ خداوند را شکر
  دائم بیاد … خود گفت وز سال تاریخ آن ۔ ز آفات و دوران زوالش مباد" — with "۱۱۴۱ [OCR?]" printed beneath
  (p. 252, folio 255); the Zakariya Khan mosque's "۱۱۴۴ [OCR?]" (p. 263, folio 266); and for Shaikh
  Muhtaram, the book works the abjad out in the open: "قدس اللہ سرہ الاشرف کے اعداد بحساب ابجد ایک ہزار ایک
  سو سات ہوتے مین … تو ایک ہزار ایک سو دو باقی رہ جاتے مین" — the numerals of *Quddasa Allahu sirrahu
  al-ashraf* come by abjad to **1107**, and after the subtraction (the operative words are flagged: "ہمین
  [OCR?] سے لگا تاریخ دو در کرین [OCR?]") **1102** remains (p. 257, folio 260).
- **Measured architecture, in gaz.** Bahadur Khan's tomb: an octagonal pukka brick chabutra, "ہر پہلو کا

  eight grand mihrabs, "اتنا بلند مقبرہ لاہور مین اور کوئی نہین" — no other tomb in Lahore so tall (p. 255,

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A prince told he will be emperor.** Of Shah Shams ud-Din Qadri: "شاہجہان بادشاہ و شہزادگی کی حالت مین
  اس کی خدمت مین اکثر حاضر ہوا کرتا تہا اور اس کی بانی [OCR?] خوشخبری پائی تہی کہ بعد جہانگیر کے تو بادشاہ
  ہو گا" — Shah Jahan, while still a prince, often attended him and received from him the good news that
  **after Jahangir you will be emperor** (p. 256, folio 259). The tomb Shah Jahan later built is the
  payment; the book puts prophecy and patronage in consecutive sentences.
- **A title explained as sustenance by divine command, in Mian Mir's own words.** Of Mir Inayatullah:
  "مسکین شاہ امری اسکو مرشد نے خطاب دیا تہا" — his **murshid** gave him the title Miskin Shah Amri, because
  [word flagged] "ورنہ معلوم ہوتا کہ یہ کہان سے کہا تا ہے", it was otherwise not known where he got his
  food; and when someone described his state to **Mian Mir**, "اس نے فرمایا کہ یہ مسکین امری ہی یعنی خدا کے
  امر سے اسکو کہانا ملتا ہے اسکو کسب کی حاجت نہین" — he said: this is a *miskin amri*, that is, **by God's
  command (امر) he receives his food; he has no need of earning** (p. 264, folio 267). Attributed directly
  to Mian Mir.
- **Lightning as the agent of a tomb's destruction.** Of Abul Hasan Khan's tomb: "اس پہ زیر [OCR?] بجلی
  گری اور وہ صدمہ اس عمارت عالیشان کو بہی جیا [OCR?] کہ بہت مین شقق [OCR?] ہو گیا اور بارونق کو آگ لگ کر وہ
  سنگین عمارت پاش پاش ہو گئی" — **lightning fell on it**, the shock cracked the grand building in many
  places, the بارونق caught fire, and that stone building was shattered to pieces (pp. 259–260, folios
  262–263). The chunk introduces this with a fatalistic clause — "چونکہ گردش دور و دوار [OCR?] کو اس مقبرہ
  کی بنیا دین اوکہاڑ دینی [OCR?] منظور نہین", since the turning of the times did not consent to leave the
  tomb's foundations standing — and follows it with a Persian couplet on the same theme, heavily broken:
  "گردش گردون گروان گرد گانہ [OCR?] اگر و کرد [OCR?] ۔ نار سوزان حر یبصان [OCR?] زمانہ سرد کرد" (p. 260,
  folio 263).
- No karamat of any kind is told of the saints whose tombs and khanqahs are described — Shah Rahmatullah
  Qureshi, Hamid Qari, Shaikh Muhtaram, Mahmud Shah (not stated). The only wonder-material in the chunk
  attaches to an emperor's accession and a servant's meals.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author signs his own verse on the vanity of grandeur, at the one tomb whose grave has been
  destroyed.** Marked "قول [OCR?] مولف", the author's saying, after Bahadur Khan's grave is reported
  levelled: "تہا وقت زندگی افلاک پر جن کا دماغ ۔ آج ان کی خاک کا نام و نشان باقی نہین" and "آگ کی مانند
  بہتے تہے جو ہر دم مشتعل ۔ دیکہہ لو ان کا نہ نام [OCR?] و نہ نشان [OCR?] باقی نہین" — those whose minds
  were on the heavens in life: today not a trace of their dust remains; they blazed like fire, ever alight:
  look, neither name nor trace of them is left (pp. 257–258, folios 260–261). **The only place in the chunk
  where the surveyor speaks in the first person as a poet**, and he does it over a grave demolished under
  the administration he elsewhere thanks for repairs.
- **He states his own editorial order of business.** Before Ali Mardan Khan's tomb: "مکان کے ذکر سے پہلے
  صاحب مقبرہ کا ذکر مقدم سمجہ کر لکہا جاتا ہے" — the account of the occupant of the tomb is judged to take
  precedence over the account of the building, and is therefore written first (p. 253, folio 256). A
  method-statement for the whole genre of entries in this stretch.
- **He rates a building by comparison rather than description.** Of the complex round Ali Mardan Khan's
  tomb: "مسلمانی سلطنت کے وقت اس کے ساتہ کی عمارت خشتی … اسے مسجد و زیر خان [OCR?] کے دوسری نہ تہی" — in the
  time of the Muslim sultanate the brick building attached to it was second to none but the Masjid Wazir
  Khan[OCR?] (p. 254, folio 257); and of the tomb itself, "بلکہ اتنا بلند مقبرہ لاہور مین اور کوئی نہین",
  there is no other tomb in Lahore so tall (p. 255, folio 258).
- **He marks a conjecture as a conjecture.** Of the arched opening inside the great well: "شاید اسکے متصل
  سرد خانہ ہوگا" — **perhaps** a cool-chamber adjoined it (p. 260, folio 263). One of the few places in the
  chunk where he separates inference from report.
- **He cites his sources for chronograms by title.** Two death-dates are taken from a named
  chronogram-collection: for Ali Mardan Khan, "کتاب گنجینہ سرودی [OCR?] المشہور گنج تاریخ" (p. 254, folio
  257), and for Nusrat Khan, "کتاب گنجینہ سروری" (p. 261, folio 264). See Doubts — the title is spelled two
  ways.
- **He records oral testimony as such.** On Bahadur Khan's marble grave: "مسن [OCR?] لوگ بیان کرتے مین کہ
  …" — aged people relate that … (p. 258, folio 261), against his own eyewitness register elsewhere ("جسکی
  خوبی دیکہنے پر منحصر ہے", whose beauty depends on being seen, p. 258, folio 261).

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the site is left unnamed.** p. 251 (folio 254) begins "مین مسجد خالی
  ہوگئی تو نواب علی رضا خان نے اسکی مرمت کرائی" — the mosque's own name, and the heading it sat under, are
  in the previous chunk. Everything recorded above for that first paragraph (the three mihrabs and three
  domes, the vanished garden, the old tank, muhalla Haji Sawari[OCR?], the nephew of Shaikh Muhammad Salih
  Haji Hanoz[OCR?], Nawab Ali Raza Khan's repair) belongs to **an unnamed mosque**. Do not name it from
  context.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** The Sharaf un-Nisa Begum account breaks at "آخر جب سلطنت مغلیہ جاتی رہی
  اور" (p. 264, folio 267) — the fate of her Quran, her sword and her tomb is in the next chunk. The
  by-name "المعروف شیر نعیم بیگم [OCR?]" is itself flagged.
- **"شاہ شمس الدین قادری لاہوری" must not be mapped to shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari.** This figure
  is **Qadiri**, at **Lahore**, near the Lieutenant Governor's kothi, murid of Shah Abu Ishaq Qadri, with a
  chronogram reading "۱۰۲۴ [OCR?]" (pp. 255–256, folios 258–259). The archive's row is Shah Shams-ud-Din
  **Sabzwari** at **Multan** with no silsila given. Shared given name only. **Leave unmapped**, and note it
  as a likely future mis-match.
- **"شاہ بلاول قادری" must not be mapped to shah-noorani-shrine-syed-bilawal-shah-noorani.** He appears only
  as the *murid* of Shah Shams ud-Din, in the Qadiri line, with no place, no date and no shrine (p. 256,
  folio 259); the archive's row is Syed Bilawal Shah Noorani in Lahoot Lamakan, **Balochistan**. **Leave
  unmapped.**
- **"شیخ محمود شاہ نقشبندی" must not be attached to ganj-e-inayat-sarkar or mohra-sharif-khanqah** on the
  strength of "خاندان مجددیہ نقشبندیہ" (p. 260, folio 263). This man died 1283 AH[OCR?] and lies north of
  the Ghore Shah / Jhulan Shah[OCR?] tomb; those rows' figures are differently placed and dated. Candidate
  for a future row, not a match for an existing one. The same applies to **Shaikh Muhtaram Naqshbandi**
  (d. 1102 AH[OCR?], pp. 256–257, folios 259–260), whose rauza the chunk says was turned into a bungalow
  with its graves destroyed — a shrine that may no longer exist.
- **Hamid Qari's dates cannot all stand.** The chunk puts him "محمد شاہ بادشاہ کے وقت" in Lahore, says the
  **present mosque was built by him** ("مسجد موجودہ اسی کی بنوائی ہوئی کی [OCR?] تہی") and that he taught in
  it, gives that mosque's chronogram as "۱۱۴۱ [OCR?]", and gives his death as "سنہ ۱۰۶۴ [OCR?] ہجری"
  (pp. 252–253, folios 255–256). **Both numerals are OCR-flagged and they are seventy-seven years apart**;
  the reign named agrees with the mosque's date, not the death year. A human must re-read both figures from
  the PDF before either is used.
- **Bahadur Khan's date is broken in two ways at once.** "دربار اکبر بادشاہ مین … اور شاہجہان [OCR?] ۱۰۱۰
  [OCR?] مین فوت ہو کر" (p. 257, folio 260) — an Akbar-court amir, a flagged emperor's name, and a flagged
  year, in one clause. The year could belong to either reign-name. Do not carry "1010" without this note.
- **"دار [OCR?] اسکو کے حکم سے" — the patron who ordered Miskin Shah Amri's tomb is unreadable** (p. 264,
  folio 267). The printed form is given above exactly as it stands. It must not be completed from context,
  and the tomb's patron should be recorded as (not stated) until the page is re-read.
- **"بارونق" is the chunk's word in four places where the sense appears to be a stored commodity, not
  liveliness.** The mosque of the Naqibs "مین بارونق بہر ہی رہتی تہی" under the Sikhs and fell empty when
  "بارونق تلف ہو کر" under the English (p. 252, folio 255); Colonel Gulab Singh "اس مسجد مین بارونق بہر دہی
  تہی" at the Qasab Khana mosque (p. 259, folio 262); and at Abul Hasan Khan's tomb "بارونق کو آگ لگ کر وہ
  سنگین عمارت پاش پاش ہو گئی" — the بارونق caught fire and the stone building was shattered (p. 260, folio
  263). **The transcriber left no flag on any of these**, so the word is carried as printed above; but a
  building that is filled with it, emptied of it by a new administration, and blown apart when it ignites is
  doing something a word meaning "flourishing" cannot do. **A human must re-read these four places from the
  PDF before any statement is made about military use of these mosques and tombs.**
- **"نزدول [OCR?]" is flagged at all three occurrences** (pp. 257, 260, 261; folios 260, 263, 264). What the
  chunk itself shows the thing doing is enough to cite: properties are entered in its **register**, and are
  **auctioned** on application, by "صاحبان عالیشان", to private buyers. The word should be quoted, not
  glossed, until the reading is confirmed.
- **"کانسی کا کام" / "کانسی کار" is unflagged but recurs for decorative surface work** — on Makhduma
  Begum's dome (p. 260, folio 263), on the Zakariya Khan mosque's mihrab inscriptions "بخط ثلث کانسی کار …
  بزنگ [OCR?] آسمانی" (p. 262, folio 265), on Miskin Shah's walls (p. 263, folio 266), and on Sharaf
  un-Nisa's tomb where it forms "بڑے بڑے سرو منقش", large figured cypresses, giving the building its
  by-name **سرو والا مقبرہ**, the tomb with the cypress (p. 264, folio 267). Whether the book means bronze
  or glazed tile should be settled from the PDF before the material of these surfaces is stated in an entry.
- **The chronogram-collection's title is spelled two ways**: "گنجینہ سرودی [OCR?] المشہور گنج تاریخ"
  (p. 254, folio 257) and "گنجینہ سروری" (p. 261, folio 264). The second is unflagged.
- **Ali Mardan Khan's name is inverted once**: "مردان علی خان [OCR?]" mid-paragraph (p. 253, folio 256),
  against "علی مردان خان" in the heading and elsewhere.
- **"نواب خان بہادر" is used for two people, or one, and the chunk does not say which.** Nawab Zakariya
  Khan is styled "المعروف خان بہادر صوبہ لاہور" (p. 262, folio 265); Sharaf un-Nisa is "ہمشیرہ نواب خان
  بہادر", sister of Nawab Khan Bahadur (p. 264, folio 267). The chunk **does not** state that the same man
  is meant. Do not join them.

  بیگم" (p. 259, folio 262). The relationship term itself is what is damaged; his own by-name "بیدف [OCR?]"
