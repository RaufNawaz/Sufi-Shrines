# tareekh_lahore — chunk 006 — pp. 76–89

**A continuous run of the author's gazetteer of the outer city: the mohallas settled beyond the old
hisar under the Mughals, who founded each, and what is left of it in the 1880s.** The chunk opens with
the close of a notice of a Kashmiri benefactor and the author's own statement of what the section will
do, then works mohalla by mohalla: Langar Khan, Pir Aziz Mazang, Mauj Darya Bukhari, Sayyid Chiragh Shah
Gilani, Dola Wari, Shah Sharaf, Lakhi, Dargahi Shah, Shah Badr, the Khojas, Jat Pura, Miani, Daya Lado,
Zain Khan, Piran ka Mohalla, Dai Anga, Sayyid Sar, Teel Pura, Ganj and Qasaban (pp. 76–89, folios
79–92). Four archive rows take material, one of them the founding account of the Miani Sahib graveyard.
The chunk is unusually dense in **saints as town-founders, mosques converted to powder magazines and
then to English bungalows, and lawsuits over mosque custodianship** — the shrine material and the
municipal material are the same material here.

**Folio markers, as printed on every page of this chunk**: p. 76 → folio 79, p. 77 → folio 80, p. 78 →
folio 81, p. 79 → folio 82, p. 80 → folio 83, p. 81 → folio 84, p. 82 → folio 85, p. 83 → folio 86,
p. 84 → folio 87, p. 85 → folio 88, p. 86 → folio 89, p. 87 → folio 90, p. 88 → folio 91, p. 89 →

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari** (محلہ موج دریا بخاری / سید میران شاہ بخاری, "also called موج دریا"):
  - He was **the first to come and settle at the site**, in the reign of Akbar: "اس مقام پر سب پہلے سید
    میران شاہ بخاری جسکو موج دریا بھی کہتے ہیں اکبر بادشاہ کے عہد میں آکر آباد ہوا" — at this place
    first of all Sayyid Miran Shah Bukhari, who is also called Mauj Darya, came and settled in the era of
    Emperor Akbar (p. 79, folio 82). The mohalla grew around his residence: "جب اُسکا قیام اس مقام پر
    ہوا تو بڑا محلہ آباد ہو گیا."
  - Described as "ایک بزرگ صاحب کرامت عابد زاہد" — an elder possessed of karamat, devout and ascetic
    (p. 79, folio 82). No silsila is stated; no death year is stated.
  - **The jagir**: "بادشاہ نے بکمال ارادت و دو لاکھہ روپیے کی جاگیر درویشان خانقاہ کے خرچ کے لئے اُسکو
    وقف ہو کے تھے" — the emperor, in complete devotion, made over to him as waqf a jagir of **two lakh
    rupees** for the expenses of the dervishes of the khanqah; the jagir contained the **qasba بٹالہ**
    (Batala) and "اگزار [OCR?]" (p. 79, folio 82).
  - The mohalla was very close to Langar Khan's mohalla and was also called "بیبی وڈی [OCR?] کا محلہ";
    "بڑی بی بی" was the title of the founder's wife — "بڑی بی بی بانی محلہ کی زوجہ کا خطاب تھا"
    (p. 79, folio 82). The chunk gives the wife no name.
  - It stayed settled to the end of the Chughtai (Mughal) sultanate and was ruined "سکھوں کی غارت اور
    احمد شاہی حملوں کے وقت" — at the time of the Sikhs' plunder and the Ahmad Shahi attacks (p. 79,
    folio 82).
  - **What survives, and what took the site**: "بانی محلہ سید موج دریا کا مقبرہ اب تک ہاں موجود ہے" —
    the founder's maqbara is there to this day; some of his descendants live in Lahore ("کچھہ تولا
    [OCR?] لاہور میں") and some reside in qasba Batala; and "اس محلے کے وسط میں اب کوٹھی مکلوڈ [OCR?]
    صاحب بہادر لفٹنٹ گورنر بہادر سابق بنی ہوئی ہے" — in the middle of the mohalla now stands the kothi
    of McLeod[OCR?] Sahib, former Lieutenant-Governor, with some of the land gone into the roads (p. 79,
    folio 82).
  - The archive's location for this row is Edward Road, Anarkali; the chunk gives no street, only
    proximity to Langar Khan's mohalla and to Mazang (pp. 76–79, folios 79–82).

- **tahir-bandagi-qadri** (محلہ میانی / شیخ محمد طاہر قادری و نقشبندی) — the fullest archive material in
  the chunk, and the origin account of the burial ground the archive row sits in:
  - **Arrival and order.** "شاہ جہانگیر بادشاہ کے وقت شیخ محمد طاہر قادری و نقشبندی نے شہر سرہند سے
    آکر اس مقام پر سکونت اختیار کی" — in the time of Emperor Shah Jahangir, Shaikh Muhammad Tahir,
    **Qadiri and Naqshbandi**, came from the city of **Sirhind** and took up residence at this place
    (p. 85, folio 88). The book gives him both affiliations in one breath, against the archive row's bare
    "Qadri".
  - "چونکہ محمد طاہر عالم و فاضل و فقیر کامل تہا چند سال میں ہزاروں لوگ اُسکے مرید و شاگرد ہو گئے" —
    since Muhammad Tahir was learned, accomplished and a perfect faqir, within a few years **thousands
    became his murids and pupils**, and a magnificent settlement grew (p. 85, folio 88).
  - **Death year.** "سنہ ۱۰۴۰ [OCR?] ہجری میں وہ مر گیا جسکی تاریخ غم کے لفظ سے نکلی" — he died in the
    year **1040[OCR?] AH**, whose chronogram came out from the word **"غم"** (gham, grief) (p. 85,
    folio 88). No month or day is stated. The year matches the archive row's "8 Muharram 1040 AH"; the
    numeral carries an `[OCR?]` flag here.
  - **The madrasa, and free teaching** — see Practices (p. 85, folio 88).
  - **The sack and the books.** The mohalla stayed settled after his death "اخیر سلطنت اسلام تک", to the
    end of the Islamic sultanate; the plunderers looted it "جبر و نذر [OCR?]" and "ہزاروں قرآن و کتابیں
    لوٹ کر بیچنے [OCR?] لگے" — looted **thousands of Qurans and books** and began to sell them; when they
    found this brought nothing, "باہر گاؤں پہینک کر چلے گئے", they threw them outside the village and
    left, and on leaving set fire to the mohalla in anger, "جس سے وہ جلکر خاکستر ہو گیا", so that it
    burned to ashes (pp. 85–86, folios 88–89).
  - **What stands.** "اب علاوہ [OCR?] ان محلہ سے ایک تو مزار شیخ محمد طاہر موجود ہے اور دوسری کسی قدر
    عمارت کہنہ مدرسہ شیخ محمد طاہر کا ابتک موجود" — of that mohalla, first the **mazar of Shaikh
    Muhammad Tahir** exists, and second some old building of **his madrasa** still exists (p. 86,
    folio 89).
  - **The graveyard.** "بعد ویرانی اس محلہ کے شہر لاہور کے لوگوں نے اس جگہ کو قبرستان بنا لیا … چنانچہ
    اب بہی قبرستان ہے ہزاروں قبریں وہان بنی ہوئی ہیں اور آئندہ دفنی جاتی ہیں پختہ احاطے بہت سے بنائے
    گئے ہیں" — after the mohalla's desolation the people of the city of Lahore **made this place a
    graveyard**; it is a graveyard still, thousands of graves are made there, burials continue, and many
    **pakka enclosures** have been built (p. 86, folio 89). This is the chunk's account of how the ground
    the archive calls Miani Sahib came to be a burial ground, and it derives the ground's existence from
    this saint's ruined mohalla.
  - **The place-name.** "چونکہ میانا پنجابی زبان میں مولوی عالم فاضل کو کہتے ہیں اور وہی لوگ اسمیں
    رہتے اسواسطے اس بستی کا نام میانی مشہور ہو گیا" — since **"میانا" in Punjabi means a learned
    maulvi**, and such people lived there, the settlement became known as **Miani** (p. 85, folio 88).
  - Location given only as "موضع مزنگ سے بسمت نیبرت [OCR?]", in the [OCR?] direction from mauza Mazang
    (p. 85, folio 88).

- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** (پیروں کا محلہ / سید شاہ ابو المعالی قادری):
  - **He founded the mohalla and lived in it.** "یہ محلہ جہانگیر بادشاہ کے وقت سید شاہ ابو المعالی
    قادری نے آباد کیا تہا خود بہی یہان ہی سکونت پذیر تہا" — this mohalla was settled in the time of
    Emperor Jahangir by Sayyid Shah Abu al-Maali Qadri, and he himself resided here (p. 87, folio 90).
    The book names him **قادری**, matching the archive id; it gives **no death date** (the archive row's
    "16 Rabi ul Awal 1024 AH" is not in this chunk) and no genealogy.
  - **Dispersal and flight into the walled city.** At the ruin of the sultanate the inhabitants scattered
    and "شاہ ابو المعالی کی اولاد شہر کے حصار کے اندر جا رہی اور محلہ ویران ہو گیا" — Shah Abu
    al-Maali's **offspring went inside the city's hisar** and the mohalla was left desolate (p. 87,
    folio 90).
  - **The rauza survives, and is a live festival site** — quoted in full under Practices: mela on both
    Eids, nazrana, annual urs (p. 87, folio 90). "پرانی عمارات میں سے روضہ یعنی مقبرہ شاہ ابو المعالی
    پختہ بنا ہوا موجود ہے" — of the old buildings the **rauza, that is the maqbara, of Shah Abu al-Maali
    stands built pakka**.
  - The chunk names no custodian, no khanqah and no endowment for this shrine — **(not stated)**.

- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** — **two figures, and the archive row may answer to either; see Doubts
  before using this bullet.** The book treats them as separate men in separate mohallas:
  - **(a) سید چراغ شاہ گیلانی** (محلہ سید چراغ شاہ گیلانی, p. 79, folio 82). His mohalla lay east of
    Mauj Darya Bukhari's; "سادات گیلانی اسمیں سکونت رکھتی تھی", Gilani sayyids resided in it; it was
    settled in the time of "شاہ جہاں شاہ [OCR?]" — "جاگیر آباد ہوا", settled as/with a jagir — and stayed
    settled a long while, until "[illegible] و یہ علی [OCR?] کے وقت غارت گروں نے برباد و ویران کر دیا",
    the plunderers laid it waste. **"سید چراغ شاہ کا مقبرہ و مسجد پختہ اب تک موجود ہے"** — Sayyid
    Chiragh Shah's maqbara and pakka mosque exist to this day; **"مسجد تو سرکاری قبضہ میں ہو"**, the
    mosque is in **government possession** and "یہ صاحب اگوٹنٹ [OCR?] جنرل کا وقت دیوان رہتا ہے"
    (a reference to the Adjutant[OCR?] General's establishment, the wording broken); **"مقبرہ یہ صاحب
    مقبرہ کی اولاد کا قبضہ ہو"**, the maqbara is in the possession of the tomb-holder's descendants.
    Site: "یہ محلا اُس موقع آباد تھا جس جگہ اب محکمہ چیف کورٹ کی عمارت بنی شروع ہوئی ہے" — the mohalla
    stood where the **building of the Chief Court department has begun to be built** (p. 79, folio 82).
    The archive row's location, The Mall near the Lahore High Court, answers to this sentence; its
    principal figure's name does not.
  - **(b) سید عبد الرزاق** (محلہ دولا واڑی, pp. 79–80, folios 82–83) — the name the archive row gives its
    figure. The book makes him **a khalifa of Sayyid Mauj Darya Bukhari**: "سید عبد الرزاق کی جو ایک
    بزرگ سید موج دریا بخاری کے خلیفوں میں سے تھا سب سے اول یہان آکر سکونت پذیر ہوا اور رونق آبادی کی بڑھ
    گئی" — Sayyid Abd al-Razzaq, an elder among Mauj Darya Bukhari's khalifas, was the first to come and
    settle here and the mohalla's bustle grew (p. 80, folio 83). Its population stayed at its height to
    the end of the Chughtai sultanate because in the khanqah "شاہ [OCR?] و عبد الرزاق کی میں کوئی نہ کوئی
    بزرگ ایسا قیام پذیر رہتا جسکے سے تمام مزارہ [OCR?] کو فیض پہنچتا تھا", some elder or other always
    resided there from whom faiz reached all (p. 80, folio 83). **His pakka rauza stands**: "ایک تو روضہ
    پختہ سید عبد الرزاق کی کا موجود ہے جسکو بازار اُرار [OCR?] گلی میں ٹبیلہ [OCR?] گنبد کہتے ہیں" —
    called "ٹبیلہ [OCR?] گنبد" in "بازار اُرار [OCR?] گلی"; and a pakka mosque stood inside his **احاطہ**
    (enclosure) (p. 80, folio 83). Its later history — powder magazine, musket[OCR?] store, sale to a
    meat contractor — is under Practices. No silsila and no death date are stated for him.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **شاہ شرف** (شاہ شرف کا محلہ [OCR?], pp. 80–81, folios 83–84): "ایک عالم و کامل شخص" with **thousands
  of murids**, founder of the mohalla outside **بہاٹی [OCR?] دروازہ** (Bhati Gate), which ran from the gate
  to "صلح [OCR?] لاہور کی کچہری و برف خانہ", the kachahri and ice-house. He built a magnificent mosque and
  **built his own pakka maqbara in his lifetime**. Both were demolished for Ranjit Singh's glacis and his
  coffin reburied — under Practices.
- **شاہ درگاہی / درگاہی شاہ** (درگاہی شاہ کا محلہ, p. 82, folio 85): "یہ شاہ درگاہی قادری سلسلہ کا ایک
  فقیر عابد و زاہد تھا" — a faqir of the **Qadiri silsila**, devout and ascetic. Of his mohalla "اب
  اُسکا کوئی نشان باقی نہیں صرف ایک مزار شاہ درگاہی کا باقی ہے جو سر راہ بجانب شرق واقع ہے" — nothing
  remains but his mazar, on the roadside to the east; the mohalla lay east of Lakhi mohalla at one
  bazaar's distance. His **khanqah's enclosure contained a well** still in use for healing — see
  Practices and Karamat. No dates.
- **شاہ بدر الدین گیلانی** (شاہ بدر کا محلہ, pp. 82–84, folios 85–87): "عابد زاہد و صاحب کرامت"; settled
  the mohalla south of Shah Dargahi's and "اپنے ہم قوم شریف سید لوگ اسمیں آباد کئے", settled his own
  noble sayyid kinsmen in it; inhabited "اکبری عہد سے اخیر سلطنت چغتائی تک", from the Akbari era to the
  end of the Chughtai sultanate. The massacre of its sayyids and the fate of his mosque are under
  Practices. No dates.
- **عبد اللہ شاہ, a Baloch of Mazang** (p. 77, folio 80): "عبد اللہ شاہ نام ایک بلوچ ایسا صاحب باطن فقیر
  ہوا جسکے لاہور کے علما و فضلا مرید تھے" — a Baloch named Abdullah Shah became so inward a faqir that
  **the ulama and learned men of Lahore were his murids**. A settlement "بستی گوشہ [OCR?] عبد اللہ شاہ
  مدت شاہ [OCR?]" with a pakka rampart and pakka houses is named after him, settled by "مسجد اللہ شاہ
  [OCR?] بلوچ" (p. 78, folio 81 — the wording is broken; see Doubts).
- **پیر عزیز مزنگ** (محلہ پیر عزیز مزنگ المشہور موضع مزنگ, p. 77, folio 80): "ایک بزرگ پیر عزیز مزنگ نے
  کابل سے آکر آباد کیا تھا" — an elder, Pir Aziz Mazang, came **from Kabul** and settled it; he lived
  there all his life and his offspring held it after him. "مزنگ ایک گوت مغلوں کی قوم میں سے ہے" — Mazang
  is a **got of the Mughal community**.
- **حاجی محمد سعید** (محلہ دولا واڑی, p. 80, folio 83): the elder whose presence saved the mohalla from
  plunder "ابد علی [OCR?] کے وقت" — "اسکو سبھی اُسکا لحاظ کرتے تھے", everyone showed him regard; **Ahmad
  Shah Durrani came to pay him ziyarat** and posted a royal guard (see Practices). After his death a Sikh
  misl sacked the mohalla. "تیسرا احاطہ مزار حاجی محمد سعید اب تک موجود ہے" — the **enclosure of his
  mazar still exists**, very large, its gate of access to the west, and among its graves is that of
  "حاجی مسروح [OCR?]" (p. 80, folio 83).
- **تاجا** (تاج پورہ, p. 78, folio 81): "اسکو ایک شخص مسمی تاجا نے آباد کیا تھا جو ایک فقیر تھا اور اُسکی
  قبر بستی کے اندر ہے" — settled by one Taja, **a faqir, whose grave is inside the settlement**.
- **میاں وڈا** (محلہ تیل پورہ, p. 89, folio 92): the **khanqah and madrasa** "مدرسہ مسجد [OCR?] تیل
  المشہور میان وڈا", famous as Mian Wada. Its dervishes and students stayed through the plundering and
  "چنانچہ وہ مدرسہ ابتک آباد ہے", the madrasa is settled to this day — see Practices.
- **گلابی شاہ** (p. 88, folio 91): named only as the site "متصل گلابی شاہ" adjoining which محلہ سید سر
  [OCR?] was settled. Nothing further.
- **The sayyids of محلہ سید سر [OCR?]** (pp. 88–89, folios 91–92): "اُن سیدوں کے ہزاروں مرید تھے" —
  those sayyids had thousands of murids; their healing tank is under Practices; their destruction by the
  Bhangi misl after an informer's charge is under Practices.
- **"قبرستان فقرائے چشتیہ خیر شاہی [OCR?]"** (p. 87, folio 90): a **graveyard of Chishti faqirs** built
  on Zain Khan's maidan, containing a pakka mosque and a pakka "متہا بر [OCR?]", the rest of the land
  under cultivation.
- **مفتی غلام سرور** (p. 78, folio 81): of mauza Mazang, "مصنف و شاعر تمام زمانہ میں مشہور", famous in
  the whole age as author and poet; "اب تک اٹھارہ [OCR?] کتابیں نظم و نثر لکھ چکا ہے مثل خزینۃ الاصفیا و
  گنجینہ سروری" — has written **eighteen[OCR?] books** in verse and prose, such as **خزینۃ الاصفیا** and
  **گنجینہ سروری**. A hagiographical author placed in a Lahore suburb by this book.
- **Other notables of mauza Mazang** (p. 78, folio 81): **سراج الدین**, "ایک بزرگ و فاضل و عابد" of Sikh
  times; a second whose name is "[illegible]"; **مفتی غلام محمد**, "بلوچوں کی مسجد کے امام" and a
  practising physician, "اکثر بیمار اُنکے معالجہ سے اچھے ہو جاتے"; **میاں ساون شاعر**, a Baloch, "اُسکے
  پنجابی اشعار ایک مشہور ہیں", his Punjabi verses famous; **میاں فرید شاعر**, "جو پنجاب کے شعرا میں سے
  استاد شمار ہوتا ہے", counted a master among the poets of the Punjab.
- **لنگر خان بلوچ ملتانی** (pp. 76–77, folios 79–80): "مدارالمہام" of the sultanate, by whose goodwill
  and "دولت سگالی [OCR?]" **Humayun took Multan**; he came to Lahore, was given a jagir and permission to
  live there, and his charge ("عہد [OCR?]") was attached to **شہزادہ کامران**, the emperor's brother, in
  whose jagir the Punjab lay. He settled a separate mohalla south of Lahore, "گنبد [OCR?] لنگر خان" by
  name, on the north-west corner of present mauza Mazang; he and his offspring built great havelis there
  and held them for hundreds of years.
- **زین خان [OCR?] نائب صوبہ لاہور** (p. 87, folio 90): "زین خان [OCR?] نائب صوبہ لاہور نے اپنے نام پر
  اسکو آباد کیا تہا قریب ایکسو برس کے آباد رہا" — the **deputy governor of Lahore** settled the mohalla
  in his own name; it lasted about **a hundred years**; his own magnificent palace stood there, of which
  a pakka hammam and cellar survived into the Maharaja's time and are now gone.
- **دایہ لاڈو** (محلہ دایہ لاڈو, p. 86, folio 89): "ابک معزز دایہ تہی" — an honoured **wet-nurse** of the
  time of the "شاہان [OCR?] سلطنت اسلام", who settled the mohalla; it was larger in length and breadth
  than all the city's mohallas, bounded west by Dola Wari ("مد لا واڑی [OCR?]") and east by mohalla
  "زمین خان [OCR?]" (i.e., Zain Khan). Her pakka **mosque** is its only relic — see Practices.
- **دائی انگا** (محلہ دائی انگا, p. 88, folio 91): "شاہان اسلام کے وقت دائی انگا نام ایک معزز دایہ نے
  آباد کیا تہا" — another honoured wet-nurse; the mohalla held "عالیشان حویلیان امرائے عظام کی" and was
  "تمام محلوں سے آراستہ محلہ", the most adorned of all. Its site is now "سٹیشن ریلوی کا کارخانہ", the
  railway station workshop, and only her pakka **mosque** survives, turned into a kothi.
- **Officials, contractors and Sikh figures named**: **فقیر عزیز الدین**, through whose agency Shah
  Sharaf's coffin was reburied (p. 81, folio 84); **جمعدار خوشحال سنگھ**, whose "ٹبلکار [OCR?]" held
  mauza Mazang "اجہرہ [OCR?]" and made a garden on Lakhi mohalla (p. 81, folio 84); **محمد سلطان ٹہیکہ
  دار**, who bought that garden and built the kothi now known as Hall Sahib's (p. 82, folio 85); **منشی
  نجم الدین ٹہیکہ دار گوشت**, meat contractor, who took Abd al-Razzaq's mosque and maqbara from
  government (p. 80, folio 83); **سردار خان بلوچ**, lambardar of Mazang, whose pakka mosque is his
  memorial and who demolished Shah Badr's mosque (pp. 77, 83; folios 80, 86); **جے سنگہ کنہیہ** and the
  grandson of **نور الدین خان**, who bought him off with ten thousand rupees (p. 87, folio 90); **کرنیل
  ہال صاحب ڈپٹی کمشنر سابق لاہور**, **مکلوڈ [OCR?] صاحب … لفٹنٹ گورنر**, **سمسن [OCR?] صاحب ڈپٹی کمشنر**,
  **ہال رائڈ [OCR?] صاحب ڈائرکٹر** (pp. 79, 81, 85; folios 82, 84, 88); **لالہ رتن چند / دیوان رتن چند**,
  whose garden covers Daya Lado's mohalla (p. 86, folio 89).
- **The Sikh misls named as plunderers**: **کنہیا** (twice repulsed, then victorious at Shah Badr's
  mohalla, p. 83, folio 86); **بہنگی و رام گڈہیہ [OCR?] و کنہیا** acting together against the Khojas
  (p. 84, folio 87); **بہنگی** against Zain Khan's mohalla and against محلہ سید سر [OCR?] (pp. 87, 88;
  folios 90, 91); "دربیٹھ [OCR?] ملکے [OCR?] مثل" against Dola Wari (p. 80, folio 83).
- **Places and quarters named in passing**: بٹالہ (in Mauj Darya's jagir, and where his descendants
  live), کابل (Pir Aziz's origin), سرہند (Shaikh Muhammad Tahir's origin), ملتان (Humayun's conquest),
  جموں (refuge of Lakhi mohalla's people), انار کلی (the cantonment moved from it), میان میر (railway
  station "کا امرتسر کی شرکت [OCR?]"), and the settlements بستی مراد بخش, قطعہ مہرا [OCR?], بخشی پورہ
  [OCR?], بہو نند پورہ, تاج پورہ, جاٹ پورہ, تیل پورہ, گنج, قصابان (pp. 76–89, folios 79–92).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **An imperial waqf of two lakh rupees for a khanqah's dervishes, with the estate named.** For Mauj
  Darya Bukhari: "بادشاہ نے بکمال ارادت و دو لاکھہ روپیے کی جاگیر درویشان خانقاہ کے خرچ کے لئے اُسکو وقف
  ہو کے تھے اور اس جاگیر میں قصبہ بٹالہ و اگزار [OCR?] تھا" — Akbar made over a jagir of **two lakh
  rupees**, as waqf, **for the expenses of the dervishes of the khanqah**, the jagir containing the qasba
  **Batala** (p. 79, folio 82). The chunk's one endowment figure, and it is attached to an archive row.
- **Mela on both Eids, nazrana and an annual urs at Shah Abu al-Maali's rauza.** "یہ وہ مکان ہے جہان
  دونو عیدوں کا میلہ ہوتا ہے اور شہر کے لوگ بکمال اعتقاد مقبرہ پر جاکر نذرانہ دیتے ہیں عرس سالانہ بہی
  ہزار ہا [OCR?] پر ہوا کرتا ہے" — this is the place where the **mela of both Eids** is held; the people
  of the city go to the maqbara with complete faith and give **nazrana**; and the **annual urs** too is
  held, with thousands[OCR?] (p. 87, folio 90). The only urs in the chunk, and the only shrine here
  described as a going concern in the author's present.
- **A shrine well with a Sunday clinic, a fixed offering in kind and a cash charaghi.** At Shah Dargahi's
  mazar: "اب بھی وہ چاہ مزار سے بجانب جنوب موجود ہے اور اتوار کے روز ہندو و مسلمان لوگ اپنے بچون کو
  جنکو پانی ولنے کی بیماری ہوتی ہے چاہ پر لیجا کر نہلاتے ہیں ہر ایک شخص چار روٹیاں میٹھی اور چار نمکین
  اپنے ہمراہ لیجاتا ہے بعد غسل کے وہ روٹیاں زمیندار چاہ کا مع ایک پیسہ چراغی کے لیے لیتا ہے" — the well
  survives south of the mazar; **on Sundays Hindus and Muslims alike** bring children with the "پانی
  ولنے [OCR?]" disease and bathe them; **each person brings four sweet rotis and four salty**; after the
  bath **the zamindar of the well takes those rotis together with one paisa as charaghi** (p. 82,
  folio 85). A priced, shared, weekly rite with a named collector — the most exactly documented shrine
  economy in the chunk.
- **A free madrasa at a Lahore shrine site.** Of Shaikh Muhammad Tahir: "شیخ محمد طاہر نے بہت برست
  [OCR?] تک اس جگہ پر مدرسہ جاری رکہا اور علوم فقہ و حدیث و تفسیر طلبا کو مفت پڑہانا رہا مرید بہی بہت
  کئے اور سلوک کی تلقین کی" — he kept a **madrasa** running at this place for many years[OCR?] and
  taught **fiqh, hadith and tafsir free of charge**, made many murids, and gave instruction in suluk
  (p. 85, folio 88). Some of the madrasa building was still standing when the author wrote (p. 86,
  folio 89).
- **A ruined shrine quarter converted into the city's burial ground.** "بعد ویرانی اس محلہ کے شہر لاہور
  کے لوگوں نے اس جگہ کو قبرستان بنا لیا … ہزاروں قبریں وہان بنی ہوئی ہیں اور آئندہ دفنی جاتی ہیں پختہ
  احاطے بہت سے بنائے گئے ہیں" (p. 86, folio 89) — Lahore's people made the desolated Miani mohalla a
  graveyard; thousands of graves, burials continuing, **many pakka enclosures** built. Institutional
  origin, not legend.
- **Mosques as powder magazines under Ranjit Singh, then as government property, then as bungalows — the
  chunk's most repeated institutional pattern.**
  - Abd al-Razzaq's maqbara and mosque: "مہاراج [OCR?] رنجیت [OCR?] سنگھ کے وقت مقبرہ اور مسجد دونو میں
    بارود [OCR?] بہری رہتی تھی اور مسجد کے صحن میں لوہا آہنی گولی بنانے تھے" — both kept full of
    gunpowder, **iron shot made in the mosque courtyard**; under the English both were emptied and a
    "مسکوٹ [OCR?]" made; when the cantonment moved from Anarkali, "مسجد و مقبرہ منشی نجم الدین ٹہیکہ دار
    گوشت نے سرکار سے لے لیا", **the meat contractor Munshi Najm al-Din took mosque and maqbara from the
    government**, repaired and reopened the mosque, and a grave was made in the maqbara (p. 80,
    folio 83).
  - Shah Badr's mosque: powder and shot stored in it, **a company of soldiers stationed there**, and
    gunpowder manufactured in the mosque's hujras; under the English all the powder was sunk in the
    mosque's well ("بہت چہوڑا نہایت گہرا [OCR?]") and earthed over (p. 83, folio 86).
  - The Khojas' mosque: "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ کے عہد میں وہی عالیشان مسجد پختہ منجملہ عمارت محلے کی باقی
    تہی اُسمیں بارود بہری رہتی تہی" — the sole surviving building, kept full of powder (p. 84, folio 87).
  - Sayyid Chiragh Shah's mosque "سرکاری قبضہ میں", in government possession, with the maqbara left to
    the tomb-holder's descendants (p. 79, folio 82).
  - Dai Anga's mosque "جسکو صاحبان انگریزوں نے کوٹہی کی صورت بنا لیا ہو", made by the English gentlemen
    into the form of a kothi (p. 88, folio 91).
- **A mosque recovered by litigation on a claim of hereditary custodianship, then sold.** In the year
  "سنہ ۱۸۵۶ [OCR?]", after the city's **nazul** was recorded, "ایک شخص مسمی سید شمسی [OCR?] کچہری ضلح میں
  دستویدار [OCR?] ہوا کہ اس مسجد کا متولی بعہد آبادی محلہ میرا دادا تہا اسرکار [OCR?] یہ مسجد مجھکو
  دیدیجیوے [OCR?] تو میں اسکو آباد کروں اور مسلمان اسمیں نماز پڑھیں" — one Sayyid Shamsi[OCR?] filed in
  the district kachahri that **his grandfather had been the mosque's mutawalli** when the mohalla was
  inhabited, and asked that it be given to him so he might restore it and Muslims pray in it. He produced
  **witnesses of the Khoja community**, who declared "مدعی کا دادا ہمارا مرشد اور امام تہا ہمارے بہی
  بزرگ اس محلے میں رہتے تہے جو اُنہون نے یہ مسجد تعمیر کرکے اُسکو دیدی تہی اب اسکا حق ہے کہ مسجد اسکو
  دیدی جانے" — the plaintiff's grandfather was **our murshid and imam**, our own elders lived in this
  mohalla and **built this mosque and gave it to him**, so it is his right. "سمسن [OCR?] صاحب ڈپٹی کمشنر
  نے مسجد اُسکو دیدی" — the Deputy Commissioner gave him the mosque; "ایک دو سال تو اُس نے مسجد کو
  مسلمانی طور پر آباد کیا پہر کوٹہی کی صورت بنا کر انگریز کرایہ دار اُسمیں رکہہ دیے اور کہ ابہ [OCR?]
  کہا نے لگا پہر فروخت کر ڈالی" — for a year or two he ran it as a mosque, then turned it into a kothi,
  **put English tenants in it and took rent, then sold it**; it stands as a kothi still (pp. 84–85,
  folios 87–88). A complete case: founder-patrons, hereditary mutawalli, community witnesses, colonial
  nazul, decree, and alienation.
- **A mosque claimed out of nazul on a village-boundary argument and mined for bricks.** Of Shah Badr's
  mosque: "سردار خان بلوچ لمبردار موضع مزنگ نے سب پہلے اسکا کہ وہ مسجد حد بست [OCR?] موضع مزنگ میں تہی
  دعوی مسجد کا کیا او مسجد پر نزول سے خارج ہو کر اسکو ملگئی" — the lambardar of Mazang claimed the
  mosque on the ground that it lay **within mauza Mazang's survey boundary**, and the mosque, **excluded
  from nazul**, came to him. "اُس نے مسجد کو چہا در ہی [OCR?] کی کان سمجھ کر گرانی شروع کی اور ہزاروں
  روپیہ کی اینٹیں بضرورت تعمیر بیچہ [OCR?] تہا و فی سرکار میں بیچی [OCR?] البین مسجد اور حجروں کی
  بنیادیں اخیر تک نکال لیں" — treating it **as a mine of bricks**, he began pulling it down and sold
  **thousands of rupees' worth of bricks** for building, including to the government, and dug out the
  foundations of mosque and hujras; the whole fell easily because the fabric was thick lime plaster over
  kachi-pakki work. He petitioned repeatedly to dig the well's bricks too, "مگر سرکار نے دبی [OCR?] ہوئی
  بارود کا پیر [OCR?] نکالنا منظور نہ کیا", but the government refused to have the buried gunpowder taken
  out (p. 83, folio 86).
- **A tomb demolished for a city fortification, and the corpse moved by order.** At Bhati Gate, when
  Ranjit Singh's pakka moat and double gates were built, "ضرور یہ بنی [OCR?] کہ بہاٹی دروازے کے باہر کے
  دروازے کے آگے کہلا ہوا میدان ہو اسلئے وہ سنگ بین [OCR?] مسجد او مقبرہ دونو گروا دیئے گئے" — an open
  field was needed before the outer gate, so **mosque and maqbara were both pulled down**; "بڑی مشکل سے
  یہ عمارت گری اور شاہ شرف کی لاش کا صندوق نکلا" — with great difficulty the building fell and Shah
  Sharaf's **coffin came out**; "آکر فقیر عزیز الدین کی معرفت احاطہ مزار حاجی محمد سعید میں مقام و دولا
  واڑی [OCR?] قتیا [OCR?] گیا اور مہاراجہ کے حکم سے چہوٹا سا چبوترہ پختہ اُسپر بنا دیا گیا جو ابتک
  موجود" — through **Faqir Aziz al-Din** it was placed in the enclosure of Haji Muhammad Saeed's mazar at
  Dola Wari, and **by the Maharaja's order a small pakka chabutra was built over it**, standing still.
  The pakka foundations of the demolished mosque and tomb are still visible in the maidan outside Bhati
  Gate (pp. 80–81, folios 83–84). A relocation of a saint's body, ordered by the state that destroyed the
  tomb.
- **A shrine as sanctuary: a royal guard posted for a living saint's sake.** "جب احمد شاہ درانی
  [illegible] لاہور آیا تو حاجی محمد سعید کی زیارت کو حاضر ہوا اور شاہی پہرہ محلہ میں بٹھا [OCR?] بیٹھے
  [OCR?] خبر دیا کہ کوئی غارت گر محلہ پر دست اندازی نہ ہو" — when **Ahmad Shah Durrani** came to Lahore
  he presented himself for **ziyarat of Haji Muhammad Saeed** and set a **royal guard** in the mohalla,
  giving notice that no plunderer should lay a hand on it; the mohalla was sacked only after the saint's
  death (p. 80, folio 83).
- **A khanqah whose continuous occupancy is given as the reason for a quarter's prosperity.** Dola Wari
  stayed at its height because in the khanqah "کوئی نہ کوئی بزرگ ایسا قیام پذیر رہتا جسکے سے تمام مزارہ
  [OCR?] کو فیض پہنچتا تھا" (p. 80, folio 83) — an economic claim about a shrine's effect on a
  neighbourhood.
- **A mosque held as a house by a sadhu, then taken back by force.** Of Daya Lado's mosque: "مہاراجہ کے
  وقت اسمیں ایک ہندو سادہ [OCR?] نے اپنا گہر بنا لیا تہا مسجد میں تہاکروں [OCR?] کی تصویریں رکہہ دی
  تہیں مگر انگریزوں کے وقت ایک مسلمان فقیر نذ شاہی [OCR?] نے چند مسلمانوں کو جمع کرکے براہ زبردستی اُسکو
  مسجد بیدخل کر دیا وہ سادہ خاموش ہو کر مسجد سے نکل گیا ابتک وہ مسجد اُسی فقیر یا قبضہ میں ہے" — under
  the Maharaja a **Hindu sadhu[OCR?] made it his house and placed images of thakurs[OCR?] in it**; under
  the English a Muslim faqir gathered some Muslims and **evicted him by force**; the sadhu left silently,
  and the mosque is in that faqir's possession still, with a room built beside it for his residence
  (p. 86, folio 89).
- **A tank credited with healing, run on the sayyids' belief.** At محلہ سید سر [OCR?]: "ایک بہت [OCR?]
  تالاب پختہ بنا ہوا تہا جسکے سبب وہ محلہ سید سر کہلا تہا … اُنکا یہ عتقاد [OCR?] تہا کہ اس تالاب کے
  پانی میں نہا ہی بیمار بچے ہو جاتے ہیں" — a large pakka **tank**, from which the mohalla took its name;
  their belief was that **sick children became well by bathing in its water**, and "اپنے اعتقاد کے پہلے
  سے اکثر اچھے ہو جاتے", by the strength of that belief most did (p. 88, folio 91). The second healing
  water in the chunk, and the author attributes this one to belief rather than to karamat.
- **A khanqah's dervishes and students as the only institution to survive a quarter's collapse.** At Teel
  Pura, after the oil mandi closed and the populace scattered, "درویش طالب علم میان وڈا کے خانقاہ پر
  بدستور بیٹہے رہی کبہی ایسے غارت گر آتے جو درویشوں کے کپڑے اُتار لیجاتی کبہی ایسے رحم دل آتے جو و
  بجامات [OCR?] چنانچہ وہ مدرسہ ابتک آباد ہے" — the dervishes and students stayed at Mian Wada's
  khanqah as before; some plunderers stripped the dervishes' clothes, some merciful ones clothed them;
  **the madrasa is inhabited to this day** (p. 89, folio 92).
- **Trades, markets and communal segregation by royal order.** An **oil mandi** at Teel Pura, closed by
  "بے حالکی [OCR?]" (p. 89, folio 92). At Qasaban: "قصابان گاؤ کش کی بستی شاہی حکم سے علیحدہ مقرر کی گئی
  تہی کیونکہ نام ہندو رعایا اُن سے نفرت کرتی تہی اور مناسب سمجہا گیا تہا کہ یہ شہر سے الگ سکونت رکہیں" —
  the settlement of the cow-killing butchers was **fixed apart by royal order** in Jahangir's reign
  because the Hindu populace abhorred them and it was thought proper they live away from the city; rich
  butchers lived there and **a pakka magnificent mosque** had been built (p. 89, folio 92 — the passage
  breaks off mid-sentence at the chunk's end).
- **Communal succession and local authority in a suburb, over three regimes.** At Mazang: the Mughal clan
  first, then the Baloch after Langar Khan's mohalla fell, then the Arains arriving in "چالیسی قحط", the
  Forty famine, when houses stood empty — "یہ بستی تین اقوام میں مشترک ہوئی". The Mughals' residual power
  was documentary: "کوئی بیعنامہ یا گروی نامہ اُنکی مُہر و دستخط کے بغیر جائز گنا نہ جاتا تھا" — **no
  deed of sale or mortgage was held valid without their seal and signature**. Under Ranjit Singh the
  Baloch dominated and the Mughals and Arains were subject; after Sardar Khan Baloch's death the Arains
  became strong, and "اب بلوچوں میں سے صرف ایک لمبردار اور ہے اور سب لمبردار ارائین میں اعلی لمبردار بھی
  شادی [OCR?] ارائین ہے" — only one lambardar is now Baloch, the rest Arain, and the chief lambardar is
  Arain too (pp. 77–78, folios 80–81).
- **Brick-robbing as a systematic trade.** "کشمیریان خشت فروش نے اُن دیواروں کو بیخ و بنیاد سے نکال
  لیا" — the **Kashmiri brick-sellers** dug out Langar Khan's walls root and foundation (p. 76,
  folio 79); the same trade destroyed Daya Lado's walls (p. 86, folio 89); and at Lakhi mohalla, when the
  brick-sellers had the foundations dug, "اکثر و بیشتر خزینے [OCR?] نکلے", **treasure came out** more
  often than not (p. 81, folio 84).
- **Gunpowder buried under a mosque well detonates.** A neighbouring zamindar made a cattle pen on the
  site of Shah Badr's filled well and lit a fire; "آگ کی گرمی نے بارود میں اثر کیا … بارود اُڑ ہی زمین
  میں ایک زلزلہ نمودار ہوا چاہ کی عمارت کی اینٹیں ہوا میں اُڑ کر دور دور جا پڑیں پچاس پچاس گز تک زمین
  میں مغ خاک [OCR?] پڑ گیا و زمیندار جان بحق تسلیم ہوئے" — an **earthquake** appeared, the well's bricks
  flew far, the ground was pitted for fifty yards, and the zamindar[s] died; "چار بہل [OCR?] مارے گئے",
  four "بہل [OCR?]" were killed (pp. 83–84, folios 86–87).
- **Buying off plunderers, in cash and by sanad.** The Khojas "ہر وقت خوجے اُنکو روپیہ دیکر ٹالتے رہے",
  each time put the Sikhs off with money, until they resolved to fight (p. 84, folio 87); Nur al-Din
  Khan's grandson paid **ten thousand rupees nazrana** to Jai Singh Kanhiya, who promised no Sikh would
  trouble them, "مگر دہ [OCR?] وعدہ برائے نام تہا", but the promise was in name only, and the mohalla
  people's appeal to his sanad was ignored by the Bhangis (p. 87, folio 90); the Mazang Mughals survived
  "کبھی بخوشامد و منت کبھی بر رشوت کبھی بزور شجاعت", sometimes by flattery and entreaty, sometimes by
  **bribe**, sometimes by force of arms (p. 77, folio 80).
- **Self-defence works built by a quarter's residents.** Dai Anga's mohalla: "پختہ دروازے اور فصیل بنالی
  مدت تک اپنے محلے میں غارت گروں کو داخل ہونے نہ دیا" — **pakka gates and a rampart**, keeping plunderers
  out for a long time until daily resistance exhausted them (p. 88, folio 91). The Khojas' havelis and
  the strength of their **mosque's four walls** were the defence works of that quarter (p. 84, folio 87).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The healing well of Shah Dargahi.** A woman brought her small child with the "پانی ولنے [OCR?]"
  disease — "بدن پر ایسی پہنسیاں [OCR?] نکلی ہوئی ہیں جنمیں سفید پانی بہرا ہوا ہے", pustules[OCR?] on
  the body filled with white water. "درگاہی شاہ نے فرمایا کہ اسکو ہمارے چاہ کے پانی سے غسل دیدو اچھا ہو
  جائیگا چنانچہ غسل دینے سے وہ اچھا ہو گیا" — Dargahi Shah said, bathe him with our well's water and he
  will be well; and so he was. The author closes with his own attribution: "یہ تاثیر چاہ کے پانی میں اب
  بھی باقی ہے جو اُس بزرگ کی کرامات و بزرگی میں دخل ہے" — **this efficacy remains in the well's water
  still, and belongs to that elder's karamat and greatness** (p. 82, folio 85).
- **Treasure under the Lakhi mohalla.** When the brick-sellers dug the foundations, "اکثر و بیشتر خزینے
  [OCR?] نکلے"; and Jamadar Khushhal Singh's man, levelling the ground for his garden, "اُسکو محمد شاہی
  [OCR?] روپیے کا بہرا ہوا ایک دیگچہ ملا تھا" — **found a degchi filled with Muhammad-Shahi[OCR?] rupees**
  (pp. 81–82, folios 84–85).
- **Sick children healed in the Sayyid Sar tank**, which the author gives as the sayyids' own belief and
  the petitioners' — "اپنے اعتقاد کے پہلے سے اکثر اچھے ہو جاتے", most recovered by the strength of their
  belief (p. 88, folio 91). The author does not call this karamat, unlike the Dargahi Shah well.
- **Mauj Darya Bukhari is called "صاحب کرامت"** and Shah Badr al-Din Gilani "صاحب کرامت" (pp. 79, 82;
  folios 82, 85), but the chunk narrates no miracle of either.
- **The powder explosion read as fate.** After the blast on Shah Badr's mosque well: "غرض کہ حکم تقدیر
  [OCR?] [illegible] برابر" — in short, the decree of fate [illegible] (p. 84, folio 87), followed by a

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author states the section's programme in his own voice.** "و دوسرا حصہ یہ اس تذکرے میں ہے کہ عہد
  سلطنت چغتائی میں جب لاہور کی آبادی کی ترقی قدیمہ حصار کے باہر ہوئی تو کس سمت کو ہوئی اور اس آبادی میں
  مشہور محلے کون کون تھے اور کس کس شخص نے اُنکو آباد کیا تھا اب بھی کوئی نشان اُنکا باقی ہے یا نہیں ہیں"
  — the second part of this account is: when Lahore's settlement grew outside the ancient hisar under the
  Chughtai sultanate, in which direction; which the famous mohallas were; **who settled each**; and
  whether any trace of them remains (p. 76, folio 79). Every mohalla notice below answers those four
  questions in order, which is why founder, patron, ruin and surviving building are given for each.
- **His thesis on the direction and scale of the outer city.** "واضح ہو کہ بیرونی آبادی لاہور کی شاہ
  [OCR?] ہمایوں کے عہد سے شروع ہوئی اور رفتہ رفتہ سمت جنوب و جنوب مشرق و مشرق شہر آباد ہوتا چلا گیا
  یہاں تک کہ اصل شہر سے دو چند شہر باہر آباد ہو گیا" — the outer settlement **began in Humayun's reign**
  and moved gradually **south, south-east and east**, until **twice as much city stood outside as the
  original city** (p. 76, folio 79). He then makes Langar Khan Baloch Multani the first such founder.
- **He writes himself into the record as a municipal officer.** The Kashmiri benefactor "اکثر شہر کے
  محکمہ تعمیر سرکاری میں جو تحت [OCR?] مولف کتاب ہذا ہوئی یا ہوئی اور بخوبی انجام و بنا ہوئی" — worked
  often in the city's government **building department, which was under the author of this book**, and
  the work was well carried out (p. 76, folio 79). The author's own department is the vantage point from
  which the surviving buildings in this chunk are reported.
- **His etymologies, each given as the reason for a name.** میانی from Punjabi "میانا" for a learned
  maulvi (p. 85, folio 88); لکھی [OCR?] محلہ from havelis built of **lakhs** of rupees by sahukars and the
  wealthy (p. 81, folio 84); تیل پورہ from the "تیلی" oil-pressers (p. 89, folio 92); سید سر from the
  pakka tank (p. 88, folio 91); جاٹ پورہ from its Jat residents (p. 85, folio 88); قطعہ مہرا [OCR?] from
  a "مہر ترکھان" named Mehra who settled there before the Arains; تاج پورہ from the faqir Taja; بہو نند
  پورہ from one کملا who settled it in his own name (p. 78, folio 81); and مزنگ as "ایک گوت مغلوں کی
  قوم میں سے", a Mughal got (p. 77, folio 80).
- **He moralises the destruction with Persian verse, twice, at the same two sites where "صاحبان عالیشان
  کی کوٹھیاں" now stand.** Over Langar Khan's mohalla: "از گردش دوران گاہے [OCR?] چنین گاہے چنان ۔ از
  انقلاب آسمان گاہے چنین گاہے چنان" (p. 77, folio 80). Over Shah Badr's: "دور گردوں [OCR?] را بیک حالت
  نمی باشد قیام [OCR?] / [illegible] گاہ روشن [illegible] گاہ بشام [OCR?]" (p. 84, folio 87). The
  rhetorical figure is the same each time — the turning of the age — and each time it is placed on a site
  now occupied by English bungalows.
- **He records colonial reward of Indian charity without comment.** Of the Kashmiris' benefactor: "سرکار
  انگریزی اس عمل سے اپنی [OCR?] خوش ہوئی تحسین و آفرین کی سند بخشی دربار میں کرسی نشین کیا" — the
  English government was pleased, granted a **sanad of commendation**, and **seated him on a chair in the
  darbar** (p. 76, folio 79).

## Doubts

- **The archive's `shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh` cannot be resolved from this chunk, and the row's name
  and location point to two different men in it.** The row reads "Shrine of Baba Shah Chiragh — The Mall
  (near Lahore High Court), Lahore — Syed Abdul Razzaq (Shah Chiragh)". The book has (a) **سید چراغ شاہ
  گیلانی**, a Gilani sayyid whose mohalla stood exactly where "محکمہ چیف کورٹ کی عمارت" was being built,
  with a surviving maqbara and a pakka mosque in government possession (p. 79, folio 82); and (b) **سید
  عبد الرزاق**, a khalifa of Mauj Darya Bukhari, in محلہ دولا واڑی, whose pakka rauza is called "ٹبیلہ
  [OCR?] گنبد" in "بازار اُرار [OCR?] گلی" (p. 80, folio 83). **The book never equates them, never calls
  Abd al-Razzaq "Chiragh", and never calls Chiragh Shah "Abd al-Razzaq"**; they have separate mohallas,
  separate tombs and separate mosques. A human must decide which the archive row is before either set of
  facts is used, and the two sets must not be merged in the meantime.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Tahir's silsila is double here and single in the archive.** The book writes "شیخ
  محمد طاہر قادری و نقشبندی" — **Qadiri and Naqshbandi** — and makes him come from **Sirhind** (p. 85,
  folio 88). The archive row `tahir-bandagi-qadri` carries only "Qadri", and the row's own note already
  flags a discrepancy in how a survey names his order. This is a second, independent witness to the
  double affiliation and should be recorded as such. The book also never uses the name element
  **"بندگی"**; it calls him only شیخ محمد طاہر.
- **His death year carries an OCR flag on the numeral.** "سنہ ۱۰۴۰ [OCR?] ہجری" (p. 85, folio 88). The
  chronogram is given only as coming out of the word "غم", with no chronogram phrase printed, so the year
  cannot be checked from the chunk. No month or day is stated — the archive's "8 Muharram" is not here.
- **`shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari`: the book's name for him differs from the archive's.** Book: "سید
  میران شاہ بخاری"; archive: "Hazrat Meeran Muhammad Shah Bukhari". The book gives **no death year** and
  **no silsila**, so the archive's 1604 and Suhrawardi are not corroborated here. "اگزار [OCR?]", the
  second place in his jagir, cannot be read; "کچھہ تولا [OCR?] لاہور" (where his descendants live) is
  also broken.
- **`darbar-abul-muali-qadri`: no date, no lineage, no custodian in the chunk.** The book gives only
  Jahangir's reign for the mohalla's founding and describes him as "سید شاہ ابو المعالی قادری" (p. 87,
  folio 90). The archive row's 1024 AH death date, its Kirmani Sadaat descent and its Dawood Bandgi
  affiliation are **(not stated)** here. The urs figure is flagged: "ہزار ہا [OCR?]".
- **Do not map "عبد اللہ شاہ" the Baloch of Mazang to `shrine-of-abdullah-shah-ghazi`.** The archive row
