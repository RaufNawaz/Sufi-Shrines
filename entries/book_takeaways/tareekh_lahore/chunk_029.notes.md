# tareekh_lahore — chunk 029 — pp. 411–425

**No shrine, tomb, mandir, gurdwara or khanqah appears anywhere in this chunk.** It is a continuous run of
British-era institutional Lahore, described in an engineer's register of feet and inches: the end of the
**Central Jail** (the entry's own heading is not printed — the chunk opens mid-sentence), then
**ڈسٹرکٹ جیل یعنی جیلخانہ شعبہ تحصیل خاص ضلع لاہور** (District Jail, i.e. the jail of the Tehsil Khas
branch of Lahore district) (p. 415, folio 422), **مکان پاگل خانہ** (the lunatic asylum) (p. 417, folio
424), **سٹیشن ریلوے** (the railway station) (p. 420, folio 427), and the opening lines of
**رومن کیتہلک والوں کا گرجا** (the Roman Catholics' church), at which the chunk breaks off (p. 425, folio
432). Its value to this project is entirely **institutional and economic**: named contractors and
superintendents, a Sikh military sarai converted by the English government, prisoner labour in
state-run factories, prison and asylum capacities, segregation by race, sex and class, canal-fed water
works, imported engine parts, shops and a weighing-house inside state enclosures, and a dated sequence
of railway extension.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker**, and in this chunk the printed folio runs seven ahead
of the PDF index throughout: p. 411 → folio 418, p. 425 → folio 432, each marker reading exactly that.
The folios below are only the ones the chunk prints; nothing is extrapolated.

**Almost every numeral in this chunk carries `[OCR?]`**, and several of the resulting dimensions are
physically impossible (a gate 734 ft high, a hospital barrack 843 ft long). They are recorded as printed
and the contradictions are kept side by side; see Doubts.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A Sikh general's cantonment sarai turned into the state asylum.** "یہ مکان سکہیوں کے وقت کا بنا ہوا
  ہے مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت جب سردار لہنا سنگہ مجیٹہیہ [OCR?] کا قیام نو لکہہ میں ہوا تو اُس نے اپنی
  فوج کے قیام کے لیے یہ مکان بنوایا جو لہنا سنگہ کی چہاؤنی مشہور ہو گیا" — the building is **of the
  Sikhs' time**: in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's time, when **Sardar Lehna Singh Majithia[OCR?]** was
  stationed at **Naulakha (نو لکہہ)**, he had it built **for his army's quartering**, and it became known
  as **Lehna Singh's chhaoni (لہنا سنگہ کی چہاؤنی)**; his grand deorhi with an upper storey survives on
  the west "اب تک موجود ہو". "سرکار انگریزی کے وقت یہ محفوظ مکان جو بطور سرائے کے بنا ہوا تہا پاگلوں کے
  رہنے کے لیے مقرر ہوا" — **in the English government's time this protected building, built as a sarai,
  was designated for the residence of the insane** (p. 417, folio 424), "اور اسکی عمارت میں بہت سی ایزادی
  [OCR?] عمل میں آئی", and much addition[OCR?] was carried out in its building (p. 418, folio 425). A
  Sikh military work repurposed by the new government without demolition — a change of use recorded
  flatly, with no comment.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **A superlative claim about the Punjab's railways, made without figures.** "پنجاب کے کسی شہر نامی گرامی
  میں ایسا سٹیشن نہیں بنا جیسا لاہور کا سٹیشن ہے" — no station such as Lahore's has been built in any
  renowned city of the Punjab (p. 420, folio 427); and of the fort, "اس عالیجاہ عمارت کی خوبیوں کی حد و
  انتہا نہیں ہے", the merits of this exalted building have no end (p. 421, folio 428). The author is a
  Lahore official writing about works of his own administration's era; the claim is his own.
- **The station read as a defensible fort.** He calls the station building "قلعہ" throughout and reads its
  loopholes militarily: "دیواروں میں مورچہ بندی ہے جس سے یہ قلعہ کسی دشمن کے ساتہ مقابلہ کر سکتا ہے" —
  there is loopholing in the walls, so that this fort can contend with an enemy (p. 421, folio 428).
- **He twice pleads length as a reason to stop describing.** Of the jail's factories, "مفصل بہمایش[OCR?]
  لکہنے میں طوالت ہوتی ہے", writing them out in detail would run long (p. 414, folio 421); of the railway
  printing works, "اس کارخانہ کے کام کا حد و حساب نہیں", the works' work is beyond reckoning (p. 425,
  folio 432). He also marks his own method: the two jail circles are described "مجملا[OCR?]", in summary,
  with only the western circle's extras itemised (p. 413, folio 420).
- **The register is an engineer's, not a chronicler's.** Every structure in this chunk is given in length,
  breadth, height, plinth, truss and roofing material, and in the count of rooms, doors and lines; no
  site in the chunk is given a date except by reign ("سکہیوں کے وقت", "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت", "سرکار
  انگریزی کے وقت" — pp. 417–418, folios 424–425) and the one unrecoverable foundation date of the station
  (p. 425, folio 432). Regime change appears here, as elsewhere in the book, mainly as a change of use:
  a Sikh sardar's cantonment sarai becomes the asylum.
- **How the machinery is explained.** The steam plant is described for a reader assumed not to know it —
  heat, steam, belts, transmission from machine to machine (pp. 423–424, folios 430–431) — and the
  printing machine is characterised by its labour saving, one man to feed paper (p. 425, folio 432).

## Doubts

- **No archive row is described in this chunk, and none is matched.** The chunk contains no shrine, tomb,
  mandir, gurdwara, samadh or khanqah. The only toponym in it that also appears in the archive's Lahore
  location fields is **انارکلی (Anarkali)**, given as the position of the Roman Catholic church (p. 425,
  folio 432); three archive rows carry Anarkali in their location. **That is a coincidence of street, not
  a description of any of them**, and no id is asserted here.
- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the site at its head is unnamed.** p. 411 (folio 418) begins in
  mid-sentence, "موقعہ پا کر بہاگ جاتے تہے", "finding the opportunity, they used to flee", with no
  subject; the institution's heading falls in the previous chunk. It is identified only indirectly by
  the next entry, which places the District Jail "متصل سنٹرل جیل بجانب غرب", adjoining the **Central
  Jail** to the west (p. 415, folio 422). The jail described on pp. 411–415 is therefore taken here to be
  that Central Jail, but **the chunk never prints its title**. The chunk also **closes mid-entry**, in the
  middle of the Roman Catholic church's description (p. 425, folio 432), so what stood inside its
  enclosure besides the church is unknown.
- **Numerals.** Nearly every dimension in the chunk carries `[OCR?]`. Several are impossible as printed
  and are **kept as printed, unreconciled**: the asylum's great gate "۷۳۴ [OCR?] فٹ اونچا سقف تک",
