# tareekh_lahore — chunk 028 — pp. 396–410

**A continuous run of British-era public buildings, with no shrine in it.** The chunk finishes an entry on
the Lieutenant Governor's kothi / Government House (a house built over an older tomb), then gives
**منٹگمری ہال و لارنس ہال** (Montgomery Hall and Lawrence Hall), **مکان عجائب گاہ لاہور** (the Lahore
museum), **بندی خانہ یعنی زنانہ جیلخانہ** (the women's jail), **پختہ پل ریلوے دریائے راوی** (the railway
bridge over the Ravi), and opens **سنٹرل جیل لاہور** (Central Jail Lahore) before breaking off
(pp. 396–410, folios 403–417). Its value to this project is **institutional and economic**: a property
exchange between the state and a jagirdar, subscription-funded memorial halls, an exhibition costing
thousands of government rupees, canal water piped into a Government House tank, well-and-filter water
reticulated through a prison, prison labour sold for lakhs of rupees, and — repeatedly — **مولف کتاب**,
the author of the book, named as the officer who built, re-roofed and still supervises these buildings
for the Municipal Committee of Lahore.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker**, and in this chunk the printed folio runs **seven**
ahead of the PDF index throughout: p. 396 → folio 403, p. 410 → folio 417, each marker reading exactly
that. (Chunk 016 of this same book prints a +3 offset, so the series has shifted between the two; nothing
below is computed, only what the chunk prints.)

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A jagirdar's land swapped for the state's future Government House.** The kothi that became Government
  House was acquired by giving **Raja Tej Singh** — then holding the Sialkot taluqa[OCR?] in jagir — the
  **haveli of Diwan Hakim Rai**, government property "سکھیون کے آخری مفسدہ میں ضبط ہوئی تھی", confiscated
  in the Sikhs' last disturbance; the alternative the passage records was cash at valuation or another
  government house in exchange (p. 396, folio 403). A confiscated Sikh-era property thus becomes the
  consideration in a transaction that produces the Lieutenant Governor's residence.
- **Recurrent state spending on Government House.** "اُس روز سے اسکی تعمیر کی ایزادی میں ہمیشہ کوشش ہوتی
  رہی ہزاروں روپیہ ہر سال خرچ ہوتے رہے" — from that day effort was constantly made to add to its
  construction and **thousands of rupees were spent every year**, until "جمعدار خوشحال سنگہ کے عہد کی
  عمارت کا اب نام و نشان باقی نہیں رہا موجودہ عمارت سب سرکاری ہے", no trace of the earlier building
  remains and the present one is entirely government's (p. 396, folio 403).
- **Canal water piped to a government garden tank.** The stepped pakka hauz east of the kothi, sixty by
  thirty feet and three[OCR?] feet deep, "نہر کے پانی سے بھرا رہتا ہے" — is **kept filled from canal
  water** (p. 398, folio 405).
- **A domestic establishment reckoned in buildings.** Inside the Government House enclosure: stable,
  godown, **camel house**, **elephant house**, "آٹہہ نو پختہ مکان نوکروں کے رہنے کے لیے", eight or nine
  pakka houses for servants, four gates, guard sepoys' quarters at the north gate, and several latrines
  (p. 399, folio 406).
- **Whose office maintains Government House — the name is lost.** "ایسے مکان کی مرمت سالانہ و جدید تعمیر و
  اہتمام و نگرانی سب [illegible] کے متعلق ہے" — the **annual repair, new construction, management and
  supervision** of such a building all belong to [illegible] (p. 399, folio 406). The responsible party is
  illegible here; see Doubts.
- **Two memorial halls paid for by subscription from the Punjab's nobles and officials.** "ان دو مکانوں کی
  تعمیر میں امرائے پنجاب و حکام نے نہایت رضامندی سے چندہ دیا اور وہ روپیہ خرچ ہوا کر[OCR?] یہ دو تو عالیشان
  مکان تعمیر ہوئے" — for the construction of these two buildings **the nobles of the Punjab and the
  officials gave subscription (چندہ) most willingly**, and that money being spent, these two grand
  buildings were built (p. 399, folio 406).
- **The subscribers' names inscribed twice, in two scripts.** "دو سلیں مستطیل سنگ مرمر کی ہال کے اندر ایک
  دوسری کے مقابل نصب ہیں ایک میں بخط انگریزی اُن لوگوں کے نام کندہ ہیں جنہوں نے اس مکان کی تعمیر میں چندہ
  دیا اور دوسری میں بخط فارسی وہی نام کندہ کئے ہیں" — two rectangular **marble slabs** are fixed inside the
  hall facing each other, one bearing **in English script** the names of those who subscribed to the
  building and the other **the same names in Persian script** (pp. 400–401, folios 407–408). A stone slab
  at the level of the upper parapet on the south carries "بخط انگریزی لفظ منٹگمری ہال کندہ", the words
  **Montgomery Hall** in English script (p. 400, folio 407).
- **Two dates for the two halls.** "لارنس ہال سنہ ۱۸۶۲ء میں بنکر ختم ہوا اور منٹگمری ہال سنہ ۱۸۶۶ء[OCR?]
  میں بنکر تیار ہوا" — **Lawrence Hall was finished in 1862 AD**; **Montgomery Hall was completed in
  1866[OCR?] AD** (p. 402, folio 409). Note the 1862 numeral carries no flag while 1866 does, and see the
  next item for a date that sits awkwardly against 1866.
- **A failed roof, and the author of the book put in charge of it.** "چونکہ منٹگمری ہال کی چہت اول مستر کار
  ڈوین[OCR?] صاحب انجنیر نے [illegible] کی ٹہیکہ داری میں تالیو[OCR?] کی پختی بنوائی تہی وہ ناقص رہ گئی اور
  چہت میں دراڑ[OCR?] آ گئی اسلیئے سرکار سے مولف کتاب اسکی درستی پر مامور ہوا اور پہلی چہت اُتار کر مولف نے

  ہوا تہا" — the building was erected when **government for the first time held an exhibition (نمایش) of
  the curiosities of the Punjab region**, and **thousands of rupees of government money** were spent in
  managing this important work (p. 402, folio 409).

  جاگیرداروں نے اپنے طرح طرح کی عجیب چیزیں … جمع کر کر" — the **great states of the Punjab, and sardars and
  jagirdars from outside[OCR?]**, gathered their various curiosities: jewellery, precious inlaid work,

  لین پر چہوٹی گاڑی چلائی جائے اس سبب اس پل کا عرض اسکے مطابق رکھا گیا تہا پہر جب وہ تجویز بدل گئی تو سڑک کی
  تبدیلی بھی عمل میں آئی اور اس پل کو اور آہنی قینچیاں اور پرزے اپنا[OCR?] دی کرکے اور پر سے چوڑا بنا لیا گیا
  جس پر اب ریل جا رہی ہو" — government's **first plan was to run a small train on the Peshawar line road**,
  so the bridge's width was set accordingly; when the plan changed, the road was changed too, and by adding
  further **iron trusses and parts** the bridge was made wider, and **the railway now runs on it** (pp.
  409–410, folios 416–417).
- **The bridge's dimensions as printed.** Pits[OCR?] were dug in the river's flow and pakka brick-and-lime
  piers built: "۳۵[OCR?] پائے اور ۳۳[OCR?] دریا کے پانی[OCR?] بکے بہاؤ کے وسط میں گاڑے گئے میں" — 35[OCR?]
  piers, and 33[OCR?] sunk in the middle of the river's flow (the two numbers stand side by side as printed;
  see Doubts); "ہر ایک درا[OCR?] ایک سو فٹ مقدار میں ہو اور کل پل تین ہزار تین سو فٹ لمبا ہو" — each span[OCR?]
  **one hundred feet** and the whole bridge **3,300 feet long**; below, "۲۰[OCR?] فٹ چوڑا راستہ لوگوں کی آمد
  و رفت کے واسطے", a 20[OCR?]-ft-wide passage for people's traffic, and above it iron trusses carrying a
  second iron deck on which the railway runs (p. 410, folio 417).
- **Prison labour as an industry, and the argument for keeping it indoors.** At Central Jail Lahore: "اس
  جیلخانے میں قیدی ہر ایک طرح کی مشقت جیل کے اندر کرتے ہیں بڑے بڑے کارخانے درسی بافی[OCR?] و شالبافی و قالین
  بافی وغیرہ بشیار[OCR?] کام جیل کے اندر ہوتا ہو چہاپے خانہ میں لاکہوں روپے کا کام ہوتا ہے" — prisoners do
  every kind of labour inside the jail; there are **great workshops — weaving[OCR?], shawl-weaving,
  carpet-weaving** and much[OCR?] other work done inside — and **in the printing press (چہاپے خانہ) work
  worth lakhs of rupees is done**; "اس سے یہ فائدہ ہوتا ہے کر[OCR?] قیدی بھی کام سیکہ جاتے ہیں سرکار کو بھی
  فائدہ ہوتا ہو قیدی بھی جیل سے باہر نہیں جاتے" — the benefit being that **the prisoners learn a trade, the
  government also profits, and the prisoners do not go outside the jail** (p. 410, folio 417). The chunk then
  begins on what used to happen when prisoners went out to work on the roads — "پہلے جو قیدی سڑکوں پر کام کرنے
  جاتے تہے تو اکثر" — and **breaks off** (p. 410, folio 417).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author writes himself into the record as the engineer of these buildings.** Montgomery Hall's
  defective roof was referred to **مولف کتاب** by government, who took the old roof down and completed the
  new one at the end of 1865 AD to the officers' approval, and the building remains "زیر نگرانی مولف کے",
  under the مولف's supervision, its annual repair running through him from the Municipal Committee of
  Lahore (p. 402, folio 409); the women's jail was built in 1868[OCR?] AD "مولف کتاب کے اہتمام و نگرانی
  میں", under the مولف's management and supervision, and its repair and supervision still belong to him
  (p. 409, folio 416). Both are recorded exactly as the book makes them; the book does not gloss who the
  مولف is.
- **British officers are praised in the language of just kingship.** Montgomery "رعایا کو اپنے حسن اخلاق و
  انصاف سے خوش رکھا", kept the subjects happy by his good character and justice; Lawrence's
  "کمال عرق ریزی[OCR?] و جانفشانی" held the Punjab's administration together during the "مفسدہ دہلی", the
  disturbance of Delhi, and earned him the Governor Generalship and Viceroyalty[OCR?] (p. 399, folio 406).
  The 1857 events are named only as a "مفسدہ", a disturbance, and the Punjab's role is given as the raising
  and despatch of troops to Delhi.
- **The Sikh collapse is likewise named as a "مفسدہ".** The Hakim Rai haveli was confiscated "سکھیون کے
  آخری مفسدہ میں", in the Sikhs' last disturbance (p. 396, folio 403) — the same word used for 1857 at
  p. 399 (folio 406). Regime change enters this chunk as a change of title to property.
- **Buildings are judged aesthetically.** Of the fish-back work on Montgomery Hall's drum: "نہایت عمدہ ماہی
  پشت کام ہے جسکو دیکہکر طبیعت خوش ہو جاتی ہے", very fine, the sight of which gladdens the temperament
  (p. 400, folio 407); the corner rooms of Government House are "نہایت مطبوع و خوبصورت" (p. 397,
  folio 404); the museum's verandah "نہایت خوبصورت و رنگین" and its turrets "مکان کی زینت کو بڑہاتی ہیں",
  increase the building's adornment (p. 405, folio 412).
- **The author measures rather than narrates.** Almost the whole chunk is dimensional survey in feet, with
  room counts, opening counts, plinth-to-parapet heights and materials — the method of an engineer's
  description, and the reason nearly every numeral here carries an `[OCR?]` flag.
- **Two small implicit claims about audience and access** are worth noting: the museum's "عام دیکھنے والے",
  ordinary viewers, are routed in at the north door and out at the south (p. 404, folio 411); and the Ravi
  bridge's lower deck is open to livestock and pedestrians but closed to a mounted man (p. 409, folio 416).
  The prison, by contrast, is described entirely from the administration's side.

## Doubts

- **No site in this chunk matches any row of the archive index.** The chunk contains no shrine, temple,
  gurdwara, samadh, mazar or khanqah as a subject; its six subjects are the Lieutenant Governor's kothi /
  Government House, Montgomery Hall and Lawrence Hall, the Lahore museum, the women's jail, the Ravi railway
  bridge and Central Jail Lahore. No archive id is claimed, and none should be inferred from the Anarkali
  location of the museum (p. 402, folio 409) or from anything else here.
- **The chunk opens mid-sentence and mid-entry.** The building whose acquisition is being described at
  p. 396 (folio 403) is never given a proper name in this chunk — only its function, "جائے قیام لفٹنٹ گورنر
  بہادر و گورنمنٹ ہوس". Its name, and any earlier account of it, lie before p. 396.
- **The "پرانا مقبرہ" in the kothi's lower storey is unnamed.** The chunk says only that an old tomb remains
  in the lower storey, that its "بیرونی و سیع چوترہ" set the octagonal plan, and that one dome's doorway is
  blocked (p. 396, folio 403). Whose tomb it is is **(not stated)** in this chunk; it is presumably named in
  the preceding pages. Do not attach it to any archive row on the strength of this chunk.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** Central Jail Lahore breaks off at "پہلے جو قیدی سڑکوں پر کام کرنے جاتے تہے
  تو اکثر" (p. 410, folio 417); the rest of that entry is in the following chunk.
- **A responsible office lost to an illegible word.** The annual repair and supervision of Government House
  "سب [illegible] کے متعلق ہے" (p. 399, folio 406). Given that the مولف is named in the same role for
  Montgomery Hall (p. 402, folio 409) and the women's jail (p. 409, folio 416), a reader may be tempted to
  fill the gap; the chunk does not, and neither do these notes.
- **Contradictory pier counts on the Ravi bridge.** "۳۵[OCR?] پائے اور ۳۳[OCR?] دریا کے … بہاؤ کے وسط میں
  گاڑے گئے میں" gives **35[OCR?]** and **33[OCR?]** in one clause (p. 410, folio 417). Both numerals are
  flagged; the total of 3,300 ft at 100 ft a span is printed unflagged. Kept side by side, not reconciled.
- **Montgomery Hall's completion date sits oddly against its re-roofing date.** The hall is said to have been
  completed in "سنہ ۱۸۶۶ء[OCR?]" (p. 402, folio 409), while the replacement roof the مولف built was ready
  "سنہ ۱۸۶۵ء کے اخیر میں", at the end of 1865 AD, on the same page. The 1866 numeral is flagged and the 1865
  is not. Kept as printed.
- **Chronogram numerals.** The gun's date "سنہ ۱۱۶۴[OCR?] ہجری" and the chronogram hemistich itself, "پیکر
  ارث[OCR?] و بہا کم آتش بار[OCR?]", are both flagged, and the introducing phrase "جسکے بغیر مصرع میں مصرع
  تاریخ" is itself garbled (بغیر where a word like آخر would be expected) (p. 404, folio 411). The
  hemistich's numerical value has not been checked here and must not be recomputed for the archive.
- **Almost every dimension in this chunk carries `[OCR?]`.** Where a figure is quoted above without a flag —
  30½ ft, 38 ft, 53 ft, 102 ft, 112 ft, 12 ft, 15 ft, 606, 150, 770, 3,300, 22, ۲۴-۲۴, forty days, seventy
  feet — that is because the chunk prints it unflagged; every other figure carries its flag as printed. No
  figure here should enter an entry without a look at the page image.
- **Lawrence Hall's great room's dimensions are unusable as printed**: "ساٹہ ہر پینتیس[OCR?] فٹ لمبا اور ساٹہ
  ہر تیس فٹ چوڑا" (p. 401, folio 408) — "sixty … thirty-five feet long and sixty … thirty feet wide". Two
  readings (sixty-five by sixty, or sixty-on-thirty-five) are possible and neither is quoted above as a fact.
- **Garbled nouns carried forward as flagged.** "کہو ٹُڑون[OCR?] کا اصطبل" (the stable's occupants,
  p. 398/folio 405); "ششئی[OCR?] شکل" (the corner rooms' shape, p. 397/folio 404); "گمیان[OCR?]" and
  "بگیاں[OCR?]" (the vehicles admitted to the two deorhis, pp. 397, 400 / folios 404, 407);
  "سنگین[OCR?]" / "سنگینیں[OCR?]" (the solitary-confinement blocks, pp. 406–407 / folios 413–414);
  "کتاب با[OCR?]" / "کتاب بوں[OCR?]" (the prison workrooms, p. 409 / folio 416); "گلشندان[OCR?]" and "چہو
  چہو[OCR?]" (the museum's finials, p. 405 / folio 412); "درسی بافی[OCR?]" (a jail workshop trade, p. 410 /
  folio 417); "مستر کار ڈوین[OCR?] صاحب انجنیر" (the engineer of the failed roof, p. 402 / folio 409).
  Each is glossed above only as far as the print allows.
- **Extended `[illegible]` runs** affect: the roof and minaret description joining the two halls (p. 401,
  folio 408, several breaks in one sentence); the taxidermy method (p. 403, folio 410); the cell heights and
  roofing of the solitary blocks (p. 407, folio 414); the structure in front of the girls' barrack and the

  "میعاد قید کی بہگتی ہیں [illegible] اور یہ لاہو[OCR?] میں عورتیں سب اسمیں سزا یاب ہونے کو آتی ہیں"
