# tareekh_lahore — chunk 008 — pp. 105–118

**A survey of Hindu and Sikh places of worship inside the bazaars of the walled city**, taken
building by building: an unnamed temple with fourteen shops (p. 105, folio 108), a Kuka dharamsal
and a Hanuman temple in Moti Bazaar, the Shivala Tarpoliya, the great **Makan Baoli Sahib** of Guru
Arjan, and then five temples of Chakla Bazaar and its lanes. One archive row is the subject of a
long entry — the Baoli — and it is the richest single piece of shrine economics in the chunk: a
treasure-trove lawsuit before the emperor, a murdered guard, a filled-in well, a Sikh-state
reconstruction funded by a month of state salaries, a jagir, a bankrupt custodian and a monthly
government allowance in its place (pp. 108–114, folios 111–117). The author writes as a surveyor:
he gives doors, compass bearings, storeys, step-counts, rents and the names of the living men in
charge.

**The folio markers in this chunk run three ahead of the PDF index on every page**: p. 105 → folio
108 through p. 118 → folio 121, each marker printed in the chunk. No folio below is computed; each
is the one the chunk prints for that page.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **gurdwara-baoli-sahib-guru-arjan-dev-ji-lahore** (**"مکان باولی صاحب"**, the building of Baoli
  Sahib):
  - **Standing and use.** "یہ متبرک مکان عبادت گاہ سکہان لاہور کے مشہور و معروف مکانات مین ہی ہے" —
    this blessed place is among the famous and well-known places of worship of the Sikhs of Lahore;
    its extent is the greatest of all the places of worship, and "جب کوئی مجمع کسی قسم کا ہندو دین
    مین ہوتا ہی تو یہی مکان انتخاب کیا جاتا ہی", when any gathering of any kind in the Hindu religion
    takes place this very building is chosen (p. 108, folio 111).
  - **Situation.** Between **"بین بازار[OCR?] و کسیرا بازار و لوہٹی بازار کے درمیان"** — Bin
    Bazaar[OCR?], Kasera Bazaar and Lohti Bazaar — with bazaars on all four sides: to the east
    **"ٹوپی[OCR?] یعنی لوہا بیچنے والون کا بازار"**, Topi[OCR?] Bazaar, that is, of the iron-sellers;
    to the north[OCR?] **"گوٹی[OCR?] بازار"**; to the south **"کسیرا بازار یعنی برتن فروخت کرنیوالون
    کا بازار"**, Kasera Bazaar, that is, of the vessel-sellers; and an open bazaar to the west, on
    which side the great door stands, reached by three[OCR?] steps (p. 108, folio 111). See Doubts —
    the archive row's own location field reads Dabbi Bazaar.
  - **Fabric, in the author's own sequence.** An arched and grand doorway with two chaukis; behind
    the deorhi two arched dalans north and south, a kothri, and a stair to a shah-nashin on the
    deorhi roof with three arched dariche facing the courtyard (p. 108, folio 111). Then a very wide
    maidan with a wheel-well (چاہ چرخی دار); sets of three kothris, **twelve upper kothris** on the
    north, two bel trees and a pipal (p. 109, folio 112). On the south the Baoli Sahib building
    itself — "گویا ایک دالان کے تین دالان نہایت وسیع ہی", as though three dalans in one, with
    east–west kothris, matting floors (فرش بوریا[OCR?]) and shah-nashins above (p. 109, folio 112).
  - **The Granth Sahib's placement.** In the middle of the third dalan a wooden takhtposh, on it a
    small palang, and on that **"گرنتہہ صاحب بڑی[OCR?] عزت کے ساتہ مکلف غلافون کے اندر رکہا رہتا ہے"**
    — the Granth Sahib is kept with great honour inside elaborate covers (p. 109, folio 112).
  - **The well itself, counted in steps.** The baoli's own stair is behind a door west of the dalan's
    outer daricha that **"اکثر مقفل رہتا ہی ضرورت کے وقت کہولا جاتا ہی"**, usually stays locked and
    is opened when needed; a second great door onto the bazaar "ہمیشہ بند رہتا ہی", always stays
    closed, though if opened a person could descend into the baoli straight from the bazaar
    (p. 109, folio 112). **"چہتیس[OCR?] سیڑہیان"**, thirty-six[OCR?] steps, reach an open intermediate
    courtyard (p. 109, folio 112); **"پینتیس[OCR?] اور سیڑہیان"**, thirty-five[OCR?] more, reach the
    bottom of the well and the water's edge, where an arched **door of red stone (سرخ پتہر)** is set
    in the wall of the well "جسکی دہل[OCR?] پانی کے اندر ہی", its threshold[OCR?] in the water
    (p. 110, folio 113).
  - **A dry tank and a dead wheel.** In the west of the upper courtyard a small pakka tank with seven
    steps: "پہلے اسمین پانی بہرا جاتا تہا مگر اب مدت مدید سے خشک پڑا ہوا ہی", formerly water was
    filled in it but now it has long lain dry; the buildings south of it held the well-wheel
    (چرخ) that used to fill the tank from the baoli's well, "مگر اب جاری نہین ہی", but now it does
    not run (p. 110, folio 113).
  - **Foundation, as the book reports it.** **"یہ باولی گرو ارجن صاحب پانچوین جانشین مذہب سکہی کی
    تعمیر کی ہوئی مشہور ہے جو اُس نے بعہدِ جہانگیر بادشاہ کے بنوائی تہی"** — this baoli is famous as
    built by **Guru Arjan Sahib, fifth successor of the Sikh religion**, in the time of **Emperor
    Jahangir** (p. 110, folio 113). The occasion is the treasure-trove case under Practices.
  - **Guru Arjan's own presence and his langar.** "خود بہی گرو کچہ اس مقام پر قیام رکہا کرتا تہا" —
    the Guru himself used to stay at this place sometimes; and immediately to the south, in a
    bazaar building, **"گرو ارجن کا لنگر ہوا کرتا تہا"**, Guru Arjan's langar used to be held, that
    building being "گویا باولی والے مکان ہی کے متعلق", as it were an appurtenance of the baoli
    building (p. 111, folio 114).
  - **Destruction under the Qazi of Lahore.** After Jahangir and Guru Arjan died and Shah Jahan's
    time came, and after Guru Har Gobind's clash with the Qazi and the emperor, "جسقدر جائداد گرو کی
    لاہور مین تہی وہ ضبط[OCR?] کرکے قاضی کے حولے ہو گئی جسمین یہ باولی اور لنگر کا مقام بہی تہا" —
    all the Guru's property in Lahore was confiscated[OCR?] and handed to the Qazi, including this
    baoli and the langar's site. The Qazi, "بسبب اپنی ذاتی عداوت کے", out of personal enmity, built
    a mosque [illegible] on the langar's site, killed **Langahiya[OCR?]** and his four companions and
    threw them into the baoli, threw Langahiya's[OCR?] property in after them, had the well closed and
    houses built over it: "اُس روز سے یہ باولی بالکل مفقود ہو گئی", from that day this baoli became
    entirely lost, "اور لنگر کے مقام پر ایک عالیشان مسجد بن گئی", and a grand mosque stood on the
    langar's site (p. 112, folio 115).
  - **Recovery and rebuilding under the Sikh state.** Under Maharaja Ranjit Singh the baoli's
    "دوبارہ ظہور کی نوبت", turn to reappear, came; the dream, the digging, the demolition of the
    houses and of the mosque, and the funding from a month of state salaries are all under
    Practices (pp. 112–113, folios 115–116).
  - **Custodianship, three hands deep.** The Maharaja handed the building to **"بہائی نہال سنگہ"**,
    Bhai Nihal Singh, with **"آٹہہ ہزار روپیے سالانہ جاگیر اس مکان کی خدمت کی عوض"**, an annual jagir
    of eight thousand rupees in return for the building's service; after him it passed into the
    custody (تحویل) of **"بہائی جواہر سنگہ کرتارپوریہ"**, Bhai Jawahar Singh Kartarpuria, who took
    the monthly shop rents (p. 113, folio 116). Jawahar Singh's bankruptcy and the state's
    replacement allowance are under Practices. The serving officer at the time of writing is
    **"بہائی خزان سنگہ نام ایک سفید ریش سکہہ"**, Bhai Khazan Singh, a white-bearded Sikh, "مکان کی
    خدمت و خبر گیری پر مامور ہی", appointed to the service and care of the building (pp. 113–114,
    folios 116–117). "اب بہائی جواہر سنگہ کو ایک سال کا عرصہ ہوا کہ مر گیا ہے" — it is now a year
    since Bhai Jawahar Singh died (p. 114, folio 117); but see Doubts, the same page has him still
    taking the offerings.
  - Silsila, urs or mela, and any annual gathering date: **(not stated)**.

- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** (**"مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ"**) — **nothing in this chunk concerns
  the samadhi itself**; the material is about the man as patron and about his death:
  - He is the ruler under whom "تمام خطہ پنجاب" the whole region of the Punjab came under Sikh rule
    (p. 112, folio 115).
  - He fell severely ill in **"سن ۱۸۹۶[OCR?] بکرمی"**, Sambat 1896[OCR?] Bikrami, was told of Guru
    Arjan's dream-command, bathed in the recovered baoli's water and recovered (pp. 112–113,
    folios 115–116).
  - He died in **"سن ۱۸۹۶ بکرمی"**, Sambat 1896 Bikrami, while the Baoli Sahib buildings were still
    going up, so that they "نہ مکمل ہو سکی[OCR?]", could not be completed; the author adds "اگر
    مہاراجہ اور دس برس زندہ رہتا تو یہ مکان لاثانی بن جاتا", had the Maharaja lived ten more years
    this building would have been unequalled (p. 113, folio 116). Era given only as Bikrami; no CE
    or Hijri equivalent is printed. See Doubts on the two 1896s.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **An unnamed temple, the entry running in from the previous chunk** (p. 105, folio 108):
  **fourteen shops (چودہ دوکانین)** belong to it, a very wide courtyard, the temple built of red
  stone (سنگِ سرخ) and pakka lime plaster (چونہ گچ), with a wholly gilded dome (گنبد تمام مطلا). Name
  and street: **(not stated)** in this chunk. Its custodianship and ruin are under Practices.
- **"دہرم سال کوکہ"**, the Kuka dharamsal, in **"موتی بازار کی غربی لین"**, the western lane of Moti
  Bazaar (p. 105, folio 108). Small, but the gathering of the Sikhs of the **Kuka[OCR?]** religion is
  held there, the Granth Sahib is read from time to time, and there is a Bhai **[illegible]** who is
  its **mohtamim (مہتمم)**, manager. Not ancient: "پندرہ سال کا عرصہ ہوا ہی کہ یہ دہرم سال بنایا
  گیا", fifteen years since it was built. Its decline is under Practices. East door, small courtyard
  with a wheel-well, a large pakka lime-plaster dalan on the north holding the Granth Sahib "اور
  معتقد لوگ اسکو پُوجتے ہین", and the devotees worship it, and a pakka kothri on the west opening
  into the great dalan.
- **"رام سنگہ"**, Ram Singh, "گرو کوکون کا مقتدا[OCR?]", guru and leader[OCR?] of the Kukas, "رنگون
  بہیجا گیا", was sent to **Rangoon** (p. 105, folio 108) — the chunk's only exile.
- **"ہنومان جی کا مندر"**, the temple of Hanuman Ji, in Moti Bazaar on the roadside toward the east,
  with a stepped west door (pp. 105–106, folios 108–109). A roofed baithak with three dariche open
  to the bazaar; on its eastern wall "بہت بڑی تصویر ہنومان جی کی", a very large image of Hanuman Ji,
  and a second of **"کرشن بہگوان جی"**, Krishan Bhagwan Ji; "ہنومان جی کی مورت پر سندہور ملا ہوا ہی
  اور کرشن جی کی مورت شام رنگ ہی", Hanuman's image is smeared with sindhur and Krishan Ji's is
  dark-coloured. Pakka houses for the mahant behind, an open courtyard with a wheel-well and a **bel
  tree (بیل کا درخت)**. Its founders and pujari are under Practices.
- **"گنیش داس و مہیش داس دو بیراگی"**, Ganesh Das and Mahesh Das, two Bairagis, the building's first
  holders (p. 106, folio 109).
- **"شوالہ تر پولیہ والہ"**, the Shivala of Tarpoliya, at the head of Tarpoliya Bazaar toward the
  east, west door (pp. 106–108, folios 109–111). The author's claim for its antiquity is under
  Arguments. **"پریم نانہہ[OCR?] جوگی"**, Prem Nanh[OCR?] Jogi, "مدت العمر اسمین قیام پذیر رہا", stayed
  in it his whole life, "اسلیئے یہ پریم نانہہ[OCR?] کے شوالے سے مشہور ہو گیا", so it became famous as
  Prem Nanh's[OCR?] shivala. Inside: an [illegible] idol on which Shiv Ji is enthroned (جلوس), a
  **silver jalhari (چاندی کی جلہری)**, a very large gagar[OCR?] of water on a chain[OCR?], a marble
  screen (سنگِ مرمر کا پنجرا) in the west daricha; walls of pakka lime plaster, a talbuti[OCR?] roof
  and above it "عالیشان گنبد کلس دار", a grand dome with a kalas; a very tall bel tree in the
  courtyard whose branches rise above the two- and three-storey buildings around. The mahants'
  samadhs, the shops, and the two named mahants are under Practices.
- **"بابا پریم ناتہ[OCR?]"**, Baba Prem Nath[OCR?], the late mahant of the Shivala Tarpoliya: "سب لوگ
  اسکی تعریف کرتے ہین اور اُسکے صاحب باطن ہونے مین شک نہین کرتے", all people praise him and do not
  doubt his being a man of inward attainment (p. 108, folio 111). No death date, no silsila,

- **"بیکنٹہہ داس کا ٹہاکر دوارہ"**, the Thakurdwara of Baikunth Das, at the head of **"چکلا بازار"**,
  Chakla Bazaar (p. 114, folio 117). "یہ مندر نہایت پُرانا مشہور ہے بعض اسکو ہزارہا سال کا کہتے
  ہین" — famous as very old, some calling it thousands of years old — "مگر وسعت اسکی بہت کم ہی", but
  its extent is very small, and "قدیمہ عمارات مین سے اب کوئی ٹکڑا عمارت کہنہ کا باقی نہین ہی", not a
  fragment of the old building now remains. Built like a haveli, three[OCR?] storeys. In a middle-storey
  dalan an elaborate niche with a stone image of **"کرشن بہگوان مہاراج"** and a second of
  **"رادہا[OCR?] جی"**, both "طلائی زیور مرصع پہنایا ہوا", clothed in jewelled gold ornament; in the
  dalan opposite, an image of **"ہنومان جی"** made in brickwork and painted red.
- **"شوالہ دیوان شنکر داس[OCR?]"**, the Shivala of Diwan Shankar Das[OCR?], in a lane east of Chakla
  Bazaar (pp. 114–115, folios 117–118). Small but of fine, heavy, pakka construction. Inside, a
  small stone chabutra bearing "شو جی کا استہان[OCR?]", Shiv Ji's asthan[OCR?], a jalhari above it and
  a gagar[OCR?] of water on the jalhari; qalbuti roof, grand kalas dome, painted lime-plaster walls,
  pakka stone floors within and without. Its founder's offices and its present servers are under
  Practices.
- **"شوالہ رکناتہہ[OCR?] مستری[OCR?]"**, the Shivala of Raknath[OCR?] Mistri[OCR?] — named in the heading
  thus and in the body as **"رگناتہہ[OCR?] مشر[OCR?] برہمن"**, Ragnath[OCR?] Mishr[OCR?], Brahmin — in a
  lane on the eastern side of Chakla Bazaar, west door (pp. 115–116, folios 118–119). A courtyard
  with a wheel-well and **ber and pipal trees (درختان بیر و پیپل)**; pakka kothris in the southern
  dalan "پجاریون کے رہنے کی خاطر", for the pujaris to live in; on a pakka rekhta[OCR?]-work chabutra to
  the west, a domed temple with an east door, inside it a small stone chabutra with Shiv Ji
  Maharaj's asthan, a **brass jalhari (برنجی جلہری)** and a gagar of water, a qalbuti roof and
  lime-plaster walls carrying "دیوتاؤن کی تصویرین", images of the deities. Its date, founder and
  mahant are under Practices.
- **"ٹہاکر دوارہ باکہو مشر"**, the Thakurdwara of Bakho Mishr, in the same lane near Ragnath Mishr's
  shivala, to its south (pp. 116–117, folios 119–120). Built like a haveli; an elaborate
  sitting-place inside the door, with a second above it; a pakka-floored courtyard; a two-storey
  dalan on the east whose "چوبی خشتی قیمتی عمارت لایق دید ہی", costly wood-and-brick work is worth
  seeing; on the south the temple of **"ٹہاکر جی مہاراج"** with a **gilded kalas (طلائی کلس)** on a
  dome "ایک عجیب و غریب عمارت کا بنا ہوا", of strange and wonderful construction, and outer walls of
  creeper-work (بیلدار) shabih[OCR?] pakka[OCR?]. Inside, stone images of **"کرشن[OCR?] جی مہاراج"** and
  **"رادہکا جی"** in elaborate clothing and costly jewellery. The courtyard is roofed over, "اگر
  مسقف نہ ہوتا تو بارش سے یہ مطلا و آئینہ دار عمارت حقیقت مین خراب ہو جاتی", since without a roof the
  rain would spoil the gilding and mirror-work.
- **"باکہو مشر المشہور لولہا مشر"**, Bakho Mishr, known as Lolha Mishr, mahant and founder: "وہ اپنے
  پاؤن سے نہین چلو سکتا اس سبب اُسکو لولہا مشر کہتے ہین", he cannot walk on his feet, which is why
  they call him Lolha Mishr, and the author glosses the word — "لولہا پنجابی زبان مین اُسکو کہتے ہین
  جسکی دو نو ٹانگین چلنے لایق نہ ہون", in the Punjabi language *lolha* is said of one whose two legs
  are not fit for walking (p. 116, folio 119). His finances are under Practices.
- **"ٹہاکر دوارہ بانکے بہاری"**, the Thakurdwara of Banke Bihari, "لاہور کے نامی گرامی مندرون مین ہی",
  among Lahore's renowned temples, located **"بہائی وستی رام کی حویلی کے قریب"**, near the haveli of
  Bhai Vasti Ram, at the head of the street toward the east (pp. 117–118, folios 120–121).
  Two-storey; the upper storey where Thakur Ji Maharaj is enthroned has walls, ceiling and floor all
  "منقش و مطلا و آئینہ دار", painted, gilded and mirrored, so that "انسان تصور کرتا ہی کہ شاید یہ
  مکان بہشت برین کے مکانات ہین", a man imagines these may be the buildings of the highest paradise.
  An outer wall with a wheel-well inside it; a grand painted door in a tall wall whose outer face
  carries "نہایت تصویرین دیوتاؤن کی", many painted images of the deities. Above: a roofed courtyard
  with three spiral railed dariche over the outer door; a large pillared dalan with gold designs in
  which "بلوری[OCR?] رنگینی ستاروں کی طرح چمکتے ہین", crystal[OCR?] colours glitter like stars, a gilded
  painted ceiling and a stone floor under costly carpets; then a three-doored sih-nashin[OCR?] with
  mirror-creepers on its columns and forehead, "گویا روزِ روشن مین ستاروں کا عالم دکہایا ہی", as if
  showing a world of stars in broad daylight. **Six images (چہہ مورتین)** in all: in the middle
  niche **"سری کرشن جی مہاراج"**, on his left **"رادہا مائی جی"** and on the right the picture[OCR?] of
  **"سری بلبدہر جی"**, Shri Balbhadar Ji, with three more deities' images in the two remaining
  niches, the images being "دہری[OCR?]", doubled[OCR?]. The mahant lives in a dalan north of the great
  dalan.

- **Places named**: **موتی بازار** (Moti Bazaar), **تر پولیہ بازار** (Tarpoliya Bazaar), **چکلا/چکلہ
  بازار** (Chakla Bazaar), **کسیرا بازار**, **لوہٹی بازار**, **بین بازار[OCR?]**, **ٹوپی[OCR?] بازار**,
  **گوٹی[OCR?] بازار**, **مکان روغن منڈی** (the Roghan Mandi building), **مالیر کوٹلہ** (Malerkotla),
  **رنگون** (Rangoon), **امرتسر** (Amritsar), **پنجاب** (Punjab) and **لاہور** (Lahore)
  (pp. 105–118, folios 108–121).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A temple financed by fourteen shops, with no custodian but its servant.** Of the unnamed temple
  at p. 105 (folio 108): **"سرپرست اس مکان کا کوئی نہین ہی سوائی ایک شخص بالکشن[OCR?] نام برہمن کے کہ
  وہ مندر کی خدمت کرتا ہی اور کرایہ جو دوکانون سے وصول ہوتا ہی اپنے گزارہ معاش مین خرچ کرتا ہی"** —
  this building has no patron except one Brahmin named Balkishan[OCR?], who serves the temple and
  spends the rent collected from the shops on his own livelihood; and "کسی وقت یہ مکان بہت بڑا بنا
  ہوا ہو گا مگر اب بے مرمت و خراب پڑا ہے", at some time this building must have been very large, but
  now it lies unrepaired and ruined. Endowment income diverted to maintenance of the servant rather
  than of the building, stated plainly.
- **A dharamsal's fortunes tied to a state prosecution.** The Kuka dharamsal was built fifteen years
  before the writing and "اس روز سے چار پانچ برس تک نہایت رونق رہی", flourished for four or five
  years; **"جب کوکون نے مالیر کوٹلہ مین فساد کیا اور سزا پائی"**, when the Kukas made a disturbance at
  Malerkotla and were punished, ab[OCR?] hue[OCR?], and Ram Singh their leader[OCR?] was sent to Rangoon,
  **"تو اس دہرم سال کی رونق بہی جاتی رہی"**, the dharamsal's flourishing too went away (p. 105,
  folio 108). The clearest instance in the chunk of state action against a movement emptying a
  building.
- **An evening assembly with singing.** "شام کے وقت شہر کے کوکے یہان آکر جمع ہوتے اور عارفانہ گیت
  گاتے ہین" — in the evening the Kukas of the city gather here and sing *arifana* songs (p. 105,
  folio 108). Attendance is otherwise given as a crowd at both times of day at the Hanuman temple —
  "دونو وقت خلقت کثیر حاضر ہوتی ہی" (p. 106, folio 109) — and at the Shivala Tarpoliya, "دونو وقت
  معتقد لوگون کا ہجوم مندر پر رہتا ہی", with people making offerings (چڑہاوہ چڑہاتے ہین) (p. 106,
  folio 109). No visitor numbers are given anywhere: **(not stated)**.
- **A private rebuilding that displaced Bairagi holders.** The Hanuman temple building "پہلے گنیش داس
  و مہیش داس دو بیراگی کا تہا پہر سیرا[OCR?] ہند[OCR?] صراف نے اپنا روپیہ صرف کرکے اسکو تعمیر کیا جو
  ابتک موجود ہی" — first belonged to two Bairagis, Ganesh Das and Mahesh Das, then a sarraf spent his
  own money and built it, and it stands to this day; "اب پجاری اس مندر کا مسمی ٹہاکر داس ہی جو ہر
  روز خدمت کرتا ہی اور آمدنی چڑہاوے سے اُسکا گزارہ ہی", now the pujari is one Thakur Das who serves
  daily and lives on the income of offerings (p. 106, folio 109).
- **Rebuilt under Sikh rule, and a public water service kept by a Brahmin.** Of the Shivala
  Tarpoliya: "تجدید عمارت کی وقت بوقت ہوتی رہی ہی خصوصاً سکہی عملداری مین اسکی سب[OCR?] عمارت نئی بن
  گئی" — renovation went on from time to time, and especially **under Sikh rule (سکہی عملداری)** all[OCR?]
  its building was made new (p. 106, folio 109). To the left of the door "باب[OCR?] و بیٹہک[OCR?] ہی
  جسمین برہمن پانی پلانے والا بیٹہتا ہی تمام دن وہ[OCR?] لوگون کو پانی پلاتا رہتا ہی", a baithak where
  a water-giving Brahmin sits and gives people water all day (p. 107, folio 110).
- **A municipal demolition at a temple door.** In front of the Shivala Tarpoliya's door, on the
  bazaar side, there had been a sitting-place with dariche on two sides, **"مگر حکام کے حکم سے مسمار
  کر دیا گیا ہی"** — but it has been demolished by order of the authorities (p. 107, folio 110). The
  only British-era municipal act in the chunk; the office is named only as *hukkam*.
- **A theft from the sanctum, and a silver fitting replaced by subscription.** The passage is heavily
  damaged, but the sequence is legible: someone who had come [illegible] water, "ٹہیکنے[OCR?] کے
  بہانے سے", on the pretext of teekhne[OCR?], put his mouth [illegible] inside the temple [illegible]
  and **"جلہری ہی بت مین کا ایک ٹکڑا کاٹ لیا"**, cut off a piece of the jalhari or of the idol; the
  news[OCR?] got out, "لوگون کے کمال افسوس ہوا", people were extremely sorry, **"اور کسی قدر روپیہ
  ارادت مند لوگون نے جمع کرکے جلہری کو نئی بنوایا چنانچہ اب وہ جلہری چار سو روپے کے وزن کی از سر نو
  تیار ہو گئی ہے"** — devout people collected some money and had the jalhari made new, so that it is
  now remade **of the weight of four hundred rupees** (p. 107, folio 110). See Doubts: what exactly
  was cut is illegible.
- **Mahants' samadhs inside the temple's own dalans.** At the Shivala Tarpoliya, "شمالی و جنوبی
  دالانون مین سابق مین اسکے مہنتون کی سمادین پختہ بنی ہوئی ہین" — in the northern and southern dalans
  the samadhs of its former mahants are built pakka; and the two- and three-storey buildings round
  the courtyard are where "جوگی پجاری مندر کے قیام پذیر ہین", the jogi pujaris of the temple reside
  (p. 107, folio 110).
- **Temple-owned shops and a mahant's rent.** "اُس سے ملتی ہوئی جو بازار کی شمالی و غربی دوکانین ہین
  وہ بہی مندر کی ملکیت مین اور کرایہ مہنت لیتا ہی" — the northern and western shops of the bazaar
  adjoining it are also the temple's property and the mahant takes the rent (p. 108, folio 111).
  "اس مندر کی وسعت مین زمین اگرچہ کم ہی مگر عمارت کثیر و منازل مین بلند ہی" — little land, much
  building, tall in storeys: the author's own summary of a walled-city shrine's economy.
- **Succession by grandson-disciple.** At the Shivala Tarpoliya, after Baba Prem Nath[OCR?]'s death,
  **"اب اُسکا پوتا چیلا بالک ناتہ[OCR?] نام پُجاری ہی مندر کا و صاحب اختیار ہے"** — now his
  grandson-disciple named Balak Nath[OCR?] is the temple's pujari and the holder of authority
  (p. 108, folio 111). The chunk's one explicit statement of a succession rule.
- **The treasure-trove case that founded the Baoli, decided by the emperor.** A Sikh of Guru Arjan
  sold his house on the site to a woman for **"تین سو روپیے"**, three hundred rupees; digging to
  rebuild, she found a patra[OCR?], a pehra[OCR?] and a palang, that is a canopied bed, "مطلا و مرصع",
  gilded and jewelled, one sona[OCR?], "قدیم مین کا[OCR?] دستیاب[OCR?] ہوا", of ancient times (pp. 110–111,
  folios 113–114). She offered it to the seller as his ancestor's; he refused — **"مین یہ مکان
  تمہارے پاس فروخت کر چکا ہون جب زمین بہی[OCR?] فروخت ہو گئی تو جو کچہ زمین کے اندر تہا وہ بہی فروخت
  ہو گیا یہ مال میرا حق نہین ہی"** — I have sold you this house; when the land was sold, whatever was
  inside the land was sold too; this property is not my right. The dispute "معاملہ قاضی تک پہنچا",
  reached the **Qazi**, and "چونکہ مقدمہ عجیب طرح کا تہا ہوتے ہوتے بادشاہ کے حضور مین[OCR?] یہ معاملہ
  پیش ہوا", because the case was of a strange kind it came in time before the **emperor**. "بادشاہ
  فریقین کی ایمانداری پر خوش ہوا اور بسبب اسکے کہ یہ مکان گرو ارجن کا سکہہ تہا وہ مال گرو ارجن کے
  پاس بہیجدیا کہ کسی نیک کام پر خرچ کرے" — the emperor, pleased with both parties' honesty and
  because the householder was a Sikh of Guru Arjan, sent the treasure to Guru Arjan to be spent on
  some good work, and **"گرو ارجن نے تجویز کی کہ اس مال سے ایک باولی تعمیر کی جائے"**, Guru Arjan
  decided a baoli should be built with it (p. 111, folio 114). A **Mughal imperial grant of
  treasure-trove to a Sikh guru for a religious building**, with the woman then relinquishing the
  site as well.
- **Five men appointed to guard a well.** "لنگاہیا[OCR?] نام ایک سکہہ کو مع اور چار سکہون کے باولی کی
  حفاظت پر مامور کر دیا" — a Sikh named Langahiya[OCR?], with four other Sikhs, was appointed to the
  baoli's protection (p. 111, folio 114). All five were later killed and thrown into it (p. 112,
  folio 115); their murder and the conversion of the langar's site into a mosque are the chunk's
  only record of a shrine's staff being killed in office.
- **Confiscation, conversion, and re-conversion of one site, three times over.** Guru property
  confiscated[OCR?] to the Qazi; a mosque built on the **langar khana**'s site and the baoli filled and
  built over (p. 112, folio 115); then under Ranjit Singh "بڑے مکانات [illegible] جو اس موقع پر تہے
  وہ سب گرا کے گئے اور میدان کیا گیا وہ قاضی[OCR?] کا عہد مسجد بہی مسمار ہوئی جو لنگر خانہ کے موقع پر
  بنی ہوئی تہی", the large houses on the spot were all pulled down and levelled and the mosque of the
  Qazi's[OCR?] time, built on the langar khana's site, was also demolished; and afterwards "مسجد جس
  جگہ سے گرائی گئی تہی وہان بہی دوکانین اور نشستگاہین اور کوٹہرے بنوایا گیا", shops, sitting-places
  and kothras were built where the mosque had stood (p. 113, folio 116).
- **A state building fund raised by docking one month of the Khalsa's pay.** **"حکم ملا[OCR?] کہ
  خالصہ[OCR?] یعنی سلطنت کی یکماہ تنخواہ وضع کرکے یہ عالیشان مکان بنوایا جائے بعد وضع تنخواہ بہتر[OCR?]
  ہزار روپیہ جمع ہوا"** — an order was given[OCR?] that one month's salary of the Khalsa[OCR?], that is of
  the state, be deducted and this grand building be built; after the deduction **seventy-two[OCR?]
  thousand rupees** were collected (p. 113, folio 116). Begun **"سن ۱۸۹۰[OCR?] بکرمی مین[OCR?]"**,
  Sambat 1890[OCR?] Bikrami, and "پانچ برس کے عرصہ مین بآہستگی یہ مکانات تعمیر ہوئے", built slowly over
  five years, unfinished at the Maharaja's death. The single largest building figure in the chunk,
  and the only one drawn from state salaries.
- **A jagir for shrine service, and its collapse into debt.** Eight thousand rupees a year to Bhai
  Nihal Singh "اس مکان کی خدمت کی عوض", in return for the building's service (p. 113, folio 116).
  Under his successor Bhai Jawahar Singh Kartarpuria, who took the monthly shop rents, "چند سال مین
  بہائی جواہر سنگہ کئی[OCR?] ہزار روپیہ کا قرضدار ہوا اور انتظام قرضہ ہو کر بہائی کی جاگیر و آمدنی
  جائداد کی سب ضبط[OCR?] ہوئی اور بہائی کا گزارہ مقرر رہا اُس قرضہ مین یہ آمدنی بہی غرق ہو گئی" — in a
  few years he fell several[OCR?] thousand rupees in debt; on the debt being administered, the Bhai's
  jagir and the property's income were all confiscated[OCR?] and a maintenance (گزارہ) fixed for him,
  and this income too was sunk in that debt (p. 113, folio 116).
- **A government monthly allowance in place of the endowment, itemised.** **"پچاس[OCR?] روپیے ماہوار
  مکان کے خدام کی تنخواہ و چراغ بتی و کڑاہ[OCR?] پرشاد کے لیئے سرکار نے واگزار کر[OCR?] رکہا جو ماہوار
  محکمہ خفیفہ[OCR?] سے ملتا ہے"** — the government has released fifty[OCR?] rupees monthly for the
  servants' salaries, lamp-wicks and **karah prashad**, received monthly from the Mahkama
  Khafifa[OCR?]; "اُسی مین فرش فروش[OCR?] وغیرہ کل اخراجات محسوب ہین", all expenses including flooring
  etc. are counted within it. And **"اڑہائی[OCR?] روپیے ماہوار [illegible] و نقارچیون[OCR?] کو ملتا ہی
  جو ہر روز علی الصباح حاضر ہو کر پرشاد یا نذر کرتے ہین"** — two and a half[OCR?] rupees monthly goes to
  [illegible] and the **naqarchis**, drummers, who attend every morning and make prashad or nazar
  (p. 113, folio 116). A named government department paying a shrine's running costs.
- **A hundred shops attached to one shrine.** "کل ایکسو[OCR?] سنگل[OCR?] دوکانین دو نو بازارون کی طرف
  اس مکان کے متعلق ہین" — in all one hundred[OCR?] single[OCR?] shops on both bazaar sides belong to this
  building (p. 113, folio 116). And the enclosure itself is let out: "احاطے کے اندر کی تمام کوٹہریان
  بازار والون نے کرایہ لیکر اپنے اسباب ڈالے ہوئے ہین", all the kothris inside the enclosure have been
  rented by the bazaar people who store their goods in them; people keep cattle within, and "اکثر
  لوگون نے اپنی نقدی[OCR?] و اسباب ڈال کر قفل لگا کے ہوئے ہین اور کرایہ دیتے ہین", many have put their
  cash[OCR?] and goods in under lock and pay rent; the **Roghan Mandi** building among the
  appurtenances (متعلقات) also holds a mandi[OCR?] "اور کرایہ لیا جاتا ہی", and rent is taken from it
  (p. 110, folio 113). A shrine complex functioning as the bazaar's warehouse.
- **Offerings before and after, and who owns which kind.** **"پہلے ہمیشہ[OCR?] چڑہاوہ گرنتہہ صاحب کا
  دو روپیے یومیہ سے کم نہین ہوتا تہا اب آمدنی چڑہاوے کی نقد کچہ نہین ہوتی"** — formerly the Granth
  Sahib's offering was never less than two rupees a day; now there is no cash income from offerings
  at all; if a raja or Sikh sardar offers rupees, Bhai Jawahar Singh takes it and "خدمتگاران کو
  اُسمین سے کچہ نہین ملتا", the servants get nothing of it; **"البتہ پتاشے[OCR?] و شیرینی[OCR?] اور
  پیسے جسقدر گرنتہہ صاحب پر چڑہتے ہین وہ پُجاریون کا حق ہوتا ہی"**, however the patashe[OCR?], sweets[OCR?]
  and coins offered on the Granth Sahib are the pujaris' right (p. 114, folio 117). A division of
  offerings by kind between custodian and servers, stated as a right.
- **The drum as a timed office.** At the Baoli, in the eastern kothri of the third dalan, "بڑا نقارہ
  رکہا ہی جو دو وقت بجایا جاتا ہی", a large naqara is kept and played at two times (p. 109,
  folio 112); at the Thakurdwara Banke Bihari, a large naqara nashtari[OCR?] with an iron bowl "صبح
  و شام آرتی کے وقت بجایا جاتا ہی", played morning and evening at arti time (p. 118, folio 121).
- **Temples built by subscription, and dated by the author's "years ago".** Baikunth Das's
  thakurdwara: the present building raised "دس سال کے عرصہ سے مسمی پریتم[OCR?] داس نے", ten years
  before writing by one Pritam[OCR?] Das, "اور اس خرچ مین بہت سے لوگ شامل ہوئے اور چندہ کرکے یہ مندر
  تعمیر ہوا", with many joining the cost and the temple built by **chanda**, subscription; its
  expenses run "چڑہاوے کی آمدنی پر", on the income of offerings (p. 114, folio 117). The Shivala
  Ragnath Mishr: **"چالیس برس کا عرصہ گزرتا ہے کہ یہ شوالہ رگناتہہ[OCR?] مشر[OCR?] برہمن نے اپنا روپیہ
  صرف کرکے بنوایا تہا"**, forty years since a Brahmin built it at his own expense, after which "یہ
  عبادت گاہ خاص عام مقرر ہو گئی[OCR?] ہی", it became an established place of worship for high and low;
  its mahant Sundar Nath Jogi and the building both live "چڑہاوے کی آمدنی سے", on the income of
  offerings (p. 116, folio 119). Bakho Mishr's thakurdwara was likewise built "چندہ کرکے", by
  subscription, and he "تمام آمدنی یہ مندر کی آراستگی پر خرچ کر دیتا ہی اپنے پاس جمع کچہ نہین رکہتا",
  spends the whole income on the temple's adornment and keeps nothing for himself; construction was
  still going on at the time of writing (p. 117, folio 120). No building is given a calendar date of
  construction: **(not stated)** for all of them.
- **A founder who held office under both regimes.** The founder of the Shivala Diwan Shankar
  Das[OCR?] "بعہدِ سکہی دیوانی کے عہدے پر ممتاز تہا اور انگریزی عہد مین آنریری[OCR?] مجسٹریٹ[OCR?] خاص
  لاہور کا مقرر رہا" — in the Sikh period he was distinguished with the office of **Diwan**, and in
  the **English period** he was appointed **Honorary[OCR?] Magistrate[OCR?] of Lahore proper**; he died
  some years before the writing, having built the shivala in his lifetime "اور اپنا یادگار دنیائے
  فانی مین چہوڑ گیا", leaving his memorial in this transient world (pp. 114–115, folios 117–118).
  The chunk's one named British-era municipal officer, and a shrine founder.
- **A salaried Brahmin under lay managers.** At the same shivala, the founder was his own caretaker
  while he lived; "اُسکے مرگ کے بعد اب پنڈت پریم ناتہ و شو ناتہ مندر کی خدمت کرتے ہین اور برہمن کو
  بہی تنخواہ دیتے ہین" — after his death Pandits Prem Nath and Shiv Nath serve the temple and also
  pay the Brahmin a salary; the Brahmin Keshav Ram, caretaker and pujari, lives in a pakka dalan on
  the south (p. 115, folio 118).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Guru Arjan appears in a dream and a buried baoli cures a king.** In Sambat 1896[OCR?] Bikrami, in
  Ranjit Singh's severe illness, "کسی مسّن[OCR?] سکہہ نے مہاراجہ کی خدمت مین یہ ظاہر کیا کہ گرو ارجن
  جی مہاراج میرے خواب مین آئے ہین" — some aged[OCR?] Sikh disclosed that Guru Arjan Ji Maharaj had come
  in his dream and said that at such a place were "ہماری باولی اور لنگر خانہ", our baoli and langar
  khana, which the Qazi had buried and built a mosque over, **"تم مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کو کہو کہ اُس
  باولی کو کہدوے اور اُسکے پانی سے غسل کرے صحت ہو جائیگی"**, tell Maharaja Ranjit Singh to have that
  baoli dug out and bathe in its water and he will recover. The author records a variant in the same
  breath: **"بعض کا قول ہی کہ گرو جی خود مہاراجہ کے خواب مین آئے اور یہ ارشاد کیا"**, some say the
  Guru came in the Maharaja's own dream and gave this command (p. 112, folio 115). On the Sikh's
  indication the digging began, "آخر باولی ظاہر ہوئی اور مہاراجہ نے اُسکے پانی سے غسل کرکے صحت پائی",
  at last the baoli appeared and the Maharaja bathed in its water and was cured (p. 113, folio 116).
  **The chunk's only karamat, and it is the direct cause of the shrine's reconstruction, of a
  mosque's demolition and of a state building fund.**
- Of Baba Prem Nath[OCR?] of the Shivala Tarpoliya the author reports only the community's verdict,
  no marvel: "اُسکے صاحب باطن ہونے مین شک نہین کرتے", they do not doubt his being a man of inward
  attainment (p. 108, folio 111).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **An antiquity asserted as beyond dispute, and one reported and doubted.** Of the Shivala
  Tarpoliya: **"اسکی قدامت مین کسی کو کلام نہین ہی لاہور کی آبادی جب سے ہوئی ہی اُسی روز سے یہ شوالہ
  ہی"** — no one has any argument about its antiquity; from the day Lahore was settled, this shivala
  has existed (p. 106, folio 109). Of Baikunth Das's thakurdwara he distances himself from the same
  kind of claim: "یہ مندر نہایت پُرانا مشہور ہے بعض اسکو ہزارہا سال کا کہتے ہین", it is famous as very
  old and some call it thousands of years old — but "قدیمہ عمارات مین سے اب کوئی ٹکڑا عمارت کہنہ کا
  باقی نہین ہی تمام عمارت مندر کی تازہ بنی ہوئی ہے", not a fragment of the old fabric remains and the
  whole is newly built (p. 114, folio 117). Two antiquity claims, one endorsed and one set against
  the physical evidence, three pages apart.
- **The author attributes the Baoli's foundation by report, not as fact.** "یہ باولی … کی تعمیر کی
  ہوئی **مشہور ہے**" — it is *famous* as built by Guru Arjan (p. 110, folio 113); and he records the
  dream in two versions with "بعض کا قول ہی" for the second (p. 112, folio 115). Elsewhere he states
  building facts flatly. The hedging is his, and should be preserved in any citation.
- **He assigns causes.** The Qazi's destruction of the baoli is put down to "بسبب اپنی ذاتی عداوت
  کے", his personal enmity (p. 112, folio 115); the Kuka dharamsal's decline to the Malerkotla
  prosecution and Ram Singh's exile (p. 105, folio 108); the roofing of Bakho Mishr's courtyard to
  the rain that would otherwise spoil the gilding (p. 117, folio 120).
- **A counterfactual regret about a Sikh-era building.** "اگر مہاراجہ اور دس برس زندہ رہتا تو یہ مکان
  لاثانی بن جاتا" — had the Maharaja lived ten more years this building would have been unequalled
  (p. 113, folio 116). The author's aesthetic judgement on the Baoli complex as left unfinished.
- **He glosses a Punjabi word for his reader.** "لولہا پنجابی زبان مین اُسکو کہتے ہین جسکی دو نو
  ٹانگین چلنے لایق نہ ہون" (p. 116, folio 119) — a lexical note inside a topographical survey, and a
  sign of the intended readership.
- **He praises living men by name.** Of Bakho Mishr: "یہ باکہو مشر نہایت خلیق آدمی ہی … اور تمام
  آمدنی یہ مندر کی آراستگی پر خرچ کر دیتا ہی اپنے پاس جمع کچہ نہین رکہتا" (p. 117, folio 120). The
  survey's standard for a good custodian is spending the income on the building.

## Doubts

- **The Baoli's street address in this book does not match the archive row's.** The chunk places it
  "بین بازار[OCR?] و کسیرا بازار و لوہٹی بازار کے درمیان" with Topi[OCR?] Bazaar east, Goti[OCR?] Bazaar
  north[OCR?], Kasera Bazaar south (p. 108, folio 111); the archive row
  `gurdwara-baoli-sahib-guru-arjan-dev-ji-lahore` carries **Dabbi Bazaar, Walled City**. Three of the
  four bazaar names here are `[OCR?]`-flagged. A human must read this page from the PDF before any
  entry repeats these bazaar names, and must not silently replace them with the row's own.
- **Two archive rows share Guru Arjan Dev as principal figure.** `gurdwara-dera-sahib` (Lahore, Guru
  Arjan Dev, 1606) and `gurdwara-baoli-sahib-guru-arjan-dev-ji-lahore`. **Everything about Guru
  Arjan in this chunk belongs to the baoli and its langar** — the book names no Dera Sahib and gives
  no death date for the Guru. Do not carry any of this to the Dera Sahib row.
- **Guru Har Gobind appears but attaches to no site the archive holds.** The hawk, Kaulan[OCR?],
  Mukhlis Khan and the confiscation (pp. 111–112, folios 114–115) are told as the cause of the
  Baoli's seizure, with no gurdwara of his named. The archive has four Guru Hargobind rows
  (`gurdwara-chowmala-sahib` and `gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-mozang` inside or near the walled city,
  plus Hadiara and Jhalian). **Map none of them from this chunk.**
- **Do not map this chunk's Krishna temples to `krishna-mandir-ravi-road`.** Baikunth Das's
  thakurdwara (Chakla Bazaar, p. 114, folio 117), Bakho Mishr's (a Chakla Bazaar lane, pp. 116–117,
  folios 119–120) and Banke Bihari (near Bhai Vasti Ram's haveli, pp. 117–118, folios 120–121) all
  hold Krishna images inside the walled city; the archive row is on **Ravi Road**. Likewise the
  chunk's Shiv temples do not match `loh-temple-lava-temple` (Lahore Fort) or any other Lahore row.
  All of these are candidates for new rows, not matches.
- **The first temple in the chunk has no name.** Its entry begins in chunk 007; p. 105 (folio 108)
  opens mid-sentence with "کے متعلق چودہ دوکانین ہین". Its fourteen shops, gilded dome and Brahmin
  servant cannot be attributed to a named site from this chunk alone.
- **The Bikrami dates conflict and are OCR-flagged.** Ranjit Singh's illness is "سن ۱۸۹۶[OCR?]
  بکرمی" (p. 112, folio 115); the building began "سن ۱۸۹۰[OCR?] بکرمی مین[OCR?]" and ran five years
  (p. 113, folio 116); and he died "سن ۱۸۹۶ بکرمی", unflagged, with the building unfinished
  (p. 113, folio 116). **An illness in 1896 cannot precede a construction start in 1890**, so one of
  the two flagged numerals is wrong in the transcription or in the print. Record all three as
  printed; do not reconcile them. No CE or Hijri equivalent is given for any of them.
- **Bhai Jawahar Singh is both dead and collecting.** On p. 114 (folio 117) the book says that if a
  raja or sardar offers rupees "بہائی جواہر سنگہ لیتا ہی", Bhai Jawahar Singh takes it, and four lines
  later "اب بہائی جواہر سنگہ کو ایک سال کا عرصہ ہوا کہ مر گیا ہے", it is now a year since he died. The
  present-tense passage is probably describing the period before his death, but the book does not say
