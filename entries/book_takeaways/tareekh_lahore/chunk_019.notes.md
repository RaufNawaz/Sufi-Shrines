# tareekh_lahore — chunk 019 — pp. 265–278

**A run of tombs, mosques and one cemetery in Lahore's eastern suburbs — Garhi Shahu, Baghbanpura,
Khoi Miran, Begampura — told largely as a history of who stripped them.** The chunk covers مقبرہ سید
عبد الوہاب قادری, مقبرہ سید جان محمد حضوری, مسجد دایہ انگا, مزار حسو تیلی, مقبرہ جانی خان, مقبرہ
شاہنزادہ پرویز, مزار فقیر سائین قطب شاہ قادری, مقبرہ نواب میان خان, مزار فتح شاہ مست, مزار شاہ ابو
تراب (شاہ گدا لاہوری), مزار شاہ حسین زنجانی, گورستان بیگم پورہ, سفید گنبد (لکا گنبد) and the opening of
مسجد خواجہ ایاز (pp. 265–278, folios 268–281). Its value to this project is overwhelmingly
**institutional and economic**: four separate statements that the government had a monument repaired
**through the author of this book**; a mosque that passed from waqf to powder magazine to *nazul* to a
printing-press superintendent's bungalow to railway property; a tomb-garden auctioned for 2,200 rupees;
revenue-free and revenue-assessed shrine land; possession suits before Maharaja Ranjit Singh and then in
British civil courts; and a long, named ledger of brick- and marble-robbing from Muslim tombs.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker**, and in this chunk the printed folio runs three ahead
of the PDF index throughout: p. 265 → folio 268, p. 278 → folio 281, each marker reading exactly that.
The folios below are only the ones the chunk prints; nothing is extrapolated.

  - **A recorded friendship and a saying.** Of Hassu Teli: "کہنے مین کہ حضرت لال حسین کے سا تہہ اسکی کمال
    محبت تہی اور کہا کرتا تہا کہ **حسو حسین اور حسین حسو ہے دو ئی باقی نہین ہے**" — it is said he had

- **shrine-of-miran-hussain-zanjani-zanjani-sahib** (the book's "مزار شاہ حسین زنجانی"):
  - **Location.** "یہ متبرک مزار موضع کہوئی میران بیرون شہر لاہور بجانب شرق بفاصلہ ایک میل کے واقع ہے" —
    this blessed mazar lies in **mauza Khoi Miran (موضع کہوئی میران)**, outside Lahore city to the
    **east**, at a distance of **one mile** (p. 275, folio 278).

    لاہور سے ہے" — though the building is small the place is ancient, and the occupant is among the
    **earliest elders of the city of Lahore** (p. 275, folio 278).
  - **Origin and career as the book gives it.** "سلاطین غوریہ کے وقت یہ شہر زنجان سے ہند کی سیر کو آیا
    اور تمام کشور ہند کی سیر کی واپسی کے وقت لاہور آ کر قیام پذیر ہوا اور یہان ہی فوت ہوا" — in the time
    of the **Ghurid sultans (سلاطین غوریہ)** he came from the **city of Zanjan (شہر زنجان)** to travel in
    Hind, toured the whole realm of Hind, and on his return came to Lahore, settled, and died here

  - **Date of death as printed.** "سال وفات اسکا سنہ ۶۰۴ [OCR?] ہے" — his year of death is **604[OCR?]**;
    no era is printed with the numeral (p. 275, folio 278). See Doubts: the archive row's own
    `figure_died` field reads otherwise, and the two are left side by side here.
  - **Fabric.** The mazar stands inside a **brick enclosure a man's height (قد آدم بلند چار دیواری
    خشتی)**; the grave itself brick, "چہ ننگ [OCR?]"; **pakka houses built for faqirs to live in
    (مکانات پختہ فقرا کی سکونت کے لئے)**; and a **Persian-wheel well running (چاہ چرخی دار جاری)**
    (p. 275, folio 278).
  - Its mela and its decline are under Practices.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The oil-seller's scales.** Of Hassu Teli: "خریدار کے ہا تہہ مین یہ ترازو دے دیتا تہا اور کہتا تہا کہ
  **خود وزن کر لے**" — he would put the scales into the buyer's own hand and tell him to weigh for
  himself; "جس جو نیا [OCR?] وہ لیتا تا تہا **اسکا غلہ گہر آ کر گہٹ [OCR?] جاتا تہا** اور جہ [OCR?] پورا
  لیجا تا تہا **اسکا بڑہ جاتا تہا**" — whoever took [less — the word is flagged], **his grain diminished
  on reaching home**, and whoever carried off the full measure, **his increased** (pp. 268–269, folios
  271–272). Stated flatly, in the author's own voice, as the reason the shop is still honoured.
- **Hundreds of karamat, none narrated.** Of Fateh Shah Mast: "**اسکی کرامتین صد ہا مشہور مین**" —
  hundreds of his karamat are famous; the chunk narrates none, giving only his standing "اکثر مستی و
  مجذوبی [OCR?] کی حالت مین", mostly in a state of intoxication and majzubi[OCR?], remaining standing
  wherever he stopped, "یا ایک دریا مین کسی ما نک [OCR?] کہہرا رہا", or standing in a river (p. 274,
  folio 277).
- **A saying of identity.** "حسو حسین اور حسین حسو ہے دو ئی باقی نہین ہے" — Hassu is Husain and Husain is
  Hassu; no duality remains (p. 269, folio 272), given as hearsay of Hassu Teli about Hazrat Lal Husain.
- **Long life as a mark of the saints.** Fateh Shah Mast "ایکسو گیارہ [OCR?] برس کی عمر پائی", attained
  111[OCR?] years (p. 274, folio 277); Shah Gada Lahori "ایکسو چودہ [OCR?] برس کی عمر پائی", 114[OCR?]
  years (p. 275, folio 278). Both figures carry [OCR?].
- **A saint's will against a pakka grave.** "یہ مزار اسکی وصیت کے بموجب پختہ نہین بنایا گیا" — by Faqir
  Sain Qutb Shah Qadri's own bequest the mazar was not built pakka (p. 271, folio 274) — reported as
  fact, without a formula of wonder.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Ruin as moral instruction, in the author's own voice.** Of Jani Khan: "**خدا کی قدرت ہے کہ اب اتنے
  بڑے امیر کبیر کی قبر بہی باقی نہین رہی اور مقبرے مین پیر از [OCR?] زمیندار نے اپنا بہوسہ [OCR?] ڈالا
  ہوا ہے**" — it is God's power that now even the grave of so great a noble no longer remains, and a
  zamindar has put his chaff-store[OCR?] in the tomb — closed with the Qur'anic tag "**فاعتبروا یا اولی
  الابصار**" (p. 270, folio 273). Of Nawab Mian Khan's garden, a couplet: "دور گردون کی گردش کو دیکہ
  [OCR?] ایک دم بہر نہی [OCR?] نہین جسکو قیام / رات پڑتی ہے کبہی ہوتا ہے دن صبح ہوتی ہے کبہی ہوتی ہے
  شام" — look at the turning of the heavens' wheel, nothing has a moment's permanence; night falls,
  sometimes day comes; morning comes, sometimes evening (p. 272, folio 275).
- **A judgement that present workmanship cannot match the old.** Twice: of the Dai Anga mosque's inlay,
  "اب یاز نگین [OCR?] و منقش کام ہونا پہ مشکل ہے" (p. 268, folio 271); of Begampura, "اس خوبی و مضبوطی
  کی عمارت **ہرگز اب بن نہین سکتی**", a building of such excellence and solidity can never now be made
  (p. 275, folio 278).
- **The author writing as the conservator he describes.** The four repair notices (pp. 265, 271, 278;
  folios 268, 274, 281) put the author inside his own narrative as the government's agent; the entries
  they attach to are precisely those he has just described as stripped. The chunk never comments on the
  arrangement.
- **Cross-references to his own larger works and to a section still to come.** "جسکا ذکر علیحدہ اس کتاب
  مین درج ہوگا" — Mian Khan's two havelis will be treated separately in this book (p. 272, folio 275);
  "اس مکان کی عمارت کی تشریح لکہنے کے لیے ایک علیحدہ کتاب چاہیئے" — a separate book would be needed to
  describe Begampura's architecture (p. 277, folio 280); "نواب خان بہادر کا حال مفصل **تاریخ پنجاب
  مولفہ راقم** مین درج ہے تا [OCR?] یقین دان [OCR?] ہو ان سے مطالعہ کرلین" — the detailed account of
  Nawab Khan Bahadur is entered in the **Tarikh-e-Punjab compiled by the present writer**, which readers
  may consult (p. 278, folio 281).
- **A titulature explained etymologically.** The "Anga" of Dai Anga (p. 267, folio 270) and the "Huzuri"
  of the Huzuri Sayyids (p. 266, folio 269) are both glossed from the word's sense rather than from a
  document — the author's habit of deriving an institution from its name.
- **Aesthetic judgement recorded as such.** "یہ دوسری مسجد صرف خوبصورتی کے لئے بنائی گئی ہے" — the eastern
  counterpart at Mian Khan's tomb was built only for beauty (p. 273, folio 276); the author calls this
  symmetry "لطف", the charm of the place.
- **Regime as the organising frame for property, not for piety.** The chunk dates everything by reign or
  regime — "شاہجہانی عہد", "محمد شاہی عہد", "بعہد عالمگیر", "سکہون کے وقت", "عملداری سکہی مین", "انگریزی
  عملداری مین", "انگریزی سلطنت کیوقت", "آخر سلطنت چغتائی تک" — and the changes it records at each turn
  are changes of **possession**: floors stripped by order, buildings put on the *nazul* schedule,
  auctions, jagirs in compensation, suits dismissed, melas shrinking (pp. 265–278, folios 268–281).

## Citable passages

- "اس مقبرے کی مرمت بہی اب سرکار نے مولف کتاب کی نگرانی مین کرائی ہے۔" — "The repair of this tomb too has
  now been carried out by the government under the supervision of the author of the book." (p. 265,
  folio 268)
- "حسو حسین اور حسین حسو ہے دو ئی باقی نہین ہے۔" — "Hassu is Husain and Husain is Hassu; no duality
  remains." (p. 269, folio 272)
- "خدا کی قدرت ہے کہ اب اتنے بڑے امیر کبیر کی قبر بہی باقی نہین رہی۔" — "It is God's power that now even
  the grave of so great a noble no longer remains." (p. 270, folio 273)
- "یہ دوسری مسجد صرف خوبصورتی کے لئے بنائی گئی ہے۔" — "This second mosque was built only for beauty."
  (p. 273, folio 276)
- "اس جگہ پیغمبر خدا کے قدم کا نشان ایک پتہر مین تہا نہین معلوم سکہون کیوقت اُسکو کون اُٹہا کر لیگیا۔" —
  "At this place the mark of the Prophet of God's foot was in a stone; it is not known who carried it off
  in the Sikhs' time." (p. 277, folio 280)

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the site is unnamed.** The Qur'an and sword taken out by the Sikhs,
  their valuation at "کسی ہزار روپیے", and the government repair through the author all belong to a tomb
  whose heading falls in the previous chunk (p. 265, folio 268). Do not attach them to Sayyid Abdul Wahab
  Qadri, whose heading comes after them.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** مسجد خواجہ ایاز breaks off at "ماتحت نواب علی مردان خان" (p. 278,
  folio 281); nothing about the mosque's fabric, custodianship or dates is available here.

  p. 275, folio 278); ۶۰۴ [OCR?] (Shah Husain Zanjani, no era, p. 275, folio 278); ۱۱۶۰ [OCR?] ہجری
