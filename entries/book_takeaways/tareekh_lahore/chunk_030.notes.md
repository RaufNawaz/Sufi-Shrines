# tareekh_lahore — chunk 030 — pp. 426–441

**The close of the book's British-institutions section, then the book's own appendix of Hindu mandirs.**
The chunk opens mid-entry inside a church (گرجا) whose name it never prints, describes it room by room to
p. 429 (folio 436), then gives **مکان میڈیکل سکول لاہور**, the Lahore Medical School, with its measurements,
teaching rooms and cost (pp. 429–432, folios 436–439). At p. 432 (folio 439) the book opens a **ضمیمہ
(appendix)** of buildings "جو بوقت تحریر مسودہ طبع کتاب کے لکہے نہیں گئے تہے", which were not written at the
time of writing the manuscript for printing, and there follow five Hindu foundations: the Thakurdwara and
Shiwala of **سنت شاہ و دیویدیال[OCR?]** (pp. 432–435, folios 439–442), the Thakurdwara of **لالہ نہال چند**
(pp. 435–437, folios 442–444), the **ٹہاکردوارہ ماکہو شاہ** (pp. 437–438, folios 444–445), the **مندر دیوی
دوارہ رجو و دہرمن** (pp. 438–440, folios 445–447), the **ہمری[OCR?] گیان مندر و شوالہ ایشری[OCR?] پرشاد
کایتہ** (pp. 440–441, folios 447–448), and an unnamed **شوالہ و ٹہاکردوارہ** at which the chunk breaks off
(p. 441, folio 448). Its value here is **institutional and economic**: rent from ground-floor shops paying a
temple's expenses, nazul land auctioned and bought for a temple, a jail contractor working under the author,
a named pujari, two melas with offerings, and a preaching platform used against the Arya and Brahmo
movements.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A church's blessed water and who touches it.** "جب عیسائی زن و مرد وہاں آتے ہیں تو اُس پانی سے
  ہاتہ بہگو[OCR?] کر آنکہوں کو لگاتے ہیں اور اُس پانی کو نہایت متبرک سمجتے ہیں" — when Christian **women and
  men** come there they wet[OCR?] their hands from that water and touch it to their eyes, and hold the water
  very sacred (p. 427, folio 434). The book describes the practice in the same register — متبرک — that it
  uses for pindis and charpais at Hindu sites in this chunk.
- **A bell rung on the clock as well as for prayer.** One of the two church bells, hung outside to the west,
  is rung "نماز کے وقت اور ہر روز بارہ بجے کیوقت[OCR?]", at prayer time and every day at twelve o'clock
  (p. 426, folio 433).
- **A government medical school: plan, teaching use and equipment.** Length east–west **two hundred feet**,
  width north–south "اکیسو[OCR?] اکتالیس فٹ" (forty-one feet, preceded by a flagged numeral word); on the
  north, within seventy feet of width, "۳۸[OCR?] - ۳۸[OCR?] فٹ تو زبر عمارت دو نو طرف ہے، اور ایک سو چہہ فٹ
  لمبا بطور صحن کہلا رکہا گیا ہے", 38[OCR?] feet of building on each side and one hundred and six feet left
  open as a **courtyard**; its middle door set opposite the hospital's middle door (p. 430, folio 437). The
  middle room, 55[OCR?] × 32[OCR?] feet and 38[OCR?] feet high excluding the truss, holds "دفتر اور کتابیں
  متعلقہ تعلیم میڈیکل", the **office and the books relating to medical education** (p. 430, folio 437). West
  of it a room 43 × 34 feet in which **"ہندوستانی طالب علموں کو تعلیم دی جاتی ہے اور لیکچر بہی سنائے جاتے
  ہیں جو متعلق علم جراحی کے ہوتے ہیں"** — Indian students are taught and **lectures on surgery (علم جراحی)**
  delivered, its roof strengthened with **iron girders (لوہے کے گارڈروں[OCR?])** (p. 430, folio 437). West
  again, past a ten-foot lane, a room 34[OCR?] × twenty[OCR?] feet in which **"مُردوں کی لاشیں چیر کر طلبا کو
  دکہلائی جاتی ہیں"**, corpses are dissected and shown to the students (p. 430, folio 437). North of it a
  large room in which "عجائب چیزیں متعلق تعلیم ڈاکٹری کے رکہی رہتی ہیں", **curiosities relating to medical
  education** are kept, with an adjoining room "جو اُسی کے تعلق سمجہا جاتا ہے" (p. 431, folio 438). East of
  the middle room two rooms of 34 × 22 feet under iron girders in which "علم طبابت جراحی و معلوم کیمیا کے
  لیکچر طلبا کو سنائے جاتے ہیں", lectures in **medicine, surgery and chemistry** are delivered; beyond, a
  room 34 × 20 feet in which "مشریا[OCR?] ڈیکا[OCR?] پڑہائی جاتی ہے", **materia medica[OCR?]** is taught; and
  a further room 42 × 20 feet in which "سمت شفت[OCR?] کام متعلق ڈاکٹری کے دکہلائے جاتے ہیں، مثلاً پتہر کی
  کانکا[OCR?] لنبا[OCR?] یا کسی عضو کا کانبا[OCR?]", operative work is demonstrated — for example the cutting
  of a stone[OCR?] or of some limb[OCR?] (p. 431, folio 438). The whole is "ایک گلی اور بارہ کمروں میں تقسیم"
  — one lane and **twelve rooms** — single-storeyed, pakka lime, with verandahs inside and out, a bathroom
  formed from one bay of the eastern verandah, tiled floors throughout, white plaster inside and "باہر سے
  سرخ اینٹ کی چہری دار عمارت", red-brick facing outside (p. 431, folio 438; p. 432, folio 439).
- **The author named as the building's supervisor, and its cost.** **"یہ مکان مولف کتاب کی سرپرستی و نگرانی
  میں سرکار کے حکم سے تعمیر ہوا، جسکو دیکہکر حکام نے خوشنودی ظاہر کی۔ اور کل روپیہ اسکی تعمیر پہ ایک
  لاکہہ ۳۲[OCR?] ہزار صرف ہوا"** — the building was constructed under the **patronage and supervision of
  مولف کتاب (the author of the book)** by order of the government, and on seeing it the officers expressed
  satisfaction; the total spent on its construction was **one lakh 32[OCR?] thousand rupees**; the cost of
  the separately built attached buildings "جو مکانات نساگر[OCR?] و پیشہ[OCR?] وغیرہ متعلق اسکے علحدہ بنے
  ہوئے ہیں اُنکا خرچ الگ ہے" is reckoned separately (p. 432, folio 439). Recorded as the book states it.
- **Shop rents endowing a temple.** Of the Sant Shah–Devi Dial[OCR?] mandir: **"مندر کا مکان دوسری منزل پہ
  بنایا گیا ہے، نیچے دوکانیں ہیں جنمیں کرایہ دار رہتے ہیں، جن کرایہ بانیان مندر لیتے اور مندر کے اخراجات میں
  خرچ کرتے ہیں"** — the mandir is built on the upper storey; **below are shops occupied by tenants, whose
  rent the mandir's founders take and spend on the mandir's expenses** (p. 432, folio 439; p. 433, folio 440).
  The same pattern recurs at the Nihal Chand mandir, where four shops north and three south are built into
  the ground floor (p. 436, folio 443), and at the unnamed Mori Gate pair, where three shops on the road are
  let to woodsellers (p. 441, folio 448).
- **A new well and a sabil open to all.** At the same mandir, a newly dug well with a pakka roofed platform
  and a red-stone sabil, "اس جگہ سے عام و خاص بہرتے[OCR?] ہیں", used by high and low; the roof over the well
  is pierced for drawing water and surfaced with lime "جہاں سے لوگ پانی بہرتے اور نہاتے ہیں", where people
  draw water and **bathe** (p. 432, folio 439; p. 433, folio 440).
- **A piped jal dhara over the Shivling.** "شیوجی کے اوپر جل دہارا کرنے کے لیے دیوار جنوبی میں آبدان بنایا
  گیا ہے، جس میں باہر کے درجے چاہ والے سے پانی ڈال دیتے ہیں اور شب جی[OCR?] پر بذریعہ ایک پیتل کے سانپ کے
  پانی پہنچتا ہے" — a water-basin was built into the south wall for the **jal dhara**, filled from the outer
  well-terrace, and the water reaches Shiv ji[OCR?] through a **brass snake (پیتل کے سانپ)** very well
  modelled, "سانپ کے منہ سے برابر باریک دہار پانی کی شیوجی پر پڑتی رہتی ہے", a fine stream falling
  continually from its mouth; in one corner "چاہ غرقی بنا ہے جسمیں جل دہارا کا پانی غرق ہوتا ہے", a soakage
  well takes the jal dhara water away (p. 435, folio 442).
- **A separate women's route to worship.** At the Sant Shah–Devi Dial[OCR?] mandir a staircase in the
  north-east corner "جو کوچہ میں جا کر کہلتا ہے اور مستورات اُس راستے سے آ کر پوجا کرتی میں" — opens into
  the lane, and **women come by that route and perform puja** (p. 435, folio 442).
- **Nazul land auctioned by government and bought for a temple.** Of the Nihal Chand site: "یہ زمین جس جگہ اب
  مندر بنتا ہے سکہوں کے عہد سے سفید بلا عمارت پڑی[OCR?] آتی تہی" — the land had lain **vacant and without
  building since the Sikhs' era**; **"اب سرکار نے بصیغہ نزول یہ زمین نیلام کر دی، اور نہال چند نے خرید کر
  اُس پہ یہ متبرک مندر بنانا شروع کر دیا"** — now the government **auctioned this land under the head of
  nazul (بصیغہ نزول)**, and Nihal Chand bought it and began building this sacred mandir on it (p. 435,
  folio 442; p. 436, folio 443). The neighbouring Kharak Singh haveli had already been sold off for its
  bricks by the same government (p. 436, folio 443).
- **A jail contractor as temple founder, working under the author.** **"یہ شخص لاہور کے الداروں[OCR?] و
  ساہوکا رتزن[OCR?] میں نامی گرامی آدمی ہے۔ ٹہیکہ داری کا کام کرتا ہے۔ سنٹرل جیل لاہور میں ہر ایک چیز کا
  ٹہیکہ اُسیکے متعلق ہے، جو جیل کی عمارت کا کام بہی مولف کتاب کے ماتحت یہی شخص کرتا ہے"** — Lala Nihal Chand
  is a renowned man among Lahore's ...[OCR?] and **sahukars**; he works as a **contractor**; the contract for

  **building work under مولف کتاب (the author of the book)** (p. 435, folio 442). Recorded as the book states

  lime with much stone (p. 435, folio 442).
- **A temple bought ready-made, dated only by an interval.** "سات برس کا عرصہ گزرا ہے کہ ماکہو شاہ نے یہ
  مکان خرید کر ٹہاکردوارہ بنا دیا" — seven years ago Makho Shah **bought a house and turned it into a
  thakurdwara**, which still has a haveli's form (p. 437, folio 444).
- **A named pujari and a temple's running costs.** "پجاری اُس مندر کا بالفعل رام کشن داس بیراگی مقرر ہے، اور
  خرچ مندر کا بندوبست باقی مکان کے" — the pujari of the Makho Shah thakurdwara is at present **رام کشن داس
  بیراگی (Ram Kishan Das Bairagi)**, appointed, and the mandir's expenses are arranged from the rest of the

- **Two annual melas and continuous jagas at the Devi Dwara.** "یہاں بہار ہی[OCR?] میلہ ہوتا ہے، ایک ماہ
  چیت[OCR?] کی اشٹمی کو، دوسرا اشٹمی ماہ اسوج[OCR?] کو۔ خلقت کثیر جمع ہوتی ہے، چڑہاوہ بہت چڑہتا ہے" — a
  mela is held here, one on the **Ashtami of the month of Chait[OCR?]** and the second on the **Ashtami of

  **jagas (جاگے)**, and all night the **bhagat** people sing the Devi ji's bhents[OCR?] (p. 439, folio 446).

  پرشاد کایتہ نے بنوایا تہا" — **much land** belongs to it, with four کوٹہر[OCR?] and a well, and the shiwala

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The Devi who came when called — told as the muhalla's testimony.** "اہل محلہ بیان کرتے ہیں کہ" — **the
  people of the muhalla state** that in **Akbar Badshah's era** two sisters named Rajo and Dharman, of the
  اڑوٹری[OCR?] qaum, lived in this muhalla, engaged in the Devi's worship; "دیوی جی اُنپر یہاں تک مہربان
  تہیں کہ جب وہ یاد کرتیں خود آ درشن دیتی" — the Devi ji was so kind to them that whenever they called she
  came herself and gave **darshan** (p. 438, folio 445). Dharman "نے شادی سے انکار کیا اور دیوی جی کی محبت
  میں ایسی محو ہوئی کہ دنیا سے علاقہ اُنکا شہ[OCR?] ہا", refused marriage and was so absorbed in the Devi's
  love that her connection with the world ...[OCR?].
- **A dead child restored, and a house given in payment.** The site's owner, "ایک شخص کہتری[OCR?] مہر و
  ترا[OCR?]", a Khatri[OCR?] of that name, lost his only young son "بقضائی آلہی"; he came weeping to
  Dharman and begged her to petition the Devi; "چنانچہ رجو و دہرمن دونوں نے دیوی جی کی طرف دہیان لگایا تو
  دیوی جی تشریف لائیں اور وہ مرا ہوا لڑکا فی الفور زندہ ہو گیا" — both turned their attention to the Devi
  ji, the Devi ji came, and **the dead boy at once came alive**; "وہ کہتری[OCR?] بہت خوش ہوا اور یہ مکان
  بجو[OCR?] و دہرمن کی نذر کر دیا", the Khatri[OCR?] in his joy **made this house over to them as a nazr**,
  and from that day they resided there on the dedication (p. 438, folio 445).
- **Akbar's test of the seeds.** "رفتہ رفتہ شہرہ اُنکی کرامت کا اکبر بادشاہ کے کان میں پہنچا" — the fame of
  their **karamat** reached Akbar Badshah's ear; he came in person and presented "چند تخم درختان آم و انار
  وغیرہ کے", some seeds of mango, pomegranate and other trees, with the demand that they be sown and the
  trees grow green and fruit that very moment; the two called on the Devi ji, "دیوی جی تشریف لائیں اور وہ
  درخت اُسی وقت سر سبز ہو گئے اور شیرہ[OCR?] وقت پہل[OCR?] لگ گیا" — the Devi ji came and **the trees grew
  green at that moment and fruit[OCR?] appeared**; the emperor's reply survives only as "کہا ایا[OCR?] چہ
  مدت تک" (p. 438, folio 445; p. 439, folio 446).
- **Two women who sank into the earth rather than marry the governor.** "جہانگیر بادشاہ کی وقت صوبہ لاہور جو
  ایک مسلمان شخص تہا، وہ دہرمن کے حسن و جمال کی تعریف سنکار اُسپر فریفتہ ہو گیا، اور اُس نے پیغام دیا کہ
  دہرمن مسلمان ہو کر اُس سے نکاح کرے" — in **Jahangir Badshah's** time the **subah (governor) of Lahore**, a
  Muslim, hearing praise of Dharman's beauty, was enamoured and sent a message that she should become Muslim
  and marry him; "وہ دونو نیک ذات صاحب عصمت عورتیں نہایت تنگ ہوئیں", the two virtuous and chaste women were
  greatly distressed, and did not wish to speak ill of the ruler of the time (the clause is damaged);
  **"آخر خود و اس جگہ جہان اب مندر بنا ہوا ہے زمین میں سما گئیں، و دیوی جی کا جلوہ بہی وہ جو اُسوقت موجود
  تہا زمین میں چلا گیا"** — in the end they sank into the earth at the very spot where the mandir now stands,
  and the Devi ji's manifestation present at that time went into the earth with them. "چنانچہ اُس روز سے اس
  جگہ دیوی دوارہ مقرر ہو گیا اور پرستش شروع ہوئی" — from that day a **Devi Dwara was established** at the
  place and worship began (p. 439, folio 446).
- **The sinking spot and the sleeping cots as the shrine's two relics.** The pindi marks "وہ موقع … جہان رجو
  و دہرمن اور دیوی جی کا جلوہ زمین میں سما گیا تہا", the spot of the sinking, and the two charpais are held
  to be theirs (p. 440, folio 447). The chunk reports these as the objects of belief without endorsing them.
- The book tells no miracle of any of the other sites in this chunk; the church's blessed water and the
  Shivling's jal dhara are given as practices, not wonders (p. 427, folio 434; p. 435, folio 442).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The book declares its own appendix, and why it exists.** "ضمیمہ — بعض ان مکانات کے ذکر میں جو بوقت تحریر
  مسودہ طبع کتاب کے لکہے نہیں گئے تہے اور اب بعد اختتام مسودہ و طبع کتاب اُنکا لکہنا واجب و منصور[OCR?] ہوا"
  — an **appendix** concerning some buildings that were not written when the manuscript was being written for
  printing, and whose writing became obligatory after the manuscript and the printing were finished (p. 432,
  folio 439). The Sant Shah–Devi Dial[OCR?] entry then repeats the reason in its own words: "اس کتاب کی تحریر
  مسودہ کے وقت اس مندر کی بنیاد کہید[OCR?] رہی تہی، اس سبب اسکا حال موقع پر درج نہوا۔ اب یہ مکان بنکر تیار
  ہو گیا ہے" — the foundation was still being dug[OCR?] then, so no account was entered in place; the building
  is now finished (p. 432, folio 439). Everything after p. 432 in this chunk is therefore, on the book's own
  statement, later than the body of the book.
- **The author writes buildings he himself supervised into his own city history.** The Medical School is
  recorded as built "مولف کتاب کی سرپرستی و نگرانی میں", under the author of the book's patronage and
  supervision, with the officers' satisfaction and the rupee total both noted (p. 432, folio 439); and Lala
  Nihal Chand, founder of a mandir the book describes at length, is identified as the contractor who does
  the Central Jail's building work "مولف کتاب کے ماتحت", under the author of the book (p. 435, folio 442).
  Recorded as the book makes the statements.
- **Buildings judged by whether they are worth seeing.** Of the church: "عرض کہ اس گرجا کی خوبی دیکہنے پہ
  منحصر ہے" — its excellence depends on being seen (p. 429, folio 436). Of the Christ image: "اُستاد نے
  اُستادی سے اُسکو بنایا ہے کہ اصل اور نقل میں کچہ فرق باقی نہیں چہوڑا", the master made it so masterfully
  that nothing remains to distinguish original from copy (p. 429, folio 436). Of the Thakurdwara's gilded
  kalas, that it "شایقین[OCR?] کی آنکہوں کو روشن کرتا ہے", brightens the eyes of devotees (p. 434,
  folio 441); of a dome that it is "نظارت بخش دیدہ اہل بصیرت", a sight granted to the discerning eye
  (p. 441, folio 448). The register is the same for a church, a mandir and a government school.
- **A superlative about a building not yet built.** Of the Nihal Chand mandir: "غرض کہ جب یہ مندر بنکر تیار
  ہو گا تو اسکا نی[OCR?] بلندی تین[OCR?] اور کوئی مندر نہو گا" — when it is complete no other mandir will
  [equal] its height — asserted while the mandir stands half-built and its corner shrines "نیم تیار", only
  half-ready (p. 437, folio 444). The clause is damaged; see Doubts.
- **A reform controversy noticed from the temple's side.** Of Sardha Ram Phaluri[OCR?]'s preaching: "اکثر
  کلام اُسکا بنر[OCR?] خلاف جدید مذاہب یا آریہ و برہمو کے ہوتا تہا، جو مورتی پوجن کے بالکل بر خلاف میں" —
  most of his discourse was against the **new religions, the Arya and the Brahmo**, which are entirely
  **against idol-worship (مورتی پوجن)** (p. 441, folio 448). The book registers the dispute by describing
  the crowds a defender of image-worship drew, and their falling off after his death, rather than by arguing
  the question.
- **Legend is attributed, fabric is not.** The whole Rajo–Dharman narrative is prefaced "اہل محلہ بیان کرتے
  ہیں کہ", the people of the muhalla state (p. 438, folio 445), while measurements, costs, rents, land titles
  and appointments throughout the chunk are stated flatly in the author's own voice.
- **Regime change appears again as a change in who owns.** A haveli of the Sikh court sold for its bricks, a
  vacant plot lying "سکہوں کے عہد سے" untouched, the same plot auctioned "بصیغہ نزول" by the British
  government and bought by a contractor for a temple (p. 435, folio 442; p. 436, folio 443); a shiwala built
  "سکہوں کے وقت" still standing with its land outside a city gate (p. 440, folio 447).

## Doubts

- **No archive id is matched in this chunk.** The sites are an unnamed church, the Lahore Medical School, and
  five Hindu foundations — the Sant Shah–Devi Dial[OCR?] Thakurdwara/Shiwala, the Nihal Chand Thakurdwara,
  the Makho Shah Thakurdwara, the Devi Dwara of Rajo o Dharman, and the Hamri[OCR?] Gyan Mandir with the
  Ishri[OCR?] Prashad Kayath shiwala — plus an unnamed Shiwala–Thakurdwara pair outside Mori Gate. None of
  them corresponds to a row in `shrine_index.tsv`; the Lahore rows there are a different set of sites. I have
  deliberately matched nothing rather than force a row.
- **The chunk opens mid-entry.** p. 426 (folio 433) begins "و مکانات متعلقہ کو بہی ہے", inside the
  description of a church whose **name is never printed in this chunk**. The building can only be called
  "the church (گرجا)"; its denomination, dedication and street are (not stated) here.
- **The chunk closes mid-sentence.** The last entry, headed only "شوالہ و ٹہاکردوارہ" (p. 441, folio 448),
  breaks off at "اُسپر شیوجی کا جلوس ہے". Its founder and any further name are (not stated) in this chunk; do
  not name it from context.
- **Bibi Maryam's image: stone or clay, in the same passage.** p. 428 (folio 435) first calls it "مورت سنگی
  کی", a stone image, then says "پہ مورت اگرچہ مٹی[OCR?] کی ہے", though this image is of clay[OCR?]; at
  p. 429 (folio 436) the western room's Maryam is again "سنگی[OCR?] مورت". Left side by side.
- **Niches described as filled and then as empty.** At p. 434 (folio 441) images are placed in the western
  and southern niches of the Thakurdwara, and the passage then reads "ہر ایک طاقچہ خالی ہے", every niche is
  empty. Recorded as printed; not reconciled.
- **Rajo and Dharman: sisters, or mother and daughter.** p. 438 (folio 445) introduces them as "رجو و دہرمن
  نام دو بہنیں", two sisters, and within the same paragraph says "مسمات رجو ایک عورت بیوہ [illegible] اور
  دہرمن اُسکی دختر باکرہ نہایت خوبصورت تہی", Rajo a widow [illegible] and Dharman her virgin daughter. Both
  stand.
- **A negated verb where the sense is positive.** p. 440 (folio 447): "دو چارپایان متبرک رکہی ہیں جو
  مستان[OCR?] رجو و دہرمن کے سونے کی نہیں" — printed with **نہیں** ("are not"), although the sentence and
  the next both treat the charpais as theirs and sacred. Carried as printed; a human should check the
  lithograph.
