# tareekh_lahore — chunk 010 — pp. 133–147

**The chunk finishes the survey of Hindu buildings inside the walled city and then opens a new class:
"دوسری[OCR?] قسم مین تشریح اُن مکانات کی جو اندرون شہر لاہور متعلق باہل اسلام ہین از قسم خانقاہ ومقبر
ومساجد ومعابد وغیرہ"** — the second class, a description of those buildings inside the city of Lahore
belonging to the people of Islam, of the kind of khanqah, tomb, mosque and place of worship (p. 138,
folio 141). The author states his own sub-division: "وہ دو قسم پر منقسم ہین ایک قدیم یعنی پُرانے زمانے
کے مکان دوم وہ مکان جو اب تعمیر ہوئے یا عہد سکہی مین بن چکے ہین" — two kinds, ancient buildings and
those built now or in the Sikh era, and the ancient ones are taken first (p. 138, folio 141). Eleven
buildings are described: a Kali Mata mandir (entry begun before this chunk), a thakurdwara, the chaubara
of Chhajju Bhagat, Sadhu the sweeper's well and shivala, the Badshahi Masjid, the Masti Darwaza mosque,
Wazir Khan's small mosque, the Taksali mosque, the khanqah of Shah Zia Qadri, the Sabz Gumbad mazar, the
old Bhati Darwaza mosque and the Sunehri Masjid. **The chunk's weight for this project is institutional
rather than hagiographic**: custodianship titles, rent-funded endowments, a nikah cess, two fatwas on
mosque land, military occupation and restitution, and two documented seizures of a mosque.

**The folio marker on every page of this chunk reads exactly three more than the PDF index** — p. 133 →
folio 136 through p. 147 → folio 150, each printed on its own page. No folio below is computed.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (میان میر بالاپیر — "Mian Mir Bala Pir", the book's title for him, p. 139,
  folio 142):
  - **His rauza is named as Dara Shikoh's second building project in Lahore.** In the account of why the
    Badshahi Masjid came to be built: in Shah Jahan's reign, when the governorship of Punjab was entrusted
    to Dara Shikoh, "اُس نے عمارتین بہت کین پہلے چوک بنوایا پہر عالیشان عمارت اپنے **دادا مرشد میان میر
    بالاپیر کے روضہ** کی تعمیر کی" — he built many buildings: first he had a chowk built, then the lofty
    building of the rauza of his *dada murshid* (his murshid's murshid) Mian Mir Bala Pir (p. 139,
    folio 142). The book gives no date for the rauza and no death year for the saint (not stated).
  - **Then the rauza of his own murshid, and a village inside its enclosure.** "پہر ملا شاہ قادری اپنے
    مرشد کا روضہ بنوایا جسکے حاطہ مین اب **موضع میانمیر ایک گاؤن آبا دہی**" — then he had the rauza of
    Mulla Shah Qadri, his own murshid, built, in whose enclosure there is now the village of Mauza Mian
    Mir, a settlement (p. 139, folio 142). The village bears the saint's name but the text puts it inside
    **Mulla Shah's** enclosure, not Mian Mir's; see Doubts.
  - **A red-stone processional road to the tomb, planned and never built.** "اُسکا ارادہ تہا کہ چوک ودارا
    شاہ[OCR?] سے میان میر کے مقبرہ تاک ایک سٹرک سنگ سرخ کی ایسی مصفا وپاکیزہ بنوائے کہ **روز سلام
    کیواسطے پا برہنہ وہان جایا آیا کرے** اور اُس سٹرک پر سوے شہزادہ کے اور کوئی آمد ورفت نکرے" — his
    intention was to build from the chowk of Dara Shah[OCR?] to Mian Mir's maqbara a red-stone road so
    clean and pure that he could go and come there **barefoot every day to pay salam**, and that none but
    the prince should have traffic on it (p. 139, folio 142). A daily princely ziyarat, an exclusive
    approach road, and a barefoot protocol — all stated as intention only: the plan was cut short when
    "عالمگیر اورنگ زیب نے باپ پر غالب آکر اُسکو قید کر لیا اور دارا شکوہ اپنے بہائی کو قتل کرا دیا",
    Alamgir Aurangzeb prevailed over his father, imprisoned him and had his brother Dara Shikoh killed
    (p. 139, folio 142).
  - **The stone left over from the tomb became the Badshahi Masjid.** "اس عمارت کے اختتام پر پتہر بہت بڑہ
    رہا تہا" — much stone was left over at the completion of that building; Aurangzeb "حکم دیا کہ موجود ہ
    پتہر کی ایک جامع مسجد بنائی جائے", ordered that a Jami Masjid be built from the stone on hand
    (p. 139, folio 142). The archive row's shrine and the city's largest mosque are materially the same
    stock of red stone, on the book's own account.
  - Nothing here on the shrine's urs, custodianship, income or present condition (not stated).
- **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri** (شاہ عنایت اللہ, named as a murid in the entry on the khanqah of Shah
  Zia Qadri, p. 144, folio 147):
  - **He is named as the murid of Shah Zia Qadri and the continuation of his line.** Of Shah Zia Qadri:
    "یہ بزرگ **خاندان[OCR?] شطاریہ[OCR?] قادریہ[OCR?]** مین سے ایک صاحب بکمال عابد وزاہد آدمی تہا اسکا
    سلسلہ بہی اسکے بعد جاری ہوا چنانچہ **یہ شاہ عنایت اللہ اسی کا مرید تہا**" — this elder was of the
    Shattariya Qadriya family, a man of utmost worship and asceticism; his silsila continued after him;
    accordingly this Shah Inayat Allah was his murid (p. 144, folio 147). The printed order
    **"شطاریہ قادریہ"** is what carries the identification: it matches this row's `silsila` exactly.
  - The chunk gives Shah Inayat Allah **no death date, no location and no shrine** (not stated). The date
    "سنہ[OCR?] ۱۱۲۶[OCR?] مین یہ بزرگ فوت ہوا اور اس مقام پر دفنایا گیا" — this elder died in the year
    1126 and was buried at this place — belongs to **Shah Zia Qadri**, not to him (p. 144, folio 147).
    Do not transfer it. See Doubts on the competing archive row.
- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** (بلہے[OCR?] شاہ, p. 144, folio 147):
  - **His Punjabi verse is recorded as sung repertoire in Lahore's assemblies**, in the same broken
    sentence that mentions Kasur: "بلکہ [illegible] **قصور** کی ایک [illegible] مشہور دلی ہوا ہے پنجابی
    زبان مین **بلہے[OCR?] شاہ** کی کافیان اور بارہ ماہ وابیات وغیرہ کلاونت[OCR?] مجلسون مین گاتے ہین" —
    in the Punjabi language the *kafis* of Bullhe[OCR?] Shah and the *barah-mah* and *abyat* and so on are
    sung in kalawant[OCR?] assemblies (p. 144, folio 147). The name is OCR-flagged and the clause that
    would state his relation to Shah Zia Qadri or to Kasur is broken by two [illegible] gaps; see Doubts.
    No grave, no date, no silsila for him in this chunk (not stated).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **A Kali Mata mandir inside the city, entry begun before this chunk** (p. 133, folio 136): its western
  part is walled off, holding "ایک پختہ سنگین چہونترہ[OCR?]", a pukka stone platform, and on it a small
  mandir in which "کالی ماتا کی سنگی تصویر سیاہ رنگ کی رکہی ہے", a black stone image of Kali Mata is
  placed, under a small gilded dome, with a small chhatr over the goddess's head and a larger gilded
  chhatr above the dome. Its custodian and its economy are under Practices. The temple's name and
  quarter are not in this chunk.
- **Thakurdwara of Pandit Radha Kishan, son of the late Pandit Madsu[OCR?]** (heading at p. 133,
  folio 136; described pp. 133–134, folios 136–137): inside the pandit's own haveli toward
  **محلہ سیدمٹہہ** (Mohalla Syed Mitha), between two lanes, one route from **لگشٹی[OCR?] بازار** and one
  from Mohalla Syed Mitha, the great gate counted as the Lagshti[OCR?] Bazaar one (p. 133, folio 136).
  Built on the north side of a wide courtyard, with a four-lattice stone doorway, gilded painted walls
  and a tall elongated dome with a gilded *kalas*; inside, two stone images, "ایک سری کرشن جی مہاراج کی
  ودوسری راد ہا مائی کی", one of Shri Krishan Ji Maharaj and the other of Radha Mai, installed with
  honour and dressed in "لباس فاخرہ وزیور شاہانہ", fine robes and royal ornaments (p. 134, folio 137).
- **Chhajju Bhagat (چہجو بہگت), and the chaubara that was his dwelling** (pp. 134–136, folios 137–139):
  the house stands in "علاقہ گنڈ[OCR?] ڈہل[OCR?] محلہ[OCR?] سرکوچہ", on the north, its gate to the south
  (p. 134, folio 137). Inside, a well with a pulley and two pukka tanks, a shivala in the great western
  dalan, and a thakurdwara of Krishan Ji Maharaj and Radha Mai with two gilded flags and two gilded
  kalas (pp. 135–136, folios 138–139). **"یہ مکان اصلی مسکونہ چہجو بہگت کا تہا جسمین وہ اپنی حین حیات
  سکونت پذیر رہا اور باہر شہر کے جہان اُوسکی سمادہ ہے وہان[OCR?] اسکی دوکان تہی ودو نو مکانون کی پرستش
  جاری ہے"** — this house was Chhajju Bhagat's original dwelling, where he lived in his lifetime, and
  outside the city, where his **samadh** is, was his shop; worship continues at both houses (p. 136,
  folio 139). The samadh itself is outside this chunk's ground. No dates for him (not stated).
- **Sadhu, a sweeper (چوہڑا) of Lahore, and his well and shivala in بازار مچہی ہٹہ[OCR?]**
  (pp. 136–138, folios 139–141): "پہلے سے صرف چاہ تہا", at first there was only the well, and the shivala
  beside it was built later (p. 136, folio 139). His story is under Legends. **شیو گر سادہ سنیاسی** —
  Shiv Gar, a plain sannyasi — serves the mandir (p. 138, folio 141).
- **Masjid Badshahi** (pp. 138–142, folios 141–145): "لاہور کی قدیمی مساجد سے بہت بڑی مسجد", a very
  great mosque among Lahore's ancient mosques, of red stone and marble, its three great domes of marble,
  the marble domelets of the four minarets "اب موجود نہین", now gone; its steps begin beyond the small
  maidan "جسکو اب حضوری باغ کہتے ہین", now called Hazuri Bagh, by the fort's western gate (p. 138,
  folio 141). Its facade inscription names "او رنگ زیب عالمگیر بادشاہ غازی بانی مسجد" as founder and the
  superintendence of **فدائی خان کوکہ**, of whom the author explains "کوکہ ترکی زبان مین دودہ پلانے والی
  کے بیٹے کو کہتے ہین", *koka* in Turkish means the wet-nurse's son (p. 139, folio 142). The minarets are
  octagonal with an internal stair; from the top the Ravi's waves are visible to north and west (p. 140,
  folio 143). Its occupation, stripping, restitution and repair are under Practices.
- **Masjid Masti Darwaza, east of Lahore Fort** (pp. 142–143, folios 145–146): "بانیہ اسکی **مریم زمانی
  زوجہ نورالدین محمد جہانگیر بادشاہ** تہی", its founder was Maryam Zamani, wife of Nur al-Din Muhammad
  Jahangir Badshah; "مسجد کے طاق **۱۰۲۳** تاریخ اسکے ودروازے پہ لکہی ہے", the date 1023 is written on the
  mosque's arch/door, and "قطعہ تاریخ پورا پورا پڑہا نہین جاتا", the chronogram cannot be read in full
  (p. 142, folio 145). Era of the 1023 not stated.
- **Masjid Khurd Nawab Wazir Khan, south of Taksali Darwaza against the city wall** (p. 143, folio 146):
  **نواب وزیر خان صوبہ لاہور** built his great palace here in the Shah Jahani era; it has now fallen, its
  ruins survived through the Sikh period, and "اب انگریزون نے اُس زمین کو ہموار کرا دیا ہے", the English
  have had the land levelled. "یہ مسجد اُس محل کے متعلق **زنانہ مسجد** تہی جہان آکر صوبہ لاہور کے گہر کے
  زنانے لوگ نماز پڑہا کرتے تہے اور رستہ محل کے اندر سے تہا" — this was the palace's zenana mosque, where
  the women of the governor's household came to pray, reached from inside the palace; it is now in use

- **Taksali wali Masjid and the mint** (p. 144, folio 147): the mosque is inside Taksali Darwaza on the

  متعلق تہی" — Shah Jahan built the Dar al-Zarb (mint) beside it where the rupee was struck, and this
  mosque belonged to that establishment; no trace of the mint now remains (p. 144, folio 147).
- **Shah Zia Qadri (شاہ ضیا قادری) and his khanqah** (p. 144, folio 147): "یہ بہی ایک قدیم ومتبرک مزار

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A nine-day Navratri mela at the Kali Mata mandir, and offerings that pay the pujaris.** "اس مندر کی
  پرستش ہر روز ہوتی ہے اور صدہا ہنود جبین[OCR?] سائی کے لئے حاضر ہوتے ہین **خصوصا نوراتون مین نو دن ہر
  روز میلا یہان جمع رہتا ہے اور چڑہاوہ بہت چڑہتا ہے ہر روزہ آمدنی بہی اسقدر ہوتی ہے جس سے گزارہ پجاریون
  کا بخوبی نہو جاتا ہے**" — worship is daily and hundreds of Hindus attend to bow their foreheads;
  especially in the nine days of Navratri a mela gathers here every day and much offering comes; the daily
  income too is such that the pujaris' livelihood is comfortably met (p. 133, folio 136). **A mela
  measured in days, an offering economy, and a stated dependence of clergy on it.**
- **Pilgrimage from outside the country, and a devotion practised in the street.** "چونکہ یہ مندر لاہور
  کے مشہور مندرون مین سے ہے **غیر ملک کے ہنود بہی یہان آکر مستفیض ہوتے ہین** اور اعتقاد کا یہ حال ہے کہ
  **جو ہندو کوچہ سے گزرتا ہے وہ بہی ودروازہ مکان پہ جبین سائی کر کر آگے جاتا ہے**" — Hindus of other
  countries also come here to benefit, and such is the belief that every Hindu who passes down the lane
  bows his forehead at the house's door before going on (p. 133, folio 136).
- **Custodial titles, one per building, and they differ by community.** **مہنت** for the Kali mandir —
  "فی زمانہ حال مہنت اس مندر کا **پنڈت بشنداس** ایک[OCR?] عالی ودماغ شخص ہے اکثر اوقات علیحدہ بیٹہہ[OCR?]
  … رہتا ہے لوگون سے کلام بہی کم کرتا ہے", at present the mahant is Pandit Bishan Das, a lofty-minded man
  who often sits apart and speaks little (p. 133, folio 136); **سرپرست** for the thakurdwara;
  **پجاری** for the Chhajju Bhagat house and the Sadhu shivala; **متولی** for the Badshahi and Sunehri
  mosques (pp. 133–147, folios 136–150).
- **A family custodianship with a title, a successor, and the expense on one man.** "پنڈت رادہاکشن بانی
  مندر کی وفات کے بعد اب **پنڈت لکہی[OCR?] کشن سرپرست** اس مندر کا ہے اور **اُنہین کے ذمہ پر اسکا خرچ
  ہے**" — after the death of Pandit Radha Kishan, the mandir's founder, Pandit Lakhi[OCR?] Kishan is now
  its patron and its expense is on his responsibility; the family of Pandit Madsudan[OCR?] has come

- **Charitable wells endowed out of the proceeds of a theft.** "چور کے پہانسی ملنے کے بعد کسیقدر مدت کے

- **Cells for dervishes and students at the Badshahi Masjid, and their loss.** "اس مسجد کے شمال وجنوب شرق

- **Demolition of the minarets' turrets by Maharaja Ranjit Singh.** "تین سکہی حاکمون کیوقت اُنمین سے ایک

  rulers one turret fell from an earthquake's shock and three were left needing repair; Maharaja Ranjit

- **The mosque as barracks, arsenal and gun platform, twice under siege.** Furnishings worth lakhs in the
  royal era — "فرش چہار فانوس وغیرہ **لاکہون روپیے** کا تہا" — gave way under the Sikh kingdom to
  "کبہی **توپ خانہ** کبہی **پلٹن** اور سواری کی فوج کی **چہاؤنی**", sometimes an artillery park,
  sometimes a battalion and the cavalry's cantonment; "سکہ لوگ پتہرون کی سلبین[OCR?] اُکہاڑ کر لیگئے …
  **فرش مسجد کا لوٹ گیا**", the Sikhs prised up the stone slabs and carried them off and the mosque's
  floor was looted (p. 140, folio 143). In Rani Chand Kaur's time Sher Singh mounted "زنبورک یعنی شتری
  چہوٹی توپین" — zamburak, camel-mounted small guns — on the minarets, shelling into the fort's courtyard
  while shot from the fort's western wall fell inside the mosque, "تین دن تک یہی حال رہا", for three days;
  Raja Hira Singh did the same during the Sandhanwalia sardars' siege (pp. 140–141, folios 143–144).
  "صاحبان انگریز کی ابتدائی عملداری مین اس مسجد مین **گورون کی فوج** نے قیام کیا اور انگریزون کے لئے
  حجرون مین دریچے نکلوائے گئے اور **برانڈے** نئے تعمیر ہوئے" — in the English's early rule a force of
  European troops was quartered in the mosque and windows were cut in the cells and new verandahs built
  for them (p. 141, folio 144).
- **Restitution, a hereditary mutawalli, and a rent endowment raised by subscription.** At Dalip Singh's

  کچہہ آبادی نہوئی" — the army was withdrawn and the government handed the mosque to the Muslims, and
  Syed Buzurg Shah Hakim, whose elders had been its imams in the royal era, was made mutawalli, but no
  revival followed. Then "**خان بہادر محمد برکت علیخان** نے اپنی تحصیلداری کیوقت مسجد کی آبادی کی طرف
  توجہ کی اور **چندہ کرکے کسی قدر جائداد وبہم پہنچائی جسکے کرایہ کی آمدنی مسجد کے مصارف مین صرف ہونے

  expenses; "چاہ روان اور حوض جاری کیا گیا امام موذن مقرر ہوئے", the well and tank were set running and an
  imam and muezzin appointed (p. 141, folio 144). **An endowment created de novo in the British period,
  in the form of rent-bearing property.**
- **A four-anna cess on every marriage in the city, as mosque revenue.** "قرار پایا کہ **شہر مین جو نکاح
  ہو چار آنہ نکاح کرنے والے سے آبادی مسجد کی امداد خرچ کیواسطے لئے جائین سالانہ یہ رقم اچہی ہوگئی**" — it
  was settled that on every nikah performed in the city four annas be taken from the person performing it

- **Congregation counted.** "عید کی نمازین **چہہ سات ہزار نمازی** جمع ہونے لگے" — six or seven thousand

  nazul; "مگر **میجر میگریگر صاحب بہادر ڈپٹی کمشنر لاہور** نے بکمال دریا دلی **سنہ ۱۸۵۰ء[OCR?]** مین یہ

  released also a baithak and two shops that did not belong to the mosque (p. 143, folio 146).
  **A dated act of restitution by a named officer, including property beyond the mosque's own title.**
- **Rent as an index of a bazaar's decline.** "جب قلعہ آباد تہا تو اُسطرف کے بازار کی ایکا ایک دوکان
  وبیٹہک مین **بیس روپیہ ماہوار کرایہ** پاتی تہین **اب خالی پڑی ہین**" — when the fort was inhabited each

  folio 146). The mosque has no rawnaq because its bazaar has none.
- **An annual mela with sama and wajd at a Qadiri-Shattari mazar.** "**ہر سال میلا بہی ہوتا ہے صوفی لوگ
  جمع ہوکر سماع سنتے ہین اور وجد کرتے ہین**" — every year a mela is held; Sufi people gather, listen to
  sama and go into ecstasy (p. 144, folio 147). **The only urs/mela at a Muslim shrine in this chunk, and
  it is reported with its music and its ecstatic practice intact.** Held at the khanqah of Shah Zia Qadri
  inside Taksali Darwaza, the pir of the archive's Shah Inayat Allah.
- **A tomb's garden absorbed into a Sikh raja's haveli, and its land now a Tehsil office.** "**نواب سعد
  اللہ خان وزیر** نے یہ مقبرہ بنوایا اور چاروں طرف مقبرہ کے ایک عالیشان باغ تجویز کیا … **راجہ سوچیت
  سنگہ** نے جبکہ یہ حویلی … تعمیر کی تو **مقبرہ کی سب زمین اپنی حویلی مین ودخل کر لی** جس سے ایک مقبرہ بے
  رونق ہو گیا ہے **غنیمت ہے کہ گرنے سے بچ گیا**" — Nawab Saad Allah Khan the wazir had the tomb built and
  planned a lofty garden on all four sides; when Raja Suchet Singh built the haveli he took all the tomb's
  land into it, leaving the tomb without rawnaq; it is a mercy it escaped falling. Only the tomb and a
  well survive, and the well "اب متعلق مکان تحصیل ہے", now belongs to the Tehsil building (pp. 144–145,

- **Shops built under a mosque as its endowment.** At the Sunehri Masjid, "مسجد کی نشستی[OCR?] عمارت …
  اونچی کرسی بقدر ایک منزل ودیکر بنائی گئی ہے مین[OCR?] **ہر طرف نیچے دوکانین بنائی گئی ہین کہ اُنکے
  کرایہ کی آمدنی مسجد کے اخراجات لائدیہ[OCR?] مین صرف ہو**" — the prayer building was raised on a plinth
  a storey high and shops built below on every side so that their rental income be spent on the mosque's
  expenses (p. 145, folio 148). And the arrangement held through the Sikh period: "مسجد کا **متولی** جو
  روپیہ دوکانو کے کرایہ کا پاتا رہا اسکی آبادی اور **اپنے گزارہ** مین خرچ کرتا رہا" — the mutawalli who
  received the shops' rent kept spending it on the mosque's upkeep and his own livelihood (p. 147,
  folio 150). **A purpose-built commercial endowment, and a custodian paid from it.**
- **Two fatwas on mosque land, one permissive and one prohibitive.** The founder asked the governor for
  the chowk's land in Kashmiri Bazaar; the governor "بذات خود حکم نہ دے سکا اور **علماء دین سے فتوی[OCR?]
  مانگا** اُنہونے اجازت دی کہ **زمین خدا کا ملک ہے خانہ خدا کی تعمیر مین جسقدر اُسمین سے دیجائے نہایت
  مناسب** … اسکے دینے مین کسی طرح کا ہرج[OCR?] واقع نہوتا ہے" — he could not order it himself and asked a
  fatwa of the ulama, who permitted it: the land is God's property, and whatever of it is given for
  building God's house is entirely proper (p. 146, folio 149). But on absorbing the small older mosque
  that stood in the chowk, "**علما نے اسمین اجازت نہ دی اور لکہا کہ ایک مسجدگاہ کی جگہ جہان لوگ نماز پڑہہ
  چکے ہون ودوسری مسجد کا زینہ ودروازہ وغسلخانہ بنانا جائز نہین ہے**" — the ulama refused, writing that it
  is not permissible to build a second mosque's stair, gate and bathhouse on the site of a masjid-gah
  where people have already prayed; the Nawab, helpless, gave the mosque a two-sided road and built stairs
  on both sides, "جو نہایت ناموزون تہا", which was most ill-proportioned (p. 146, folio 149). **A
  jurisprudential constraint on urban form, and its architectural consequence.**
- **The colonial officer does what the fatwa forbade.** "**کپتان نسبٹ صاحب بہادر ڈپٹی کمشنر لاہور** نے وہ
  پرانی خورد مسجد جو غیر آباد پڑی تہی **گروا کر** شرقی ودروازہ مسجد کا بنوا دیا وزینہ بہی شرقی بہی تجویز
  ہو گیا … وہ دو طرفہ قدیم زینہ گرا کر **زمین مسجد مین ایزاد کر دی**" — the DC had the old small mosque,

  two-sided stair and added the land into the mosque (pp. 146–147, folios 149–150). The author frames it
  as fulfilment: "اب نواب مرحوم کی مراد بر آئی اور روح خوش ہوئی", the late Nawab's wish is now fulfilled
  and his soul pleased (p. 146, folio 149).
- **An Akali seizure of the Sunehri Masjid: the azan as the stated grievance, and the confiscation of its
  rents.** When "باولی کا مکان مسجد کے متصل" was built and "اسمین گرنتہہ صاحب رکہا گیا", the Granth
  Sahib placed in it, "**باولی کے بہائی سکنہہ اور اکالیہ** اس مسجد کے در پے ہو گئی" — the Bhais of the
  Baoli, the Saknah[?] and the Akalis went after the mosque, petitioning the Maharaja that "**اس مسجد کا
  ملا ان بآواز بلند اذان دیتا ہے تو ہمارے کان مین پڑتی ہے** یہ مسجد بہی باولی کے ساتہہ شامل ہمارے قبضہ

  mosque's mulla gives the azan in a loud voice and it falls in our ears; this mosque too should stay in
  our possession along with the Baoli, or be demolished, for Muslims' neighbourhood with the Guru's Sikhs
  is not desirable. "مہاراجہ نے فی الفور حکم دیدیا کہ مسجد سے ملا ان نکالدیا جائے اور گرنتہہ رکہا جائے …
  اکالیون نے قبضہ کر لیا اور تمام مسجد مین گورو کا بیسین ودیکر گرنتہہ رکہہ دیا گیا **ودوکانون کی آمدنی
  ضبط ہوکر باولی کے محال کے شامل کر دی گئی**" — the Maharaja ordered at once that the mulla be expelled
  and the Granth placed; the Akalis took possession, the Guru's seat was set in the whole mosque and the
  Granth placed, and the shops' income was confiscated and merged into the Baoli's **محال** (estate)
  (p. 147, folio 150). **Two institutions, one property: the endowment is transferred with the building.**
- **The community's recourse is intercession, not petition.** "شہر کے مسلمان نہایت غمگین ہوئے اور سبنے
  مجمع **فقیر عزیزالدین ونورالدین** کے مکان پر گیا اور چاہا کہ اُنکے وذریعہ سے مہاراجہ کے حضور مین **مسجد
  کی واگزاری** کے لئے عرض کی جائے" — the city's Muslims, much grieved, went in a body to the house of
  Faqir Aziz al-Din and Nur al-Din so that through them a petition for the mosque's release be made before
  the Maharaja (p. 147, folio 150). **The chunk breaks off mid-sentence here**; the outcome is in the next
  chunk.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Chhajju Bhagat makes the Ganga flow in a tank in his own house, so that his servant need not travel.**
  Reported as what the visitors say — "**کہتے ہین کہ یہ چو بچہ چہجو بہگت کے وقت کا ہے اور چہجو بہگت نے
  تلا سمین[OCR?] سے گنگا جاری کی تہی**": an old woman (پیرزالہ) used to serve him; when the mela of
  Masia Kahi[OCR?] (مسیا کہی[OCR?] کا میلہ) drew near she asked leave to go to the Ganga; "بہگت جی نے کہا
  کہ وہان[OCR?] جاکر تو گنگا کا اشنان کریگی **ہم بہی جسکو اسی جگہ گنگا اشنان کرا دیتے ہین**", Bhagat Ji
  said, you will go there and bathe in the Ganga; we too will have [you] bathe in the Ganga right here;
  whereupon from that tank "پانی نے[OCR?] جوش مارا اور اُس عورت نے بخوبی اشنان کر لیا **وہ متبرک چو بچہ
  اب تک رکہا ہے**", the water surged, the woman bathed fully, and that blessed tank is kept to this day
  (p. 135, folio 138). **A miracle that substitutes a local tank for a pilgrimage, and the tank is still
  the object of the practice.**
- **The water of both tanks is drunk and applied to the eyes.** Of the smaller tank, "اسمین صاف پانی
  بہار رہتا ہے اسمین لوگ آکر منہہ دہوتے ہین اور **نہایت اعتقاد سے اس پانی کو پی بہی لیتے ہین**"; of the
  larger, "**بکمال اعتقاد لوگ اسکو پیتے اور آنکہون پر لگاتے ہین**" — with utmost faith people drink it
  and apply it to their eyes (p. 135, folio 138).
- **Sadhu the sweeper-thief, who repays a shopkeeper with stolen goods and then dies protecting the
  receiver.** The author marks the story's status: it is "**وجہ تسمیہ** یہ جو اسطرح پر **زبان زد خلق** ہے",
  the reason for the name, as current on the people's tongues (p. 136, folio 139). In "عملداری اسلامیہ",
  the Islamic rule, Sadhu took pakoris from the shop by force daily until the shopkeeper closed it; Sadhu
  bade him keep it open one night, robbed a rich man's house and handed the whole booty over — "مینے تیرا
  جسقدر کہایا اوسکا حق ادا کر دیا", I have paid the due of all I ate from you; arrested next morning by
  the "بادشاہی ملازم", he denied it, and "حاکم وقت" warned "اگر مال نہ دوگے[OCR?] تو بہی جان نہ پاوگے",
  then in fury "سادہو کی پہانسی کا حکم صادر کر دیا", issued the order for his hanging, with a منادی
  through the city; at the gallows Sadhu, "تہا ور", called out that whoever held his stolen goods should
  not reveal it, "کیونکہ اگر وہ مال اُٹہین دیدیگا تو **میرا بہی نہین بچہو[OCR?]** [illegible]" (pp. 136–137,
  folios 139–140). **The page is heavily damaged and the trial's end is unrecoverable; see Doubts.** The
  well and its name are the surviving monument (p. 138, folio 141).
- **A punishment story is absent.** No karamat is attributed to any Muslim saint in this chunk, and the
  Sikh-era seizures of the Badshahi and Sunehri mosques are told without any retribution following
  (pp. 140–141, 147; folios 143–144, 150) — unlike the sibling volume's pattern.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author states his classification and admits its two-part structure**, dividing the Muslim
  buildings into "قدیم یعنی پُرانے زمانے کے مکان" and those "جو اب تعمیر ہوئے یا عہد سکہی مین بن چکے ہین",
  ancient and recent/Sikh-era, and taking the ancient first (p. 138, folio 141). The chunk's Muslim
  entries are therefore all from the "ancient" list, which is why every one of them is imperial or
  pre-colonial in foundation.
- **A comparative claim about scale, made flatly**: "**جامع مسجد دہلی سے یہ مسجد بڑی ہے**" — this mosque
  is larger than the Jami Masjid of Delhi (p. 139, folio 142), and "یہ مسجد بہت بڑی مسمی[OCR?] ہے صحن
  نہایت چوڑا اور وسیع ہے".
- **He records what he cannot establish rather than filling it.** Of the Bhati Darwaza mosque: "بسبب
  قدامت کے **معلوم نہین ہوتا کہ کسنے بانی نے اسکی بنیاد رکہی تہی**" (p. 145, folio 148). Of the Masti
  Darwaza mosque's inscription: "**قطعہ تاریخ پورا پورا پڑہا نہین جاتا**", the chronogram cannot be read
  in full (p. 142, folio 145). Of the Sabz Gumbad's occupant, the claim is attributed rather than
  asserted: "**سادات[OCR?] بخاری کہتے ہین** کہ ہمارے شجرے کے بزرگون مین سے یہ بزرگ تہا" (p. 145,
  folio 148).
- **He addresses the administration directly, in his own voice, as an engineer.** "غرض سرکار [illegible]
  **اگر ایک مرتبہ پہر توجہ کریگی** اور **محمد برکت علیخان بہادر** اس کار خیر مین کمر بستہ ہو ینگے **تو
  پوری پوری مرمت مسجد کی ہو جائیگی**" — in short, if the Sarkar turns its attention once more and Muhammad
  Barkat Ali Khan girds himself in this good work, the mosque's repair will be complete (p. 142,
  folio 145). The Badshahi entry is written as a repair brief, itemising the outstanding works, and closes
  "اورنگ زیب عالمگیر بادشاہ کی یادگار **تا وقیامت باقی رہیگی**" (p. 142, folio 145).
- **His evaluations of custodians and clergy are explicit and unequal.** Pandit Bishan Das is
  "عالی ودماغ" who "لوگون سے کلام بہی کم کرتا ہے" (p. 133, folio 136); the Sunehri Masjid's founder is
  "نہایت دیندار سخی فقیر ودوست ناظم عالم فاضل" (p. 146, folio 149); Fidai Khan Koka built "کمال جان
  فشانی" (p. 139, folio 142); the expelled mulla of the Sunehri Masjid is "**ملا ان بیچارہ**", the poor
  mulla (p. 147, folio 150) — the chunk's one open editorial sympathy in a dispossession narrative.
- **Colonial and Sikh agency are reported in the same register but with different verbs.** The English
  demolish cells "کسی مصلحت کیوقت" (at some time of expediency), cut windows and build verandahs for their
  own troops (pp. 140–141, folios 143–144), and also convene darbars, subscribe from their own pockets and
  restore mosques by name and date (pp. 142–143, 146; folios 145–146, 149); the Sikh regime confiscates,
  quarters artillery, prises up flooring and transfers endowments (pp. 140, 143, 147; folios 143, 146,
  150). The author draws no general conclusion from the contrast.

## Doubts

- **The archive also carries a second "Shah Inayat", and the mapping above is a judgement.**
  `dargah-roza-sufi-shah-inayat-shaheed` is Sufi Shah Inayat Shaheed of Jhok Sharif, Sindh, d. 1718;
  `shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri` is Shah Inayat Qadiri of Mozang, Lahore, silsila "Qadri Shattari". The
  chunk's "شاہ عنایت اللہ" is given **no place and no date** (p. 144, folio 147) — the only thing tying
  him to a row is his pir's printed order, "خاندان[OCR?] شطاریہ[OCR?] قادریہ[OCR?]", which matches the
  Lahore row's silsila and not the Sindh row's blank. **A human must confirm before this enters an
  entry**, and all three words of the silsila are OCR-flagged.
- **The sentence that would link Shah Inayat Allah, Kasur and Bullhe[OCR?] Shah is broken.** As printed:
  "چنانچہ یہ شاہ عنایت اللہ اسی کا مرید تہا بلکہ **[illegible]** قصور کی ایک **[illegible]** مشہور دلی
  ہوا ہے پنجابی زبان مین بلہے[OCR?] شاہ کی کافیان … گاتے ہین **[illegible]** ایک زنانہ **[illegible]**
  بزرگی کا قایل ہے" (p. 144, folio 147). Two or three gaps fall exactly where the relationship between the
  three names would be stated. **Do not reconstruct it.** The name بلہے شاہ is itself OCR-flagged, and
  "ایک زنانہ [illegible] بزرگی کا قایل ہے" cannot be construed from what survives.
- **Whose enclosure holds Mauza Mian Mir.** The text reads "پہر ملا شاہ قادری اپنے مرشد کا روضہ بنوایا
  **جسکے حاطہ مین** اب موضع میانمیر ایک گاؤن آبا دہی" (p. 139, folio 142) — grammatically the village is
  in **Mulla Shah Qadri's** enclosure, though it bears Mian Mir's name. The archive's
  `shrine-of-mian-mir` row locates the shrine at Alam Ganj (Dharampura); this chunk gives no quarter for
  either rauza. Also note the relation term: Mian Mir is Dara Shikoh's "**دادا مرشد**", his pir's pir, and
  Mulla Shah his own — do not flatten the two.
