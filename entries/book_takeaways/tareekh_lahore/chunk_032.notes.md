# tareekh_lahore — chunk 032 — pp. 457–463

**The last chunk of the book: two and a half Hindu temple entries inside the walled city, then the
author's own closing matter.** The chunk opens mid-entry on a Mata temple in a mohalla called
کوچہ بال ناتا (see Doubts — the site is unnamed at the opening), then gives **شوالہ سکہہ دیال سود**
(the Shiwala of Sukh Dayal Sud) and **ٹہاکردوارہ لالہ بہگوان داس خلف دیوان زین چند [OCR?] صاحب
مرحوم** (the Thakurdwara of Lala Bhagwan Das, son of the late Diwan Zain Chand[OCR?]), and closes
with **خاتمہ** (the conclusion, pp. 462), **تمت** (finis), and on p. 463 a **خاتمۃ الطبع**
(conclusion of the printing) carrying three chronogram pieces. **No archive row is matched in this
chunk**; its value to the project is (a) an origin legend of a temple image bought from a Turkish
soldier, (b) a fully endowed new temple with fourteen waqf shops, and (c) the author's own statement
of how the book's religious material was gathered and who helped him gather it.

**Folio markers are printed unevenly here and the printed series is broken.** The chunk prints
folio 464 at p. 457, folio 465 at p. 458, folio 466 at p. 459, folio 467 at p. 460, **no folio marker
at all at p. 461**, and folio 469 at p. 462; p. 463 carries the marker `[colophon]` instead of a
folio. Folio 468 is never printed. Nothing below is extrapolated: a page reference carries a folio
only where the chunk prints one for that page.

- **شوالہ سکہہ دیال سود, the Shiwala of Sukh Dayal Sud** (pp. 458–459, folios 465–466):
  - **Location as printed.** "یہ شوالہ سرد یوی کے [OCR?] کوچہ میں جو ما بین دروازہ شاہ عالمی و موچی

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Eight vigils a month on a fixed weekday-and-lunar calendar.** At the Mata temple: "ایک ماہ میں
  آٹہہ بار اس مندر میں جاگا ہوتا ہے تمام رات بہگت بیدار رہکر دیوی کی بہیشین [OCR?] گاتے ہیں" — a
  **jaga (جاگا, night vigil) is held eight times in a month**, the bhagats staying awake all night
  singing the Devi's bhajans[OCR?]; "ایک ہر اتوار کے روز [OCR?] دوسرا ہر منگل کے روز جاگا برپا ہوتا
  ہے" — one **every Sunday[OCR?]**, a second **every Tuesday**; "پہر ایک پنچمی کے روز بہی جاگا ہوتا
  ہے اور کڑاہ [OCR?] پرشاد تقسیم ہوتا ہے" — then a jaga on the **panchami (پنچمی)** day as well, at
  which **karah[OCR?] prashad (کڑاہ [OCR?] پرشاد) is distributed**, followed by a flagged phrase about
  flowers ("پہول تقسہ [OCR?]") (p. 457, folio 464).
- **A temple image bought for cash from a soldier.** The mohalla folk "بہت اسی منت کی روپیہ بہی دینا
  کیا" — pleaded much and also undertook to pay money; the soldier would not consent; "آخر کار تیسرے
  [OCR?] روز بہت سا روپیہ لیکر مسلمان نے دیوی جی کی مورت ہندوؤں کو دیدی اور اُنہون نے آکر اس حویلی
  میں رکہدی" — at last on the **third[OCR?] day** the Muslim **took a large sum of money (بہت سا
  روپیہ; no figure stated)** and gave the image of Devi ji to the Hindus, who came and placed it in
  **this haveli**; "اُس روز سے یہ دیوی اسی جگہ پر رکہی [OCR?] ہے اور عام [OCR?] پرستش جاری ہے", from
  that day the Devi has been kept in this very place and public[OCR?] worship has continued (p. 458,
  folio 465). The transaction is the temple's own founding act as the chunk tells it.
- **A domestic house partitioned into a temple, with the founder's family retained as caretakers.**
  Sukh Dayal cut a portion out of his own house to make the shiwala; the pujari's dalan is the
  mandir's below and the founder's family's above; after Sukh Dayal's death his son Amar Nath both
  keeps the building and serves the pujari (pp. 458–459, folios 465–466). The chunk names no
  endowment, no land and no offering income for this shiwala (not stated).
- **A sarai, a tank and a market built by the founder's father — cross-referenced, not repeated.**
  "اُنہون نے بکمال ہمت بیرون دروازہ شاہ عالمی کے باہر سرائے عالیشان تعمیر کی اور تالاب بنوایا جس سے
  عام و خاص کو فیض پہنچتا ہے اور دوکانیں بہی سرائے کے دو طرف تعمیر کیں جنمیں گہی تیل نمک وغیرہ کی
  منڈی لگتی ہے" — with great resolve he built a **grand sarai outside the Shah Alami gate (بیرون
  دروازہ شاہ عالمی)** and had a **tank** made, from which high and low benefit, and built **shops on
  two sides of the sarai in which a mandi of ghee, oil, salt and the like is held**; "اس سرائے اور
  تالاب کا ذکر پہلے درج کتاب ہو چکا ہے اسواسطے اب دوبارہ لکہنے کی ضرورت نہیں ہے" — the sarai and the
  tank have **already been entered earlier in the book**, so there is no need to write them again
  (p. 459, folio 466).
- **Shop rents assigned to the temple, and fourteen shops made waqf in its name.** "اس مندر کا کل خرچ
  جو یہ ذمہ [OCR?] بانی ہے اور جو دوکانات مندر کی منزل زیریں میں بنائی [OCR?] ہیں اُنکا کرایہ بہی سب
  مندر کو ملتا ہے" — the mandir's entire expense is the founder's charge[OCR?], and the **rent of the
  shops built in the mandir's lower storey all goes to the mandir**; "علاوہ اسکے چودہ دوکانات جو

  shops (چودہ دوکانات)** built to the **east of the road[OCR?]** are likewise **endowed (وقف) in the

- **An Ekadashi assembly with qawwals singing a monotheist rag in a Thakurdwara.** "ہر ایک اکادشی کے
  روز یہان بڑا اجتماع ہوتا ہے اور قوال حاضر ہوکر راگ موحدانہ گاکر سامعین کو خوش کرتے ہیں" — on
  **every Ekadashi (اکادشی)** day a great assembly gathers here, and **qawwals (قوال)** attend and
  delight the listeners by singing **راگ موحدانہ**, a monotheist rag (p. 462, folio 469).

  Tarikh-e-Lahore has been printed most excellently and luminously — and "کلہی [OCR?] سرور نے اُس

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A Turkish soldier's wish-granting image, and how the mohalla got it — told as oral testimony.**
  "اس ماتا کے لاہور میں آنیکا [OCR?] احوال [OCR?] بہ زبانی اہل محلہ و جوندا بہگت کے سنا گیا ہے" — the
  account of this Mata's coming to Lahore was **heard from the mouths of the mohalla's people and
  Junda Bhagat**: "عہد سلطنت لودہی [OCR?] میں ایک لشکر مسلمانان ترکوں کا ہندوستان سے کابل کو جانے کو
  لئے لاہور میں آ اُترا" — in the **era of the Lodhi[OCR?] sultanate (عہد سلطنت لودہی [OCR?])** an
  army of **Muslim Turks** going from Hindustan to **Kabul** halted at Lahore. Lahore's brokers
  (دلال [OCR?]) and sellers, one trade flagged (کہمیار فروش [OCR?]), took goods to the army and saw
  that a **Muslim soldier** took this figure out of a ٹہایہ [OCR?], "غسل دیکر اور پوشاک پہنا کر پہر
  پٹارے میں رکہ دیا", **bathed it, dressed it in clothes and put it back in a basket (پٹارا)**. Asked
  about it, he said "ہندوستان میں ایک مندر ترکوں نے گرا دیا تہا وہان سے مجہکو یہ مورتی ملی" — **the
  Turks had demolished a mandir in Hindustan** and he got this image from there (p. 457, folio 464);
  he carried it away with his own belief[OCR?] ("اپنے ولی [OCR?] بعقیدے [OCR?] کے ساتہہ"), and "اب
  میرے دل کی جو آرزو ہوتی ہے اسمیں سے مانگ لیتا ہوں مجہکو مل جاتی ہے اسواسطے میں اُسکی خدمت کرتا ہوں
  ہر روز نہلاتا ہوں بہوگ دیتا ہوں" — **whatever desire is in his heart he asks of it and obtains it**,
  and therefore he serves it, bathes it daily and **gives it bhog** (p. 458, folio 465). The purchase
  is under Practices.
- **The image's name supplied by the Muslim soldier.** "بال بائی [OCR?] ماتا نام اس دیوی کا اُسی
  مسلمان نے بتلایا تہا" — the name of this Devi, **بال بائی [OCR?] ماتا (Bal Bai[OCR?] Mata)**, had
  been **told by that same Muslim** (p. 458, folio 465). The chunk offers no other derivation of the
  name and does not connect it to the mohalla's name کوچہ بال ناتا (see Doubts).
- No karamat, miracle or legend at all is attached to the Sukh Dayal shiwala or to the Bhagwan Das
  Thakurdwara; both are described purely as fabric, family and endowment (pp. 458–462).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **A claim of priority for the book.** "اس سے پہلے کوئی ایسی کتاب اس قدیم شہر کے حالات میں تصنیف
  نہیں ہوئی تہی جو ہر ایک قسم کے حال کا مجموعہ ہو" — **before this, no such book had been composed on
  this ancient city's conditions that would be a compendium of every kind of account** (p. 462,
  folio 469). The claim is the author's own and carries no supporting survey of earlier works.
- **The book's own plan, stated by its author.** "اس تاریخ میں ہند و مسلمان قدیم و جدید مذہبی مکانات
  کا مفصل حال لکہا گیا ہے" — in this tarikh the detailed condition of **Hindu and Muslim religious
  buildings, ancient and modern**, has been written; "اور جن مکانات کا تعلق کسی مذہب سے نہ تہا اُنکی
  تشریح علیحدہ باب میں تحریر ہوئی ہے", buildings with **no connection to any religion** are described
  in a **separate chapter (باب)**; "اور سرکاری مکانات جو میری عمر سے [OCR?] اور کچہ [OCR?] گذرانہ
  [OCR?] میں تعمیر ہوئے اُنکا ذکر علیحدہ فصل میں ہے", **government buildings** built within his
  lifetime[OCR?] and some[OCR?] earlier[OCR?] are in a **separate section (فصل)** (p. 462, folio 469).
  This is the book's own three-way division of its material, useful for placing any entry in it.
- **The motive the author reports for writing.** His close friends and intimates instructed him — the
  clause is damaged where it describes his employment, "تم قیس تک [OCR?] پریس [OCR?] سرکاری کی طرف سے
  بہتر تعمیر سرکاری کے کام کر رہے ہو", you are doing **government construction work on the
  government's behalf** — that "تمہاری طرف سے ایک ایسی کتاب یادگار ہونی چاہئے جو سب سے عام و فہام
  [OCR?] نفع ہو سب اور اُسکے ذریعہ سے قیامت تک تمہارا نام زندہ رہے", a book should stand as a
  **memorial from him, of general benefit, through which his name would live until Qiyamat**; he
  obeyed and "اس کتاب کو بکمال مشقت و جان نثاری لکہکر ختم کیا", finished writing it with utmost toil
  and self-sacrifice (p. 462, folio 469). The book here presents itself as a public-benefit memorial
  produced by a man in government building service; the chunk states this as the friends' words and
  the author's own, and identifies him no further in this passage (see Doubts).
- **An entry openly declared to post-date the book.** "بوقت تحریر حالات سابق کے یہ مندر موجود نہ تہا
  اب بعد تحریر و طبع کتاب اسکی عمارت اسی سال میں ختم ہوئی اسواسطے اسکا حال یہی زیب اندراج پاتا ہے" —
  **at the time the earlier accounts were written this mandir did not exist; now, after the writing
  and printing of the book, its building was completed in this same year**, and therefore its account
  finds its place here (p. 459, folio 466). The author is explicit that the Bhagwan Das Thakurdwara
  entry is an addition later than the body of the work; see Doubts.
- **Cross-reference discipline.** Rather than repeat the sarai and tank he had already described, he
  writes "اس سرائے اور تالاب کا ذکر پہلے درج کتاب ہو چکا ہے اسواسطے اب دوبارہ لکہنے کی ضرورت نہیں ہے"
  (p. 459, folio 466); and he cuts short the pujaris' quarters with "جسکی تشریح سے بہت طوالت ہوتی ہے",
  describing which would run to great length (p. 461).
- **Testimony marked as testimony.** The Mata's origin is given as "بہ زبانی اہل محلہ و جوندا بہگت کے
  سنا گیا ہے", heard from the mohalla's people and the pujari (p. 457, folio 464), while the fabric,
  the shop rents and the waqf are stated flatly in his own voice (pp. 457–462) — the same division of
  register the book uses elsewhere.
- **Buildings judged aesthetically.** Of the Thakurdwara's upper court, "جسکے دیکہنے سے انسان کی
  طبیعت میں فرحت آجاتی ہے", the sight brings delight to the temperament (p. 460, folio 467); of the
  mandir, its excellence is "حد و حساب سے باہر", beyond limit and reckoning (p. 460, folio 467); of
  the northern dalan, "بےنظیر", unequalled (p. 461).
- **A closing theological frame.** The خاتمہ opens "خداوند لایزال بےہمتا و بےمثال کا کمال شکریہ ہے کہ
  یہ کتاب جامع احوال دارالسلطنت لاہور ختم ہوئی حقیقت میں یہ بہت بڑا کام تہا" — utmost thanks to the
  everlasting, peerless and matchless Lord that this book **comprehending the conditions of
  دارالسلطنت لاہور (the seat of government, Lahore)** is finished; in truth it was a very great work,
  accomplished only by infinite divine favours with order[OCR?] ("صرف تفضلات [OCR?] نامتناہی الٰہی با
  انضباط [OCR?] نبہیا یا [OCR?]"), followed by a Persian couplet, "اگر ہر موے من گردد زبانے | شود ز
  حق رائم [OCR?] بہر نیک دستان [OCR?]" (p. 462, folio 469). The section ends "خدا اسکو قبولیت کے
  رتبہ پر پہنچائے", may God bring it to the rank of acceptance, and then **تمت**, finis (p. 462,
  folio 469).

## Doubts

- **No archive id is matched in this chunk.** The three sites here are a Mata temple in a walled-city
  kucha, a Shiv shiwala between the Shah Alami and Mochi gates, and a Thakurdwara on the south bank
  of a tank outside the Shah Alami gate (pp. 457–462); none corresponds to a row in
  `shrine_index.tsv` on the evidence in this chunk. In particular the Thakurdwara's principal image is
  "سری کرشن جی مہاراج", Sri Krishan ji Maharaj (p. 461), and the archive does hold a Krishna temple in
  Lahore (`krishna-mandir-ravi-road`), but the chunk places this building at the Shah Alami tank, not
  on Ravi Road, so **no id has been assigned** — a human should decide whether the two are the same
  foundation.
- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the site is unnamed at the opening.** p. 457 begins "بہت پُرانا بہی
  ہے یہاں تک کہ…", in the middle of a sentence about a building's antiquity (p. 457, folio 464). The
  entry's heading fell in the previous chunk. Within this chunk the only names attached are the
  mohalla's, **کوچہ بال ناتا [OCR? on the whole phrase as printed]**, and the image's, **بال بائی
  [OCR?] ماتا** (pp. 457–458, folios 464–465); the two are not connected by the text, and the
  building's own name is not stated here. Do not name this site from context.
- **Folio series broken across this chunk.** Printed markers: 464 (p. 457), 465 (p. 458), 466
  (p. 459), 467 (p. 460), **none at p. 461**, 469 (p. 462), and `[colophon]` at p. 463. Folio 468 is
  never printed and has not been inferred; facts from p. 461 above carry the page reference only.
- **Material that post-dates the main manuscript.** The Thakurdwara of Lala Bhagwan Das is entered
  with the author's own statement that it did not exist when the earlier accounts were written and
  that its building was completed "بعد تحریر و طبع کتاب", **after the writing and printing of the
  book**, "اسی سال میں", in that same year (p. 459, folio 466). The year itself is not stated, and no
  edition is named, so this entry cannot be dated from the chunk; it should be treated as later than
  the body of the work, and possibly later than a first printing.
- **Two printing numerals, side by side, neither with an era.** The Persian chronogram carries
  "۱۸   ۸۴ [OCR?]" (printed with a gap between the digit pairs), and the Mufti Ghulam Sarwar
  chronogram carries "۱۳۰۱ [OCR?]" (p. 463). Both are OCR-flagged, neither is labelled ھ, ء or
  بکرمی, and the intervening Urdu piece speaks of a "ہندی سال" of printing without giving it. The
  three are left as printed and are not reconciled.
- **No press, place, edition or printing patron is named.** The page is headed "خاتمۃ الطبع" (p. 463)
  but names no press or printing house. The only occurrence of "پریس [OCR?]" in the chunk is inside a
  badly damaged clause in the خاتمہ about the author's government employment — "تم قیس تک [OCR?] پریس
  [OCR?] سرکاری کی طرف سے بہتر تعمیر سرکاری کے کام کر رہے ہو" (p. 462, folio 469) — where it cannot
  be read as the imprint of this book. Provenance of the edition therefore remains open.
- **The first chronogram's heading cannot be parsed as printed.** "قطعہ تاریخ از تاریخ طبع شاعر شیریں
  مقال رسا [OCR?] بہاور [OCR?] کنہیا لال صاحب ایکسٹرا [OCR?] کنٹو [OCR?] لاہور بحضور مصنف کتاب"
  (p. 463) contains a flagged poetic name (رسا[OCR?] بہاور[OCR?]), the name **کنہیا لال صاحب**, a
  flagged office (ایکسٹرا[OCR?] کنٹو[OCR?]), Lahore, and the phrase **بحضور مصنف کتاب**, "to the
  presence of the author of the book". Whether the piece is **by** Kanhaiya Lal or **addressed to**
  him, and what the office title reads, cannot be determined from this line; the second chronogram is
  separately labelled "از مصنف", by the author (p. 463). The chunk does not otherwise identify the
  author by name or office, and no identification is asserted here.
- **The father's name is OCR-flagged and printed inconsistently.** The heading and text give "دیوان
  زین چند [OCR?]" (p. 459, folio 466) and then "دیوان [OCR?] زینہ [OCR?]" for the owner of the tank
  (p. 459, folio 466; p. 460, folio 467). Since the book states that this sarai and tank were
  "پہلے درج کتاب", already entered earlier in the book (p. 459, folio 466), the spelling should be
  checked against that earlier passage before the name is used.
- **Flags affecting facts above**, in order of consequence: the caretaker-pujari's name, "جوندا بہگت
  [OCR?]", and the weekday of one vigil, "ہر اتوار کے روز [OCR?]" (p. 457, folio 464); the dynasty in
  the origin story, "عہد سلطنت لودہی [OCR?]", and the trade of the informants, "کہمیار فروش [OCR?]",
  and the container, "ٹہایہ [OCR?]" (p. 457, folio 464); the day of sale, "تیسرے [OCR?] روز", and the
  image's name, "بال بائی [OCR?] ماتا" (p. 458, folio 465); the bull's name, "نندی گن [OCR?]"
  (p. 459, folio 466); the founder's liability clause, "جو یہ ذمہ [OCR?] بانی ہے", and the location of
  the fourteen shops, "سڑک [OCR?] سے بسمت شرق" (p. 462, folio 469); the second image in the middle
  niche, "انکا می [OCR?]", which is unrecoverable (p. 461); and in the خاتمہ the pandit's name,
  "پنڈتہ [OCR?] جنسی [OCR?]", the phrase for the helpers' assistance, "مددوی [OCR?]" and "کافی
  امدادی", and the closing "یہ نسخہ جسکر [OCR?] تیار ہو گیا" (p. 462, folio 469).
- **Provenance about the file.** p. 463 carries, at the foot of the page, "[image: partial circular
