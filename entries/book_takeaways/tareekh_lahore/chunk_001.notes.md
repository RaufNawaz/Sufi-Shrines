# tareekh_lahore — chunk 001 — pp. 1–14

**The book's front matter entire: title page, four pages of table of contents, the opening hamd, the
author's own preface (عرض حال مصنف), his plan of the book in four parts, his composition chronogram, and
the first two pages of Part One on the founding of the city** (pp. 1–14). No shrine is described in this
chunk. Its value is of three kinds: the **table of contents is a locator index** that gives the book's own
printed page number for a dozen archive shrines and for roughly a hundred further mazars, mosques,
samadhs, shivalas, havelis and British-era buildings (pp. 2–5); the preface states **who the author was,
what he had already written, why he was commissioned and how he classified religious buildings**
(pp. 7–12, folios 2–7); and the opening of Part One sets out **four incompatible traditions of the city's
foundation**, which the author reports without settling (pp. 13–14, folios 8–9).

**The folio markers restart inside this chunk, and the chunk must not be read as one series.** The
contents quire is foliated p. 3 → `[folio 2]`, p. 4 → `[folio 3]`, p. 5 → `[folio 4]`; the body then
starts again at p. 7 → `[folio 2]`, p. 8 → `[folio 3]`, p. 9 → `[folio 4]`, p. 10 → `[folio 5]`,
p. 11 → `[folio 6]`, p. 12 → `[folio 7]`, p. 13 → `[folio 8]`, p. 14 → `[folio 9]`. **Folios 2, 3 and 4

uses only the marker the chunk prints for that page. See Doubts.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

book's own printed page number and nothing else: no date, no location, no custodian. Each bullet records
the entry as printed so that a later chunk covering that printed page can be found; where the chunk says
nothing beyond the title, that is stated.

  - Contents, printed page **۲۲۰** (p. 4, folio 3), immediately after "مکان شہید گنج" at ۲۱۹ (p. 4,

- **loh-temple-lava-temple** — **the figure, not the temple.** The chunk's account of the city's founding

  mentioned anywhere in this chunk**, and the identification of لو[OCR?] with the archive row's principal
  figure rests on the name alone. See Doubts.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The book's classification of every building in Lahore by religion, stated as its organising
  principle.** Part Three, "تشریح اُن مکانات اندرونی و بیرونی شہر لاہور کی جو زمانہ سلف یا حال میں تعمیر
  ہوئے اور ابتک موجود ہیں" — a description of the buildings inside and outside the city of Lahore built
  in past or present times and still standing, "از قسم عمارات حویلی و باغچہ و مقبرہ و مسجد و مندر
  وغیرہ", of the kind of havelis, garden-houses, tombs, mosques, temples and so on — is divided into
  three qisms (p. 11, folio 6):
  - **پہلی قسم**: "میں ذکر اُن مکانات کا ہے جو ہندوؤں کے مذہب سے متعلق ہیں یعنی شوالہ و ٹھاکر دوارہ و
    دیوی دوارہ وغیرہ" — buildings belonging to the religion of the Hindus, that is shiwala, thakurdwara,
    devi-dwara and the like (p. 11, folio 6).
  - **دوسری**: "اُن مکانات کی جو ملت اسلام سے علاقہ رکھتے ہیں مثل مسجد و خانقاہ وغیرہ" — buildings
    connected with the millat of Islam, such as mosque and khanqah (p. 11, folio 6).
  - **تیسری قسم**: "اُن مکانات کی جو کسی مذہب و ملت سے علاقہ نہیں رکھتے مثل حویلی و باغ و کٹڑہ وغیرہ" —
    buildings connected with no religion or community, such as haveli, bagh and katra (p. 11, folio 6).
  - And each qism is subdivided into [illegible] fasls: "ایک میں شہر کے اندرونی مکانات کا ذکر ہے دوسرے
    میں بیرونی کا" — one for the buildings inside the city, the second for those outside (p. 11,
    folio 6). **This is why the shrines listed from printed page ۲۳۶[OCR?] onward are shrines the book
    places *outside* the city**, and why Sikh samadhs and dharamsalas appear in the contents inside the
    first, Hindu, qism rather than in a section of their own (pp. 3–4, folios 2–3).
- **A thirteen-fold scheme of the city's destructions.** The contents number the "صدمے" — shocks — of
  plunder and demolition from پہلا صدمہ at printed page ۱۷ to تیرہواں صدمہ at printed page ۳۱, followed
  by "صورت شہر موجودہ لاہور", the present form of the city of Lahore, at ۳۲ (p. 2); the ninth stands at
  an [illegible] page and the tenth at ۲۸[OCR?]. Part One is announced as covering "کس کس زمانہ میں غارت
  و انہدام کے صدمے پہنچتے رہے" — in which periods the shocks of plunder and demolition kept arriving
  (p. 11, folio 6; repeated p. 12, folio 7).
- **The author as the state's own builder of Lahore, and the book as a by-product of that office.** He
  writes that authorship was not his proper work and that he had no leisure from "سرکاری مفوضہ خدمات",
  the government-assigned services; that "بڑے بڑے اہم کام کارخانہ تعمیر میں جو گورنمنٹ عالیہ کے حکم سے
  میری تفویض رہے بخیر و خوبی انجام پاتے رہے" — the great and important works in the **building department**
  entrusted to him by order of the exalted Government were completed well; and that from the officials'
  discernment he received promotion after promotion and "خلعت[OCR?] و خطاب و انعام", a robe of
  honour[OCR?], a title and a reward (p. 7, folio 2). All his writing was therefore done at night: "یہ سب
  کام رات پر منحصر رکھا گیا اور چراغ کی روشنی اسکی ممد و معاون رہی" — all this work was confined to the
  night and the lamp's light was its helper (pp. 7–8, folios 2–3).
- **The commission, quoted in his friends' words, and the claim of authority it rests on.** They told him:
  "تیس برس سے افسر و سرپرست محکمہ [illegible] ہو مکانات قدیمہ و جدیدہ موجودہ شہر لاہور کا حال جیسا تم کو
  معلوم ہے کسی کو نہیں" — for **thirty years** you have been officer and superintendent of the
  [illegible] department, and no one knows the condition of the old and new buildings of the present city
  of Lahore as you do; "بڑی عمارتیں سرکاری جو فی الحال باعث زیب و زینت و فخر و اقتدار شہر لاہور میں سب
  تمہارے ہاتھ سے تعمیر ہوئی ہیں" — the great government buildings that are now the adornment, pride and
  authority of Lahore were all built by your hand; therefore write "ایک تاریخ خاص شہر لاہور کی", a history
  special to the city of Lahore, "تاکہ یہ تاریخ اور توالیف و تصانیف کی طرح تمہارے نام سے زمانہ [illegible]
  میں یادگار رہ کر رہے", so that this history too, like your other compositions, may remain a memorial in
  your name (p. 10, folio 5).
- **Government purchase of his previous history, in quantified copies — the clearest economic datum in the
  chunk.** Of تاریخ پنجاب: "دو بار طبع ہو چکی ہے پہلے چھاپہ میں جسکے چار سو پچاس[OCR?] جلدیں تو چند بار

  میں طلب ہوئی ہیں" — it has been printed twice; of the first printing **four hundred and fifty[OCR?]

  of every country might share in it (p. 10, folio 5).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author refuses to name the founder of Lahore and prints four traditions instead.** "اس شہر کے
  بانی کا نام بسبب گزر جانے مدت دراز کے بخوبی معلوم نہیں ہوتا اور نہ بخوبی ثابت ہوتا ہے کہ پہلے پہل اس
  نامور شہر کی بنیاد کس نے رکھی ہے کیونکہ کتب تواریخ میں مختلف روایتیں لکھی میں" — the name of this
  city's founder is not well known, owing to the long lapse of time, nor is it well established who first
  laid the foundation of this famous city, because differing traditions are written in the history books
  (p. 13, folio 8). He then sets out, in order and without adjudicating: (1) the **common report**,
  "عموماً مشہور ہے", that Ram Chandar Avatar's son Lo[OCR?] settled it as Lopur, which over "صدہا بلکہ
  ہزار ہا سال" — hundreds, indeed thousands of years — was corrupted into Lahore (pp. 13–14, folios 8–9);
  (2) the **author of خلاصۃ التواریخ**, who "اسی قول تصدیق کرتا ہے", confirms that same statement, and
  adds Kasho and Kasur (p. 14, folio 9); (3) **Shaikh Ahmad Zanjani**, who "صاحب خلاصۃ التواریخ کے قول کے
  برخلاف لکھتا ہے" — writes **contrary** to the statement of the author of Khulasat al-Tawarikh — and
  gives a raja of the Pandava line (p. 14, folio 9); and (4) the refoundation sequence of
  Bikramajit[OCR?], Samand Pal Jogi and Lohar Chand, with three successive names for the city (p. 14,
  folio 9). The one thing he commits to is the **lateness of the evidence**, not the founder.
- **He dates the name, not the city, and does it from two literary witnesses.** From Amir Khusrau's verse:
  "اس سے معلوم ہوا کہ آٹھویں صدی ہی [illegible] ہجری کی ابتدا میں جب امیر خسرو شاعر نامور دہلوی زندہ تھا
  تو نام اس شہر کا لاہور ہی تھا" — from this it is known that at the beginning of the **eighth
  century[illegible] Hijri**, when Amir Khusrau the famous Dehlvi poet was alive, the name of this city
  was Lahore (p. 13, folio 8); and the malfuzat فوائد الفوائد of Nizam-ud-Din Badayuni Dehlvi is adduced
  for the same (p. 13, folio 8). Against this he sets the earlier variety of forms in "پہلی تاریخ
  [illegible]", the earlier histories: "کہیں لہا اور کہیں لہانور اور کہیں لوپور اور کہیں لاہور" —
  somewhere **لہا** Laha, somewhere **لہانور** Lahanawar, somewhere **لوپور** Lopur, somewhere **لاہور**
  Lahore (p. 13, folio 8). The method is philological: a name attested in datable texts, and two proposed
  etymologies (Lopur → Lahore; Lohar Pur → Lahore) offered side by side without a choice between them
  (pp. 13–14, folios 8–9).
- **He invites correction, in terms that concede the book will contain errors.** "اگرچہ اس کتاب کے مندرجہ
  حالات کی تلاش و تحقیق میں بہت سی عرق ریزی و محنت مولف کی طرف سے وقوع میں آ کر یہ عجیب و غریب مجموعہ لکھا
  گیا ہے مگر عندالمطالعہ اگر مورخان ذی استعداد و منشیان [illegible] اسکے کسی تاریخی حال یا تحریر میں نقص
  پائیں تو [illegible] از راہ پردہ پوشی [illegible] اصلاح فرمائیں" — although much sweat and labour went
  into the search and investigation of the circumstances recorded in this book, yet if capable historians
  and munshis[illegible] on reading find a defect in any of its historical statements or writing, may
  they, by way of covering faults, correct it — followed by the Persian line "آدمی از سہو خطا پاک نیست /
  آب روان بے خس[OCR?] و خاشاک نیست", man is not free of slip and error, running water is not without
  straw and rubbish (p. 12, folio 7).
- **His stated evidentiary method for the buildings is autopsy plus search.** "و تلاش ضروری حال ہر ایک
  مکان کا لکھ کر یہ مسودہ بنایا تاریخ لاہور نام رکھا چار حصوں میں تقسیم کیا" — and having made the
  necessary search and written the condition of **every single building**, he made this draft, named it
  Tarikh-e Lahore and divided it into **four parts** (p. 11, folio 6). Read with the commission at p. 10
  (folio 5), the book's claim to authority is that its author built much of what it describes.
- **A universalist theology stated at the head of a book that will classify buildings by religion.** The
  opening hamd holds that God is "بہر جا و بہر ملک و شہر و دیار … وہ کردگار" worthy of worship in every
  place, country, city and land; "وہ ہر ایک صورت میں موجود ہے / وہ ہر ایک ملت کا مسجود ہے" — He is present
  in every form, He is the one prostrated to by **every millat**; and He leaves none empty-handed and
  turns His face from none (p. 6). Three pages later the same author divides the city's buildings into the
  Hindu, the Islamic and the confessionally neutral (p. 11, folio 6). Of his own earlier works he says
  that یادگار ہندی treats in four parts "اوتاروں[OCR?] کا حال", the avatars[OCR?], then the prophets,
  then the philosophers, then some good-natured kings (p. 8, folio 3), and that ہندی نامہ's "موحدانہ
  اشعار اور تصوفانہ مضامین", monotheist verses and Sufi themes, are read with great love by "مردان خدا
  پرست و خدا دوست", God-worshipping and God-loving men (p. 8, folio 3).
- **The author's own name and pen-name, as he gives them.** "بندۂ نیاز مآل [illegible] اہل کمال **رائے
  بہادر کنہیا لال المتخلص بہندی** خلف جنت مکان عرش آشیان لالہ [illegible] حال متوطن شہر لاہور" — the
  humble servant, [illegible] of the accomplished, **Rai Bahadur Kanhaiya Lal, whose pen-name is
  "Hindi"**, son of the late Lala [illegible], at present resident of the city of Lahore (p. 7, folio 2).
  The takhallus explains the titles of five of his eight books — گلزار ہندی, ہندی نامہ, یادگار ہندی,
  مناجات ہندی, اخلاق ہندی (pp. 8–9, folios 3–4) — and reappears in the chronogram line (p. 12, folio 7).
