# tareekh_lahore — chunk 003 — pp. 31–45

**No shrine, tomb, mosque-as-shrine, gurdwara or temple is the subject of any passage in this chunk.**
It closes the narrative of the Sikh occupation of Lahore and then opens the book's survey of the city
as it stood, under six printed section headings: **"صورت موجودہ شہر لاہور"**, the present state of the
city of Lahore (p. 33, folio 36); **"مدارس کا ذکر"**, account of the madrasas (p. 38, folio 41);
**"لاہور کے چھاپے خانے"**, the printing presses of Lahore (p. 40, folio 43); **"کتاب فروشی کی تجارت کا
ذکر"**, account of the book-selling trade (p. 42, folio 45); **"لاہور کی رعایا کا ذکر"**, account of the
people of Lahore (p. 43, folio 46); and **"لاہور کے اہل حرفہ و کسب کا ذکر"**, account of the artisans
and trades of Lahore (p. 45, folio 48). The value to this project is almost entirely
institutional and economic: municipal works, sanitation, a failed waterworks with a compensation
record, sarais and bazaars founded by named contractors, schools, presses, the book trade, the
communities of the city and a doctrinal list of Sufi orders.

**Folio markers in this chunk run uniformly folio = PDF index + 3**, p. 31 → folio 34 through
p. 45 → folio 48, every page marked. Only the printed markers are used below; the relation is not
extrapolated past this chunk.

## Practices, institutions, economy

### The Sikh occupation: ransom, mutilation, partition

- **A ransom demand enforced by sack, and a mutilation.** The Sikh armies surrounded Lahore and sent
  word to Kabuli Mal over the **butchers (قصابان [OCR?])**: "[illegible] جب نقد لاہور میں ہمیں دیدیو یا
  قتل کر دو ورنہ ہم شہر کو لوٹ لینگے" — hand [them] over to us in Lahore, or kill [them], otherwise we
  will plunder the city (the sentence is damaged; see Doubts). When the Sikhs broke the [illegible] gate
  and the city began to be looted, Kabuli Mal complied: "چند قصابوں کے ناک کان کاٹ کر شہر سے نکال دیا
  اور بڑی رقم نذرانہ کی دیکر سکھوں کو شہر سے نکالا" — he cut off the noses and ears of several butchers
  and expelled them from the city, and got the Sikhs out by paying a large sum as **nazrana** (p. 31,
  folio 34). A named communal grievance, a corporal punishment and a cash indemnity in one sentence.
- **The city divided in three and held under oath-breaking.** The citizens opened the gate to the three
  sardars only "بعد لینے قول و قسم اس بات کے کہ شہر لوٹا نہیں جائیگا بلکہ رعایا کی حفاظت ہوگی", after
  taking word and oath that the city would not be plundered but the people protected; the Sikhs "شہر
  میں جاتے ہی غارت شروع کی جبتک تھک نہ گئے باز نہ آئے", began plunder on entering and did not stop until
  they tired. **"شہر کو لوٹ کر تینوں نے شہر کے تین حصے کر لئے اور اپنے اپنے حصے میں حاکم بن بیٹھے"** —
  having plundered it, the three made three parts of the city and each sat as ruler over his own share;
  "مدت مدید تک یہ انتظام رہا", the arrangement lasted a long time, and the people remained "ان کی
  ظالمانہ حکومت کی بلا میں مبتلا", afflicted by their tyrannical rule (p. 31, folio 34).
- **A ratio of settlement for the city under the three sardars**: "ان کے وقت اندرونی شہر بھی ایک حصہ آباد
  تہا اور دو حصے ویران باہر کی آبادیاں تو جلکر خاکستر ہو چکی تھیں" — in their time even the inner city
  was one part settled and two parts desolate, while the outer settlements had burnt to ashes (p. 31,
  folio 34). The one quantified statement of the city's condition before Ranjit Singh.
- **Eight days of targeted plunder of a professional class, with the motive quoted.** On Sher Singh's
  entry the Sikhs looted the two-sided bazaar from the Delhi Gate to the fort and set fire to
  [illegible] بکتی [OCR?] in the **Chhatta [OCR?] Bazaar**, then "آٹھ روز تک سکھون نے منشیوں کو لوٹا اس
  عداوت سے کہ **یہ منشی دفتر میں نوکر ہو کر ہماری تنخواہ میں سے کاٹ قصور [OCR?] کرتے ہیں**" — for eight
  days they plundered the **munshis**, out of the enmity that these munshis, employed in the office, make
  cuts from our pay; "بہت سے مولوی [illegible] اس جرم میں غارت ہوئے کہ یہ لوگ منشیوں کو پڑھاتے ہیں",
  many maulvis were ruined on the charge that they teach the munshis, and the army went "کوچہ کوچہ",
  lane by lane, searching out the houses of munshis and maulvis (p. 32, folio 35). **A teaching class
  destroyed for its pupils' employment** — directly relevant to the madrasa section eight pages later.
- **Lane-closing as civil defence.** At the second disturbance "رعایا نے کوچہ بندیاں بند کر لیں جس سے شہر
  پر حسب دلخواہ فوج کا ہاتھ نہ پڑا" — the people shut the lane-barriers, so the army's hand did not fall
  on the city as it wished (p. 32, folio 35).
- **The garrison plunders its own treasury.** On the fort's fall and the killing of the Sandhanwalia
  sardars, the troops "اپنے مالک کے خزانہ پر ہاتھ صفا کئے", cleaned out their own master's treasury:
  "لاکھوں روپیے کا پشمینہ و جواہرات و ظروف نقرئی و طلائی و نقد و جنس", lakhs of rupees' worth of
  pashmina, jewels, silver and gold vessels, cash and goods (p. 33, folio 36); and the jewels, ashrafis
  and goods Hira Singh had loaded on elephants for Jammu were all taken (p. 33, folio 36).

### Brick-robbing of the ruins, and the making of the British city

- **Forty years of brick-robbing as an industry, and the order that stopped it.** Where the outer
  settlements had stood, "خشت [OCR?] فرد و شان [OCR?] شہر نے ان کی دیواروں کی بنیادوں کی اینٹیں نکال کر
  [illegible] بدستور چھوڑ دیے تھے"; "ہر چند [OCR?] چالیس سال تک بیرونی آبادیوں کی بنیادیں سکھی عہد میں
  کھودی گئیں اور [illegible] برس تک انگریزی عہد میں کھدتی رہیں مگر اینٹیں ختم نہ ہوئیں" — for forty
  years the foundations of the outer settlements were dug up in the Sikh period and for [illegible] years
  more under the English, yet the bricks did not run out. "آخر صاحبان انگریز نے بڑے بڑے [illegible] بند
  کرا کر حکم دیدیا کہ کوئی شخص زمین سے اینٹ نہ نکالے" — finally the English gentlemen ordered that no
  person extract brick from the ground (p. 33, folio 36). **A ban on quarrying the city's own ruins.**
- **Refugee labour enriched by it, and treasure found in the process.** The "آفت زدہ کشمیری" who came
  from Kashmir "لاہور میں آ کر اینٹوں کے کھودنے اور بیچنے میں مشغول ہو کے لکھ پتی بن گئے", became
  **lakhpatis** digging and selling bricks; "اکثر اینٹیں کھودنے کے وقت انکو دفینے بھی حاصل ہوتے رہے",
  and they often got **buried hoards (دفینے)** while digging (p. 33, folio 36).
- **Lahore made the capital, Delhi subordinated to it.** "انگریزی عہد میں یہی شہر دار الحکومت پنجاب کا
  قرار پایا بلکہ دہلی اس صوبہ کے ماتحت ہو گئی" — in the English period this city was fixed as the
  **dar al-hukumat** of the Punjab, and indeed **Delhi came under this province** (p. 33, folio 36).
- **The Anarkali cantonment and Sadr Bazaar Anarkali, measured.** The army's first cantonment was fixed
  "انار کلی کے مقام پر" with "بہت سی پختہ بارکین [OCR?] اور کوٹھیان"; a pukka bazaar arose "صدر بازار
  انار کلی کے نام سے"; **"یہ بازار طول میں لہاری دروازے سے لیکر [illegible] صاحب کے پرانے بازار تک قریب
  ایک میل کے ہو گا"** — about **one mile** from the Lohari Gate to the old bazaar of [illegible] Sahib;
  shops on the east and west sides with rooms above; "سرکاری مکانات عجائب گاہ وغیرہ اسی بازار کے اندر
  بنوالئے گئے", government buildings and the **museum (عجائب گاہ)** were built inside this bazaar;
  "لاکھون روپیے کا بیوپار ہوتا ہے", lakhs of rupees of trade is done; and although the cantonment "اٹھا
  [OCR?] ہے", has been removed, the bazaar keeps growing for its nearness to the city — **"گویا شہر کے
  باہر دوسرا شہر آباد ہو گیا ہے"**, as though a second city has been settled outside the city (p. 34,
  folio 37).
- **The Mian Mir [OCR?] cantonment.** Because the Anarkali maidan was not [illegible] for the government
  army, "بنظر و سعت زمین کے میانمیر [OCR?] کے میدان میں چھاؤنی کی بنا ڈالی گئی", the cantonment was
  founded in the **maidan of Mian Mir [OCR?]** with a view to extent of land; a grand bazaar, "صدر بازار
  میانمیر [OCR?]", was built there, with "بڑی بڑی عالیشان بارکین [OCR?]" for the troops and "لاکھون
  روپیے کی لاگت کی بیشمار کوٹھیان", countless kothis costing lakhs of rupees, for the sahibs; in a few
  years the whole tract became "غیرت خلد بریں", the envy of paradise (p. 34, folio 37). **The name is a
  toponym here and nothing more — see Doubts before mapping it.**
- **Suburban spread, doubled population, and the railway as its cause.** From Anarkali to the end of the
  Mian Mir [OCR?] cantonment, and around **Mauza Mozang [OCR?]**, **Garhi Shahu [OCR?]** and **Qila
  Gujjar Singh**, "اتنی کوٹھیان اور باغیچہ تعمیر ہوے ہین جو شمار کے اندازے سے باہر ہین", so many kothis
  and garden-plots have been built as to be beyond counting; "آبادی حال کی سابق آبادیون سے دو چند بڑھ
  گئی ہے", the present settlement has grown **twofold** over the former; "شہر کے باہر کوسون تک آبادی نظر
  آتی ہے", settlement is visible for **kos** outside the city. **"شہر لاہور کی زیادہ ترقی کا باعث نئی
  [OCR?] زمانا [OCR?] محکمہ ریل ہے"** — the cause of Lahore's greater growth is the railway department,
  and "فی الحقیقت ایسا عالیشان سٹیشن جیسا لاہور کا ہے کہین نہین بنایا گیا", in truth such a grand station
  as Lahore's has been built nowhere (p. 35, folio 38).

### Sarais, tanks and bazaars founded by named men

- **Muhammad Sultan's sarai outside the Delhi Gate, and the Landa [OCR?] Bazaar.** "لاہور کے دہلی
  دروازے کے باہر ہر دروازے سے زیادہ رونق و آبادی ہے جس کا بانی سابق محمد سلطان ٹھیکہ دار مرحوم تہا" —

  founder was the late Muhammad Sultan, contractor. "اس نے زر کثیر خرچ کر کے ایسی عالیشان و عمدہ و وسیع
  سرائے پختہ تعمیر کی جس میں ہزار ہا مسافر ملکون کے آئے ہوے ہر روز آرام پاتے ہین" — he spent much money
  on a grand, fine, spacious pukka sarai in which **thousands of travellers from many countries rest
  every day**. North of it he settled a new pukka bazaar, "لنڈا [OCR?] بازار", with pukka shops on both
  sides and a pukka [illegible] before each shop; the railway station adjoins sarai and bazaar, and
  "خرید فروخت کا بازار گرم رہتا ہے", the market of buying and selling stays hot (p. 35, folio 38).
- **Diwan Ratan Chand's sarai and tank outside the Shah Alami Gate, with a bathing practice.** "شاہ
  عالمی دروازے کے باہر دیوان رتن چند کی سرائے اور تالاب نہایت عمدہ موقع پر بنا ہے"; "[illegible] قسم کے
  اناج اور گھی اور تیل کی منڈی" is held there and "دور دور کے سوداگر و بیوپاری وہان آ کر قیام کرتے اور
  مال بیچتے ہین", merchants and traders from far off stay and sell there. **"تالاب ہر وقت پر آب رہتا ہے
  شہر سے غسل کرنے کے لئے ہزارون زن و مرد وہان آتے ہین"** — the tank stays full at all times and
  **thousands of women and men come there from the city to bathe** (pp. 35–36, folios 38–39). A named
  private foundation in daily religious-cum-sanitary use; the book attaches no shrine or festival to it.
- **Mela Ram's sarai outside the Bhati Gate, and why it failed.** "بھاٹی دروازے کے باہر ایک عالیشان
  سرائے اور عمدہ کوٹھی رئیس میلا رام ٹھیکہ دار نے بنوائی"; Mela Ram lives in the kothi himself, and in
  the sarai "لکڑی وغیرہ سامان پڑا رہتا ہے", timber and other stores lie. Travellers' traffic is not there
  "سبب اس کے کہ وہ سرائے سٹیشن ریلوے سے بہت دور اور شہر کے جنوب غربی گوشہ پر ہے", because that sarai is
  far from the railway station and on the south-western corner of the city (p. 36, folio 39). **The
  railway, not the gate, decides which foundation lives.**

### Sanitation, the waterworks, and compensation for land

- **The city's condition under the Sikhs, in detail.** "سکھون کے وقت شہر لاہور نہایت گندہ تہا": heaps of
  refuse (کوڑیون) and filth in every lane and quarter bazaar; "سینکڑون مردہ جانورون کی لاشین گلتی",
  hundreds of dead animals rotting; the bazaar drains ran down the middle, making much mud; "بازارون اور
  کوچون کی صفائی کبھی نہین ہوتی تھی نہ کوئی محکمہ صفائی مقرر تہا", the bazaars and lanes were never
  cleaned and **no sanitation department existed**, nor were the sewers (بد رویین [OCR?]) cleared; "تمام
  سال کی گند بلا بارش کا پانی بھا کر لیجاتا تہا", the whole year's filth was carried off by the rain;
  and the nobles' horses and elephants passing through the bazaars splashed the drains so that "لوگون کے
  کپڑے رنگین ہو جاتے تھے", people's clothes were coloured (p. 36, folio 39).
- **The British sanitary regime, with two dates and two named officers.** Filth cleared, mid-bazaar
  drains abolished, paving laid, and **"بازار کی زمین جو دوکانون کے چبوترون کے نیچے دبے ہوے تھے چھڑائے
  گئے"** — the bazaar land buried under the shops' **chabutras was reclaimed**; refuse removed; "محکمہ
  صفائی مقرر ہوا دو وقتہ بازار صاف ہو کر چھڑکاؤ ہونے لگا", a **sanitation department** was appointed and
  the bazaar cleaned and sprinkled **twice a day** (pp. 36–37, folios 39–40). "یہ درستی شہر کی پہلے پہل
  **۱۸۵۰ [OCR?] ع** میں بعہد نہار ان ہل [OCR?] صاحب اسسٹنٹ کمشنر کے ہوئی" — this setting-right of the
  city happened first in **1850 CE [OCR?]** in the time of [نہار ان ہل [OCR?]] Sahib, Assistant
  Commissioner; "پھر **۱۸۶۰ [OCR?] ع** میں کوچہ و بازارون کے فرش دیوان بیجا ناتھ [OCR?] تحصیلدار کی
  ماتحت درست کرائے گئے" — then in **1860 CE [OCR?]** the pavements of the lanes and bazaars were repaired
  under **Diwan Bija Nath [OCR?], tehsildar** (p. 37, folio 40). Later the brick pavements were torn up,
  gravel pounded on the roads, and pukka drains built on both sides (p. 37, folio 40).
- **A piped-water scheme, its engineering failure, and what it cost the city.** The well water of the
  city "رعایا کے لئے صحت بخش نہ تہا", was not healthy for the people, so the proposal was made "کہ صحت
  بخش عمدہ پانی شہر میں بذریعہ نلکے کے پہنچایا جائے", that wholesome water be brought into the city by
  pipe. Wells were dug in the **Parade maidan (پریڈ کے میدان)**; a grand roofed pukka tank was built in
  the bazaar [illegible] mandi; and "شہر کے تمام بازار ایک سمت سے سیدھے کر کے زمین کے اندر نلکے آہنی
  ڈالے گئے", all the city's bazaars were straightened from one side and **iron pipes** laid underground.
  On filling, **"سبب اس کے کہ تالاب کی بنیاد میں کنکریٹ بہت ہی کم کٹوایا گیا تہا پانی فی الفور بنیادون
  میں گھس گیا اور تالاب [OCR?] کی سنگیین [OCR?] و مضبوط عمارت ایک گھنٹہ میں پاش پاش ہو گئی"** — because
  far too little concrete had been beaten into the tank's foundation the water at once soaked in and the
  stone and solid structure was smashed to pieces **in one hour**, and "زر کثیر جو تالاب کی عمارت پہ خرچ
  ہوا تہا ضایع ہو گیا", the great sum spent was wasted; "اسی روز سے تالاب کا اجرا موقوف ہوا", from that
  day the tank's working was suspended (p. 37, folio 40). Houses fell in two ways: rain sinking into
  unrammed trenches, and water escaping from the buried iron pipes into house foundations (pp. 37–38,
  folios 40–41).
- **Compensation as the book records it — the fullest statement of the state and property in this
  chunk.** "اگرچہ نقصان شہر کا بہت سا ہوا مگر **سرکار نے براہ معدلت معاوضہ ہر ایک نقصان کا رعایا کو
  پورا پورا دیا**" — though the city's loss was great, the government out of justice paid the people full
  compensation for every loss; "پہلے جس قدر زمین لوگو کی مکانات سے لی گئی تو معاوضہ زمین اور عمارات کا
  حسب دلخواہ رعایا کو دیا گیا **جس سے کوئی ناراض نہوا**", for land taken from people's houses
  compensation for land and buildings was given as they wished, so that no one was displeased; and the
  flood and rain damage too "اس نقصان کا عوض بھی دام دام ادا کیا", was paid **coin for coin** (p. 38,
  folio 41). The author is describing compulsory acquisition inside the walled city and asserting that
  it gave no grievance.
- **The tank rebuilt, and the site cleared.** "اب گورنمنٹ سے حکم ہو گیا ہے کہ تالاب دوبارہ تعمیر ہوا اس
  استحکام کے ساتھ کہ کبھی پانی کا صدمہ اس کو نہ پہنچے چنانچہ بن رہا ہے" — an order has now come from
  Government that the tank be rebuilt so solidly that water never damages it, and accordingly it is being
  built. The site "سابق گنجان آبادی شہر لاہور کی تھی", was formerly a dense quarter of Lahore; the
  government chose it "اس سبب اس کے کہ یہ جگہ تمام شہر کی زمین سے بلند تھی", because it stood higher than
  all the city's land, and "[illegible] مکانات کو پوری پوری قیمت دیکر مکانات کروا دیئے اور صاف میدان بنا
  کر تالاب کی عمارت شروع کی", paid full price for the houses, had them [cleared], made a clean maidan and
  began building (p. 38, folio 41).

### Schools, before and after

- **No state school under the Sikhs; four mosque and faqir-khana madrasas instead.** "سکھون کے عہد میں
  کوئی سرکاری مدرسہ شہر لاہور میں جاری نہ تہا صرف علما لوگون کے مدارس جاری تھے" — under the Sikhs no
  government madrasa ran in Lahore, only the ulama's. The four great ones: Khalifa Ghulam Rasul and
  Ghulam al-Madad [OCR?] in "[illegible] طوالف [OCR?] کی مسجد", "جس میں ہزارون ہند و مسلمان تعلیم پاتے
  تھے", where **thousands of Hindus and Muslims** were taught; Maulvi Jan Muhammad in the Masjid Nur
  Iman Wali; a third in "مسجد خیر [illegible]"; the fourth in the faqir-khana [OCR?] of Faqir Aziz al-Din
  and Nur al-Din. "ان کے علاوہ اور بھی طالبان [illegible] ہر ایک مسجد میں لڑکون کو پڑھاتے تھے", besides
  these, others taught boys **in every mosque** (p. 38, folio 41).
- **The curriculum named**: "ہر ایک علم فارسی عربی صرف نحو منطق معانی حدیث تفسیر" — Persian, Arabic,
  sarf, nahw, mantiq, maani, hadith, tafsir (p. 38, folio 41). The order of a Muslim boy's schooling:
  "سبق اول قرآن پڑھایا جاتا تہا پھر فارسی کتابین پڑھائی جاتی", first lesson the Quran, then Persian
  books, and the whole "خام سی [OCR?] تعلیم ہوا کرتی تھی", a rather raw education (pp. 38–39,
  folios 41–42).
- **Government College**: "ہزار ہا طالب علم تحصیل کے رتبہ پر پہونچ گئے", thousands of students reached
  the rank of tahsil; branches in "شہر کے مختلف و متفرق محلون", scattered quarters of the city, feeding
  the college; "گورنمنٹ کالج میں [illegible] تعلیم نہین دی جاتی علوم ہر وجہ پڑھائے جاتے ہین اس سبب ہند و
  مسلمان دونو [illegible] اپنے لڑکون کو لے [illegible] کالج مین داخل کر دیتے ہین" — no [illegible]
  instruction is given there and all sciences are taught, for which reason **both Hindus and Muslims**
  enrol their boys (p. 39, folio 42).
- **University College Lahore, and its staff by subject** (pp. 39–40, folios 42–43): "علوم مشرقی کی
  تعلیم ہوتی ہے" — oriental learning, with "عربی فارسی سنسکرت پشتو", verse and prose, mathematics,
  **Hindi medicine**, and **Gurmukhi**. Principal **Dr. Leitner [OCR?]**. Arabic: Maulvi Faiz al-Hasan,
  Qazi Zafar al-Din, Hafiz Abd al-Aziz. Persian: Maulvi Abd al-Hakim, Muhammad al-Din, Shah [illegible].
  Mathematics: Pirzada Muhammad Husain, assistant professor. Urdu: Maulvi Ghulam Mustafa. Hindsi [OCR?]
  mathematics: بہائی [OCR?] گورمکھی [OCR?] سنگھ, assistant professor. Gurmukhi: بہائی [OCR?] ہربیاس
  سنگھ [OCR?] and a جوگی [illegible]. Natural science: ڈاکٹر امیر شاہ [OCR?], assistant surgeon.
  Engineering: Lala Ganga Ram, assistant engineer. Hindsi and Vaid: a Pandit [illegible]. **Yunani
  medicine**: Maulvi Ghulam [illegible]. Pashto: Mir Abdullah Dehli [OCR?]. **"ہندوؤ قانون یعنی شاستری
  کی تعلیم متعلق پنڈت گور پر شاد [OCR?] اور مسلمانی شرعیہ مسایل کے متعلق مولوی عبد الحکیم کی ہے"** —
  Hindu law, that is Shastri, belongs to Pandit Gaur Parshad [OCR?], and questions of Muslim sharia to
  Maulvi Abd al-Hakim; English government law is lectured by مشر [OCR?] یا رکر [OCR?] Sahib, **Judicial
  Assistant**. A single state college teaching Hindu and Muslim religious law side by side with English
  law, by named men.
- **Normal School, with stipends**: also called "تعلیم المعلمین"; it trains the masters "جو شہر کے باہر
  چھوٹے بڑے دیہات و قصبون و تحصیلی مدارس مین پڑھاتے ہین", who teach in the villages, qasbas and tehsil

  ملتا رہتا ہے", during training they receive a **wazifa** (p. 40, folio 43).

  سنایا جاتا ہے مگر **عیسی مسیح کی مدحیہ غزلین لڑکون کو یاد کرائی جاتی ہین جسکو وہ خوش آواز کے ساتھ
  پڑھتے ہین**" — English, Persian and [illegible] are taught, but the Bible too; sermons are given to

  جہان لڑکیان تعلیم پاتی ہین" — besides these madrasas there are also [illegible] madrasas in the city

### Print and the book trade

- **From manuscript to press, with a founding date.** "پہلے سکھون کے وقت کوئی چھاپہ کے نام سے بھی واقف
  نہ تہا سب کتابین قلمی تہین" — in the Sikhs' time no one even knew the name of printing; all books were
  manuscript (p. 40, folio 43). "لاہور میں مطبع کوہ نور **۱۸۵۰ [OCR?] ع** میں منشی ہر سکھ رے [OCR?] نے
  جاری کیا" — the **Matba Koh-i-Nur** was started in **1850 CE [OCR?]** by Munshi Har Sukh Rai [OCR?],
  with a weekly paper of the same name "جو اب تک چھپتا ہی ہے", still printed (p. 41, folio 44).
- **Eleven numbered presses with owners and papers** (pp. 41–42, folios 44–45): Koh-i-Nur; **Punjabi
  Matba [OCR?]**, owner Munshi Muhammad Azim, weekly *Punjabi Akhbar*; **Matba Anjuman**, run by the
  Anjuman's members' [مشہوری [OCR?]] under the patronage of جی ڈبلیو ڈاکٹر لیٹنر [OCR?] Sahib Bahadur,
  weekly *Anjuman Akhbar*; **Matba Aftab-e Punjab**, owner Diwan Bota Singh, a daily of the same name
  edited by Maulvi Faqir Muhammad; a fifth press [illegible] whose owner is Pandit Bal Kund [OCR?], "ایک
  سادہ لوح آدمی", a simple-hearted man, from which the daily ***Akhbar-e Aam*** comes out, "اس اخبار کے
  مضامین بہت عمدہ ہوتے ہین جس سے عام کو فایدہ پہنچتا ہی", whose articles are very fine and benefit the
  public; **Matba Saifi**, owner Sayyid Nadir Ali Shah Saifi; **Matba Lahore Punch**; **Matba Dehli
  Punch**, managed by its owner Fazl al-Din; **Victoria Press**, established by Munshi Aziz al-Din, who
  "**۱۲۹۱ ہجری** میں مر گیا", **died in 1291 AH**, now owned by his brother Munshi Chiragh al-Din, doing
  much Persian and English work; **Matba Mustafai**, whose owner (میر الدین [OCR?] …) "اسی سال ہین مر گیا
  ہے", died this very year, the work now carried on by one ہیرا [OCR?], his بہا [OCR?]; and **Matba
  Qanuni Lahore**, printing law books and the fortnightly ***Makhzan al-Qawanin***, whose owner Pandit
  Suraj Bhan [OCR?], **pleader (پلیٹڈر [OCR?])**, having died, the work runs "اس کے وارثون کے اہتمام
  مین", under his heirs' management.
- **Government and English printing**: the Director Sahib Bahadur's press, "جہان طالب علمون کی پڑھنے کی
  کتابین فارسی بکثرت چھپتی ہین", where students' Persian textbooks are printed in quantity, with a
  Persian *Government Gazette*; the government press from which the English *Punjab Gazette* is published,
  with **one branch attached to the Chief Engineer Bahadur's office and a second to the Central Jail**;
  and **Matba Sol Militry**, publishing the *Civil & Military Gazette* daily (p. 42, folio 45). Other
  Persian presses named: Gulshan-e Rashid, Muhammadi, Qadri, Mufid-e Aam, and Matba Bal Sahib Saudagar;
  monthly journals *Hafiz-e Sehat* and *Takmil al-Hikmat* (pp. 41–42, folios 44–45).
- **The manuscript trade it displaced, with figures.** The general book trade was in the hands of "وزیر
  خان کی مسجد گنبد بیرونی کے دوکاندار", the shopkeepers at the outer dome of Wazir Khan's mosque, "وہی
  لوگ جلد بندی کا کام کرتے", the same men doing the binding (p. 42, folio 45). **Mian Muhammad Bakhsh
  Sahhaf's** house "جس کے پاس پچیس تیس کاتب ملازم کتابین لکھا کرتے تہو", employed **twenty-five or thirty
  scribes** writing books; "اس کے کارخانہ کی کتابین تمام پنجاب مین جاتی تہین بلکہ **ایران و خراسان** تک
  سوداگر خرید کے لیجاتے تہو", its books went throughout the Punjab and merchants carried them as far as
  **Iran and Khurasan**; "اب وہ کارخانہ برہم و درہم ہو گیا ہی", now that house is broken up and
  scattered, and Maulvi Fazl al-Din of that family is employed "سرکاری مطبع ڈائرکٹری مین بعہدہ
  سپرنٹنڈنٹی", as Superintendent in the government Directory press (pp. 41, 43; folios 44, 46).
- **The three great booksellers and their reach.** **Chiragh al-Din**, "جسکی دوکان پر ہزارون روپیہ کے
  کتابون کی تجارت ہوتی ہی", thousands of rupees of book trade at his shop, who has "ایک ہزار روپیے ما
  ہدار [OCR?] کی کتاب" — a thousand rupees' worth of books [monthly? see Doubts] — sent from the **Nawal
  Kishore press of Lucknow (لکھنو مطبع نو لکشور [OCR?])**, and prints on his own account, the Victoria
  Press being his [illegible]'s. **Faqir Allah**, "قوم سادہ", whose shop is thriving and who "مسلمانی
  دینی کتابین بہت بہت چھپوانا اور فروخت کرتا ہی", has many Muslim religious books printed and sells
  them. **Ilahi Bakhsh**, bookseller in تولا [OCR?], "صاحب ثروت مشہور", famous as a man of wealth. Their
  books go to "پیشاور و ملتان و ڈیرجات [OCR?] و ہزارہ"; other dealers named are Karim Bakhsh, Malik
  Hira, Yahya al-Din [OCR?] and Gulab Singh. **"عمدہ اور معتبر کتابین … خاصہ لاہور مین کم چھپتی ہین
  اکثر دہلی و لکھنو و ممبئی وغیرہ سے آ کر فروخت کی جاتی ہین"** — fine and reliable books are little
  printed in Lahore itself; most come from Delhi, Lucknow and Bombay to be sold (p. 43, folio 46). **A
  named supply chain for religious books into Lahore and out to Peshawar and Multan.**

### The communities of Lahore, and a list of Sufi orders

- **Priority of settlement asserted.** "لاہور کی قدیم رہنے والی ہندو قومین ہین **ایک ہزار برس سے پہلے
  کوئی غیر مذہب کا آدمی اس شہر مین سکونت پذیر سوائے ہندوؤ کے نہ تہا**" — Lahore's ancient inhabitants are
  the Hindu qaums; before a thousand years ago no man of another religion resided in this city but
  Hindus; Muslim entry dates from Mahmud Ghaznavi's conquests, "رفتہ رفتہ کثرت ہو گئی", and Christians
  "بھی بہت ہو گئی ہین اور ہوتے جاتے", are now many and increasing (p. 43, folio 46).
- **The Hindu qaums ranked by wealth.** "برہمن کھتری اروڑہ راجپوت پوربیہ سکھ وغیرہ" are the chief;
  "سب سے بڑا تعداد کھتریون کی ہی اور یہی قوم مالدار اور ساہوکار ہی" — the **Khatris** are the most
  numerous and are the wealthy and **sahukar** (moneylending) qaum; the **Arora** qaum is counted next
  after them; **Brahmins**, though few among the wealthy, are "اپنے علوم سنسکرت و شاستر و جیوتش وغیرہ
  مین صاحب کمال", masters in Sanskrit, Shastra and Jyotish (pp. 43–44, folios 46–47).
- **A reform party noted as recent.** "ہندوؤ کا ہندو ست [OCR?] بھی قدیم برہمنی مذہب ہی مگر **چند سال سے

### Crafts, wages and the displacement of an art

- **Desi work giving way to wilayati, told as advance.** The blacksmiths and [illegible] work so well
  "کہ ولایتی [illegible] کا نمونہ مین جاتا ہے"; in the Sikhs' time guns, swords and cannon were made fine
  but "وہ سب کام دیسی ہوتا تہا", all that work was **desi**; now they do wilayati work "کہ انگریز دیکھکر
  آفرین کہنے ہین", so that the English say bravo; in the railway department they carry out all kinds of
  wilayati work. Lahore's **goldsmiths (زر گر)** now make wilayati work finer than wilayati make, and
  high-grade desi goldsmithing too (p. 45, folio 48).
- **Bookbinding as a named master-and-pupil trade**: the binders "ولایتی جلدین کے بنانے اور سونا چڑھانے
  مین ایسے مین استاد ہو گئے ہین کہ انگریز ان کی بھی ہوئی جلدین دیکھکر تحسین کرتے ہین", have become such
  masters of European binding and **gilding** that the English praise their work; the great master is
  حسین [OCR?] خان [OCR?] with his pupil **Haji Abdullah**, and others are Mir, بشیر [OCR?], Hasan Din
  and Muhammad Bakhsh (p. 45, folio 48).
- **Masons' wages, doubled, with the figures.** They built desi buildings finely in the Sikh era and are
  now masters of English building too, "اور پہلے سے دو چند اجرت ان کی بڑھ گئی ہی", their wage having
  doubled: **"سکھون کے وقت چار آنہ یومیہ معمار اور دو آنہ مزدور کا [illegible] اب اونے [OCR?] معمار کی
  اجرت آٹھہ آنہ اور مزدور کے چار آنہ مین اور استاد معمار ایک روپیہ دو روپیہ یومیہ تک پاتے مین"** — in the
  Sikhs' time four annas a day for a mason and two annas for a labourer; now eight annas and four annas,
  and master masons receive up to one or two rupees a day (p. 45, folio 48). **The only wage series in
  the chunk, and directly usable for dating building costs.**
- **Photography kills the painters' trade.** Lahore's painters were famous "ملکون مین", and the foremost,
  میا [OCR?] Imam Bakhsh, "تشبیہ ایسی کھینچتا تہا کہ اصل و نقل مین کچھہ تمیز نہین ہوتی تھی", drew a
  likeness so that original and copy could not be told apart; **"اب یہ فن عکسی تصویرون نے بالکل کھو دیا
  ہی سب لوگ اسی کو پسند کرتے ہین"** — now photographic pictures have entirely destroyed this art and
  everyone prefers them; "نقاشی کام البتہ فروغ پر ہی", naqqashi work, however, is flourishing (p. 45,
  folio 48). The chunk ends mid-sentence on this point.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **A three-stage judgement on the city's history, asserted rather than argued.** The chunk's structure
  carries the argument: Sikh rule is plunder, filth and desolation ("ظالمانہ حکومت", tyrannical rule,
  p. 31, folio 34; "شہر لاہور نہایت گندہ تہا", p. 36, folio 39); Ranjit Singh is restoration ("گویا شہر
  کے بخت جاگے", p. 32, folio 35); the British period is order, growth and justice (pp. 33–38, folios
  36–41). The author does not weigh counter-evidence; the one qualification he allows is that Ranjit
  Singh's own army damaged the city after his death (p. 32, folio 35).
- **British rule credited with removing the defects of indigenous education.** Having described the
  ulama's madrasas with evident respect, he writes: **"سرکار انگریزی نے وہ سب نقص دور کر دیئے جابجا
  سرکاری مدرسے جاری کیے"** — the English government removed all those defects and started government
  madrasas everywhere (p. 39, folio 42). The judgement sits awkwardly beside his own statement that
  thousands of Hindus and Muslims were taught in one pre-British madrasa and that the whole Punjab
  respected its master (p. 38, folio 41) — an internal tension worth citing as such.
- **The state's fairness in taking property is asserted as a fact, twice, with no dissent recorded.**
  "براہ معدلت", out of justice, and "جس سے کوئی ناراض نہوا", so that no one was displeased (p. 38,
  folio 41). For a project reading endowment and land material, note that this is the author's own
  characterisation of a compulsory acquisition inside the walled city, not a record of claims made or
  refused.
- **Failure described in technical terms.** The collapse of the tank is explained by a construction fault
  — too little concrete beaten into the foundation — and its consequences traced to unrammed trenches and
  leaking iron pipes (pp. 37–38, folios 40–41). The account reads as an engineer's post-mortem and is the
  most technically specific passage in the chunk.
- **The railway, not the gates, is made the organising fact of the modern city.** "شہر لاہور کی زیادہ
  ترقی کا باعث … محکمہ ریل ہے" (p. 35, folio 38), and Mela Ram's sarai is judged a failure precisely by
  its distance from the station (p. 36, folio 39). A causal claim about urban form, stated twice.
- **Sufism presented as the irenic option among four Muslim positions.** Sunni, Shia and Wahhabi are each
  defined by what they oppose; the Sufis alone "کسی سے عداوت نہین رکھتے" and are "صلح کل و بے تعصب"
  (pp. 44–45, folios 47–48). This is the author's own framing, in a survey written for a general
  readership, and is quotable as a nineteenth-century Lahore insider's definition of the orders' place in
  the city — but it is not evidence about any shrine.

## Doubts

- **"میانمیر [OCR?]" in this chunk is a cantonment, a maidan and a bazaar, not a shrine — do not map it
  to shrine-of-mian-mir.** The name appears five times at pp. 34–35 (folios 37–38), always as
  "چھاؤنی میانمیر [OCR?]", "میانمیر [OCR?] کے میدان" or "صدر بازار میانمیر [OCR?]", and always
  OCR-flagged. **The chunk names no saint, grave, mazar, urs or custodian there, and does not say the
  place is named after anyone.** The archive row is a Qadiri shrine at Alam Ganj (Dharampura) with a 1635
  death year; none of that is in this chunk. Record the toponym as topography; a human must decide, from
  outside this chunk, whether the two are connected.
- **Several archive rows' localities are named in this chunk without their shrines.** **انار کلی**
  (pp. 34–35, folios 37–38) — where the archive places tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak and
  shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari; **بھاٹی دروازہ** (p. 36, folio 39) — peer-makki and
  gurdwara-chowmala-sahib; **موضع مزنگ [OCR?]** (p. 35, folio 38) — gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-mozang and
  shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri; **دہلی دروازہ**, **شاہ عالمی دروازہ**, **لہاری دروازہ**, **قلعہ گوجر
  سنگھ**, **گڑھی شاہو [OCR?]** (pp. 32, 34–36, folios 35, 37–39). In every case the chunk speaks of
  cantonments, sarais, bazaars and quarters only. **A shared place name is not a match; none of these
  should be cited as evidence about a shrine.**
- **Mahmud Ghaznavi is named without any companion, officer or governor** (p. 43, folio 46) — so nothing
  here supports darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz, whose archive row describes its figure as Mahmud's slave,
  minister and governor of Lahore. The chunk names only the conquests and the resulting Muslim
  settlement.
- **The samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh material is about the man, not the monument.** No samadhi,
  Hazuri Bagh, cremation, tomb or death year appears; "مہاراجہ کی وفات" is mentioned without a date
  (p. 32, folio 35). Do not let a consolidation pass turn his building works into shrine material.
- **The Sufi-order list (p. 44, folio 47) is doctrinal and unattached.** Two of the seven names are
  OCR-flagged as printed — **"مجدویہ [OCR?]"** and **"شطاریہ [OCR?]"** — and are recorded here exactly
  as the chunk prints them; they are **not** corrected or expanded. A reader tempted to align them with
  archive `silsila` values such as "Naushahia Qadiri" (ranmal-sharif) or "Qadri Shattari"
  (shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri) must note that the chunk connects the list to no shrine, no saint and no
  place.
- **Dates: all three CE/AH figures in the chunk need checking, two of them flagged.** "۱۸۵۰ [OCR?] ع"
  appears twice — the first sanitary reform (p. 37, folio 40) and the founding of the Matba Koh-i-Nur
  (p. 41, folio 44); "۱۸۶۰ [OCR?] ع" for the paving (p. 37, folio 40). Only "۱۲۹۱ ہجری", the death of
  Munshi Aziz al-Din of the Victoria Press (p. 42, folio 45), is unflagged. **No folio or date is
  computed or converted here.**
