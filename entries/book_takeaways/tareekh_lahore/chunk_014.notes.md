# tareekh_lahore — chunk 014 — pp. 192–206

**What this slice is: a survey of the samadhs, shivalas and thakurdwaras standing on the open ground
north of Lahore Fort and on the bank of the Ravi nala, described one building at a time with its
founder, its cost, its endowment and its present servant.** Eleven named sites in fifteen pages
(pp. 192–206, folios 195–209), all Sikh-period or Sikh-patronised, all recorded with an engineer's
eye: plinth heights, door orientations, vault type, whether the kalas was ever raised. Two archive
rows take material; the value of the chunk is otherwise in its custodianship and endowment detail —
a Brahmin on the Jammu government's payroll, a bairagi mahant holding **مالکانہ قبضہ** (proprietary
possession) of a temple's wells and land, a purchased-and-endowed (**وقف**) estate for a temple's
expenses, a Mughal grant of a paisa per shop, and a faqiri **گدی** whose house the British-era
authorities repeatedly tried to demolish for overlooking the fort.

**The chunk prints a `[folio N]` marker on every page** and the printed folio runs three ahead of the
PDF index on each of them, as printed: p. 192 → folio 195 … p. 206 → folio 209. No folio below is
computed; each is the one the chunk prints for that page.

**A change of transcription hand falls inside the chunk** — see Doubts; it affects how the same word
is spelled before and after p. 201 (folio 204), including the word *samadh* itself.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **gurdwara-dera-sahib** (the book's name for it: **"سمادہ گرو ارجن"**, the Samadh of Guru Arjan —
  it never uses "Dera Sahib"; see Doubts on the mapping):
  - **Location, given by three bearings.** "یہ سمادہ روشنائی دروازے شہر لاہور کے سامنے واقع ہے" — this
    samadh is situated opposite the **Roshnai Gate** of the city of Lahore; to its east, at the
    distance of one road ("بفاصلہ ایک رستہ[OCR?]"), is the wall of the fort, and toward the west
    ("غریب کی طرف", for غربی) at a remove of [illegible] is the wall of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's samadh
    (pp. 205–206, folios 208–209).
  - **"گرو ارجن پانچواں گرو قوم سکھہ کا تھا"** — Guru Arjan was the fifth Guru of the Sikh qaum
    (p. 206, folio 209). No date of any kind is given for him in this chunk — **(not stated)**.
  - **The book states there is no body here.** "اگرچہ گرو ارجن دریا میں بوقت غسل مفقود ہو چکا تھا نعش
    اسکی جلائی نہیں گئی مگر یہ سمادہ یادگار کے طور پر بنائی گئی" — although Guru Arjan had disappeared
    in the river at the time of his bath, his corpse was not cremated; but this samadh was built **by
    way of a memorial** (p. 206, folio 209). For the manner of his death the author refers the reader
    to his own book: "اسکی وفات کا قصہ مفصل مندرج کتاب تاریخ پنجاب مولفہ بندہ ہے", the story of his
    death is recorded in detail in *Tarikh-e Punjab*, compiled by this humble writer (p. 206,
    folio 209).
  - **Two building phases.** "پہلے یہ مختصر مکان تھا مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت یہ عالیشان مکان بنایا
    گیا" — formerly this was a small building; in **Maharaja Ranjit Singh's** time this grand building
    was made (p. 206, folio 209). No year is given for either phase — (not stated).
  - **Fabric and furnishings** (p. 206, folio 209): a pukhta chuna-gach enclosure with a parapet
    ("منڈیر"); two of its doors kept shut and all traffic through the great eastern door, on whose
    "پیشانی" (brow) letters are written **in Gurmukhi script**, "بخط گرمکھی حروف لکھے ہوئے ہیں", which
    the text goes on to call "تصویر[OCR?] یہ بھی گرو ارجن کی تحریر ہے"; to the south of the four walls
    the grand domed samadh building, pukhta chuna-gach; to its north a "سہ دالان" in whose middle bay
    "اسمیں گرنتہ صاحب بآداب تمام رکھا رہتا ہے", the **Granth Sahib** is kept with full reverence; the
    samadh's own chabutra pukhta chuna-gach, where the text breaks off at "[illegible]".
  - **A baoli, a bhang house and four small samadhs** in the same enclosure: see Practices.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** (the book's phrase: **"دیوار سمادہ مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کی"**):
  - The only thing this chunk says about it is where it stands: its wall lies to the **west** of the
    samadh of Guru Arjan, "غریب کی طرف ایک [illegible] ہٹکر … دیوار سمادہ مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کی ہے"
    (pp. 205–206, folios 208–209). The distance is lost to an `[illegible]`. Nothing on its fabric,
    its date, its custodian or its income — (not stated) for all of these here.
  - Maharaja Ranjit Singh himself appears throughout the chunk as patron and as a name in others'
    titles; those facts belong to the buildings they concern and are kept there.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Jamadar Khushal Singh** and his samadh, the chunk's first named building ("سماوہ جمعدار خوشحال
  سنگہ", pp. 192–193, folios 195–196): "ایک امیر کبیر و رکن اعظم سلطنت سکہہی", a great noble and chief
  pillar of the Sikh kingdom, greatly honoured in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's darbar; "اس امیر کو عمارت کا
  بڑا شوق تہا", this noble had a great passion for building, and "لاہور و امرتسر میں اسکی عالیشان
  عمارتیں ابتک موجود ہیں", his grand buildings in **Lahore and Amritsar** still stand. He died "سنہ
  ١٨٤٤[OCR?] ع" (1844 CE[OCR?]) and — note the verb — "اسی موقع پر دفن دیا گیا", was **buried** at the
  very spot where the samadh building now stands (p. 192, folio 195).
- **Raja Udham Singh** ("سماوہ راجہ اودہم سنگہ", pp. 193–194, folios 196–197): eldest son of **Maharaja
  Gulab Singh**; he "قلعہ کے دروازے کی دیوار کے نیچے آکر کنور نو نہال سنگہ خلف مہاراجہ کہڑک سنگہ کے
  سانتہہ ہلاک ہوا تہا", was killed under the wall of the fort gateway together with **Kunwar Nau Nihal
  Singh**, son of **Maharaja Kharak Singh**. The author's judgement on him: "یہ نوجوان بہادر کوہستانی
  سردار نہایت خوبصورت زور آور پہلوان خلیق و سخی تہا مگر اُسکی عمر نے وفا نکی[OCR?] اور مفت مارا گیا" —
  a young, brave hill sardar, very handsome, strong, a wrestler, affable and generous, but his life
  did not keep faith with him and he was killed for nothing (p. 194, folio 197). No date — (not
  stated).
- **Raja Suchet Singh** ("سماوہ راجہ سوچیت سنگہ", pp. 194–195, folios 197–198): brother of "راجہ و میان
  سنگہ" [so printed] **وزیر اعظم سلطنت سکہی** and of **Maharaja Gulab Singh**; killed during and by
  order of **Raja Hira Singh's** wizarat "بمقام خانقاہ میان وڈا[OCR?]", at the place Khanqah Mian
  Wadda[OCR?]. The killing is narrated at length: he had come from Jammu to have the Sikh army depose
  Hira Singh and make him wazir; the army stayed with Hira Singh; Hira Singh, learning that "چچا میری
  جان کا دشمن بنکر آیا ہے", his uncle had come as the enemy of his life, ordered the troops to surround
  him; "فوج پیادہ و سواری نے جاکر خانقاہ میان وڈا کو جسمیں وہ تہا گہیر لیا اور توپ چلنو[OCR?] لگی" —
  foot and horse surrounded the khanqah in which he was and **cannon began to fire**; he came out into
  the open with **Rai Kesari Singh** and his companions and was killed (pp. 194–195, folios 197–198).
  Casualties: see Practices. His corpse was cremated in the same building where the samadh now stands
  (p. 195, folio 198).
- **Sardar Jawahar Singh**, "مامون مہاراجہ دلیپ سنگہ", maternal uncle of Maharaja Dalip Singh
  (pp. 196–197, folios 199–200): he took the wizarat when the Sikh army killed Raja Hira Singh, and
  was himself killed by the same soldiery — "سردار جواہر سنگہ سنہ ١٨٤٧[OCR?] ع کے اخیر میں خود سر سکہی
  فوج کے سانتہہ سے بمقام لاہور قتل ہوا تہا", killed at Lahore at the end of the year 1847[OCR?] CE by
  the unruly Sikh army (p. 197, folio 200). For the detailed account he again refers the reader to
  "تاریخ پنجاب مولفہ راقم", *Tarikh-e Punjab* compiled by the writer (p. 196, folio 199). His body was
  cremated where the samadh stands; his largesse to the army is under Practices.
- **Bhai Rupa** ("سماوہ بہائی روپا", pp. 197–198, folios 200–201): "یہ بہا کی[OCR?] روپا مہاراجہ رنجیت
  سنگہ کے عہد میں ایک خدا پرست عابد زاہد آدمی تہا" — in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's time a God-fearing,
  devout, ascetic man; "شہر لاہور کے ہندو مسلمان اُسکی بزرگی کے قایل تہے", the **Hindus and Muslims of
  the city of Lahore alike acknowledged his sanctity**; universally liked and "بے طمع", free of greed.
  Ranjit Singh's "آمد و رفت اُسکے پاس بہت تہی", coming and going to him was frequent, "مگر وہ اپنے
  پیسے کی طمع مہاراجہ سے نہیں رکہتا تہا", but he kept no greed for the Maharaja's money; "جب وہ مر گیا
  تو مہاراجہ کے حکم سے یہ مقبول صورت سماوہ اُسکے نام سے تعمیر ہوئی", when he died this pleasing samadh
  was built in his name **by the Maharaja's order** (p. 198, folio 201). No date — (not stated).
- **Bawa Shiv Gar Sanyasi** and his shivala ("شوالہ شیو گر سنیاسی", pp. 198–199, folios 201–202): a
  two-level building, the shivala above and his samadh in the lower, sunk storey, "چونکہ زمین کے اندر
  ہے مکان اندہیرا ہے چراغ لیکر اندر جاتے ہیں", being underground the room is dark and one goes in with
  a lamp. **"وسط مکان میں باوا شیو گر کی سماوہ ہے جسمیں وہ زندہ بیٹہہ گیا تہا اور سماوہ کا دروازہ چن
  دیا گیا تہا"** — in the middle of the room is Bawa Shiv Gar's samadh, in which he sat down **alive**
  and the samadh's door was walled up (p. 198, folio 201). He built the whole house himself in his
  lifetime and it was still unfinished when he wearied of the faithless world ("دنیا نا شہید[OCR?] سے
  [illegible] وہ بیزار ہو گیا") and "اس سماوہ میں بیٹہکر اُس نے مٹی لے لی", sitting in this samadh he
  took the earth. **"اُسکی وفات کو چالیس برس گزرے ہیں"** — forty years have passed since his death
  (p. 199, folio 202) — a relative date only; the chunk gives no year, and no year for the book's
  present — (not stated).
- **Ganita[OCR?] Nand Gar Bawa Sanyasi**, "جو شیو گر کا پوتا چیلا ہے", Shiv Gar's grand-disciple, who
  lives in the shivala with his wife (p. 199, folio 202) — the succession and his title to the land are
  under Practices.
- **Rani Lachhmi**, a rani of Maharaja Ranjit Singh, founder ("بانیہ") of the **ٹہاکر دوارہ رانی لچہمی**
  on the Ravi bank: "اُس نے بکمال ارادت یہ ٹہاکر دوارہ بناکر اپنا یادگار دنیا سے نا پایدار میں چہوڑا",
  with complete devotion she built it and left her memorial in this impermanent world (p. 199,
  folio 202).
- **Bhauri Sarkar**, another rani, founder of the **ٹہاکر دوارہ بہوری سرکار**: "بانیہ اس مندر کی
  مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کی رانیون میں سے ایک ذی عزت رانی تہی"; the name is explained by the author —
  "چونکہ بال اُسکے سرخ رنگ کے تہے اس واسطے اُسکو بہوری سرکار کہتے تہے کہ بہورا پنجابی زبان میں سرخ رنگ
  مائل سیاہی کو کہتے ہیں", her hair being red, she was called Bhauri Sarkar, for *bhaura* in Punjabi
  means a red inclining to black (p. 200, folio 203). Her building, land purchase and endowment are
  under Practices; "مہاراجہ شیر سنگہ کیوقت اسکی تعمیر عمل میں آئی", the construction was carried out in
  **Maharaja Sher Singh's** time (p. 200, folio 203).
- **Bawa Lachhman Das Bawa[OCR?] Bairagi[OCR?]**, whose pukhta chuna-gach domed samadh stands in front
  of the door of Rani Lachhmi's thakurdwara (p. 200, folio 203); and **Bawa Ram Das Bairagi[OCR?]**,
  "اس مندر کا پجاری و مالک", its pujari **and owner** (p. 200, folio 203).
- **Madho Das Bairagi[OCR?]**, mahant of the Bhauri Sarkar thakurdwara (p. 201, folio 204) — see

- **Bhai Wasti Ram[OCR?]** ("سمادہ بہائی وستی رام[OCR?]", pp. 201–203, folios 204–206): "ایک نامی
  گرامی گرنتہ[OCR?] خوان خدا پرست عابد زاہد شخص تھا", a renowned **Granth-reciter**, God-fearing,
  devout and ascetic; "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ اسکو اپنا گرو کہتے تھے اور بہت ادب کرتے تھے", Maharaja Ranjit
  Singh called him his **guru** and showed him great respect; "ہر ایک امیر و وزیر سے اسکا رتبہ از روئے
  خدا پرستی کے زیادہ تر تصور کیا جاتا تھا", his rank was reckoned above every noble and minister by
  virtue of his piety. He was cremated where his samadh stands (p. 201, folio 204). His nazr, jagirs
  and allowances are under Practices. The author closes the entry with a cross-reference: "بہائی وستی
  رام کے خاندان کا حال اس کتاب کی ابتدا میں خاص شہر کے حال میں تحریر ہے" — the account of Bhai Wasti
  Ram's family is written at the beginning of this book, in the account of the city proper
  (pp. 202–203, folios 205–206).
- **Bawa Jhingar Shah, famous as Suthra** ("سمادہ باوا جہینگر شاہ المشہور ستہرا", pp. 203–204,
  folios 206–207): "جہینگر شاہ ستہرا شاہ عالمگیر اورنگ زیب بادشاہ کے عہد میں خاص لاہور کا رہنے والا
  فقیر تھا" — a faqir resident of Lahore proper in the time of Shah **Alamgir Aurangzeb**; "ہنود اسکو
  فقیر صاحب کمال جانتے ہیں", the Hindus know him as a faqir of accomplishment; "اسکا سلسلہ ابتک باقی
  چلا رہی ہے ستہرا[OCR?] شاہ کے فقیر ہند اور پنجاب میں گدائی کرتے پھرتے ہیں", his silsila continues to
  this day and the faqirs of Suthra Shah go about begging in Hind and Punjab. **"جہینگر شاہ ستہرا گرو
  ہر رائے کا چیلہ تھا"** — he was a chela of **Guru Har Rai** (p. 203, folio 206). The author explains

  اسی جگہ وہ فوت ہوا اور اسی جگہ جلایا گیا", his real home was Lahore; he died there and was cremated

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A temple servant on a foreign government's payroll.** Of the Udham Singh / Suchet Singh enclosure:
  **"اس سماوہ کی خدمت کے لئے ایک برہمن مقرر ہے جو تنخواہ وکیل سرکار جموں سے پاتا ہے وہ ہر وقت حاضر
  نہیں رہتا ہر روز صبح کو آتا ہے اور سماوہیون اور شو جی پر پہول پیر[OCR?] چڑہا کر چلا جاتا ہے"** — for
  the service of this samadh a Brahmin is appointed who draws his **salary from the wakil of the Jammu
  government**; he is not in attendance at all hours, but comes every morning, makes his offering of
  flowers on the samadhs and on Shiv ji, and goes away (pp. 195–196, folios 198–199). A Lahore
  memorial maintained from Jammu through a political agent, with a named ritual round and an explicit
  statement of part-time service.
- **A pujari housed inside the enclosure.** In the same enclosure, in the three-bay dalan on the south,
  "ایک کوٹہری بنی ہوئی ہے جسمیں پجاری رہتا ہے", a small room in which the pujari lives; the enclosure
  also has a wheel-well, "چاہ چرخی دار" (p. 193, folio 196).
- **Shiv ji's asthan inside a Sikh noble's samadh.** Both Udham Singh's and Suchet Singh's samadh
  buildings have a pukhta chabutra in the middle carrying "شو جی کا استہان ہیں[OCR?]", the asthan of
  Shiv ji, with the samadh itself set in an arched niche in the western wall facing the door
  (pp. 194, 195; folios 197, 198). The building type here is a memorial and a working shrine at once.
- **Endowment by purchase, stated as waqf.** Of Bhauri Sarkar: **"اس نے اپنی حین حیات یہ مندر بنوایا
  بہت سی زمین زرعی خرید کر اسکے اخراجات کیواسطے[OCR?] وقف کی باغ بہی نہایت عمدہ لگوایا جو ابتک مندر
  کے سانتہہ شامل ہے"** — she had this mandir built in her lifetime, **purchased much agricultural land
  and endowed (waqf) it for its expenses**, and had a very fine garden laid out which is still attached
  to the mandir (p. 200, folio 203). The word used for a Hindu temple endowment is *waqf*.
- **The Bhauri Sarkar thakurdwara as a working estate** (p. 201, folio 204): attached to it are
  "نہ چار دہ نہ[OCR?] چاہ" [numeral garbled] wells, "بہت سی زرعی زمین اور بہت بہت بڑا باغ", much
  agricultural land and a very large garden; "فقرا و سادہ بہت رہتے ہیں", many faqirs and sadhus live
  there; "ایک خراس بھی آٹا پیسنے کے واسطے بنا ہے", a **khiras** (mill) built for grinding flour; many
  pukhta kothas "جس میں باغات و چاہات کے مویشی رات کو باندھے جاتے ہیں اور باغبان و کاشتکار سکونت پذیر
  ہیں", where the garden and well cattle are tied at night and where **gardeners and cultivators
  reside**; one "کوٹھا[OCR?] کلاں" built specially for fodder; besides the working wells, two
  wheel-wells "آبانوشی کے لیے", for drinking water; "بہت سی پختہ گنبد وار سماد ہیں جانشینان و
  مہتممان[OCR?] سابق کی بنی ہوئی موجود ہیں", **many pukhta domed samadhs of former successors

  thakurdwara. "اس مندر میں رونق زیادہ تر ہے اور آبادی بھی بہت" — this mandir has the most liveliness
  and a large resident population.
- **A mahant with proprietary possession.** **"مادہو داس بیراگی[OCR?] کی مہنت ہے جس کے قبضہ میں مندر
  اور چاہات و باغ و زمین بین[OCR?] متعلقہ مندر ہیں اور مالکانہ قبضہ رکھتا ہے"** — Madho Das
  Bairagi[OCR?] holds the mahantship; the mandir and the wells, garden and land belonging to it are in
  his possession, and he holds **مالکانہ قبضہ**, proprietary possession (p. 201, folio 204). Compare
  the plainer formula at the neighbouring temple: "باوا رام داس بیرا گی[OCR?] اس مندر کا پجاری و مالک
  ہے", pujari **and owner** (p. 200, folio 203). Two temples on the same bank, two statements of
  ownership vested in the officiant rather than in a trust.
- **Land and income at the Shiv Gar shivala, held by the grand-disciple.** "اسمیں[OCR?] شوالے کے متعلق
  ایک چاہ جاری اور آٹہہ[OCR?] گہاؤن[OCR?] اراضی چاہی زرعی ہے آمدنی اُسکی گنیتا[OCR?] نند گر لیتا ہے
  اور مالک اراضی کا بہی وہی ہے" — attached to the shivala are one working well and eight[OCR?] ghumaon

  land** (p. 199, folio 202). He is Shiv Gar's "پوتا چیلا", grand-disciple, lives there with his wife,
  and "اس شوالہ کو اُس نے اپنا گہر بنار[OCR?] کہا ہے", has made the shivala his own home — succession
  by discipleship and a residence claim in one sentence.
- **Land at Rani Lachhmi's thakurdwara.** "چاہ مع رہٹ کے جاری ہے قریب دس گہاؤن[OCR?] کے اراضی زرعی بہی
  شامل اس مندر کے ہے جو ہر فصل میں کاشت ہوتی ہے" — a well with a Persian wheel is working, and about
  ten[OCR?] ghumaon of arable land is also attached to the mandir, cultivated every harvest (p. 200,
  folio 203). A verdant garden with fruit trees surrounds it on all four sides.
- **A Mughal grant of a paisa per shop, and a faqiri gaddi.** Of Jhingar Shah Suthra: **"عالمگیر بادشاہ

  Suthra Shahi faqirs of the whole Punjab know this house as the house of the **گدی** and regard it as
  their **مسند** (p. 203, folio 206). The Lahore samadh is thus the order's seat for the province.
- **Repeated official attempts to demolish a shrine that overlooks the fort.** **"حکام وقت چند بار اسکے
  گرا دینے کے درپے ہو چکے ہیں کیونکہ اسکی دیواروں اور اسکے درختوں کی شاخوں سے اگر کوئی چاہے تو قلعہ

  ہیں"** — the **authorities of the time** have several times set about demolishing it, because from
  its walls and the branches of its trees anyone who wished could get inside the fort; but it was
  passed over each time, because **thousands of people acknowledge the sanctity of this house**. The
  next clause, "کئی بار اس مکان کی قیمت کی فرو دہی میں حکی[OCR?] ہے", appears to concern a price or a
  sale of the house and is too damaged to gloss (p. 203, folio 206). A security objection, not a
  doctrinal one, and popular veneration as the reason it failed.
- **The Suthra establishment's layout and services** (pp. 203–204, folios 206–207): a pukhta enclosure,

  ہے دل و جان سے خدمت کرتا ہے **چڑھاوہ وہی لیتا ہے**" — the mahant has been in office [number OCR?]

- **Cash nazr, jagirs and allowances to a living holy man.** Of Bhai Wasti Ram[OCR?]: "لاکھوں روپیے نقد

  nazr**, and **jagirs and روزینے (daily allowances) were fixed** (p. 201, folio 204). The most direct

  تہی" — two samadhs, one of Jamadar Khushal Singh and the other of his wife, **who was burnt sati

  کوڑہ[OCR?] پتھر کا زمین کے اندر گاڑا ہوا ہے اور ایک لکڑی موٹی جسکو ونڈ[OCR?] کہتے ہیں بنگ کوٹنے کے

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A living entombment, told without comment.** Bawa Shiv Gar "زندہ بیٹہہ گیا تہا" in his own samadh
  and its door was walled up; sitting in it "اُس نے مٹی لے لی", he took the earth (p. 198, folio 201).
  The author reports it as fact, with no marvel attached.
- **A guru with no body.** Guru Arjan "دریا میں بوقت غسل مفقود ہو چکا تھا", disappeared in the river at
  his bath, so that "نعش اسکی جلائی نہیں گئی", his corpse was not cremated, and the samadh is a
  memorial only (p. 206, folio 209).
- **Sanctity acknowledged across communities, and taken as the reason a shrine survived**: Bhai Rupa,
  accepted by "شہر لاہور کے ہندو مسلمان" (p. 198, folio 201); the Suthra house spared because "اس مکان
  کی بزرگی کے ہزاروں لوگ قائل ہیں" (p. 203, folio 206). No karamat story is narrated anywhere in this
  chunk.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author judges buildings as an engineer and mourns them as a moralist.** Of Khushal Singh's
  unfinished samadh: "جسکے دیکہنے سے نہایت افسوس سے آتا ہے", the sight of it brings deep regret,
  followed by a two-line Urdu verse on the passing of "اہل تاج و اہل تخت و اہل زور و اہل شور", men of
  crown, throne, force and clamour, and the dust blowing over their graves (pp. 192–193, folios
  195–196; the verse is OCR-damaged — see Doubts). Of the same building: "اگر یہ مکان اپنی پوری تیاری
  میں تکمیل پاتا تو نہایت عمدہ عالیشان مکان بن جاتا" — had it been completed it would have been a very
  fine grand house (p. 193, folio 196).
- **Solidity as an argument against the need for repair.** Of the Shiv Gar shivala, unrepaired for forty
  years: "حقیقت میں سبب پختگی کے اُسکو مرمت کی حاجت بہی نہیں" — in truth, because of its solid
  construction, it has no need of repair (p. 199, folio 202). The author's own verdict, not a
  custodian's.
- **He twice refers the reader out to his own earlier book** for political narrative he will not repeat
  here: Jawahar Singh's killing, "جسکا مفصل حال تاریخ پنجاب مولفہ راقم میں تحریر ہے" (p. 196,
  folio 199), and Guru Arjan's death, "مندرج کتاب تاریخ پنجاب مولفہ بندہ ہے" (p. 206, folio 209); and
  once inward, to the account of Bhai Wasti Ram's family "اس کتاب کی ابتدا میں خاص شہر کے حال میں"
  (pp. 202–203, folios 205–206). The topographical survey is explicitly built on top of his history.
- **He glosses Punjabi words for his reader** — *bhaura* as a red inclining to black (p. 200,
  folio 203), *suthra* as "پاک و بے لگاؤ", pure and unattached (p. 203, folio 206) — and names local
  usages as usages: "اس مکان کو سکھوں کی دیگ[OCR?] کہتے ہیں" (p. 206, folio 209), "ایک لکڑی … جسکو
  ونڈ[OCR?] کہتے ہیں" (p. 206, folio 209).
- **He imposes his own descriptive vocabulary where the building has none**, repeatedly with "کہنا
  چاہیے", one should call it: "اس برانڈے کے دروازے کو بیرونی دروازہ مندر کا کہنا چاہیے" (p. 199,
  folio 202), "جسکو پانچہوان درجہ کہنا چاہیے" (p. 197, folio 200), "جبکو سہ درہ نشستگاہ کہنا چاہیے"
  (p. 200, folio 203), "جسکو شمالی دیوار کہنا چاہیے" (pp. 199–200, folios 202–203). His terminology is
  his own, and a reader matching his descriptions to standing fabric should know it.
- **A recurring technical vocabulary, flagged by the transcriber almost every time**: "قالبوتی[OCR?]"
  for the vaulting or moulded-form work of nearly every ceiling in the chunk (pp. 192–205), with
  "ریختہ[OCR?] کار" for the exterior finish (pp. 193, 197, 199, 200) and "پختہ چونہ گچ" for lime work
  throughout. Any architectural use of this chunk turns on terms the transcription itself is unsure of.

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the site is left unnamed.** The mandir and shivala described in the
  first paragraph (p. 192, folio 195) — fine building, a great chabutra, a hall housing its servants,
  its expenses formerly met by Sardar Bhagwan Singh — carries **no name in this chunk**; its heading
  falls in the preceding chunk. Do not name it from context, and do not attach its funding fact to any
  named site. The named heading "سماوہ جمعدار خوشحال سنگہ" begins only after it.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** The Guru Arjan samadh description breaks off at "سمادہ کا چبوترہ و
  پختہ چونہ گچ [illegible]" (p. 206, folio 209); anything about that building's interior, its kalas,
  its custodian or its income is in the next chunk, not here.
- **The mapping of "سمادہ گرو ارجن" to gurdwara-dera-sahib needs a human sign-off.** The archive index
  carries **two** Lahore rows whose principal figure is Guru Arjan Dev: `gurdwara-dera-sahib` (location
  given only as "Lahore") and `gurdwara-baoli-sahib-guru-arjan-dev-ji-lahore` (Dabbi Bazaar, inside
  the walled city). The chunk's site is **outside** the city, opposite the Roshnai Gate, with the fort
  wall on the east and Ranjit Singh's samadh on the west (pp. 205–206, folios 208–209), which does not
  fit Dabbi Bazaar — but the chunk also describes **a baoli** at this site (p. 206, folio 209), which is
  exactly the feature that could pull it toward the Baoli Sahib row. I have mapped on the locational
  bearings and flag the collision; the book itself never uses either name.
- **Two Krishna thakurdwaras on the Ravi bank, and one archive row — do not map either.** Rani
  Lachhmi's thakurdwara and the Bhauri Sarkar thakurdwara both stand "نالہ دریاے راوی کے کنارے"
  (pp. 199, 200; folios 202, 203) and both have Sri Krishan and Radha ji as their installed images
  (pp. 200, 201; folios 203, 204). The archive's `krishna-mandir-ravi-road` row is at Ravi Road,
  Lahore, with Krishna as principal figure. **Either, neither or both could be it**; the chunk gives no
  street and no modern name. Left unmapped.
- **"خانقاہ میان وڈا[OCR?]" is left unmapped.** The khanqah stormed with cannon (pp. 194–195, folios
  197–198) is named only by this phrase, itself OCR-flagged. Do **not** attach it to
  `shrine-of-mian-mir` (Alam Ganj / Dharampura) on the strength of "میاں" alone; the chunk gives no
  saint, no order, no location for it beyond the name.
- **An internal contradiction in the Suchet Singh narrative.** The text reads "راجہ ہیرا سنگہ اپنے
  برادر زادہ کے برخلاف یہ جموں سے آیا تہا", making **Hira Singh** the one who came from Jammu against
  his own nephew; but the surrounding sentences make Suchet Singh the uncle who came to displace Hira
  Singh, and quote Hira Singh saying "چچا میری جان کا دشمن بنکر آیا ہے", my **uncle** has come as the
  enemy of my life (pp. 194–195, folios 197–198). One of the two names is wrong on the page or in the
  transcription. A human must read folio 197 from the PDF before this passage is cited.
- **The founder of Jawahar Singh's samadh is named only by an OCR-garbled string.** "اس سماوہ کی
  بنیا[OCR?] درانی[OCR?] خندان[OCR?] درانی والدہ مہاراجہ دلیپ سنگہ سردار جواہر سنگہ کی ہمشیرہ نے
  رکہی" (p. 196, folio 199) identifies her only as **the mother of Maharaja Dalip Singh and sister of
  Sardar Jawahar Singh**; the personal name as printed is unreadable. The heading nine pages later
  gives "رانی جنداں والدہ مہاراجہ دلیپ سنگہ" (p. 205, folio 208), but that is a **different building**
  (the Masti Gate thakurdwara). Do not silently merge the two; record the relationship, not a name.
- **One open ground is printed in five forms and is left unidentified**: "پیر بٹ کے میدان" (p. 197,
  folio 200), "پیرنٹ[OCR?] کی سر زمین" (p. 198, folio 201), "پیرٹ[OCR?] کی سر زمین" (p. 199,
  folio 202), "میدان پریٹ[OCR?]" and "پریٹ[OCR?] کے میدان" (p. 204, folio 207). Four of the five are
  OCR-flagged. At least four buildings in this chunk — Bhai Rupa's samadh, the Shiv Gar shivala, Rani
  Lachhmi's thakurdwara and (by a window view) the Suthra house — are placed on it, so the reading
  matters for locating them all. **Not glossed here**; a human must settle it from the lithograph.
- **A change of transcription hand falls at p. 201 (folio 204).** Up to p. 200 the transcription writes
  "تہا", "بہی", "سانتہہ", "سماوہ", "ٹہاکر دوارہ", "چڑہاوہ"; from p. 201 onward the same words appear as
  "تھا", "بھی", "ساتھ", "سمادہ", "ٹھاکردوارہ", "چڑھاوہ", and the flag density changes with it. The two
  halves are the same text in two orthographies — **search both spellings** when looking for anything
  in this book, and do not treat "سماوہ" and "سمادہ" as different words.
- **Both dates in the chunk are OCR-flagged**: Khushal Singh's death "سنہ ١٨٤٤[OCR?] ع" (p. 192,
  folio 195) and Jawahar Singh's killing "سنہ ١٨٤٧[OCR?] ع کے اخیر میں" (p. 197, folio 200). The only
  other temporal statement is relative and unanchored: "اُسکی وفات کو چالیس برس گزرے ہیں", forty years
  since Shiv Gar's death (p. 199, folio 202), with no date given for the book's present. No Hijri date
  appears anywhere in this chunk.
- **Every quantity in the chunk is flagged or garbled.** "گیارہ[OCR?] شو جی کا استہان ہیں[OCR?]",
  eleven[OCR?] asthans of Shiv ji at Udham Singh's samadh (p. 194, folio 197); "ایکسو[OCR?] درویش" of
  the khanqah (p. 195, folio 198); "آٹہہ[OCR?] گہاؤن[OCR?]" at the Shiv Gar shivala (p. 199,
  folio 202); "قریب دس گہاؤن[OCR?]" at Rani Lachhmi's (p. 200, folio 203); "نہ چار دہ نہ[OCR?] چاہ",
  an unreadable count of wells at Bhauri Sarkar's (p. 201, folio 204); "بیشمار[OCR?] روپیہ" spent on
  Bhai Wasti Ram's samadh (p. 202, folio 205); "ادہیس[OCR?] سال" of the Suthra mahant's tenure
  (p. 204, folio 207); "گیارہ[OCR?] میں بیٹھے" in the baoli description (p. 206, folio 209). The
  unflagged numbers are "پانچ فٹ" plinth (p. 193, folio 196), "تین سیڑہی[OCR?]" steps (p. 193,
  folio 196 — flagged), "بیس قدم" twenty paces (p. 196, folio 199), "ڈیڑہ گز" (p. 200, folio 203),
  "قریب ڈیڑہ سو آدمی" (p. 195, folio 198) and "پچیس پچیس روپے" per soldier (p. 196, folio 199). **No
  land or income figure from this chunk should enter an entry without a check against the lithograph.**
- **Personal names carried with their flags**, each on the word the transcriber flagged: "بہا کی[OCR?]
  روپا" (p. 198, folio 201); "گنیتا[OCR?] نند گر" (p. 199, folio 202); "باوا لچہمن داس باوا[OCR?]

  "ستہرا[OCR?]" (p. 203, folio 206); "رُڑ کی[OCR?] شاہ ستہرا" (p. 204, folio 207); "بہائی[OCR?]" for
  Raja Teja Singh's relationship to Khushal Singh (p. 192, folio 195); "راجہ و میان سنگہ" as printed
  for the wazir (p. 194, folio 197); "بار[OCR?]" among the images at Rani Lachhmi's (p. 200,
  folio 203); "دہ بائنتی[OCR?]" before the Krishan and Radha images at Bhauri Sarkar's (p. 201,
  folio 204).
- **Three passages are too damaged to gloss and should not be paraphrased from context**: the last
  clause about Khushal Singh's unfitted door, "دروازے کو جو دلی[OCR?] کو اڑ[OCR?] دن کی بہی نہیں
  چڑہائی گئی" (p. 192, folio 195); the two-line verse on p. 193 (folio 196), whose rhyme words carry
  four flags ("آئی[OCR?]", "اینکے[OCR?]", "جبکا[OCR?]", "الی[OCR?]"); and "کئی بار اس مکان کی قیمت کی
  فرو دہی میں حکی[OCR?] ہے" in the Suthra demolition passage (p. 203, folio 206), which appears to
  concern a price or sale but cannot be rendered. Two further `[illegible]` gaps fall in the Shiv Gar
  entry (p. 198, folio 201) and in the Guru Arjan bearings (p. 205, folio 208), the latter swallowing
  the distance between that samadh and Maharaja Ranjit Singh's.
- **A possible dropped negative.** Of Bhai Rupa's samadh the text reads "مگر طلائی کلس لگایا گیا" —
  "but a golden kalas was fitted" — where the "مگر" ("but") and the pattern of every other building in
  the chunk suggest a negative has been lost (p. 198, folio 201). Do not assert either way.
- **The chunk carries no library watermark, digitisation stamp or rights line** — nothing to record as
  provenance about the file beyond the `[folio N]` markers and the transcriber's flags.
