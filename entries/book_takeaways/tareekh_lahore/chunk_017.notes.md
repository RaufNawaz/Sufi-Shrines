# tareekh_lahore — chunk 017 — pp. 236–250

**A run of nine building-by-building entries in the eastern suburb of Lahore — the belt between the
Akbari and Delhi gates, Qila Gujar Singh, Begampura and Mughalpura, out along the old Shalamar road.**
The author writes as an engineer: every entry gives orientation of the door, what is *pukhta* (burnt
brick and lime) against what is *kham* (unbaked), ceilings, domes, wells, plinth heights and floor
materials, and then what the Sikh period did to the fabric and what the British period did or did not
repair. Two archive rows take material. The chunk's real weight, though, is institutional: an endowed
teaching khanqah whose intake of blind and disabled pensioners is counted; a five-mela year with an
offerings income; a rauza used as a gunpowder magazine and then restored on a Resident's permission;
a licensed wine still inside a Suhrawardi sub-sect's lodge; and the author's own signature on a
government repair.

**The folio markers in this chunk run three ahead of the PDF index on every page** — p. 236 → folio
239 through p. 250 → folio 253, each marker reading exactly that. Only the printed marker is used
below.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (شیخ محمد میر المشہور میان میر بالا پیر لاہوری — Shaikh Muhammad Mir, famous
  as Mian Mir Bala Pir Lahori, pp. 242–245, folios 245–248):
  - **Named among the great buildings of the city**: "لاہور کی مشہور و معروف عالیشان عمارات میں سے"
    (p. 242, folio 245).
  - **Silsila and pir, as the book gives them.** He was "سلسلہ عالیہ قادریہ میں مریدو خلیفہ شیخ خضر
    سیستانی" — murid and khalifa of **Shaikh Khizr Sistani** in the exalted **Qadiriyya** silsila, "اور
    اُسی سے اس نے تکمیل پائی", and from him he received completion; he then "ہندوستان میں آکر لاہور میں
    سکونت پذیر ہوا", came to Hindustan and settled in Lahore (p. 242, folio 245). The book calls him
    **Lahori** and gives no birthplace and no other nisba (not stated).
  - **Character**: "نہایت درجہ کا عابد و زاہد و متقی و خدا پرست تارک الدنیا", devout, ascetic, pious,
    God-worshipping, a renouncer of the world (p. 242, folio 245).
  - **Death** "سنہ ۱۰۴۵[OCR?] ہجری عہد شاہجہان بادشاہ میں" — in 1045 AH[OCR?], in the era of Emperor
    Shah Jahan (p. 242, folio 245); the chronogram over the tomb door carries the marginal figure
    "۱۰۴۵[OCR?]" and the text says the year of his death is obtained from it (p. 244, folio 247).
  - **A Mughal prince wrote his life.** "سکینتہ الاولیا نام ایک کتاب بزبان فارسی دارا شکوہ شاہجہان
    بادشاہ کے بیٹے نے اسکے حالات میں لکہی ہے جسمیں ہزارون کرامتین اس بزرگ کی درج ہیں" — a book in
    Persian named *Sakinat-ul-Auliya*, written on his circumstances by **Dara Shikoh**, son of Emperor
    Shah Jahan, in which thousands of this elder's karamat are recorded (p. 242, folio 245). No karamat
    from it is retold here.
  - **Patronage, and why.** Dara Shikoh was "پوتا خلیہ[OCR?] میان میر کا" — Mian Mir's
    grand-khalifa[OCR?] — "یعنی ارادت اُسکی شیخ محمد المشہور ملا شاہ کے ہاتہہ پر تہی", that is, his own
    *iradat* was at the hand of **Shaikh Muhammad famous as Mulla Shah**; so he gathered much stone and
    building material, and **first built his own pir's rauza**, "جسکی چار دیواری میں اب موضع میان میر
    آباد ہے", in whose enclosure the village of **Mian Mir** is now settled (p. 242, folio 245). The
    building history that follows is under Practices.
  - **The tomb as it stood when the author measured it** (pp. 243–245, folios 246–248): enclosure
    *pukhta chuna-gach* with qalbuti arches in all four walls, its height[OCR?] "پانچ گز کم[OCR?] نہیں",
    not less than five gaz; door on the **south**; rauza on a grand chabutra "مربع ۱۹ ۔ ۱۹ ذرعہ طول و
    عرض کا", square, 19 by 19 *zar'a*; marble stair on the south; marble slabs[OCR?] set into the four
    faces of the chabutra; stone paving on top; the tomb itself "قدآدم بلند سنگ مرمر کی", of marble to
    a man's height; door panel marble, frame **red stone**; inside, a marble floor inlaid with black
    "سنگ موسی[OCR?]"; three qalbuti arches with stone frames[OCR?] and the door in the fourth, southern
    one; the interior marble work "عمارت دارا شکوہ کے عہد میں بنائی گئی تہی", built in Dara Shikoh's
    era; glass-work with vine pattern and a large mirror on the flanks of the northern arch and in the
    dome ceiling; the mazar on a marble chabutra one foot high, its ta'wiz marble, "جسپر ہمیشہ غلاف
    قیمتی کپڑون کا پڑا رہتا ہے", on which a cover of costly cloths always lies; dome finial (کلس) also
    marble.
  - Nothing in the chunk on his birth year, his family, or a death date within the year beyond the month
    (not stated).
- **langer-makhdoom** (موضع لنگر مخدوم — mauza Langar Makhdum, p. 238, folio 241): named only as the
  **place where Shaikh Muhammad Ismail's teacher lived**, and located on the Chenab: "مخدوم عبدالکریم
  ساکن موضع لنگر مخدوم جو دریائے چناب پر واقع ہے" — **Makhdum Abdul Karim**, resident of mauza Langar
  Makhdum, which lies on the river Chenab (p. 238, folio 241). Ismail became his pupil at five, and it
  was from this pir — "اپنے پیر روشن ضمیر سے" — that he received the khirqa of khilafat in the
  **Suhrawardiyya** silsila (p. 238, folio 241). The chunk gives no shrine, no dates and no other
  figure for the place. **See Doubts: the archive row's principal figure is a different man.**

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **شاہ بلاول — Shah Bilawal**, whose *pukhta* mazar stands in its own enclosure at the centre of a
  khanqah "لاہور سے بجانب شرق بفاصلہ ایک کوس کے", one *kos* east of Lahore, adjoining "راجہ دینا ناتہہ
  کے باغ", Raja Dina Nath's garden, with its gate on the south toward "شالا مار باغ کے پُرانے رستہ", the
  old Shalamar Bagh road (p. 236, folio 239). The chunk opens mid-entry, so the entry's own heading is
  not in it — see Doubts. Condition, land and mela under Practices.
- **مزار گہوڑے شاہ بخاری — Ghore Shah Bukhari** (pp. 236–237, folios 239–240), "اصلی نام سید جہولن شاہ
  بخاری", original name Sayyid Jhulan Shah Bukhari; later in the same entry "جسکا اصلی نام بہا والدین
  تہا", whose original name was Baha-ud-Din (see Doubts). A **three-generation Bukhari line into
  Lahore**: his grandfather **سید عثمان — Sayyid Usman** "پہلے پہلے قصبہ اُچ سے لاہور میں آیا", was the
  first to come from the qasba of **Uch** to Lahore, and "چونکہ اُسکو جہولے[OCR?] یعنی رعشہ کی بیماری
  تہی اس سبب سے اسکا خطاب جہولہ بخاری مقرر ہو گیا" — because he had the *jhule*[OCR?], i.e. the palsy,
  his title was fixed as **Jhula Bukhari**; "اُسکی قبر لاہور کے قلعہ کے اندر ہے", his grave is **inside
  the Fort of Lahore**. His son **سید شاہ محمد — Sayyid Shah Muhammad** was his father's *janishin*;
  Shah Muhammad's son was this Jhulan Shah, called so after his grandfather's title (p. 236, folio 239).
  He was "مادر زاد ولی", a wali from birth, and **died aged five**; death "ماہ ربیع الاول سنہ ۱۰۰۳[OCR?]
  ہجری", in Rabi-ul-Awwal 1003 AH[OCR?] (p. 237, folio 240). His death and the horse offerings are under
  Legends and Practices.
- **شیخ محمد اسمعیل المشہور میان وڈا — Shaikh Muhammad Ismail, famous as Mian Wada** (pp. 237–239,
  folios 240–242), founder of the madrasa a mile south of Shalamar. Born "سال نوسو نا نوین[OCR?] ہجری",
  the year 999 AH[OCR?]; pupil of Makhdum Abdul Karim at five; memorised the Quran, took the outward
  sciences, then the inward, "اور ولایت کے درجے پاس پہنچا", reached the degrees of wilayat, and took the
  khirqa of khilafat in the **Suhrawardiyya**; came to Lahore and founded the madrasa "بیرونی آبادی شہر
  لاہور بمحلہ تیل پورہ[OCR?]", in the outer settlement of the city at mohalla Tel Pura[OCR?], teaching
  Quran, tafsir, hadith and fiqh (p. 238, folio 241). Died "سنہ ۱۰۴۵[OCR?] ہجری" and was buried on the
  spot (p. 239, folio 242). **Origins**: "اصلی وطن … موضع ترکگران[OCR?] علاقہ پوٹہوہار[OCR?] تہا اور قوم
  کا کہہ[OCR?] تہا" — mauza Tarkgaran[OCR?] in the Potohar[OCR?] country, of the Kah[OCR?] qaum; father
  **فتح الدین بن عبدالصمد[OCR?] بن سرفراز خان — Fath-ud-Din b. Abd-us-Samad[OCR?] b. Sarfaraz Khan**,
  also an honoured man; "قدیم زمانہ میں اسکے بزرگ کشتکاری کرتے تہے", in old times his forebears were
  cultivators, but Fath-ud-Din turned to learning and became a scholar of the outward and inward
  sciences; his maqbara is "موضع چہیبہ[OCR?] میں دریا میں چناب کے کنارے پر", at mauza Chhaiba[OCR?] on
  the bank of the Chenab (p. 238, folio 241).
- **Three khalifas buried beside Mian Wada** (p. 238, folio 241): besides Shaikh Ismail's own *kham*
  grave, "دوسری قبر شیخ جان محمد تیسری[OCR?] شیخ نور محمد چوتہی[OCR?] حافظ محمد صالح کی ہے" — the
  second grave **Shaikh Jan Muhammad**, the third[OCR?] **Shaikh Nur Muhammad**, the fourth[OCR?]
  **Hafiz Muhammad Salih**; "یہ تین بزرگ شیخ اسماعیل کے خلیفے تہے", these three were his khalifas.
- **خواجہ خاوند محمود المشہور حضرت ایشان — Khwaja Khawand Mahmud, famous as Hazrat Ishan**
  (pp. 239–242, folios 242–245), the rauza near **موضع بیگم پورہ**, two miles from Lahore, west and
  north of "سڑک شالا باغ[OCR?]". "ذات کا یہ سید اور سلسلہ نقشبندیہ کا فقیر تہا", a Sayyid by descent and
  a faqir of the **Naqshbandi** silsila; home city "شہر بخارا", **Bukhara**; at "ابیس[OCR?] برس کی
  عمر", twenty[OCR?], he set out and reached **Kashmir** by way of "سمرقند و ہرات اور قندہار و کابل";
  stayed years in Kashmir, "لاکہون لوگ مرید ہوے", lakhs became murids. Then the expulsion, the Mughal
  transfer and the Lahore khanqah — under Arguments. Died "سنہ ۱۰۵۲[OCR?] ہجری" and was buried on the
  spot; a chronogram is printed, ending "کہ قطب الاصفیا خاوند محمود" (p. 240, folio 243). **His son بہا
  والدین — Baha-ud-Din** lies in a second *pukhta* grave under the chabutra in the south-east corner of
  the same rauza (p. 241, folio 244). **Not an archive row — see Doubts.**
- **شیخ موسی آہنگر — Shaikh Musa the Blacksmith** (pp. 245–246, folios 248–249), green-domed maqbara
  north of Qila Gujar Singh outside the Akbari gate. "یہ بزرگ لودیہ سلطنت کے وقت زندہ تہا آہنگری کا کام
  کرتا تہا" — he was alive in the time of the **Lodhi** sultanate and worked as a blacksmith; "سلسلہ
  سہروردیہ میں ارادت بخدمت شیخ عبدالجلیل چوتہر[OCR?] بندگی کی تہی", his *iradat* in the
  **Suhrawardiyya** was at the service of Shaikh Abdul Jalil Chauthar[OCR?] Bandagi. Died "۹۲۵[OCR?]
  ہجری" and was buried here (p. 246, folio 249). His blacksmith murids and their urs are under
  Practices; the spindle miracle under Legends. **The woman of the miracle is buried in the same
  enclosure**, by its northern wall, under a small dome now fallen "مگر بقیہ اُسکا موجود ہے", though its
  remains survive (p. 246, folio 249).
- **شیخ چوتہر عبدالجلیل قطب عالم — Shaikh Chauthar Abdul Jalil Qutb-e Alam** (p. 247, folio 250), buried
  "بہت نزدیک" — very near — Shaikh Musa's tomb, in a *tah-khana* (underground chamber) whose door is on
  the west, with a second grave built over the vault's ceiling. Silsila **Suhrawardiyya**; "اب بھی
  ہزارون لوگ اسکے گہر کے مرید ہیں", even now thousands are murids of his house. "سلطان بہلول لودی بادشاہ
  ہند کا یہ داماد و تہا[OCR?]" — he was **son-in-law of Sultan Bahlol Lodi, king of Hind**[OCR?] — and
  from her his son **شیخ ابوالفتح[OCR?] — Shaikh Abul Fath**[OCR?] was born. Died "سنہ ۹۱۰[OCR?] ہجری",
  "اور وہ تاریخ لفظ شیخ سے ہے", and that date is from the word *shaikh*. A very large book, "تذکرہ
  قطبیہ" — *Tazkira-e Qutbiya* — was written on his circumstances and karamat "جو اب تک موجود ہے", which
  survives to this day; it is also the source cited for Shaikh Musa's miracle (pp. 246–247, folios
  249–250).
- **The Rasul Shahi faqirs and نور حسین — Nur Husain** (pp. 247–248, folios 250–251), at a famous house
  outside the **Delhi gate**, north of the Nashta[OCR?] Bazar. "یہ فرقہ خاندان سہروردیہ کی ایک شاخ ہے
  اور رسول شاہ نام اس فرقہ کا مقتدا تہا" — this sect is a **branch of the Suhrawardi family**, and
  **Rasul Shah** was its leader. Dress and rule: "سر پر یہ لوگ کہڑی نہیں باندہتے رومال لپیٹا رکہتے
  ہیں", they tie no *karhi* on the head but wrap a *rumal*; a *tah-band* in place of pyjama; "تمام جسم و
  چہرے پر برا کچہہ[OCR?] ملی رکہتے ہیں", they keep something[OCR?] smeared over the whole body and face;
  and "اس فرقہ کے لوگ لکھنے پڑہنے[OCR?] ہے ضرور ہوتے ہیں کوئی ناخواندہ نہیں ہوتا", the people of this
  sect are necessarily lettered, none is unlettered. The wine still is under Practices. Two men named
  Nur Husain are reported dead in successive clauses, the second "المتخلص بہا", with the pen-name Baha,
  "ایک عمدہ شاعر سخن فہم", a fine poet and connoisseur of verse (p. 248, folio 251) — see Doubts.
- **شاہ محمد غوث قادری — Shah Muhammad Ghaus Qadri** (pp. 248–250, folios 251–253), outside the eastern
  wall "ما بین دروازہ اکبری و دہلی کے", between the Akbari and Delhi gates. "تمام مسلمان لاہور بلکہ تمام

  of outward and inward power; home city "شہر پشاور", **Peshawar**, where his father **سید حسن — Sayyid
  Hasan**'s maqbara is and where his progeny still live; "نسبی شجرہ اس بزرگ کا طرے[OCR?] پیر غوث الاعظم
  کے ساتہہ ملتا ہے", his genealogy joins that of[OCR?] Pir **Ghaus-ul-Azam**; he travelled through all

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A khanqah that owns the ground inside its wall, and a mela shared by Hindus and Muslims.** Of Shah
  Bilawal's khanqah: inside the gate "ایک وسیع میدان آتا ہے یہ زمین اندرون چار دیواری سے متعلق خانقاہ
  ہے" — a wide open ground, and this land inside the enclosure **belongs to the khanqah** (p. 236, folio
  239). Condition: "بسبب نہ ہونے مرمت و عدم خبر گیری کے دیواریں گری ہوئی ہیں اور چار دیواری کے اندر کی
  سب مکانات خستہ و شکستہ ہو رہے ہیں" — for want of repair and of oversight the walls have fallen and all
  the buildings within are decayed and broken. And: "اس خانقاہ برس میں ایک بار ۲۸ شعبان کو میلہ ہوتا ہے
  اس رات لاہور کے ہندو مسلمان بکثرت یہان آتے ہیں اور شوقین آتشبازی[OCR?] اسباب آتشبازی[OCR?] آکر
  چہوڑتے ہیں" — a mela once a year on **28 Shaban**; that night the Hindus and Muslims of Lahore come in
  great numbers, and fireworks[OCR?] enthusiasts come and let off fireworks[OCR?] material (p. 236,
  folio 239). A dated shared festival with a specified pyrotechnic practice.
- **Clay horses by the thousand: an offering in kind, counted, and its residue visible on the fabric.**
  At Ghore Shah's mazar, "ہر سال اس جگہ میلا ہوتا ہے اور اعتقاد مند لوگ حاضر ہو کر چڑہاوا[OCR?] و چڑہاتے
  ہیں گہوڑے گلی اب بھی میلے کے روز ہزارون مزار پر چڑہتے ہیں چنانچہ مزار کی منڈیرون پر گلی گہوڑون کے
  انبار لگے رہتے ہیں" — every year a mela is held here and devotees come and make offerings[OCR?];
  **clay horses even now, on the mela day, are offered at the mazar in thousands**, so that heaps of clay
  horses lie on the mazar's parapets (p. 237, folio 240). The practice is traced in the entry to the
  saint's own childhood, when a real horse and then a clay horse brought "خدمت میں" secured the giver's
  wish (p. 236, folio 239).
- **Resident staff and buildings at Ghore Shah**: "مزار کو متعلق مکانات مسجد و چاہ و چند کوٹہے[OCR?]

  *pukhta*; "گنبد مزار پر نہیں بنا", no dome is built on it; the chabutra abuts the road (p. 237,

- **A teaching khanqah with a working economy, described at the level of a ledger** (Mian Wada's
  madrasa, pp. 238–239, folios 241–242). Inside the *pukhta* enclosure: "بہت سے حجرے درویشون کی سکونت
  کے لئے", many **hujras** for dervishes; "ایک خراس بھی آٹا پیسنے کے لئے بنا ہے جو ہمیشہ جاری رہتا ہے",
  a **kharas** (animal-turned flour mill) that runs continuously; a *pukhta* mosque with a wide well.
  Foundation and completion: built in the **Akbari** era, "سنہ ۱۰۰۰[OCR?] ہجری میں اس مدرس کی تعمیر ختم
  ہوئی", construction finished in 1000 AH[OCR?], commemorated by a chronogram (p. 237, folio 240).
- **A refused tomb.** Of Mian Wada's grave: "اس مقبرہ پر گنبد نہیں بنایا گیا بلکہ قبر بھی خام ہے کیونکہ
  اُنکی وصیت تہی کہ ہماری قبر پختہ نہ بنائی جائے" — no dome was built over this maqbara and even the
  grave is *kham*, **because his will was that our grave should not be built pukhta** (p. 239,
  folio 242). Compare Khawand Mahmud's octagonal rauza a mile away and Mian Mir's marble.
- **Sikh-era patronage of the same madrasa, and a named mujawir.** "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت اس مدرسہ

  حتی الوسع مدد دینی تہی" — in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's time the madrasa's prosperity increased, because
  the nobles of the state and the sardars of the realm all honoured this blessed place and helped as far
  as they could; besides which "میان شرف الدین مجاور و سجادہ نشین خانقاہ نہایت متدین و منظم آدمی تہا",
  **Mian Sharaf-ud-Din, mujawir and sajjada nashin**, was a very religious and orderly man (p. 239,
  folio 242).
- **Welfare intake, counted twice over.** Under the Sikhs: "اندہے لنگڑے اپاہج[OCR?] سو درویش فقیر طالب
  علم سینکڑون اس مدرسہ سے روٹی کپڑا پاتے رہتے تہے" — the blind, the lame, the crippled[OCR?], a hundred
  dervishes, faqirs and students in hundreds kept receiving **bread and clothing** from this madrasa. At
  the time of writing: "فی زمانہ حال تخمیناً دو سو اندہا و لنگڑا اور پانچ درویش اس خانقاہ سے پرورش پاتا
  ہے اور تعلیم قرآن کی سب کو ہوتی ہے اندہون کو قرآن حفظ کرایا جاتا ہے" — at present roughly **two
  hundred blind and lame persons and five dervishes** are maintained by this khanqah; all are taught the
  Quran, and **the blind are made to memorise it** (p. 239, folio 242). A disability-specific charitable
  institution with a headcount and a curriculum.
- **A named waqf of agricultural land, with the produce assigned to running costs.** "میان محمد سلطان
  ٹہیکہ دار نے رکھہ چلو[OCR?] کی زمین میں سے بہت سی زمین اس خانقاہ کے نام وقف کر دی ہوئی ہے جسقدر غلہ
  اس اراضی میں پیدا ہوتا ہے وہ سب اس مدرسہ کے اخراجات میں صرف ہوتا ہے" — **Mian Muhammad Sultan,
  thekadar (contractor)**, has endowed much land out of the land of **Rakh Chalo**[OCR?] in the
  khanqah's name; all the grain produced on it is spent on the madrasa's expenses (p. 239, folio 242).
  The book links this directly to a rise in income: "رونق مدرسہ کی اسکے وقت میں دو چندان ہو گئی آمد بھی
  زیادہ ہو گئی", in the present sajjada nashin's time the madrasa's prosperity doubled and its income
  also increased.
- **Succession in office, British period.** "صاحبان انگریزی کے وقت سے ایا[OCR?] احمد الدین سجادہ نشین
  اس خانقاہ کا ہے یہ شخص بھی اپنے باپ کی طرح حافظ و عالم و فاضل ہے" — from the time of the English
  gentlemen, [?][OCR?] **Ahmad-ud-Din** is sajjada nashin of this khanqah, and like his father he is a
  hafiz, an alim and a *fazil* (p. 239, folio 242). Hereditary succession stated, with scholarly
  qualification as the warrant.
- **A khanqah turned into a battlefield.** "بڑا صدمہ اس مدرسہ پر مہاراجہ دلیپ[OCR?] سنگہ کی سلطنت اور
  راجہ ہیرا سنگہ کی وزارت کے وقت آیا کہ راجہ سوچیت سنگہ جمہون[OCR?] آکر اسمیں فروکش ہوا اور سکہی فوج
  نے راجہ ہیرا سنگہ کے حکم کے بموجب اُسپر حملہ کیا اور وہ مارا گیا مکان کی دیواریں توپیون کے گولون سے
  سمار یہ گئیں[OCR?] خانقاہ کے درویش بہت مارے گئے" — the great blow came in **Maharaja Dalip[OCR?]
  Singh's** reign and **Raja Hira Singh's** wazirship: **Raja Suchet Singh** came from Jammu[OCR?] and
  lodged in it, the **Sikh army** attacked him on Hira Singh's order and he was killed; the building's
  walls were smashed[OCR?] by cannon shot and **many of the khanqah's dervishes were killed** (p. 239,
  folio 242).
- **A shrine built by a descendant who was also a provincial governor, and a quarter settled around
  it.** "نواب زکریا خان خان بہادر نے یہہ مقبرہ بنوایا اور سبب اسکے وہ اسکی اولاد میں تہا اُس نے اس مکان
  کو زیادہ زینت دی اور مسجد موجودہ کی بنا قائم کی اور باغیچہ کو بڑہا دیا اور محلہ مغل پورہ اس مزار
  کے[OCR?] چارون طرف آباد کیا" — **Nawab Zakariya Khan Khan Bahadur** had Khawand Mahmud's maqbara
  built, the reason being that **he was among his descendants**; he adorned it further, laid the
  foundation of the present mosque, enlarged the garden, and **settled mohalla Mughalpura on all four
  sides of the mazar** (p. 241, folio 244). The shrine is thus given as the nucleus of a quarter.
- **Shrine quarter as plunder.** "اور یہ وہ متمول محلہ تہا جبکہ غارت کرکے احمد شاہ درانی کی فوج مالا
  مال ہو گئی تہی اور باقیماندہ شہر کے لوٹنے کی حاجت اُنکو نہیں رہی تہی" — and this was that wealthy
  mohalla by sacking which **Ahmad Shah Durrani's army** became rich, so that they had no need to loot
  the rest of the city (p. 241, folio 244).
- **A rauza used as a powder magazine, stripped for building material, then emptied under the British.**
  "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت میں اس روضہ کے نزدیک سردار گلاب سنگہ پہوندیہ[OCR?] نے چہاونی بنائی جب سے
  یہ عالیجاہ مکان بر باد ہو گیا چار دیواری گرالی[OCR?] گئی باغ اجہڑ گیا مسجد کے صحن کی اینٹیں اور
  فرشون[OCR?] کی اینٹیں خشت فروشون نے نکال لیں روضہ میں جبرنیل گلاب سنگہ نے بارود بہر دی مزار کا سنگ
  مرمر اُتار لیا گیا" — in Ranjit Singh's time **Sardar Gulab Singh Powindia**[OCR?] built a
  **cantonment** beside the rauza, from which time the building was ruined: enclosure pulled
  down[OCR?], garden withered, the bricks of the mosque courtyard and floors[OCR?] carried off by
  **brick-sellers (خشت فروشون)**, **General Gulab Singh filled the rauza with gunpowder**, and the
  mazar's marble was taken off. "چند سال اس متبرک روضہ میں بارود بہری رہی اور قفل بند رہا"; when Sikh
  rule ended and the British era came, the powder was removed "اور دریا میں پہنکوائی[OCR?] گئی", thrown
  into the river, and the rauza emptied (p. 241, folio 244). The same use is reported of **Muhammad
  Salih Sindhi's mosque**: "سکھون کے وقت مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے حکم سے اسمین بارود بہری رہتی تہی", by
  Ranjit Singh's order gunpowder was stored in it (p. 250, folio 253).
- **Restoration by a descendant on a Resident's permission, and the transfer of *tauliyat*.** "سر ہنری
  لارنس صاحب بہادر کی رزیڈنٹی کے وقت خواجہ احمد کشمیری جو خواجہ خاوند محمود صاحب روضہ کی اولاد سے تہا
  لاہور میں آیا اور اُس نے صاحب بہادر سے اجارت لیکر اس جگہ پر قبضہ کیا اور بہت سا روپیہ خرچ کرکے مرمت
  اُس روضہ کی کرائی مزارات دوبارہ بنوائے مسجد کی بھی مرمت کرائی اور تولیت مکان کی محمد بخش صاحب لاہوری
  کے دیکر[OCR?] کشمیر کو چلا گیا" — in **Sir Henry Lawrence's Residency**, **Khwaja Ahmad Kashmiri**, of
  the progeny of the tomb's occupant, came to Lahore, **took permission from the Sahib Bahadur and took
  possession of the place**, spent much money repairing the rauza, rebuilt the mazars, repaired the
  mosque, and **gave the *tauliyat* (trusteeship) of the building to Muhammad Bakhsh Sahib
  Lahori**[OCR?] before going away to Kashmir (pp. 241–242, folios 244–245). And the reason it failed:
  "مگر بسبب اسکے کہ اس مکان کے ضروری اخراجات کے لئے کوئی صورت قائم نہ تہی پہر یہ مکان بدستور ویران ہو
  گیا" — because **no arrangement was established for the building's necessary expenses**, it fell
  desolate again (p. 242, folio 245). An endowment-less restoration, explicitly diagnosed.
- **The author's own hand in the repair.** "اس مقبرہ و مسجد کی مرمت مولف کتاب کی نگرانی میں پہر اب
  سرکار کی طرف سے ہونے کی ہے ورنہ مسجد کی حالت بہت خراب تہی یقین تہا کہ بہت جلد گر جاتی" — the repair of
  this maqbara and mosque is now to be done by **the government under the supervision of the author of
  the book**; otherwise the mosque's condition was very bad and it was certain it would soon have
  collapsed (p. 242, folio 245).
- **Two seats of office built into the fabric at Mian Mir.** On the west of the enclosure "ایک عالیشان

  **special seat of the sajjada nashin** (p. 243, folio 246). The same provision is made at Shah
  Muhammad Ghaus: "بڑی چار دیواری کی غربی حد میں ایک پختہ نشستگاہ سجادہ نشین کے بیٹھنے کے لئے دریچہ دار
  بنی ہوئی ہے", a *pukhta* latticed seat for the sajjada nashin on the western boundary (p. 249,
  folio 252).
- **A mosque built across two reigns, and a building programme interrupted by a succession war.** At
  Mian Mir: "اس مسجد کی کرسی دارا شکوہ کے وقت بنی تہی جسپر پتہر لگا ہے و باقیماندہ تعمیر عالمگیر کے وقت
  ہوئی تہی" — the mosque's plinth was built in Dara Shikoh's time, with stone laid on it, and the rest of
  the construction in Alamgir's time (p. 243, folio 246). The interruption: "اتفاقاً اُسی زمانہ میں
  سلطنت کا انقلاب وقوع میں لگیا[OCR?] شاہجہان بادشاہ کو عالمگیر اورنگ زیب نے قید کر لیا دارا شکوہ مقتول
  ہوا اس سبب سے عمارت اسکی چند سال بند رہی" — the revolution of the sultanate fell in that very time,
  Shah Jahan was imprisoned by Alamgir Aurangzeb and Dara Shikoh was killed, so the building stood
  stopped for some years; finally "متو سلطان[OCR?] اسی خاندان[OCR?] نے عالمگیر کی خدمت میں معروض کر کر
  دوبارہ اسکی تعمیر شروع کرائی اور مکان شکر تیار ہوا مگر اُس قسم کی تیاری کا مکان نہ بنا جس صورت سے
  دارا شکوہ چاہتا تہا" — [a person of][OCR?] the same family petitioned Alamgir and had construction
  restarted, and the building was completed — **but not to the finish Dara Shikoh had intended** (pp.
  242–243, folios 245–246).
- **Five melas a year, an annual urs of two days and two nights, and an offerings income.** At Mian Mir:
  "اس مزار پر سال بہر میں پانچ میلے ہوتے ہیں میں ایک عرس سالیتہ[OCR?] ہوتا ہے اُس روز دو دون اور دو رات
  ہجوم خلقت اللہ کا رہتا ہے اور چار میلے برسات کے موسم میں ہوتی ہیں یعنی چار چہار شنبے جو ماہ ساون میں
  آتے ہیں ہر ایک چہار شنبہ کو یہان میلہ ہوا کرتا ہے" — **five melas in the year**, of which one is the
  annual[OCR?] **urs**, when the crowd stays two days and two nights, and **four are in the rains, on
  the four Wednesdays of the month of Sawan**, a mela on each. "اگرچہ سکھون کے وقت بھی یہان بڑا میلہ ہوتا
  تہا مگر اب میانہ میں رونق زیادہ ہوتی ہے کیونکہ لاہور خاص و چہاوُنی میان میر و دیہات کی خلقت میلے کے

  and caretaker is now **Khurshid Alam, son of Ghulam Muhi-ud-Din Shah, resident at mauza Ratta Piran,
  district Sialkot** — an absentee custodian in another district — with "دو کوٹہیں مقبرہ کے رہنے کے لئے",
  two rooms built for living at the tomb (p. 247, folio 250).
- **A shrine-lodge financed by a Sikh minister and licensed by the British to distil liquor.** Of the
  Rasul Shahi house outside the Delhi gate: "یہ موجودہ مکان راجہ دینا ناتہہ رئیس لاہور نے مہاراجہ رنجیت
  سنگہ کے وقت نور حسین رسول شاہی کو بنوا دیا تہا" — the present building was built for **Nur Husain

  "سکھون کے وقت ہر ایک امیر سردار راجہ مہاراجہ کی آمد و رفت اس مکان میں بہت تہی … اور وہ امیرانہ معاش

  his own livelihood **lordly**. The reason: "اس فرقہ رسول شاہی میں شراب کا پینا ہر ایک عبادت سے مقدم
  الامور ہے دن رات شراب کی بہٹی اس جگہ چڑہی رہتی تہی اور طرح کی مصالح دار شراب کہہوانی جاتی تہی" — in

  set up there and spiced liquors of various kinds were distilled; "پس امراء لوگ جنکو دختر رز سے کمال
  محبت ہوتی تہے اس جگہ دو وقتہ آمد و رفت رکھتے تہے", so nobles who loved the daughter of the vine came

  دیدی کہ یہ لوگ اپنے پینے کے لئے شراب کہہ کہہوا لیا کریں مگر کسی غیر کو غیر اسواسطے[OCR?] کہہو
  اپنینگے[OCR?] تو مجرم ہونگے" — on **Dina Nath's recommendation** the government permitted them to
  distil **for their own drinking only**, on pain of being offenders if they distilled for any outsider;

  on someone's information[OCR?] a suspicion arose in the government's mind, the still was closed, and
  from that day nobody came (pp. 247–248, folios 250–251). A colonial excise decision named as the cause
  of a shrine's collapse in patronage.
- **Municipal clearance of a half-mile belt around the walled city, and shrine demolition inside it.**

  چارون طرف کف و سبت[OCR?] میدان کر دیا جائے عمارتین گرا[OCR?] دی جائیں درخت کٹوا دئے جائیں" — under
  **Kunwar Nau Nihal Singh's** authority it was decided that **half a mile outside Lahore's rampart on
  all four sides** be levelled, buildings pulled down and trees felled; the work began outside the
  **Delhi gate** with an Englishman named "ولا روس[OCR?]" in charge; "شاہ محمد غوث کے مزار کی مسجد بھی
  گر گئی چار دیواری بھی اندرونی و بیرونی مسمار ہوئی", the mosque of Shah Muhammad Ghaus's mazar fell and
  both inner and outer enclosures were demolished (pp. 249–250, folios 252–253). **Protest is recorded
  and its failure noted**: "اس حال کے وقوع سے لاہور کی رعایا ہنود و مسلمان نے بہت واویلا[OCR?] کی اور
  چاہا کہ یہ متبرک مکان گرنے سے بچ جائے مگر کسی نے نہ سنا" — the subjects of Lahore, Hindus and Muslims
  alike, raised a great outcry[OCR?] and wished the blessed building saved, **but no one listened**. The
  sequel and the rebuilding are under Legends.
- **Loss of a western wall to a government garden.** At Shah Muhammad Ghaus: "پہلے چارون دیواریں نہیں
  مگر جبر دور[OCR?] کہ سرکاری باغ متعلقہ دیوان بیجناتھ[OCR?] نہہ شہر کی خندق بہر کر لگا یا گیا تو غربی
  دیوار گرا دی گئی" — when the **government garden belonging to Diwan Baijnath**[OCR?] was laid out by
  filling in the city ditch, the western wall was pulled down; the boundary now adjoins that garden, with
  garden on three sides and a public road on the east (p. 249, folio 252).
- **A mosque built at the shrine by a named donor, with a permanent tank.** "فرش کے غرب کی سمت پختہ
  مسجد گنبد وار غلام نبی کوٹہی[OCR?] وار کی بنائی ہوئی ہے … اور حوض دہ در دہ عرض و طول کا ہمیشہ پر آب
  رہتا ہے" — a *pukhta* domed mosque built by Ghulam Nabi Kothi[OCR?]-war, and a **tank ten by ten**
  which always stays full of water (p. 249, folio 252).
- **A mosque that survived because it was well built.** Of Muhammad Salih Sindhi's mosque: "جب بیرون شہر
  کی آبادی سکھون نے ویران کر دی تو یہ مسجد بسبب پختگی اپنی کے مسماری سے بچ رہی" — when the Sikhs laid
  waste the settlement outside the city, this mosque escaped demolition **because of its own solidity**
  (p. 250, folio 253).
- **Glass and mirror work at Mian Mir paid for in the British period by a European employee.** "یہ شیشہ
  کا کام اب ایک انگریز نے بنوایا تہا جسکا نام بیل[OCR?] صاحب اور گنبد[OCR?] صاحب سوداگر کا نوکر تہا
  باہر کی سفیدی بھی اس نے کرائی تہی" — this glass work had now been done by an Englishman named
  Bell[OCR?] Sahib, servant of [a] merchant[OCR?] Sahib; **he also had the exterior whitewashed**
  (p. 245, folio 248).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A saint of five killed by his father's rebuke, for making divine secrets public.** Ghore Shah's
  entry: when it was reported to Sayyid Shah Muhammad that whoever offered[OCR?] a real or a clay horse
  had his object granted at once, "شاہ محمد کمال غضب میں آیا اور لڑکے کو روبرو بلا کر فرمایا کہ اے
  نامراد اسی عمر میں تو باعث انکشاف اسرار الہی ہے تو اس زندگی سے تیرا مر جانا بہتر ہے بمجرد کہنے اس بات
  کے جہولن شاہ اُسیوقت جان بحق تسلیم ہو گیا" — Shah Muhammad came into utter fury, summoned the boy and
  said, "O luckless one, at this age you are the cause of the disclosure of divine secrets; better that
  you die than live this life"; **at the mere speaking of it Jhulan Shah surrendered his life on the
  spot** and died at five, buried where the mazar now stands (p. 237, folio 240). The offering of clay
  horses at the mela is presented as the continuation of the very practice that killed him.
- **The spindle through the eyes, and the iron turned to gold** — attributed by the book to *Tazkira-e
  Qutbiya* (تذکرہ قطبیہ میں لکہا ہے). A Hindu[OCR?] woman came to Shaikh Musa's smithy to have a *takla*
  (spindle) made[OCR?]; being young and beautiful, she held his gaze — he put the spindle in the forge
  and "خود اُسکے جمال باکمال کی طرف کیفیت وجہ[OCR?] ہوا کہ ایک گہنٹہ گزر گیا", was absorbed in her
  perfect beauty until an hour passed. She rebuked him: "اے شخص تو کیسا آدمی ہے کہ کام کی طرف خیال نہیں
  کرتا اور میرے مُنہ کی طرف آنکہیں پہاڑ پہاڑ کر دیکہہ رہا ہے". At that the Shaikh drew the red-hot
  spindle from the forge and "سل[OCR?] کی طرح دونو آنکہون میں پہیر لیا", passed it through both his
  eyes, saying: "اگر میں نے تجھکو بد نظر سے دیکہا ہے تو میری آنکہیں جل جائیں اور اگر تیری صورت میں میں
  نے مصور ازلی کا جمال دیکھتا تہا تو یہ لوہا سونہ ہو جائے" — if I have looked on you with an evil eye,
  let my eyes burn; and if in your form I was seeing the beauty of the eternal Painter, let this iron
  become gold. "چنانچہ اسیوقت تکلا سونے کا ہو گیا" — the spindle at once became gold; the woman fell at
  his feet and became a murid, and her grave is in the same enclosure (p. 246, folio 249).
- **The fall of Nau Nihal Singh read as the shrine's revenge, and the demolition reversed.** The
  demolition of Shah Muhammad Ghaus's mazar proper was still pending "کہ اُس رات مہاراجہ کہہڑک سنگہ مر
  گیا", when that night **Maharaja Kharak Singh died**, and the work stood stopped for a day[OCR?]; "اُسی
  روز قدرت الہی نے یہ رنگ دکھلایا" — that same day divine power showed this colour: as Kunwar Nau Nihal
  Singh, having cremated his father, reached the fort gate, "بسبب تزلزل[OCR?] آواز توپہائے[OCR?] سلامی
  کے دروازے کی دیوار کی منڈیر گر کر کنور نو نہال سنگہ اور میان اودہم سنگہ کے سر پر آپڑی اور وہ دو نوجوان
  فرمان فرمایان[OCR?] اُسکے صدمہ سے جان بحق تسلیم ہو گئے" — the shaking[OCR?] of the salute guns[OCR?]
  brought the parapet of the gateway wall down on the heads of **Kunwar Nau Nihal Singh and Mian Udham
  Singh**, and the two young rulers died of the blow. "اس واقع کے وقوع سے تمام شہر میں مشہور ہو گیا کہ
  بسبب گرانے مزار شاہ محمد غوث کے یہ صدمہ کنور نو نہال سنگہ پر آیا ہے" — **it became famous throughout
  the city** that this calamity fell on Nau Nihal Singh because of the demolition of Shah Muhammad
  Ghaus's mazar. "فی الفور مسمار شدہ عمارت دوبارہ بنی شروع ہو گئی اور چند سال میں وہی درخت جو کاٹے گئے
  تہے پہر شاخیں پہوٹکر سر سبز ہو گئے اور مکان کی رونق بدستور ہو گئی" — at once the demolished building
  began to be rebuilt, and within a few years the very trees that had been cut put out branches and grew
  green again, and the place's prosperity was as before (p. 250, folio 253). Note the attribution: the
  author reports the city's belief, and separately calls the accident "قدرت الہی".
- **Thousands of karamat asserted but not retold**: of Mian Mir, "جسمیں ہزارون کرامتین اس بزرگ کی درج
  ہیں", thousands are recorded in Dara Shikoh's *Sakinat-ul-Auliya* (p. 242, folio 245); of Abdul Jalil
  Qutb-e Alam, the whole of *Tazkira-e Qutbiya* was written "اسکے حالات و کرامات میں" (p. 247,
  folio 250).
- **Mass conversion as the ground of a political expulsion** — Khawand Mahmud "چونکہ ہزارون شیعہ امامیہ
  مذہب کے لوگ اس نے سنی بنا دیے تہے", because he had made thousands of Shia Imamiya people into Sunnis
  (p. 240, folio 243); see Arguments.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author explains a shrine's Lahore presence by a sectarian expulsion from Kashmir, and names the
  ruler who ordered it.** Of Khawand Mahmud: because of the thousands of Imamiya Shia he had converted,
  "خاندان قوم چاک جنکی[OCR?] حکومت کشمیر میں تہی اور مذہب امامیہ رکھتے تہے اسکے[OCR?] دشمن ہو گئے اور
  حسین چک[OCR?] حاکم کشمیر نے اُسکو بلا کر کہا کہ یہان سے چلے جاؤ ورنہ قتل کئے جاؤگے" — the **Chak**
  family, then ruling Kashmir and of the Imamiya madhhab, became his enemies, and **Husain Chak**[OCR?],
  ruler of Kashmir, summoned him and told him to leave or be killed. "اس نے ایک ماہ کی مہلت چاہی ابہی
  پندرہ دن گزرنے نہ پائے تہے کہ قاسم خان میر بحری[OCR?] اکبری فوج لیکر کشمیر پہنچا اور کشمیر کے علاقہ کی
  حکومت قوم چاک کے ہاتہہ سے جاتی رہی" — he asked a month's grace, and before fifteen days had passed
  **Qasim Khan**, mir-e bahri[OCR?], reached Kashmir with the **Akbari army** and the Chaks lost the
  government. The threat is thus cancelled by the Mughal conquest, and the saint "من بعد یہ چند سال
  کشمیر میں رہا" stayed on some years (p. 240, folio 243). **His move to Lahore is then given as
  imperial devotion, not compulsion**: "جب جہانگیر بادشاہ کشمیر میں گیا تو بفرط اعتقاد وہ اسکو اپنے
  ساتہہ ہند و ستان کو لے آیا" — when **Emperor Jahangir** went to Kashmir, out of excess of devotion he
  **brought him with him to Hindustan**; he stayed long in Agra, Delhi and other cities, then came to
  Lahore, to mohalla Mughal[OCR?]pura, then a populated place outside the city with good air and water,
  and built a khanqah (p. 240, folio 243).
- **The book's causal vocabulary for shrine decay is consistently institutional, not moral.** Three
  times over it names the mechanism: no repair and no oversight (Shah Bilawal, p. 236, folio 239); a
  cantonment beside the building, then brick-sellers and a powder store (Khawand Mahmud, p. 241,
  folio 244); and, most explicitly, restoration failing for want of a revenue arrangement — "بسبب اسکے
  کہ اس مکان کے ضروری اخراجات کے لئے کوئی صورت قائم نہ تہی" (p. 242, folio 245). The author writes as
  the engineer who must cost the repair.
- **A ruler's building is judged against its patron's intention.** Of Mian Mir's tomb, completed under
  Aurangzeb: "اُس قسم کی تیاری کا مکان نہ بنا جس صورت سے دارا شکوہ چاہتا تہا" — the building was not
  finished in the manner Dara Shikoh had wanted (p. 243, folio 246). An aesthetic verdict on a Mughal
  succession, delivered through masonry.
- **Popular belief is reported as belief.** The Nau Nihal Singh episode is given as what "تمام شہر میں
  مشہور ہو گیا", became famous in the whole city, while the accident itself is narrated as "قدرت الہی نے
  یہ رنگ دکھلایا" (p. 250, folio 253). Similarly the wine licence is traced to a named recommendation and
  a named suspicion rather than to divine displeasure (pp. 247–248, folios 250–251).

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and its site is left without a heading.** p. 236 (folio 239) begins in
  mid-sentence, "حال کے موقع پر دفن کیا گیا یہ خانقاہ …", so the entry's own title is in the previous
  chunk. The mazar inside the khanqah is named "مزار شاہ بلاول" (p. 236, folio 239), but whether that is
  also the entry's heading, and whose burial the first clause is describing, cannot be told from this
  chunk. **Do not name the site from context.**
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** The last entry, "مسجد محمد صالح سندہی", breaks off at "انگریزی
  عملداری" (p. 250, folio 253) — the British-period sentence is unfinished and continues in chunk 018.
- **"شاہ بلاول" must not be mapped to shah-noorani-shrine-syed-bilawal-shah-noorani.** The chunk's Shah
  Bilawal is at a khanqah one *kos* east of **Lahore**, by Raja Dina Nath's garden on the old Shalamar
  road (p. 236, folio 239); the archive's row is **Syed Bilawal Shah Noorani** in the Lahoot Lamakan
  valley, **Khuzdar District, Balochistan**. Different province, and the chunk gives no dates, no
  silsila and no figure's name beyond "شاہ بلاول". **Leave unmapped**, and note the shared name as a
  likely future mis-match.
- **The langer-makhdoom mapping is a place-name match only, and the figures do not agree.** The chunk
  names "موضع لنگر مخدوم جو دریائے چناب پر واقع ہے" and its resident pir as **مخدوم عبدالکریم**, Makhdum
  Abdul Karim (p. 238, folio 241); the archive row's principal figure is **Makhdoom Burhan-ud-din**
  (d. 1245), at Langer Makhdoom, Chiniot. The chunk says nothing of Burhan-ud-din, gives no dates for
  the place, and does not call it a shrine. A human should decide whether the two are the same site
  before this is used; the safe reading is that this is the same *place name on the Chenab*, with a
  different saint attached.
- **Ghore Shah Bukhari has two "original names" in one entry.** The heading's figure is "اصلی نام سید
  جہولن شاہ بخاری" (p. 236, folio 239), and three lines later the same man is "جہولن شاہ … جسکا اصلی نام
  بہا والدین تہا", whose original name was Baha-ud-Din. The entry's own explanation is that *Jhulan
  Shah* was his grandfather's epithet transferred to him, which makes the first statement the loose one —
  but the book says both. Record both.
- **Ghore Shah's death year cannot be reconciled with the story as told.** He "پانچ برس کی عمر میں وفات
  پائی", died aged five (p. 237, folio 240), yet the death is dated "ماہ ربیع الاول سنہ ۱۰۰۳[OCR?]
  ہجری" (p. 237, folio 240) with no birth year given (not stated) and no dates at all for his
  grandfather Sayyid Usman or his father Sayyid Shah Muhammad. **The year is OCR-flagged.**
- **Do not attach Ghore Shah Bukhari to shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari.** The chunk's figure is a Bukhari
  Sayyid of Uch descent with a Lahore mazar, which is also how the archive's Mauj Darya row reads; but
  the names, the ages and the place are different, and the chunk never mentions Mauj Darya. **He has no
  archive row; treat him as a candidate for a future one.** The same caution applies to **Shah Muhammad
  Ghaus Qadri** (Lahore, Qadiri, d. 1152 AH[OCR?]) against the archive's other Lahore Qadiri rows, none
  of which the chunk names.
- **Khawand Mahmud has no archive row**, and neither does his son **Baha-ud-Din**, buried in the same
  rauza (p. 241, folio 244). Do **not** attach him to a row on the strength of the Naqshbandi label
  alone; the chunk names no other Naqshbandi site.
- **Abdul Jalil Qutb-e Alam's enclosure date and its chronogram disagree.** The prose says the outer
  enclosure was built "سنہ ۱۲۷۴[OCR?]" (era unstated) and the chronogram printed on the same page carries
  the marginal figure "۱۲۸۵[OCR?]" (p. 247, folio 250) — an eleven-year gap, both figures OCR-flagged,
  with the chronogram's second line itself flagged ("بنا سے[OCR?] از غلام محی و بین گفت[OCR?]").
- **Two men named Nur Husain die in consecutive clauses.** "نور حسین جو ایک نہایت خلیق شیرین زبان آدمی
  تہا وہ بھی مر گیا نور حسین المتخلص بہا جو ایک عمدہ شاعر سخن فہم اس فرقہ میں سے تہا وہ بھی فوت ہو گیا"
  (p. 248, folio 251). Either the second name is an OCR corruption of a different name or the book lists
  two members of the sect; the transcription gives no way to tell.
- **The location of the Rasul Shahi leader's tomb is lost to an `[illegible]`.** "بار[OCR?] اگر وہ اس
  فرقہ کا [illegible] میں سر جہان[OCR?] انکے پیشوا کا مقبرہ ہے" (p. 248, folio 251) — the clause naming
  where their *pesh-wa*'s maqbara stands is broken, with "سر جہان[OCR?]" the only surviving place-like
  word. **A human must re-read this page from the PDF before anything is said about the tomb of Rasul
  Shah.** The preceding sentence about their body-marking is likewise flagged ("برا کچہہ[OCR?]").
- **Mian Mir's death year rests on two OCR-flagged witnesses only**: the prose "سنہ ۱۰۴۵[OCR?] ہجری"

  `[OCR?]` flag — "میان میر مسعود فتر جار خان[OCR?]", "رشک اکسیر شد[OCR?]", "سفر جانب شہر چار
  بکرد[OCR?]", "دلگیر شد[OCR?]", "بفردوس والامیا تمبر شد[OCR?]" (p. 244, folio 247). The chronogram
  cannot be arithmetically checked from this transcription. Note that **Mian Wada's death year is given

  coincidence rather than an OCR repetition.
- **"پوتا خلیہ[OCR?]" is the hinge of the Dara Shikoh patronage and it is flagged.** The text explains
  the phrase itself — "یعنی ارادت اُسکی شیخ محمد المشہور ملا شاہ کے ہاتہہ پر تہی", his *iradat* was at
  Mulla Shah's hand (p. 242, folio 245) — so the sense is a disciple at one remove; the reading of the

- **A garbled clause puts Ranjit Singh inside the Mughal building history of Mian Mir's tomb.** "مہاراجہ
  رنجیت سنگہ نے اس مقبرہ کے قیمتی پتہر اوتر والئے[OCR?] میں پہر اس عالیشان مکان کی بنیاد رکھی" (p. 242,
