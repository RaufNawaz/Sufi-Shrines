# tareekh_lahore — chunk 022 — pp. 307–320

**A continuous run of Mughal-era funerary and garden architecture outside and north of Lahore, narrated
almost entirely as a history of spoliation and repair.** The chunk finishes an entry on the Gulabi Bagh
and the **مقبرہ دائی انگا** (tomb of Dai Anga), then works through **قبور تکیہ انبلی والہ گورستان سادات
گیلانی** (the graves of Takiya Ambli Wala, the graveyard of the Gilani Sayyids) outside Lohari Gate, the
**مقبرہ زیب النسا بیگم** at the village now called Naya Kot / Nawan Kot, **مزار شاہ رستم غازی**, the
**مقبرہ محمد صالح و شیخ عنایت اللہ کمبو**, **مقبرہ سید شاہ کمال سہروردی** and **مزار شیخ شاہ جمال
سہروردی** near Ichhra, **جنگلی چراغ شاہ**, the **مقبرہ نور الدین جہانگیر بادشاہ غازی** on the Ravi with
its adjoining Shahjahani sarai, and it breaks off opening the **مقبرہ آصف جاہ** (pp. 307–320,
folios 310–327). Its value to this project is overwhelmingly **institutional**: stone stripped from tombs
and carted to Amritsar, jagirs and salaried Quran-readers, tombs turned into kitchens, powder magazines,
kothis and railway stores, a Mughal gate let out to tenants by a lambardar, rents taken by a shrine's
"مالک", and two repairs that the author says he himself obtained from the government.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker, but the printed series jumps.** pp. 307–313 carry
folios 310–316, running three ahead of the PDF index; then **p. 314 prints [folio 321]**, not 317, and
pp. 314–320 carry folios 321–327, running seven ahead. Nothing below is extrapolated: each reference uses
only the folio the chunk actually prints on that page. The jump falls exactly where the **مزار شیخ شاہ
جمال سہروردی** entry breaks off after a single damaged line (see Doubts).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal** (the book's "مقبرہ سید شاہ کمال سہروردی", the tomb of Sayyid Shah Kamal
  Suhrawardi):
  - **Location as printed.** "موضع اچہرا اور رانوان کے قریب لاہور بفاصلہ تین میل بجانب جنوب" — near
    **mauza Ichhra (موضع اچہرا)** and **Ranwan (رانوان)**, at a distance of **three miles south** of
    Lahore (p. 313, folio 316).
  - **The saint as the book gives him.** "یہ بزرگ سید تہا اور سہروردی خاندان میں صاحب کرامت و خوارق مشہور
    تہا" — this elder was a **Sayyid** and was famous **in the Suhrawardi family (سہروردی خاندان)** as a
    possessor of **karamat and khawariq (صاحب کرامت و خوارق)**, marvels (p. 313, folio 316). No silsila
    beyond "Suhrawardi family" is named; no pir, no murshid, no genealogy — (not stated). See Doubts: the
    archive row's silsila field reads otherwise.
  - **Death date.** "سنہ ۱۰۸۰[OCR?] ہجری عہد شاہ جہانی میں فوت ہو کر یہاں دفنایا گیا" — he died in
    **1080[OCR?] AH, in the Shah Jahan era (عہد شاہ جہانی)**, and was buried here (p. 313, folio 316).
    The year and the reign are printed together in one clause; both are reported here exactly as printed
    and not reconciled.
  - **Who built the tomb.** "مقبرہ اسکے خاندان کے مریدوں نے بنوایا" — the tomb was built by the **murids
    of his family (خاندان کے مریدوں)**, not by a ruler or a state (p. 313, folio 316).
  - **Fabric.** A pakka, grand brick tomb (پختہ عالیشان مقبرہ خشتی) standing on an **octagonal pakka
    platform**; in its middle the dome is also octagonal, with an arched opening ("محرابی دہیں[OCR?]") in
    each side; inside the tomb a second pakka brick platform on which **Shah Kamal's grave is built
    pakka**; four doors on four sides, "جنہیں سے اب تین بند کر دئے گئے میں اور ایک جنوبی دروازہ کہلا ہے" —
    of which **three have now been closed and one, the southern door, is open** (p. 313, folio 316).
  - **Condition.** "پہلے یہ مکان بہت آباد و تہا و چار دیواری بہت بلند و پختہ بنی ہوئی تہی مگر اب گر گئی
    ہے" — formerly this building was **much frequented / populous (بہت آباد)** and its enclosure wall very
    high and pakka, but **it has now fallen** (p. 313, folio 316). The tomb itself "ابتک بہیج[OCR?] و سالم
    ہو اگر چہ مرمت طلب ہے" — is still sound[OCR?] and whole, though **in need of repair** (p. 313,
    folio 316).
  - **Why it survived, in the author's own judgement.** "اگر شہر کی عمارت کا مقبرہ ہوتا تو گرا لیا جانا" —
    had it been a tomb among the city's buildings, it would have been pulled down (p. 313, folio 316).
    Set this against the demolitions he records page after page in this chunk.
  - Nothing here on urs, mela, offerings, custodianship, endowment or land at this shrine — (not stated).

  - **What the chunk therefore does and does not give.** It gives the shrine's name as "شیخ شاہ جمال

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **مقبرہ دائی انگا and the Gulabi Bagh** (pp. 307–308, folios 310–311; the entry's heading falls before
  this chunk begins — see Doubts): the garden "بہت بڑا تہا" was very large, with **four grand deorhis on
  all four sides (چاروں طرف چار ڈیوڑیاں عالیشان)**, a very wide well and other buildings, and in the
  middle "یہ بارہ دری عالیشان بنائی گئی گویا نمونہ خلد بریں باغ بنا" — this grand **baradari** was built,
  so that the garden became as it were a model of **Khuld-e-Barin**. Of that elaborate complex two things
  survive: one good deorhi, "جسکو گلابی باغ کی ڈیوڑہی کہتے ہیں", called the **deorhi of Gulabi Bagh
  (گلابی باغ کی ڈیوڑہی)**, and this dome, the **tomb of Dai Anga** which stood at the garden's centre;
  "سب عمارتیں تمام سکہ گردون[OCR?] سنے گرا کر خاک میں ملا دیں" — all the buildings were razed by the
  Sikh[OCR?] [gardi, disorder] and levelled with the dust, and "قبر کا تعویذ جو سنگ مرمر کا تہا وہ بہی
  اوکہاڑ لیا گیا", the grave's **marble taʿwiz was torn out** as well (p. 307, folio 310).
  - **How she came to own it.** "چونکہ سلطان بیگم کی دایہ انگا تہی بعد تیاری باغ کے اس نے یہ باغ اسکو بخش
    دیا تہا اور اسی میں اسکی قبر ہوئی" — because she was the **daya/anga (wet-nurse) of Sultan Begum
    (سلطان بیگم)**, after the garden was finished [Sultan Begum] **made a gift of this garden to her**,
    and her grave came to be in it (p. 307, folio 310). An endowment by gift, recorded without a date.
  - **Tilework dated by an elapsed span.** On the deorhi's outer Mughal arched gate "کاشی کا کام ایسا تر و
    تازہ ہوا ہوا ہے با وجود کہ گزر جانے دو سو پینتیس ۲۳۵ برس کے تازہ بتازہ معلوم ہوتا ہے" — the **kashi
    (tile) work** is so fresh that, despite the passing of **two hundred thirty-five, ۲۳۵, years**, it
    appears entirely fresh (p. 307, folio 310). The numeral is printed **without** an [OCR?] flag.
  - **Inscriptions.** On the upper dome's [cornice] the **Kalima Sharif**; on the inscriptions flanking the
    great gate, two Persian couplets, "والی باغ سعادت فاتح باب کرم … باغ چون ارم[OCR?]" and "اہل سنی بر و
    دمشت[OCR?] خواستند از حق دعا بیگ سلطان را الہی دار دایم محترم" — the second praying that **"بیگ
    سلطان" (Beg Sultan)** be kept ever honoured (p. 307, folio 310). Then "اور تاریخی[OCR?] یعنی سہ
    اعمی[OCR?] دو کتبوں میں لکہی ہے", the chronogram[OCR?] is written in two inscriptions, closing
    "**گلابی باغ ۱۰۶۶ شنید تاریخ باغش**" — Gulabi Bagh, **۱۰۶۶**, heard the date of his garden (p. 307,
    folio 310). This numeral too is printed **without** a flag. A further Nastaliq inscription reads
    "محمد عربی کا بر دنے[OCR?] ہر دو سرست کسے کہ خاک درش نیست خاک بر سر اوست".
  - **Fabric.** The tomb proper is **square**, with four small domes at the four corners, three doors on
    each of the four sides, and a **large dome in the middle**; kashi work on both the small domes and the
    dome, "مگر بوسیدہ ہو چکا ہے", but it has decayed; in the tomb's middle storey there was a grave "مگر
    اب مسمار ہو چکی ہے", but it has now been demolished; the outer [gallery] built pakka as a
    ghulam-gardish (p. 307, folio 310; p. 308, folio 311). In the middle storey "آیات قرآنی چاروں طرف خوش
    خط بخط نسخ جلی[OCR?] لکہی ہیں جو ابتک بدقت و ضعف پڑہی بہی جاتی ہیں" — **Quranic verses** on all four
    sides in fine naskh jali[OCR?], still read, with difficulty and faintly (p. 308, folio 311). Its repair
    is under Practices.
- **قبور تکیہ انبلی والہ گورستان سادات گیلانی** (pp. 308–309, folios 311–312): "یہ ایک بڑا احاطہ بیرون
  دروازہ لوہاری کے ہے" — a large enclosure **outside Lohari Gate (بیرون دروازہ لوہاری)**. "بادشاہان اسلام
  کے وقت ان مزارات پہ سنگ مرمر نصب تہا اور نہایت زیب و زینت کا مکان بنا ہوا تہا" — in the time of the
  **Muslim kings (بادشاہان اسلام)** marble was set on these mazars and it was a building of great
  adornment. Its stripping, its later settlement with tenants and its rent are under Practices. What
  remains: "اب اسمیں مزارات کی دو چار دیواریاں اور تین چبوترے پختہ عمارت کے موجود ہیں" — **two enclosure
  walls of the mazars and three platforms** of pakka building (p. 308, folio 311). The graves, as the
  chunk lists them:
  - **سید صوفی (Sayyid Sufi)**, on one platform, "جو اپنے وقت کا ایک بزرگ صاحب عبادت و ریاضت و کرامت تہا",
    an elder of his time, a man of worship, austerity and **karamat**, who "سنہ ۱۰۵۰[OCR?] ہجری میں فوت ہو
    گیا", died in **1050[OCR?] AH** (p. 308, folio 311).
  - On the same platform, "قبور سید عمر و سید ہاشم و سید عبد القادر المشہور شاہ گدا وغیرہ" — the graves of
    **Sayyid Umar**, **Sayyid Hashim**, and **Sayyid Abdul Qadir, known as Shah Gada (المشہور شاہ گدا)**,
    and others; no dates for any of them — (not stated) (p. 308, folio 311).
  - **سید قاسم (Sayyid Qasim)** and a **سید صوفی** with their descendants in the second enclosure; "ان
    قبور پہ ایک عالیشان سنگ مرمر کا گنبد تہا" — over these graves there was a grand **marble dome**, which
    Ranjit Singh pulled down (under Practices); "یہ بزرگ سید قاسم سنہ ۱۰۸۸[OCR?] ہجری میں فوت ہو کر یہاں
    دفنایا گیا" — Sayyid Qasim died in **1088[OCR?] AH** and was buried here (p. 308, folio 311). Note that
    a "سید صوفی" is named on both the first platform and in the second enclosure; the chunk does not say

  - **سید اسماعیل (Sayyid Ismail)** on the third platform, "جسکی اولاد سے پیر محمد شاہ و شاہ سروار[OCR?]
    لاہور میں رہتے تہے اور اب تہوڑے عرصے سے فوت ہو گئے میں" — from whose progeny **Pir Muhammad Shah** and
    **Shah Sarwar[OCR?]** lived in Lahore and **died a short while ago** (p. 308, folio 311). This is the
    chunk's only glimpse of a living descendant line, and it is given as recently extinguished.
  - **سید جعفر (Sayyid Jafar)** on the "جو تہے[OCR?]" (fourth[OCR?]) platform, "جسکی وفات ماہ صفر
    ۱۰۶۵[OCR?] ہجری میں وقوع میں آئی تہی" — whose death occurred in the **month of Safar 1065[OCR?] AH**
    (p. 308, folio 311).
  - **پیر محمد شاہ and his son شاہ سردار** on the fifth platform, "پانچویں چبوترے پر قبر پیر محمد شاہ اور
    اسکے بیٹے شاہ سردار کی ہے" (p. 309, folio 312). Note the son's name is printed **شاہ سردار** here and
    **شاہ سروار[OCR?]** on the previous page (see Doubts). "ان قبور کے علاوہ اور قبور سادات کی بہی اس
    احاطے میں موجود میں" — besides these, other Sayyid graves are also present in this enclosure.
  - **سید نظام الدین گیلانی المشہور پیر بودیاں والے** (Sayyid Nizamuddin Gilani, known as **Pir Budiyan
    Wale**), who "اسکی درستی میں بہت کوشش کی مگر یہ پہلی رونق پہر نہ آیا", made much effort at its repair
    but the former splendour never returned (p. 308, folio 311); he reappears as "پیر نظام الدین مالک",
    Pir Nizamuddin **the owner**, under Practices.
- **زیب النسا بیگم and her tomb at Naya Kot / Nawan Kot** (pp. 309–312, folios 312–315): "زیب النسا بیگم
  اورنگ زیب عالمگیر بادشاہ کی بہن نہایت حسینہ و عالمہ و فاضلہ شاعرہ تہی" — the book calls her the
  **sister (بہن) of Aurangzeb Alamgir Badshah**, most beautiful, learned, accomplished, a poetess;
  "مخفی اسکا تخلص تہا", her takhallus was **Makhfi (مخفی)**; she composed very polished Persian verse, and
  "ابتک اسکا دیوان[OCR?] مقبول طبایع شعرا کے نامدار[OCR?] ہے", her divan[OCR?] is to this day accepted
  among renowned[OCR?] poets (p. 309, folio 312).
  - **Her buildings.** "اسکو عمارات کا شوق بہت تہا بہت سی عمارتیں اس نے لاہور و دہلی وغیرہ مقامات پر
    بنوائیں" — she had a great fondness for building and built many buildings at Lahore, Delhi and other
    places; of Lahore two survive as her memorial: **the gate of her garden, called Chauburji (چوبرجی)**,
    of which "تین مینار اسکے قائم میں اور ایک گر گیا ہے", three minarets stand and one has fallen; and
    this garden, "جسمیں اس نے اپنا مقبرہ بہی اپنی زندگی میں بنوایا تہا", in which **she also built her own
    tomb in her own lifetime** (p. 309, folio 312).
  - **Gates and their present use.** Pakka enclosure and four grand gates; now the **northern gate** and
    the **great eastern gate** survive. Of the eastern great gate: "دروازے کی وسعت اس قدر ہے کہ ہاتہی مع
    عماری اسمیں سے گزر جاتا ہے مگر زمینداروں نے اب اسکو بند کر دیا ہوا ہے" — its width is such that an
    **elephant with its howdah** passes through, but the **zamindars have now closed it**; "یہ دروازہ اب
    ایک لمبردار کے قبضہ میں ہے اور اجلاسہ[OCR?] کرایہ دار رہتے میں" — this gate is **now in the possession
    of a lambardar (لمبردار)** and **rent-paying tenants (کرایہ دار)** live in it (p. 309, folio 312). On
    the eastern wall's corners two burjs "شمالا مار[OCR?] باغ کے برجوں کی طرح میں", like the burjs of
    Shalamar[OCR?] Bagh, still standing; the northern deorhi is an ancient one "جسمیں سے اب گاؤں والوں کی
    آمد ورفت ہے", through which the villagers now come and go, and a small gate on the south.
  - **The village inside the garden.** "نصف باغ میں اب موضع نیا کوٹ آباد ہے کیونکہ مقبرہ زیب النسا بیگم اس
    باغ کے وسط میں تہا اور اب غربی دیوار موضع کے ساتہہ ملحق ہے" — **half the garden is now settled as
    mauza Naya Kot (موضع نیا کوٹ)**, since the tomb stood at the garden's centre, and the western wall now
    adjoins the village (p. 309, folio 312).
  - **The garden at its height.** "یہ باغ عبد شاہان چغتائی میں نہایت آراستہ تہا" — it was most adorned in
    the era of the **Chughtai kings** (printed عبد for عہد); indeed "لاہور کے باغوں میں سے یہ باغ شالامار
    باغ سے دوسرے[OCR?] درجے پر گنا جاتا تہا کیونکہ اسکی مالکہ و بانیہ نہایت زندہ دل شاعرہ عورت تہی" —
    among Lahore's gardens it was counted **second[OCR?] in rank to the Shalamar Bagh**, because its owner
    and founder was a most lively poetess woman; its roads were all of **red stone (سنگ سرخ)**, with tanks,
    [رشتہ[OCR?] اشیئنیں[OCR?]], baradaris, and a **marble rauza, i.e. the tomb, of marble from bottom to
    top**, with a kalas of pure [علاوی[OCR?] — the metal is unrecoverable] set on it (p. 310, folio 313).
  - **Two irreconcilable death dates, side by side on one page.** In prose: "زیب النسا بیگم سنہ ۱۰۸۰ ہجری
    میں فوت کمہ[OCR?] کر اسی مقبرے میں مدفون ہوئی جسکو اس نے اپنے ذوق و شوق سے بنوایا تہا" — she died in
    **۱۰۸۰ (1080) AH**, the numeral printed **without** an [OCR?] flag, and was buried in the very tomb she
    had built out of her own taste and eagerness. Then, immediately, "تاریخ وفات اس پاک دامنہ کی جو کسی
    شاعر نے لکہی ہے عجیب ہے", the chronogram of this chaste woman's death, written by some poet, is
    remarkable — three couplets ending "سال تاریخ نغش[OCR?] از خرد جستم گفت ہاتف کہ ماہ مخفی شد
    **۱۰۹۰[OCR?]**", giving **1090[OCR?]** (p. 310, folio 313). The book prints both; they are kept apart
    here and not reconciled. The couplets themselves: "آہ زیب النسا بحکم قضا ناگہان از نگاہ مخفی شد" and
    "منبع علم و فضل و حسن و جمال ہمچو یوسف بچاہ مخفی شد" — the pun on **مخفی** (Makhfi / hidden) carrying
    the chronogram.
  - **The tomb's fabric and its present state.** Square; each side with three doors, one large arched and
    two small square, i.e. twelve arched openings in all; marble lattice screens (پنجرے) were set in the
    doors; "اب ایک دروازہ شمالی میں جو [illegible] کی لگا کہی[OCR?] ہے اور باقی دروازے کچی دیواروں سے بند
    کر دئے ہوئے میں" — now one northern door [illegible] and **the rest are blocked with kachcha walls**;
    inside, a little of the old flooring remains, "جسکو دیکہکر طبیعت میں ایک وحشت سی پیدا ہوتی ہے", the
    sight of which raises a kind of desolation in the temperament (p. 311, folio 314). At the centre there
    had been "ایک عالیشان چبوترہ سنگ مرمر کا" with a **marble taʿwiz** and, around the platform,
    **man-height marble jalis**, "گویا قبر کے چاروں سمت پردہ کیا ہوا تہا", as if the grave had been
    screened on all four sides; "مگر قبر کی جگہ ایک مٹی کا تودہ پڑا ہے" — **but in the grave's place a

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A garden given as a personal endowment to a wet-nurse.** "چونکہ سلطان بیگم کی دایہ انگا تہی بعد تیاری
  باغ کے اس نے یہ باغ اسکو بخش دیا تہا اور اسی میں اسکی قبر ہوئی" — because she was **Sultan Begum's
  daya/anga**, the garden was **made over to her once finished**, and her grave came to be in it (p. 307,
  folio 310). Ownership of a garden and burial in it are treated as one transaction.
- **A tomb repaired by the government through the author of this book.** "اس مقبرے کی مرمت بہی اب سرکار نے
  بذریعہ مولف کتاب کے کرائی ہے جس سے پہر بارونق ہو گیا ہے" — the repair of the Dai Anga tomb has now been
  carried out by the **sarkar (government) by means of the author of the book (بذریعہ مولف کتاب)**, by
  which it has become lively again (p. 308, folio 311). The author is inside his own record as the channel
  of state conservation money.
- **A second state repair, obtained by the author's report to his superiors.** At Jahangir's tomb: "مولف
  کتاب نے اسکی ریپوٹ حکام بالا دست کے حضور میں کی تو حکم اسکی مرمت کا نافذ بہو گیا اور کہی ہزار روپیہ خرچ
  ہو کر اسکی مرمت ہوئی" — the author of the book made a **report (ریپوٹ) before the higher authorities
  (حکام بالا دست)**, whereupon the **order for repair was issued** and **several thousand rupees** were
  spent on it (p. 319, folio 326). The precipitating defect was the badly decayed upper roof and flooring.
- **A tomb entered on the government's conservation schedule.** Of Zeb-un-Nisa's tomb: "یہ مقبرہ بہی اب
  سرکار کی طرف سے مرمت کیا جائیگا اور نام اسکا نقشہ میں درج ہو چکا ہے" — this tomb too **will now be
  repaired by the government**, and **its name has already been entered in the نقشہ (schedule / list)**
  (p. 312, folio 315). A monument listed but not yet worked on, recorded as of the time of writing.
- **Systematic stripping of marble from graves by Maharaja Ranjit Singh, itemised site by site.** At the
  Gilani Sayyids' graveyard: "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ نے مزارات سے پتہر اتروا لئے جس سے مزارات گر گئے اور مکانات
  بالکل مسمار و برباد ہو گئے" — he had the stones taken down from the mazars, **whereby the mazars
  collapsed** and the buildings were wholly demolished and ruined (p. 308, folio 311); the grand marble

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Karamat asserted but not narrated.** Of Shah Kamal Suhrawardi: "صاحب کرامت و خوارق مشہور تہا", famous
  as a possessor of karamat and marvels (p. 313, folio 316). Of Sayyid Sufi at the Gilani graveyard: "ایک
  بزرگ صاحب عبادت و ریاضت و کرامت تہا", an elder of worship, austerity and karamat (p. 308, folio 311). The
  chunk tells **no** miracle story for either — no incident, no witness, no formula of wonder.
- **A fatwa about rain on graves, given as one of three rival explanations.** "بعض کہتے میں کہ محمد شاہ
  بادشاہ کے عہد میں کسی ملا نے فتوی دیا کہ جب قبر پر بارش نہ ہو وہ رحمت الہی سے محروم ہے اسلئے بادشاہ نے
  چہت اترد اکر[OCR?] قبر کو بے پردہ کر دیا" — **some say** that in **Muhammad Shah Badshah's** era a
  certain **mulla gave a fatwa** that a grave on which no rain falls is deprived of divine mercy, and so
  the emperor had Jahangir's tomb roof taken down and **left the grave uncovered**; when rain began to
  damage the building, a wooden roof was laid by Sobha Singh's order (p. 317, folio 324). Attributed as
  hearsay, and immediately contradicted (see below).
- **A garden praised as a model of paradise, and a tomb that still compels the couplet.** The Gulabi Bagh
  "گویا نمونہ خلد بریں باغ بنا", became as it were a model of Khuld-e-Barin (p. 307, folio 310); of
  Jahangir's tomb, "دیکہتے ہی انسان بے اختیار کہہ دیتا ہے کہ اگر فردوس بر روئے زمین است ہمین است و ہمین است
  و ہمین است" — on seeing it a person involuntarily says, if there is a paradise on the face of the earth,
  it is this, it is this, it is this (p. 316, folio 323).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Three mutually exclusive accounts of one alteration, printed one after another and not chosen between.**
  Of the removal of the marble roof and upper grave at Jahangir's tomb, the author first says plainly "و
  نہیں معلوم کہ کس وقت اتروا لی گئیں", **it is not known when they were taken down** (p. 317, folio 324);
  then gives (i) "بعض کہتے میں", **some say** it was Muhammad Shah on a mulla's fatwa, with Sobha Singh
  later laying the wooden roof; (ii) "بعض بہمر و مسن[OCR?] لوگ کہتے میں کہ سو بہا سنگہ نے ہی چہت کا پتہر
  اتروا دیا اور اسی نے چوبی چہت قائم کی", **some aged[OCR?] people say** Sobha Singh himself removed the
  roof stone and put up the wooden roof; (iii) "بعض کا قول ہے کہ جب مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ نے اس مقبرے کا پتہر
  اتروا دیا تو اسی وقت یہ چہت بہی اتروا لی گئی تہی", **some hold** that it went at the same time as Ranjit
  Singh's general stripping of the tomb (p. 317, folio 324). This is the chunk's clearest demonstration of
  the author's practice: he reports the competing testimonies and leaves them competing.
- **He declines to repeat what is already in books, and states his own subject.** "اس موقع پر کہ تحریر
  حالات مکانات منظور ہوا اعادہ اسکا ضرور نہیں" — since the purpose here is the **description of buildings
  (تحریر حالات مکانات)**, repeating the emperor's life is unnecessary (p. 314, folio 321). A statement of
  the book's scope, made in the one place where the subject is a famous ruler rather than a saint.
- **The regime change is periodised morally, not neutrally.** "جب سلطنت اسلامیہ جاتی رہی اور سکہا شاہی
  تاریکی نے زمانہ کو گہیر لیا" — when the Islamic sultanate passed away and the **darkness of Sikh rule**
  enveloped the age (p. 310, folio 313); the Sikh era arrives with "بادشاہ گردی", the anarchy of kings
  (p. 315, folio 322); and of the British, "گویا اس مکان کی قسمت پلٹی", as it were the building's fortune
  turned (p. 315, folio 322). In this chunk regimes are distinguished almost entirely by whether they take
  stone away or pay to put it back.
- **Distance from the city treated as a cause of survival.** Of Shah Kamal's tomb: "اگر شہر کی عمارت کا
  مقبرہ ہوتا تو گرا لیا جانا" — had it been one of the city's buildings it would have been pulled down
  (p. 313, folio 316). An explicit causal claim about why some monuments survived the Sikh period, offered
  without evidence beyond the pattern the author has been documenting.
- **Aesthetic and emotional judgements carry the descriptions.** The kashi work is "تر و تازہ" after 235
  years (p. 307, folio 310); the old flooring of Zeb-un-Nisa's tomb raises "ایک وحشت سی", a kind of
  desolation (p. 311, folio 314); the tomb is now in a "نہایت ناوید نی[OCR?] حالت", a most wretched[OCR?]
  state (p. 312, folio 315); Jahangir's tomb makes "معمار خرد حیران", the wise builder astonished (p. 314,
  folio 321).
- **A woman patron placed at the centre of two monuments.** Zeb-un-Nisa is credited as owner, founder,
  poetess and builder of her own tomb in her lifetime, and the garden's rank is explained by her character:
  "کیونکہ اسکی مالکہ و بانیہ نہایت زندہ دل شاعرہ عورت تہی" (p. 310, folio 313); she is also named as the
  builder of Shah Rustam Ghazi's tomb and marble grave (p. 312, folio 315). Both her monuments are then
  narrated as having been dismantled by men.

- **Placement of the Ichhra pair.** "مقبرہ سید شاہ کمال سہروردی" is mapped to **shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal**
  (the only Shah Kamal row; the archive locates it near Ichhra, and the chunk places this tomb "موضع اچہرا
  اور رانوان کے قریب"). But **the silsila does not agree**: the book puts him in the **سہروردی خاندان**
  (Suhrawardi family), the archive row's silsila field reads **Qadiri**. Similarly "مزار شیخ شاہ جمال
  سہروردی" is mapped to **shah-jamal** on the strength of the name plus "موضع اچہرا کے علاقے", matching the
  archive row's location — but the chunk gives nothing else to test the identification against, no date and
  no figure's full name, so the match rests on name and locality alone.
- **Zeb-un-Nisa Begum's death year is given twice, incompatibly, on the same page**: the prose has
  "سنہ ۱۰۸۰ ہجری" (unflagged) and the poet's chronogram ends "۱۰۹۰[OCR?]" (p. 310, folio 313). Both are
  above; neither should be preferred without external checking.
- **Jahangir's tomb carries two years**: construction "سنہ ۱۰۳۴[OCR?] ہجری" by Shah Jahan's order (p. 314,
  folio 321) and the grave inscription "سنہ ۱۰۳۷[OCR?]" (p. 316, folio 323). Both flagged, both kept.
- **The Kambo entry contradicts itself about who died first.** "عنایت اللہ نے اسکی وفات کے بعد اسکی تکمیل
  کی" makes **Inayatullah complete Bahar-e-Danish[OCR?] after Muhammad Salih's death** (p. 312, folio 315);
  but the dates state **Inayatullah died first, in 1051[OCR?] AH**, and Salih in **1080[OCR?]**, with Salih
  building the tomb over Inayatullah's grave (p. 313, folio 316). The two cannot both be right; neither is
  corrected here.
- **The numeral ۱۰۸۰ recurs three times in this chunk** for three different people: Zeb-un-Nisa (unflagged,
  p. 310, folio 313), Muhammad Salih (1080[OCR?], p. 313, folio 316) and Shah Kamal (1080[OCR?], p. 313,
  folio 316). Shah Kamal's is additionally printed together with "عہد شاہ جہانی" (the Shah Jahan era) in
  the same clause. Treat all three as unverified.

- **Words and readings that affect facts above.** "علمی استہا[OCR?] اور زیب النسا بیگم کا" (p. 312,
  folio 315) is the only basis for making Shah Rustam Ghazi **Zeb-un-Nisa's teacher**, and the word is
  damaged — the relationship is not secure. "یا نسو[OCR?] آدمی" (p. 315, folio 322) is the only figure for
  the **number of Quran-readers** at Jahangir's tomb and the numeral is unreadable. "دو سو ہاٹہہ[OCR?] فٹ"
  (p. 319, folio 326) and "اکسٹہہ[OCR?] سیڑہیان" (p. 318, folio 325) are likewise the only figures for the
  platform's length and the minaret stairs. "گرا یہ لیبا[OCR?] مقرر کر لیا" (p. 308, folio 311) is read
  here as **fixing a rent (کرایہ)**; the second word is unrecoverable, so the exact arrangement at the
  Gilani graveyard is uncertain. The Sikh occupant of Jahangir's tomb — "اجن[OCR?] جین سنگہ سندہا والی سنگہ
  ملوا[OCR?]" (p. 315, folio 322) — cannot be identified from this text and **should not be guessed**. The
  destination garden of Zeb-un-Nisa's marble is printed "حضور سی[OCR?] باغ" (p. 311, folio 314); the archive
  row **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** is located in the Hazuri Bagh, but the chunk mentions **no samadh
  there and no monument other than an intended marble baradari**, so nothing here has been filed under that
