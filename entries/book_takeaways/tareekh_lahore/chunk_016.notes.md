# tareekh_lahore — chunk 016 — pp. 222–235

**A continuous run of Lahore's non-Muslim religious topography, closing with two Muslim shrines.** The
chunk finishes the Sikh samadh complex beside Maharaja Ranjit Singh's, then walks outward from the city
gates: the Hindu cremation ground outside Taksali Gate, Diwan Ratan Chand's shiwala–tank–sarai outside
Shah Alami Gate, Guru Hargobind's house east of Mozang, the Multani Dharmsala, Tali Sahib (Baba Sri
Chand), Mandir Bhadar Kali at Niaz Beg, Mandir Than Bhairo at Ichhra, and then **خانقاہ مادہو لال حسین**
and **مزار سید شاہ بلاول قادری** (pp. 222–235, folios 225–238). Its value to this project is mostly
**institutional**: per-corpse fees, offering income and who takes it, a daily state stipend stopped, land
revenue remitted, a possession suit dismissed by a Settlement officer, and a Deputy Commissioner turning
an urs into a trade fair.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker**, and in this chunk the printed folio runs three ahead
of the PDF index throughout: p. 222 → folio 225, p. 235 → folio 238, each marker reading exactly that.
The folios below are only the ones the chunk prints; nothing is extrapolated beyond p. 235.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** (the chunk's "سمادہ مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ", named only as the anchor
  for the neighbouring building):
  - **Position and shared platform.** The joint samadh building of Maharaja Kharak Singh and Kunwar Nau
    Nihal Singh "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کی سمادہ سے بجانب غرب بنا ہوا ہے اور اُسی چبوترے کی غربی حد پر جو
    زیر سمادہ مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ ہے" — built to the **west** of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's samadh and on the
    **western edge of the same chabutra that is beneath** Ranjit Singh's samadh (p. 222, folio 225). This
    is the chunk's only statement about the archive row itself: the row's site carries a platform that
    also carries the two later samadhs.
  - **A building sequence dated by reigns.** "بعد وفات مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے بنیاد اس عالیشان مکان کی
    مہاراجہ کہڑک سنگہ کیوقت رکھی گئی تہی" — the foundation of this grand building was laid **after
    Maharaja Ranjit Singh's death, in Maharaja Kharak Singh's time**; work continued somewhat "مہاراجہ
    شیر سنگہ کیوقت", in Maharaja Sher Singh's time; then it stopped "بسبب کشت وجون[OCR?] با ہمی و برچہ
    گردی [OCR?]", because of mutual killing and [OCR?] disorder; and "صاحبان انگریز نے با حتمام[OCR?]
    پہنچایا" — the English gentlemen brought it to completion (pp. 222–223, folios 225–226). One clause
    between the foundation and Sher Singh's reign is unrecoverable ("پہر دور دو مہنگو [OCR?] بایا [OCR?]
    تہا").
  - Nothing here on custodianship, offerings or urs at Ranjit Singh's own samadh; the chunk does not
    describe that monument.
- **gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-mozang** (the book's "مکان جہیٹی [OCR?] بادشاہی یعنی گرو ہر گوبند کا
  مکان", the house of the Sixth[OCR?] Guruship, i.e. Guru Hargobind's house):
  - **Location as printed.** "یہ مکان موضع مزنگ کے شرق کی طرف سر راہ اُس سڑک کے ہے جو جیلخانہ [OCR?] کو
    جاتی ہے" — east of **mauza Mozang (موضع مزنگ)**, on the road that goes to the **jail (جیلخانہ
    [OCR?])** (p. 225, folio 228). This is the only Chhevin Patshahi site in the chunk and the only one
    the chunk places at Mozang; see Doubts on the archive's other Guru Hargobind rows.
  - **Attribution given as Sikh testimony, not as the author's own.** "سکھوں کا بیان ہے کہ اس مکان میں
    گرو ہر گوبند چھٹا گرو سکھوں کا اکثر سکونت رکھتا تھا اس سبب سے اس کو سکھ اسکا ادب کرتے ہیں" — it is
    **the Sikhs' statement** that Guru Hargobind, the sixth Guru of the Sikhs, mostly resided in this
    building, and for that reason the Sikhs honour it (p. 226, folio 229).
  - **Fabric.** A very spacious pakka enclosure; inside on the west "چار پانچ سمام [OCR?]" (four or five
    samadhs[OCR?]) built pakka; within that a second enclosure holding a pakka Persian-wheel well
    (چاہ پختہ چرخی وار) and a **sabil for giving water (سبیل پانی پلانی کی)**, an adjoining small garden,
    a **three-storey house of the gaddi-nashin mahant (مکان سہ منزلہ گدی نشتین[OCR?] مہنت کا)**, two
    kothas and an elaborate lime-plastered chaubara which is the sitting place; a **marble staircase**
    and a plastered brick platform three feet high (p. 225, folio 228).
  - **Mandir Baba Sahib.** Above the platform an arched, railinged doorway "اسی کو مندر بابا صاحب کہتے
    ہیں"; on its walls "تصویر گرو نانک و نانکٹ[OCR?] وغیرہ گروؤن کی کلھی[OCR?] ہوئی ہیں", pictures of
    **Guru Nanak** and نانکٹ[OCR?] and other Gurus are drawn[OCR?]; the ceiling vaulted, mirror-set and
    painted (p. 226, folio 229).
  - **Two Granths under a silk canopy.** In the eastern dalan "دو گرنتھ ہیں جو اب تمام رکھے ہیں اور
    گرنتھوں کے اوپر ریشمی سائبان تنا ہوا ہے" — two Granths, now kept complete, with a silken canopy
    stretched over them (p. 226, folio 229).
  - **Status, income and rite** are under Practices: the place's income goes to the pujari of Guru Arjan's
    samadh, and Sikhs gather on the fifth of every moon (p. 226, folio 229).
- **madho-lal-hussain** (the book's "خانقاہ مادہو لال حسین", with the saint as "لال حسین" and his
  companion as "مادہو"):
  - **Life as the book gives it.** Lal Husain was "ایک [illegible] و سالک اکبری عہد میں لاہور میں رہتا
    تہا", a [illegible] and *salik* living in Lahore in **Akbar's era**; by caste "ڈہو بند[OCR?] بافندہ",
    a weaver; among his forebears one "کمس[OCR?] راے[OCR?]" was a Hindu of Hind who became Muslim in the
    پونی[OCR?] era, and "اُسکا پوتا یہ حسین تہا", this Husain was his grandson (p. 233, folio 236).
  - **Pir.** "شیخ بہلول قادری کا مرید ہوا" — he became the murid of **Shaikh Bahlol Qadri** (p. 233,
    folio 236). See Doubts: the archive row's silsila field reads otherwise.
  - **Why "Lal".** "چونکہ یہ بزرگ سرخ پوشاک رکھتا تہا اس سبب سے لال حسین خطاب پایا" — because this elder
    wore **red clothing** he received the title Lal Husain (p. 233, folio 236).
  - **A named hagiography.** "ایک کتاب منظوم فارسی پیر محمد نام ایک مصنف نے اسکے حال میں لکہی ہے اُسکا
    نام حقیقتہ الفقرا ہے اسمیں ہزار ہا[OCR?] کرامات حسین کی اُس نے چشم دیدہ لکہی ہیں" — an author named
    **Pir Muhammad (پیر محمد)** wrote a **Persian verse book** on his state called **حقیقتہ الفقرا
    (Haqiqat al-Fuqara)**, recording thousands[OCR?] of Husain's karamat **as an eyewitness** (p. 233,
    folio 236).
  - **Madho.** "مادہو ایک خوبصورت لڑکا برہمنون کا تہا جو شاہدرہ کے میں رہتا تہا اُس سے حسین کی کمال محبت
    تہی آخر وہ بھی مسلمان ہوا اور حسین کا مرید ہوا" — Madho was a beautiful Brahmin boy who lived at
    **Shahdara (شاہدرہ)**; Husain had the utmost love for him; in the end he too became Muslim and became
    Husain's murid (p. 233, folio 236).
  - **Dates and a reburial.** "لال حسین سنہ ۱۰۰۸[OCR?] ہجری میں مر گیا اور شاہدرہ کے پاس مدفون ہوا" —
    Lal Husain died in **1008[OCR?] AH** and was buried near Shahdara; some years later the river came
    near the tomb, "مادہو صندوق اپنے مرشد کا وہان سے نکال لایا اور اس جگہ دفن کیا جہان اب مزار بنا ہے",
    Madho brought his murshid's coffin out from there and buried it at the place where the mazar now
    stands; "مادہو سنہ ۱۰۵۷[OCR?] ہجری میں فوت ہو کر اسی جگہ مدفون ہوا", Madho died in **1057[OCR?] AH**
    and was buried in the same place (p. 233, folio 236).
  - **Site.** "یہ خانقاہ موضع باغبانپورہ کے متصل بجانب شمال واقع ہے" — adjoining **mauza Baghbanpura
    (موضع باغبانپورہ)** on the north; pakka enclosure; the great gate used for traffic on the **west**; a
    south gate toward Baghbanpura; a large north gate; and on the east "اسکو بہشتی دروانہ کہتے ہیں" —
    called the **Bihishti Darwaza** (printed دروانہ), "کیونکہ شیخ مادہو حکم فرما چکے ہیں جو کوئی اسکے
    داخل ہوگا بہشتی ہوگا", because Shaikh Madho has decreed that whoever enters it will be of paradise
    (p. 233, folio 236).
  - **The two graves.** In the middle of the enclosure a large lime-plastered platform with pakka lattice
    on the parapets and four waist-high minarets at the corners; on it a second platform bearing "تعویذ
    قبر لال حسین کا پختہ بنا ہے اُسپر ہمیشہ غلاف پڑا رہتا ہے", the pakka taʿwiz of Lal Husain's grave,
    always covered by a **ghilaf**; and on a second platform **to its east**, "قبر مادہو کی پختہ بنی ہوئی
    ہے", Madho's grave, built pakka (p. 234, folio 237).
  - **Condition.** "مگر بے سبب بے خبری و بے مرمتی کے بہت سے مکانات گرے پُرے ہیں چار دیواری بھی اکثر
    مقامات سے گری ہوئی ہے" — for want of attention and repair many buildings have collapsed, and the
    enclosure wall has fallen in many places (p. 234, folio 237).
  - Its two melas, the Maharaja's attendance, the 1880[OCR?] conversion into a trade fair and the
    mujawir's income are all under Practices.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Kunwar Nau Nihal Singh and Maharaja Kharak Singh** (pp. 222–223, folios 225–226): the chunk opens
  mid-episode with a parapet falling at the fort's outer gate, "قلعہ کے بیرونی دروازے روشنائی کے باہر",
  outside the **Roshnai (روشنائی)** gate, crushing the skulls of two persons the chunk does not name (see
  Doubts); "دوسرے روز کنور نو نہال سنگہ کی نعش بھی اُسی موقع پر جلائی گئی جس جگہ مہاراجہ کہڑک سنگہ کی
  جلائی گئی تہی", the next day Nau Nihal Singh's corpse was cremated at the same spot as Kharak Singh's.
  Their joint samadh building is described in detail: oblong, three doors east and three west, one each
  north and south, frames of **marble and red stone**, three internal sections; the north holds Kharak
  Singh's samadh on a brick platform with **four samadhs "ان ستنیوں [OCR?] کی جو مہاراجہ کی نعش کے
  ساتھہ جل گئی تہیں"**, of the women[OCR?] who burned with the Maharaja's corpse; the south holds Nau
  Nihal Singh's brick samadh with **two small samadhs** of the same kind; the middle section is where
  "گرنتہ [OCR?] رکہا جاتا ہے اور پڑہنے بیٹھتے ہیں", the Granth[OCR?] is kept and people sit to read. On
  the roof two octagonal[OCR?] bungalows "آٹھہ آٹھہ پہلو اور آٹھہ آٹھہ دہنے", eight sides and eight
  openings each, under two grand domes with **gilded kalas (طلائی کلس)** — one over each samadh (p. 222,
  folio 225).
- **مکان مرگہٹ, the Hindu cremation ground** (p. 223, folio 226): "دروازہ ٹکسالی شہر لاہور کے باہر بجانب
  غرب واقع ہے", outside **Taksali Gate (دروازہ ٹکسالی)** to the west, where the city's Hindus' dead are
  burnt. Formerly unenclosed; "صاحبان انگریز کے حکم سے بڑا وسیع پختہ احاطہ بنا", a large pakka enclosure
  was built **by order of the English gentlemen**; the east door is for traffic and bodies enter that way;
  the corpse is set down under the **peepal** inside the gate; many peepal and banyan (بڑہ [OCR?]) trees
  and many pakka samadhs stand inside. Its staff and fees are under Practices.
- **شوالہ لالہ نہال چند** (p. 223, folio 226): a pakka shiwala with an oblong pakka dome on the northern
  bank of the tank by the marghat, built by **Lala Nihal Chand (لالہ نہال چند)**; "مسمی نرائن پوری
  سنیاسی اس شوالہ کا پجاری ہے", one **Narain Puri Sanyasi (نرائن پوری سنیاسی)** is its pujari.
- **شوالہ لالہ رتن چند مع تالاب و سرائے** (pp. 223–225, folios 226–228): outside **Shah Alami Gate
  (دروازہ شاہ عالمی)** on the northern bank of the tank; two storeys; ground floor dalans, corner
  kothris, a Persian-wheel well at the north-west corner, and beside the door "دروازہ زنانہ گھاٹ تالاب کا
  جہان پردہ دار عورتیں نہا بین", the door of the tank's **women's ghat** where purdah-observing women
  bathe; above, a courtyard with pakka houses on all four sides "پجاریوں کی سکونت کے لئے", for the
  pujaris' residence, and at the centre a platform carrying the shiwala's mandir, oblong dome, gilded
  kalas, and inside "ایک سنگین چبوترہ اُس پر شو جی کا استہان ہے", a stone platform with the **asthan of
  Shiv ji**. The founder "دیوان رتن چند بانی اس شوالے کا" was "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کیوقت ایک معزز امیر اور
  حضور نویس", an honoured noble and **huzur-navis** in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's time, and under the English
  government "معزز و آنریری مجسٹریٹ شہر لاہور کا رہا", honoured and **Honorary Magistrate of the city of
  Lahore**; he built the building, tank and sarai "بعہد سلطنت انگریزی", in the era of English rule
  (p. 224, folio 227). **His samadh**, north of the tank, was built after his death by his son "لالہ
  بہگوان داس [OCR?]" on a brick-and-lime platform, with three-opening arched verandahs, an east door and
  a small second-storey bungalow over the roof; and on the south bank the same son, "دیوان رتن چند کے
  جانشین و بڑے بیٹے", his **successor and eldest son**, built "ایک عالیشان ٹھاکر دوارہ", a grand
  Thakurdwara, square and two-storeyed, with a very high oblong dome and gilded kalas, where "بہت سے
  ہندو ہر روز وہان جا کر ماتہا ٹیکتے ہیں", many Hindus come daily to bow their heads (p. 225, folio 228).
- **ملتانی دہرم سالہ, the Multani Dharmsala** (pp. 226–227, folios 229–230): outside **Shah Alami Gate**
  to the south, north of a road described through a damaged clause ("سرکاری باغ منسوخہ لالہ دین چند[OCR?]
  والا ہی[OCR?] والے کے گول سڑک[OCR?]"), with a Muslim baker's (نان پز) and a grain-seller's shop to its
  west. Pakka enclosure; along the south wall a pakka kotha for giving water, where "یہاں برہمن پانی
  پلانے والا رہتا ہے", a **Brahmin water-giver** lives; two Persian-wheel wells. In the middle a vaulted
  building with an east door holding "بہیرو جی کا تھان", the **than of Bhairo ji**, and two samadhs, one
  round, of those named **سنبل ناتہہ (Sambhal Nath)** and **بہول[OCR?] ناتہہ (Bhol[OCR?] Nath)**; a domed
  mandir and shiwala against the north wall, which is worshipped; many peepal trees. The name is
  explained: "چونکہ ابتدا اس مکان کی مسمی گرو ہاری[OCR?] ملتانی سے ہوئی ہے اور وہ سنبل ناتہہ کا چیلہ
  شنکر[OCR?] جوگی ہوا تھا اسواسطے اس مکان کو دہرم سالہ ملتانی کہتے ہیں" — because its beginning was with
  one **Guru Hari[OCR?] Multani**, who had become the chela of Sambhal Nath, the Shankar[OCR?] jogi, this
  building is called the Dharmsala Multani (p. 227, folio 230).
- **حال مکان ٹالی صاحب, Tali Sahib** (pp. 227–229, folios 230–232): "یہ سکھوں کا مذہبی متبرک مکان پاگل
  خانہ سے بطرف جنوب ریلوے کے احاطہ کے ساتھہ ملا ہوا ہے" — a **Sikh sacred building south of the lunatic
  asylum (پاگل خانہ), abutting the railway enclosure**; brick enclosure, north and east doors, a
  Persian-wheel well outside; inside, an elaborate building where "گرنتہہ رکھا رہتا ہے", the Granth is
  kept, a dalan for the pujaris, a samadh of **ناگ دیوتا[OCR?] (Nag Devta)**, and at the centre of the
  enclosure the domed **samadh of "بابا اسری چند خلف بابا نانک", Baba Sri Chand, son of Baba Nanak**, on
  an eight-sided brick platform, "پختہ کام نہایت اعلی ہے", the pakka work of very high quality (p. 227,
  folio 230; p. 228, folio 231). The chunk also names, among many samadhs inside the enclosure, that of
  "دیوان کبیا[OCR?] پشاوری", **Diwan Kabia[OCR?] Peshawari**, "ایک امیر کبیر عہد مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ
  تھا", a great noble of Maharaja Ranjit Singh's era who "دیوان خطاب پایا", received the title Diwan —
  the rest of that sentence is unrecoverable (p. 227, folio 230; see Doubts).
- **Baba Sri Chand and the Udasi order, as the book defines it** (p. 228, folio 231): "اگرچہ یہ بزرگ
  سکھوں کے دسوں گروؤن میں شمار نہیں ہوتا مگر بسبب صاحبزادگی کے سکھہ اسکا کمال ادب کرتے ہیں" — although
  this elder is **not counted among the ten Gurus** of the Sikhs, the Sikhs honour him greatly because he
  is a *sahibzada*, and "بدل و جان حاضر ہو کر ماتہا ٹیکتے ہیں", attend with heart and soul to bow their
  heads. "اس بزرگ کا فقر علیحدہ جاری ہوا ہے جسکو اودواسی فقر کہتے ہیں" — a **separate faqr issued from
  this elder, called the Udwasi (اودواسی, i.e. Udasi) faqr**; its faqirs are "لنگوٹ بند", loincloth-bound,
  "سر پر جٹان رکھتے ہیں اور انہیں بالون کو بطور دستار سر پر باندہ لیتے ہیں اوپر کمبل اوڑھتے ہیں", keep
  matted hair, bind it on the head as a turban, and wear a blanket over; "وہ یہی ایک نانک پنتہی فرقہ
  کہلاتا ہے اس سب قوم کا مرجع یہی گرو دوارہ ہے" — this is the sect called **Nanakpanthi**, and this very
  **gurdwara is the marja (resort) of that whole community**.
- **مندر بہدر کالی, Mandir Bhadar Kali** (pp. 229–230, folios 232–233): "لاہور سے چہ میل[OCR?] کے فاصلے
  پر بجانب جنوب متصل موضع نیاز بیگ کے واقع ہے" — six miles[OCR?] south of Lahore, adjoining **mauza Niaz
  Beg (موضع نیاز بیگ)**; "مرجع خاص و عام", a resort of high and low. "اس مقام پر کوئی تصویر نہیں ہے" —
  **there is no image at this place**; a **pindi (پنڈلی[OCR?])** stands at the devi's spot under a small
  vaulted dome. "یہ مکان بہت پرانا ہے جسکی ابتدا کی تشریح کسی کی زبان سے سننے میں نہیں آئی ہر کوئی یہی
  کہتا ہے کہ پرانا مکان ہے" — very old; no account of its beginning has been heard from anyone's tongue;
  everyone only says it is an old building.
- **مندر تہان بہیرو, Mandir Than Bhairo** (pp. 230–233, folios 233–236): "لاہور سے بفاصلہ تین میل[OCR?]
  بجانب جنوب موضع اچہرہ کے متصل واقع ہے" — three miles[OCR?] south of Lahore, adjoining **mauza Ichhra
  (موضع اچہرہ)**; its many buildings "تمام و کمال بعہد سکہی تعمیر ہوئی ہیں", built entirely in the
  **Sikh era**. Before Sikh rule the site held only a kaccha platform with one samadh, worshipped by
  "مسمی گودڑ[OCR?] قوم بنیا", one **Godar[OCR?] of the Bania caste**; on his death his son **Masti Ram
  (مستی رام)** served it, built a brick platform, dug a well and gave the place the form of a building.
  Architecture at pp. 231–232 (folios 234–235): a deorhi so large "کہ ہاتہی مع عماری اُسمیں سے گزر سکتا
  ہے", that an elephant with its howdah can pass through; a building called **"لانگریون[OCR?] والا"** for
  the mela-day langar; a two-storey **"دہونی والا"** in which "دن رات چراغ روشن رہتا ہے کبھی گل نہیں
  ہوتا", a lamp burns day and night and is never extinguished; the mandir itself eight-sided on a
  twelve-sided platform, with eight outer arches, three small domes over each, a kalas above, a **large
  brass bell (روئین گھنٹہ کلان)** hung inside, and a minaret-shaped eight-sided pillar carrying a lamp lit
  day and night; an eastern five-opening dalan with a two-gaz-square lime platform bearing "شیو[OCR?] جی
  کا استہان", the asthan of Shiv ji, where "یہان جا تری[OCR?] لوگ اکثر شیو[OCR?] جی کی پوجا کرتے ہیں",
  yatris[OCR?] often worship; a large dalan "جسکو گدی کا دالان کہتے ہیں", called the dalan of the
  **gaddi**; and a northern enclosure planted with trees of every kind with kothris for the mandir's
  servants and pujaris.
- **مزار سید شاہ بلاول قادری, Shah Bilawal Qadri** (p. 235, folio 238; entry incomplete): "یہ بزرگ اکبری
  و جہانگیری و شاہجہانی عہد میں اپنے وقت کا علامہ تہا" — the allama of his time across the eras of Akbar,
  Jahangir and Shah Jahan. Father **سید عثمان (Sayyid Usman)**, grandfather **سید عیسی (Sayyid Isa)**;
  their earlier residence **ہرات (Herat)**. When "ہمایون بادشاہ" **Humayun** returned to Hind with the aid
  of "شاہ ایران", the Shah of Iran, and regained throne and crown, Sayyid Isa came with him to Hind
  together with Sayyid Usman; "بادشاہ نے قلعہ شیخو پورہ مع متعلقات اُسکی جاگیر میں دیا", the emperor gave
  him the **fort of Sheikhupura (قلعہ شیخو پورہ) with its dependencies in jagir**, and he lived there all
  his life; "ولادت شاہ بلاول کی بھی شیخو پورہ میں ہوئی ہے", Shah Bilawal too was born at Sheikhupura. In
  Akbar's era he settled in Lahore, read the outward sciences with **مولوی ابوالفتح لاہوری (Maulvi Abul
  Fath Lahori)** and completed the inward with **شیخ شمس الدین قادری (Shaikh Shamsuddin Qadri)**, then
  "مسند ہدایت و ارشاد پر بیٹھکر ہزار ہا مخلوق کو فیض پہنچایا", sat on the masnad of guidance and brought
  faiz to thousands. He died "آخر ایک ہزار چہیالیس[OCR?] عہد شاہجہانی میں", in **one thousand forty-six
  [OCR?]**, in Shah Jahan's era — **the era of the numeral is not printed** — and was buried on the bank
  of the river Ravi "اور عالیشان گنبد اسکے مزار پر بنایا گیا", with a grand dome built over his mazar.
  His langar and his exhumation are under Practices and Legends.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A per-corpse fee at the cremation ground, and a government guard.** In the marghat's eastern dalan,
  "میلا رام ٹھیکہ دار کا بنوایا ہوا", built by **Mela Ram the contractor**, "اُسمیں سرکاری سپاہی محافظ
  مرگہٹ کا رہتا ہے" — the **government sepoy who guards the marghat** lives there; and a man, "ایک شخص
  تابا [OCR?] نام کتہری [OCR?] داد و بہنتہیہ [OCR?] قیام پذیر ہے", named Taba[OCR?] with two further
  flagged words, also resides there: **"اُسکو فی مردہ ایک آنہ ملتا ہے وہ ضروری کام مردہ جلانے میں مدد
  دیتا ہے"** — he receives **one anna per corpse** and helps with the necessary work of burning the dead
  (p. 223, folio 226).
- **A private bathing tank with segregated ghats at the cremation ground.** "احاطہ مرگہٹ کے باہر گوشہ شرق
  و جنوب کی سمت کو راے میلا رام نے ابکہ چھوٹا سا پختہ تالاب بنوایا ہوا ہے جسمیں ہمیشہ پر آب رہتا ہے" —
  **Rai Mela Ram** has now built a small pakka tank at the south-east corner outside the enclosure, always
  full of water, in which mourners bathe after the burning; "زنانہ گہہاٹ [OCR?] اسکا الگ اور مردانہ الگ
  ہے", its **women's ghat is separate and the men's separate** (p. 223, folio 226).
- **A shrine's income assigned to another shrine's pujari.** Of Guru Hargobind's house at Mozang:
  "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ کے وقت اس مکان کی بڑی عزت تھی اب متعلق سمادہ گرو ارجن کے یہ مکان بھی ہے **جو آمدنی
  اس جگہ کی وہی شخص لیتا ہے جو گرو ارجن کی سمادہ کا پجاری ہو**" — in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's time this
  building had great honour; now it is attached to the **samadh of Guru Arjan**, and whatever income this
  place has is taken by the same person who is the pujari of Guru Arjan's samadh (p. 226, folio 229). A
  dependent shrine whose revenue follows another shrine's office; see Doubts on which archive row that
  samadh is.
- **A fortnightly rite at Mozang.** "ہر ایک چاندنی پانچویں تاریخ یہاں سکھوں کا مجمع ہوتا ہے کہ پرشاد
  تقسیم کیا جاتا ہے چراغان بھی ہوتا ہے" — on the **fifth date of every moon** a gathering of Sikhs takes
  place here, **prashad is distributed** and there is **chiraghan** (illumination) (p. 226, folio 229).
- **A daily state stipend, stopped, and offerings that replaced it.** At Tali Sahib: "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ
  کے وقت ایک روپیہ یومیہ اس جگہ کے مہنت کو ملتا تہا مگر اب بند ہے" — in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's time the
  mahant of this place received **one rupee a day**, but it is **now stopped**; nevertheless "مہنت آسودہ
  حال ہے کہ چڑہاوے کی آمدنی اچہی ہے", the mahant is comfortably off, because the **offerings income is
  good** (pp. 228–229, folios 231–232).
- **A dated rebuilding, a well, and land lost to the railway.** "بابا اسری[OCR?] کی سمادہ پہلے مختصر سی
  تہی مگر چمنا داس[OCR?] مہنت نے سمبت ۱۸۹۸ بکرمی میں یہ عالیشان مندر بنوایا اور چاہ لگوایا جسپر چرخ چلتا
  تہا اور بہت سی زمین شامل چاہ کے کاشت ہوتی تہی" — Baba Sri[OCR?] Chand's samadh was formerly small, but
  **Chamna Das[OCR?], mahant, built this grand mandir in Sambat 1898 Bikrami (سمبت ۱۸۹۸ بکرمی)** and had
  a well sunk with a wheel, and much land attached to the well was cultivated; "اب وہ زمین ریل کے پڑاؤ
  میں آگئی ہے صرف اٹھارہ کنال زمین شمال کی سمت چار دیواری کی باقی ہے اسکی آب پاشی اسی چاہ سے ہوتی ہے" —
  that land has now **gone into the railway's stopping-ground**, and only **eighteen kanals** remain north
  of the enclosure, irrigated from the same well (p. 228, folio 231). The railway also "اُسکو کسی سبب سے
  کاٹ دیا تہا", cut down for some reason the **tahli/shisham tree (درخت ٹہالی شیشم[OCR?])** present since
  Sri Chand's time, "اسکی پرستش ہوتی تہی", which used to be worshipped and from which the place takes the
  name **Tali Sahib**; its root has greened again and it has taken the form of a tree (p. 229,
  folio 232).
- **The largest mela in Lahore, and its logistics.** Of Bhadar Kali: "ہر سال ماہ باہ[OCR?] جیٹہہ اس مندر
  پر میلا جمع ہوتا ہے" — every year in the month of **Jeth (جیٹہہ)** a mela gathers; most Hindus, women
  and men, come to bow their heads; **"اس میلے سے بڑا کوئی میلا لاہور میں نہیں ہوتا مسلمان بھی اکثر جاتے
  ہیں"** — no mela in Lahore is greater than this, and **Muslims too mostly attend** (p. 229, folio 232).
  Because it falls in the hot season people suffer; relief is provided largely "امراؤ[OCR?] شہر کی طرف
  سے", by the city's nobles: "میلے کے موقع پر شربت اور پانی اور کوٹی ہوئی بہنگ کی چہبیلیں لگ جاتی ہیں اور
  سب کو برابر شربت پلایا جاتا ہے" — **chhabils of sharbat, water and pounded bhang** are set up and
  sharbat is given to all alike; there is little shade and people pitch tents; "ایک رات اور ایک دن برابر
  مجمع اس میلے کا رہتا ہے", the crowd lasts one night and one day; shopkeepers take their shops there in
  advance and prepare sweets (p. 230, folio 233).
- **Amenities built up around a growing shrine.** At Bhadar Kali: "اس مکان پر پہلے کچہہ عمارت نہ تہی اب
  دن بدن رونق بڑہتی جاتی ہے تالاب باغیچہ وغیرہ و عمارت رفاہ عام بنتی جاتی ہیں" — formerly no buildings;
  now its liveliness grows daily, and a **tank, garden and buildings for public benefit (عمارت رفاہ
  عام)** are being built; the offerings "پجاریون کے گزارے کے لئے کافی ہو جاتا ہے", suffice for the
  pujaris' livelihood, and besides, Hindus serve them "بدل و جان" and "انکی خدمت کو اپنا فخر جانتے ہیں",
  count that service their pride (p. 230, folio 233).
- **A cure repaid in building materials.** At Than Bhairo, Jawala Nath "سے موران طوایف[OCR?] معشوقہ
  مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کی والدہ کا آسیب دور کیا تو موران نے ایک سو گاڑی اینٹہون کی اُسکو دی اور زر نقد بھی
  دیا اُس نے بہت سے مکانات اس جگہ بنائے" — when he removed the *asaib* (possession) of the mother of
  **Moran**, the courtesan[OCR?] beloved of Maharaja Ranjit Singh, Moran gave him **one hundred cartloads
  of bricks** and cash as well, with which he built many buildings here (p. 230, folio 233).
- **State officers as shrine patrons.** The present pakka mandir of Bhairo ji was built by "لالہ دیا[OCR?]
  چند رام چند برادر و برادرزادہ[OCR?] سانون مل ناظم ملتان", Lala Diya[OCR?] Chand and Ram Chand, brother
  and nephew[OCR?] of **Sawan Mal, Nazim of Multan**; the great deorhi is associated with "راجہ لہل[OCR?]
  سنگہ" (p. 230, folio 233); and the square pakka tank east of the mandir is "دیوان مول راج ناظم ملتان کا
  بنوایا ہوا", built by **Diwan Mul Raj, Nazim of Multan** (p. 232, folio 235). The large well with two
  wheels inside was built by "بابا جوالا ناتہہ مہنت", Baba Jawala Nath, mahant (p. 231, folio 234).
- **A custodianship dispute that reached a British Settlement court, and was lost by the claimants.** At
  Than Bhairo: "اب اس مکان پر قدیمی قابضان کا قبضہ نہیں ہے فقرا سے جوگی کا قبضہ ہے" — the **ancient
  occupants (قدیمی قابضان)** no longer hold the place; possession is with a **Jogi**; the first was
  "دہنی ناتہہ جوگی", **Dhani Nath Jogi**, whom "جوالا ناتہہ نے اس جگہ تقرر کیا" Jawala Nath appointed
  here in **سمت ۱۸۸۰[OCR?] بکرمی (Sambat 1880[OCR?] Bikrami)**. Dhani Nath "نے لاہور کے سکہی امرا سے
  اختلاط پیدا کر لیا اور قدیم قابضان کو پنجاہ روپیہ سال دینا[OCR?] کر دیا اور خود قابض مکان بنا" — built
  up connections with **the Sikh nobles of Lahore**, fixed **fifty rupees a year (پنجاہ روپیہ سال)** to be
  paid to the ancient occupants, and made himself the possessor. His successor on the gaddi, **رام ناتہہ
  (Ram Nath)**, paid the fifty rupees for a few years and then stopped it; "اب بھی قابضان حال کی اولاد
  لاہور میں موجود ہے", their descendants are still in Lahore. "سرکار انگریزی کے عہد میں بھی بند و بست کے
  محکمہ میں اس بات کا دعوی قابضان قدیم کی طرف سے دائر ہوا اور بدری داس مدعی نے چند ماہ مقدمہ کیا اور
  پنڈت من پہول[OCR?] حاکم بند و بست بہر سر موقع گیا" — in the **English government's** time the ancient
  occupants filed a claim in the **Settlement department (بند و بست کے محکمہ)**; the plaintiff **بدری
  داس (Badri Das)** litigated some months and the Settlement officer **پنڈت من پہول[OCR?] (Pandit Man
  Phul[OCR?])** went to the spot; **"لیکن جوگیون کے قبضہ کی شکست عمل میں نہ آئی اور دعوی ڈسمس ہوا"** —
  but the Jogis' possession was not broken and **the claim was dismissed** (p. 231, folio 234).
- **Mela-day langar, weekly pilgrimage, offerings and remitted land at Than Bhairo.** "ایک پختہ مکان
  لانگریون[OCR?] والا کہلاتا ہے یعنی اس جگہ میلے کے روز لنگر لگایا جاتا ہے" — one pakka building is
  called the langar-keepers'[OCR?], i.e. **langar is served there on mela day** (p. 231, folio 234). "بڑا
  میلا تو اس مندر پر ماہ بہادوں کے نو چند سے اتوار کو ہوتا ہے" — the **great mela** falls on the **Sunday
  of the ninth[?] of the month of Bhadon (ماہ بہادوں)**; that day brings "نذر و نیاز", nazr and niaz, and
  the devout keep vigil all night "جاگتے اور بہجن سنتے ہیں", listening to bhajans; besides, "ہر اتوار کے
  روز سینکڑون لوگ شہر لاہور سے یہان آکر ماتہا ٹیکتے ہیں", **every Sunday hundreds** come from Lahore city
  to bow their heads. "سالانہ میلے کا چڑہاوہ بہت ہوتا ہے اٹہویں[OCR?] روز[OCR?] کا چڑہاوہ بھی پجاری ہی
  لیتے ہیں جس سے اُنکا گزارہ بخوبی ہوتا ہے" — the annual mela's offerings are large, and the offerings of
  the eighth[OCR?] day[OCR?] too are taken by the **pujaris**, on which they live well. **"اور ایک چاہ مع
  زمین بارہ گہماؤن[OCR?] کے جو اسکے پاس ہے مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت سے معاف ہے"** — a well with
  **twelve ghumaon[OCR?] of land** beside it has been **revenue-free (معاف) since Maharaja Ranjit Singh's
  time**. "میلے کے روز جو فقرا جوگی جمع ہوتی ہیں اُنکو کہانا مندر کا مہنت دیتا ہے اور جو دنیا دار جاتے
  ہیں وہ اپنا کہانا آپ پکا کر کہاتے ہیں" — on mela day the **mandir's mahant feeds the faqirs and jogis**
  who gather, while lay visitors cook their own food (p. 232, folio 235).
- **A markets-and-water complex attached to a temple foundation.** At Diwan Ratan Chand's sarai: rooms on
  all four sides "مسافرون کے اُترنے کے لئے", for travellers; a grand deorhi and gate on the north; two
  pakka shops flanking the gate; many shops along the tank bank on the west, "سب میں بیو پاری رہتے ہیں",
  all occupied by traders; "باہر کا مال ہر قسم کا نمک کہی و تیل و غلہ وغیرہ یہان آ کر فروخت ہوتا ہے اور
  بڑی منڈی سی لگتی ہے" — outside goods of every kind, **salt, [کہی — ghee?], oil and grain**, are brought
  and sold here and **a great mandi assembles**. The tank is "بہت وسیع پختہ بنا ہوا ہے نہر کے پانی سے پر
  آب کیا جاتا ہے", very large and pakka, **filled from canal water (نہر کے پانی)**, with steps, four
  corner burjs and an enclosing pakka wall (pp. 224–225, folios 227–228). A damaged clause at p. 225
  (folio 228) appears to add that a mela stays there all day ("تمام دن ہا میلا لگا رہتا ہے"), but the
  words around it are illegible and flagged; see Doubts.
- **The Basant mela at Madho Lal Husain, and a Maharaja's annual offering.** "اس خانقاہ پر سال بھر میں دو
  مرتبہ بڑا بہاری میلہ ہوتا ہے ایک تو بروز بسنت میلہ ہوتا ہے اور ہندو مسلمان اس میلے میں آتے ہیں" — twice
  a year a great spring mela; the first **on the day of Basant (بروز بسنت)**, attended by **Hindus and
  Muslims**. "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت مہاراجہ خود بسنت کے روز یہان آکر دیدار کرتا تہا" — in Maharaja
  Ranjit Singh's time the Maharaja came in person on Basant day; a **silver bungalow (چاندی کا بنگلہ)**
  was set up north and west of the enclosure for his court; "قلعہ لاہور سے اس خانقاہ تک دو طرفہ فوج
  پرا[OCR?] باندہ کر کٹہری نہو[OCR?] جاتی تہی", troops were drawn up in a double line from **Lahore Fort**
  to the khanqah with a railing[OCR?]; he rode out in state, held a darbar in the bungalow, "تمام امرا سے
  دربار سے نذریں لیتا اور خلعتیں دیتا", took **nazrs** from all the nobles and gave **khilats**, and
  **"پانسو[OCR?] روپیہ خانقاہ پر چڑہاتا تہا"** — offered **five hundred[OCR?] rupees at the khanqah**.
  "اب جب سے سکہی سلطنت جاتی رہی ہے عام میلہ ہوتا ہے" — now that the Sikh kingdom is gone it is an
  ordinary public mela (p. 234, folio 237).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The falling parapet read as retribution.** After the two deaths outside the Roshnai gate the author
  sets a Persian couplet: "از مکافات عمل غافل مشو ہو [OCR?] گندم از گندم بروبید [OCR?] جو ز جو" — be not
  heedless of the recompense of deeds; from wheat wheat is reaped, from barley barley (p. 222,
  folio 225).
- **The Devi who refused the larger dome.** At Bhadar Kali, a great dome was built beside the small one in
  Sikh times, "مگر دیوی جی نے اسمیں جانے سے انکار کیا اور پجاریون کو خواب میں اشارہ ہوا کہ ہم اسی چہوٹے
  گنبد میں خوش ہیں ہم بڑے گنبد میں نہیں جاتے" — but the **Devi ji refused to go into it** and gave the
  pujaris a sign in a dream that she is happy in the small dome and will not go into the big one; the
  order was obeyed, the Devi remains in the small dome, and the grand dome "اسی طرح بیکار ہے پڑا ہے",
  lies useless, with "متفرق[OCR?] حساب اسمیں پڑا رہتا ہے", miscellaneous[OCR?] stores kept in it (p. 229,
  folio 232).
- **The snake that asked to be worshipped — told by the mahant.** "اسکا عجیب طرح کا فسانہ مہنت کی زبانی
  سنا گیا" — a strange tale heard **from the mahant's own mouth** at Tali Sahib: about a hundred years
  ago a long snake came out of this building and harmed people; the hard-pressed mahant began placating
  it, leaving out a bowl of milk which it drank; at last the snake relented and came in his dream:
  "تو یہاں مکان بنا کر اس جگہ پر پرستش کیا کرے اور ناگ دیوتا اسکا نام رکھیو تو تجھکو کوئی غم نہ ہو[OCR?]
  … بلکہ جو کوئی صدق دل سے ہماری پوجا کریگا جو مقصود مانگیگا حاصل ہوگا" — build a house and worship here
  and name it **Nag Devta**, and you will have no sorrow and we will do no further harm; rather whoever
  worships us with a sincere heart will obtain what he asks. "چنانچہ مہنت نے فی الفور حکم کی تعمیل کی
  اور یہ سمادہ بنوادی اُس روز سے برابر اسکی پوجا ہوتی ہے" — the mahant complied at once, had this samadh
  built, and it has been worshipped ever since (p. 228, folio 231).
- **The Bihishti Darwaza's promise.** "کیونکہ شیخ مادہو حکم فرما چکے ہیں جو کوئی اسکے داخل ہوگا بہشتی
  ہوگا" — because Shaikh Madho has decreed that whoever enters the eastern gate will be of paradise
  (p. 233, folio 236).
- **An incorrupt body after two hundred years.** Of Shah Bilawal: "دو سو برس کے بعد یہ تابوت زمین سے نکلا
  مگر نعش کی رنگت ہرگز بدلی نہ تہی اور معلوم ہوتا تہا کہ اسی وقت یہ شخص فوت ہوا ہو چنانچہ دوبارہ جنازہ
  کی نماز پڑہی گئی" — **after two hundred years** the coffin came out of the earth, yet the corpse's
  colour had not changed at all and it seemed the man had died that very moment; so **the funeral prayer
  was read a second time** — and the chunk breaks off here (p. 235, folio 238).
- Of Lal Husain the book says only that "کرامات[OCR?] اسکی مسلمانون میں بہت مشہور ہیں", his karamat[OCR?]
  are very famous among Muslims, and refers the reader to *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* for thousands of them
  (p. 233, folio 236); the chunk narrates none of them.
- **A cure of possession as a shrine's founding event**: Jawala Nath's removal of the *asaib* from Moran's
  mother, which paid for the buildings (p. 230, folio 233) — reported as fact, without a formula of
  wonder.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author distinguishes testimony from his own assertion.** The Guru Hargobind residence is given as
  "سکھوں کا بیان ہے کہ", the Sikhs' statement (p. 226, folio 229); the Nag Devta story as "مہنت کی زبانی
  سنا گیا", heard from the mahant (p. 228, folio 231). By contrast the building histories, fees and
  stipends are stated flatly in his own voice.
- **He admits when a site's origin is unrecoverable.** Of Bhadar Kali: "جسکی ابتدا کی تشریح کسی کی زبان
  سے سننے میں نہیں آئی ہر کوئی یہی کہتا ہے کہ پرانا مکان ہے" — no explanation of its beginning has been
  heard from anyone; everyone only says it is old (p. 229, folio 232). Set against the Multani Dharmsala,
  whose name he does explain from a founder and his chela (p. 227, folio 230), and Than Bhairo, whose
  pre-Sikh worshipper he names down to caste (p. 230, folio 233).
- **A superlative claim about Lahore's religious calendar**: "اس میلے سے بڑا کوئی میلا لاہور میں نہیں
  ہوتا" — no mela in Lahore is bigger than Bhadar Kali's Jeth mela (p. 229, folio 232). The author is a
  Lahore official writing about his own city; the claim is his, unsupported by figures.
- **Buildings judged aesthetically and morally.** Of the Thakurdwara: "ٹھاکر دوارہ ایسا عمدہ بنایا گیا
  جسکے دیکھنے سے انسان کی طبیعت بشاش ہو جاتی ہے" — built so finely that the sight gladdens a person's
  temperament (p. 225, folio 228). Of Ratan Chand: he built these works "اور اپنی یادگار زمانہ نا پائیدار
  میں چھوڑ گیا", and left his memorial behind in this impermanent age (p. 224, folio 227). Of Madho Lal
  Husain: the collapse is blamed on "بے خبری و بے مرمتی", neglect and want of repair (p. 234, folio 237).
- **The Sikh era and the British era are both used as dating frames**, without comment: "بعہد سکہی",
  "سکھی وقت میں", "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت", "سرکار انگریزی کے عہد میں", "بعہد سلطنت انگریزی" (pp. 224,
  229, 230, 231, folios 227, 232, 233, 234). Changes of regime appear in this chunk mainly as changes in
  who pays: stipends stopped, jagirs remitted, suits dismissed, melas re-scheduled.

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the two dead are unnamed.** p. 222 (folio 225) begins in the middle of a
  sentence: the parapet "ناگہان دو نو کے اوپر گر گئی اور دو نو کے مغز پاش پاشی ہو گئے", fell suddenly on
  **the two of them** and shattered both their skulls, outside the fort's Roshnai gate. **The chunk never
  names these two persons**; only the next sentence names Nau Nihal Singh, as the body cremated the
  following day. Do not infer who they were from context — the naming is in the previous chunk.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** The Shah Bilawal Qadri account breaks off at "چنانچہ دوبارہ جنازہ کی
  نماز پڑہی گئی اور" (p. 235, folio 238). **Where the coffin was reburied, and where the present mazar
  stands, are not in this chunk** — the archive must take that from chunk 017, not from here.
- **"سمادہ گرو ارجن" cannot be mapped from this chunk.** The income of the Mozang building goes to the
  pujari of "گرو ارجن کی سمادہ" (p. 226, folio 229), with **no location given**. The archive carries two
  Lahore rows whose principal figure is Guru Arjan Dev — **gurdwara-dera-sahib** (Lahore) and
  **gurdwara-baoli-sahib-guru-arjan-dev-ji** (Dabbi Bazaar, Walled City). **Do not pick one.** The fact
  should be recorded as "the samadh of Guru Arjan" until a later chunk locates it.
- **Four archive rows carry Guru Hargobind; only the location decides.** Besides
  **gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-mozang**, the archive has
  **gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-jhalian-jhalian-dhilwan**, **gurdwara-patshahi-chhevin-hadiara-lahore** and
  **gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-chitti-gatti**. The match above rests solely on the chunk's own words
  "موضع مزنگ کے شرق کی طرف … جو جیلخانہ [OCR?] کو جاتی ہے" (p. 225, folio 228), and the heading word
  "جہیٹی [OCR?]" (for چھٹی/چھیویں, sixth) is itself OCR-flagged. A human should confirm the heading before
  the id is relied on.
- **Shah Bilawal Qadri must not be mapped to shah-noorani-shrine-syed-bilawal-shah-noorani.** That archive
  row is **Syed Bilawal Shah Noorani at Lahoot Lamakan, Khuzdar District, Balochistan**. The chunk's man
  is **سید شاہ بلاول قادری**, born at Sheikhupura, resident and teacher in Lahore, trained by Shaikh
  Shamsuddin Qadri, buried on the bank of the Ravi and reburied under Ranjit Singh (p. 235, folio 238).
  Leave unmapped — a candidate for a new row, not a match.
