# tareekh_lahore — chunk 012 — pp. 162–176

**A street-by-street survey of the Wazir Khan's Mosque precinct and then of mosque after mosque in the
Kashmiri Bazaar / Lohari Mandi quarter.** The chunk opens mid-sentence on the endowed shops under the
mosque's vestibule, gives five shrine entries inside or beside that mosque (Sayyid Ishaq Gazruni, Imam
Ghulam Muhammad, the tomb of Masjid Suf[OCR?], Sayyid Sarbuland, Pir Zaki[OCR?]), then Pir Bai[OCR?]
in Kashmiri Bazaar, an unheaded Huzuri sayyid complex with two twelve-doored tombs, Daya Anga's mosque
turned railway bungalow, the shrine of Hassu Teli the oil-seller, and a run of eight mosques described
with their founders, plinths, shops, rents, inscriptions and rebuilding dates. Only two archive rows are
touched, and both only in passing — but the chunk is unusually rich in **waqf, rent, compensation,
custodianship and building-date material**, which is where its value lies.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shah-jamal** (the book: "شاہ جمال سہروردیہ", Shah Jamal Suhrawardi):
  - Named only as the **pir of Shaikh Hassu Teli**: "شاہ جمال سہروردیہ کا یہ مرید تہا جسکا مقبرہ و مدرسہ
    کے اوپر موضع اجہرہ[OCR?] کے پاس ہے" — Hassu Teli was his murid; **his tomb and madrasa** are above …
    near mauza Ijhra[OCR?] (Ichhra) (p. 169, folio 172).
  - The chunk gives the shrine a **madrasa as well as a tomb** — a teaching institution attached to the
    site. Nothing else: no date, no death year, no custodian, no urs, no endowment (not stated).
  - The affiliation is stated as Suhrawardi in the same phrase, and the placing near Ichhra matches the
    archive row; see Doubts for the basis of the match.

- **madho-lal-hussain** (the book: "حضرت لال حسین", Hazrat Lal Husain):
  - Named only in the closing line on Hassu Teli: "کہنے میں کہ حضرت لال حسین کے ساتہہ اسکی کمال محبت تہی
    اور کہا کرتا تہا کہ **حسو حسین اور حسین حسو ہی۔ دو کی باتی نہیں ہے**" — it is said he had utter love
    with Hazrat Lal Husain and used to say "Hassu is Husain and Husain is Hassu; there is nothing of two
    about it" (p. 169, folio 172). The author reports it as hearsay ("کہنے میں کہ").
  - A **claimed intimacy between two Lahore figures**, one of them an archive shrine, stated by the book
    without a source. No date, no place, no shrine detail attaches to Lal Husain here (not stated). See
    Doubts on the identification.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **سید اسحاق گاذرونی — Sayyid Ishaq Gazruni**, whose mazar inside Wazir Khan's Mosque "زیارت گاہ خاص و
  عام ہے", is a place of pilgrimage for elite and commoner alike (p. 162, folio 165). A God-worshipping
  sayyid, resident of "فارس کے ملک شہر[OCR?] گاذرون", the city[OCR?] of Gazrun in the land of Fars; came
  to Lahore "تغلقیہ سلطنت کے وقت", in the time of the Tughlaq sultanate, and stayed. Being a
  murshid[OCR?] [illegible], "رجوع خلائق کا اسکی طرف بہت تہا۔ ہزاروں مرید ان میں تہے" — the resort of the
  people to him was great; he had thousands of murids. Died "سنہ ۷۰۶[OCR?] میں"; "بسم اللہ الرحمن الرحیم"
  is given as the chronogram of his death (p. 163, folio 166). Called **پیر سبز**, Pir Sabz, from the
  tree on his grave — see Legends. He is the reference point for three other graves in the precinct.
- **The occupant of مقبرہ مسجد صوف[OCR?] (Maqbara Masjid Suf)**, in the outer chowk of Wazir Khan's
  Mosque facing its great door (pp. 163–164, folios 166–167): "صاحب مزار سید اسحاق گاذرونی کا ہم عہد و ہم
  محلس تہا" — contemporary and fellow-sitter of Sayyid Ishaq. Name not given (not stated). "بعض لوگ اس
  بزرگ کو سید اسحاق کا بہائی ظاہر کرتے ہیں" — some make him Sayyid Ishaq's brother; the author's reply is
  under Arguments.
- **مزار سید سر بلند — Mazar Sayyid Sarbuland** (p. 164, folio 167): inside a room set back from the
  bazaar north of Wazir Khan's Mosque; one grave, pukhta, on a high chabutra. "یہ بزرگ بہی سید اسحاق
  گاذرونی کے ہم دستون[OCR?] و ہم نشینیوں میں سے تہا" — also one of Sayyid Ishaq's companions; "عام لوگ
  اسکو بہی سید اسحاق کا بہائی کہتے ہیں", common people call him too Sayyid Ishaq's brother. No date
  (not stated).
- **مزار پیر ذکی[OCR?] — Mazar Pir Zaki[OCR?]** (pp. 164–165, folios 167–168): "اس بزرگ کے جسم کا دو جگہ
  مزار ہے" — his body has a mazar in two places: the blessed head buried in a corner of the **western
  dalan of the city gate**, and the rest of the body inside the gate "ایک طویلہ میں", in a stable, the
  graves built in two places. "اسی بزرگ کے نام سے وہ دروازہ ذکی[OCR?] دروازہ کہلاتا ہے" — the gate is
  called **Zaki[OCR?] Gate** after him. On the authority of "تحفتہ الوصلین[OCR?]" he was martyred in the
  Mughals' war; he lived in this gate, "حفاظت اس دروازے کی اسکے متعلق تہی", its defence was his charge,
  and he fought and was martyred when the Mughals took the city. No date (not stated).
- **پیر بائی[OCR?] — Pir Bai[OCR?]**, "یہ مشہور مزار قدیم عمارت کا پختہ بنا ہوا کشمیری بازار میں بر سر
  راہ واقع ہے" — a famous mazar of solid ancient construction on the roadside in **Kashmiri Bazaar**
  (p. 165, folio 168). "اصلی نام صاحب مزار کا بسبب گزرنے عرصہ دراز کے کسی کو معلوم نہیں ہے اور نہ مصنف
  تحفتہ الواصلین نے اسکا نام اپنی کتاب میں درج کیا ہے" — his real name is known to no one for the long
  lapse of time, nor did the author of *Tuhfat al-Wasilin* record it. On that book's authority: he was in
  **Balkh**, with many murids for his worship and austerity; "شہزادہ سلطان جلال الدین خوارزمی بادشاہ کابل
  و قندہار و غزنی و بلخ اسکا کمال معتقد تہا" — Prince Sultan Jalal-ud-Din Khwarazmi, king of Kabul,
  Qandahar, Ghazni and Balkh, was utterly devoted to him. When Chingiz Khan overcame the Khwarazmi
  kingship and Jalal-ud-Din, like his father Sultan Muhammad, was beaten in the Mughal[OCR?] war, crossed
  the Indus and entered the Punjab, and Balkh was sacked by the Chingizi army, the saint too left Balkh
  for the Punjab and settled in Lahore. When "چنگیز کے پوتے قلی خان", Chingiz's grandson Quli Khan,
  invaded the Punjab and the Delhi army fought the Mughals at Lahore, he was among the ghazis, "جو لڑائی
  میں مارا گیا اور اس مقام پر اپنے حجرے کے اندر مدفون ہوا", killed in the battle and buried on that spot
  inside his own hujra. No date (not stated). His shrine's later rebuilding is under Practices.
- **سید محمود حضوری و سید شاہ نور الدین** — Sayyid Mahmud Huzuri and his son Sayyid Shah Nur-ud-Din, the
  two graves in the first of two grand tombs in an enclosure with an ancient mosque opposite the gateway
  (p. 166, folio 169). Twelve doors, three to a side, qalbuti[OCR?] roof, round dome; stair from the south.
- **سید جان محمد حضوری و سید سرور دین** — Sayyid Jan Muhammad Huzuri and his son Sayyid Sarwar Din, the
  two graves in the second tomb, in its own char-diwari with a wheel-well beside the mosque courtyard
  (p. 166, folio 169). "یہ دونو بزرگ اپنے وقت کے مقتدا و پیشوائے طریقت تہے" — both were the leaders and
  guides of tariqat of their age; **the title Huzuri explained**: "حضوری اسواسطے خطاب تہا کہ ان کا مرید
  بہت جلد اوج طریقت پر پہنچکر حضوری ہو جاتا تہا", because their murid very quickly reached the summit of
  tariqat and became *huzuri* (present). "یہ خاندان کوہ غور سے بمقام اوچ اور اوچ سے لاہور آکر سکونت پذیر
  ہوئے اور سلاطین[OCR?] بہمانی[OCR?] عہد میں بہت عروج پایا" — the family came from **Koh-e Ghor to Uch,
  and from Uch to Lahore**, and rose greatly in the Bahmani[OCR?] sultans'[OCR?] era. Called
  "موسوی سید", Musavi Sayyids. **Sayyid Jan Muhammad Huzuri died "سنہ ۷۶۴[OCR?] ہجری میں"** and was buried
  here (p. 167, folio 170).
- **The living descendants of the Huzuri line, named and with an occupation**: "ان کی اولاد سید احمد شاہ
  و حسین شاہ موجو ہیں۔ جابکگری[OCR?] کا کام کرتے ہیں" — Sayyid Ahmad Shah and Husain Shah are living and
  work at "جابکگری[OCR?]" (p. 166, folio 169). Their **thirty-odd-name shajra** is printed in full, from

- **امام غلام محمد المشہور امام کامون[OCR?]** — Imam Ghulam Muhammad, famous as Imam Kamun[OCR?], son of
  Hafiz Muhammad Siddiq, **imam of Wazir Khan's Mosque**, "عالم فاضل سلسلہ قادریہ کا فقیر صاحب تصانیف
  مفیدہ", a learned man, a faqir of the **Qadiriya silsila** and author of useful works, of which
  "گنج مخفی نام ایک منظوم کتاب تصوف کے علم میں اسکی یادگار ہے" — *Ganj-e Makhfi*, a versified book in
  the science of tasawwuf, is his memorial. Died "سنہ ۱۲۳۴[OCR?] ہجری میں" (p. 163, folio 166). His
  burial, dome, mela and line of successors are under Practices.
- **عبد اللہ بلوچ قادری** — Abdullah Baloch Qadri, **pir of Imam Ghulam Muhammad**: "صاحب مقبرہ عبد اللہ
  بلوچ قادری کا مرید تہا جسکا روضہ موضع مزنگ میں مشہور ہے" — whose rauza is famous in **mauza Mozang**
  (p. 163, folio 166). No date (not stated).
- **شیخ حسو تیلی — Shaikh Hassu Teli** (pp. 168–169, folios 171–172): "یہ بزرگ لاہور کے خدا پرست عابد
  زاہدون لوگون میں مشہور ہے۔ قوم کا تیلی اور خاص لاہور کا رہنے والا تہا" — famous among the
  God-worshipping devout of Lahore; a **Teli (oil-presser) by caste and a native of Lahore itself**. His
  shop was in "چوک جہنڈے[OCR?]", Chowk Jhande[OCR?]; he traded in oil. Died "سنہ ۱۰۰۰[OCR?] ہجری میں" and
  was buried here (p. 169, folio 172). A rare **artisan-caste saint** entry: the book gives his trade, his
  shop's location, his pir, his khalifas, his land and his urs day.
- **شیخ سعد الدین ستر پوش[OCR?] اور میان خان** — Shaikh Sa'd-ud-Din Sitr Posh[OCR?] and Mian Khan, whose
  elaborately built graves are on the lower chabutra at Hassu Teli's shrine, "جو اس بزرگ کے خلیفے و جان
  نشین تہے", his khalifas and successors (p. 169, folio 172).
- **ضابطہ خان — Zabita Khan**, "ایک عابد[OCR?] زاہد شخص", a devout ascetic who lived in the mosque later
  rebuilt as Mirza Muhammad's; "لوگ اسکے معتقد بہت تہے", people were much devoted to him; the mosque was
  formerly called his. Died "سنہ ۱۲۵۱[OCR?] ہجری میں" (p. 171, folio 174).
- **مقبرہ جانی خان — Maqbara Jani Khan** (p. 169, folio 172): "یہ مقبرہ احمد شاہ بادشاہ کے عہد میں بنا
  تہا" — built in the era of Emperor Ahmad Shah; of the older tombs "الواح یا غبان[OCR?] پوسیدہ[OCR?]

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A waqf of shops for a named trade, and the trade's collapse.** "سو دوکانیں [illegible] مسجد کی اوپر کی
  جو زیر گنبد دیوڑہی اور اسکے دونو رستوں [illegible] و شمال کی سمت میں ہیں۔ [illegible] وزیر خان نے جلد
  گروں[OCR?] کے بیٹہنے کے لئے [illegible] وقف[OCR?] کی ہوئی ہیں" — shops above the mosque, under the
  domed vestibule and on both its roads, endowed[OCR?] by Wazir Khan **for bookbinders (jild-garan) to sit
  in** (p. 162, folio 165). "سکہوں کے وقت ان دوکانوں پر بہی بڑی رونق رہتی تہی۔ کتاب سازی کی و دوکانیں
  سوا[OCR?] اس جگہ شہر کے کسی جا میں نہ تہیں" — in Sikh times they flourished, and bookmaking shops
  existed nowhere else in the city. Now "کتاب فروشی[OCR?]", bookselling[OCR?], goes on; "مسجد کے سب
  دوکانیں شہید ہیں", all the mosque's shops are "شہید" (see Doubts on the word), and "سازوں کی اولاد و
  مستکاری[OCR?] دفتروں اور چہاپہ خانوں میں نوکری کر رہے ہیں" — the bookbinders' descendants are in
  service in **offices and printing presses**. A named craft guild attached to a shrine-mosque by
  endowment, and its displacement by colonial print employment, in one paragraph.
- **Who occupies a shrine-mosque's rooms.** Of the cells round the courtyard east, north and south:
  "کسی میں کوئی نقاش نقاشی کر رہا ہے کسی میں کوئی کاتب بیٹہتا ہے **کسی میں مزار کے مجاور قیام پذیر ہیں**"
  — in one an illuminator works, in one a scribe sits, in one **the mujawirs of the mazar reside**; and
  "دو نو چو کہنڈیان و دو حجرے امام مسجد کے قبضہ میں ہیں", two chau-khandian[OCR?] and two hujras are in
  the **imam's possession** (p. 162, folio 165). Occupancy divided between craft tenants, shrine servants
  and the imam.
- **A will that specified an unbaked grave inside a solid enclosure.** Of Sayyid Ishaq Gazruni: "حسب وصیت
  حاطہ مزار کا پختہ اور قبر خام بنائی" — **in accordance with his will** the shrine enclosure was built
  pukhta and **the grave kham** (unbaked earth) (p. 162, folio 165). The grave was later altered by
  building: "جب نواب نے اس جگہ مسجد بنوائی تو قبر پستہ بن گئی اور نہال کا خاتمہ ہو گیا" — when the Nawab
  had the mosque built there the grave was lowered and the tree came to an end (p. 163, folio 166).
- **A domed tomb built over an imam's grave in the first years of British rule.** Imam Ghulam Muhammad was
  buried in the ground attached to the "آسمانی چاہ" of Wazir Khan's Mosque and a pukhta grave raised on a
  chabutra; "صاحبان انگریز کی ابتدا عملداری میں کسی ارادت مندنے اس مزار پر پختہ گنبد بنوا دیا جو ابتک
  موجود ہے" — at the beginning of the English gentlemen's administration **a devotee had a solid dome
  built over the mazar**, still standing. It stands outside the mosque's southern wall, in the enclosure
  containing the "چاہ کلان" whose water fills the mosque's hauz (p. 163, folio 166).
- **Three generations of one family in the imamat of Wazir Khan's Mosque.** "صاحب مقبرہ کی وفات کے بعد

- **Thursday assembly and an annual urs in decline.** At Sayyid Sarbuland's mazar: "سکہی عہد میں اس مزار

  بازاری نہیں رہی" — in the Sikh era crowds gathered on Thursdays and the annual urs was held with pomp,

  inside it.

  تعمیر سے مزار کی رونق بڑہ گئی اور چوک کی زینت دو چندہ ہو گئی" — when the chowk's inner houses were

  clearance that endowed rather than removed a shrine.

  Masjid straightened the bazaar for his mosque's adornment; Pir Bai's[OCR?] mazar came into the road, so

  same mechanism as the British bazaar-straightening a century later (below).
- **A mela of two days, with its programme.** At the Huzuri khanqah: "اس خانقاہ پر ہر سال میلا ہوتا ہے۔
  **ایک روز چراغ دوسرے روز قوالی ہوتی ہے**" — an annual mela; one day lamps, the second day qawwali
  (p. 167, folio 170).
- **Shrine land sold by mujawirs for a private family graveyard.** West of Jan Muhammad Huzuri's tomb "اب
  ایک اور زنیا[OCR?] احاطہ پختہ دو بل اینٹون[OCR?] کا بنایا لیا ہے … یہ احاطہ **مفتی غلام سرور شاعر
  لاہور** نے اپنے خاندان کے مقابر کے لئے اپنا روپیہ خرچ کرکے بنوایا ہے اور **زمین مجاور دون خانقاہ سے
  خرید کی ہے**" — Mufti Ghulam Sarwar, poet of Lahore, built it at his own expense for his family's tombs
  and **bought the land from the khanqah's mujawirs**; its door opens from inside the tomb enclosure. "اسمیں
  اب صرف تین[OCR?] قبریں بنی ہوئی ہیں مگر احاطہ وسیع ہے" — only three[OCR?] graves so far, one of Ghulam
  Husain [illegible], one of a granddaughter[OCR?] and one of a "ہوتی[OCR?]" (p. 167, folio 170).
  **Mujawirs alienating shrine land by sale, to a named buyer, recorded without comment.**
- **Six bighas of shrine land with a well, its income assigned to upkeep.** At Hassu Teli's shrine: "چار
  دیواری کے باہر شرق کی سمت کو **چہہ بیگہ زمین مزروعہ مع چاہ و چرخ چوب** موجود ہے۔ **آمدنی اس اراضی کی
  مصارف کے لئے و گزارہ ہر سال ہے**" — six bighas of cultivated land east of the enclosure with a well and
  wooden wheel; the land's income is for the annual expenses and maintenance (p. 169, folio 172). The only
  landed endowment quantified in this chunk.
- **A dated annual urs.** "**تیسری شوال** کو اس بزرگ کا سالانہ عرس ہوتا ہے" — Hassu Teli's annual urs is
  on **3 Shawwal** (p. 169, folio 172); the present buildings were raised by Chin[OCR?] Shah Faqir.
- **A saint's shop still functioning as a shrine.** "چنانچہ اس دوکان پر اب بہی جہنڈا لگا ہوا ہے اور روز

  Hassu Teli's shop and a lamp is lit daily; **on Thursdays many lamps are lit** (pp. 168–169, folios
  171–172). A second, commercial site of cult away from the grave; a chiragh-dan is also built at the
  head of the grave itself (p. 169, folio 172).
- **A mosque's endowed property, then its military and industrial afterlives.** Daya Anga's mosque "مغلیہ
  سلطنت کے وقت بڑی رونق پر رہی کہ **بہت سی جائداد بانیہ[OCR?] نے اسکے ساتہہ وقف کی ہوئی ہے**" — much
  property was endowed with it by the foundress (p. 167, folio 170). Then: "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے وقت
  اسمیں **سالہا سال بارود و گولہ بہرا رہا**", under Ranjit Singh it held powder and shot for years; under
  the English it fell empty; "مسٹر کوپ[OCR?] صاحب … نے **سرکار سے اجازت لیکر اس مسجد کو کچہی بنا لیا**",

  جاری ہوا تو **یہ مسجد بہی ریل والون نے لے لی اور روپیہ قیمت کا کوپ[OCR?] صاحب کو ادا کر دیا**" — when
  the railway workshop began here the railway took the mosque and **paid Cope the purchase price**; a
  workshop officer now lives in it (p. 168, folio 171). **A waqf mosque sold on between private and
  corporate occupants under state licence**, with the money going to the occupant, not the endowment.
- **Conservation by the colonial state, on aesthetic grounds.** Of the same mosque's bronze-work
  ("کانسی کار") arches bearing Quranic verses and durood in naskh: "صاحبان انگریز نے بہی بسبب خوبصورتی
  کے بحال و برقرار رکہا ہوا ہے کہ ایسا رنگین و منقش کام ہونا اب مشکل ہے" — the English too have kept it
  intact for its beauty, since such coloured painted work is now hard to produce (p. 168, folio 171).
  Fabric losses are recorded alongside: of four three-storey minarets, two[?] remain, and each has lost a
  storey in the last[OCR?] season (p. 168, folio 171).
- **A land seizure at a mosque, litigated before the Sikh court, settled by a rent deed.** "ایک شخص
  **دلاور خان** نام داروغہ اصطبل کنور نو نہال سنگہ نے **زیر دستی سے مسجد کے صحن کی زمین امام سے چہین لی
  اور اپنی حویلی تعمیر کر لی**۔ **وارثان مسجد** مفتی غلام رسول و مفتی غلام محمد نے **راجہ کہڑک سنگہ کے
  پاس مستغیث ہوئے**۔ مہاراجہ نے دلاور خان کو سخت تنبیہ کی اور **کرایہ نامہ زمین کا امام کو لکہوا دیا**" —
  Dilawar Khan, daroga of Nau Nihal Singh's stable, high-handedly took the mosque courtyard's land from

  the mutawalli** — imam and mutawalli as distinct offices. It fell in last year's rains and was rebuilt
  by Nawab Abd al-Majid Khan, rais of Lahore (p. 170, folio 173).
- **A mosque built on a takiya's courtyard, and the migrant community that funded it.** Masjid Mohalla
  Takiya Sadhuwan: built "سنہ ۱۲۶۶[OCR?] ہجری میں نور محمد سادہو کے زر لاگت سے". The Sadhus, "ایک مسلمان
  قوم کشمیری", a Muslim Kashmiri people, came from Kashmir under the Sikh rulers and settled in the
  mohalla then known as "محلہ علما ول لوہانی[OCR?]"; "پہلے سب کرایہ دار تہے۔ اب صاحب الملاک و متمول ہو
  گئے ہیں" — once all tenants, now proprietors and wealthy, the mohalla now named for them. "مسجد کے موقع
  پر پہلے ایک میدان تہا و ان سادہوون نے اپنی نشست کے لئے **تکیہ** بنایا اور ایک مختصر مسجد کی بنیا د
  رکہی۔ نور محمد نے وہ مسجد گرا کر اور **بہت سی زمین تکیہ کے صحن سے لیکر** یہ عالیشان مسجد بنوائی" — the
  Sadhus built a **takiya** for their own sitting and a small mosque; Nur Muhammad demolished it and
  **took much land from the takiya's courtyard** for the grand mosque (pp. 170–171, folios 173–174). Three
  shops stand below its courtyard to the south; "آبادی روز افزون ہے", the population grows daily.
- **Shop rents as mosque revenue, stated three times.** Mirza Muhammad "شرق و جنوب کی طرف مسجد کے نیچے

  Wali Masjid: "مسجد کے نیچے بازار کی طرف دو دوکانیں ہیں **جنکا کرایہ حاجی مولی داد بانی مسجد لیتا ہے**"
  — two shops whose rent **the founder himself takes** (p. 173, folio 176). Three different destinations
  for the same kind of income, recorded without comment.
- **An ejectment suit over a mosque, won in the colonial courts.** Of Sufi Wali Masjid: "جب اسکی بیٹے نے
  مسجد و مکان متعلقہ مسجد کی نسبت **مالکانہ دعوی** ظاہر کیا تو محلے والے کہ ہی[OCR?] داروں نے اسکو
  **بذریعہ عدالت بیدخل کیا**" — when the son of the mulla Sufi advanced a proprietary claim to the mosque
  and its attached house, the mohalla's people **evicted him through the court** (p. 173, folio 176). A
  hereditary claim defeated by the neighbourhood.
- **The bazaar-straightening and water-pipe scheme, with compensation figures.** "عرصہ دو سال سے جب سرکار
  کی یہ تجویز ہوئی کہ **شہر کے ہر ایک بڑے بازار کو ایک سمت سے سیدہا کرکے پانی کے نل قائم کئے جائیں** تو
  اس مسجد کا بہی کچہ حصہ سڑک میں آ گیا **جسکی قیمت سرکار سے پانسو روپیہ ملا**" — for two years past the

  بزرگون کے تعمیر کئے**" — he also built many mosques and constructed **tombs and rauzas of the elders**.
- **A preacher's Friday audience and an extraordinary lifetime gift.** Maulvi Jan Muhammad, "عہد سکہی میں
  ایک مشہور مولوی و واعظ و مدرس شہر لاہور", appointed imam of the second Nur Muhammad mosque, preached

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The tree that made the name Pir Sabz, and its leaves as medicine.** On Sayyid Ishaq Gazruni's kham
  grave "ایک نہال ایسا پیدا ہو گیا جس نے بیل کی طرح بڑہ کر قبر کو ڈہانپ لیا۔ اس سبب سے لوگ اس بزرگ کا
  نام **پیر سبز** مشہور کرنے[OCR?] تہے" — a sapling sprang up which spread like a creeper and covered the
  grave, whence people made his name Pir Sabz; "قریب دو صدیں[OCR?] سکے وہ نہال سر سبز رہا اور **بیمار
  لوگ اسکے پتے پیتے[OCR?] یا پکا کر کہاتے[OCR?] اور شفا پاتے رہے**" — for nearly two centuries[OCR?] it
  stayed green and the sick drank[OCR?] or cooked and ate[OCR?] its leaves and were healed (p. 162, folio
  165). The tree ends when the mosque is built over the site (p. 163, folio 166).
- **The oil-seller's scales.** Hassu Teli "خریدار کے ہاتہہ میں یہ ترازو دے دیتا تہا اور کہتا تہا کہ خود
  وزن کر لے۔ پس **جو زیا دہ لیجاتا تہا اسکا غلہ گہر آ کر گہٹ جاتا تہا** اور جو پورا لیجاتا تہا اسکا پورہ
  جاتا تہا" — he put the scales in the buyer's own hand and told him to weigh for himself; whoever took
  more found his grain diminished on reaching home, and whoever took the exact measure found his full
  (pp. 168–169, folios 171–172). The flag and daily lamp at the shop are recorded as the continuing
  observance (p. 169, folio 172).
- **Pir Zaki's[OCR?] severed head.** "مشہور یہ ہے کہ سر اسکا یہین[OCR?] دروازے کے اندر جسم سے جدا ہو گیا
  تہا۔ جہان اب سر کی قبر بنی ہوئی ہے مگر جسم ہے سر بہی [illegible] میں [illegible] رہا اور بہت سے آدمی
  [illegible] آخر الامر شہر کے اندر جا کر سر دہو[OCR?] دیا" — it is famous that his head was severed from
  his body here[OCR?] within the gate, where the grave of the head now stands, while the body, headless
  too, [illegible] remained, and many men [illegible], and at last, going inside the city, [he/they]
  placed[OCR?] the head. The author closes: "**یہ امر خدا کی قدرت سے بعید نہیں**" — this matter is not
  beyond God's power (pp. 164–165, folios 167–168). Note the transcription is broken through the crux
  (see Doubts).
- **The Huzuri name as a karama of method.** The title is explained by the effect on disciples: "ان کا
  مرید بہت جلد اوج طریقت پر پہنچکر حضوری ہو جاتا تہا" (p. 166, folio 169).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author refuses an unsourced kinship claim, twice, and grades his own uncertainty.** Of the Masjid
  Suf tomb's occupant, said by some to be Sayyid Ishaq's brother: "**شاید ہووگا مگر تصدیق اسکی کسی عمدہ
  ذریعہ سے نہیں ہوتی**" — perhaps he was, but it is not confirmed by any good source (p. 164, folio 167).
  Of Sayyid Sarbuland he records the same claim as popular speech only: "**عام لوگ** اسکو بہی سید اسحاق
  کا بہائی کہتے ہیں" (p. 164, folio 167). The most explicit statement of evidentiary standard in the
  chunk, and it is applied against local tradition.
- **He names his source and notes its silence.** For Pir Bai[OCR?] he cites "مصنف تحفتہ الواصلین" twice
  and states both that the saint's real name is unknown to anyone and that this author **did not record
  it either**, before relaying the Balkh–Khwarazm–Chingiz narrative as "اسکا بیان ہے کہ", his statement
  (p. 165, folio 168). *Tuhfat al-Wasilin* is also his authority for Pir Zaki's[OCR?] martyrdom
  (p. 164, folio 167) — the one named textual source in this chunk.
- **An etymology offered for a Mughal court title.** "اکثر مقرب دایہ عورتون کو جو شہزادوں کو دودہ دیتی
  تہیں **انگا** خطاب ملاکرتا تہا کیونکہ انگا جسم کو کہتے ہیں اور انگا اسکو کہتے ہیں **جسکے ساتہ ایک جان
  دو قالب ہو**" — favoured wet-nurses of princes were commonly titled *anga*, because *anga* means body
  and *anga* is said of one with whom one is one soul in two bodies (p. 167, folio 170). The author's own
  gloss, not attributed.
- **A hostile ethnography of the shrine-mosque's patron community.** Of the Sadhus of Takiya Sadhuwan:
  "یہ لوگ ظاہرا امیر کوئی کسب و پیشہ نہیں کرتے۔ ہند و ستان کے مختلف شہروں میں نکل جاتے ہیں … کوئی گدائی
  کرتا ہے کوئی تجارت پر گزارہ کرتا ہے۔ **غرض ہر حیلہ سے روپیہ پیدا کر لانا انکا کام ہے**۔ **ہر جا کر یہ
  اپنے نام بہی بدل ڈالتے ہیں**۔ کوئی صوفی شاہ کوئی شطرنجی شاہ کوئی مسکین شاہ نام رکہ لیتا ہے" — they
  practise no visible trade, disperse to the cities of Hindustan, some beg and some trade, and in short
  it is their business to raise money by every device; wherever they go they change their names, taking
  names like Sufi Shah, Shatranji Shah, Miskin Shah; and in the preceding line, "کوئی فقیر سالک کوئی
  مجذوب کوئی شہزادہ … بن جاتا ہے", one becomes a faqir-salik, one a majzub, one a prince (pp. 170–171,
  folios 173–174). **A named author accusing a named Lahore community of professional impersonation of
  faqirs** — directly relevant to how this book treats mendicancy and shrine income; it should be cited
  as the author's judgement, not as description.
- **The author as architectural critic.** Of Sufi Wali Masjid: "**افسوس کہ گنبد نہیں بنا۔ اگر یہ بنتا تو
  اسکی زینت المضاعف ہوتی**" — a pity no dome was built; had it been, its adornment would have doubled
  (p. 173, folio 176). Of Mirza Muhammad's mosque, where the parapet is shaped to imitate domes: "گویا
  وضعی گنبد بنا دیئے ہیں جو دور سے گنبد دکہائی دیتے ہیں", as if artificial domes had been made, which
  look like domes from afar (p. 172, folio 175). Of Amir Shah's mosque: no dome, "گر اور سب قطع سنہری
  مسجد کی ہے", but otherwise entirely in the form of the Sunehri Masjid (p. 172, folio 175).
- **A one-line verdict on a converted mosque.** "**اب وہ کوٹہی جسکو مسجد کہنا چاہئے** ریل والون کے قبضہ
  میں ہے" — now that bungalow, **which ought to be called a mosque**, is in the railway's possession
  (p. 168, folio 171). The author's sharpest aside in the chunk.
- **The author registers that a usurper was a co-religionist.** Of Dilawar Khan: the mosque is small
  "بسبب اسکے کہ اسکو صحن کی زمین میں دلاور خان نے **با وجہ و مسلمان ہونے کے** حویلی بنالی تہی" — because
  Dilawar Khan built a haveli on the courtyard land **despite being a Muslim** (p. 170, folio 173).
- **The author writes himself into the last entry.** Of Mian Umar Din: "عمر دین بانی اس مسجد کا ایک
  دیندار و نیک خوا ومی[OCR?] **راقم مولف کتاب ہذا** کے با تحت[OCR?]" — the founder is a religious and
  good man, and the writer, the author of this book, … (p. 176, folio 179). The sentence is cut by the
  chunk boundary; the relationship claimed is unrecoverable here (see Doubts).

## Doubts

- **Why shah-jamal is matched, and the one weakness.** The chunk names "شاہ جمال سہروردیہ" with tomb and
  madrasa "موضع اجہرہ[OCR?] کے پاس", near mauza Ijhra[OCR?] (p. 169, folio 172); the archive row
  **shah-jamal** is at "Shah Jamal/Ichhra, Lahore" with silsila Suhrawardi. Name, order and locality all
  agree and the book is a history of Lahore. The row's figure is dated 1671 and the chunk gives **no
  date**, so the match rests on name, order and place; the spelling "اجہرہ" is OCR-flagged. No other
  archive row carries a Shah Jamal.
- **madho-lal-hussain rests on a name alone.** The chunk says only "حضرت لال حسین" (p. 169, folio 172) —
  no place, no date, no shrine, and the statement is hearsay ("کہنے میں کہ"). The archive row is
  **madho-lal-hussain** at Baghbanpura, Lahore. A human should confirm before this is cited as a fact
  about that shrine; nothing else in the chunk connects the name to a site.
- **عبد اللہ بلوچ قادری, "whose rauza is famous in mauza Mozang" (p. 163, folio 166), is not matched.**
  The archive has a Mozang row — **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri**, "Mozang Chungi, Queens Road, Lahore",
  also Qadiri — but the name is different and the chunk gives neither date nor description. **Left
  unmapped**; recorded because the shared locality and order make it a likely future mis-match.
- **Two different Nur Muhammads, and three mosques, must not be conflated.** "نور محمد سادہو", builder of
  Masjid Takiya Sadhuwan in ۱۲۶۶[OCR?] (p. 170, folio 173), and "نور محمد" titled **نورا ایمان والہ** by
  Ranjit Singh, builder of the kotwali mosque in ۱۲۳۹[OCR?] (pp. 173–174, folios 176–177) and of
  "مسجد ثانی نور محمد ایمان والہ" in Kashmiri Bazaar (p. 175, folio 178), are separate men in separate
  entries. The chunk nowhere links them.
- **A section heading is missing at the p. 165 / p. 166 boundary.** The Pir Bai[OCR?] paragraph ends
  mid-clause, "مزار کی بہی دوبارہ تعمیر" (p. 165, folio 168), and p. 166 (folio 169) opens "اور محاذی
  دروازے کے پختہ مسجد عہد قدیم کی بنی ہوئی ہے" — already inside a description of the enclosure holding the
  two **Huzuri** tombs. **The Huzuri complex therefore has no heading and no stated location in this
  chunk**, and it is not clear whether it is continuous with the Pir Bai / Sunehri Masjid site in
  Kashmiri Bazaar or a fresh entry whose title was lost. Do not assign the Huzuri tombs an address from
  this chunk.
- **The Maqbara Jani Khan entry breaks off and the next mosque is unnamed.** The entry ends "تا حال ڈیوڑہی
  اس باغ کی" at the foot of p. 169 (folio 172), and p. 170 (folio 173) opens "[illegible] نے زیادہ ترستایا
  تو محلہ اجہڑ گیا" — mid-account of a mosque whose name is not in the chunk. **The mosque in the Dilawar
  Khan land case is therefore unidentified here**, and it cannot be assumed to be Jani Khan's.
- **The word "شہید" applied to shops** (p. 162, folio 165) is used of buildings, not persons — "مسجد کے
  سب دوکانیں شہید ہیں" — and is glossed above as "ruined". A human should confirm the reading against the
  lithograph; the following clause about locks is broken by [illegible] and cannot be construed.
- **Sayyid Ishaq Gazruni's year and his dynastic placing sit together unexplained.** The chunk gives both
  "تغلقیہ سلطنت کے وقت لاہور میں آیا" and death "سنہ ۷۰۶[OCR?]" (pp. 162–163, folios 165–166). The year
  is OCR-flagged, the era statement is not, and the book does not reconcile them. **Record both as
  printed; a human must check the numeral on the page before either is used.** The chronogram "بسم اللہ
  الرحمن الرحیم" is given without an arithmetic value, and the clause naming it is broken: "اسکی تاریخ
  شیخ[OCR?] وفات ہے".
- **"قریب دو صدیں[OCR?]"** (p. 162, folio 165) is the duration of the Pir Sabz tree's survival and is
  OCR-flagged; so are the verbs of the healing practice, "پتے پیتے[OCR?]" and "پکا کر کہاتے[OCR?]".
- **Sayyid Jan Muhammad Huzuri's date and his family's era are both flagged.** Death "سنہ ۷۶۴[OCR?] ہجری"
