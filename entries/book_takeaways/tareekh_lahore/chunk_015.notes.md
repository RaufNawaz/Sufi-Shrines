# tareekh_lahore — chunk 015 — pp. 207–221

**A run of eight Sikh and Hindu sites north and east of the walled city, surveyed building by building
and ending in the Sikh royal samadhs.** The chunk opens mid-description of an unnamed samadh (p. 207,
folio 210), then gives, each under its own printed heading: **مکان سمادہ حقیقت رائے** (p. 207, folio 210),
**مکان سمادہ دریاگر سنیاسی** (p. 209, folio 212), **سمادہ مہاراجہ شیر سنگہ والی پنجاب** (p. 210,
folio 213), **دہرم سال بہائی گوما سنگہ [OCR?]** (p. 212, folio 215), **شوالہ نزد چاہ بدہو والہ [OCR?]**
(p. 214, folio 217), **شوالہ و دیوی دوارہ گنگا پربت [OCR?]** (p. 214, folio 217), **مکان شہید گنج**
(p. 216, folio 219), **حال سمادہ مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ** (p. 217, folio 220), and
**سمادہ مہاراجہ کہڑک سنگہ و کنور نونہال سنگہ** (p. 221, folio 224), which breaks off mid-sentence.

Two archive rows take material. The chunk's largest value to this project is elsewhere, in three
things: **the author writes himself into the record as the Executive Engineer who underpinned the
Ranjit Singh samadh** (p. 219, folio 222); **a revenue-free grant is traced across three regimes — Sikh
jagir, British life-grant, confiscation, cash pension** (p. 209, folio 212); and **every site's income
is named** — offerings, revenue-free bighas, wells, shop rents, a government salary (pp. 208–216).

**The folio markers.** Every page of this chunk prints one, and each reads exactly three above the PDF
index: p. 207 → folio 210 through p. 221 → folio 224. Only the printed marker is used below.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

  - **Why the place bears the name, and how the samadh is named.** The domed mandir on the pakka
    chabutra in front of the great western dalan "اسی کا نام شہید گنج ہے کیونکہ **نواب خان بہادر زکریا
    خان و میر منو** کیوقت جو سکھہ مقتل ہوتے تھے وہ اسی جگہ **داب [OCR?] دیئے [OCR?]** جاتے تھے" — this
    is what is called **Shahid Ganj**, because in the time of **Nawab Khan Bahadur Zakariya Khan and Mir
    Mannu** (میر منو) the Sikhs who were executed were buried[OCR?] at this very place; "اُنہیں سے ایک
    نامی سکھہ **تارو سنگہ** تہا جسکے نام سے یہ سمادہ **بابا تارو سنگہ** کی کہلاتی ہے" — among them was a
    famous Sikh, **Taru Singh**, after whose name this samadh is called that of **Baba Taru Singh**

  - **The mandir's fabric**: pakka lime, plastered white; roof "قالبوتی"; above it a round dome with a

  - **A second, larger samadh in the same courtyard**: "تیسرا مکان **سمادہ بابا دیب سنگہ [OCR?]** کا",
    the third building is the samadh of **Baba Deb Singh[OCR?]**, in a corner of the courtyard and "بہت
    بڑا مکان", a very large building — verandahs[OCR?] on all four sides, a domed samadh chamber in the

    the corner to the roof (p. 216, folio 219). The book gives no relation between Deb Singh[OCR?] and
    Taru Singh **(not stated)**.
  - **Liturgy and furniture**: in the great arched dalan to the west "ایک تخت چوبی بچھا ہوا ہے اُس پر
    گرنتہ رکھا رہتا ہے جسکو لوگ پڑہا کرتے ہیں" — a wooden takht is spread on which the **Granth** is

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **حقیقت رائے / Haqiqat Rai, and the samadh over his cremation place** (pp. 207–209,
  folios 210–212). The book's account: in the time of "**نواب زکریا خان بہادر صوبہ لاہور**", Nawab
  Zakariya Khan Bahadur, **subah of Lahore**, he was a boy of "**ستو [OCR?] برسا [OCR?]**" years and
  read Persian in the maktab of a Muslim maktab-dar mulla (p. 207, folio 210). The teacher went out, the
  boys quarrelled, "ایک مسلمان لڑکے نے **دیوی** کے حق میں کوئی نا شائستہ کلمہ کہا", a Muslim boy spoke
  an unbecoming word about the **Devi**; Haqiqat Rai took it ill "اور اُس نے **پیغمبر صاحب** کی اٹکل کی
  نسبت کوئی ایسا لفظ کہہ دیا جو کمال بے ادبی پر دلالت کرتا تھا", and said of the **Prophet** a word
  amounting to utter irreverence (pp. 207–208, folios 210–211). The teacher, told by the Muslim boys,
  "غصہ کے مارے لال ہو گیا", went red with rage and took him "**قاضی شہر** کے روبرو", before the **Qazi of
  the city**, who on hearing the statement "حقیقت رائے کے حق میں **قتل کا فتوی** لکھا اور منظوری کے لئے
  صوبہ لاہور کے پاس بھیجا" — wrote a **fatwa of death** against him and sent it to the subah of Lahore
  for approval (p. 208, folio 211). Zakariya Khan summoned him and said "تو نے کمال بے ادبی **اہل بیت**
  کے حق میں کی ہے تو بیشک **واجب القتل** ہے مگر اگر تو مسلمان ہو جائے تو تیری جان بچ سکتی ہے ورنہ گردن
  مارا جائیگا" — you have committed utter irreverence regarding the **Ahl-e Bait**, you are certainly
  liable to death, but if you become a Muslim your life can be saved, otherwise you will be beheaded;
  "حقیقت رائے نے مسلمان ہونے سے انکار کیا … یعنی **گردن مارا گیا**" — he refused, and was beheaded
  (p. 208, folio 211). **No date of any kind is given for the execution (not stated.)** Note the book
  gives the offence twice and differently: the boy's words are said to concern "پیغمبر صاحب" in the
  narration and "اہل بیت" in the governor's sentence (pp. 207–208, folios 210–211).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A grant followed across three regimes — the single most useful passage in the chunk.** Of the Haqiqat
  Rai samadh: "**مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ** کیوقت اس مکان کی قدر توقیر بہت تھی ایک **و دہ [OCR?] چاہ مع بیس بیگہ
  اراضی** جو اسی سمادہ کی زمین کے متعلقہ ہیں **سرکار سے معاف** تھا اور **دو گاؤں ایک سلہریا [OCR?] دوم
  منڈی ضلع سیالکوٹ** میں جمع **[illegible] سال نام جاگیر** میں تھے مگر وہ گاؤں و چاہ مسمی **سروپ سنگہ
  خادم سمادہ** کے نام **واگزار** ہے" — in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's time this place was much honoured; a
  well[OCR?] with **twenty bighas** of land belonging to the samadh was **revenue-free from the state**,
  and **two villages — first Salhariya[OCR?], second Mandi, in district Sialkot** — were held in **jagir**
  with a revenue of [illegible] a year; but the villages and the well stood released in the name of one
  **Sarup Singh, khadim of the samadh**. Then: "**صاحبان انگریز کیوقت** وہ سب معافی **سروپ سنگہ کی عین
  حیات** مقرر ہو گئی وہ مر گیا تو سب کے سبب **ضبطی** میں آ گئی اور **نہال سنگہ سروپ سنگہ کا پوتا** محروم
  رہ گیا آخر بہت سی کوشش کے بعد **[illegible] روپیے سالانہ نقد** اب بنام سمادہ **واگزار** ہوئی ہیں اور
  نہال سنگہ **خادم و پجاری** سمادہ قرار پایا ہے یہ **روزینہ** اب ہمیشہ کو گئے مقرر رہیگا کبھی جا بہین
  [OCR?] منزل نہ آ ئیگا" — **in the time of the English gentlemen** the whole muafi was fixed **for Sarup
  Singh's lifetime only**; when he died the lot fell under **confiscation** and **Nihal Singh, Sarup
  Singh's grandson**, was left deprived; finally, after much effort, **[illegible] rupees a year in
  cash** has now been released in the name of the samadh, and Nihal Singh has been settled as **khadim
  and pujari**; this **stipend** is now fixed in perpetuity (p. 209, folio 212). **Four stages — Sikh
  revenue exemption and jagir, a grant vested in a person rather than the shrine, British resumption at
  the grantee's death, and a cash pension re-granted to the institution with the office re-created.**
  Both money figures are lost to [illegible] (see Doubts).
- **A government post and salary at the Sher Singh samadhs, with the whole establishment itemised.** "ان

  **سرکار سے مقرر** ہے اُسکو **ایک سو [OCR?] روپیہ سالانہ سرکار سے** ملتا ہے چنانچہ وہ مع **اپنی والدہ**

  گرنتہ پڑہتا ہے" — for the sweeping and protection of these samadhs **only one person** is appointed by

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A saint who dies of his own sigh, at the hour he chose.** Daryagar Sanyasi: "جس روز اُسکی **دفنات**
  کا دن تھا حاضرین وقت کو اُس نے کہا کہ **مجھکو تہہ خانے کے اوپر بیٹھالو [OCR?]** چنانچہ وہ اُسکو اوپر
  لے آ ئے جب وہ اوپر آ کر بیٹھا تو **ایک آہ جان گداز اُس نے ایسی ماری کہ مغز اُسکا پھٹ گیا اور اسی وقت
  جان بحق تسلیم ہوا**" — on the day appointed for his interment he told those present to seat him above
  the cellar; when they had brought him up and he sat, **he gave so soul-melting a sigh that his brain
  burst and at that moment he surrendered his life** (p. 209, folio 212). Told by the author without
  attribution to any narrator.
- **Two pigeons that flew into the pyre.** At Ranjit Singh's samadh the two extra graves are explained:
  the pigeons "**بوقت داغ [OCR?] دینے نعش کے چتا کے اندر اُڑتے ہوئے آ پڑے تھے اور مہاراجہ کی نعش کے ساتھہ
  بھی جل گئے تھے**" — came flying and fell into the pyre at the time of the cremation[OCR?] and burned
  with the Maharaja's corpse (p. 219, folio 222). Reported flatly, as fact, with the two samadhs as its
  standing evidence.
- **A dying king's prophecy that his son will follow him, and its fulfilment.** Kharak Singh "پہلا کہتا
  تھا کہ **اگر میں مر جاؤں گا تو نو نہال سنگہ کو اپنے ہمراہ لیجاؤں گا** چنانچہ **اُسی طرح وقوع میں آ
  ایا**" — kept saying that if he died he would take Nau Nihal Singh with him, and so it came to pass
  (p. 221, folio 224). The chunk breaks off before the fulfilment is narrated.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author writes his own professional act into the city's history, and names his other book.** The
  two first-person interventions in this chunk are both proprietorial: the referral to "**کتاب تاریخ
  پنجاب مولفہ راقم**", his own *Tarikh-e Punjab*, in place of narrating Ranjit Singh's reign (p. 217,
  folio 220), and the account of his own underpinning of the samadh as **Executive Engineer, Buildings
  Division, Lahore** (p. 219, folio 222). **A city history written by the officer who repaired the
  monuments it describes** — the survey's authority on fabric, and its interest, come from the same place.
- **A moral reading of a modest tomb.** Of Sher Singh's samadh: "انسان جب اس سمادہ پر جا تا ہے **بے
  اختیار اُسکی آنکھوں سے آنسو ٹپک پڑتے ہیں** اور یقین آ جاتا ہے کہ **دنیا ناپائیدار ہے** اور یہ مال و
  دولت و عالیجاہی **صرف چند روزہ بات ہے اور خواب و خیال**" — a man who goes to this samadh weeps
  involuntarily and becomes certain that the world is impermanent, and that wealth and high station are a
  matter of a few days, a dream and a fancy (p. 210, folio 213). The author reinforces it with two verses
  on the same page (p. 212, folio 215; quoted under Citable passages).
- **A death recorded as martyrdom for religion, in the author's own words.** Of Haqiqat Rai: "**جان
  شیریں اپنے ملت و مذہب پر قربان کردی**" — he sacrificed his sweet life for his community and religion
  (p. 208, folio 211); and "وہ سنتے ہی **غصہ کے مارے لال ہو گیا**" of the teacher, "**ناحق** مارا گیا" of
  Sher Singh (pp. 208, 210; folios 211, 213). The sympathies are not concealed.
- **A custodian's dealings with the colonial administration treated as venality.** Nihang Singh's use of
  the Lieutenant-Governor's portrait is reported with the judgement attached: "اُس نے اُس تصویر کو **اپنی
  بہبود کا ایک ذریعہ** بنا رکھا ہے" (p. 213, folio 216). Read against the author's own colonial office on
  p. 219 (folio 222), it is the chunk's only overt comment on how shrine-keepers handle the new power.

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the site is left unnamed.** Page 207 (folio 210) begins "کے ایک گوشہ
  ایسان [OCR?] میں ایک اور چھوٹا چبوترہ ہوا ہے اسمیں جھنڈا نصب ہے …" and continues for a full paragraph of
  description — a square pakka samadh chamber with two north doors, a marble chabutra with marble
  railings, a gilded mirrored ceiling, a silk or pashmina canopy, a roof stair, a tall central dome with
  four large corner domelets and "اونیس [OCR?]" small finialled domelets on the parapets, a Granth dalan,
  and gilded portraits of the ten Gurus. **The heading that named it falls in the previous chunk.** No
  name is guessed here; a human must read the tail of chunk 014 to attach it. Note that the same paragraph
  also says "سات سات گنبدیوں منڈیروں پر", seven and seven domelets on the parapets, which does not agree
  with "اونیس [OCR?]" in the previous clause.
- **The chunk closes mid-sentence.** The entry **سمادہ مہاراجہ کہڑک سنگہ و کنور نونہال سنگہ** (p. 221,
  folio 224) breaks off at "تو توپیوں کی سلامی" — the gun salute — before the accident that the previous
  sentence foreshadows, and before any description of the samadh itself, its location, or its fabric.
  Everything about that monument is in chunk 016.
- **"شہید گنج" could be either of two archive rows, and the chunk does not separate them.** The archive
  carries **gurdwara-shaheed-bhai-taru-singh** (Lahore, Bhai Taru Singh, d. 1745) and
  **gurdwara-shaheed-ganj-singh-singhnian** (Lahore, Sikh women and children martyrs). The chunk's site is
  named "شہید گنج" and its central domed mandir is said to be "**سمادہ بابا تارو سنگہ** کی" (p. 216,
  folio 219) — which is why the material is placed under the Taru Singh row above. **But the book gives
  the place only one name, Shahid Ganj; it names no women or children; and its one location statement —
  outside Delhi Gate on the eastern edge of Bazar Landa — is not enough to tell the two rows apart.** A
  human must decide, and should not let this chunk be cited for either row without that decision.
- **An "old mosque" is listed among Shahid Ganj's own appurtenances.** "ایک **مسجدہ کہنہ** و خراس وغیرہ
  چند سمادہیں **اسی مکان کے متعلق** ہیں" (p. 217, folio 220). The word is OCR-clean but the syntax makes
  the mosque one of the establishment's properties; given what this site later becomes, **this single
  clause should be re-read from the PDF before it is quoted**.
- **Both money figures in the Haqiqat Rai endowment passage are lost.** The Sikh-period jagir revenue
  reads "جمع **[illegible]** سال" and the British cash pension reads "**[illegible]** روپیے سالانہ نقد"
  (p. 209, folio 212). **The structure of the grant survives; neither amount does.** A human must re-read
  this page before any sum is attached to this shrine.
- **Ranjit Singh's death year has no era and is flagged.** "آخر **۱۸۹۶ [OCR?]** مہینہ **ہگرمی [OCR?]** میں
  مر گیا" (p. 217, folio 220). The numeral is OCR-flagged, the month name is OCR-flagged and unreadable,
  and **the calendar is not stated**. Nothing is supplied here. Note also that the chunk gives the reign
  length as "چالیس سال", forty years, with no start date.
- **The two dated inscriptions are the least secure text in the chunk.** Rani Rind Awi's[OCR?] reads
  "سرگباشی [OCR?] **۱۴ [OCR?] مگہر سمت ۱۹۲۷ [OCR?]** اتوار کے روز" — day, month, era-year and the verb of
  death all flagged (p. 211, folio 214). Rani Partab Kunwar's date survives only as "**سمت ۱۹۱۴ [OCR?]**"
  in a sentence that also carries an [illegible] and the unreadable run "رانی ماہ بین [OCR?] اُسکا نام
  مشہور نہایہ [OCR?] رانی ۔ [illegible] ماہ بہاو ہوں [OCR?]" (p. 212, folio 215) — **her name, her title
  and her date are all in doubt together**.
- **"رانی رند اوی [OCR?]" appears three times and is flagged every time** (pp. 210, 211; folios 213, 214),

  builder of Sher Singh's samadh and the occupant of a second one.
