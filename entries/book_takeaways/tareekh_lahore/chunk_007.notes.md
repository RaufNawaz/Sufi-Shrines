# tareekh_lahore — chunk 007 — pp. 90–104

**The chunk closes the survey of the mohallas and opens the book's third part, the register of standing
buildings.** It runs from the tail of a mohalla account (butchers driven out under Sikh pressure) through
Mohalla Mughalpura and Mohalla Chowk Dara Shikoh (p. 90–92, folios 93–95), then prints the plan of
"تیسرا حصہ", the third part, and its "پہلی قسم", first category — buildings connected with the religion of
the Hindus (pp. 92–93, folios 95–96). From p. 93 (folio 96) to the end the chunk is a building-by-building
survey of five Lahore places of worship with their founders, endowments, rents, staff, timings and repair
history: a shiwala by Masjid Wazir Khan, Shiwala Raja Dina Nath by the Kotwali, Shiwala Bakhshi Bhagat Ram,
the Gurdwara Janam Asthan of Guru Ram Das, the dharamsala of Baba Khuda Singh, the Thakur Dwara of Raja
Teja Singh, and the opening lines of Shiwala Gulab Rai Jamadar. **One archive row is the direct subject of
an entry**; the chunk's larger value to this project is its economy of shrine finance — shop rents, a
confiscated jagir village, a government stipend, a caste marriage-offering, a compensation payment — all
stated in figures.

**Every page of this chunk prints a `[folio N]` marker**, and over pp. 90–104 the markers read 93 to 107,
one per page, in step. Folios below are only those the chunk actually prints; nothing here is computed, and
the relation must not be extended past p. 104.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **gurdwara-guru-ram-das-ji** ("گردوارہ جنم ستہان گرو رامداس", Gurdwara Janam Asthan Guru Ram Das,
  pp. 101–102, folios 104–105):
  - **The book's claim for the site is birth and upbringing, not only birth.** "یہ عبادت گاہ سکہی مذہب سے
    متعلق ہی" — this place of worship belongs to the Sikh religion; Sikhs "اس متبرک مکان کا کمال ادب کرتے
    ہین", show the utmost respect to this sacred building, and Hindus high and low also attend for worship
    and hold the place extremely pure. It is "جس مقام پر گورامداس صاحب مذہب سکہی کے جانشین چہارم کا جنم
    ہوا" — the place where **Guru Ram Das, fourth successor of the Sikh religion**, was born, "اور اسی جگہ
    پرورش پائی", and was also reared there (p. 101, folio 104).
  - **It names the site as the parents' own dwelling, and their departure from Lahore.** "خاص جائے سکونت
    گورامداس کے ماباپ کی اس جگہ پر تہی" — the particular residence of Guru Ram Das's mother and father was
    at this place; then "سبب انقلاب زمانہ" (through the revolution of the times) they left Lahore and went
    to "گوبندوال" (Gobindwal), where — the clause is corrupt, "جس جگہ گورامداس نے میرا جانشین[OCR?] قیام
    پذیر تہا" — and "وہان ہی شادی گورامداس کی امرداس کی لڑکی کے ساتہ ہو گئی", there Guru Ram Das's
    marriage took place **with Amar Das's daughter** (p. 101, folio 104). No date of any kind is given for
    the birth, the departure or the marriage: **(not stated)**.
  - **A building date, a patron and a mode of acquisition.** "پہلے اس جگہ چہوٹا سا مکان تہا" — formerly
    there was only a small house here. "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کی اخیر عملداری مین" — in the **last period of
    Maharaja Ranjit Singh's rule** — the attention of the Guru's Sikhs turned this way, and
    **"مہاراجہ کے حکم سے محلہ سوداگران کے مکانات جو اس کے ساتہ ملحق تہے لے گئے"** — by the Maharaja's
    order the houses of **Mohalla Saudagran** adjoining it were taken, the land enlarged, and the
    foundation of the present grand building laid; "ایک سال کے عرصے مین تیار ہو گیا", it was ready within
    **one year** (p. 101, folio 104). A ruler's order expropriating a merchants' quarter to extend a
    shrine; no compensation is mentioned either way.
  - **Location as printed**: "بازار چوک متی[OCR?] کے جنوبی لین مین واقع ہو" — situated in the southern
    lane of Bazar Chowk Matti[OCR?], with "سرِ بازار ایک عالیشان دروازہ پختہ" a grand masonry gateway on
    the bazaar front, and a stair in the deorhi (p. 101, folio 104).
  - **Fabric**: an upper courtyard reached by that stair, a well in its north-west corner
    ("گوشہ شمال و مغرب مین چاہ"), **a bel tree** ("ایک بیل کا درخت") and a standard raised in the
    courtyard, "ایک علم یعنی چہنڈا کہڑا کیا گیا ہی"; on the east a stepped, highly ornamented brick
    jharokha (p. 101, folio 104). **"یہ عالیشان عمارت دربار امرتسر کی قطع پر ہی ہوئی ہے"** — this grand
    building is made on the pattern of the **Darbar of Amritsar** (pp. 101–102, folios 104–105). Four doors
    on four sides; "بیچ مین اگرچہ گنبد نہین ہی" — there is **no dome** in the centre — but four
    two-storeyed shah-nashins on the four sides, reached by a stair (p. 102, folio 105).
  - **Liturgy and assembly, timed and counted as far as the book counts.** An elaborate floor covering
    stays spread and "گرنتہہ صاحب کمال آداب سے رکہا رہتا ہی", the Granth Sahib is kept with the utmost
    reverence; Sikhs come in numbers and perform "جبین سائی[OCR?]", prostration; **"دو وقت روز مرہ گرنتہہ
    پڑہا جاتا ہی"**, the Granth is read twice daily, with large gatherings of the devout; there is singing,
    and **"قوال موحدانہ کافیان گا کر لوگون کو محظوظ کرتے ہین"** — qawwals delight the people by singing
    monotheist kafis (p. 102, folio 105). See Doubts on the qawwals.
  - **A weekly (or calendrical) great assembly**: "آٹہوین روز انوار[OCR?] کے دن تیسرے پہر بڑا مجمع زن و
    مرد کا ہوتا ہی" — on the eighth day, the day of "انوار"[OCR?], in the third watch, a great assembly of
    women and men; "گرووں کے گرنتہون کا انتخاب پڑہا جاتا ہی", a selection from the Gurus' Granths is read,
    and "شبد توحید آمیز گائے جاتے ہین", shabads mixed with tauhid are sung: "جلسہ لایق دید ہوتا ہی", an
    assembly worth seeing (p. 102, folio 105).
  - **A dated reform-association event.** "ابہی[OCR?] کے ہولیون کے دنون مین سنگہ سبہا کے ممبرون[OCR?] نے
    یہان بڑا جلسہ کیا تہا" — recently[OCR?], in the days of the **Holis**, the members of the **Singh
    Sabha**[OCR?] held a great assembly here; "تمام مندر اور بازار مین روشنی کی اور تمام رات شبد گاتے رہے
    تہے", they illuminated the whole temple and the bazaar and sang shabads all night (p. 102, folio 105).
    The only organised association named anywhere in this chunk. Year **(not stated)**.
  - **Custodianship, as of the book's present.** "فی الحال پجہاری اس گردوارے کا جیت سنگہ اور معتمد و
    معاون حلقہ کا ایک شخص کنہیا لال ہے" — at present the **pujari** of this gurdwara is **Jeet Singh**,
    and the trusted agent and helper of the circle is a person named **Kanhiya Lal**; the author's verdict

    author bears this name, and the chunk does not say whether this is he.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A mosque endowment appropriated, then leased out, then rebuilt as a temple — the chunk's single most
  citable institutional sequence.** Of the shiwala north of Masjid Wazir Khan: **"یہ احاطہ عہد شاہان اسلام
  میں مسجد وزیر خان کے متعلق تہا گر ایہ [OCR?] کی آمدنی مسجد کے اصراف میں صرف کی جاتی تہی"** — in the era
  of the Muslim kings this enclosure belonged to Masjid Wazir Khan, and its [rent?][OCR?] income was spent
  on the mosque's expenses. **"جب سلطنت جاتی رہی تو تین حاکموں کے وقت باوا ٹہالا گر [OCR?] نے اسجگہ سکونت
  اختیار کی اور گوچہ [OCR?] سنگہ احد الحاکم سے اجارت لیکر چہوٹا سا شوالہ کچی عمارت کا بنوالیا"** — when the
  sultanate passed away, in the time of the three rulers, Bawa Thala Gir[OCR?] took up residence in the
  place and, **taking a lease ("اجارت") from one of the rulers ("احد الحاکم")**, Gocha[OCR?] Singh, built a
  small shiwala of unbaked construction. Then "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے دور دوران میں راجہ دینا ناتہہ نے یہ
  موجودہ عمارت بنوائی بہت سا روپیہ خرچ کیا", in Ranjit Singh's reign Raja Dina Nath built the present
  structure at great expense (p. 94, folio 97). A waqf income stream, a regime change, a lease from the new
  power, and a change of cult on the same plot — all on one page.

- **Marble stripped from graves by name of the ruler.** Of Zakariya Khan's burial enclosure: the graves
  "جن پر لاکہوں روپیے کا سنگ مرمر تہا وہ سب مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ نے اُتروا لیا" — the marble on them, worth
  lakhs of rupees, **was all taken off by Maharaja Ranjit Singh** (p. 91, folio 94).
- **Brick-sellers as a named trade acting on monuments.** "خشت فروشوں کی دستبرد" at Zakariya Khan's
  graveyard (p. 91, folio 94); "کشمیریان خشت فروش" — the Kashmiri brick-sellers — who demolished what
  remained of the Dara Shikoh chowk in Ranjit Singh's time and "بنیادین نکالیں", dug out the foundations
  (p. 92, folio 95).
- **A colonial demolition contract on a Mughal monument, and what was built from it.** Of Mian Muhammad
  Sultan, contractor: "انگریزی عہد میں وہ کہند [OCR?] میان محمد سلطان ٹہیکہ دار کا ٹہیکہ لگ گئے گویا اینٹ
  کی کان ہاتہہ آ گئی اُس نے اُسکی بنیادوں سے ہزار ہا روپیے کی اینٹیں نکلوائیں اور لنڈہ [OCR?] بازار و
  سرائے کی تعمیر میں وہی اینٹیں صرف کیں کئی کوٹہیان بنائیں غرض اس خطہ میں جسقدر اینٹیں تہیں سب نکلوالیں" —
  in the **English era** the ruins came under his contract; he had bricks worth thousands of rupees taken
  from the foundations and **spent those same bricks on building Landa[OCR?] Bazar and a sarai**, and built
  several kothis, in short took out every brick in the tract (p. 92, folio 95).
- **The mosque of Dara Shikoh: an Englishman's house in it, then its demolition for bricks.**
  "دارا شکوہ کی ایک مسجد عالیشان جو چوک کے متصل تہی وہ البتہ قائم تہی انگریزی عہد میں اُسمیں ایک انگریز
  نے کوٹہی بنالی تہی وہ یہی محمد سلطان نے اینٹون کی طمع سے لے لی اُسکے چار طرف کے حجرے جو مسجد کی کرسی کے
  اندر بنے ہوئے تہے گرا لئے مسجد کے دو گنبد گرا چکا تہا کہ اُسکی عمر کا خانمہ [OCR?] ہو گیا" — Dara
  Shikoh's grand mosque adjoining the chowk still stood; in the English era **an Englishman made a kothi in
  it**; Muhammad Sultan took it over out of greed for bricks, pulled down the cells built inside the
  mosque's plinth on its four sides, and had demolished **two of the mosque's domes** when his own life
  ended. "اب اُس مسجد کا ایک گنبد بدستور ہو باقی گر چکی ہو" — **one dome of that mosque now stands as
  before, the rest has fallen** (p. 92, folio 95). A dated-by-regime condition report a fieldworker could
  test against the fabric.
- **A gate-and-shop commercial precinct as shrine-adjacent economy.** The Dara Shikoh chowk had "چاروں طرف
  چوک کے چار عالیشان دروازے" and, between the gates, "درست دو ہرہی دوکانین پختہ دو منزلہ", masonry
  two-storeyed double shops; "بڑے بڑے ساہہ کار تجار کی اُس میں کوٹہیان تہین لاکہوں روپیے کا بیوپار ہوتا
  تہا ہر جنس کے مال و غلہ کا ذخیرہ رہا کرتا تہا" — great banker-merchants had kothis in it, **trade of
  lakhs of rupees** went on, and stores of every kind of goods and grain were held (p. 91, folio 94). It
  emptied when "بادشاہ گر دی ہوئی اور بسبب [OCR?] خوف غارت مال کا آنا بند ہو گیا", the anarchy of kings
  came and fear of plunder stopped the inflow of goods: traffic ceased, stock was looted, residents fled
  (pp. 91–92, folios 94–95).
- **A communal expulsion settled by mutilation, with the state as broker.** The butchers (قصاب) of the
  quarter fought the Sikhs twice and in many small encounters, then abandoned the mohalla for the inside of
  the city wall; Kabuli Mal gave them a separate place within the city. "چونکہ سکہیوں کی اُنکے ساتہہ جانی
  دشمنی تہی سکہیوں کی سب مثلیں جمع ہو کر لاہور پر چڑہ آئین" — since the Sikhs bore them mortal enmity, **all
  the Sikh misls assembled and marched on Lahore**, sending word to Kabuli Mal: **"ہم قصابان گاؤ کش کو قتل
  کرو یا ہمارے حوالے کر دو"** — kill the cow-slaughtering butchers or hand them over to us — or "ہم شہر کے
  دروازے توڑ کر اندر گہس آئینگے اور تمام شہر کو لوٹ کر برباد و کر دینگے", we will break the city gates and
  plunder and ruin the whole city. **"کابلی مل نے اُسوقت تنگ آگر چند قصابان کے ناک کان کٹوا کر شہر سے باہر
  نکال دیا جبکو سکہیوں نے اپنی آنکہہ سے دیکہہ لیا اور خوش ہو کر چلے گئے"** — hard-pressed, Kabuli Mal had
  the noses and ears of several butchers cut off and expelled them from the city, which the Sikhs saw with
  their own eyes and departed satisfied (p. 90, folio 93). Of that quarter's buildings "صرف ایک پختہ مسجد
  ابتک چہو دہر [OCR?] اور کچہہ باقی نہیں", only one masonry mosque remains and nothing else (p. 90,
  folio 93).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The contractor ruined by demolishing a mosque, told as popular report, not as the author's claim.**
  **"لوگون میں مشہور ہے کہ اگر محمد سلطان اس مسجد کو نگر و آتا نو [OCR?] اُسکا کارخانہ بہرہم و درہم نہ
  ہوتا یہ اسی مسجد کے گرو انے کی نکبت ہو جس سے وہ قرضدار ہو گیا تمام جائداد میں پر گئی کارخانہ لگر [OCR?]
  لگ گیا تندرستی میں فرق آ گیا اور سالہا سال بیمار رہکر مر گیا"** — it is famous among the people that had
  Muhammad Sultan not come to demolish[OCR?] this mosque his business would not have been broken up; this
  is the **nikbat** (misfortune) of pulling the mosque down, through which he fell into debt, all his
  property went [under mortgage?], his works were [ruined?][OCR?], his health declined, and after years of
  illness he died (p. 92, folio 95). The author marks the attribution ("لوگون میں مشہور ہے") and does not
  endorse it; note that the same page also has his life ending mid-demolition, "اُسکی عمر کا خانمہ [OCR?]
  ہو گیا".
- **A guru's karamat reported to exist but withheld.** Of Bhai Prem Singh: "یہ پریم سنگہ اپنے گرو خدا سنگہ
  کی بڑی بڑی کرامتین بیان کرتا ہی جنکا بیان طول طویل ہے" — he relates great karamat of his guru Khuda
  Singh, **the narration of which is very long** (p. 102, folio 105). The book names a living transmitter of
  a karamat corpus and prints none of it: for this shrine the miracles are **(not stated)**.
- **A Muslim amir's regard for a Sikh faqir, framed against expectation.** "امیر دوست محمد خان باوجود یکہ
  متعصب مسلمان تہا مگر اسکی خاطر بہت کرتا تہا" — Amir Dost Muhammad Khan, though a bigoted Muslim, showed
  Khuda Singh much regard (p. 102, folio 105). Presented as a mark of the faqir's standing rather than as a
  miracle.
- **Merit claimed for darshan.** At the Teja Singh thakurdwara, worshippers "کرشن بہگوان کا درشن کرکے
  سعادت دارین حاصل کرتے ہین", obtain the felicity of both worlds by the darshan of Krishna Bhagwan
  (p. 103, folio 106) — stated in the author's own voice.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The book states its own plan for Part Three, and classifies buildings by religion.** "اس حصہ میں تشریح
  اُن مکانات اندرونی بیرونی شہر لاہور کی ہو جو زمانہ سلف میں تعمیر ہوئے اور ابتک باقی ہیں از قسم عمارات
  حویلی و باغیچہ و مقبرہ و مسجد و مندر و غیرہ" — this part describes the buildings inside and outside
  Lahore built in former times **and still standing**: havelis, gardens, mausolea, mosques, temples and the
  like. "اسمیں تین قسمیں ہیں": (i) "اُن مکانات کا جو ہندوُن کے مذہب سے متعلق ہیں", buildings connected with
  the religion of the Hindus; (ii) "اُن مکانات کا تذکرہ جو ملت اسلام سے علاقہ رکہتے ہیں", those belonging
  to the millat of Islam; (iii) "اُن مکانات کی جو کسی اہل مذہب و ملت سے متعلق نہیں ہیں", those belonging
  to no religion or community (pp. 92–93, folios 95–96). **The survey's frame is standing fabric sorted by
  confession** — which tells the archive both what to expect from later chunks and what the criterion of
  inclusion is.
- **His criterion for which temples get an entry, stated plainly.** "واضح ہو کہ پرانی عمارت کے مندر اور
  مندروُن کی عمارت کا نہیں [OCR?] بہت ہیں جنکا شمار نہیں ہو سکتا چہوٹے چہوٹے شوالے و ٹہاکر دوارے و دیوی
  دوارے بیشمار ہیں" — old temples and temple buildings are so many they cannot be counted, and small
  shiwalas, thakurdwaras and devi-dwaras are innumerable; **"لیکن بعض مندر ایسے ہو ان [OCR?] نامی گرامی
  ہیں اور خاص و عام میان باغ [OCR?] کر پوجا کرتی ہو ان اس قسم میں لکہے جاتے ہیں"** — but some temples are
  renowned and high and low [come and?][OCR?] worship there, and those are the ones written under this
  head (p. 93, folio 96). So the chunk's five temples are, by the author's own statement, **the famous ones
  only**; absence from this book is not evidence a site did not exist.
- **A dating caution the author supplies himself, and the archive should carry.** **"مگر سکہی عہد میں
  پرانی عمارت کے مندر بہی از سر نو بنا لئے گئے جنکی عمارت تازہ نظر آتی ہو"** — in the Sikh era even temples
  of old fabric were rebuilt from new, so that their building looks fresh (p. 93, folio 96). He then
  demonstrates it on the very next site, where an old mosque enclosure becomes a kachcha shiwala and then a
  Ranjit-Singh-era masonry temple (p. 94, folio 97). **A standing warning against reading a nineteenth-
  century fabric as a nineteenth-century foundation.**
- **He repeatedly explains a shrine's vitality by its resident's character, and its decline by his
  departure.** Of Bhai Prem Singh: "بہائی پریم سنگہ جانشین خدا سنگہ کا بہی متبرک و خلیق آدمی ہے اور اسی کے
  سبب سے یہ مکان بخوبی آباد ہے" — he is a blessed and courteous man **and by his means this house is well
  frequented** (p. 103, folio 106). Of the Teja Singh thakurdwara's departed sadhu: "ٹہاکر دوارے کی رونق
  بہی اُسکے سبب سے دوبالا تہی اب چند سال سے وہ یہان سے چلا گیا ہے", the thakurdwara's splendour too was
  doubled by him, and for some years now he has gone (pp. 103–104, folios 106–107). Of the Kotwali shiwala,
  the same causal idiom applied instead to family spending: "میں جب بہی سے مندر کی رونق روز افزون ہی"
  (p. 96, folio 99). **"رونق" is the author's working measure of a shrine's health, and he attributes it to
  persons and money, never to the saint.**
- **An aesthetic judgement used as historical argument about Mughalpura's scale.** "اس محلے کی نقشہ عمارت
  جو کسی سے کہ نہیں سکی دیکہکر انسان حیرت میں آجاتا ہی کہ جب کہ یہ محلہ آباد ہو گا تو کیا صورت ہوگی" — the
  plan of this quarter's buildings, which no one can describe, so astonishes a man on sight that he wonders
  what it must have looked like when inhabited (p. 91, folio 94). The argument from surviving footprint to
  vanished grandeur, which is how this chunk handles every destroyed site.
- **He dates a temple by reference to its own inscription and by an elapsed interval, not by a fixed
  era, twice.** Samvat 1900[OCR?] "اور یا تاریخ اختتام مندر پر لکہی ہی", the completion date written on the
  temple (p. 100, folio 103); and "پچیس برس کا عرصہ گزرا ہے", twenty-five years elapsed, for the Teja Singh
  thakurdwara (p. 103, folio 106); and "اب ۱۸ برس ہوئے ہین", eighteen years, for Khuda Singh's death
  (p. 102, folio 105). **Three of the chunk's four dates are relative to the moment of writing**, so none
  can be converted without the book's own date of composition, which this chunk does not give: **(not
  stated)**.
- **He cross-references his own book.** Of Bakhshi Bhagat Ram's fall: "مختصر حال جسکا رو سا [OCR?] کے ذکر
  میں لکہا ہے", the short account of which is written in the notice of the ru'asa[OCR?] (p. 97, folio 100) —
  a section not in this chunk.
- **Religious traffic across community lines is recorded without comment, in both directions.** Hindus high
  and low attend the Guru Ram Das gurdwara and "اس جگہ کو نہایت پاک تصور کرتے ہین" (p. 101, folio 104);
  qawwals sing at that gurdwara (p. 102, folio 105); a Sikh faqir is honoured by the Amir of Kabul (p. 102,
  folio 105); Kashmiri Pandits staff two Shaiva temples founded by officers of the Sikh state (pp. 94, 96;
  folios 97, 99); and the Bhagat Ram shiwala's Shaiva sanctum holds images of Vishnu, Lakshmi, Rama, Sita,
  Krishna, Sarasvati, Ganesh, Bhairo and the Sun (p. 100, folio 103).

## Citable passages

- "اس مندر کے متعلق کوئی روزینہ یا جاگیر سرکار سے مقرر نہیں ہے معتقد ہندو روز مرہ یہان آکر ماتہا ٹیکتے
  اور چڑہاوہ چڑہاتے ہیں اُسی پر یہ جاریوں کا گزارہ ہے۔" — "No stipend or jagir is fixed for this temple
  from the government; devout Hindus come here daily, bow their foreheads and make offerings, and on that
  the pujaris' livelihood depends." (p. 94, folio 97)
- "اُس نے اس شوالہ کے خرچ کے لئے چار سو روپیہ سالانہ کا گاوُن اپنی جاگیر سے الگ کر دیا تہا مگر بسبب اسکے
  کہ وہ تقریری بخشی کی طرف سے تہی سرکار کی طرف سے نہ تہی وہ گاوُن بہی یوقت ضبطی جاگیر ضبط ہو گیا۔" — "For
  the shiwala's expenses he set apart from his jagir a village of four hundred rupees a year; but because
  the grant was on the bakhshi's own authority and not the government's, that village too was confiscated
  when the jagir was resumed." (p. 97, folio 100)
- "مگر سکہی عہد میں پرانی عمارت کے مندر بہی از سر نو بنا لئے گئے جنکی عمارت تازہ نظر آتی ہو۔" — the
  author's own dating caution: "But in the Sikh era even temples of old fabric were rebuilt anew, so that
  their building looks fresh." (p. 93, folio 96)
- "مہاراجہ کے حکم سے محلہ سوداگران کے مکانات جو اس کے ساتہ ملحق تہے لے گئے اور زمین کو وسعت دیکر اس
  عالیشان مکان کی تعمیر کی بنیاد رکہی گئی۔" — on the Guru Ram Das gurdwara: "By the Maharaja's order the
  houses of Mohalla Saudagran adjoining it were taken, and the land being enlarged, the foundation of this
  grand building was laid." (p. 101, folio 104)
- "ہم قصابان گاؤ کش کو قتل کرو یا ہمارے حوالے کر دو۔" — the Sikh misls to Kabuli Mal: "Kill the
  cow-slaughtering butchers, or hand them over to us." (p. 90, folio 93)

## Doubts

- **"ٹہاکر دوارہ راجہ تیجا سنگہ" must not be mapped to shahwala-teja-singh-mandir.** The chunk's site is a
  **Thakur Dwara at the end of Moti Bazar, Lahore**, built by Raja Teja Singh, officer of the Sikh army,
  under English rule, twenty-five years before writing, holding images of **Sri Krishn ji and Radha[OCR?]
  ji** (pp. 103–104, folios 106–107). The archive row **shahwala-teja-singh-mandir** is in **Mohallah
  Dharowal, Sialkot**, with **Shiva (Mahadev)** as principal figure. Shared personal name, different city,
  different deity, different building type. **Leave unmapped**, and note that "Shahwala"/"Shiwala" plus
  "Teja Singh" makes this a likely future mis-match.
- **None of the four Hindu foundations here matches a Lahore archive row.** The archive's Lahore Hindu rows
  are **krishna-mandir-ravi-road** (Ravi Road, Krishna), **valmik-mandir-naqi-road** (Naqi Road, Valmiki),
  **jain-mandir-lahore** (Lytton Road) and **loh-temple-lava-temple** (Lahore Fort). The chunk's sites are
  north of **Masjid Wazir Khan**, beside the **Kotwali**, the **Bhagat Ram shiwala** (lane not named), the
  end of **Moti Bazar**, and inside **Masti Gate** behind Jamadar Khushhal Singh's haveli. The Teja Singh
  thakurdwara and krishna-mandir-ravi-road share only the deity. **Nothing should be mapped on deity
  alone**; all five are candidates for new rows, not matches.
- **"کنہیا لال" at the Guru Ram Das gurdwara and the book's own author share a name** (p. 102, folio 105).
  The chunk says only that a person named Kanhiya Lal is the "معتمد و معاون حلقہ" of the gurdwara, and the
  author's evaluative clause is lost to "[illegible]". **The chunk does not identify them**, and nothing
  should be inferred either way. A human must decide from the book's front matter and from the surrounding
  chapters.
- **The samadh date at the Wazir Khan shiwala is both OCR-flagged and grammatically misattached.** As
  printed, "یہ سمادہ سمت ۱۹۰۰ [OCR?] بکرمی میں مرا تہا" makes the **samadh** die in Samvat 1900[OCR?]
  Bikrami, where the sense must be the occupant (p. 94, folio 97). The occupant's name is also badly
  damaged: "اس سمادہ والے کا نام و نہ کا سایئن [OCR?] تہا". **Do not carry either the name or the year
  without re-reading the page.**
- **The same numeral, "سمت ۱۹۰۰ [OCR?] بکرمی", is given for the building of the Bhagat Ram shiwala**
  (p. 100, folio 103) as for the majzub's samadh at the Wazir Khan shiwala (p. 94, folio 97). Both carry the
  flag. Two unrelated sites with an identical flagged Samvat year is a pattern worth suspecting as an OCR
  artefact; the Bhagat Ram date is said to be inscribed on the building, so it is checkable.
- **The Assistant Commissioner's name and his year are both flagged.** "سنہ ۱۸۵۰ [OCR?] ء میں تہارت مل
  [OCR?] صاحب اسٹنٹ کمشنر لاہور" (p. 96, folio 99). The surname as transcribed is not a recognisable
  reading; the Kotwali construction and bazaar-widening date depends on this numeral alone.
- **The brotherhood that pays the eight-anna marriage offering cannot be named from this chunk.**
  "کہتربان گوٹ [OCR?] میرین [OCR?] کی برادری" (p. 97, folio 100) — two flagged words in a three-word name.
  The **custom and the amounts are clear; the community is not.**
- **The image list at the Bhagat Ram shiwala does not count consistently.** Twelve ordinals are printed but
  Ganesh ji appears twice ("پانچویں گنیش جی" and "ساتویں اور گنیش جی کی"), and the eighth conflates two
  deities, "آٹہویں دیوی اِشٹ [OCR?] بہیرو جی کی" (p. 100, folio 103). **Do not report a count of images
  from this list.**
- **The kalas count at the same temple is self-contradictory as transcribed.** "سب کلس طلائی شمار میں اکسہہ
  [OCR?] میں" gives a flagged total, then "باقی کلس طلائی چہوٹے ساتہہ عدد گنبد کے" gives seven small ones,
  while the jag mohan earlier has "سات کلس طلائی معہ سات سورج کہبیوں [OCR?]" (pp. 98–99, folios 101–102).
  The large one is "پانچ ہاتہہ لمبا" with gilded "جہندی [OCR?]". **No total should be quoted.**
- **The village name on Zakariya Khan's graveyard is flagged**: "بیگم پچہرہ [OCR?]" (p. 91, folio 94). So is
  the second mausoleum's occupant there, "مقبرہ حضرت ایشان [OCR?]" — **a Lahore tomb named in one flagged
  phrase, with no date, order or custodian**, and a candidate for a future archive row only after the page
  is re-read.
