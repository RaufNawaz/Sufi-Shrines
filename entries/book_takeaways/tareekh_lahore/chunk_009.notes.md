# tareekh_lahore — chunk 009 — pp. 119–132

**This chunk is a continuous survey of Hindu places of worship inside the walled city**, in the
author's house-by-house manner: a thakurdwara whose entry begins before this chunk, then the mandir
of Bishno Devi, Shivala Wilbagh/Dilbagh Rai, Shivala Thibi Wala, Thakurdwara Chor Mor Wala, Bhadr
Kali, Thakurdwara Jwala Dai, Ram Dwara, the Asthan of Bhairo ji, the four-mandir enclosure of Bawa
Mehr Das, Shivala Pandit Radha Kishan, and the opening of Mandir Kali Devi, which breaks off
mid-sentence (pp. 119–132, folios 122–135). No Muslim shrine appears, and **no archive row is the
subject of any entry** — the section's value to this project is almost entirely in
`## Practices, institutions, economy`: named custodians and their chelas, succession lists, women
founders, waqf, offerings as the sole revenue, an explicit statement that no jagir exists, melas,
langar, inscribed donor bricks, and building dates in Samat/Bikrami.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker three higher than the PDF index** — p. 119 → folio
122 through p. 132 → folio 135, each marker reading exactly that. Only the markers the chunk prints
are used below; nothing is extrapolated.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Offerings as the sole revenue, and an explicit statement that no endowment exists.** Of the
  unnamed thakurdwara: **"اس مندر کے ضروری اخراجات کا صرف ... چڑہاوے کی آمدنی پر ہی جاگیر روزینہ
  کوئی مقرر نہین ہو"** — the mandir's necessary expenses fall only on the income of offerings; **no
  jagir and no daily allowance (روزینہ) is fixed** (p. 120, folio 123). The same formula recurs at
  four more sites: Bishno Devi, "مکان کی آمدنی چڑہاوے سے خرچ مکان کا چلتا ہے اور اُسی پر گزارہ
  پجاری کا ہے" (p. 121, folio 124); the Wilbagh Rai shivala, where the offering "چہپ[OCR?]
  ہوتا[OCR?] ہی جو شوالہ کے خرچ اور پجاری کے گزارہ کے لیئے کفایت کرتا ہی", suffices for the
  shivala's expenses and the pujari's livelihood (p. 122, folio 125); the Thibi Wala shivala,
  "چڑہاوہ بہت بہی[OCR?] وہ بہی چڑہتا ہی جسپر مہنت اور پجاریون کا گزارہ ہی" (p. 124, folio 127); and
  Chor Mor Wala, "گزارہ مہنت و پجاریون کا صرف چڑہاوے کی آمدنی پہ ہی اور اُسی سے اخراجات ضروری مندر
  کے چلتے ہین" (p. 125, folio 128). **For this book's Hindu establishments in the walled city, the
  chunk records no land, no revenue grant and no state stipend at all.**
- **A mela on every Ekadashi, with night-long bhajan.** "ہر ایک ایکادشی کے دن یہان بڑا میلہ ہوتا ہے
  اعتقاد مند لوگ کثرت سے آکر جمع ہوتے ہین اور تمام رات بہگت لوگ بہجن گاتے ہین اور چڑہاوہ کثرت سے
  چڑہتا ہے" — on every Ekadashi day a great mela is held; devotees gather in numbers, bhagats sing
  bhajans all night, and offerings are made in abundance (p. 120, folio 123). The mandir's
  custodian: "اس مندر کا مہنت بہیراگی ایک خدا پرست شخص مہتمم و منتظم اس مندر کا ہے" — the mahant is
  a bairagi, a God-fearing man, and is the **mutamim and muntazim** of the mandir (p. 120, folio
  123).
- **A mela held outside the city, with a substitute observance inside it.** Of Bhadr Kali[OCR?]:
  "مگر میلوں[OCR?] مین جب بہدر کالی کا میلہ موضع نیا زبیگ[OCR?] مین ہوتا ہے اُس روز اُسی جگہ بہی زن
  ومرد کا اجتماع بحد نہایت ہوتا ہے جو لوگ وہان نہین جاتے وہ یہان آکر پوجا کرتے ہین" — when the
  Bhadr Kali mela is held at **mauza Naya Zabeg[OCR?]**, on that day there is an extreme concourse
  of men and women at this place too, and those who do not go there come here and worship (p. 126,
  folio 129). A city shrine functioning as the overflow of a rural mela.
- **Custodial succession recorded name by name.** Chor Mor Wala: the building was given to **Mahant
  Balram Das**, "جو ایک مشہور خدا پرست سادہ تہا"; "آخری عمر تک مہنت موصوف اس مین قیام پذیر رہا
  اُسکے پیچہے جے رامداس مہنت ہوا اب بہگوان داس[OCR?] مہنت ہے" — he resided in it to the end of his

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **An image recovered from the ground at Hardwar on the strength of a dream.** Thakur ji appeared
  to Sobha[OCR?] Ram Kardar[OCR?] of Lahore at Kankhal[OCR?] and said "ہم یہان فلان جگہ مدفون ہین
  تو ہمکو نکال لیجیو[OCR?]" — we are buried here at such a place, take us out; he dug at the spot,
  took the image out and brought it to Lahore (p. 119, folio 122).
- **The deity's stomach-ache and the fennel water.** Daya Kunwar unknowingly offered bread of
  "مسی[OCR?] یعنی چہینے[OCR?]" as bhog; Thakur ji came in a dream: "بسبب کہانے مسی روٹی کے ہمارے
  پیٹ مین درد ہی ہمکو عرق بادیان پلاؤ" — because of eating missi bread our stomach hurts, give us
  araq-e-badiyan to drink; she offered it as bhog "اور ٹہاکر جی کو آرام ہو گیا", and Thakur ji was
  relieved (p. 119, folio 122).
- **Liquor in the bhog and the fall of the man who seized the image.** Sardar Mohar Singh took Thakur
  ji forcibly to his own house against Daya Kunwar's refusal and "یہ بے[OCR?] اعتدالی کی کہ ایک روز
  بہوگ مین شراب رکہہ دی", committed the impropriety of putting liquor in the bhog one day; "ٹہاکر
  جی کی اُسپر کمال خفگی ہوئی", Thakur ji was extremely displeased at it, "اِنہین دنون مین رنجیت سنگہ
  نے لاہور لے لیا اور سردار موہر سنگہ قید ہو گیا", and in those very days Ranjit Singh took Lahore
  and Mohar Singh was imprisoned (p. 119, folio 122). The book does not gloss the sequence as
  punishment; it reports it in sequence and attributes the whole account to common report — "یہ بات
  لاہور مین بہت مشہور ہے".
- **The hiding place disclosed in a dream.** A zamindar devotee stole the murti and hid it in a
  straw store-room (توڑی[OCR?] کی کوٹہی); Thakur ji told Mohar Singh's wife in a dream exactly which
  zamindar and which room, and "چنانچہ یہ عورت وہان سے نکال لائی", she brought him out (p. 119,
  folio 122).
- **The Ravi undoes a refusal.** The sadhu bairagi of Rangilpur repeatedly refused to give up the
  murti; "آخر اتفاق یہ ہوا کہ جسجگہ ٹہاکر جی کا مندر تہا اُس طرف دریائے راوی چڑہ آیا اور مندر کی
  دیوار کو گرا لیا" — at last the Ravi rose on that side and brought down the mandir's wall; when
  that had passed, the sadhu handed the murti over (p. 120, folio 123).
- **A Devi who came out of the ground during a brick-dig, in Samat 1931 / 1875 AD.** "اس احاطہ مین
  سے مہنت نے اینٹین کہہ دوانی شروع کین ... یہ دیوی جی زمین سے نکل آئین معلوم ہوا کہ دیوی جی نے خود
  ظہور کیا ہے" — the mahant began having bricks dug out of the enclosure and the Devi came out of
  the ground; it was understood that she had manifested herself; the news spread through the whole
  city, the crowd was such that "چند روز میلا لگا رہا", a mela continued for several days, and
  "اصدہا[OCR?] روپیے چڑہت[OCR?] چڑہ گئے", hundreds of rupees were offered — after which it was
  resolved to build her a mandir in the same courtyard (p. 131, folio 134). **A shrine, a mela and a
  donated building all originating in one excavation, dated by the book to Samat 1931 = 1875 AD.**
- **A tale that renamed a shrine.** The chor-and-mor katha told to Ranjit Singh, the title "چور مور
  والا سادہ", and the mandir's consequent name (pp. 124–125, folios 127–128) — the book presents
  this as reputation, not miracle.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author dates a building stratigraphically, and says so.** Of Thibi Wala: "اسکی قدامت کی کوئی
  حد و اندازہ نہین ہے مشہور یہ ہی کہ یہ شوالہ شہر لاہور کی آبادی کے وقت تعمیر ہوا تہا جسکو کئی
  ہزار برس کا گزر چکے ہین" — its antiquity has no limit or estimate, and it is *famous* that it was
  built at the founding of the city; **but he then gives his own floor**: "بہر حال تین ہزار برس سے
  کم مدت نہین گزر رہی", in any case not less than three thousand years — and his reason is the
  building itself: "اسکی قدامت ملاحظہ مکان سے پائی جاتی ہے کہ مندر کا مکان حال کی سطح زمین سے
  ڈیڑہ[OCR?] منزل نیچے ... جب یہ بنایا گیا ہو گا تو زمین سے اونچا کسی قدر کرسی دیکر تعمیر اسکی عمل
  مین آئی ہو گی اب سبب اسکے کہ ہزارہا برس گزر چکے ہین باہر زمین اونچی ہو گئی ہی" — a shrine now a
  storey and a half underground must have been built on a plinth *above* ground, so the ground
  outside has risen over thousands of years (pp. 122–123, folios 125–126). The inference is the
  author's own, and it is an argument from fabric, not from report.
- **He separates report from knowledge.** "ابتدا اس مندر کی کسیکو معلوم نہین" (p. 119, folio 122);
  "اُسکی بنیاد کا زمانہ کسیکو معلوم نہین قدیم و پُرانا چاہ ہی" of the well (p. 122, folio 125);
  "یہ بات لاہور مین بہت مشہور ہے کہ..." for the Mohar Singh episode (p. 119, folio 122); "مشہور یہ
  ہی کہ..." for Thibi Wala's date (p. 122, folio 125). Where he has nothing he says so rather than
  supplying a date.
- **He treats a donor's inscription as self-advertisement.** Wasdev's marble brick is described as
  set up "اپنی تعمیر کے نام نہاد سے ... تاکہ لوگون پر ظاہر ہوجائے کہ یہ مندر واسدیو نے سمت
  ۱۹۳۶[OCR?] مین بنوایا ہے" — under the so-called name of his own construction, so that it should
  appear to people that he built it — placed immediately after the author's own statement that the
  mandir is not less than two hundred years old and that Wasdev's act was a تجدید, a renovation
  (pp. 129–130, folios 132–133). **A nineteenth-century warning against reading a foundation date
  off a donor plaque**, useful wherever this archive relies on inscriptions.
- **He makes a shrine's condition depend on the man in charge of it.** Stated twice as cause and
  effect: "اُسکے سبب سے شوالہ نہایت رونق پر ہے" of the Brahman Dev Yadatta[OCR?] (p. 122, folio
  125), and "جسکے سبب سے مکان کی روپئی[OCR?] دن بدن زیادہ ہوتی جاتی ہے" of Pandit Kanshi Ram
  (p. 128, folio 131) — against the absentee heir at Kalka Galla[OCR?] who "اس مندر کی خبر گیری وہ
  کچہ نہین کرتا" (p. 122, folio 125).
- **He classifies where the buildings resist a single name.** Of the Mehr Das enclosure: "اس مکان کو
  اگر مندرون کا مجموعہ کہا جائے تو بجا[OCR?] ہے", and then his ranking of its four mandirs by
  antiquity, "یہ سبکے پہلا اور قدیم مکان ہے" (p. 129, folio 132); and of the two shivalas both dated
  Samat 1930, "گویا وہ نو شوالے ایک ہی[OCR?] سال مین اس احاطہ کے اندر بنی تہے" (pp. 130–131, folios
  133–134 — see Doubts on "نو").
- **His datings are relative to an unstated present.** The chunk gives only one statement of the
  author's own moment — "فی زمانہ حال یعنی سمت ۱۹۳۷[OCR?] مین" (p. 130, folio 133). Every "عرصہ …
  برس کا گزرا" in this chunk (forty years, fifty years, nine years, six[OCR?] years) is reckoned
  from that unstated present and **cannot be converted without a human fixing the book's date of
  composition**.

## Doubts

- **No archive id is matched in this chunk, and none should be.** Every site here is a Hindu
  establishment inside the walled city, named by mohalla, and none of the names, locations or
  figures corresponds to an archive row. In particular: **مندر کالی دیوی** in Kali Mata ki Gali,
  Lahore (p. 132, folio 135) must **not** be attached to `kali-bari-mandir` (Peshawar),
  `kalat-kali-temple` (Kalat) or `kalka-cave-temple-asthan-of-kalka-devi` (Aror, Sukkur); **مکان
  بہدر کالی** in Mohalla Pahla[OCR?] Lakhpat Rai (pp. 125–126, folios 128–129) likewise. The Krishna
  and Radha murtis of **Thakurdwara Jwala Dai** (p. 127, folio 130) and the Krishna murtis of the
  **Mehr Das** enclosure (pp. 129–130, folios 132–133) must **not** be attached to
  `krishna-mandir-ravi-road` or `krishna-mandir-kabari-bazar` — the chunk places both in Mohalla
  Wachhowali, and neither name nor street matches. The Hanuman murti on the Radha Kishan shivala's
  dome (p. 132, folio 135) is not `shri-panchmukhi-hanuman-mandir-karachi`. **Treat the whole chunk
  as candidate material for future rows, not as evidence about existing ones.**
- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the first mandir has no name in it.** The thakurdwara of pp.
  119–120 (folios 122–123) is described in full — image, custody, mela, economy — but its heading
  fell on the page before this chunk. **A human must read the preceding page of the PDF to name it**
  before any of this material is used.
- **The chunk breaks off mid-sentence** in the Mandir Kali Devi entry at p. 132 (folio 135), at "گویا
  وہ دروازہ اُس نشستگاہ". The rest of that entry is in the next chunk.
- **"کنہیا لال کمبو[OCR?] کی والدہ" (p. 124, folio 127).** The founder of Chor Mor Wala is named as
  the mother of a Kanhaiya Lal Kamboh[OCR?]. Whether this is the author of this book is **(not
  stated)** in the chunk and must not be assumed; the nisba is OCR-flagged.
- **The founder of the Talwara shivala is spelled two ways on facing pages** — "ولباغ رے[OCR?]"
  (pp. 121–122, folios 124–125) and "دلباغ رے[OCR?]" (p. 122, folio 125), both flagged. One of the
  two readings is wrong; the section heading uses the first.
- **The bell-count of the fourth Mehr Das mandir has three readings on one page.** The heading gives
  "اسی گہنٹے والی دیوی"; the text gives "جو اسی[OCR?] عدد گہنٹے" with **"۸۲[OCR?]" printed in the
  margin**; and the custodianship note calls her "دیوی چوراسی گہنٹے والی", the Devi of eighty-four
  bells (p. 131, folio 134). Eighty, eighty-two and eighty-four cannot be reconciled from the chunk.
- **"گویا وہ نو شوالے ایک ہی[OCR?] سال مین" (pp. 130–131, folios 133–134).** Read literally, "those
  nine shivalas"; but this book writes دونوں as "دو نو" throughout (e.g. "دو نو وقت", "دو نو شوالون
  کی پوجا", "یہ بہی دو نو مل کر"), and only **two** shivalas in the enclosure are dated Samat 1930,
  so "both shivalas" is the likelier sense. Do not carry a count of nine.
- **The Wasdev renovation carries two years, one line apart** — "فی زمانہ حال یعنی سمت ۱۹۳۷[OCR?]"
  for the renovation and "سمت ۱۹۳۶[OCR?] مین بنوایا" on the brick (p. 130, folio 133). Both are
  flagged; the discrepancy may be real (work in one year, brick claiming another) or an OCR digit.
- **Radha Kishan's shivala is dated "سمت ۱۸۴۷[OCR?] بکرمی" (p. 132, folio 135)** — flagged, and much
  earlier than every other Samat date in the chunk (1930, 1931, 1936/1937). Verify the digits from
  the lithograph before using it.
- **The land donor of the first thakurdwara is unreadable.** "یہ[OCR?] مہاراج نے اپنی زمین ٹہاکر
  دوارے کیواسطے دی" (p. 120, folio 123) — which Maharaj gave the land is not recoverable from the
  chunk; do not supply one.
- **The elapsed time before the Hardwar recovery has lost its numeral**: "عرصہ قریب یہ[OCR?] سو برس
