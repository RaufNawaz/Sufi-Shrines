# tareekh_lahore — chunk 013 — pp. 177–191

**Six mosques inside the walled city, then a new section opens on the buildings outside the wall**: the
chunk runs from the end of an unnamed entry through six mosque notices — all six founded or rebuilt by
Sikh-court officials, a courtesan, a merchant and a British-era rais — and then, at p. 184 (folio 187),
under the heading **"ذکر معابد و مقابر و مندرہاے بیرونی شہر لاہور"**, an account of the temples, tombs and
mandirs outside the city of Lahore, beginning with the Hindu buildings: the chaubara of Chhajju Bhagat,
the asthan of Sitla Mata, the Thakur Dwara of Bawa Mangal Das and the marble samadhs outside Masti Gate
(p. 184, folio 187). **No archive row is the subject of anything in this chunk** (see Doubts). Its value
is in institutions: shop rents made waqf, an imamat appointed by Maharaja Ranjit Singh, a sajjada
appointed by three rulers of the city, a celibate mahant's rule-book, a shrine resorted to by Hindus and
Muslims alike, and land taken by the colonial state for a hospital road and paid for in cash and land.

**Every page here prints a `[folio N]` marker**: folio 180 at p. 177 through folio 194 at p. 191, one per
page, and each reference below uses only the folio that page actually prints.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **مسجد شیخ نواب امام الدین خان** — the mosque of Shaikh Nawab Imam ud Din Khan, "محلہ چہلہ[OCR?] میں حمام
  میں", in Mohalla Chehla[OCR?], in the hammam (p. 177, folio 180). Its founder was "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے
  دربار میں امیر کبیر و ناظم صوبہ کشمیر", a great noble at Maharaja Ranjit Singh's darbar and **nazim of
  the province of Kashmir**. His father **شیخ غلام محی الدین** (Shaikh Ghulam Muhi ud Din) held the
  nizamat of Kashmir before him and died there; then the son **شیخ امام الدین** was appointed to the same
  service and held it "جبتک کہ صاحبان انگریز نے لاہور فتح کر لیا اور صوبہ کشمیر کا علاقہ ... مہاراجہ گلاب
  سنگہ کے پاس فروخت ہو گیا" — until the English gentlemen conquered Lahore and the territory of the
  province of Kashmir **was sold to Maharaja Gulab Singh** (p. 177, folio 180). Built "سنہ ١٢٦٦ ہجری"
  (1266 AH) and left "یادگار نیک نامہ میں", as a memorial of good name (p. 177, folio 180). Fabric: head
  of the lane facing west, a plinth (کرسی) one storey high, wide pukka courtyard, a wooden ornate valan on
  the north outside, three grand masonry mihrabs, a marble slab on the middle mihrab with the kalima above
  and four verses below (p. 177, folio 180); inside, moulded ceilings "سقف قالبوتی[OCR?]" and three tiers
  of domed vaults, a four-verse qita around the middle dome, three domes and two minarets on the roof, and
  further chronogram verses on the wall of the southern valan (p. 178, folio 181). See Practices for its
  caretaker and the chronograms.
- **مسجد حفیظ چابک سوار** — the mosque of Hafiz the horseman, "بازار رنگ محل متصل مشن سکول", in Rang Mahal

  mosque on the site before; Hafiz Chabuk Sawar rebuilt it anew on a plinth a storey high, built shops
  beneath it on the south and west, and made their rent waqf (see Practices). Grand and domed, three
  mihrabs, a marble slab with the kalima over the middle one, verses on the mihrabs inside, moulded
  ceiling, three domes and two small minarets (p. 178, folio 181).

  folios 181–182): he was the horseman of the household of "سرداران سندہانوالیہ" (the Sandhanwalia
  sardars); when they fled Lahore in the reign of "مہاراجہ شیر سنگہ" (Maharaja Sher Singh) he stayed on. A
  report reached Sher Singh that the sardars' property[OCR?] was deposited with the sarrafs of Lahore and
  that money for their expenses was being sent through Muhammad Hafiz (pp. 178–179, folios 181–182). He
  denied it flatly; the Maharaja "قرآن منگکو[OCR?] آکر کہا کہ اگر تو سچا ہی تو کہہ[OCR?] قسم", had the
  Quran brought and told him to swear if he were truthful, and he swore; investigation showed the oath
  false, and the Maharaja "اسکے دونو ہاتہ قطع کرادئے", had both his hands cut off — after which he was
  known as **Hafiz Tunda[OCR?]**, the handless (p. 179, folio 182). "ایک سواری میں پنجاب کے ملک میں اسکا
  کوئی ثانی نہ تہا", in horsemanship he had no equal in the land of the Punjab; in old age "مہاراجہ نبہا
  پال[OCR?]" summoned him and he stayed about ten years, went blind there, returned to Lahore and died
  "سنہ ١٢٩٣[OCR?] ہجری" (1293 AH[OCR?]) (p. 179, folio 182).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Shop rents made waqf for a mosque.** At the mosque of Hafiz Chabuk Sawar, he raised the plinth a
  storey high, built shops beneath the mosque on its south and west — "دو کا تین[OCR?] بنوائیں" — and
  **"اور کرایہ دوکانون کا وقف کر دیا"**, made the rent of the shops waqf (p. 178, folio 181). The clearest
  statement of shop-rent endowment in this chunk, and the model for the arrangements below.
- **Shop rents taken by the imam, and darweshes housed above the shops.** At Moran's mosque, shops stand
  east and west of the stair and door "جنکا کرایہ امام مسجد لیتا ہے", whose rent the imam of the mosque
  takes; above them are lattice-windowed seating rooms "جسمیں مسجد کے درویش قیام پذیر ہیں", in which the
  darweshes of the mosque reside (p. 180, folio 183). The endowment supports an office (imamat) and a
  resident body of darweshes at once.
- **An imamat appointed by the Maharaja's order, and a madrasa that died with British schooling.** When
  Moran's mosque was finished "مسجد کی امامت مولانا غلام رسول و غلام اللہ کو مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ کے حکم سے
  سپرد ہوئی" — the imamat was entrusted to **Maulana غلام رسول** and **غلام اللہ** by the order of Maharaja
  Ranjit Singh, "کیونکہ اُس وقت لاہور میں اُن سے زیادہ کوئی مولوی و مدرس نہ تہا اور وہ تمام پنجاب میں
  اُستاد الکل تہے", because at that time there was no greater maulvi and teacher in Lahore and they were
  the teachers of all the Punjab. With their residence a madrasa began, the mosque's fame "ملکوں میں ہو
  گئی", spread across countries, and students came from far off; the madrasa ran until the end of Sikh
  rule[OCR?] ("اخیر علمداری[OCR?] سکہی[OCR?] تاک[OCR?]"). "جب وہ دونو حضرات ... فوت ہو گئے اور عملداری
  انگریزی میں علمی[OCR?] دہ[OCR?] مدارس جاری ہو گئے تو سلسلہ اس مدرسہ کا ٹوٹ گیا" — when both died and
  schools were started under English administration, the chain of this madrasa broke, and their sons
  **خلیفہ احمد الدین** and **حمید الدین** took government service instead (p. 180, folio 183). A
  teaching institution displaced by the colonial school system, stated as cause and effect by the author.
- **The imam's family, traced to the present day of writing.** Khalifa Hamid ud Din, "اُن خاندان کا چشم و
  چراغ", still holds the imamat of the mosque and students of the religious sciences profit from him
  (pp. 180–181, folios 183–184). Ghulam Rasul's only son **خلیفہ غلام یاسین[OCR?]** died young, leaving
  **مولوی غلام محمد** and **غلام مرتضی**. Ghulam Allah had five sons: **امام الدین** (dead); **نظام الدین**,
  who "شہر بمبئی میں فی سبیل اللہ درس پڑہاتا ہے", teaches without payment in the city of Bombay;
  **احمد بخش**, "بمقام جہلم سرکاری مدرسہ میں مدرس", teacher in the government madrasa at Jhelum;
  **خلیفہ غلام محی الدین**, teaching in the "راہوں[OCR?] ضلع جالندہر" area (Rahon[OCR?], Jalandhar
  district); and **خلیفہ حمید الدین**, "لاہور گورنمنٹ سکول میں مدرس", teacher in the Lahore Government
  School, learned in both religious and worldly sciences. Their father was **غلام حبیب**, but they took
  their learning from **مولوی غلام غوریہ[OCR?]**, their real maternal uncle and also Ghulam Rasul's
  father-in-law (p. 181, folio 184). **A shrine-mosque lineage moving wholesale into government
  employment within two generations** — three of the five sons hold state teaching posts.
- **Caretaking vested in the founder's son.** Of the mosque of Nawab Imam ud Din Khan: "بانی مسجد کا بیٹا
  نواب محبوب سبحانی[OCR?] تا دم تحریر موجود ہے اور مسجد کی خبر گیری اسکو متعلق ہے" — the founder's son
  Nawab Mahbub Subhani[OCR?] is alive as of writing and the care of the mosque belongs to him (p. 178,
  folio 181).
- **A mosque built by community subscription after the founder's death, with a named official's
  donation.** Mulla Majid died before his rebuilt mosque was whitewashed; "محلے والون نے روپیہ جمع کیا",
  the people of the mohalla collected money, and of it "بہت سا روپیہ سید حیدر شاہ سپہر[OCR?] و بزر[OCR?]
  محکمہ انہار نے فی سبیل اللہ دیا" — a large sum was given fi sabil Allah by **سید حیدر شاہ** of the
  **محکمہ انہار** (the Canal Department); with the remainder the repair and whitewash were done and the
  mosque completed (p. 183, folio 186). A colonial department's officer funding a walled-city mosque.
- **A rais of Lahore rebuilding a mosque at his own cost**, "اپنی لاگت سے", in 1294 AH[OCR?] (p. 182,
  folio 185).
- **Rebuilding on older foundations is the rule, not the exception.** Four of the six mosques replace an
  earlier mosque on the same ground: Hafiz's ("پہلے بہی اس جگہ مسجد تہی", p. 178, folio 181); Bokan Khan's,
  where a wide mosque "زمانہ سلف کی" was demolished first (p. 181, folio 184); Imam Shah Wali's ("پہلے
  یہاں مسجد تہی", p. 182, folio 185); and Mulla Majid's own first mosque, demolished by himself (p. 183,
  folio 186).
- **Liveliness tied to the founder's life, and its opposite.** Of Bokan Khan's mosque: **"جبتک بانی
  کعبہ[OCR?] زندہ رہا اس مسجد میں کمال رونق رہی اب کم رونق ہے"** — as long as the founder lived the mosque
  had the fullest liveliness; now it is little frequented (p. 182, folio 185). Of Mulla Majid's: "اس مسجد
  میں رونق بہت رہتی ہے ۔ سامان روشنی و فرش و فروش[OCR?] وغیرہ سب مہیا و موجود رہتا ہے وعظ بہی اکثر ہوتا
  ہے" — this mosque keeps much liveliness, lighting equipment and floor coverings are all provided, and
  preaching (وعظ) is frequent (p. 184, folio 187).
- **Hujras for darweshes and a garden inside the mosque enclosure.** Bokan Khan's mosque area is divided
  in three: a garden, "درویشون کے رہنے کے لئے چند حجرے" on the south, and the mosque with its well,
  bath-house and saqawa (p. 182, folio 185).
- **Teaching as a resident use of the mosque hujra.** In the wide double-doored hujra at Mulla Majid's
  mosque, "اسمیں مولوی محمد بخش درس پڑہاتا ہے", Maulvi Muhammad Bakhsh gives instruction (p. 183,
  folio 186).
- **Building dates as this chunk prints them**: "سنہ ١٢٦٦ ہجری" for Nawab Imam ud Din Khan's mosque
  (p. 177, folio 180), with marginal numerals "٧٦[OCR?] ١٢" beside its first chronogram (p. 177,
  folio 180) and "٩٦[OCR?] ١٢" beside the second (p. 178, folio 181); "سنہ ١٢٢٣[OCR?] ہجری" for Moran's
  (p. 179, folio 182) with marginal "٢٤[OCR?] ١٢" beside its chronogram (p. 180, folio 183);
  "سنہ ١٢٥٥[OCR?] ہجری" for Bokan Khan's (p. 181, folio 184) with marginal "١٢٥٧[OCR?]" beside its
  chronogram (p. 182, folio 185); "سنہ ١٢٩٤[OCR?] ہجری" for Imam Shah Wali's (p. 182, folio 185);
  "سنہ ١٢٣٥[OCR?]" and "سنہ ١٢٩٤" together on the inscription at Mulla Majid's (p. 183, folio 186);
  "سمت ١٨٩٥ بکرمی" for Ram Singh's samadh (p. 190, folio 193). **The text's year and the marginal numeral
  disagree at three of the four mosques that have both** — see Doubts before using any of them.
- **A named local poet supplies a chronogram.** Of the qita over Bokan Khan's door: "چونکہ یہ قطعہ تصنیف
  کیا ہوا فرید شاعر لاہور کا ہے مقولہ فرید نیچے تحریر ہے" — since this qita is the composition of
  **فرید**, poet of Lahore, "the saying of Farid" is written beneath it (p. 182, folio 185).
- **Twice-weekly attendance and arifana qawwali at Chhajju Bhagat's chaubara.** "آٹھ روز میں ہر روز دو
  شنبہ و سہ شنبہ معتقد لوگ ہندو زن و مرد حاضر ہوتے ہیں اور قوالی عارفانہ راگ میں ہوتی ہے" — in eight days,
  on every Monday and Tuesday, devout Hindu men and women attend, and qawwali in the arifana raga is
  performed (p. 185, folio 188). Qawwali at a Hindu bhagat's samadh, on a fixed weekly rhythm.
- **A sajjada nashin installed by the rulers of the city.** "سب کے پہلے تین حاکمان شہر لاہور کیوقت باوا
  پرسوتم داس جے پور سے آیا" — earliest of all, in the time of the **three rulers of the city of Lahore**,
  **باوا پرسوتم داس** came from **جے پور** (Jaipur); being an ascetic and God-fearing man, "اُن
  میتون[OCR?] حاکموں نے ملکر اُسکو اس مندر کا سجادہ نشتین[OCR?] مقرر کر دیا", those rulers together
  appointed him **sajjada nashin of this mandir**, when only the old chaubara and a boundary wall stood.
  He worked hard at populating the place and "بڑا مندر طلائی کام والا اُس نے بنوایا", had the big mandir
  with gold work built, then died (p. 186, folio 189). **The state naming the sajjada of a Hindu samadh,
  and the term سجادہ نشین used for it.**
- **The succession of mahants at Chhajju Bhagat's.** After Parsotam Das, "باوا گودڑ[OCR?] جانشین و مہنت
  بنا", Bawa Godar[OCR?] became janishin and mahant; after him **دوارکا داس**; after him "باوا ہر
  بہجن[OCR?] و سفر[OCR?] مہنت ہوا جو نامور و مشہور ہوا", Bawa Har Bhajan[OCR?], who became renowned
  (p. 186, folio 189). The chunk also names the "گدی نشتین[OCR?]" of the mandir in the same breath as the
  "داو پنتہے[OCR?]" — gaddi nashin and sajjada nashin used of the same office on one page.
- **A celibacy rule and a sect's own rule-book.** "مہنت اس مندر کے جو داو پنتہے[OCR?] میں یا مجرد[OCR?]
  رہتے ہیں شادی کرنا اُنہیں ممنوع ہے" — the mahants of this mandir who are of the Dau Panth[OCR?] or live
  celibate are forbidden to marry; "اس فرقہ کا گرنتہہ مصنفہ مہنت داو مرام[OCR?] علیحدہ ہے جو بخط
  شاستری[OCR?] لکہا ہوا ہے", this sect's granth, composed by Mahant Dau Maram[OCR?], is a separate book
  written in Shastri[OCR?] script, containing the themes of the worship of the one God; and
  **"گوشت کہانا شراب پینا جہوٹ بولنا اور سود کہانا ... منع ہے"** — eating meat, drinking wine, telling
  lies and **taking interest** are forbidden, as is being dependent on anyone (p. 186, folio 189). A
  monastic rule stated in full, from a money-lending city's own history, with usury named.
- **The chaubara's estate.** "یہ مکان چوبارہ بہت بڑا مکان ہے جبکو[OCR?] ایک چہوٹا اُسما[OCR?] گاوں تصور کر
  لیں تو بجا ہے" — the chaubara property is so large one might reasonably reckon it a small village; it
  has a boundary wall, **two wells and cultivated land** ("دو چاہ و زمین مزروعہ[OCR?]"), and "باہر کی
  ملحقہ زمین میں اب مہنت نے دوکانیں بنوالی ہیں", the mahant has now had shops built on the adjoining land
  outside (p. 186, folio 189). The mahant developing endowment land commercially, as of the date of
  writing.
- **Vows at Sitla Mata tied to smallpox, and offerings on recovery.** Worship is constant but "خاص پرستش
  اس حالت میں ہوتی ہے جب چیچک کی بیماری کا زور ہوتا ہے", the special worship comes when smallpox
  (چیچک) is at its height; "ہزاروں لوگ جنکے بچے چیچک کی بیماری سے اچہے ہوتے ہیں" come in great joy to
  worship the devi and "حتی المقدور نذرانہ چڑہاتے ہیں", offer nazrana as far as they can (p. 187,
  folio 190).
- **A shrine shared by both communities, stated as such.** **"یہ استہان ہندو مسلمان دو نو قومہون[OCR?] کا
  مرجع ہے"** — this asthan is the resort of both the Hindu and the Muslim communities; besides Hindus,
  "اہل اسلام بہی بعد شفا پانے بمارضہ[OCR?] چیچک کے اپنے بچون کو یہان لاکر نذرانہ چڑہاتے ہیں", the people
  of Islam too bring their children here after recovery from smallpox and offer nazrana (p. 187,
  folio 190). The single most citable line in the chunk for the archive's shared-shrine material.
- **The mahant's income at Sitla Mata: rents plus daily offerings, on land absorbed into the endowment.**
  Before the Sikh kingdom the site was a small chabutra; a very large building followed, and "بہت
  لاعداد[OCR?] امناوہ[OCR?] زمین جو مہنت نے اپنے مکان کے متعلق کر لی تہی وہ اب لکہبت[OCR?] اس استہان کی
  قرار پا گئی ہے" — much land which the mahant had attached to his own building has now been settled as
  belonging[OCR?] to the asthan; "کرایہ اُسکا سب مہنت لیتا ہے اور چڑاوے کی روز مرہ آمدنی علاوہ ہے", the
  mahant takes all its rent, and the daily income from offerings is besides (p. 187, folio 190). Pukka
  shops flank the gate "جسمین کرایہ دار رہتے ہیں", occupied by tenants (p. 187, folio 190).
- **Endowment land mortgaged, and the colonial state buying land for a hospital road.** Outside the
  southern wall much land belongs to the mandir, and "باہر کی سب زمین شہا[OCR?] گر مہنت سے گروی[OCR?]
  ہوئی ہے" — all the land outside is mortgaged (گروی) from the mahant; "اور کسی عقد[OCR?] جد[OCR?] سے
  زمین اُسمیں سے سرکار نے لیکر ہوسپتال کیو سطر[OCR?] سیدہی سڑک نکلوالی ہے اور اُسکی عوض میں زر نقد بہی
  دیا اور زمین کی عوض زمین بہی مرحمت کی" — the **government (سرکار)** took land out of it and had a
  straight road cut toward the **hospital (ہوسپتال)**, and in exchange gave cash and also granted land in
  place of land (p. 188, folio 191). Acquisition, compensation in cash and in kind, on temple endowment
  land: the chunk's most directly usable piece of colonial revenue material.
- **The mahant's livestock kept inside the enclosure** — "مہنت کے گائے بیل بہینس وغیرہ باندہے جاتے ہیں",
  his cows, bullocks and buffaloes tethered in the rooms beside the mandir (p. 188, folio 191).
- **Cultivated land and offerings as the mahant's income at the Thakur Dwara.** "احاطہ کے باہر پیچہ[OCR?]
  مزروعہ زمین بہی متعلق [illegible] س مکان کے ہے جسکی آمدنی مہنت لیتا ہے", cultivated land outside the
  enclosure also belongs to this building and the mahant takes its income; and "چڑہاوہ کی آمدنی بہی مہنت
  لیتا ہے", the income from offerings is likewise taken by the mahant (p. 189, folio 192).
- **A state-funded samadh left unfinished, and its patronage passing to an heir.** Raja Teja Singh died
  "سمت ١٩١٩" (Sammat 1919); after his death "را[OCR?] سے مول سنگہ مختار ریاست نے اہتمام اس سادہ[OCR?] کی
  تعمیر کا کیا" — Mul Singh[OCR?], **مختار ریاست** (agent of the state), undertook the building of the
  samadh; "اب راجہ ہر بنس[OCR?] سنگہ وارث ریاست نے اختیار کامل پایا ہے شاید وہ اس عمارت کو با انجام
  پہنچایا[OCR?] ہیں", now Raja Har Bans[OCR?] Singh, heir of the state, has gained full authority, and
  perhaps he will bring the building to completion (p. 190, folio 193). Construction of a tomb as a
  charge on a princely estate, with the author noting the work stalled at the date of writing.
- **An oath on the Quran as court procedure, and mutilation as its penalty.** In Maharaja Sher Singh's
  inquiry into Muhammad Hafiz, the Quran was brought for the oath, and the false oath was answered by the
  amputation of both hands (pp. 178–179, folios 181–182).
- **Coinage struck in a courtesan's name** — "مورانشاہی[OCR?] روپیہ پیسے" still held as keepsakes at the
  date of writing (p. 179, folio 182); the mosque she built came out of the same period of authority
  (p. 179, folio 182).
- **A daroga of the Ravi appointed by the author of this book.** The chunk's opening fragment ends: an
  officer served the author faithfully "آخر میں دم تک ... بکمال دیانت و امانت" to his last breath, and
  "اب اسکا بیٹا عبدالرحیم پل[OCR?] را[OCR?] جگہ ہاٹ[OCR?] راوی کا داروغہ بانتخب[OCR?] مولف کتاب نے مقرر کیا
  ہے" — now his son **عبدالرحیم** has been appointed daroga of the ... of the **Ravi** by the selection of
  the author of the book (p. 177, folio 180). Office held in patrimony, and the historian himself as the
  appointing authority.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Of Chhajju Bhagat generally**: "کی کرامتیں بہت سی زبان زد خلایق ہیں" — his karamat are many and on the
  tongues of the people (p. 185, folio 188; the sentence's opening is lost to an `[illegible]` at the foot
  of p. 184, folio 187).
- **The stolen ashrafi and the whipped saint.** Told as "نقل": a royal servant kept a bag of ashrafis with
  his wife, from which she stole one; he brought the bag to Chhajju's shop; on counting, one ashrafi was
  short; he accused Chhajju — "اشرفی میری تو نے چہرالی[OCR?] ہے" — struck him with a whip (چابک) and
  searched the shop, found nothing and went away ashamed. At home he found his wife stricken and at the
  point of death, and no treatment helped; everyone told him "تو نے آج چہجو بہگت صراف کو جسنے کبہی کسیکا
  مال نا حق نہیں کہایا چابک مارا ہے یہ اُسی ظلم کی نکبت ہے" — you have today whipped Chhajju Bhagat the
  sarraf, who never wrongfully ate anyone's property; this is the calamity of that wrong. The wife
  confessed she had taken the ashrafi; husband and wife went to the shop and had their fault forgiven,
  "جب چہجو رضی ہوا تو اُسی وقت عورت رضی ہو گئی", and the moment Chhajju was satisfied the woman was well
  (p. 185, folio 188). A money-changer saint whose honesty in trade is the ground of the miracle.
- **A disappearance instead of a death, on common report.** "عام مشہور اسطرح پر ہے کہ چہجو بہگت کی وفات
  ظاہر طور پر وقوع میں نہیں آئی بلکہ چوبارہ میں بیٹہا ہوا ہی غائب ہو گیا اُسی مقام پر سماوہ[OCR?] بنائی
  گئی" — it is commonly said that Chhajju Bhagat's death did not occur outwardly, but that sitting in the
  chaubara he vanished, and the samadh was built at that very spot (p. 186, folio 189). Attributed by the
  author to common report, and set immediately after the dated death he has just given.
- **A cure from the leaves of the worshipped tree at Sitla Mata.** On the raised chabutra stand pipal
  trees and a "خنڈری[OCR?]" tree "جسکو لوگ پوجتے ہیں", which people worship; and if smallpox pustules
  [OCR?] appear on the eyelid[OCR?], "اسی درخت کے پتون کا سفوف اسپر ٹوانتے[OCR?] ہیں جسے بیمار اچہا ہو
  جاتا ہے", the powder of this tree's leaves is applied, by which the sick person recovers (p. 187,
  folio 190).
- **A worshipped stone lion** in the verandah of the Sitla Devi mandir, on a chabutra, "اُسکی بہی پوجا
  ہوتی ہے" (p. 188, folio 191).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author states the plan of the new section and its principle of selection.** "یہ تذکرہ بہی دو حصون
  میں منقسم ہوکر بیان ہوگا" — this account too will be given in two parts: first the buildings of the
  religion of the Hindu people that are famous outside the city's wall (حصار), then the mosques, tombs and
  shrines of the people of Islam. And the selection rule: "واضح ہو کہ ہندؤن کے شوالے دہاکر[OCR?] دوارے و
  دیوی دوارے و دہرم سالے اس شہر کے باہر بہی بہت ہیں جسطرح پر کہ مسلمانون کی مسجدین و مزار ہیں **مگر اس
  موقع پر نامی مکانات کی تشریح سے غرض ہے**" — let it be clear that Hindus' shiwalas, thakur dwaras[OCR?],
  devi dwaras and dharamsalas outside this city are as many as the Muslims' mosques and mazars, but on
  this occasion the purpose is the description of the **notable** buildings only (p. 184, folio 187).
  **The stated criterion is fame, not completeness** — decisive for what this book can and cannot be
  expected to yield on the smaller sites.
- **He exercises that discretion explicitly.** Of the Thakur Dwara's enclosure: "احاطے کے اندر اور بہی
  مکانات ہیں جنکی تحریر کی ضرورت نہیں" — there are other buildings inside the enclosure which there is no
  need to write down (p. 189, folio 192).
- **He writes himself into the administration he describes.** The opening fragment records the author
  (مولف) as satisfied with an officer's service and as the appointing authority for that officer's son
  (p. 177, folio 180); the present tense of the appointment dates the passage to the time of writing.
- **A recurring "second Kaaba" formula in the chronograms he transcribes.** Three of the four chronogram
  qitas in this chunk end in it: "بگفتا فی الحقیقت کعبتہ اللہ" (p. 177, folio 180); "بہ دنیا از امام
  الدین بنا شد کعبہ ثانی" (p. 178, folio 181); "کعبہ ثانی بنا شد این مقام" (p. 182, folio 185); and all
  four put the date in the mouth of the unseen voice — "ہاتف غیب", "ہاتف", "ہاتفی" (pp. 177, 178, 180,
  182; folios 180, 181, 183, 185). A house style for Lahore foundation inscriptions, as the book preserves
  them.
- **He judges buildings aesthetically and records decline without comment.** "یہ عمارت نہایت مطبوع و
  دلپسند بنائی گئی ہے جسکے دیکہنے سے انسان کی طبیعت و مہتا[OCR?] میں آجاتی ہے" of Raja Teja Singh's samadh
  (p. 190, folio 193), against the flat "اب کم رونق ہے" of Bokan Khan's mosque (p. 182, folio 185) and the
  bare "یہ مکان ابہی نا تیار ہے" of the samadh (p. 190, folio 193).

## Citable passages

- "یہ استہان ہندو مسلمان دو نو قومہون[OCR?] کا مرجع ہے۔" — "This asthan is the resort of both the Hindu
  and the Muslim communities." (p. 187, folio 190)
- "اور کرایہ دوکانون کا وقف کر دیا۔" — "And he made the rent of the shops waqf." (p. 178, folio 181)
- "اسکے حکم کو لوگ مہاراجہ کے حکم سے زیادہ مضبوط تصور کرتے تہے۔" — of Moran: "People reckoned her order
  stronger than the Maharaja's order." (p. 179, folio 182)
- "گوشت کہانا شراب پینا جہوٹ بولنا اور سود کہانا ... منع ہے۔" — the Dau Panth[OCR?] rule: "Eating meat,
  drinking wine, telling lies and taking interest are forbidden." (p. 186, folio 189)
- "جبتک بانی کعبہ[OCR?] زندہ رہا اس مسجد میں کمال رونق رہی اب کم رونق ہے۔" — "As long as the founder lived
  this mosque had the fullest liveliness; now it is little frequented." (p. 182, folio 185)

## Doubts

- **No archive row is the subject of any entry in this chunk, and nothing here should be mapped to one.**
  The chunk describes six mosques, one bhagat's chaubara, one devi asthan, one thakur dwara and three
  samadhs, none of which carries an archive id. Three near-misses to guard against:
  - **"شاہ بلاول لاہوری"** (p. 184, folio 187), named only as a friend of Chhajju Bhagat, must **not** be
    mapped to `shah-noorani-shrine-syed-bilawal-shah-noorani`. That row's figure is Syed Bilawal Shah
    Noorani in the Lahoot Lamakan valley, Khuzdar, Balochistan; the chunk's man is styled *Lahori*, and the
    chunk gives him no grave, no date and no order. Leave unmapped.
  - **The Thakur Dwara of Bawa Mangal Das**, whose large shrine holds images of Sri Krishan Ji and Radha
    Ji (p. 189, folio 192), must **not** be mapped to `krishna-mandir-ravi-road`. The chunk places it
    outside the Delhi[OCR?] Gate, not on Ravi Road, and names it a thakur dwara after its founder.
  - **Maharaja Ranjit Singh is named on six pages** (pp. 177, 179, 180, 181; folios 180, 182, 183, 184) but
    only ever as patron or ruler; his own samadhi is not described here, so nothing attaches to
    `samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh`.
- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the subject is unnamed.** p. 177 (folio 180) begins inside a notice about
  an officer of "عہدہ اسپ اور سپیر[OCR?]" whose son Abdul Rahim the author appointed daroga of a Ravi
  ...[OCR?]; the building or institution the notice belongs to is in the previous chunk. **Do not name it
  from context.**
- **The chunk closes mid-sentence.** p. 191 (folio 194) breaks off in the description of the dome over
  Ram Singh's samadh/shiwala — "طولانی گنبد عالیشان سر بغلک[OCR?] جو دو ہی جسپر طلائی کلس چڑہا[OCR?]
  چکا[OCR?] [illegible]" — so whether the gilded kalas was in place is not established, and the entry
  continues beyond this chunk.
- **Text year against marginal numeral: three disagreements, none of them resolvable here.** Nawab Imam ud
  Din Khan's mosque is dated "سنہ ١٢٦٦ ہجری" in the text (p. 177, folio 180) while the margin beside its
  first chronogram reads "٧٦[OCR?] ١٢" and beside the second "٩٦[OCR?] ١٢" (pp. 177–178, folios 180–181) —
  three different values for one mosque, two of them OCR-flagged. Moran's mosque: text "سنہ ١٢٢٣[OCR?]"
  (p. 179, folio 182) against margin "٢٤[OCR?] ١٢" (p. 180, folio 183). Bokan Khan's: text
  "سنہ ١٢٥٥[OCR?]" (p. 181, folio 184) against margin "١٢٥٧[OCR?]" (p. 182, folio 185). **Carry both
  numbers wherever either is used.** The marginal numerals are printed split and out of order in the
  transcription ("٧٦[OCR?] ١٢"), which is itself a transcription artefact a human should check against the
  lithograph.
- **A marginal "١٢٩٦" with nothing to attach it to.** p. 186 (folio 189) prints "[margin] ١٢٩٦"
  immediately before Chhajju Bhagat's death date, where there is no chronogram verse. Unlike the other
  margins it is unflagged. It cannot be assigned from the chunk. These marginal numerals are the only
  non-body material in the chunk; there is no watermark, library stamp or rights line anywhere in it.
- **The samadh heading names a different raja from the entry body.** The heading reads "سماوہ راجہ سچا
  سنگہ[OCR?] و جمعدار خوشحال سنگہ و رام سنگہ" (p. 189, folio 192), but the entry itself twice calls him
  "راجہ تیجا سنگہ" (p. 190, folio 193). One of the two readings is wrong; the heading's name is
  OCR-flagged and the body's is not. **A human must read the printed heading before either name is used.**
- **Chhajju Bhagat's two dates are both flagged and both unverified.** "سمت ایک ہزار چہہ[OCR?] سو
  چہیانوین[OCR?] بکرمی" and "سنہ ١٠٥٣[OCR?] ہجری" are given as equivalents (p. 186, folio 189), while the
  entry opens by placing him in the reign of Shah Jahan (p. 184, folio 187). The Bikrami year is spelled
  out in words with two flags inside the number; do not convert or reconcile either date from here.
- **"مسجد بیچ گئی ہے" — the mosque appears to have been sold, in a badly damaged sentence.** At p. 178
  (folio 181) the passage on the back of Hafiz Chabuk Sawar's mosque reads "مسجد کی پشت پر پشت سنگ کا
  ہے[OCR?] متعلق مسجد ہی تہی میں جو نہر[OCR?] کی شرک میں اُسی میں ہیں مگر مسجد بیچ گئی ہے", which is not
  construable as transcribed but ends in a statement that the mosque was sold. **This is potentially the
  most consequential sentence in the chunk for the mosque's status and it must be re-read from the PDF
  before anything is said about it.**
- **Muhammad Hafiz's patron and death year are unresolved.** "مہاراجہ نبہا پال[OCR?]" (p. 179, folio 182)
  cannot be identified from the chunk, and his death is "سنہ ١٢٩٣[OCR?] ہجری" with the year flagged. The
  substance of the charge against him — "لکہار[OCR?] و پیہ[OCR?] سرواران[OCR?] سندہانوالیہ کا" (p. 178,
  folio 181) — is illegible enough that what was deposited with the sarrafs cannot be stated.
- **The Thakur Dwara's builder is lost.** "یہ مندر در بسبت[OCR?] سنگہ مہاراجہ کی علمداری[OCR?] میں تعمیر
  ہوا تہا" (p. 189, folio 192) — a Maharaja's name is there in the text and the OCR has not recovered it.
  Do not supply it.
- **The location of the first mosque is doubtful in a way that affects the address.** "محلہ چہلہ[OCR?]
  میں حمام میں" (p. 177, folio 180): both whether the mohalla is Chehla and whether "حمام" is a
  place-name or a bath-house are unsettled by the chunk. Likewise "دہل[OCR?] محلہ گزر موچی دروازے" recurs
  three times (pp. 181, 182, 183; folios 184, 185, 186) with the same flagged first word each time, so the
  qualifier before "مohalla/guzar of Mochi Gate" is unknown.
- **The sect, its founder-author and its script at Chhajju Bhagat's are all flagged**: "داو پنتہے[OCR?]"
  (twice, pp. 186; folio 189), "مہنت داو مرام[OCR?]" and "بخط شاستری[OCR?]" (p. 186, folio 189). The
  panth's name is the single most useful term in the passage and is the least secure.
- **"تین حاکمان شہر لاہور" are unnamed** (p. 186, folio 189) — the three rulers of Lahore who appointed the
  first sajjada are not identified, and the chunk gives no date for the appointment.
- **The list of subsidiary samadhs at Chhajju Bhagat's is heavily damaged** and should be treated as
  unusable until re-read: "جیرام بہگت ۔ ودا گہو بہگت[OCR?] و جوالا بہشت[OCR?] و باوا کہاکر داس[OCR?] و
  دوارکا داس و کہاگر داس[OCR?] و کور داس[OCR?] و را[OCR?] ایہمت[OCR?] و بدر[OCR?] کا بہگت[OCR?]"
  (p. 185, folio 188) continuing "دیا بہگت و گہمنڈ[OCR?] لمی بہگت و دیالو بہگت و شیو دیال و شیو[OCR?]
  چوبرے[OCR?]" (p. 186, folio 189). Note that "دوارکا داس" appears both in this list and as a mahant in
  the succession (p. 186, folio 189); whether these are the same man is not stated.
- **Three `[illegible]` spans**: at the foot of p. 184 (folio 187), losing the transition into Chhajju
  Bhagat's karamat; inside the Thakur Dwara's land sentence at p. 189 (folio 192), "متعلق [illegible] س
  مکان کے ہے"; and at the end of p. 191 (folio 194).
- **Mulla Majid's two dates are internally consistent but unevenly flagged**: "سنہ ١٢٣٥[OCR?]" appears
  twice with a flag (p. 183, folio 186) while the rebuild year "سنہ ١٢٩٤" on the same inscription is
  unflagged; the same "سنہ ١٢٩٤[OCR?]" is flagged one page earlier for Imam Shah Wali's mosque (p. 182,
  folio 185). Two neighbouring mosques dated to the same year, one flagged and one not.
- **The donor of the completion money is only half legible**: "سید حیدر شاہ سپہر[OCR?] و بزر[OCR?] محکمہ
  انہار" (p. 183, folio 186) — his post in the Canal Department cannot be read.
- **The imam-family names carry flags that affect the lineage**: "خلیفہ غلام یاسین[OCR?]", "راہوں[OCR?]
  ضلع جالندہر", "مولوی غلام غوریہ[OCR?]", "صاحبزادوین[OCR?]" (pp. 180–181, folios 183–184), and
  "نواب محبوب سبحانی[OCR?]" for the caretaker of the first mosque (p. 178, folio 181).
- **Architectural vocabulary recurs under a flag throughout and should be normalised by a human once**:
  "سقف قالبوتی[OCR?]" on seven pages (pp. 177, 178, 180, 183, 188, 191; folios 180, 181, 183, 186, 191,
  194), "سقاوہ[OCR?]" (pp. 182, 183; folios 185, 186), "سماوہ[OCR?]" / "سادہ[OCR?]" for samadh throughout
  (pp. 184–191; folios 187–194), "کلس[OCR?]" (pp. 180, 191; folios 183, 194) and "زینہ چہرہ[OCR?]/
  چہڑہ[OCR?] کر" (pp. 177, 178, 180; folios 180, 181, 183). The flags are on the spelling, not on the
  sense, but the samadh/سادہ alternation makes the two marble buildings outside Masti Gate easy to confuse
  in any index built from this text.
- **Both Persian chronogram qitas are transcribed with flags inside the rhyme words** — "حسب
  ولنخواہ[OCR?]" (p. 177, folio 180), "موفق شدہ[OCR?]", "خدا دادی[OCR?]", "فیض ربانی[OCR?]",
  "میبرد[OCR?] از بہترین[OCR?] تعمیر تسخیر[OCR?]", "سر امجد افگندہ[OCR?]" (p. 178, folio 181),
  "ہست ہ[OCR?] تعمیر لہند[OCR?]" and "و را[OCR?] و ارا[OCR?]" (p. 180, folio 183), and
  "چون زمکن[OCR?] خان" (p. 181, folio 184) — so **no chronogram in this chunk can be counted to check a
  date**, and the name in Bokan Khan's own chronogram ("زمکن[OCR?] خان" against the entry's "بوکن خان") is
  itself in doubt.
