# tareekh_lahore — chunk 027 — pp. 380–395

**A run of British-era public buildings in Lahore, described by the man who built several of them.** The
chunk opens mid-entry inside an unnamed **ہسپتال** (hospital), then gives, as titled sections, **مکان نیو
کالج لاہور** (the New College building), **مکان آرٹ سکول یعنی مدرسہ صنعت کاری** (the Art School, i.e. the
school of craftsmanship), **کوٹھی تار گھر** (the telegraph office), **چھاونی میانمیر کا گرجا** (the church
of Mian Mir cantonment), and **گورنمنٹ ہوس یعنی کوٹھی نواب لفٹنٹ گورنر بہادر** (Government House, the
Lieutenant Governor's kothi), breaking off mid-sentence in the last (pp. 380–395, folios 387–402). No site
in this chunk matches an archive row. Its value to this project is **institutional and economic**:
sanctioned sums and actual expenditure on four buildings, land compensation paid to village zamindars,
religiously segregated hospital kitchens, a craft exhibition run as a sales fair, an 800-seat church kept
by government grant plus subscription, and — at Government House — a Sufi tomb whose grave was demolished,
whose dome became a wrestling ground and then an officer's kothi, and whose site became the seat of the
Punjab government.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker**, and in this chunk the printed folio runs seven ahead of
the PDF index throughout: p. 380 → folio 387, p. 395 → folio 402, each marker reading exactly that. The
folios below are only the ones the chunk prints; nothing is extrapolated.

  lamps "ایک سے ایک قیمتی", each costlier than the last, and **چھہ عدد جھاڑ[OCR?]**, six very costly

  the **kitchen, with four separate rooms**: in one, food is cooked for **Hindu** patients, in the second

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author writes as the builder and reports official approval as the measure of success.** Twice he
  records that officials were pleased: of the Art School, "جس سے حکام عالی مقام بھی خورسند ہو گئے" (p. 384,
  folio 391), and of the telegraph office, "مصالح عمدہ ادل درجہ کا لگایا گیا جسکو دیکھ کر حکام نہایت خوش
  ہوئے", first-class materials were used, at the sight of which the officials were very pleased (p. 389,
  folio 396). Of the same building he writes "نہایت غور و محنت و جانفشانی سے بنوایا گیا", it was built
  with the utmost care, labour and self-sacrifice.
- **Buildings judged aesthetically, with the reader's pleasure as the criterion.** The New College hall's
  height "نہایت خوبصورت و مطبوع نظر آتی ہے" and its upper seating[OCR?] "مکان کی زینت کو دو چند کرتی ہیں",
  doubles the building's ornament (p. 381, folio 388); its iron chain-work makes "مکان کی شان و شوکت دو
  بالا نظر آتی ہے" (p. 383, folio 390); the Art School's carved ceiling is such "جسکو دیکھ کر صاحب نظر لوگ
  خوشتر ہو جاتے ہیں", that people of discernment are gladdened by it, and its corner minarets are "فرحت
  بخش چشم اہل نظارہ", delightful to the eye of the beholder (p. 386, folio 393); the church's marble
  columns are such "جنکے … دیکھنے سے انسان کی طبیعت میں فرحت آ جاتی ہے" (p. 392, folio 399).
- **Admiration for imitation as craftsmanship.** He dwells twice on plaster made to pass for stone — the
  church's exterior, carved so that "دور سے پتھر ہی معلوم دیتا ہے نزدیک جا کر کوئی غور سے دیکھے تو معلوم
  کرتا ہے کہ یہ پلستر چونہ کا ہے" (p. 390, folio 397), and its interior, where the ruled lines make marble
  slabs appear on the walls (p. 392, folio 399). The deception is presented as a merit, not a defect.
- **He exculpates Maharaja Ranjit Singh over the destruction of the Gilani tomb, and leaves the blame
  divided.** "یہ مقبرہ خشتی عمارت کا تھا پتھر اسپر نہیں لگایا تھا اور نہ مہاراجہ اسکو پتھر کی طمع سے
  مزور[OCR?] گروا دیتا خشتی عمارت بھی گرنے والی نہ تھی اور کشمیریان[OCR?] خشت فروش گرا لیتے" — the tomb
  was a brick building with no stone on it, **nor would the Maharaja have had it pulled down out of greed
  for stone**; the brick building was not about to fall of itself, and **the Kashmiri[OCR?] brick-sellers
  would have pulled it down** (p. 395, folio 402). In the same passage the actual demolition of the grave
  is laid at the door of **the jamadar**, not the Maharaja. The clause is partly flagged and internally
  strained; kept as printed.
- **An internal cross-reference as his authority for a ruin.** Of the Gilani mosque he does not repeat the
  account but refers to himself: "جسکی … ویرانی و بربادی کا حال پہلے حصہ کتاب ہذا میں تحریر ہو چکا ہے",
  the state of its desolation and ruin **has already been written in the first part of this book** (p. 395,
  folio 402).
- **Regnal and colonial frames used side by side without comment**: "شاہجہانی عہد", "بعہد سکھی", "مہاراجہ
  رنجیت سنگہ کیوقت", "اخیر عہد مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ میں", "زیر سایہ ابد پایہ سرکار انگریزی", "بسرکار رفیع
  مدار انگریزی کے حکم سے" (pp. 381, 388, 394–395; folios 388, 395, 401–402). In this chunk the change of
  regime appears chiefly as a change in **who commissions and who pays**: sanctioned sums, compensation to
  zamindars, a government grant matched by subscription, and a private kothi proposed for acquisition.
