# tareekh_lahore — chunk 031 — pp. 442–456

**A street-by-street survey of Hindu and Brahmo places of worship in Lahore, with two Sikh royal
women's samadhs in the middle of it.** The chunk opens mid-entry on an unnamed Thakurdwara dependent
on **مندر بانکے بہاری** inside the city (p. 442, folio 449), then runs through **مندر ٹہاکردوارہ باوا
دیہو نا داس** outside Mori Gate, the joint **سمادہ رانی راجکوران المشہور رانی نکائین و رانی چند کنور**,
four Anarkali foundations (**ٹہاکردوارہ و شوالہ لالہ جسنا[OCR?] دیبر[OCR?] و جنگم**, **ٹہاکردوارہ لالہ
مہین لال[OCR?]**, **مندر شوالہ لالہ چہوٹے لال**, **مکان مندر برہم سماج**), then **ٹہاکردوارہ پنڈت
اوقت[OCR?] ناتہہ**, **ٹہاکردوارہ چہوجہی مل کہتری**, **مندر سیتلا دیوی اندرون شہر**, **ٹہاکردوارہ عطر
سنگہ صوبہ دار**, **شوالہ دہرم چند والہ** and **ٹہاکردوارہ رائے سنگہ رسالدار**, breaking off in the
first line of **مکان مندر بال ناتہا[OCR?]** (pp. 442–456, folios 449–463).

**No site in this chunk maps to a row of the archive index**, so the "Shrines and figures in the
archive" heading is omitted; see Doubts for the two near-misses. The chunk's value to this project is
almost entirely **institutional and economic**: dated foundations in Sambat Bikrami, waqf deeds of
shops and havelis, rent assigned to a temple's expenses, a salaried Brahmin pujari paid from a
banker's kothi, a per-wedding fee, an endowment diverted by the founder's brother, and land taken
from a temple by the government for Lahore's water supply.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker**, and in this chunk each printed pair runs seven
apart: p. 442 → folio 449 and p. 456 → folio 463, each marker reading exactly that. Only the printed
folios are used below; nothing is extrapolated, and the offset is not assumed to hold outside this
chunk.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A dependent Thakurdwara whose offerings go to another temple's mahant, who names its pujari.** Of
  the unnamed building at p. 442 (folio 449): "یہ مکان متعلق مندر بانکے بہاری کے ہے جو شہر کے اندر ہے
  اور یہی مہنت اسکا چڑہاوہ لیتا ہے اور پوجاری اسکی طرف سے ٹہہرا داس[OCR?] مقرر ہے" — this building is
  **attached to Mandir Banke Bihari inside the city**, and **that same mahant takes its offerings**, and
  on his behalf **Tehra Das[OCR?]** is appointed pujari (p. 442, folio 449). The same structure of
  dependency appears elsewhere in this book, where a shrine's income follows another shrine's office.
- **A sadhu's foundation enlarged by taking in waste land.** Baba Dehona Das "بہت سی زمین کہہ
  شدرات[OCR?] کی جو چاروں طرف بیکار پڑی تہی شامل مندر کے کر لی" — took into the mandir much land lying
  idle on all four sides (the descriptor of the land is OCR-flagged) (p. 442, folio 449).
- **A courtesan's patronage of a temple: labour, a well and a sitting-place.** Moran, "سادہ مذکور کی
  معتقد تہی اُس نے مندر کی تعمیر میں بہت مدد دی چاہ بہی نیا لگوا دیا نشستگاہ بہی سراہ[OCR?] بنوا دی" —
  being the said sadhu's devotee she **gave much help in the mandir's construction, had a new well sunk
  and had a nishastgah built** (p. 442, folio 449).
- **Temple land let to a timber dealer.** "بہت سی زمین جو متعلق مندر کے مستقیمہ[OCR?] ہے اُسمیں ایک لکڑی
  فروش دوکان کرتا ہے اور کرایہ یہ مہنت کو دیتا ہے" — on much land belonging to the mandir a
  **wood-seller (لکڑی فروش)** keeps a shop and **pays the rent to the mahant** (p. 443, folio 450).
- **Succession the book will not settle, and a founder's samadh on site.** "بالفعل اس مندر کا مہنت رام
  داس سادہ پوتہ یا چیلا دیہو نی داس سکا ہے" — at present the mandir's mahant is **Ram Das Sadhu,
  grandson or chela (پوتہ یا چیلا)** of Dehoni Das — the book gives the alternative without choosing;
  "اور دیہو نی داس بانی مندر کی سمادہ پختہ مندر کے غرب کی سمت علیحدہ بنی ہوئی ہے جسکے اوپر چہوٹا سا
  گنبد ہے", and the **founder's own pakka samadh** stands separately west of the mandir under a small
  dome (p. 443, folio 450).
- **A jagir held by a Maharaja's wife.** "شیخو پورہ اور کل علاقہ ساندل بار اُسکی جاگیر میں تہا" —
  **Sheikhupura and the whole ilaqa of Sandal Bar were in Rani Nakain's jagir**, and she lived in
  Sheikhupura fort all her life[OCR?] (p. 443, folio 450).
- **A monument left unfinished by a change of regime, and then left to fall.** Of Rani Chand Kaur's
  samadh: no kalas raised, iron spike in place, doors without leaves, "جس سے معلوم ہوتا ہے کہ یہ مکان
  کسی سبب[OCR?] انقلاب سلطنت سے ناتمام رہ گیا تہا" (p. 444, folio 451). Of both samadhs' platform:
  "چار دیواری اس چبوترے کی جسپر سمادہیں بنی ہیں بہت جگہ سے بسبب عدم خبر گیری کے گر گئی ہے اور سیڑہیاں
  بہی گر گئی ہیں کوئی خبر گیر و مرمت[OCR?] کرنیوالا مکان کا موجود نہیں ہے گویا مکان لاوارث ویران ٹہرا
  ہوا ہے" — the enclosure wall has fallen in many places **for want of anyone to attend to it**, the
  steps have fallen too, **there is no one to look after or repair the building**, and it stands as
  though **ownerless and desolate** (pp. 444–445, folios 451–452).
- **A temple built in two stages out of begging and subscription, on land bought from the state.**
  "سمت ۱۹۱۰ بکرمی میں جسنا[OCR?] دیبر و جنگم نے پہلے زمین سرکار سے لی اور چاہ کلاں کہدوا لی اپنے رہنے
  کے لئے خام مکان بنوایا پہر گدائی کرکے روپیہ جمع کیا اور شوالہ کی تعمیر کی بنیاد رکہی سمت ۱۹۳۷ میں پہر
  اس نے ارادت مند لوگوں سے روپیہ جمع کرکے ٹہاکردوارہ بنوایا" — in **Sambat 1910 Bikrami (سمت ۱۹۱۰
  بکرمی)** he first **took land from the government (زمین سرکار سے لی)**, had a large well dug and built
  a kaccha house to live in; then **collected money by begging (گدائی)** and laid the foundation of the
  shiwala; then in **Sambat 1937 (سمت ۱۹۳۷)** he **collected money from devout people** and built the
  Thakurdwara, "اور اپنے نام کی نیک یادگار دنیائے ناپائیدار میں قائم کی", establishing a good memorial of
  his name in this impermanent world (p. 445, folio 452).
- **A neighbourhood's whole Hindu population as the offering base.** "تمام انار کلی کے ہندو اس جگہ
  پرستش کے واسطے حاضر ہوتے ہیں اور چڑہاوہ دیتے ہیں" — **all the Hindus of Anarkali** attend this place
  for worship and give offerings, to its owner-pujari (p. 445, folio 452).
- **A temple opening into its founder's own house, and maintained by his family.** At Mahin Lal's[OCR?]
  Thakurdwara the north door gives into the founder's house and the south into a public lane (p. 446,
  folio 453); "بانی اس مندر کا و قبیل[OCR?] اسکا اخراجات انکا ہے" — the founder of this mandir and his
  family[OCR?] bear its expenses (p. 447, folio 454).
- **A banking family's absorption of an older sadhu's shiwala, and its rebuilding.** "جب بازار انار کلی
  سرکار انگریزی [illegible] کے وقت آباد ہوا اور لالہ چہوٹے لال کے خاندان کی دوکانیں ساہوکاری[OCR?] کی
  اس موقع پر جاری ہوگئیں تو یہ مکان بہی انکے تصرف میں آگیا اسکے گرد نواح اُنہوں نے بڑی بڑی عمارتیں تعمیر
  کیں اور اس شوالہ کو از سر نو بنوایا جو قابل دید ہے" — when **Bazaar Anarkali became populated in the
  time of the English government [illegible]** and the family's **banking (ساہوکاری[OCR?]) shops** began
  at this spot, **this building too came into their possession**; they built large buildings around it
  and **rebuilt this shiwala anew**, which is worth seeing (p. 447, folio 454). The build cost is given
  as "ہزار[OCR?] روپیہ", a thousand[OCR?] rupees (p. 447, folio 454).
- **A well at a temple serving the street.** At the Chhote Lal shiwala: "اس ڈیوڑہی کے جنوبی حصہ میں چاہ
  ہے اور لوگ اُس چاہ سے پانی باہر سے بہی بہرتے ہیں اور ڈیوڑہی کے[OCR?] اُسکے اندر سے بہی پانی بہرنے کا
  راستہ رکہا ہوا ہے" — people draw water from that well **from outside as well**, and a way to draw from
  inside the deorhi is also kept (p. 447, folio 454). At the Akbari Mandi Thakurdwara the well is on
  the bazaar front "جسمیں عام و خاص پانی بہرتے ہیں اور نہاتے ہیں", where high and low draw water and
  bathe (p. 451, folio 458). At Rai Singh's Thakurdwara, by contrast, "مگر چاہ نہیں ہے جس سے کمال تنگ
  مندر میں رہنے والوں کا ہے", there is no well and those living in the mandir are in the utmost straits
  (p. 456, folio 463).
- **A salaried pujari paid from a banker's kothi, and eight shops' rent for the temple's expenses.**
  "اس مندر کا پوجاری سوج بہان[OCR?] کوکہ[OCR?] برہمن مقرر ہے جو تنخواہ مع دیگر خادمان مندر کے لالہ چہوٹے
  لال کی کوٹہی سے پاتا ہے" — the pujari **سوج بہان[OCR?] کوکہ[OCR?], Brahmin**, is appointed, who with the
  mandir's other servants **receives a salary from Lala Chhote Lal's kothi**; "باہر بازار کی طرف آٹہہ
  دوکانیں اس مندر کے متعلق ہیں جنکا کرایہ مندر کے اخراجات میں صرف ہوتا ہے" — outside, toward the bazaar,
  **eight shops (آٹہہ دوکانیں)** belong to this mandir and **their rent is spent on the mandir's

- **A bottomless water-sink under a pindi — told by the pujari.** At the Sitla Devi mandir: two holes in
  the ground beside the pindi's platform; "پوجاری کہتا ہے کہ اس جگہ قدیمی غرقی پانی کی بنی ہوئی ہے" —
  **the pujari says** an ancient water-sink was built at this place; when worshippers bathe the pindi
  "لسی[OCR?] و پانی سے", with lassi[OCR?] and water, the water goes into it, "عمیق اتنی ہے اگر سو گاگر
  پانی کی اسمیں ڈال دیں تو سما جاتا ہے باہر نہیں معلوم ہوتا کہ کدہر گیا" — it is so deep that if a
  **hundred gagars (سو گاگر)** of water were poured in it would be absorbed, and it is not known outside
  where the water went (p. 453, folio 460). The book attributes this to the pujari and does not endorse
  it.
- **A rumoured murder in the palace.** Of Rani Chand Kaur: "مشہور یہ تہا کہ مہاراجہ شیر سنگہ کے ایماء سے
  اُسکی کنیزوں نے اُسکو مار دیا تہا" — **it was rumoured** that at Maharaja Sher Singh's instigation her
  maidservants killed her (p. 444, folio 451). Reported as report, not as fact.
- No karamat of any saint is narrated in this chunk; the book's mode here is survey and account, not
  hagiography.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author marks his sources and his uncertainty.** The water-sink is "پوجاری کہتا ہے", what the
  pujari says (p. 453, folio 460); the rani's death is "مشہور یہ تہا", what was rumoured (p. 444,
  folio 451); of Bodhar Das, "اصلی نام اُسکا شاید کوئی اور تہا", **his real name was perhaps something
  else** (p. 447, folio 454); of a mahant's standing he writes "پوتہ یا چیلا", **grandson or chela**,
  without deciding (p. 443, folio 450). Building fabric, dates, rents and salaries are by contrast
  stated flatly in his own voice.
- **He cross-references his own earlier entry rather than repeating it.** Of the two Sitla Devi
  mandirs: "ایک بیرون دروازہ شاہ عالمی جسکا ذکر تحریر پر ہو چکا ہے" — one outside Shah Alami Gate,
  **whose mention has already been made in the writing** (p. 452, folio 459). The survey is being
  treated as a single cumulative text.
- **He infers a building's history from its unfinished details.** The absent kalas, bare iron spike and
  leafless doors of Rani Chand Kaur's samadh lead him to conclude "جس سے معلوم ہوتا ہے کہ یہ مکان کسی
  سبب[OCR?] انقلاب سلطنت سے ناتمام رہ گیا تہا", that the building was left unfinished by the revolution
  of the kingdom (p. 444, folio 451) — physical evidence read as political history.
- **Neglect is named as neglect, and made the explanation of ruin.** "بسبب عدم خبر گیری کے گر گئی ہے …
  کوئی خبر گیر و مرمت[OCR?] کرنیوالا مکان کا موجود نہیں ہے گویا مکان لاوارث ویران ٹہرا ہوا ہے" (pp. 444–445,
  folios 451–452) — the same diagnosis this book applies to other unmaintained monuments.
- **A comparative superlative about Lahore's temples.** Of the Shiv image at Baba Dehona Das's mandir:
  "ایسی کہ لاہور کے اور کسی ٹہاکردوارہ میں نہیں پائی جاتی" — such as is not found in any other
  Thakurdwara of Lahore (p. 443, folio 450). A further ranking claim about the Anarkali mandirs survives
  only in a damaged clause (p. 447, folio 454; see Doubts).
- **Buildings are appraised as well as described.** "اس مکان کی خوبی و رنگینی و استحکام عمارت بدرجہ غایت
  ہے" — the merit, colour and solidity of Rai Singh's building are in the highest degree, "اور نشستگاہ
  بہی عجیب و غریب سر بازار بنی ہے" (p. 456, folio 463); marble columns are "مطبوع طبع اہل دید", pleasing
  to the taste of the discerning (p. 446, folio 453); a kalas "آنکہوں کو درخشت[OCR?] کرتا ہے" (p. 448,
  folio 455) and a gilded trishul "جسکے دیکہنے سے آنکہوں میں روشنی آتی ہے" (p. 455, folio 462).
- **Foundation as memorial in an impermanent world** — the same formula this book uses of other
  builders: "اپنے نام کی نیک یادگار دنیائے ناپائیدار میں قائم کی" (p. 445, folio 452).
- **Dating frames used side by side without comment**: **Sambat Bikrami** for every Hindu and Sikh
  foundation whose year is given (سمت ۱۹۱۰, ۱۹۳۷, ۱۹۰۴[OCR?], ۱۹۳۳[OCR?], ۱۹۳۴, ۱۹۳۵, ۱۹۳۸); and for
  eras, "عہد شاہان سلف" (the era of the former kings), "سکہوں کے وقت" (the Sikhs' time), "سرکار انگریزی
  [illegible] کے وقت" (the time of the English government), "انقلاب سلطنت" (the revolution of the
  kingdom) (pp. 444–455, folios 451–462). No hijri or Christian year appears anywhere in this chunk.
- **Regime change appears here mainly as a change in who holds property**: a rani's jagir at
  Sheikhupura, a samadh left unfinished and unowned, a sadhu's shiwala absorbed by a banking family as
  Anarkali is built up under the English government, and temple land bought out by the municipality
  for a water main (pp. 443–455, folios 450–462).

## Doubts

- **No archive id is matched in this chunk.** Every site here is a Hindu, Brahmo or Sikh-founded
  building of Anarkali, Mori Gate, Lohari Gate, Mochi Gate, Akbari Mandi or Shah Alami Gate, or a
  Sikh royal samadh; none of them is named as any row of `shrine_index.tsv`. Two near-misses were
  deliberately **not** mapped: (i) "تکیہ[OCR?] بابا فرید گنج شکر" is used only as a **landmark in
  Lahore** for locating the two ranis' samadhs (p. 443, folio 450), whereas the archive's
  `shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar` is the Pakpattan shrine — the book here describes a Lahore takia,
  not that site, and says nothing else about it; (ii) the samadhs of **Rani Nakain and Rani Chand
  Kaur** are separate monuments and are **not** the archive's `samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh`, which
  this chunk does not describe.
- **The chunk opens mid-entry.** At p. 442 (folio 449) the description of an enclosure, well and inner
  Thakurdwara is already in progress; **the site's name is not in this chunk** and must come from
  chunk 030. It is identifiable only as a building "متعلق مندر بانکے بہاری" (attached to Mandir Banke
  Bihari inside the city). Do not attach that name to it without the preceding page.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** "مکان مندر بال ناتہا[OCR?]" at Kucha Bal Nata, Shah Alami Gate, has
  only its first line (p. 456, folio 463); the rest is in chunk 032, and the name itself carries an
  OCR flag.
- **Heading and body disagree on three founders' names**, and no reconciliation should be attempted
  from outside the text: the heading **ٹہاکردوارہ پنڈت اوقت[OCR?] ناتہہ** against the body's "بانی اس
  مندر کا پنڈت وشنا تہہ" (pp. 449–450, folios 456–457); the heading **مندر شوالہ لالہ چہوٹے لال** against
  the body's founder "لالہ درگا[OCR?] پرشاد خلف الرشید لالہ چہوٹے لال مرحوم" (p. 447, folio 454) — the
  temple is named for the father, the building attributed to the son; and the heading's "چہوجہی مل کہتری
