# tareekh_lahore — chunk 021 — pp. 293–306

**A long run of Muslim tombs, khanqahs and mosques outside the Lahore gates, closing on Dai Anga's
tomb.** The chunk opens mid-entry on **رانی گل بیگم**'s garden-tomb, then works through a numbered
series of walled enclosures (the third to the tenth) in a tract the chunk never names, and then gives
separate headed entries: **مقبرہ پیر ہادی رنجہا [OCR?]**, **مزار شاہ شرف**, **مقبرہ شاہ اسماعیل**,
**مزار شیخ سعدی بلخاری لاہوری**, **مزار شاہ درگاہی**, **مسجد قدیمہ دایہ لاڈو**, **مقبرہ شاہ یعقوب
زنجانی المشہور صدر دیوان**, **امام بارہ شیعہ امامیہ**, **مقبرہ شاہ سروانی**, **مقابر … میدان زرین
خان[OCR?]**, **مقبرہ شاہ کنہہ[OCR?]**, **حال مزارات بی بی پاک دامناں**, and **مقبرہ دائی انگا و گلابی
باغ** (pp. 293–306, folios 296–309). Its weight for this project is **institutional and economic**:
revenue-free bigha figures, a fee of one paisa to a well's zamindars, a mujawir selling shrine land to
the English, marble and red stone stripped to Jammu, a mosque occupied, re-occupied and litigated
before a British Assistant to the Resident, a sectarian riot suppressed by cavalry, and a sajjada
nashin's shajra recorded in full. It also carries the book's sharpest piece of source criticism, on
Bibi Pak Daman.

**Every page in this chunk prints a folio marker**, and here the printed folio runs three ahead of the
PDF index throughout: p. 293 → folio 296, p. 306 → folio 309, each marker reading exactly that. The
folios below are only the ones the chunk prints; nothing is extrapolated.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **bibi-pak-daman** (the book's "حال مزارات بی بی پاک دامناں"):
  - **Antiquity asserted, origin denied.** "لاہور کے مزارات و مقابر اہل اسلام سے یہ مزارات نہایت قدیم
    اور متبرک کی مشہور ہیں" — among Lahore's mazars and tombs of the people of Islam these are famous as
    the most ancient and blessed; "اگرچہ ان کی قدمت نہایت ہے ہر کوئی قائل[OCR?] ہے مگر یہ امر کسی طرح
    سے ثابت نہیں ہوا کہ یہ بی بیاں لاہور میں کب آئیں اور کس ملک سے آئیں" — everyone acknowledges[OCR?]
    their great antiquity, but **it has in no way been proved when these Bibis came to Lahore or from
    what country** (p. 304, folio 307).
  - **The mujawirs' account, rejected.** Orally "زبانی مجاوروں" a thing is found "جسکو عقل مانتی ہی
    نہیں", which reason simply does not accept: that these **six girls (چہہ لڑکیاں)** — "بی بی ملچ[OCR?]
    ۔ بی بی تاج ۔ بی بی نور ۔ بی بی حور ۔ بی بی گوہر ۔ بی بی شہباز" — were daughters of **عقیل (Aqil)**,
    brother of "علی المرتضی پیغمبر صاحب کے داماد", Ali al-Murtaza the Prophet's son-in-law; and that
    when "واقع کربلا", the event of Karbala, befell Imam Husain they left **مکہ (Mecca)** at the time of
    that calamity, came to Hind, reached the city of Lahore, settled, and died and were buried there
    (pp. 304–305, folios 307–308).
  - **The written account the author prefers.** "مگر جو مصنف حدیقتہ الاولیا بحوالہ تذکرہ حاکمیہ[OCR?]
    لکہتا ہے وہ بات البتہ قرین قیاس ہے" — what the author of **حدیقتہ الاولیا (Hadiqat al-Auliya)**
    writes **on the authority of تذکرہ حاکمیہ[OCR?] (Tazkira-e Hakimiyya)** is indeed close to reason:
    that in the **sixth century Hijri (چہٹی صدی ہجری)** a person came from **کرمان (Kirman)**, "سید خدا
    پرست عابد زاہد ولی اللہ سید احمد تخنہ[OCR?] نام", a God-worshipping, devout, ascetic wali-ullah named
    **Sayyid Ahmad Takhna[OCR?]**, and settled in Lahore; in his house were the same six girls, "اور وہ
    چہوں تارک الدنیا مجرد عابدہ زاہدہ تہیں", and all six were world-renouncing, celibate, devout
    ascetics (p. 305, folio 308).
  - **The father's own grave, and its fate.** "سنہ ۶۰۲[OCR?] ہجری میں سید احمد مر گیا" — Sayyid Ahmad
    died in **602[OCR?] AH** and was buried inside Lahore in "محلہ چہیل بہیویاں[OCR?]", Mohalla Chihal
    Bihiwiyan[OCR?], where his grave still exists; formerly a large tomb stood over it, but "جب سنگ مرمر
    اسکا مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگ نے اتروا لیا تو مقبرہ گر گیا" — when **Maharaja Ranjit Singh had its marble
    taken down the tomb collapsed**; and "اسکے گرد و نواح کے قبرستان کو مسمار کرکے غلام محی الدین
    شاہ[OCR?] پیر زادہ رتا[OCR?] نے اپنی حویلی بنالی" — **Ghulam Muhiuddin Shah[OCR?] Pirzada
    Rata[OCR?]** demolished the surrounding graveyard and built his haveli; "وہ قبر اب ایک طویلہ کے اندر
    پختہ بنی ہوئی موجود ہے", that grave now stands, built pakka, **inside a stable (طویلہ)** (p. 305,
    folio 308).
  - **Why the daughters left the city.** After his death "اسکی لڑکیاں لاہور کے حصار سے باہر جا کر قیام
    پذیر ہو گئیں", his daughters went outside **Lahore's hisar (fortification)** and settled, remaining
    engaged in the worship of the Truth apart from people (p. 305, folio 308).
  - **The invasion dated.** "جب سنہ ۶۱۵ ہجری میں کفار مغل نے بتعاقب سلطان جلال الدین خوارزمی کے پنجاب پر
    لشکر کشی کی اور لاہور کی رعایا بجرم مقابلہ و مجادلہ کے قتل ہو لی[OCR?]" — in **615 AH** (printed
    without a flag) the infidel Mughals, in pursuit of **Sultan Jalal-ud-Din Khwarizmi**, led an army
    against the Punjab and the populace of Lahore was killed for the crime of resistance (p. 305,
    folio 308). Their prayer and disappearance are under Legends.
  - **Site and layout.** "یہ متبرک مزارات لاہور سے بفاصلہ دو میل بجانب کو مشہ گگنی[OCR?] واقع" — **two
    miles from Lahore**, in a direction whose name is flagged and damaged; many buildings belong to this
    graveyard and "بہت سی عمارتیں بسبب عدم خبر گیری کے گر بہی گئی ہیں", many have fallen for **want of
    care**; the entrance is on the **west**, then a lane-like passage to a second gate, then north to the
    mazars (p. 306, folio 309).
  - **Three enclosures.** "یہ مزارات تین احاطے میں" — an **outer enclosure** with the graves of "بی بی
    صاحبان کی خادماؤں", the Bibi Sahibans' maidservants, and a pakka domed mosque built by "نورا
    ایمان[OCR?] وہ اب اے[OCR?]" (unrecoverable); the **large second enclosure** with the graves of **Bibi
    Milch[OCR?], Bibi Taj and Bibi Nur**; the **third** with the graves of **Bibi Hur, Bibi Gauhar and
    Bibi Shahbaz**, built pakka lime-plaster; each enclosure "نہایت عمدہ مقطع پختہ چونہ گچ بنا ہوا ہے",
    very finely built (p. 306, folio 309).
  - **A dated tomb in the corner.** In the corner of the eastern[OCR?] enclosure a pakka domed tomb whose
    doorway is described through a damaged clause ("اسے اندر سے اسکا دروازہ نہ و ہے[OCR?]"); "وہ سنہ ۱۰۱۷
    ہجری میں بنا تہا" — it was built in **1017 AH** (unflagged) — "اور سید جلال الدین بخاری میران تہہ[OCR?]
    شاہ موج دریا بخاری کا بہائی اسمیں مدفون ہے", and **Sayyid Jalal-ud-Din Bukhari, brother of Miran
    [تہہ OCR?] Shah Mauj Darya Bukhari**, is buried in it (p. 306, folio 309). See Doubts.
  - **Standing.** "اس مکان کو تمام اہل اسلام متبرک جانتے ہیں اور بدل و جان خدمت کرتے ہیں" — all the
    people of Islam hold this place blessed and serve it with heart and soul (p. 306, folio 309).
- **data-darbar** (the book's "گنج بخش کے مزار پر انوار", used only as a bearing):
  - The Shia Imambara is placed "بیرون دروازہ بہاٹی … گنج بخش کے مزار پر انوار کے جنوب کی سمت کو" —
    outside **Bhati Gate (دروازہ بہاٹی)**, **to the south of the light-filled mazar of Ganj Bakhsh**
    (p. 301, folio 304). This is the chunk's only reference to the archive row, and it says nothing else
    about it.
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** (the book's "مقبرے شاہ ابو المعالی", used only as a bearing):
  - The tomb of Shah Kanhh[OCR?] is "شاہ ابو المعالی کے مقبرے کے شمال کی سمت موچی دروازے کے باہر کے
    علاقے میں" — **north of Shah Abul Maali's tomb, in the area outside Mochi Gate (موچی دروازہ)**
    (p. 304, folio 307). Nothing further is said of the archive row here.
- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari** (the book's "شاہ موج دریا بخاری", named only as a relative):
  - The man buried in the 1017 AH tomb at Bibi Pak Daman is given as "سید جلال الدین بخاری میران تہہ[OCR?]
    شاہ موج دریا بخاری کا بہائی", **Sayyid Jalal-ud-Din Bukhari, brother of Miran [تہہ OCR?] Shah Mauj
    Darya Bukhari** (p. 306, folio 309). The chunk says nothing about Mauj Darya's own shrine.
- **ranmal-sharif** (the book's "نو شاہ گنج بخش", named only as the head of a silsila — identification
  flagged in Doubts):
  - The sajjada nashin of Shah Kanhh[OCR?]'s tomb, **جواہر شاہ (Jawahar Shah)**, is placed in the
    **Naushahi silsila (نو شاہی سلسلے)**, whose shajra the book carries "پاک رحمان تک", up to Pak
    Rahman, thus: "جواہر شاہ خادم آلہی شاہ او وہ گامی شاہ کا او وہ محمد زبان[OCR?] کا او وہ شاہ عصمت
    المند[OCR?] کا او وہ شیخ عبد الرحمن المعروف پاک رحمان کا او وہ نو شاہ گنج بخش کا" — Jawahar Shah
    khadim of **Alahi Shah**, he of **Gami Shah**, he of **Muhammad Zaban[OCR?]**, he of **Shah Ismat
    al-Mand[OCR?]**, he of **Shaikh Abdul Rahman known as Pak Rahman**, he of **Nau Shah Ganj Bakhsh**
    (p. 304, folio 307). The chunk says nothing about the site at Ranmal Sharif.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** (the book's "سید شاہ چراغ" — **identification uncertain, see Doubts**):
  - Of Shah Dargahi: "یہ درگاہی شاہ سید شاہ چراغ کا مرید تہا جسکا ذکر پہلے درج ہو چکا ہے" — this Dargahi
    Shah was the **murid of Sayyid Shah Chiragh, whose account has already been entered earlier** in the

    **living** "سید چراغ شاہ حکیم" is described three pages earlier in the same chunk (p. 296, folio 299);
    the two are not distinguished in the text.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **رانی گل بیگم, Rani Gul Begum** (p. 293, folio 296; entry begins before this chunk): the chunk opens on
  a fragment, "مین سے تہی", then: **Maharaja Ranjit Singh** was so kind to her that "اسکے ساتہہ حسب رسوم
  اہل اسلام کے شادی کی", he married her **according to the customs of the people of Islam**, and the
  **barat came from Amritsar (امرتسر)** with great pomp[OCR?]; her honour in the Maharaja's household was
  very great and "سبسے زیادہ اسکی جاگیر تہی", **her jagir was the largest of all**. Her allowance and her
  garden-tomb are under Practices. Her grave stands on a **black stone platform (کالے پتہر کے چبوترے)**
  with marble laid over it; "یہ رانی سنہ ۱۲۸۲[OCR?] ہجری مین مر گئی اور اس مقبرے[OCR?] مین مدفون ہوئی" —
  she died in **1282[OCR?] AH** and was buried in this tomb[OCR?]; afterwards her **adopted son (متبنی
  بیٹا) سردار خان (Sardar Khan)** died and was buried in the same place; of the family two persons now
  survive, "ایک بہاول خان اور ایک جہانگیر خان دو بیٹے سردار خان کے", **Bahawal Khan and Jahangir Khan,
  two sons of Sardar Khan** — the sentence breaks off at "موجود".
- **میرزا ہدی[OCR?] / محمد مسلم, the third tomb of the tract** (pp. 293–294, folios 296–297): "تیسرا
  مقبرہ متعلقہ اس خطہ کے میرزا ہدی[OCR?] کا ہے" — the **third tomb belonging to this tract is Mirza
  Hadi's[OCR?]**; pakka, of ancient times, a dome on a pakka platform with four arched doors on all four
  sides and a pakka grave in the middle. The elder is then named otherwise: "یہ بزرگ سلسلہ نقشبندیہ
  مجددیہ کا فقیر تہا محمد مسلم اسکا نام تہا چونکہ زہد و ریاضت بہت کی تہی اس سبب سے پیر زیدی[OCR?] کا
  خطاب پایا تہا" — a faqir of the **Naqshbandiyya Mujaddidiyya** silsila, his name **Muhammad Muslim**,
  who because of much abstinence and austerity received the title **Pir Zaidi[OCR?]** (p. 294,
  folio 297). See Doubts on the heading/name mismatch.
- **مسجد میان محمد سلطان ٹہیکہ دار, the fourth enclosure** (p. 294, folio 297): a mosque of white
  lime-plaster (printed "مسجد نام سفید چونہ گچ"), east door "مع چاہ و سقاوہ", **with a well and a
  saqawa** (water-house); pakka courtyard; three arched doors and a qalbuti roof under a high dome; three
  small domes over the mihrab head; four turrets at the corners; the **kalima sharif** written above the
  central mihrab. Its marble slab and founder are under Practices.
- **مولوی جامی لاہوری, Maulvi Jami Lahori, the fifth enclosure** (p. 294, folio 297): in the north-east
  corner, by "چار دیواری شیخ طاہر", the **enclosure wall of Shaikh Tahir**, a broken arched pakka mosque
  wall survives, and in front of it Maulvi Jami's pakka grave "قدیم زمانہ کی بنی ہوئی", built in ancient
  times, with an old well beside it. "عہد جہانگیری شاہجہانی مین یہ بزرگ لاہور مین بڑا عالم و فاضل تہا
  اسکے فتوے کو سب اہل اسلام قبول کرتے تہے بادشاہ کے دربار مین بہی اسکا دخل تہا" — in the **Jahangiri and
  Shahjahani era** he was a great scholar of Lahore, **all the people of Islam accepted his fatwa**, and
  he had entry to the emperor's court; "سنہ ۱۰۵۳[OCR?] ہجری مین یہ مر گیا", he died in **1053[OCR?] AH**
  and was buried here.
- **نواب سعادت یار خان والی معزول بہاولپور, the sixth enclosure** (pp. 294–295, folios 297–298): the
  **deposed ruler of Bahawalpur**; the building stands on a tila[OCR?] (mound) with access from two sides
  within the enclosure, one west and one south; inside a handsome bungalow with two graves, "ایک نواب
  سعادت یار خان کی جو سنہ ۱۲۴۹[OCR?] ہجری مین مر گیا تہا اور دوسری اسکی زوجہ کی", one of Nawab Saadat
  Yar Khan, who died in **1249[OCR?] AH**, the other of his wife, built pakka.
- **حاجی نور, Haji Nur, the seventh enclosure** (p. 295, folio 298): "یہ شخص مالدار مشہور قوم پہراچہ
  لاہور مین رہتا تہا" — a famous wealthy man of the **Pahracha (پہراچہ)** caste living in Lahore; he
  performed many hajj and gave much in charity. The crore-rupee loan is under Practices. "یہ احاطہ پختہ
  خانہ کعبہ کی قطع پر بنا ہوا ہے جو اُس نے اپنی زندگی مین بنوایا تہا" — this pakka enclosure is built
  **on the plan of the Khana-e Kaaba (خانہ کعبہ کی قطع پر)** and he had it built in his own lifetime;
  four doors on four sides, now qalbuti; his pakka grave in the eastern mihrab. "یہ شخص سنہ ۱۰۵۵ ہجری مین
  مر گیا" — he died in **1055 AH** (printed without a flag); and from his progeny "میان گلا[OCR?] پہراچہ",
  **Mian Gula[OCR?] Pahracha**, an honoured man, survives to this day.
- **شیخ عارف چشتی, the eighth enclosure** (p. 295, folio 298): east of Haji Nur's enclosure, his mazar on
  a pakka platform; "اس بزرگ کی بزرگی کے سب[OCR?] لوگ قائل ہین", all[OCR?] people acknowledge his
  sanctity; "اب اس قبر کی مرمت دوبارہ کی گئی ہے", the grave **has now been repaired again**. "یہ بزرگ سنہ
  ۱۰۶۴[OCR?] ہجری مین مر گیا" — he died in **1064[OCR?] AH**, and "عارف چشتی کے لفظ سے تاریخ وفات حاصل
  ہوتی ہے", the **date of death is obtained from the words "عارف چشتی"**, with the numeral printed
  separately as "۱۰۶۴[OCR?]". The lamp seen on his grave is under Legends.
- **مستان شاہ, Mastan Shah** (p. 295, folio 298): on another white, ornamented pakka platform in the same
  enclosure; "یہ مستان شاہ سکہی عہد مین ایک مجذوب بے پرواہ پہرا کرتا تہا" — in the **Sikh era** he

  بہیجتا[OCR?] مگر وہ ایک[OCR?] نہ لیتا اور پہینک کر پہرا [illegible] جاتا" — Maharaja Ranjit Singh was

- **سید چراغ شاہ حکیم, the tenth enclosure** (p. 296, folio 299): "یہ شخص زندہ و حیات لاہور مین موجود

- **مقبرہ پیر ہادی رنجہا[OCR?], Pir Hadi Ranjha[OCR?]** (pp. 296–297, folios 299–300): "یہ نہایت پُرانا

  verandah; five doors on each of the four sides, "کل چارون طرف مین بیس[OCR?] در ہین", twenty[OCR?] doors

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A Rani's jagir, her stipend under two regimes, and a tomb built in her own lifetime.** Of Rani Gul
  Begum: "سبسے زیادہ اسکی جاگیر تہی انگریزی کیوقت بہی روزینہ اسکا نام رانیون[OCR?] سے زیادہ مقرر تہا اور
  ایک ہزار دو سو روپیہ ماہوار ملتا تہا" — **her jagir was the largest of all**; **in English times too
  her daily allowance was fixed higher than the [other] Ranis'[OCR?]**, and she received **twelve hundred
  rupees a month (ایک ہزار دو سو روپیہ ماہوار)**; "اُس نے اپنی زندگی مین یہ باغیچہ اور مقبرہ اپنے واسطے
  بنوایا تہا", and she had this **garden and tomb built for herself in her own lifetime** (p. 293,
  folio 296).
- **A dated garden-tomb with its chronogram in place.** The west gate carries an upper storey with

  progeny came to Lahore **with the Superintendent of Lahore[OCR?]**, repaired the tomb again, had it
  whitewashed, built a **new verandah**, repaired the **well and set it working**, built a **kotha and
  seated a faqir in it**, and planted trees in the **land belonging to the tomb**, by which the place
  became inhabited again; "فقیر خادم مقبرہ کا ہمیشہ حاضر رہتا ہے", the faqir servant of the tomb is
  always in attendance (p. 303, folio 306).
- **A succession at the Zarin Khan enclosure, with a centenarian in it.** After Abdul Khaliq "خضر شاہ
  جانشین ہوا اسکی عمر ایک سو ایک برس کی ہوئی یہ سانتویں ذی الحج سنہ ۱۲۴۶[OCR?] ہجری میں فوت ہوا اسکی قبر
  بہی اسی احاطہ میں ہے" — **Khizr Shah succeeded**, reached the age of **one hundred and one years**, and
  died on **7 Zil-Hajj 1246[OCR?] AH**, his grave in the same enclosure; "بعد ازان رکن الدین جانشین ہوا وہ
  بہی اب چند سال ہوئے میں کہ مر گیا ہے مگر یہ رکن الدین اس مکان کی بہت خدمت کرتا تہا" — then
  **Rukn-ud-Din succeeded**, who died a few years ago, and **served this place greatly**; "خاندان اسکا
  صابریہ چشتیہ ہے اور سلسلہ شاہ سروانی کے ساتہہ ملتا ہے", the family is **Sabiriya Chishtiya** and the
  silsila **connects with Shah Sarwani** (pp. 303–304, folios 306–307).
- **A dated rebuilding, a mujawir's chaubara, and a named sajjada nashin.** At Shah Kanhh[OCR?]'s tomb:
  rebuilt "سنہ ۱۲۹۴[OCR?] ہجری میں مسمی جواہر شاہ نے از سر نو", **anew in 1294[OCR?] AH by one Jawahar
  Shah**, who also built the mosque pakka; the two-storey chaubara inside the enclosure was built by
  **آلہی شاہ مجاور (Alahi Shah, mujawir)**; "جواہر شاہ سجادہ نشین بنا ہے" — **Jawahar Shah has become
  sajjada nashin**, in the **Naushahi silsila**, with the shajra given in full up to Pak Rahman and Nau
  Shah Ganj Bakhsh; dalan, kothri and well are "فقرا کے آرام کے لئے", for the faqirs' comfort (p. 304,
  folio 307).
- **Neglect and collapse at Bibi Pak Daman.** "عمارات اس گورستان کے متعلق بہت ہیں بہت سی عمارتیں بسبب عدم
  خبر گیری کے گر بہی گئی ہیں" — many buildings belong to this graveyard, and many have fallen **for want
  of care (عدم خبر گیری)**; the maidservants' enclosure carries a pakka domed mosque built by a founder
  whose name is unrecoverable (p. 306, folio 309).
- **A grave stripped and built over inside a private house.** Sayyid Ahmad's tomb collapsed after
  **Maharaja Ranjit Singh** had its marble removed, and the surrounding graveyard was demolished by
  **Ghulam Muhiuddin Shah[OCR?] Pirzada** for his haveli; the grave now stands pakka **inside a stable**
  (p. 305, folio 308).
- **A Mughal-era garden foundation.** The Dai Anga tomb and **Gulabi Bagh** were built in **Shah Jahan's**
  time by a founder connected by marriage to the emperor's daughter, who "اس تمام پر ایک عمدہ باغ بنوایا",
  had a fine garden made over the whole (p. 306, folio 309).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A karamat described as current, not past.** "اسی بزرگ کی کرامت فی زمانہ یہ جاری ہے" — the vow of
  rotis fulfilled and distributed at the third tomb's grave is given as the elder's karamat **running in
  our own time** (p. 294, folio 297).
- **A lamp that vanishes on approach — attributed to named villagers.** "موضع مریدکے کے مسن[OCR?] و ضعیف
  لوگ بہت بیان کرتے ہین کہ ناگاہ[OCR?] دور سے اس قبر پر چراغ جلتا نظر آتا ہے جب نزدیک آتے ہین تو کوئی
  چراغ نہین ہوتا" — the **aged[OCR?] and infirm people of mauza Muridke** relate much that suddenly[OCR?]
  a **lamp is seen burning on Shaikh Arif Chishti's grave from a distance**, and on coming near there is
  no lamp (p. 295, folio 298).
- **A majzub who would not touch a Maharaja's money.** Mastan Shah threw away the thousand-rupee nazrs
  Maharaja Ranjit Singh sent (p. 295, folio 298) — reported flatly, without a formula of wonder.
- **A preacher's voice.** Of Shah Ismail: "آواز اسکی ایسی خوش تہی کہ جسکے کان مین پس[OCR?] جاتی
  [illegible] ہوا چلا جاتا تہا" — his voice was so sweet that whoever's ear it fell upon [illegible]
  followed him; the clause's outcome is illegible (pp. 297–298, folios 300–301).
- **Karamat referred to a book rather than narrated.** Of Shaikh Sadi Balkhari: "اسکی سینکڑون کرامتین
  لکہی ہین خزینۃ الاصفیا مین اُنکی تشریح ہے" — hundreds of his karamat are written, their exposition
  being in **Khazinat al-Asfiya**; the chunk narrates none (p. 298, folio 301).
- **A well whose water still heals by a saint's prayer.** "یہ تاثیر چاہ کے پانی مین ابتک شاہ درگاہی کی
  دعا سے باقی ہے" — this property remains in the well's water **to this day by Shah Dargahi's prayer**
  (p. 299, folio 302).
- **The earth opening for the Bibis.** "سب نے ملکر دست وعا خدا کے حضور میں اٹہائے اور سکہا[OCR?] کہ یا
  الہی ہمکو زمین کلا[OCR?] بیو[OCR?] ندر کرلے چنانچہ ایسا ہی ہوا زمین جابجا سے پہٹ گئی اور وہ چہوں بی
  بیاں مع اپنی خادمہ عورتوں بی بی نور ہی[OCR?] وغیرہ کے زمین میں سما گئیں اور انکی اوڑہنیوں کے پلے ذرا
  ذرا سے زمین سے باہر رہ گئے" — all together raised hands of prayer before God, saying "O God, take us
  into the earth" (the wording flagged and damaged); **and so it happened: the earth split in places and
  all six Bibis, with their maidservant women — Bibi Nur Hi[OCR?] and others — were absorbed into the
  earth, and the ends of their scarves (اوڑہنیوں کے پلے) remained just a little above the ground**;
  "چہر[OCR?] بعد اس چہیں[OCR?] کے لوگوں نے قبریں بنا دیں", after that people built the graves (pp. 305–306,
  folios 308–309). The author gives this within the account he calls close to reason; the Karbala descent
  he rejects (see Arguments).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The chunk's central act of source criticism.** On Bibi Pak Daman the author sets **oral mujawir
  testimony against a written tazkira** and chooses the latter: the mujawirs' Karbala-flight account is
  "ایسی بات … جسکو عقل مانتی ہی نہیں", a thing **reason simply does not accept**, while the
  **حدیقتہ الاولیا** account on the authority of **تذکرہ حاکمیہ[OCR?]** is "البتہ قرین قیاس", indeed
  **close to reason** (pp. 304–305, folios 307–308). He also states plainly that the Bibis' date and
  country of arrival "کسی طرح سے ثابت نہیں ہوا", have in no way been proved (p. 304, folio 307) — a
  refusal to fill a gap, on the most venerated site in his own list.
- **Testimony is marked as testimony.** The lamp on Shaikh Arif Chishti's grave is given as what the
  aged people of Muridke "بہت بیان کرتے ہین", relate much (p. 295, folio 298); the Bibi Pak Daman
  genealogy as "زبانی مجاوروں", from the mujawirs orally (p. 304, folio 307). Building histories, land
  figures, court outcomes and stipends are stated flatly in his own voice.
- **Chronograms are recorded as evidence for dates.** Five in this chunk: the Persian quatrain with
  "۱۲۴۷[OCR?]" at Gul Begum's garden (p. 293, folio 296); "عارف چشتی" yielding 1064[OCR?] (p. 295,
  folio 298); "مہتاب" yielding 458[OCR?] (p. 298, folio 301); "زندہ دل سعدی بلخاری" (p. 298, folio 301);
  and "پیر سروانی[OCR?]" yielding 631[OCR?] (p. 303, folio 306). Where an inscription exists he quotes it
  (the 1247[OCR?] AH marble slab naming a woman founder, p. 294, folio 297).
- **Regime change appears as a change in who owns the stone, the land and the water.** Sikh-era removals
  (Dhian Singh's marble and red stone to Jammu[OCR?], p. 296, folio 299; the Sikhs prising up Shah
  Sarwani's buildings, garden and well bricks, p. 303, folio 306; Ranjit Singh's removal of Sayyid
  Ahmad's marble, p. 305, folio 308) stand beside British-era transfers (the mujawir's sale to the
  English and the kothi drawing the shrine's water, p. 298, folio 301; an Englishman's kothi made of a
  tomb's mosque and well, p. 297, folio 300; Ratan Chand's sarai hemming in a khanqah and its graveyard
  closed, p. 301, folio 304) and British-era restoration (the descendant who rebuilt Shah Sarwani's tomb
  with the Superintendent, p. 303, folio 306).
- **Cross-references to his own earlier pages.** "اسکا ذکر محلہ زرین خان میں اول تحریر ہو ہو چکا ہے"
  (p. 303, folio 306); "سید شاہ چراغ … جسکا ذکر پہلے درج ہو چکا ہے" (p. 299, folio 302); and of the
  Zarin Khan family's silsila, "سلسلہ شاہ سروانی کے ساتہہ ملتا ہے جنکا ذکر مذکور ہو چکا" (p. 304,
  folio 307) — the book treats itself as a reference work with internal citations.
- **A city consensus used as a measure of sanctity.** "اس شخص کی بزرگی کا تمام شہر قایل ہے", the whole
  city acknowledges Shah Dargahi's sanctity (p. 299, folio 302); "اس بزرگ کی بزرگی کے سب[OCR?] لوگ قائل
  ہین" of Shaikh Arif Chishti (p. 295, folio 298); "اس مکان کو تمام اہل اسلام متبرک جانتے ہیں" of Bibi
  Pak Daman (p. 306, folio 309). The author reports assent rather than asserting the claim himself.

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry.** The **Rani Gul Begum** entry begins before p. 293; the first words of the
  chunk are the fragment "مین سے تہی", and the heading that named the site is not in the chunk. The
  subject is recoverable only because the text later names "رانی گل بیگم کا مقبرہ" (p. 293, folio 296).
- **The numbered enclosures belong to a tract the chunk never names.** The text runs "تیسرا مقبرہ متعلقہ
  اس خطہ کے" (third), "چوتہا احاطہ" (fourth), "پانچوان" (fifth), "چہٹا" (sixth), "ساتوان" (seventh),
  "آٹہوان" (eighth) and then "دسوان احاطہ" (tenth) — the **first, second and ninth are not in the
  chunk**; the ninth falls at the p. 295/296 break, where the surviving words are only "اور چبوترہ
  [illegible]" and "احاطہ مین بہت ہین". The name of the burial ground or quarter that contains all of
  them is nowhere printed here; do not infer it from the individual graves.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** The **Dai Anga / Gulabi Bagh** entry breaks off at "اس نے اس تمام پر
  ایک عمدہ باغ بنوایا وسعت" (p. 306, folio 309); the garden's extent and everything after it are in the
  next chunk.
- **Heading against name, third tomb.** The heading gives the occupant as "میرزا ہدی[OCR?]" (p. 293,
  folio 296) but the next page names him "محمد مسلم" with the title "پیر زیدی[OCR?]" (p. 294,
  folio 297). Both readings are flagged; whether "میرزا ہدی" names the man, the tract, or is an OCR
  error cannot be settled from the chunk.
- **Shah Ismail's three figures do not agree.** Arrival "سنہ ۴۱۸[OCR?] ہجری", death "سنہ ۴۵۸[OCR?]
  ہجری", residence "۳۶[OCR?] برس", and the chronogram "مہتاب" given for "۴۵۸[OCR?]" — all four flagged,
  and the stated residence does not match the interval between the two stated years. Kept side by side
  (p. 298, folio 301).
- **Muhammad Fazil's era against his death year.** He is placed "شاہجہانی عہد مین" but his death is given
  as "۱۱۱۰[OCR?] ہجری", and his son Shah Sharf's as "۱۱۴۷[OCR?] ہجری" (p. 297, folio 300). Both numerals
  flagged; recorded as printed, not reconciled.
- **Shah Sarwani's year carries no era.** "سنہ ۶۳۱[OCR?] میں اسکا انتقال ہوا" — no ہجری or other era is
  printed (p. 303, folio 306); and the same entry makes him the murid of Shaikh Shams-ud-Din Turk
  Panipati, while the Zarin Khan family whose "سلسلہ شاہ سروانی کے ساتہہ ملتا ہے" is dated 990–1246 AH
  (pp. 303–304, folios 306–307). The two chronologies are left as the book leaves them.
- **"۱۲۴۷[OCR?]" appears twice** in three pages — as the Gul Begum garden's chronogram numeral (p. 293,
  folio 296) and on the Muhammad Sultan mosque's founding slab (p. 294, folio 297). Both flagged; a human
  should check whether both are genuine.
- **Which Shah Chiragh?** Shah Dargahi's pir is "سید شاہ چراغ … جسکا ذکر پہلے درج ہو چکا ہے" (p. 299,
  folio 302). This could be the archive row **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** (Lahore, Syed Abdul Razzaq
  Shah Chiragh), or the **living** "سید چراغ شاہ حکیم" of the tenth enclosure three pages earlier
  (p. 296, folio 299), who also has murids in the Chishtiya and Qadiriya silsilas. The archive also holds
  **darbar-sakhi-shah-chan-charagh** (Rawalpindi), a different place. I have not chosen; the bullet above
  is flagged accordingly.
- **Shams-ud-Din Tabrizi at Multan, not asserted as an archive row.** The chunk says Pir Hadi Shah, Muhsin
  Shah and Abdul Samad Shah[OCR?] are "شمسی سید شمس الدین تبریزی کی اولاد مین سے … جسکا روضہ ملتان مین
  زیارت گاہ ہے" (p. 296, folio 299). The archive's **shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari** (Multan) is a
  possible referent, but the chunk prints "تبریزی", not "سبزواری", and identifying them would be an
  addition from outside the chunk. Left for a human.
- **Sayyid Jalal-ud-Din Bukhari at Bibi Pak Daman is not the archive's Uch figure.** The chunk identifies
  him only as "میران تہہ[OCR?] شاہ موج دریا بخاری کا بہائی", brother of Mauj Darya Bukhari, buried in a
  tomb of **1017 AH** at Bibi Pak Daman (p. 306, folio 309). The name coincides with
