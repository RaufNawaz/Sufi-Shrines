# tareekh_lahore — chunk 005 — pp. 61–75

**No saint, shrine, mazar or urs appears anywhere in this chunk, and no archive row is informed by
it.** The slice is entirely (a) the British administrative anatomy of Lahore district and city —
Deputy Commissioner's office, four tehsils, honorary magistracy, Municipal Committee, police and
kotwali (pp. 61–62, folios 64–65) — and (b) the chapter **"لاہور کے رؤسا کا ذکر"**, the account of
the notables of Lahore, opened at p. 62 (folio 65) and continuing through the Hindu families, then
**"مسلمان رؤسائے شہر لاہور"** at p. 69 (folio 72) and **"خاندان نوابان ملتانی"** at p. 73
(folio 76), breaking off mid-sentence in the Karim Bakhsh entry at p. 75 (folio 78). Its value to
this project is not hagiographic but institutional: it is a dense, dated register of **jagirs,
pensions, revenue remissions, muafi villages, contract income, and religious endowment and charity
funded out of that income** — shivalas, thakurdwaras, mosques, imambaras, a free hospital and
madrasa, ta'ziyadari with cash nazr, and famine relief — attached to named families with named sums.

**The chunk prints a `[folio N]` marker on every page**, and across pp. 61–75 each marker reads
exactly three more than the PDF index (p. 61 → folio 64 … p. 75 → folio 78). That is an observation
about the markers this chunk actually prints; it must not be extrapolated to any other chunk, since
the book's offset is known to shift.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Jagir income, stated in rupees, for a single Lahore family**: Raja Teja Singh, after being titled
  Raja by the English, "اب ۹۳ ہزار روپیے کی جاگیر مقرر ہوئے" — a jagir of **93 thousand rupees** was
  now fixed; the Sialkot area was given in jagir, then exchanged for the Batala area in Gurdaspur
  district; and his successor Raja Harbans Singh additionally holds the **Sheikhupura area in
  Gujranwala district** in jagir from the government (p. 63, folio 66; p. 63, folio 66).
- **A jagir figure and a revenue-free village for a family of Brahman ritual specialists**: "نو ہزار
  نو سو پچپن ۹۹۵۵[OCR?] روپیے کی جاگیر پنڈت مدسودن کو ما گزار تہی[OCR?] اور ایک گاؤں قلعہ گوجر سنگھ کو
  نسلاً بعد نسلاً معافت تھا" — a jagir of nine thousand nine hundred and fifty-five rupees to Pandit
  Madsudan[OCR?], and one village, **Qila Gujjar Singh**, held *muafi* generation after generation
  (p. 67, folio 70). The figure is written out in words and again in flagged numerals.
- **Charity channelled through a Brahman office, counted in lakhs**: of Pandit Madsudan — "لاکھوں روپے
  اُسکی معرفت خیرات ہوکر برہمنوں کو دیا جاتا تھا", lakhs of rupees were given in charity through his
  agency to Brahmans (p. 67, folio 70). He held the position by succeeding his father in the
  "پاشو پوجا" (p. 67, folio 70).
- **An inheritance dispute over a perpetual jagir, decided by gift (hiba)**: "پنڈت مدسودن کا اخیر عمر
  میں تینوں بڑے بیٹوں کے ساتھ تنازع رہا۔ اسلئے کہ اُس نے کل جائداد جمع دوامی جاگیر کے دیوبیدت[OCR?]
  پرشاد و اپنے چھوٹے بیٹے کو جو دوسری زوجہ سے تھا ہبہ کر دی تھی" — in his last years he was in dispute
  with his three elder sons because he had made over the whole property together with the perpetual
  jagir by *hiba* to Deobidat[OCR?] Prashad, his youngest son by a second wife (p. 67, folio 70). A
  documented case of religious-office property passing by gift against the claims of elder heirs.
- **A jagir confiscated over unaudited accounts, and the pension that replaced it, confiscated in
  turn**: in "سنہ ۱۸۴۷ع" John Lawrence, acting Resident at Lahore, demanded accounts from Bakhshi
  Bhagat Ram; he delayed, "اسوابسطے[OCR?] جاگیر ضبط ہوئی اور عہدہ سے معطل ہوا", so the jagir was
  confiscated and he was suspended; when the accounts came in, "پانچ لاکھ روپیہ باقی نکلا", five lakh
  rupees showed as balance, though much of it lay against officers, and what was specifically his was
  paid; the jagir had not been released when the Punjab was annexed; after annexation no right of his
  was established against the English government, but the Chief Commissioner fixed "بارہ سو روپیے
  اسکی بقیہ حیات پنشن", a twelve-hundred-rupee life pension, and when he died in "سنہ ۱۸۷۶[OCR?]ع"
  "وہ پنشن بھی ضبط ہو گئی", that pension too was resumed (p. 68, folio 71). The clearest fiscal
  sequence in the chunk: audit → confiscation → life pension → lapse.
- **A talluqadari of 14 villages granted in place of a cash pension, then converted into a perpetual
  landed grant with revenue remitted and a named line of succession.** For the Qizilbash Nawabs: after
  1857, "۱۴ دیہات کی تعلقہ داری بہر ایک ملک[OCR?] و دہ[OCR?] میں اسکو ملی۔ دو ہزار روپیہ نقد پنشن
  قرار پائی", the talluqadari of fourteen villages and a cash pension of two thousand rupees; earlier,
  on arrival from Kabul, "آٹھہ سو روپیہ ماہوار", eight hundred rupees a month (p. 70, folio 73). Then
  "رکھپہ[OCR?] کہمینہ[OCR?] واقع ضلع لاہور نقد پنشن کی عوض میں اس خاندان کو دوام کے لیئے سرکار سے عطا
  ہوئی۔ ملکیت بھی ملی۔ جمع سرکارہ بھی معاف ہوئی" — an estate in Lahore district was granted in
  perpetuity in exchange for the cash pension, **proprietary right** was given, and the **government
  revenue was remitted**; and it was written that Nawazish Ali Khan should hold it, then Nasir Ali
  Khan, then "جو لایق ہو گا قابض و سرپرست مقرر ہو گا۔ پرورش کل خاندان کی اُسکے ذمہ ہو گی", whoever is
  fit shall be made possessor and guardian, with **the maintenance of the whole family his
  responsibility** (p. 71, folio 74). A state-created corporate endowment with a single trustee — the
  nearest thing in this chunk to a *sajjada*-style office, and it is wholly secular in form.
- **What the grantee then built on it**: "نوازش علی خان نے اُس رکھپہ[OCR?] بنخوبی آباد کیا ہی۔ بہت سے
  گاؤں بسائے میں۔ عالیشان کوٹھیاں، مسجدیں، امام بارے بنوائے میں۔ زمینداروں کو نہایت آسودہ رکھا ہوا ہی"
  — he settled the estate well, founded many villages, built magnificent kothis, **mosques** and
  **imambaras**, and keeps the zamindars very comfortable (p. 71, folio 74). Grant revenue converted
  into village foundation and Shia religious building.
- **A Muharram observance costed at ten thousand rupees a year, with cash nazr per head, and food
  distributed across the sectarian line.** "یہ خاندان شیعہ مذہب ہے۔ عشرہ محرم میں دس روز تک انکے ہاں
  تعزیہ داری ہوتی ہی۔ شب و روز مکلف کہانے[OCR?] تقسیم ہوتے میں۔ سنی شیعہ مذہب والوں کو سب یہ تقسیم
  ہوتا ہے" — this family is of the Shia madhhab; *ta'ziyadari* is held at their house for the ten days
  of Muharram; elaborate meals are distributed night and day; and the distribution is to Sunnis and
  Shias alike. Then: "ساتوین محرم کو مہدی کے دن جو لوگ علم نکالتے میں سب اسکے گھر پہ جاتے میں۔ فی کس
  ایک روپیے سے دس روپیہ تک یہ رئیس نذر دیتا ہو۔ غرض ہر ایک برس دس ہزار روپیے سے کم روپیہ اسکا خرچ نہیں
  ہوتا" — on the **seventh of Muharram, the day of Mahdi**, all those who bring out the *alam*
  (standards) come to his house, and this rais gives **nazr of one to ten rupees per head**; in short,
  not less than **ten thousand rupees** a year is spent (p. 71, folio 74). A household festival
  economy with a per-capita payment schedule and an annual total — the single most quantified ritual
  expenditure in the chunk, and the flow of *nazr* runs **from** the patron **to** the participants,
  the reverse of the shrine-offering direction.
- **Hajj and ziyarat as elite expenditure**: Nawab Nawazish Ali Khan "دو بار حج کو گیا۔ کربلا معلے کی
  زیارت کی۔ اس کا خیر میں بہت سا روپیہ صرف کیا" — went twice on Hajj, performed the ziyarat of Karbala
  Mualla, and spent much money in charity (p. 71, folio 74). Of Nawab Muzaffar Khan of Multan: he went
  for the Hajj with a company at a time when Hindustani coin did not reach[OCR?] there, and "لاکھون
  روپیہ اُسنے حج کے کام میں صرف کئے", spent lakhs of rupees on the business of the Hajj (p. 73,
  folio 76). And the corpse-transport of Ali Raza Khan to Karbala Mualla (p. 70, folio 73).
- **A free hospital and free school run out of a noble house under Ranjit Singh**: of the Faqir
  brothers — "انکے گھر شفا خانہ و مدرسہ جاری تھا اور لوگ مفت تعلیم پاتے تہے", at their house a
  *shifakhana* and a *madrasa* were running and people received free education (p. 71, folio 74).
- **Free medicine dispensed by a Muslim notable at his own door, and mosque-building and repair as the
  return in reputation**: of Nawab Abdul Majid Khan — "طبابت کا بازار گرم۔ بیمارون کا ہجوم شب و روز
  دروازے پہ رہتا ہی۔ صدہا روپیے کی ادویات انگریزی و دیسی بیمارون کو اپنے پاس سے تقسیم کرتا ہی", his
  practice is brisk, a crowd of the sick stands at his door night and day, and he distributes hundreds
  of rupees' worth of **English and indigenous** medicines from his own means; and "بہت سی مسجدین اُس
  نے لاہور میں بنوائیں اور مرمت کیں جس سے کمال نیک نامی ہے", he had many mosques built and **repaired**
  in Lahore, from which comes the utmost good name (p. 75, folio 78). The book states the exchange
  plainly: building and repair purchase *nek nami*. **No mosque is named and no date is given.**
- **Hindu religious building financed from jagir and office wealth** (p. 65, folio 68): Diwan Ratan
  Chand's road, tank, garden and shivala outside Shah Alami Gate, and his son's thakurdwara on the
  tank's south bank; Bakhshi Bhagat Ram's surviving shivala in Lahore (p. 68, folio 71). In all three
  cases the patron is a state servant and the endowment is urban infrastructure plus a temple.
- **Famine relief organised on lines of community** (p. 75, folio 78): "چند سال ہوئے کہ کشمیر میں قحط
  پر گیا۔ ہزار ہا قحط زدہ کشمیری پنجاب میں آئے۔ لاہور میں خاص میں صدہا کشمیری بحالت زار آ موجود ہوئے۔
  اس نے ہم قومی و…" — some years ago famine struck Kashmir, thousands of famine-stricken Kashmiris
  came into the Punjab, hundreds arriving in Lahore itself in a wretched state, and Mian Karim Bakhsh,
  out of **fellow-community feeling (ہم قومی)** and… — **the chunk breaks off here mid-sentence**
  (see Doubts). No year given.
- **Contract income as the new route to wealth and title.** Rai Maila Ram "سرکار انگریزی عہد میں اس نے
  ٹھیکہ داری کے کام سے دولت و عزت حاصل کی۔ رائے کا خطاب سرکار سے لیا" — gained wealth and honour by
  contracting under the English government and took the title of Rai; he runs a large timber trade and
  "لاکھوں روپے کے ٹھیکے یہ ریلوے کے محکمے سے لیتا ہو", takes railway-department contracts worth lakhs,
  including in "سنہ ۱۸۸۲[OCR?]ع" the contract for the **Pathankot[OCR?] railway road**, jointly with
  Krumi[OCR?] Sahib (pp. 68–69, folios 71–72). Lala Nihal Chand holds "سنٹرل جیل لاہور کی ٹھیکہ داری",
  the contracting of the Central Jail, Lahore, and "ہر ایک چیز اسکی معرفت جاتی ہے", everything passes
  through him (p. 69, folio 72).
- **A contractor's fortune and its collapse, with terms**: Mian Muhammad Sultan made soap under the
  Sikhs and "انگریزی عہد میں اسنے لاکھون روپیہ کمایا", earned lakhs under the English, though
  "ناخواندہ و اُمی محض", wholly unlettered; he built government houses and buildings and his own, and
  "ایک عمدہ و عالیشان سرک و بازار اپنی یادگار چھوڑ گیا", left a fine road and bazaar as his memorial.
  Then, through overspending, falling income and the embezzlement of dishonest staff, the whole estate
  "کسی[OCR?] لاکھہ روپے کو مہاراجہ جموں کے پاس رہن ہو گئی", was mortgaged to the **Maharaja of Jammu**;
  the Maharaja paid off all the creditors and agreed to pay "پانسو روپیے ماہوار" from the estate's
  income for his maintenance; he died eight years ago with "صلبی بیٹا اُسکا یا اور کوئی مستنظم[OCR?]
  موجود نہ تھا", no natural son nor any manager, "اس سبب سے خاندان برباد ہو گیا", and so the family was
  ruined (p. 75, folio 78). Mortgage, receivership and failure of succession, in four sentences.
- **The administrative frame in which all of this sits** (pp. 61–62, folios 64–65): the Deputy
  Commissioner holds "فوجداری و دیوانی و کلکٹری", criminal, civil and collectorate jurisdiction, and
  is "حاکم با اختیار" with the district's protection and administration "جز و کل", in part and whole,
  entrusted to him; Assistant Commissioner and Collector, Assistant Commissioner and Judicial
  Assistant hold separate courts, and every case reaches their departments by his order; **four
  tehsils** — Lahore (a tehsildar and a munsif competent only for civil suits), Kasur (an Extra
  Assistant, a tehsildar and a munsif), Chunian and Sharaqpur (a tehsildar and a munsif each).
- **The honorary magistracy as a communal quota, and its working rule** (p. 61, folio 64): a separate
  department of honorary[OCR?] magistrates, "لاہور کے معزز رؤسا میں سے آٹھ کس[OCR?] چار ہندو اور چار
  مسلمان", eight persons from the honoured notables of Lahore, **four Hindu and four Muslim**, who
  settle the city's petty criminal cases; "دو مجسٹریٹ ہر روز اجلاس پر بیٹھتے میں", two sit in session
  each day, and each case is settled on both men's opinion and discretion. The eight are named on this
  page and seven of them reappear in the Municipal Committee list on the next.
- **The Municipal Committee: a district officer presiding over a named local elite** (pp. 61–62, folios
  64–65): "پریزیڈنٹ اسکے صاحب ڈیپٹی کمشنر بہادر لاہور میں", the Deputy Commissioner is its President;
  the general committee meets monthly and every question is settled "تمام ممبروں کی رے سے", by all the
  members' opinion; the Secretary is the Assistant Commissioner, with an Editor, i.e. an accountant
  (محاسب), and a vakil; and some twenty-two Indian members are named, including a Khan Bahadur
  pensioner, four Faqir/Nawab honorary magistrates, pandits, lalas, a **Bhai**, a **Syed**, a
  contractor (کریم بخش ٹھیکہ وار) and a **Shaikh Nanak Bakhsh**. Crucially: "دیسی ممبر اکثر اوقات
  بدلتے رہتے میں۔ انکی جگہ اور جدید ممبر شہر کی رعایا کی رضامندی ہی سے مقرر ہوتے میں" — the native
  members change often and new members in their place are appointed **only with the consent of the
  city's people** (p. 62, folio 65).
- **Police and kotwali** (p. 62, folio 65): the District Superintendent of Police is the chief officer
  with all police staff under him; a Deputy Inspector does the kotwal's work at the Lahore Kotwali;
  one at Barkley[OCR?] and one military *sarishta* under the Cantonment[OCR?] Magistrate at Mian
  Mir[OCR?]; Deputy Inspectors also at the outer thanas.
- **Continuity of office across the annexation, stated as a fact about one man**: Khuda Bakhsh Lakhe
  Zai[OCR?] was kotwal of Lahore city "سکھوں کے وقت سالہا سال" and "انگریزی عہد میں بھی تا دم اخیر
  اُس نے کوتوالی کی", kept the kotwali under the English too until his last breath (p. 62, folio 65).
  The same continuity runs through almost every family in the chapter: office under Ranjit Singh,
  title and office under the English.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The book dates itself.** Of Rai Mool Singh: "آخر سال سنہ ۱۸۸۳ع یعنی اسی سال میں مر گیا" — he died
  in the year 1883 CE, **"that is, in this very year"** (p. 67, folio 70); and of Sardar Bhagwan Singh,
  "افسوس کہ اسی سال میں مر گیا", he died this very year (p. 63, folio 66). The chunk is therefore
  written or revised in **1883 CE** by its own statement — a hard anchor for dating every present-tense
  claim in it ("اب … ہی", "موجود ہے"). The same page's earlier notice at p. 61 (folio 64) that Mool
  Singh "فوت ہو گیا ہی" and a successor "مقرر ہوا ہے" is the same event reported twice.
- **The author writes himself into the text.** At the Lala Nihal Chand entry: "جیل کی عمارت کا ٹھیکہ
  بنگر[OCR?] بھی مولف کتاب اسکے پاس ہی" — the jail-building contract … "**the author of the book**" …
  with him (p. 69, folio 72). The sentence is damaged and its sense cannot be recovered from the chunk
  (see Doubts), but the self-reference is unambiguous, and it matters: the writer is Lahore's own
  engineer describing a building contractor he has dealings with. Read with "جسکا ذکر تحریر ہو چوکا",
  mention of which has already been written (p. 68, folio 71), the chunk shows an author cross-
  referencing his own earlier chapters and placing himself among his subjects.
- **Character assessment is the book's organising judgement, and it is applied to officials as
  freely as to notables.** Of the kotwal Chanda Singh: "یہ شخص نہایت لائق ایماندار نیکنام و فہیم و
  ذکی آدمی ہو۔ حاکم و رعایا دونو اس سے خوش میں" — a most capable, honest, well-reputed, discerning
  and sharp man, with **both ruler and subjects pleased with him** (p. 62, folio 65). The double
  approval — from above and below — is the author's standard of a good officer, and he repeats it for
  Faqir Shams-ud-Din, whom "تمام شہر کے ہندو مسلمان" praised (p. 72, folio 75), and for Diwan
  Bijnath, under whom "اہلکار کچہری کے نہایت خوش و خورم تھے", the court staff were delighted
  (p. 64, folio 67).
- **Wealth without learning is explained by fortune, and ruin by the failure of an heir.** Of Muhammad
  Sultan: "اگرچہ ناخواندہ و اُمی محض تھا اگر[OCR?] بخت یاور تھی" — though wholly unlettered, fortune
  was his helper; and the family's destruction is attributed not to the mortgage but to the absence of
  a "صلبی بیٹا … یا اور کوئی مستنظم[OCR?]", a son of the body or any other manager (p. 75, folio 78).
  The author's causal account of an endowment's survival is **succession**, not income.
- **Martyrdom narrated approvingly of a Muslim governor fighting the Sikh state the author elsewhere
  serves.** Of Nawab Muzaffar Khan of Multan, after Ranjit Singh's repeated attacks and the lakhs
  extracted at each: "شہادت کا جامہ پہن کر مقابل ہوا۔ بہت سے سکھون کو تہہ تیغ کرکے خود بھی مع پانچ
  فرزندان دلبند کے شہید ہوا" — he put on the garment of martyrdom and gave battle, put many Sikhs to
  the sword and was himself martyred with five beloved sons (pp. 73–74, folios 76–77). Written by an
  author who records Ranjit Singh's darbar with equal warmth throughout the chapter; the chunk holds
  both valuations without reconciling them.
- **Loyalty to the English is the chapter's ordering merit for Muslim families.** Ali Raza Khan is
  introduced as "خیر خواہ نمک حلال و دوستدار سرکار انگریزی", a well-wisher, faithful and friend of
  the English government (p. 69, folio 72), and the entry then reads as a service ledger — provisions
  to the besieged force, bribes to captors' staff, a risala raised in 1857 **at his own cost**, "اس
  رسالہ کی بہرتی[OCR?] میں اس نے سرکار سے روپیہ نہ لیا" (p. 70, folio 73) — against which "بعوض ان
  خدمات کے روز افزوں عنایت سرکار کی اسکے حال پر ہوئی", in return for these services the government's
  favour towards him grew daily. The pensions, talluqadari and perpetual grant recorded above are
  presented as the price of that ledger.

## Doubts

- **No archive id is matched in this chunk, and none should be forced onto it.** The chunk names no
  saint, no mazar, no darbar, no urs and no sajjada nashin. Four place-names in it coincide with
  archive rows and **must not be mapped**:
  - **"بمقام میا میر[OCR?]"** (p. 62, folio 65) is a police-posting location under the Cantonment[OCR?]
    Magistrate. The archive row **shrine-of-mian-mir** is a Lahore shrine at Alam Ganj (Dharampura).
    The chunk says nothing about a shrine, a saint or a date here, and the name is itself OCR-flagged.
    Leave unmapped; flag as a likely future mis-match.
  - **"تحصیل قصور"** (p. 61, folio 64) is a tehsil headquarters of Lahore district. Nothing links it to
    **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** at Kasur; the chunk gives no shrine there.
  - **"تحصیل شرقپور"** (p. 61, folio 64) likewise carries no shrine material, and must not be attached
    to **shrine-of-pir-sher-muhammad** at Sharaqpur.
  - **"بیرون دروازہ شاہ عالمی"** (p. 65, folio 68) names the gate as the site of a road, tank, garden
    and shivala. It is not evidence about **darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz** in Shah Alam Market.
  - Similarly, **Maharaja Ranjit Singh** is named on nearly every page but his samadhi is never
    mentioned: nothing here bears on **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**; and **Guru Gobind Singh**
    appears only as the giver of pahul (p. 65, folio 68), which is not evidence about any gurdwara row.
- **The word for "honorary" is corrupt throughout and is spelled four different ways**, all flagged:
  "انزریری[OCR?]" (p. 61, folio 64), "انزیری[OCR?]" (p. 63, folio 66), "آنزیری[OCR?]" (pp. 64, 65, 66,
  67, 70, 71, 72, 74; folios 67, 68, 69, 70, 73, 74, 75, 77). Every "honorary magistrate" and
  "honorary Assistant Commissioner" in these notes rests on that flagged word. The office is
  identifiable from context — the p. 61 passage describes its function — but a human should confirm
  the reading once and apply it throughout.
- **Nawab Nawazish Ali Khan's name is mangled twice before it is given correctly.** "نواب اینڈ
  ٹش[OCR?] علیخان" among the four Muslim honorary magistrates (p. 61, folio 64) and "نواب نواز
  نش[OCR?] علی خان" in the Municipal Committee list (p. 62, folio 65) are almost certainly the
  "نوازش علی خان" of pp. 70–71 (folios 73–74), since that man is stated there to hold the honorary
  magistracy of Lahore — but the identification is mine from the chunk, not stated by it. Likewise
  **"نواب عبد المجید خان ملتانی"** at p. 61 (folio 64) and "نواب عبد المجید خان" at p. 62 (folio 65)
  are, on the chunk's own evidence, the Abdul Majid Khan bin Shah Nawaz Khan of the Multani Nawabs at
  pp. 74–75 (folios 77–78), who holds the same office. Both links should be checked against the
  printed page.
- **Two men called Rahim Bakhsh, and it is not clear they are one.** At p. 61 (folio 64) "شیخ رحیم بخش
  تسو[OCR?] وا گر[OCR?] مقرر ہوا ہے", Shaikh Rahim Bakhsh — with his descriptor illegible-flagged — is
  appointed honorary magistrate in Mool Singh's place. At p. 75 (folio 78) "اب رحیم بخش فوت ہو گیا
  ہی", Rahim Bakhsh, brother of the contractor Mian Karim Bakhsh, has died. The Municipal Committee
  list at p. 62 (folio 65) carries "کریم بخش ٹھیکہ وار" but no Rahim Bakhsh. The chunk never connects
  the two men; do not merge them.
- **Rai Mool Singh is dead on p. 61 and a sitting committee member on p. 62.** p. 61 (folio 64) reports
  his death and the appointment of a successor; p. 62 (folio 65) lists "رے مول سنگھ" among the
  Municipal Committee's honoured members in the present tense; p. 67 (folio 70) dates the death to
  1883, "this very year". Not a contradiction in fact but a seam in the book's revision: the committee
  list was evidently not updated. Any entry using the membership list must date it *before* 1883.
  His name is also corrupt at first appearance: "ہندو چار رئیس ہر سے[OCR?] مول سنگھ" (p. 61, folio 64)
  and "ایک رئیس[OCR?] مول سنگھ" (p. 61, folio 64).
- **The Ajodhya Prashad / Bijnath passage is broken and its genealogy cannot be trusted.** "اُسکے بعد
  دیوان اجودھیا پرشاد و اُسکا بیٹا فوج کا دیوان بنا" leaves it unclear whether the father, the son, or
  both became Diwan of the army; and "دیوان بیجناتھ[OCR?] نہ اسکے بیٹے کو تحصیلداری کا عہدہ ملا"
  (p. 64, folio 67) contains a stray negative and cannot be read as it stands. The name
  "بیجناتھ[OCR?]" is flagged at three of its four occurrences. Also flagged is "پسنجاہا[OCR?]" in the
  sentence about the father disliking his son's transfer (p. 64, folio 67).
- **The self-reference at p. 69 (folio 72) is in a broken sentence.** "جیل کی عمارت کا ٹھیکہ بنگر[OCR?]
  بھی مولف کتاب اسکے پاس ہی" — the flagged word and the syntax leave it undetermined what relation the
  "author of the book" has to the jail-building contract. **A human must re-read this line from the
  PDF** before anything is claimed about the author's own dealings with Lala Nihal Chand.
- **Dates that are OCR-flagged or partly illegible, and must not be used without checking the page:**
  "دسمبر سنہ ۱۸۶۲[OCR?]ع" (Raja Teja Singh's death, p. 63, folio 66); "سنہ ۱۸۷۸[OCR?]ع" (Diwan Ajodhya
  Prashad, p. 64, folio 67); **"آخر سنہ ۱۸۵[illegible]ع"** (Raja Dina Nath's death — the decade digit
  is lost, p. 64, folio 67); "سنہ ۱۸۷۶[OCR?]ع" (Har Krishan, p. 68, folio 71, and Bakhshi Bhagat Ram,
  p. 68, folio 71 — the same flagged year for two different men); "سنہ ۱۸۸۲[OCR?]ع" (the Pathankot
  contract, p. 68, folio 71); **"سنہ ۱۸۳[illegible]ع"** (the English army's arrival at Kabul with Shah
  Shuja, p. 69, folio 72); "سنہ ۱۸۴۵[OCR?]ع" (Faqir Aziz-ud-Din, p. 71, folio 74); "سنہ ۱۸۴۷[OCR?]ع"
  (Faqir Imam-ud-Din, p. 72, folio 75); "سنہ ۱۸۷۲[OCR?]ع" (Faqir Shams-ud-Din, p. 72, folio 75).
  Unflagged, by contrast: 1815, 1826, 1837, 1840, 1845, 1846, 1847, 1848, 1849, 1852, 1857, 1858,
  1860, June 1865, 1863, 1864, 1823, 1883.
