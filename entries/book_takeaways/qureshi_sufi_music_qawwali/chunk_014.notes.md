## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The Glossary defines the genre itself, and the definition carries the book's thesis.** "Qawwali
  Indo-Pakistani Sufi music a group song genre of Hindustani light classical music set to mystical
  poetry in Farsi, Urdu and Hindi and performed in Sufi assemblies for the purpose of arousing
  mystical emotion. Also: assembly where Qawwali is performed" (p. 265, folio 245). The parallel
  entry "mahfil-e-sama'  'gathering, assembly for listening (to spiritual music)'; formal Sufi term
  for Qawwali assembly" (p. 264, folio 244) and "sama'  'listening, audition' the Sufi practice of
  listening to spiritual music the performance of Sufi music" (p. 265, folio 245) place the genre
  inside the samaʿ institution rather than beside it.
- **A whole vocabulary of graded ecstatic arousal is glossed as technical terminology**, from "kaif
  'delight, pleasure', initial state of spiritual arousal" and "halki kaifiyat  'light state of
  ecstasy', initial state of spiritual arousal" through "jhumnd [text-layer?: jhūmnā]  'to sway',
  expressing mild spiritual arousal", "hath batdnd", "hath uthdnd", "khard hona  'to stand up'
  indicating strong spiritual arousal", "girid  'weeping' (syn. with riqqat)", "lotnd [text-layer?:
  lotnā]  'to toss, roll about' in a transported state during Qawwali", to "hdl [text-layer?: ḥāl]
  '(transported) condition', God-given state of spiritual exhilaration, ecstatic state" and "wajd
  [text-layer?: wajd]  'rapture, trance', ecstasy" (pp. 263–266, folios 243–246). Two entries are
  marked as judgements of decorum rather than description: "huhd  noise, cry, expressing spiritual
  arousal (onomat., hence somewhat derogatory)" and "hdl khelna  to act out an ecstatic state
  (somewhat derogatory)" (p. 263, folio 243).
- **The bibliography shows the two literatures the book is written between.** Its Sufi-studies and
  South Asian history side includes "NIZAMI, Khaliq Ahmad 1953/1958 Tarikh-e-Mashaikh-e Chisht
  (Delhi; in Urdu)" and three further Nizami titles (p. 273, folio 253), "RIZ VI, Saiyid Athar Abbas
  1978 [text-layer?: 1978] A History ofSufism in India (New Delhi: Munshiram Manoharlal), 2 vols"
  (p. 274, folio 254), four SCHIMMEL titles (p. 274, folio 254), "TRIMINGHAM, J. Spencer 1971 The
  Sufi Orders in Islam" and "SUBHAN, John A. i960 [text-layer?: 1960] Sufism, its Saints and Shrines"
  (p. 275, folio 255). Its ethnomusicology and social-anthropology side includes Merriam, Blacking,
  Nettl, Seeger, Feld, Herndon, Nattiez, Schenker, Geertz, Goffman, Dumont and Marx (pp. 267–276,
  folios 247–256). Index headwords "ethnomusicology, xiii, 6, 8f, 231-3", "Indie musicology
  [text-layer?: Indic musicology], xiv, jf, I'ili", "cultural performance, 9, 232" and "performance
