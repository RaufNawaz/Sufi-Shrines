## Practices, institutions, economy

The chapter is where the book converts shrine-assembly social structure into an analytic scheme, so
status, authority, custodial identity and the offering are all stated abstractly but explicitly.

- **The assembly's audience is ranked by a composite of status attributes, spiritual first.** Table 30
  sets out the "Attributes of audience (in order of importance)": a "Spiritual dimension" comprising
  "descent affiliation: ancestral pedigree" and "spiritual function: representational pedigree
  (seniority), leadership pedigree"; a "Socio-economic dimension" comprising "patronage/power:
  socio-political pedigree" and "wealth: economic pedigree"; and a "Personality dimension" comprising
  "personal culture: cultural level (of sophistication)" and "age: (physical) seniority" (p. 229,
  folio 209).
- **The criteria of spiritual identity answering to those attributes are "saint, shrine, lineage
  representation", "spiritual guidance", "devotion" and "stylistic preference"** (Table 30, p. 229,
  folio 209). This is the book's formal statement that representing a saint, a shrine or a lineage
  *is* the primary spiritual identity a listener can carry into a Qawwali assembly.
- **The standard audience categories of a Qawwali assembly**, in Table 31 "Status Identification:
  Audience Categories", run from high to low as "titular or outstanding shrine representative",
  "shrine representative", "descendant", "guide", "senior devotee", "patron", "rich man", "devotee",
  "young man", grouped under the "basic status type" headings "spiritual" and "worldly" and the
  "status attributes" "saintly descent", "personal spirituality", "power", "money", "some devotion",
  "some money" (p. 229, folio 209). The table's own key reads "I = basic status indicator / 11 =
  basic status type / III = status attributes / I V = resulting audience categories / > direction of
  decreasing status" (p. 229, folio 209) — see Doubts on the damaged numerals and scrambled layout.
- **Individual listeners are ranked by combined attributes.** Table 32, "Status Identification:
  Individual Listener according to combined status attributes (in order of decreasing individual
  status)", ranks by "Outstanding asset / Primary enhancement (who is a) / Secondary enhancement (and
  also a)": "senior saint's rep." + "patron" + "guide"; "shrine rep." + "patron" + "guide"; "patron" +
  "devotee" + "rich man"; "guide" + "shrine rep." + "rich man"; "rich man" + "devotee"; "descendant" +
  "guide (minor)"; "senior devotee" (p. 230, folio 210).
- **The leader of the assembly is himself evaluated.** "The leader of the assembly is of course
  subjected to the same evaluation procedure. While by definition his overall status is above that of
  the other listeners, it is relevant to measure his relative spiritual status. What is of prime
  concern is to assess the features of his spiritual identity." (p. 231, folio 211)
- **Occasion and place carry spiritual identity; time and locale carry status.** "Occasion and place
  are significant in terms of their spiritual identity component, while time and locale indirectly
  inform the status assessment of the audience." (p. 231, folio 211)
- **Language is a status marker.** As a status criterion the text dimension runs "Farsi-Hindi-Urdu",
  and as an identification criterion "formal-intimate" and "foreign-indigenous" (Table 33, p. 231,
  folio 211). Applied, high status takes "Farsi / Hindi", "sophisticated", "'veiled'"; lower status
  takes "Urdu", "simple", "'open'" (Table 34, p. 233, folio 213).
- **The status distinctions carried by the music are socio-economic, not spiritual.** "The status
  distinctions suggested by these musical dimensions are in essence socio-economic ones; it is
  noteworthy that dimensions indicative of spiritual status in particular have a most limited
  representation in the form of language and style." (p. 232, folio 212)
- **The offering as evidence of arousal, and its limits.** "The formal offering, too, is evaluated by
  the performer as an indicator of the presence of spiritual arousal; however, its constancy of form -
  even where it contains an intensification such as prostration - limits it as an indicator of the
  degree of arousal. Furthermore, the performer is quite aware of the social motivation that may
  underlie an offering." (p. 235, folio 215)
- **Offering and embrace are classed as "Link manifestations".** Table 36 lists "offering, with
  possible prostration" against "any state", with the dynamic "sustain + increase"; and "embrace, with
  possible offering" against "ecstatic or intensely aroused", with the dynamic "sustain + bring to
  completion" (p. 237, folio 217).
- **Repetition is driven by the hope of offerings.** "No less a motivation for multiple repetition is
  the performer's hope of getting offerings following an effective reiterated text unit or, even more,
  an appropriate Insert. Continuing repetition serves to invite and facilitate the mechanics of
  multiple offerings. Indeed, once offerings are coming, no matter what level of arousal stimulates
  them, the performer will keep up multiple repetition of the text unit that generated them, hoping
  for more." (p. 239, folio 219)
- **An offering fixes what gets repeated.** "The same is the case for the offering response, where the
  performer will automatically identify and then repeat the same verse during which an offering is
  first made." (p. 240, folio 220) The offering is also listed among the "Signalling responses" in
  Table 38 (p. 240, folio 220).
- **The performer's selection of listeners is economically motivated.** "While this represents the
  stage to be given highest priority, the performer often prefers the less intense stages which allow
  him to shift his focus between a number of high-status listeners. The obvious reason is economic:
  they generally result in more earnings. Having to cater to a single person in an ecstatic state may
  yield definite blessings, but economically it can be risky, especially where a low-status person is
  in ecstasy." (p. 243, folio 223)
- **The leader polices the performer's material motive.** Performer initiative "is always open to
  criticism and even censure from the assembly leader, especially when he uses it too obviously for
  his material objective" (p. 242, folio 222).
- **Validation of a common listener's ecstasy is a leader's act.** "for the impact of their arousal
  much depends on the general mood of the gathering, and, ultimately, on the validating response of
  the spiritual leader(s) present. In this context, the collective gesture of standing up when a
  common listener rises in ecstasy is seen as a formalized validation by the leader, who initiates and
  terminates the gesture." (p. 236, folio 216)
- **A norm of restraint binds the leader and high-status listeners.** "In congruence with the social
  norm of restrained self-management governing the assembly leader as well as listeners of elevated
  status, the performer expects restraint on the free expression of ecstatic abandonment where such
  high-status listeners are concerned." (p. 235, folio 215)
- **Who may command the singer.** "Such requests, or assertion of control, are normally limited to
  listeners with high spiritual status, except where a state of intense arousal gives even a lowly
  devotee the spiritual justification to assert his wish or need." (p. 241, folio 221)
- **Sufi constraint on performance devices.** Accentuation and acceleration "not only are not textual,
  but they can interfere acoustically with the text message; therefore Sufis consider their excessive
  use inappropriate, if not vulgar, and the aware performer restricts their use accordingly, though he
  may choose to inspire a common audience with their help" (p. 238, folio 218). Of the performer's
  gestures and facial expressions, "collectively called 'actions' by performers and Sufis", their
  "use, even more than that of the other two non-structural intensifiers, is subject to strict
  constraints by Sufis… their excessive use is seen as a distortion of the spiritual message to a
  personal, human level" (p. 238, folio 218).
- **The stated purpose of the assembly.** "the explicit and primary purpose of the Qawwali assembly:
  spiritual arousal" (p. 234, folio 214).
- **Arousal in a spiritually superior listener is received as a blessing by the gathering.** "This
  impact is fairly predictable when it emanates from spiritually superior listeners, since the
  spiritual content of their arousal is recognized by everyone as a blessing." (p. 236, folio 216)
- **Four types of selective focus are defined**, with a maximum crowd size stated for one of them:
  "(Collective focus = no individual catering)"; "Plural focus = cater to several listeners by turn,
  equal attention (maximum 5-6, usually no more than 3)"; "Priority focus = cater to plural needs but
  give priority to one"; "Single focus = cater to single listener, disregarding all others (audience
  usually focussed on single person)" (Table 40, p. 244, folio 224).
- **Status and focus are correlated, and ecstasy overrides status.** Table 40's grid crosses the
  status columns "Leader, top rep.", "Special category" and "Common category" with the state rows
  "Neutral", "Incipient arousal", "Intense arousal" and "Ecstasy", assigning priority values from 1 to
  9: neutral and incipient arousal give 4 / 3 / 1 (priority / plural / collective focus), intense
  arousal 6 / 5 / 2 (single / priority / plural focus), and ecstasy 9 / 8 / 7, all three "single
  focus" (p. 244, folio 224). In the prose: "the pattern is broken only at the stage of extreme
  arousal. Then every listener, from leader to common backbencher, is accorded sole attention by the
  performer." (p. 243, folio 223)

## Arguments and interpretations

This is the chapter in which the book states what it has shown, so the bullets below are the author's
own summary claims, in her order.

- **The chapter's stated task.** "In this chapter we are finally ready to tackle directly the central
  question of this study: how to programme Qawwali music in performance by introducing contextual
  input into the sound rule system." (p. 228, folio 208) It is expressly the terminus of the book's
  argument: "In a sense, all preceding chapters can be considered preliminary to this step; at least
  they each are indispensable as prerequisites to it" (p. 228, folio 208).
- **The operation is reduction.** "this step essentially consists of abstraction and simplification,
  of reducing processes governing complex and diverse variables to analytically manageable dimensions.
  This of course makes sense only because those variables and processes have been identified first"
  (p. 228, folio 208). "What remains is to systematize the performance dimension by expressing the
  diverse and particular in terms of the simple and general." (p. 228, folio 208)
- **The book quotes its own governing question from its preface.** "'How variations in the music are
  generated by variations in the performance situation' (p. xiii) has thus been shown descriptively in
  the previous chapter." (p. 228, folio 208) The "(p. xiii)" is the book's own printed locator; see
  Doubts.
- **The central analytical premise.** "This analysis starts from the premise that the context-music
  interaction operates on the basis of certain underlying principles which constitute the common
  denominators linking the musical idiom with the context of performance. Those principles serve as
  the referents, 'translating' context into music, and in turn expressing context musically." (p. 228,
  folio 208)
- **What the referents deliver is meaning.** "These principles or referents, then, provide the key to
  the way context is evaluated and expressed in music. What this key unlocks is meaning, non-musical
  meaning. It refers to the context, both in its spiritual and social aspects, and is considered to be
  manifest in the music by general agreement among performers as well as listeners of Qawwali." (p.
  228, folio 208)
- **Method: ethnographic data, generalized; emic premises, etic logic.** "The foundation of the entire
  analytical procedure is ethnographic, which is in keeping with the empirical approach followed
  throughout the study. But the data here is of necessity generalized; it includes much that is
  already presented in earlier chapters, as well as much observation and talk with informants. The
  analytical dimension, unlike the ethnographic one, is arrived at by means of what amounts to an
  'etic' or analyst's logic, although based on the application of 'emic' or culture-derived premises."
  (p. 228, folio 208)
- **Two founding premises are stated.** "(a) the idiom is semantically capable of being a referent to
  the context, and - following logically - (b) the idiom can express or manifest the context as well
  as suggest or motivate it" (pp. 228–229, folios 208–209).
- **The analysis runs from context to music because performance does.** "Its starting point is always
  the evaluation of the audience and its responses, or the context. Hence my analytical
  interpretation, too, proceeds from context evaluation to musical expression, and then considers
  their interaction both ways." (p. 229, folio 209)
- **Three semantic referents are established across the chapter**, each tied to a perspective: status
  and identity to the structural perspective (p. 229, folio 209), spiritual state and its
  intensification to the process perspective (p. 234, folio 214), and selective focus to the
  performance perspective (p. 242, folio 222). Their relation is summarized at p. 242 (folio 222):
  "Three semantic referents have been identified as to their function and domain, and their
  inter-relation or intersection has been outlined."
- **Status outranks identity analytically.** "Of the two referents, status is of prime importance
  analytically, because it provides the performer with an ordering frame for the diversity of
  contextual variables he encounters, particularly in the audience." (p. 229, folio 209) Conversely:
  "The second operational principle, identity, is of secondary importance analytically, for its
  function as an ordering device is limited. Only attributes within the spiritual dimension are
  relevant to the performer for his evaluation of the audience, due, obviously, to the spiritual
  function of the Qawwali assembly." (p. 231, folio 211)
- **The musical markers of status are many but coarse.** "what becomes apparent immediately is their
  great number but limited differentiation, that is, all musical dimensions indicative of status
  basically serve the designation of only two broad status categories: high and low." They add up to
  "two opposite musical prototypes - one high-status and resembling classical music and poetry, one
  low-status and resembling popular music" (p. 232, folio 212).
- **Spiritual state differs from status and identity in three stated ways.** First, "spiritual state
  is experiential and has an immediate temporal dimension, that is, it varies with time throughout a
  performance - unlike status and identity which remain unchanged". Second, it "is seen as founded in
  intuitive individual experience, hence it can occur in any listener, regardless of status and
  identity". Third, status and identity "are structural attributes representing established social
  facts or shared assumptions" whose manifestations are consistent and verifiable, whereas "State of
  arousal… is entirely individual and situational and thus subject to interpretation on the basis of
  outward manifestation only." (p. 235, folio 215)
- **Arousal is an inference, not a behaviour.** "Yet, state of arousal is not behaviour - it is an
  abstraction inferred from it, and individual manifestations of such states vary widely." (p. 235,
  folio 215) Hence "the same raised arm may mean to the performer either a significant signal of
  incipient arousal or nothing more than a habitual gesture of appreciation" (p. 235, folio 215).
- **Intensification is derived from Sufi doctrine, not from musicology.** "Sufi tenets makes it clear
  that the primary need inherent in every spiritual state is to realize the arousal potential
  contained in it, in other words to intensify it to its culmination. Indeed, intensification is the
  dynamic inherent in spiritual arousal itself, and a continuum of increasing intensity encompasses
  all stages of arousal." (p. 236, folio 216) The sentence as printed reads "tenets makes"; see
  Doubts.
- **The book's strongest claim in the chunk: at this point music stops reflecting and starts
  producing.** "The crucial point is that in this process the music takes on a much more dynamic
  function than in the application of status and identity criteria. For there the music no more than
  reflects and reinforces the status and identity of listeners, whereas here the principle of
  intensification, realized musically, operates not only to reinforce but actually to generate and
  increase states of arousal." (p. 236, folio 216)
- **Repetition is identified as the musical form of intensification.** "Thus it is not musical units
  or attributes but the principle of structuring such units or attributes which represents
  intensification musically. This structuring principle is repetition." (p. 236, folio 216) The author
  cross-refers the point back to her own musical analysis: "That repetition is indeed a primary
  structuring principle for the Qawwali idiom has already been established in the musical analysis
  (Chapter 2)" (p. 236, folio 216).
- **Three repetition processes are named and glossed**, in the book's romanization: "simple reiteration
  (dohrdnd [text-layer?: dohrānā]), multiple repetition (takrdr [text-layer?: takrār]), and recurrence
  based on recombination and insertion (girah lagdnd [text-layer?: girah lagānā])" (p. 237, folio
  217). "All repetition basically works the same way: it intensifies by reimpressing on the listener
  the same message over and over." (p. 237, folio 217)
- **The idiom gives primacy to text over music**, and this is what differentiates the three: "in
  Qawwali they operate on a musical idiom which gives primacy to text over music. Thus, reiteration
  serves to impress the message on the mind (samjhdnd [text-layer?: samjhānā]) by means of simple
  restatements. Insertion-recurrence serves to make the message explicit by the insertion of an
  amplifier between recurrent statements. Multiple repetition, finally, is the intensifier par
  excellence in a Qawwali performance, impressing the message fully and continuously." (p. 237, folio
  217)
- **Repetition type maps loosely onto arousal stage.** "Reiteration belongs to the lowest stage, where
  no or litde [text-layer?: little] arousal is present… Insertion-recurrence generally presupposes
  that some reiteration has already taken place, and some enthusiasm or mild arousal is present.
  Multiple repetition, finally, implies intense arousal and even ecstasy." (p. 237, folio 217) The
  book qualifies the mapping as "more or less loosely" (p. 237, folio 217).
- **Accentuation, acceleration and 'actions' are secondary and subordinate.** "Acoustically - or
  visually - prominent as non-structural intensifiers might appear, they are in fact entirely
  secondary, since they are essentially subordinate to repetition, the primary musical expression of
  intensification in Qawwali." (p. 238, folio 218) Both acoustic devices "are used sparingly, because
  they retain their effectiveness only in contrast to the unaccented or unaccelerated norms
  respectively" (p. 238, folio 218).
- **The performer evokes states as well as answering them.** "But the performer's strategy consists of
  more than responding to listeners' states; he is as much concerned with evoking such states. This
  means that he may in fact use repetition expressing intense arousal before such arousal is actually
  present, in order to evoke it." (p. 239, folio 219)
- **Ecstasy removes the performer's discretion.** "This flexibility is greatest at the lowest levels of
  arousal, for once intense arousal or ecstasy occurs, the rules of Sufism give the performer little
  choice but to sustain that state by multiple repetition." (p. 239, folio 219)
- **The repeat unit is determined by the timing of the listener's response.** "it is logical for the
  performer to identify as the repeat unit whatever he is singing at the point when he perceives the
  response. Normally - and in conformity with the performer's expectation - that unit is a verse
  line" (p. 240, folio 220); "In analytical terms, this means that by its very occurrence a response
  expressing arousal identifies the unit of repeat, in addition to signalling the need for repetition
  as such." (p. 240, folio 220) The performing norm behind it: "follows a norm of proceeding line by
  line, restating each line in sequence and then repeating the entire verse" (p. 239, folio 219).
- **Three signal types are distinguished** in Table 38 — "Signalling responses" ("i.e. Direct response
  to message statement"), "Emotive responses" ("= Indirect response to message statement (through
  expression of resultant state)"), and "Referential response as signal" ("= Direct message"), the
  last glossed as "request/confirmation: 'Repeat this.'" and "correction: 'Don't repeat this, repeat
  that.'" (p. 240, folio 220).
- **Intensity replaces reference at the top of the arousal scale.** "Once a state of intense arousal
  has been reached, the manifestations of the state are entirely expressive, not referential, so that
  it is the unambiguous intensity that links the response more clearly to the unit of content." (p.
  241, folio 221)
- **Unit identification is analytically separable but practically inseparable from repetition.**
  "Analytically speaking, this process of repeat unit identification can be isolated as an independent
  semantic component in the musician's response to the spiritual state of the audience. But in
  practical terms, it is integral to actualizing the repetition principle, and therefore subsumed
  within the semantic referent of intensification." (p. 241, folio 221)
- **The 'reverse' strategy, and the phrase that names the skill.** "it is equally possible for the
  performer to generate with it the same semantic content independently, not in response to, but in
  anticipation of, corresponding audience reactions. Examples of this 'reverse' strategy abound" (p.
  242, folio 222). Success is described as being "praised for his ability to perceive correctly the
  'colour of the assembly' (mahfil kd rang dekhnd [text-layer?: mahfil kā rang dekhnā])" (p. 242,
  folio 222).
- **The comparative claim that closes section C.** "Observation suggests that consistently successful
  performers take a good deal of initiative of this sort, informed, of course, by a continual
  assessment of their audience, and tempered by immediate responsiveness to its reaction. This aspect
  of the Qawwali performance brings the Qawwal in line with any other type of performer whose
  initiative is ultimately responsible for his success." (p. 242, folio 222)
- **The one-to-one model is justified from Sufi doctrine.** "All aspects of the context-music
  relationship have been mapped out in terms of a one-to-one interaction between performer and
  listener. This procedure is entirely in tune with the individual basis of the Sufi quest as well as
  its realization. Indeed, even where many listeners are present, the performer in principle always
  interacts with individual members of his audience, even though he is dealing with the entire body."
  (p. 242, folio 222)
- **A single channel, whose prime determinant is the text.** "What the performer has at his disposal
  for responding to this multiplicity of needs is in essence one channel of communication, albeit a
  composite and structurally very flexible one: the Qawwali musical idiom. The structural flexibility
  occurs at all levels of the musical idiom, but its prime determinant is the text." (p. 242, folio
  222) Once the song has begun, "the total constellation of text, music and presentation remains
  constant, with only a limited scope left for variation in the area of melodic and rhythmic
  elaboration, or of presentational emphasis" (p. 243, folio 223).
- **Simultaneity is impossible; succession substitutes for it.** "the performer can only make one
  musical response at any one point in time… He can thereby not only accommodate the changing needs of
  one individual but also respond to the differing needs of several, though doing so in immediate
  succession rather than simultaneously." (p. 243, folio 223)
- **Selection is forced by that limit.** "In practical terms, this means that at any one time the
  number of listeners a performer can cater to specifically has to be limited. Clearly, a selection
  process operates to identify those listeners whose needs are to be given first consideration." (p.
  243, folio 223)
- **Only two of the three referents can rank, and the reason is given.** "Two criteria of assessment
  are already established which rank the individual listener on a continuum… One is spiritual status,
  the other spiritual state of arousal (spiritual identity cannot contribute to this assessment
  directly, since it is not applied against a continuum that could serve as a ranking scale)." (p.
  243, folio 223)
- **Monitoring is unequal by status.** "the performer individually monitors all special listeners,
  including of course the leader, while keeping only a casual eye on the common audience." (p. 243,
  folio 223) Earlier, on the breadth of attention required: "the performer, while assessing each
  individual, does so with reference to the audience as a whole, which really means keeping an eye on
  everyone - no wonder he claims to need an eagle's power of observation." (p. 236, folio 216)
- **What the multiple audience adds is quantity of choice, not new kinds of choice.** "In sum, what is
  added by the multiple audience dimension is the motivation for more flexibility, simply because more
  factors are thereby identified and isolated. For the performer, this means making more choices, but
  within the basic range of options already established to deal with the individual listener." (p.
  244, folio 224)
