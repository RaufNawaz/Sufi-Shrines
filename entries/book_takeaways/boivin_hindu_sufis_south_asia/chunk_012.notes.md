# boivin_hindu_sufis_south_asia — chunk 012 — pp. 228–249

Pure reference apparatus, and the best finding aid in the book. The chunk opens mid-sentence at the
head of p. 228 (folio 215) — "did in the rest of South Asia. Notwithstanding, music appeared very
early in the Sufism of Sindh" — inside the tail of the **`Annexes`**, whose last full paragraph runs
to the foot of that page; the sentence it opens with continues from chunk 011. **`Bibliography`**
opens on p. 229 as a display opener with no running head and **no folio at the top or the bottom of
the page**, and runs to the foot of p. 238 (folio 225). It is set in two numbered parts: **`1 Corpus
and references`**, p. 229 to the middle of p. 232 (folio 219), and **`2 Studies`**, which opens lower
on p. 232 and runs to p. 238. **`Index`** opens on p. 239, again a display opener with no running
head and **no folio at top or bottom**, and runs to the end of the chunk at the foot of p. 249
(folio 236), breaking off inside the `Sindhi Hindus` entry at "syncretism of 15–16" — the letters U
to Z continue into chunk 013.

**Folios: every one cited below was read off the page that carries it, none computed.** In this chunk
the folio is set in a **running head at the top of the page in every case**; I checked the foot of
each page as well and **no page in this chunk carries a folio in a footer**. The heads follow folio
parity, not PDF parity: an even folio is set first and the section name after it (`228
Index`, p. 241), an odd folio last (`Index    229`, p. 242). Back matter substitutes the section name
for the book's title, so the head reads `Annexes`, `Bibliography` or `Index`, never *The Hindu Sufis
of South Asia*. **The measured relation printed folio = PDF page − 13 held on all twenty pages of
this chunk that print a folio** (p. 228 → 215 … p. 249 → 236). It fails only at the two display
openers, **p. 229 and p. 239**, which are cited throughout as `(p. 229, folio not stated)` and
`(p. 239, folio not stated)`; on the −13 relation they would be folios 216 and 226, recorded here as
a derived cross-check only and **never used as a citation**.

**The interleaving bites on essentially every line of the index.** The eleven index pages carry about
**542 physical lines, of which all but roughly a dozen set two unrelated columns side by side**. It
is worse than simple adjacency: in reading order the right column *follows* the left column's last
entry, so the top of the right column continues the bottom of the left. Two worked examples, both
from this chunk. On p. 247 (folio 234) the line reads `nirguna prem (mystical love) 153   hindu
references in 91–5` — but `hindu references in 91–5` is a **subentry of `poetry (Sufi)`**, which sits
at the *bottom* of the same page's left column; it has nothing to do with *nirguna prem*. On p. 241
(folio 228) the line reads `Dadi Dhan see Samtani, Dadi Dhan   as pilgrimage 12` — and `as pilgrimage
12` is a subentry of **`darbars (shrine)`**, running on from the left column's last subentry
`Nimano's`. I reconstructed the two columns separately by horizontal position and then **verified
every column break and page break against alphabetical continuity** (p. 239 left ends `al-Qadiri,
Rakhiyyal Shah` → right begins `al-Qonyawi, Sadr al-Din`; p. 242 right ends `hari om` → p. 243 left
begins `Harjani`; and so on for all twenty-two columns). Every break checked out, which is the only
independent evidence I have that the reconstruction is right. Where a fragment's column was genuinely
unclear it is marked `[text-layer?]` and listed under Doubts.

**Index locators are printed folios, not PDF pages**, and are labelled as folios everywhere below.
Locators of the form `201 nn.9–10` and `199 n.8` are folio-plus-note references and are reproduced
exactly in that form.

**This chunk maps sixteen archive rows**: `bhit-bhit-shah`, `shrine-of-sachal-sarmast`,
`lal-shahbaz-qalandar`, `dargah-roza-sufi-shah-inayat-shaheed`, `dargah-fateh-pur-sharif`,
`shrine-of-shah-abdul-karim-bulri`, `shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam`,
`shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht`, `sant-bhagat-kanwar-ram-temple-chak`,
`ramapir-temple-tando-allahyar`, `dargah-pir-ratan-nath-jee`,
`sadh-belo-sadh-belo-island-temple`, `shrine-at-odero-lal-udero-lal-teerath-asthan`,
`mithankot-kot-mithan`, `mazar-of-bulleh-shah` and `mazar-e-iqbal`. The check was run against all 169
rows of `shrine_index.tsv` under the author-only ruling of 18 Sep 2026; the near-misses considered and
refused are set out under Doubts so the next reader does not redo them. **Hinglaj appears at p. 243,
folio 230** and is left unmapped because the archive row carries no id.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author's inference from the Naqshbandi treatise literature, with his own hedge attached.**
  "Deduction shows that the Sindhis, even when they were officially Muslim, did not practise 'Orthodox
  Islam'. Unfortunately, the lack of sources does not allow the researcher to know more on the topic."
  (p. 228, folio 215) The quotation marks around 'Orthodox Islam' are the book's own.
- **A general claim about the Naqshbandis, stated without qualification.** "All over South Asia, the
  Naqshbandis are orthodox Sufis since they give great importance to following sharia. No Muslim Sufi,
  at any level he could have reached, is exempt from submitting himself to the prescriptions of
  Islamic law." (p. 228, folio 215)
- **A counter-example admitted and then restricted.** "As we saw in the introduction, this does not
  mean that a Hindu cannot be a Naqshbandi follower. Thomas Dahnhardt has studied a Hindu branch of
  the Naqshbandiyya (Dahnhardt 2002). Nonetheless, it is hardly the case in Sindh." (p. 228,
  folio 215) The back-reference to the introduction is the book's own.
- **The chunk's one causal argument about why Hindus followed some orders and not others**, quoted
  above under Practices: the Naqshbandi prohibition on music "can explain why the Hindu Sindhis did
  not look after spiritual masters belonging to the Naqshbandiyya" (p. 228, folio 215) — immediately
  qualified, "But, here again, there are exceptions", and the exception given is a book, not a person:
  "the official book devoted to the Naqshbandis of Lowari Sharif was written by a Hindu follower"
  (p. 228, folio 215). **The book's title and the follower's name are not given: (not stated).**

## Doubts

**Damage class 1 — column interleaving (dominant).** Of roughly **542 physical lines across the
eleven index pages, all but about a dozen set two unrelated columns side by side**; the exceptions are
lines where one column has run out, e.g. "Ali, Ghulam 94" standing alone (p. 239, folio not stated).
