# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 004 — pp. 48–57

Qadiri chapter, entries 8 to 19 (Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh to Shah Abul Muali Lahori, whose entry runs
past the end of the chunk). The richest chunk so far for the archive: Shergarh, Musa Pak, Madho Lal
Hussain and Abul Muali all have substantive entries, and the editor's footnotes overturn three of the
author's dates.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shergarh** (شیخ داؤد چونی وال شیر گڈھی [OCR?], "Shaikh Daud Chuniwal Shergarhi" — entry no. 9):
  - Famous among the Qadiri elders of Punjab as a master of *hal* and *qal*, shariat and tariqat; he
    was at worship from evening to morning and never slept the whole night, passing it now standing,
    now prostrate, now bowing, now seated; from the abundance of austerity a special *nisbat* with
    Ghaus al-Azam arose in him, and by Ghaus al-Azam's inner permission he presented himself to Sayyid
    Hamid Ganj Bakhsh, reached completion, and took the *khirqa* of khilafat (p. 48, folio 36).
  - **Died 982 AH**, and "مزار گوہر بار بمقام شیر گڈھ زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے اور ہر سال بڑا ہجوم حضرت
    کے مزار پر ہوتا ہے" — his pearl-scattering mazar at the place Sher Garh is a place of pilgrimage
    for God's creatures, and every year a great crowd gathers at his mazar (p. 49, folio 37). This is
    the chunk's only explicit statement of an annual gathering.
  - Two chronograms are given for the death year: "پیر حق پرست", from which his death date appears,
    and "مشتاق منان", the chronogram recorded in *Akhbar al-Akhyar* (p. 49, folio 37).
  - Descent: *Shajarat al-Anwar* carries his line to Imam Musa Kazim — Sayyid Daud b. Sayyid Fath
    Allah Kirmani b. Sayyid Mubarak b. سید قمیص اللہ [OCR?] b. Safi al-Din Adam Kirmani b. Sayyid Taqi
    al-Din Ahmad, and so on to Musa Mubarqa b. Imam Musa Raza (pp. 48–49, folios 36–37). The author
    then records a correction from another genealogy: Sayyid Muhammad Aaraji b. Sayyid Musa Mubarqa b.
    Imam Muhammad Taqi b. Imam Ali Musa Raza, "کیونکہ سید مبرقع کوئی صاحبزادہ حضرت امام رضا کا نہ
    تھا" — because Musa Mubarqa was no son of Imam Raza (p. 49, folio 37).
  - Migration and birth, on *Safinat al-Auliya*'s authority: his father Sayyid Fath Allah came first
    from Arabia to Hind and settled at **Haibatpur Patti**, then moved to **qasba Chunian**; when he
    died, Sayyid Daud was born four months after his death; he took a full share of the exoteric
    sciences from Maulana Ismail Lahori and then turned to the inner sciences (p. 49, folio 37).
  - He was one of the two "کاملین خلفا", perfect khalifas, of Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh, the other
    being Sayyid Sher Shah Multani (p. 48, folio 36).
  - Seven further sources on him are listed (p. 49, folio 37): Abul Muali Shah Lahori,
    *Naghmat-e Daudi*; Abdul Baqi b. Jan Muhammad Haji Qadri, *Maqamat-e Daudi*, 1056 AH, copied
    1201 AH, manuscript owned by Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi; Abdul Qadir Badayuni, *Muntakhab
    al-Tawarikh*, Nawal Kishore edition, 287–291; Nizam al-Din Ahmad, *Tabaqat-e Akbari* ۱۱ [OCR?];
    Abdul Haq, *Akhbar al-Akhyar* pp. 193–195; Ghausi, *Azkar-e Abrar* / *Gulzar-e Abrar* 207; and
    Dara Shikoh, *Safinat al-Auliya* 193.
  - His nephew and murid was Shah Abul Muali of Lahore, who spent 32 years with him (p. 57,
    folio 45) — see below.
- **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak** (سید موسیٰ پاک شہید — entry no. 15):
  - Beloved son of Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh Gilani Uchi; on completing his training before his father
    he was given the title "جمال الدین ابوالحسن", Jamal al-Din Abul Hasan (pp. 52–53, folios 40–41).
  - Great ulama became his murids, "چنانچہ شیخ عبد الحق محدث دہلوی بھی انہیں کے مرید با اخلاص تھے" —
    Shaikh Abdul Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi among them; the editor dates that attachment precisely to
    6 Shawwal 985 AH / 1577 CE and adds that the shaikh gave him special attention (p. 53, folio 41,
    and حاشیہ).
  - He had a special *nisbat* with the spirituality of Ghaus al-Azam, so that presence was constant,
    and "صدہا دفعہ" — hundreds of times — he benefited from visions of the Prophet waking and
    sleeping; his whole life passed in austerity, struggle, worship, teaching and instruction (p. 53,
    folio 41).
  - **Martyrdom**: "آخر سال ایک ہزار ایک ہجری میں بدخواہان قوم لنگاہ کے ہاتھ سے شہید ہوئے" — finally,
    in the year one thousand and one Hijri, he was martyred at the hands of the ill-wishers of the
    Langah tribe (p. 53, folio 41).
  - "روضہ حضرت کا ملتان میں زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے" — his rauza in Multan is a place of pilgrimage for
    God's creatures (p. 53, folio 41).
  - **The editor rejects the date**: "حضرت موسیٰ پاک شہید کا سال وفات ۱۰۰۱ھ غلط ہے" — the year 1001 AH
    is wrong; Shaikh Abdul Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi derived 998 AH from the chronogram
    "شمع جمع سیادت" (manuscript *bayaz* in the Punjab University library) (p. 53, folio 41, حاشیہ 1).
  - A book of his own, *Taisir al-Thaqalain* (awrad and ma'mulat), has been published — printed at
    Ferozepur 1309 AH — and further sources are listed: *Bahr al-Sarair* on his conditions and
    practices (manuscript owned by Maulvi Hakim Allah Bakhsh Ansari), *Akhbar al-Akhyar* p. [OCR?]
    193–430, and Badayuni's *Muntakhab al-Tawarikh* (p. 53, folio 41, حاشیہ 2).
  - See Practices for the succession dispute with his brother and the mansab Akbar gave him.
- **madho-lal-hussain** (شیخ حسین المشہور بہ لال حسین لاہوری — entry no. 17; شیخ مادھو named at
  p. 55, folio 43):
  - "طریق ان کا ملامتیہ تھا۔ پوشاک سرخ رکھتے تھے۔ اس لیے لال حسین مشہور ہوئے" — his path was
    *Malamati*; he wore red dress, and so became famous as Lal Husain (p. 54, folio 42). The editor
    glosses Malamatiyya as the name of a silsila of free-spirited sufis (p. 54, folio 42, حاشیہ 3).
  - Khalifa of Shaikh Bahlul Daryai; famous as a man of *hal*, *jazb*, longing, taste, *wajd* and
    *sama* (p. 54, folio 42).
  - **Family origin**: his grandfather, (کلجس رائے) [OCR?], was a Hindu who accepted Islam in the
    time of Firoz Shah Tughlaq; his son Usman was a religious man who earned lawful sustenance
    "جولاہوں کے کام سے", by the work of weavers; Shaikh Husain was born in his house (p. 54,
    folio 42).
  - At the age of seven, reading the Quran with a learned man named Abu Bakr, Shaikh Bahlul happened
    to pass through that mosque and his glance so fell on Husain that he became a perfect *wali* while
    still a child (p. 54, folio 42). The editor notes that *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* instead has him become
    the pupil of Hafiz Abu Bakr at the age of ten, in 955 AH (p. 54, folio 42, حاشیہ 4).
  - **He performed several chillas at Data Ganj Bakhsh's shrine**: "کئی چلے مزار گوہر بار علی مخدوم
    گنج بخش ہجویری رح پر کیے اور تکمیل کو پہنچے" — he did several forty-day retreats at the
    pearl-scattering mazar of Ali Makhdum Ganj Bakhsh Hujwiri and so reached completion (p. 54,
    folio 42). See also **data-darbar** below.
  - **Scale of his following**, quoted from *Haqiqat al-Fuqara*: nine thousand perfected servants and
    "ایک لاکھ پچپن ہزار" — one hundred and fifty-five thousand murids — of whom sixteen became famed
    khalifas, four titled Gharib, four Diwan, four Khaki and four Bilawal, with the burial place of
    each given: the Gharibs at Ratti Mittha next to Wazirabad, at لنگرہی [OCR?] in Wazirabad
    district, at جیلا پور [OCR?] in the Deccan, and beside Shah Husain's own grave; the Diwans —
    "پہلے دیوان حضرت کے معشوق و محبوب شیخ مادھو", the first Diwan being **Shaikh Madho, the Hazrat's
    beloved** — with Diwan Gorakh and Allah Diwan buried at Lahore and Diwan Bakhshi at Bijapur in the
    Deccan; the Khakis (Maula Bakhsh Khaki and Khaki Shah at Lahore, Khaki Shah at Wazirabad, Haidar
    Khaki in the Deccan); and the Bilawals (Shah Rang Bilawal, Budhu Bilawal and Shah Mast Bilawal at
    Lahore, Shah Bilawal in the Deccan) (p. 55, folio 43). A footnote directs the reader to Madho's
    own entry in this book — "رجوع کنید بہ احوال مادھو کتاب ہذا" (p. 55, folio 43, حاشیہ 1). **This
    is the book's own link between Shaikh Husain and Shaikh Madho**, which the contents alone did not
    supply.
  - Birth "سال نوسو پینتالیس", the year nine hundred forty-five; death 1008 AH in the reign of Shah
    Akbar; "مزار گوہر بار لاہور میں ہے", his mazar is in Lahore (pp. 55–56, folios 43–44), followed by
    a chronogram quatrain ending "سال ترحیل شمع عشق حسین" with "۹۴۵ھ [OCR?]" printed beside it
    (p. 56, folio 44) — see Doubts.
  - **The editor's verdict on the death year, reached by argument**: all the local histories give
    1008 AH / 1599 CE, of which *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* (1041 AH) is the oldest; *Miftah al-Arifin*,
    composed seven years later in 1048 AH, gives 1013 AH, which makes the *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* figure
    doubtful; but the contemporary historian Badayuni recorded that while composing *Najat al-Rashid*
    in 999 AH / 1590 CE "I learnt at Lahore that Shah Husain, hearing the song of a sweet-voiced
    woman singer, became beside himself and fell from the roof of a house and died" (Najat al-Rashid
    p. 320). The editor prefers the contemporary to the later tazkira-writers: "تحقیق کا حاصل یہ ہے
    کہ شاہ حسین کا صحیح سال وفات ۹۹۹ھ ہے نہ کہ ۱۰۰۸ھ" — the result of the research is that Shah
    Husain's correct death year is 999 AH, not 1008 AH (p. 55, folio 43, حاشیہ 2).
  - **His turn to the Malamati path**, from *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* 58–64: at first an orthodox Muslim, he
    was reading Tafsir-e Madarik with his teacher Shaikh Saad Allah Lahori (d. 999 AH / 1590 CE) when
    the teacher explained "وما الحیوٰۃ الدنیا الا لہو ولعب" and Shah Husain understood the world to be
    mere play; he told his teacher "مجھے حال درکار ہے قال نہیں" — I need *hal*, not *qal* — abandoned
    the student's life and took to the Malamati way. The editor adds that Saad Allah himself often
    voiced the Malamati school, quoting Nizam al-Din Ahmad on him: "بروش ملامتیہ سلوک می نمود"
    (Tabaqat-e Akbari, Lucknow, 391) (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1).
  - **His repentance at the end of life**: it is a fact that in his last years he repented of all acts
    contrary to the shariah, became regular in fasting and prayer, and died in that state (Abdullah
    Khweshgi Qasuri, *Maarij al-Wilayat*, ms folio 519b) (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1).
  - **Shrine practice recorded in the same footnote**: Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori (d. 1040 AH), an
    extremely shariah-bound khalifa of the Mujaddid Alf-e Sani, is quoted as saying "اگر مجھے
    علمائے ظاہر کے طعنوں کا خدشہ نہ ہوتا تو میں اکثر شیخ حسین کے مزار پر جا کر استمداد کرتا" — "If I
    did not fear the taunts of the exoteric ulama, I would often go to Shaikh Husain's mazar and seek
    help" (Akhbar al-Auliya, ms folio 57ab). The editor takes this both as confirmation of the
    repentance and as "بین ثبوت", clear proof, that Shah Husain was an orthodox Muslim whose
    *wilayat* was accepted (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1).
  - **His writings**: two works have so far come to light — *Kafian* in Punjabi and *Risala-e
    Tahniyat* in Persian prose, which presents the problems of tasawwuf in an easy manner; the full
    text, edited and introduced by Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi, was printed in Majalla Maarif,
    Azamgarh, August 1970 and again in Sahifa, Lahore, July 1972 (p. 56, folio 44). Nine further
    sources on him are listed (pp. 56–57, folios 44–45), including Dara Shikoh's *Hasanat al-Arifin*
    pp. 18, 74, and the editor's own article "مادھو لال حسین" in the Urdu Daira-e Maarif-e Islamia of
    Punjab University.
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri** (شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری): named in the footnote above as a khalifa of the
  Mujaddid Alf-e Sani, extremely strict in shariah, and **died 1040 AH** (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1).
  His one recorded act here is the wish to visit Shah Husain's mazar for *istimdad*. The death year
  agrees with the archive row exactly; the affiliation does not — see Doubts.
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** (عاشق لا ابالی شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی لاہوری بن سید رحمۃ اللہ بن سید
  فتح اللہ کرمانی قادری — entry no. 19):
  - "یہ بزرگ برادر زادہ حقیقی شیخ داؤد کرمانی شیر گڈھی کے ہیں اور انہیں کے مرید تھے" — he was the
    true brother's son (nephew) of Shaikh Daud Kirmani Shergarhi and was his murid (p. 57, folio 45).
    His own line — b. Sayyid Rahmat Allah b. Sayyid Fath Allah Kirmani — makes Daud's father his
    grandfather, consistent with the relationship as stated.
  - He attended on his pir for thirty-two years before reaching completion, and after the grant of
    the khirqa of khilafat "لاہور کو مامور ہوئے", was deputed to Lahore (p. 57, folio 45).
  - "راستے میں جس جس مقام پر یہ منزل گزیں ہوئے۔ چاہ و باغیچہ و تالاب بجنہ [OCR?] بنوائے" — at every
    place where he halted on the journey he had a well, a small garden and a tank built (p. 57,
    folio 45). The entry continues past the end of this chunk.
  - He is also cited twice in this chunk as an author: *Naghmat-e Daudi*, the first-listed source on
    Shaikh Daud Kirmani (p. 49, folio 37, حاشیہ).
- **data-darbar** (علی مخدوم گنج بخش ہجویری): appears only as the site of Shah Husain's chillas — his
  mazar is called "مزار گوہر بار", the pearl-scattering mazar, and is where Shah Husain reached
  completion (p. 54, folio 42). The chunk's evidence that the shrine was already an established site
  of forty-day retreats in the sixteenth century.
- **ranmal-sharif** (سلسلہ نوشاہیہ): the Naushahia silsila's rise in Punjab is attributed to Shah
  Maruf Chishti wa Qadri (entry no. 12) — "انہیں کی ذات بابرکات سے پنجاب میں سلسلہ نوشاہیہ نے فروغ
  پایا", through his blessed person the Naushahia silsila flourished in Punjab (p. 50, folio 38). The
  chunk says nothing of Nausha Ganj Bakhsh himself or of Ranmal Sharif; this is a statement about the
  order, not the shrine.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** (حضرت فرید الدین گنج شکر): invoked as the terminus of a lineage
  — Shah Maruf's father, his first pir, had a *shajara* of both descent and spiritual line reaching to
  Farid al-Din Ganj Shakar (p. 50, folio 38).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh b. Sayyid Abdul Razzaq b. Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani** (entry no. 8): a
  great elder of the Qadiriyya Azamiyya; thousands of attendants reached completion through him;
  "بادشاہان وقت بھی ان کی آستان بوسی کو اپنا افتخار جانتے تھے" — the kings of the time counted
  kissing his threshold an honour; he lived at Uch all his life. His perfect khalifas included Sayyid
  Sher Shah Multani and Shaikh Daud Kirmani. Died 978 AH — the footnote sharpens it to 19 Zi Qada
  978 AH and makes him a murid of his grandfather Abdul Qadir Sani (Akhbar al-Akhyar 192) — buried at
  Uch; after his death Sayyid Jamal al-Din Musa sat on the *masnad-e mashikhat*; his death chronogram
  is "شیخ محبوبی" (p. 48, folio 36).
- **Shaikh Bahlul Qadri** (entry no. 10): a great shaykh of the Qadiriyya who took faiz from Shah
  Latif Bari; his Suhrawardi faiz came through the same channel, "کہ شاہ لطیف نے سوائے حیات المیر
  زندہ پیر کے شیخ نصیر الدین قرشی ملتانی سے بھی فیض حاصل کیا تھا" — because Shah Latif, besides Hayat
  al-Mir Zinda Pir, had also taken faiz from Shaikh Nasir al-Din Qurashi Multani. He travelled much:
  i'tikaf at Najaf and Karbala, the Hajj rites at Mecca, several months of khalwat at Medina, a
  forty-day retreat at Ghaus al-Azam's rauza in Baghdad, benefits at Mashhad, and finally the inner
  blessing from an elder who was Qutb of the age on Koh-e Panj Shir Ghazi. Died in the year nine
  hundred eighty-three, chronogram "شیخ بہلول" (given as 983 AH), "روضۂ مبارک چنیوٹ کے علاقہ میں ہے"
  — his blessed rauza is in the area of **Chiniot** (p. 50, folio 38). This also adds a second pir
  for Shah Latif Bari, relevant to **bari-imam**.
- **Shaikh Abu Ishaq Qadri Lahori** (entry no. 11): great khalifa of Shaikh Daud Kirmani; thousands of
  karamat; came to Lahore by Daud's permission and settled in **Muhalla Pir Aziz, Mozang** منعل
  [OCR?]; thousands entered his bai'at and hundreds took instruction in the sciences, fiqh, hadith
  and tafsir. Died in the year nine hundred eighty-five Hijri; "روضۂ حضرت کا موضع مزنگ میں جو لاہور
  سے جانب جنوب دو میل پر واقع ہے۔ زیارت گاہ خلق ہے" — his rauza is in mauza Mozang, two miles south
  of Lahore, and is a place of pilgrimage (p. 50, folio 38).
- **Shah Maruf Chishti wa Qadri** (entry no. 12): first a murid of his own father in the Chishti way
  and perfected in it; hearing of Sayyid Mubarak Haqqani's karamat and absorption he went to him at
  **لکھی جنگل** (Lakhi Jangal) and took the khirqa of khilafat in the Qadiri silsila. Died in the
  year nine hundred eighty-seven Hijri (pp. 50–51, folios 38–39).
- **Sayyid Muhammad Nur b. Sayyid Bahawal Sher Gilani Jarwi** (entry no. 13): eldest son and khalifa
  of Bahawal Sher; his mother's father was Shah Kamal Bukhari, whose mazar is in **qasba Chunian** and
  who is famous by the title **Pir Jahanian**. Died in the year nine hundred eighty-eight Hijri
  (p. 51, folio 39). The editor holds that the contemporary source *Tazkira-e Muqimi* writes the name
  as نور محمد, "جو درست سمجھنا چاہیے", which should be considered correct (p. 51, folio 39,
  حاشیہ 3).
- **Shah Qamees b. Sayyid Abi al-Hayat Gilani Sadhauri** (entry no. 14): his father came from Baghdad
  to Hind, stayed a while in **Bengal**, then settled at **qasba Sadhaura Khizrabad in the Ambala
  area**, where a scholar named Nasr Allah gave him his daughter; Shah Qamees was born of her and was
  "ولی مادر زاد", a saint from birth. "گویا سلسلہ قادریہ ان کی ذات بابرکات سے ہند میں شائع ہوا" — the
  Qadiri silsila spread in Hind through him; "ان کی اولاد اب بھی سادھورہ میں رہتی ہے", his descendants
  still live at Sadhaura. Died 3 Zi Qada, year nine hundred ninety-two Hijri; maqbara at Sadhaura
  (p. 52, folio 40). The footnote carries his line down to Sayyid Rahm Ali Shah (d. 1214 AH) and
  thence, khalifa by khalifa, to Haji Shah Abdul Rahim Shaheed, Mian Nur Muhammad Jhanjhanwi and Haji
  Imdad Allah Muhajir Makki (p. 52, folio 40, حاشیہ 1).
- **Sayyid Kamil Shah Lahori** (entry no. 16): a great elder of the region of Lahore; took bai'at in
  the Qadiriyya and became a perfect wali, then took faiz from Shaikh Ilah Dad داری [OCR?]; "پنجاب
  میں لوگ ان کو دیوان کامل کہتے تھے", in Punjab people called him Diwan Kamil. He came to Lahore in
  Emperor Akbar's time from بخارا [OCR?] and settled in mauza باوساہو [OCR?], dying there in the year
  one thousand and five (p. 54, folio 42). The footnotes add the author's Persian on his dwelling
  "درمیان نیستان متصل موضع باٗو سا ہو" — in a reed-bed adjoining that village — and that one
  عبد الرحیم, a پسر دار [OCR?] خاص شاہی, was his murid (Khazina 1/141) (p. 54, folio 42).
- **Shah Shams al-Din Qadri Lahori** (entry no. 18): took khilafat from Shah Abu Ishaq Qadri Lahori
  and settled in Lahore teaching and instructing; even worldly petitioners never left him
  empty-handed; his perfect khalifas included Shah Bilawal Lahori. Died in the year one thousand and
  twenty-one Hijri (printed in the margin as ۱۰۲۱ [OCR?]); his illuminated rauza is in Lahore (p. 57,
  folio 45). See Practices for Jahangir's devotion to him.
- **Shah Bahlul Daryai**, pir of Shah Husain (p. 54, folio 42) — named in this chunk with the
  attribute "Daryai", where entry no. 10 has only "Shaikh Bahlul Qadri": see Doubts.
- **Shaikh Abdul Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi** — murid of Musa Pak from 6 Shawwal 985 AH / 1577 CE (p. 53,
  folio 41); his *Akhbar al-Akhyar* is cited throughout as the corrective authority.
- **Maulana Ismail Lahori** — the teacher of Shaikh Daud Kirmani in the exoteric sciences (p. 49,
  folio 37).
- **Shaikh Saad Allah Multani then Lahori**, d. 999 AH / 1590 CE — Shah Husain's teacher and, on the
  editor's reading, the source of his Malamati leanings (p. 56, folio 44).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A succession dispute between brothers, lasting years.** After Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh's death a
  quarrel over the *sajjada nashini* broke out between his sons Shaikh Musa and Shaikh Abdul Qadir
  and ran on a long time; the editor quotes Badayuni: "درمیان شیخ عبد القادر و شیخ موسیٰ برادر خوردش
  سالہائے دراز بر سر سجادہ و مشیخت مناقشہ افتاد" — between Shaikh Abdul Qadir and his younger brother
  Shaikh Musa a dispute over the sajjada and the shaykhhood fell out for long years (Muntakhab
  al-Tawarikh vol. 3 p. 91) (p. 53, folio 41, حاشیہ).
- **A shrine family absorbed into Mughal service.** Shaikh Musa left Uch and came to Akbar's court,
  "اور یہاں اکبر نے آپ کو پانچ سو کا منصب دیا" — where Akbar gave him a *mansab* of five hundred
  (ibid. vol. 3 p. 92). Badayuni adds that in religious matters he did not defer even to the emperor:
  if he was in the royal presence when the prayer time came, he would give the azan himself in the
  *diwan khana* and begin congregational prayer, and no one dared stop him (p. 53, folio 41, حاشیہ).
- **Imperial attendance as an annual habit.** "شاہ جہانگیر بادشاہ ان کا کامل معتقد تھا۔ ہر سال جب
  کشمیر کے سفر کو جاتا۔ ان کی خدمت میں حاضر ہوتا" — Emperor Jahangir was a complete devotee of Shah
  Shams al-Din Qadri Lahori, and every year when he set out on the journey to Kashmir he attended on
  him (p. 57, folio 45).
- **An annual gathering at a shrine**, the only one named in the chunk: "ہر سال بڑا ہجوم حضرت کے مزار
  پر ہوتا ہے" at Shaikh Daud's mazar at Sher Garh (p. 49, folio 37).
- **Building works along a route.** Shah Abul Muali, travelling from Shergarh to Lahore, had a well,
  a garden and a tank built at every stage where he halted (p. 57, folio 45).
- **Tomb construction.** Sayyid Muhammad Nur resolved some years after his father's death to build a
  *gunbad* over the grave at Hujra, and the dome was in fact built by the murid-mason of the story
  below (p. 51, folio 39).
- **Land and settlement.** Shaikh Daud's father settled first at Haibatpur Patti, then at Chunian
  (p. 49, folio 37) — Haibatpur Patti is the same place granted as *madad-e maash* to the author's
  ancestor Mian Kalan (p. 27, folio 15, chunk 002), though the book does not connect the two.
- **Shrines as places of pilgrimage**, stated in this formula for three sites: Sher Garh (p. 49,
  folio 37), Mozang (p. 50, folio 38) and Multan for Musa Pak (p. 53, folio 41), each called a
  "زیارت گاہ خلق" or "زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ".
- **Numbers of followers**, as the book gives them: 9,000 perfected servants and 155,000 murids for
  Shah Husain (p. 55, folio 43); "ہزاروں آدمی" in Abu Ishaq's bai'at with "صدہا" taught (p. 50,
  folio 38); "ہزاروں خادم" for Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh (p. 48, folio 36).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Shaikh Daud Kirmani: born four months after his father's death (p. 49, folio 37).
- Sayyid Muhammad Nur and the blinded mason, narrated by the author as "نقل ہے": absent at his
  father's burial, he pitched a tent over the grave, cleared everyone away and dug it open with his
  own hand to see his father's face; a mason among his murids came in without permission and was
  struck blind; years later, when a dome was to be built, the mason asked to be given his sight so
  that he might build the tomb himself, and was told he would see as long as he was at work and go
  blind whenever he rose from it — "چنانچہ جب تک مقبرہ تیار ہوتا رہا۔ ایسا ہی ہوتا رہا", and so it
  continued as long as the tomb was being built (p. 51, folio 39).
- Shah Qamees: a *wali* from his mother's womb (p. 52, folio 40).
- Shah Husain: made a perfect wali at seven by a single glance from Shaikh Bahlul (p. 54, folio 42);
  his karamat beyond counting are said to be recorded in *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* and some in *Khazinat
  al-Asfiya*, and the author explicitly declines to reproduce them — "اس مختصر میں ان کی گنجائش نہیں۔
  اس لیے ترک کی گئیں", there is no room for them in this brief work, so they are left out (pp. 54–55,
  folios 42–43).
- Sayyid Musa Pak: constant presence with the spirituality of Ghaus al-Azam and hundreds of visions of
  the Prophet, waking and sleeping (p. 53, folio 41).
- Shaikh Daud: the special *nisbat* with Ghaus al-Azam arising from sheer austerity, and Ghaus
  al-Azam's inner permission directing him to his pir (p. 48, folio 36).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor's judgement on the main source for Shah Husain**: *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* was written by
  Shaikh Mahmud, known as Pir Muhammad, in Persian verse in 1041 AH and published by the Majlis-e
  Shah Husain, Lahore, with an Urdu prose translation also printed; but "یہ کتاب زیادہ تر سماعی
  حکایات پر مبنی ہے۔ بغیر کسی تحقیق کے رطب و یابس اس میں شامل کر دیا گیا ہے۔ جس سے شاہ حسین کی ذات
  پر کئی الزامات عائد کرنے کا موقع ملتا ہے" — it is mostly based on hearsay tales, the sound and the
  rotten were included without any research, and this is what gives occasion for the several
  accusations laid against Shah Husain's person (pp. 54–55, folios 42–43, حاشیہ 5 and its
  continuation). Every large number, name of a khalifa and burial place in the list at p. 55 comes
  from this book.
- **Method: a contemporary witness beats a later tazkira.** The editor sets out the reasoning
  explicitly on Shah Husain's death year — Badayuni was writing at Lahore in 999 AH, the
  tazkira-writers decades later — and concludes for 999 AH against the whole local tradition of
  1008 AH (p. 55, folio 43, حاشیہ 2). The same preference, stated as a rule, appears in his editorial
  method at p. 35, folio 23 (chunk 002).
- The author preserves rival genealogies rather than choosing: for Shaikh Daud he prints
  *Shajarat al-Anwar*'s line and then the correction "مگر ایک اور شجرہ میں اس طرح صحت ہوئی", with a
  reason (p. 49, folio 37).
- The author also prints two chronograms where two sources disagree — "پیر حق پرست" and
  "مشتاق منان" for Shaikh Daud (p. 49, folio 37) — the same practice noted in the previous chunk.
- The editor's rehabilitation of Shah Husain is an argument in three moves: the Malamati turn is
  traced to a named teacher; the repentance is documented from *Maarij al-Wilayat*; and the visit to
  the mazar by a shariah-strict Mujaddidi khalifa is read as "بین ثبوت" of accepted sainthood (p. 56,
  folio 44, حاشیہ 1). It is the fullest defence of a saint's orthodoxy in the chunk.

## Citable passages

- "طریق ان کا ملامتیہ تھا۔ پوشاک سرخ رکھتے تھے۔ اس لیے لال حسین مشہور ہوئے۔" — "His path was
  Malamati. He wore red dress. For that reason he became famous as Lal Husain." (p. 54, folio 42)
- "مجھے حال درکار ہے قال نہیں۔" — Shah Husain to his teacher: "I need the state, not the speech."
  (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1)
- "اگر مجھے علمائے ظاہر کے طعنوں کا خدشہ نہ ہوتا تو میں اکثر شیخ حسین کے مزار پر جا کر استمداد
  کرتا۔" — Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori: "If I did not fear the taunts of the exoteric ulama, I would
  often go to Shaikh Husain's mazar and seek help." (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1)
- "مزار گوہر بار بمقام شیر گڈھ زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے اور ہر سال بڑا ہجوم حضرت کے مزار پر ہوتا ہے۔" —
  "The pearl-scattering mazar at Sher Garh is a place of pilgrimage for God's creatures, and every
  year a great crowd gathers at the mazar." (p. 49, folio 37)
- "آخر سال ایک ہزار ایک ہجری میں بدخواہان قوم لنگاہ کے ہاتھ سے شہید ہوئے۔" — of Musa Pak: "Finally,
  in the year one thousand and one Hijri, he was martyred at the hands of the ill-wishers of the
  Langah tribe." (p. 53, folio 41)

## Doubts

- **madho-lal-hussain — three death years and a fourth numeral.** The text gives 1008 AH (p. 55,
  folio 43); *Miftah al-Arifin* gives 1013 AH; the editor concludes 999 AH (p. 55, folio 43,
  حاشیہ 2). Separately, the chronogram quatrain closing the entry is printed with "۹۴۵ھ [OCR?]" beside
  it (p. 56, folio 44), which is the year the text gives for his **birth**, not his death; either the
  numeral is misplaced in the source or it is an OCR artefact, and it should not be read as a death
  date. Nothing here should be carried into an entry without the editor's footnote beside it.
- **madho-lal-hussain — age at first instruction.** Seven years old in the text, ten years old in
  955 AH per *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* (p. 54, folio 42 and حاشیہ 4).
- **madho-lal-hussain — the identification with Shaikh Madho is now textual but still partial.** The
  book names "شیخ مادھو" as the first of Shah Husain's four Diwan khalifas and his "معشوق و محبوب",
  and refers the reader to Madho's own entry (p. 55, folio 43 and حاشیہ 1); the editor's own article
  is titled "مادھو لال حسین" (p. 57, folio 45). But this chunk still does not state that the two are
  buried together or that a single shrine holds them. Confirm at Madho's entry (printed p. 52).
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri — the silsila contradicts itself inside this book.** The footnote here makes
  Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori a khalifa of the Mujaddid Alf-e Sani, i.e. Naqshbandi-Mujaddidi, and
  dates him 1040 AH (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1); the table of contents places him at no. 20 inside the
  **Qadiri** chaman as "شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری قادری" (p. 15, folio 3, chunk 001). The archive row also
  carries a recorded discrepancy about his order. The mapping is made on the 1040 AH death year and
  the Lahore attribution; his entry at printed p. 47 must be read before the *silsila* is settled.
- **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak — 1001 AH against 998 AH.** The author's martyrdom year is rejected by the
  editor in favour of Abdul Haq Dehlvi's chronogram-derived 998 AH (p. 53, folio 41, حاشیہ 1). Note
  also that the text says Sayyid Jamal al-Din Musa succeeded to the *masnad* after his father's death
  (p. 48, folio 36) while the footnote records a dispute of "long years" over that same succession
  (p. 53, folio 41) — the book asserts a clean succession and its own apparatus denies it.
- **shergarh — the name in the entry heading differs from the contents.** Here "شیخ داؤد چونی وال شیر
  گڈھی [OCR?]" (p. 48, folio 36), where the contents had "شیخ داؤد جہنی وال شیر گڈھی" (p. 15,
  folio 3, chunk 001). The entry's own account of the father moving to **qasba Chunian** (p. 49,
  folio 37) favours چونی وال, but the heading carries an `[OCR?]` flag and the contents form does not,
  so neither reading can be preferred on the transcription alone. The name "Bandagi" still does not
  appear anywhere.
- **Entry no. 11's name also differs from the contents**: "شیخ ابواسحاق قادری لاہوری" here (p. 50,
  folio 38) against "شیخ ابوالحق قادری" at contents no. 11 (p. 15, folio 3, chunk 001). Entry no. 18
  confirms the entry-heading form by naming "شاہ ابو اسحٰق قادری لاہوری" as Shah Shams al-Din's pir
  (p. 57, folio 45), so the contents reading is the likelier error — but the archive should not
  silently correct either.
- **Shah Husain's pir is named two ways**: "شیخ بہلول دریائی" in entry no. 17 (p. 54, folio 42) and
  simply "شیخ بہلول قادری" in his own entry no. 10, whose rauza is placed in the Chiniot area (p. 50,
  folio 38). Whether they are one man is not stated.
- **Shah Maruf's Naushahia attribution is not explained.** The text says the Naushahia silsila
  flourished in Punjab through him (p. 50, folio 38) but gives no connection to Nausha Ganj Bakhsh,
  who has his own entry at printed p. 58. Do not build a Ranmal Sharif lineage claim on this line
  alone.
- **Pir Jahanian of Chunian is not the archive's Makhdoom Jahaniyan.** Shah Kamal Bukhari, Muhammad
  Nur's maternal grandfather, is said to be famous as "Pir Jahanian" with a mazar at qasba Chunian
  (p. 51, folio 39) — a different place and person from Uch Sharif's Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht.
  Note it so the titles are not conflated.
- **Shah Bilawal Lahori is not the archive's Bilawal Shah Noorani.** The chunk has a Shah Bilawal
  Lahori as Shah Shams al-Din's khalifa (p. 57, folio 45) and four separate Bilawals among Shah
  Husain's khalifas, three in Lahore and one in the Deccan (p. 55, folio 43). None is placed in
  Balochistan.
- **Contradiction in the editor's own citation of *Haqiqat al-Fuqara*'s printing**: published by the
  Majlis-e Shah Husain Lahore in 1966 in one footnote (p. 54, folio 42, حاشیہ 5) and "پیر محمد:
  حقیقت الفقرا ۱۰۴۱ھ لاہور ۱۹۶۵ء" in the source list (p. 57, folio 45).
- **A Quran citation that does not resolve**: the verse behind Shah Husain's turn is referenced
  "(قرآن ۳۲- انعام ۶)" (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1), which pairs a sura number with a differently named
  sura. Carried over as printed.
