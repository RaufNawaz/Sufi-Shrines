# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 018 — pp. 187–195

**The Suhrawardi chapter closes at entry no. 128, the fifth chapter opens with its own rule of
inclusion, and the book reaches its two oldest Lahore shrines: Malik Ayaz (no. 130) and Data Ganj
Bakhsh (no. 131).** This is the chunk with the most direct value for the archive so far. Its three
most substantial features: **the editor rejects a death year by 532 years and calls the source behind
it a fabrication** (Shaikh Ismail Muhaddith, 448 AH → 980 AH); **Data Darbar is described as a
working shrine with a weekly Thursday vigil, a Friday milad, a dome built twelve years before the
author wrote, and a mosque the Auqaf Department was about to enlarge in 1974**; and **the text dates
itself**, saying 827 years had passed since a death it places in 465 AH.

**Folio markers: all nine are ten too high.** As transcribed they run **folio = PDF index − 2**,
p. 187 → folio 185 through p. 195 → folio 193, continuing the anomaly that begins at p. 176.
**Chunks 019–022 settle it against the markers**: the − 2 run ends at p. 197 and the markers revert to
− 12 at p. 198 (p. 197 → "folio 195" then p. 198 → "folio 186", impossible in a printed sequence), and
"folio 194" is claimed for both p. 196 and p. 206. **The relation folio = PDF − 12 holds throughout;
the true folios here are p. 187 → 175 through p. 195 → 183.** Folios are cited **as printed** below —
**subtract ten from every folio in this file.**

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **data-darbar** (شیخ علی مخدوم جلالی غزنوی ہجویری المخاطب بہ داتا گنج بخش — entry no. 131,
  pp. 194–195, folios 192–193 as printed):
  - **Death and burial.** "آخر سال چار سو پینسٹھ ۴۶۵ میں فوت ہو کر **اپنے خانقاہ کے صحن میں مدفون
    ہوئے**" — he died in the year **465** AH and was buried **in the courtyard of his own khanqah**
    (p. 195). The archive row's `figure_died` of 1072 is the CE equivalent of 465 AH. The book adds
    what the row does not: the grave is in the courtyard of the khanqah he himself built.
  - **He built the house and the mosque himself, and they stood when the book was written.** "حضرت
    نے اپنے رہنے کا مکان اور مسجد خود تعمیر کی **جو اب تک موجود ہے**" — the Hazrat himself built his
    dwelling house and the mosque, **which exists to this day**; and "محراب اس وقت اس مسجد کا اور
    مساجد کی نسبت سے کچھ ٹیڑھا بطرف جنوب رہ گیا تھا", at that time the mihrab of this mosque had been
    left **somewhat crooked, toward the south**, relative to other mosques, "علما نے اس بات کا اعتراض
    کیا", and the ulama objected to it (p. 194). The qibla miracle that answers the objection is under
    Legends.
  - **The mosque's later history, from the editor, including the Auqaf Department.** **"مسجد کو
    کشادہ کرنے کے لیے اسے شہید کر کے نئی مسجد بنائی گئی ہے اور اس قدیم محراب کا نشان ایک پتھر کے
    ذریعہ ظاہر رکھا گیا۔ اب محکمہ اوقاف اس مسجد کو مزید وسعت دینے والا ہے"** — to widen the mosque it
    was **demolished** ("شہید کر کے") and a new mosque built, and **the mark of that ancient mihrab
    was kept visible by means of a stone**; and **now the Auqaf Department is about to give this
    mosque further extension** (p. 194, folio 192 as printed, حاشیہ 4). **Three separable facts**: the
    Hujwiri-era mosque no longer stands; a stone marker preserves the crooked mihrab's line; and as of
    this 1974 edition the **Auqaf** was planning another expansion. The editor gives no dates for
    either the demolition or the plan.
  - **The dome is dated, and its two patrons named.** **"حضرت کے مرقد مقدس پر پہلے گنبد نہ تھا۔ بارہ
    برس گذرے ہیں کہ حاجی نور محمد سادھو نے تعمیر کیا اور پھر مرمت سفیدی وغیرہ کی میاں محمد جان رئیس
    امرتسر نے کرائی۔ خدا دونوں کو جزائے خیر عطا فرمائے"** — there was **no dome** over the Hazrat's
    holy tomb before; **twelve years ago Haji Nur Muhammad Sadhu built it**, and then **Mian Muhammad
    Jan, ra'is of Amritsar**, had the repair, whitewashing and so on done; may God reward them both
    (p. 195). A dated building event in the author's own lifetime, and **private patronage from
    Amritsar**, not from the state. On what "twelve years ago" means in absolute terms, see the
    self-dating note under Arguments.
  - **A weekly vigil and a weekly milad, in the present tense, with the congregation's two cities
    named.** **"اب بھی ہر ایک جمعرات کو معتقدان شہر لاہور و شہر امرتسر جمع ہو کر حضرت کے مزار پر
    تمام رات بیدار رہتے ہیں۔ شام سے صبح تک درود شریف و نعت شریف کا ذکر ہوتا ہے۔ پھر دن کو ہر ایک جمعہ
    کے روز میلاد ہوتا ہے۔ عام و خاص حضرت کے سلام سے بہرہ مند ہوتے ہیں"** — even now, **every
    Thursday**, the devotees of the **city of Lahore and the city of Amritsar** gather and **stay
    awake all night** at the Hazrat's mazar; from evening until morning there is recitation of *darud*
    and *naat*; then **by day, every Friday, there is a milad**, and high and low share in the
    Hazrat's salam (p. 195). **No annual urs is mentioned anywhere in this entry** — the observance
    the book records is weekly, in two parts, on two consecutive days.
  - **Dara Shikoh quoted for a votive practice.** "حضرت شاہزادہ دارا شکوہ فرماتے ہیں کہ **چالیس
    جمعرات جو کوئی پیہم ان کے مزار پر جلائے۔ خدا سے جو مانگے سو پائے**" — Prince Dara Shikoh says
    that whoever lights [a lamp] at his mazar for **forty consecutive Thursdays** receives from God
    whatever he asks (p. 195). This is the stated authority for the Thursday cycle.
  - **"اس وقت لاہور مرجع علما و فضلا تھا"** — at that time Lahore was the resort of the learned
    (p. 194); he taught students by day and instructed seekers by night; "ہزاروں جاہل ان کے ذریعہ سے
    عالم، ہزاروں کافر مسلمان، ہزاروں گمراہ روبراہ، ہزاروں دیوانے صاحب عقل و ہوش ہزاروں ناقص کامل و
    ہزاروں فاسق نیکو کار ہوئے" — thousands of the ignorant became learned, thousands of unbelievers
    Muslim, thousands of the misled set straight, thousands of the mad sane, thousands of the deficient
    perfect and thousands of the sinful righteous through him.
  - **Descent and initiation as printed.** "حضرت حسنی سید تھے" — a **Hasani** sayyid: Makhdoom Ali
    Ganj Bakhsh Hujwiri bin Sayyid Usman bin Sayyid Ali bin Abd al-Rahman bin Shah Shuja bin Abu
    al-Hasan Ali bin Husain Asghar bin **Sayyid Zaid Shahid** bin Imam Hasan bin Ali. Bai'at with
    **Shaikh Abu al-Fazl bin Hasan Khuttali Junaidi**, he with Shaikh Husri, he with Shaikh Abu Bakr
    Shibli; his pir gave him leave, after completion, for the guidance of the people of Hind, "انھوں
    نے لاہور میں آ کر ہنگامہ فضیلت و مشیخت گرم کیا" (p. 194). The genealogy is contested by the
    editor: see Arguments.
  - **"تمام عمر حضرت لاہور میں رہے"** — he remained in Lahore his whole life (p. 195).
  - **His grave as a station for other saints** — Muin al-Din Chishti's chilla and Baba Farid's
    "ذوق و شوق" — is under Practices, with the cross-reference to
    **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar**.
  - **Three Persian chronograms, each resolving to 465**, are printed after the entry, with "۴۶۵"
    against each: "علی غزنوی آن شاہ ہجویر / سراپا نور روشن ماہ ہجویرے [OCR?]"; "عیاں تاریخ او چوں ماہ
    گفتم / علی ہجویری عالی جاہ گفتم ۴۶۵"; "چو جستم از خرد تاریخ سالش / عیاں شد کاشف دین ارتحالش ۴۶۵"
    (p. 195). And then a footnote opening **"سال وفات میں اختلاف ہے"** — there is disagreement about
    the year of death — which **breaks off mid-sentence at the page's end** and continues in chunk 019:
    see Doubts.
- **darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz** (شیخ ایاز لاہوری — entry no. 130, pp. 192–193, folios 190–191 as
  printed):
  - **Who he is, in the book's words.** "یہ شخص **شاہ محمود غزنوی کا غلام و محبوب** تھا۔ حق جل شانہ
    نے اس کو دولت ظاہری و باطنی عطا کی تھی" — he was the **slave and beloved of Shah Mahmud
    Ghaznavi**, and God gave him both outward and inward wealth (pp. 192–193). This matches the
    archive row's description of the figure as the slave of Mahmud Ghaznavi.
  - **A tutor to a rebel prince, and the political sequence in full.** After Mahmud's death Masud
    took the throne; in his reign his son **Majdud**, "جو نو جوان لڑکا تھا", a young boy, was made
    **governor of the Punjab**, and **Ayaz was appointed his atalik (tutor) and came with him**. When
    Masud died and his son **Maudud** took the throne, Majdud **rebelled and set up a separate
    sultanate in Hindustan**; Maudud marched against him; "جب لاہور کا محاصرہ ہوا تو **بروز عید
    ناگاہ مجدود بمرگ مفاجات مر گیا**", when **Lahore was besieged**, on the **day of Eid** Majdud
    suddenly died a sudden death, and Maudud established his hold over the Punjab (p. 193).
  - **A refusal to return to Ghazni, and a renunciation.** Maudud tried hard to take Ayaz with him to
    Ghazni, "مگر اس نے منظور نہ کیا اور تارک الدنیا ہو کر صحبت فقرا اختیار کی اور **بزرگان لاہور سے
    فیض کامل حاصل کیا** اور بے انتہا دولت جو اس کے پاس تھی، **براہ خدا فقرا و غربا کو دے دی**" — he
    did not agree, renounced the world, took to the company of faqirs, **received complete faiz from
    the elders of Lahore**, and **gave away the boundless wealth he had to the faqirs and the poor for
    God's sake** (p. 193). This is the book's account of how a Ghaznavid officer becomes a shrine.
  - **The claim that he refounded Lahore.** **"یہ بزرگ بانی لاہور کا بھی شمار کیا جاتا ہے"** — this
    elder is also **counted the founder of Lahore**: when Sultan Mahmud Ghaznavi attacked Lahore and
    **Raja Anangpal, son of Raja Jaipal**, was overthrown and fled to Hind after the encounter,
    Mahmud's army plundered the city, all the subjects left, "شہر میں کوئی متنفس باقی نہ رہا", not a
    breathing soul remained in the city; **"اس وقت ایاز نے سلطان سے اجازت لے کر اس شہر کو پھر آباد
    کیا اور رعایا کو دور دور سے طلب کر کے اس میں قیام پذیر کیا"** — then Ayaz, taking the sultan's
    permission, **settled the city again** and, summoning subjects from far and wide, settled them in
    it (p. 193). **The editor rejects this tradition outright: see Arguments.**
  - **Death and grave.** "آخر سنہ چار سو پچاس ہجری میں وفات پائی اور **شہر لاہور کے اندر دفن ہوا**"
    — died in the year **450** AH and was **buried inside the city of Lahore** (p. 193). No quarter is
    named; the archive row's "Shah Alam Market" is not in this chunk. **The editor replaces the year
    with 449 AH: see Arguments.** Note that the archive row's "8 August 1041, calendar era unstated"
    agrees with neither: see Doubts.
- **Chain and cross-reference mentions only:**
  - **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** (خواجہ فرید الدین گنج شکر چشتی): "خواجہ فرید الدین گنج شکر
    چشتی نے **ذوق و شوق کا مذاق بھی اسی دربار سے پایا**" — Khwaja Farid al-Din Ganj Shakar Chishti
    **also got his taste of ardour and longing from this very darbar** (p. 195). A claim about Data
    Darbar's formative role, not a fact about Pakpattan; nothing about his own shrine, dates or urs is
    said here.
  - **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی): the terminus of **three** chains
    in this chunk — Mian Wada's thirteen-step silsila (p. 188), Muhammad Aqil Lahori's *isnad* for the
    *Qasida-e Amali* and *Qasida-e Burda* (p. 187, حاشیہ 1), and, from chunk 017, Shah Jamal's and
    Shah Daula's. In this book he is the root of the Punjab's Suhrawardi teaching and reading chains
    alike.
  - **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht** and **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**: two links in Mian
    Wada's chain — "سید جلال الدین مخدوم جہانیاں" and "شیخ رکن الدین ابوالفتح ملتانی" (p. 188). Also
    in that chain, "**سید نہ ناصر الدین**", here **without an OCR flag**, where chunk 017 flagged the
    same syllable: see Doubts.

- **Shaikh Muhammad Ismail Lahori, famous as Mian Kalan — also Mian Wada** (entry no. 126,
  pp. 187–188, folios 185–186 as printed): among the elders of **Lahore**, a man of the shari'a,
  renowned as devout, God-fearing and abstinent; **"تمام عمر انہوں نے قرآن کا درس جاری رکھا"**, he
  kept the teaching of the Quran running his whole life. "تمام رات عبادت میں اور تمام روز تدریس
  قرآنی میں گذر جاتا" — all night in worship and all day in Quranic teaching. **Father Fath Allah bin
  Abdullah bin Sarfaraz, "اور قوم کے زمیندار کھوکھر تھے"** — by community **zamindar Khokhars** (one
  of very few statements of a saint's caste or clan in these chunks). Born **995**, died **1085** AH;
  "مزار گوہر بار لاہور میں زیارت گاہ خلق ہے". The shrine's living institutions and its named sajjada
  nashin are under Practices; his thirteen-step chain to Baha al-Din Zakariya runs Shaikh Ismail ←
  Shaikh Abd al-Karim ← Makhdum Tayyib ← Shaikh Bahan al-Din ← Makhdum Chin ← Shaikh Milo ← Shaikh
  Husam al-Din Multani Muttaqi ← Sayyid Shah Alam ← Bahan al-Din Qutb ← Sayyid [نہ] Nasir al-Din ←
  Sayyid Jalal al-Din Makhdoom Jahaniyan ← Shaikh Rukn al-Din Abu al-Fath Multani ← Shaikh Sadr al-Din
  Arif ← Shaikh Baha al-Din Zakariya Multani (p. 188). The editor adds his students **Mian Taimur
  Lahori** and **Mian Hamid Qari Lahori**, "پورے پنجاب کے علما نے ان سے ظاہری و باطنی فیض حاصل کیا",
  and his parents' graves: per Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi, his **mother Mai Farbad[OCR?] is buried at
  qasba Joga Kalyan[OCR?], district Gujrat**, and his **father at mauza Jaiba Chima[OCR?] adjoining
  Ahmad Nagar, district Gujrat** (p. 187, حاشیہ 1).
- **Shaikh Jan Muhammad Thani Lahori Suhrawardi — Jan Muhammad Parvezabadi** (entry no. 127, p. 189,
  folio 187 as printed): khalifa of Shaikh Ismail Mudarris Lahori; first murid of **Shaikh Abd
  al-Hamid**, himself Ismail's khalifa, then completed under Ismail; "ظاہری علوم میں بھی یہ بزرگ
  عالم متبحر تھا۔ **تمام پنجاب کے علما اس کے قول پر صاد کرتے تھے**", all the ulama of the whole Punjab
  set their seal on his word. Died **1100** AH and was buried in **Mohalla Parvezabad**. **His
  reburial three years later is under Practices.** The editor: he also drew on Mian Wada and Mian
  Taimur Lahori, and **"پرویز آباد میں اپنی رہائش اور درس و تدریس کے لیے خاص حجرہ بنوایا تھا"**, had
  a special **hujra** built in Parvezabad for his residence and his teaching (p. 189, حاشیہ 1).
- **Shaikh Karam Shah Qureshi Harithi Hankari[OCR?]** (entry no. 128, p. 190, folio 188 as printed):
  of the Suhrawardi family; pedigree in seven steps to Abd al-Jalil Chuhar Lahori (chunk 016, entry
  no. 110) — Karam Shah bin Shaikh Abu al-Fath bin Shaikh Abu al-Hasan bin Shaikh Fakhr al-Din bin
  Shaikh Abu al-Fath bin Barkhurdar bin Shaikh Abu al-Fath bin Shaikh Abd al-Jalil Chuhar Lahori.
  **"پہلے اس کی سکونت لاہور میں تھی۔ جب غارت گر ملا [OCR?] قوم سکھ نے پنجاب میں ہنگامہ غارت گری گرم
  کیا تو یہ بزرگ لکھنؤ کو چلا گیا"** — he first lived in **Lahore**; when the **plundering Sikh
  people** set the tumult of plunder warm in the **Punjab**, this elder went away to **Lucknow** and
  spent some years with his maternal grandfather **Shaikh Nur al-Hasan Qureshi**. "مراجعت کے وقت
  **متصل شاہجہان پور** سال ایک ہزار دو سو میں **قزاقوں کے ہاتھ سے شہید ہوا**" — on his return, near
  **Shahjahanpur**, in the year **1200**, he was **martyred at the hands of bandits**; "۱۲۰۱ھ اس کا
  سال وفات ہے", **1201 AH is his death year** (two years in one sentence: see Doubts). **Three sons**:
  Sikandar Shah, Shah Murad, **Qalandar Shah** (d. **1228**). Qalandar Shah's grandson-line continues:
  his son **Shaikh Ghulam Muhyi al-Din Qureshi**, "مرد صاحب خلق و مروت و فتوت", died **1279**. The
  food-multiplication miracle at **mauza Sanda** is under Legends. **The only displacement of a Lahore
  shrine family by the Sikh period recorded in these chunks.**
- **Shaikh Muhammad Ismail Muhaddith wa Mufassir Lahori** (entry no. 129, p. 191, folio 189 as
  printed): of the great **Bukhari** sayyids; he came and settled in **Lahore in the time of Sultan
  Masud Ghaznavi** and began the teaching of hadith and tafsir. **"صاحب کتاب تحفۃ الواصلین لکھتا ہے
  کہ اول جو شخص داعیان اسلام سے لاہور میں آیا اور لوگوں کو ہدایت طریق اسلام کی کی یہی بزرگ تھے"** —
  the author of *Tuhfat al-Wasilin* writes that **the first person of the callers to Islam to come to
  Lahore** and guide people to the path of Islam was this elder; "ان کے وعظ کی تاثیر سے ہزار ہا کفار
  داخل اسلام ہوئے … اور **دین اسلام ان کے طفیل سے پنجاب میں شائع ہوا**", thousands of unbelievers
  entered Islam by the effect of his preaching, and the religion of Islam **was published in the
  Punjab through him**. Death **448** AH, the chronogram being "متاب"; **"مزار شہر لاہور کے جنوب کی
  طرف ہے"**, his mazar is **to the south of the city of Lahore**. **The editor demolishes all of this:

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A named living sajjada nashin holding one office over both a langar and a Quran school.**
  **"سجادہ نشین و مہتم لنگر و تدریس قرآنی حافظ احمد الدین ہے۔ اس نے کمال خوبی و خوش اسلوبی لنگر بھی
  جاری رکھا ہوا ہے اور تدریس قرآنی بھی بدستور جاری ہے"** — the **sajjada nashin and manager
  (مہتم) of the langar and of the Quranic teaching is Hafiz Ahmad al-Din**; he has kept the langar
  running with the utmost excellence and good management, and the Quranic teaching also continues as
  before (p. 188, folio 186 as printed). **The clearest statement of shrine administration in these
  chunks**: one named man, one composite office, two functions, both reported as operating at the time
  of writing.
- **A shrine-based madrasa counted, and fed twice a day.** At Mian Wada's mazar: **"اس مزار پر انوار
  پر درس قرآن جاری ہے۔ سو دو سو رویش [OCR?] حافظ و ناظر ہر وقت موجود رہتا ہے۔ کھانا بھی ان کو دو
  وقتہ ملتا ہے"** — Quran teaching **continues at this shrine**; **one or two hundred** [رویش OCR?]
  **hafiz and nazir** students are present at all times; and **they also receive food twice a day**
  (p. 188). A student count, a residential arrangement and a feeding regime at a single Lahore shrine,
  set against the author's own gloss on his times: "اس زمانہ ناپرسان میں کہ اہل زمانہ دین کی راستی
  کو بالکل بھول گئے ہیں", in this unregarded age, when the people of the time have altogether
  forgotten the straightness of religion.
- **A weekly two-day observance at Data Darbar, with no annual urs recorded**: an all-night Thursday
  vigil of *darud* and *naat* by the devotees of **Lahore and Amritsar**, then a **Friday milad**
  (p. 195, folio 193 as printed). Authorised by **Dara Shikoh's forty-Thursday lamp** on the same page.
  For an archive entry this is the single most specific statement of practice at Data Darbar in the
  book so far — and it is **weekly, cross-city and lay-organised**, with no custodian named.
- **A dome built by private patronage twelve years before the writing, repaired by an Amritsar
  notable** — Haji Nur Muhammad Sadhu and Mian Muhammad Jan (p. 195). Note that **the tomb had no
  dome at all before that**: a checkable architectural claim about Data Darbar's fabric.
- **A mosque demolished to be widened, its old mihrab marked by a stone, and the Auqaf Department
  planning a further extension** (p. 194, folio 192 as printed, حاشیہ 4). **The only appearance of
  Auqaf in these chunks**, and it is a statement of intent, not of completed work.
- **A grave dug up and moved to lie beside the pir's, on a dream's instruction, three years after
  burial.** Of Jan Muhammad Thani: he was buried in Mohalla Parvezabad, and **"بعد تین سال کے مقدم
  محمد نے اس کو خواب میں دیکھا اور اس کو ارشاد ہوا کہ ہماری نعش اس جگہ سے نکال کر شیخ اسماعیل کے
  مزار کے پاس دفن کر"** — after three years **Muqaddam Muhammad** saw him in a dream and was
  instructed: take our corpse from this place and bury it near Shaikh Ismail's mazar. "مقدم نے قبر
  اس کی کھود کر نعش نکالی اور شیخ اسماعیل کے مزار کے پاس دفن کی مگر **بلحاظ ادب سر اس کی قبر کا شیخ
  اسماعیل کی قبر کے برابر رکھا**، مگر دوسرے روز جب دیکھا تو **دونوں قبریں برابر نظر آئیں**" — the
  muqaddam dug up the grave, took out the corpse and buried it near Shaikh Ismail's mazar, but **out
  of deference set the head of his grave level with Shaikh Ismail's grave**; yet next day, when they
  looked, **both graves appeared level** (p. 189, folio 187 as printed). For the archive: a dated
  exhumation and reburial, an explicit etiquette of relative grave position, and the mechanism by
  which a single shrine becomes a two-grave complex.
- **A named Lahore teaching quarter and its four teachers.** Of Mohalla Parvezabad: it is generally
  said to have been settled by **Prince Parvez bin Jahangir**, whose **garden** was here; "اس کے آس
  پاس **علما کا ایک عظیم مرکز** تھا جس میں **حضرت میاں وڈا کا مدرسہ** تھا اور اس مدرسہ میں **میاں
  جان محمد قصاب پوری، میاں جان محمد پرویز آبادی، میاں حامد قاری اور میاں محمد عاقل لاہوری بحیثیت
  مدرس پورے پنجاب کو سیراب کرتے رہے**" — around it was a **great centre of ulama**, in which stood
  **Mian Wada's madrasa**, and in that madrasa Mian Jan Muhammad Qassabpuri, Mian Jan Muhammad
  Parvezabadi, Mian Hamid Qari and Mian Muhammad Aqil Lahori, as teachers, **watered the whole
  Punjab** (p. 189, folio 187 as printed, حاشیہ 1). A shrine, a madrasa, a princely garden and four
  named chairs in one Lahore quarter.
- **A reading-licence chain written out as a document.** Muhammad Aqil Lahori's own *sanad* for the
  *Qasida-e Amali* and the *Qasida-e Burda*, quoted in Persian: "فقیر محمد عاقل از حضرت حامد القاری
  … مرخص شدہ والیشاں مرخص از میاں تیمور والیشاں از میاں حافظ اسمٰعیل جیو والیشاں از مخدوم طیب جیو
  والیشاں از مخدوم خیر الدین والیشاں از مخدوم بہان والیشاں از مخدوم میلو تا چند جا مرتبہ بمرتبہ رسید
  بہ حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی" — licensed by Hamid al-Qari, he by Mian Taimur, he by Mian Hafiz
  Ismail Jio, he by Makhdum Tayyib Jio, he by Makhdum Khair al-Din, he by Makhdum Bahan, he by Makhdum
  Milo, and so through several stages, rank by rank, **up to Baha al-Din Zakariya Multani** (*Tuhfat
  al-Muslimin* folio 44[OCR?]) (p. 187, folio 185 as printed, حاشیہ 1). **A pedagogical, not
  initiatic, chain terminating at a shrine** — the same names as Mian Wada's silsila, in a different
  order.
- **A shrine as a place where later saints earned their rank.** Muin al-Din Chishti's **chilla "پاؤں
  کی طرف"**, at the feet of Data's grave, and Baba Farid's "ذوق و شوق کا مذاق … اسی دربار سے", and
  then the general claim: "علیٰ ہذا القیاس تمام بزرگان اقلیم ہند جس قدر ہوئے ہیں **سب نے اس کی
  آستانہ بوسی کی ہے**" — all the elders of the realm of Hind, however many there have been, **have
  kissed this threshold** (p. 195). The book's statement of Data Darbar's rank among Indian shrines.
- **A wealthy convert's fortune distributed to the poor** — Ayaz's "بے انتہا دولت … براہ خدا فقرا و
  غربا کو دے دی" (p. 193) — the third such renunciation-and-distribution in these chunks after Hassu
  Teli and Jan Muhammad's petitioner in chunk 017.
- **A resettlement of a depopulated city, as the book tells it** (p. 193): Lahore emptied after
  Mahmud's plunder, and repopulated by Ayaz "رعایا کو دور دور سے طلب کر کے". Whatever its historical
  standing — and the editor denies it — this is the tradition attached to this shrine.
- **Displacement by the Sikh period, and a death on the road.** Karam Shah's family leaves Lahore for
  Lucknow during Sikh plundering and he is killed by bandits near Shahjahanpur on the way back
  (p. 190, folio 188 as printed). The only Sikh-era event in these chunks, and it is recorded as the
  cause of a Lahore lineage's absence.
- **Manuscripts in named private and institutional hands**, several the editor's own: Mian Hamid
  Qari's maktubat and "بہت سے قلمی رسائل **ہمارے ذاتی کتب خانے** میں ہیں", **in our personal
  library**; more of his treatises in Ghulam Muhyi al-Din Qasuri's collection at the **Ganj Bakhsh
  Library, Rawalpindi**; *Tuhfat al-Muslimin* "بخط مصنف", in the author's own hand, owned by
  **Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi**; a **"رسالہ در عملیات و تعویذات میاں اسماعیل"** — a treatise on
  operations and amulets — owned by **Ghulam Rasul Makhdum, imam of the mosque of Chhani Lak[OCR?],
  district Gujrat**; *Silsilat al-Awliya* (1267 AH[OCR?]) and *Ibrat Nama* (1135 AH[OCR?]) in
  manuscript (pp. 187–194).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The Kaaba shown to settle an objection about a crooked qibla.** When the mosque was ready, Data
  Ganj Bakhsh invited all the ulama, led the prayer himself as imam, then "بعد نماز سب کو روبقبلہ
  کھڑا کیا اور کہا کہ **دیکھو قبلہ کس طرف ہے** فوراً بحکم خلاق اکبر **پردے اٹھ گئے اور کعبہ سامنے سے
  نمودار ہوا** اور سب نے بچشم ظاہر دیکھ لیا اور اپنے اعتراض سے نادم ہوئے" — after the prayer he stood
  them all facing the qibla and said, see which way the qibla is; at once, by the command of the great
  Creator, **the veils were lifted and the Kaaba appeared before them**, and all saw it with their
  outward eyes and were ashamed of their objection (pp. 194–195). The karamat is a verification of a
  building's orientation, and the building is the one whose mihrab-mark the editor says is now
  preserved under a stone.
- **A congregation made hafiz by one glance, split left and right.** Of Mian Wada: a young murid's
  bride, herself a hafiza, refused his company until he had memorised the Quran — "جب تک تو قرآن حفظ
  نہ کرے۔ میری صحبت کے لائق نہیں ہے اور میں نہیں چاہتی کہ ناخواندہ آدمی مجھ سے ہم صحبت ہو کر قرآن کی
  بے ادبی … کرے" — and the shaikh told him to stand at his right hand at the dawn prayer. After the
  salam, "**نظر فیض اثر داہنی طرف کے نمازیوں پر پڑی تو سب کے سب قرآن کے حافظ ہو گئے اور بائیں طرف کے
  ناظر ہوئے**" — his gaze of effectual grace fell on the worshippers to the right and every one of
  them became a **hafiz**, while those on the left became **nazir** [readers]; the murid was among the
  hafizes and lived out his life at home in gratitude (p. 188, folio 186 as printed). Note that the
  miracle produces exactly the two categories of student the same page says are resident at the
  shrine.
- **Two graves that levelled themselves overnight** — Jan Muhammad Thani's, under Practices (p. 189).
- **Food for five multiplied to feed five hundred, with the count of vessels.** Of Qalandar Shah at
  **mauza Sanda**: five men's food was cooked, then murids gathered until "نوبت پانچ سو آدمی کی پہنچ
  گئی", the turn reached **five hundred men**; he had the pot of curry and the available breads brought
  before him, **cast his blessed mantle (ردائے مبارک) over the breads**, distributed **two breads per
  person** and then the curry, and **"دو سو پچاس کس برتن منقسم ہوا"** — two hundred and fifty vessels
  were distributed; and when all had eaten, "اسی قدر طعام باقی تھا جس قدر تھا", as much food remained
  as there had been (p. 190, folio 188 as printed).
- **A prince dies suddenly on the day of Eid during the siege of Lahore** — "بروز عید ناگاہ مجدود
  بمرگ مفاجات مر گیا" (p. 193), told in the entry of the saint who was his tutor, and dated by the
  editor to **10 Zil-Hijja 433 AH / 17 July 1042 CE**.
- **"اِنَّ اَولِیاءَ اللّٰہ لا یموتون"** — the friends of God never die — given with the author's own
  parenthetical Urdu gloss "(تحقیق اولیائے اللہ کبھی نہیں مرتے ہیں ۱۲)", closing the statement that
  Data's wilayat is "آج تک گرم ہے، بلکہ روزافزوں", warm to this day, indeed increasing by the day
  (p. 195).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **A death year moved by 532 years, and the source behind it called a fabrication.** Of Shaikh Ismail
  Muhaddith Lahori, whom the text places at 448 AH, the editor argues at length across two footnotes
  and concludes: **"بحث کا حاصل یہ ہے کہ شیخ اسمٰعیل محدث لاہوری کا صحیح سال وفات ۹۸۰ھ ہے نہ کہ
  ۴۴۸ھ"** — the upshot of the discussion is that **the correct death year of Shaikh Ismail Muhaddith
  Lahori is 980 AH, not 448 AH** (p. 192, folio 190 as printed). The argument, in his order:
  - the year "محل نظر ہے", is open to question; the Shaikh Ismail Muhaddith Lahori whom **Baghawi**
    mentioned is exactly this shaikh, and **Ghausi** gives his death year as **980 AH [OCR?]**;
  - **"ان کا ذکر معاصر کتب میں نہیں ملتا"** — no contemporary book mentions him, and the reliable later
    tazkiras **Nafahat al-Uns** and **Akhbar al-Akhyar** are likewise "ان کے ذکر سے خالی", empty of
    any mention of him;
  - **"مفتی صاحب کی محولہ کتاب تحفۃ الواصلین ایک وضعی تذکرہ ہے۔ جس کا وجود اس وقت نہیں ہے"** — the
    book the Mufti cites, *Tuhfat al-Wasilin*, is **a fabricated tazkira, which does not now exist**,
    "ورنہ اس کی حیثیت کے بارے میں مزید بحث کی جاتی", or its standing would be discussed further;
  - the author of **Nuzhat al-Khawatir**, "جن کا پنجاب کے سلسلہ میں سب سے بڑا ماخذ خزینۃ الاصفیا ہے
    اور انہوں نے عموماً خزینۃ الاصفیا کی روایات **بغیر کسی تحقیق کے** شامل کتاب بھی کر لی ہیں" —
    whose greatest source for the Punjab is the Mufti's own *Khazinat al-Asfiya*, and who generally
    took its reports into his book **without any investigation** — nonetheless has **no** Ismail Lahori
    d. 448 AH in volume 1, but does have, on Ghausi's authority, **Ismail Naqshbandi Lahori d. 980 AH**
    at 6/30[OCR?]; "مگر اکثر متاخرین نے مفتی صاحب کی تقلید کی ہے", yet most later writers have
    followed the Mufti;
  - and **Sayyid Hashimi Faridabadi's** identification of him with **Shaikh Ismail al-Shashi**, whom
    Ali Hujwiri names among his Ghazni contemporaries in *Kashf al-Mahjub* (p. 178[OCR?]), "جو درست
    نہیں۔ ان کا لاہور سے کسی قسم کا تعلق ثابت نہیں ہے" — is not correct, and no connection of his with
    Lahore is established (*Maathir-e Lahore* part 2, p. 1[OCR?]).
  **For the archive this is the book's most aggressive editorial act: it removes the claimed first
  preacher of Islam in Lahore from the eleventh century altogether and puts him in the sixteenth,
  and it does so by an argument from silence in contemporary sources plus a judgement that a cited
  source is forged.** The Ghausi notice he prefers gives Ismail Naqshbandi Lahori's studies in
  **Iran** with **Saif al-Din Ahmad Shahid Harawi** and **Amir Sayyid Jamal al-Din Ata Allah[OCR?]
  Muhaddith**, a **Naqshbandi** affiliation, discipleship toward **Amir Abdullah Harawi (Mir
  Qibti[OCR?])**, and that "ہجری سنہ نو سو اسی میں فرمان طلب قبول فرما کر لاہور میں خواب گاہ اختیار
  کی", in 980 AH he accepted a summoning farman and took his resting place in Lahore (*Azkar-e Abrar*,
  translation of *Gulzar-e Abrar* 498) (p. 192).
- **A cited source dated out of existence, with the editor's own article as the authority.** Of
  *Tuhfat al-Wasilin*: it "شیخ احمد زنجانی سے منسوب ہے", is **attributed to Shaikh Ahmad Zanjani**;
  **Kanhiya Lal** called it a composition of **435 AH [OCR?]** containing the lives of Lahore's
  learned (*Tarikh-e Lahore* p. 9[OCR?]) — "لیکن خود ہی آٹھویں صدی ہجری کے اعیان کے حالات کے سلسلہ
  میں تحفۃ الواصلین کے حوالے دیے ہیں", **but he himself gave references to it for notables of the
  eighth century AH**; and Mufti Ghulam Sarwar cited it throughout *Khazinat al-Asfiya*, for instance
  for **Sayyid Aziz al-Din Lahori, d. 796 AH [OCR?]** (2/255) and **Sayyid Ishaq Gazruni Lahori,
  d. 890 AH [OCR?]** — "جس سے ثابت ہوتا ہے کہ تحفۃ الواصلین آٹھویں صدی ہجری کے بعد کی تصنیف ہے",
  **from which it is proved that *Tuhfat al-Wasilin* is a composition later than the eighth century
  AH** (p. 191, folio 189 as printed, حاشیہ 1). The authority cited for the full argument is
  **"محمد اقبال مجددی: تحفۃ الواصلین کا سال تصنیف، معارف اعظم گڈھ، نومبر ۱۹۶۷ع"** — the editor's own
  article, in *Ma'arif*, Azamgarh, **November 1967**. **This confirms from inside the book that the
  editorial apparatus is Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi's**, as chunk 017's "راقم" and "ہمارے ذاتی کتب
  خانے" also indicate. **A dating argument from internal anachronism — the strongest piece of
  source criticism in these chunks.**
- **A founding tradition refused, and a rival's inscription called plainly wrong.** Of Ayaz as founder
  of Lahore: **"ایاز کے بانی لاہور ہونے کی روایت متاخرین کے قیاس کا نتیجہ ہے"** — the tradition of
  Ayaz being the founder of Lahore is **the result of later writers' conjecture**; "کنگھم اور ڈپٹی
  محمد لطیف وغیرہ نے اس قسم کی عام قیاس آرائی سے کام لیا ہے", **Cunningham and Deputy Muhammad
  Latif** and others worked by this sort of general conjecture; Latif even quoted a building
  chronogram of that era "جس سے ۳۰۵ھ [OCR?] برآمد ہوتا ہے **جو صریحاً غلط ہے**", from which 305 AH
  [OCR?] emerges, which is **manifestly wrong**; and "خود لطیف ہی محمود کے الحاق کے بعد لاہور کو
  دوبارہ **محمود کے ہاتھوں** آباد ہونے اور یہاں کی **چھاؤنی ایاز کے سپرد** کرنے کا ذکر (ص ۴۵ [OCR?])
  پر کر چکے ہیں" — **Latif himself had already recorded, at his p. 45[OCR?], that after Mahmud's
  annexation Lahore was resettled by Mahmud's own hands and its cantonment entrusted to Ayaz**
  (p. 193, folio 191 as printed, حاشیہ 4). **The editor turns the shrine's foundation legend into an
  administrative fact — Ayaz as garrison commander, not city founder — using the very author who
  propagated the legend.**
- **A death year replaced by weighing two historians against each other, with the rule stated.**
  **"فرشتہ نے مودود کی وفات کے چند دن بعد ایاز کے انتقال کر جانے کی اطلاع دی ہے۔ لیکن ابن اثیر نے
  لکھا ہے کہ ایاز ۴۴۹ھ/۱۰۵۷ع میں فوت ہوا۔ فرشتہ کے مقابلہ میں ابن اثیر کی وفیات درست تسلیم کی گئی
  ہیں۔ اس لیے ہمارے نزدیک ابن اثیر کا قول (۴۴۹ھ) معتبر ہے"** — **Firishta** reports Ayaz dying a few
  days after Maudud's death, but **Ibn Athir** wrote that he died in **449 AH / 1057 CE**; **against
  Firishta, Ibn Athir's obituaries have been accepted as correct; therefore in our view Ibn Athir's
  statement (449 AH) is reliable** (*Hashimi Faridabadi, Maathir-e Lahore* 1/56[OCR?]) (p. 193,
  حاشیہ 5). The text says 450 AH. Note the form of the argument: not a nearer witness (as in chunks
  012 and 016) but **a general reputation for accuracy in a genre** — obituary notices.
- **A famous romance called exaggeration, and dated to a later literature.** **"محمود و ایاز کے جن
  معشوقانہ تعلقات کا ذکر ادبیات میں ملتا ہے۔ وہ یقیناً مبالغہ آمیزی سے خالی نہیں ہیں۔ سلطان محمود
  جیسا جابر اور مدبر محلہ [OCR?] اپنے غلام کو کہاں اتنی ڈھیل دے سکتا تھا۔ نیز محمود و ایاز کے یہ
  قصے سلجوقی عہد کے بعد فارسی ادبیات میں شہرت پاتے ہیں"** — the amorous relations of Mahmud and Ayaz
  found in the literature are **certainly not free of exaggeration**; how could a tyrant and
  calculating ruler like Sultan Mahmud give his slave so much licence; moreover these tales of Mahmud
  and Ayaz **gain currency in Persian literature only after the Seljuq era** (*Mahmud Shirani,
  Maqalat* 5/95[OCR?], 485[OCR?]) (p. 192, folio 190 as printed, حاشیہ 1). A literary-history
  argument used on a shrine's central story.
- **A laqab dated by its earliest attestation.** Of "Ganj Bakhsh": **"لقب گنج بخش بہت بعد میں معروف
  ہوا ہے۔ ہمارے علم میں اس وقت تک قدیم ترین مصنف جس نے سب سے پہلے گنج بخش لکھا ہے۔ وہ محمد قاسم
  عبرت لاہوری مصنف عبرت نامہ (بسال ۱۱۳۵ھ [OCR?]) ہے"** — the title *Ganj Bakhsh* became known **much
  later**; to our knowledge the oldest author so far who first wrote *Ganj Bakhsh* is **Muhammad Qasim
  Ibrat Lahori, author of the *Ibrat Nama* (of the year 1135 AH [OCR?])** (p. 194, folio 192 as
  printed, حاشیہ 2). **For the archive: the name "Data Ganj Bakhsh" is attested from the twelfth
  Hijri century, not the fifth, on this edition's evidence** — a citable point about the shrine's
  naming, and it is the editor's own finding ("ہمارے علم میں").
- **A pedigree's transmission traced, and one word in it rejected.** The editor notes that **before the
  Mufti, Maulvi Muhammad Salih Kunjahi copied this genealogy in *Silsilat al-Awliya* (1267 AH
  [OCR?]) on the authority of *Ghunya-e Muhammadi*** (p. 194, حاشیہ 1); and then, from Nami:
  **"مفتی غلام سرور نے زید کے ساتھ جو لفظ شہید لکھا ہے وہ ٹھیک نہیں۔ کیونکہ جو زید شہید مشہور ہیں وہ
  امام زین العابدین بن امام حسین بن علی کے فرزند تھے"** — the word *Shahid* that Mufti Ghulam Sarwar
  wrote with Zaid is **not right**, because the famous Zaid Shahid was the son of Imam Zain
  al-Abidin bin Imam Husain bin Ali (*Bazurgan-e Lahore* p. 198[OCR?]) (p. 194, حاشیہ 3). **Data Ganj
  Bakhsh's Hasani descent turns on this word, and the footnote unsettles it without replacing the
  chain.**
- **A tomb's location denied.** Of Mohalla Parvezabad: "متاخرین نے لکھا ہے کہ اس محلہ میں شہزادہ
  پرویز کا مقبرہ بھی ہے **جو محض غلط ہے**۔ اس لیے کہ شہزادہ پرویز کو وفات کے بعد **برہان پور سے آگرہ
  میں لا کر دفن کیا گیا تھا**" — later writers have written that Prince Parvez's tomb is also in this
  quarter, **which is utterly wrong**, because after his death Prince Parvez was brought **from
  Burhanpur to Agra** and buried (*Abdullah Chughtai, Lahore Sikhon ke Ahd mein* pp. 118[OCR?],
  119[OCR?]) (p. 189, folio 187 as printed, حاشیہ 1). The third "محض غلط" verdict in two chunks, and
  the second about where a grave is.
- **The fifth chapter's rule of inclusion, stated by the author.** At the head of "**پانچواں چمن /
  مشائخ متفرقات خاندانوں کے تذکرے میں**", the fifth garden, on the memorial of the mashaikh of
  **miscellaneous families**: **"مخفی نہ رہے کہ اس باب میں قید ایک سلسلہ کی نہیں۔ ہر ایک سلسلہ کے
  بزرگ کا حال سوائے ان چار سلاسل کے جن کا ذکر اول تحریر ہو چکا ہے۔ اس میں تحریر ہو گا یا ان بزرگوں کا
  ذکر اس میں درج ہو گا جو پنجاب میں عالم و فاضل صاحب نام گذرے ہیں"** — let it not be hidden that in
  this chapter there is **no restriction to one silsila**; the circumstances of an elder of **every**
  silsila other than the four already written of will be written here, **or** the mention will be
  entered of **those elders who passed in the Punjab as scholars and men of name** (p. 191, folio 189
  as printed). **Read with chunk 012's rule — a mazar in the Punjab — this completes the book's
  stated scope: the first four chapters are order-based, and the fifth admits anyone of standing in
  the Punjab, learned as well as Sufi.** It is the single most useful sentence in these chunks for
  judging what a search of this book can yield.
- **The book dates itself, by arithmetic on its own figures.** Of Data Ganj Bakhsh: "حضرت کی ولایت کا
  بازار باوجودیکہ **آٹھ سو ستائیس ۸۲۷ برس** ان کی وفات کو گذرے ہیں آج تک گرم ہے" — the market of the
  Hazrat's wilayat is warm to this day, although **827 years** have passed since his death (p. 195).
  With the death at **465 AH** on the same page, **827 years later is 1292 AH**, i.e. the mid-1870s
  CE — which would place the original composition in the author's thirteenth Hijri century and make
  the dome, "بارہ برس گذرے ہیں", built around **1280 AH**. This is arithmetic on two numbers the
  chunk itself prints, and it is offered as such: see Doubts before relying on it.

## Doubts

- **The folio offset is − 2 throughout this chunk, not the − 12 the job states.** Nine markers,
  pp. 187–195 → folios 185–193, all consistent with each other and with chunk 017 and with chunk 016's
  last two pages. **The break is at p. 176 and the offset has not returned.** Every folio in these
  notes is marked "as printed". A human must resolve this against the PDF before any folio is
  published, and **chunk 016's reading of it as a two-page digit slip must be corrected.**
- **data-darbar: "بارہ برس" and "۸۲۷ برس" are the author's, not the editor's, and this is a 1974
  edition of a nineteenth-century text.** The dome's date, the Auqaf note and the weekly vigil
  therefore belong to **two different presents**: the vigil and the dome to the author's time
  (827 years after 465 AH, i.e. about 1292 AH by arithmetic on the chunk's own numbers), the mosque's
  demolition and the Auqaf plan to the editor's (1974). **An entry must not write that the Thursday
  vigil, the Friday milad or the dome's condition are current**, and must not present the 1292 AH
  figure as the book's own statement of its date — the chunk gives 827 and 465 and does not add them.
- **data-darbar: no annual urs, no custodian and no endowment are mentioned.** The absence is worth
  recording: for the archive's largest shrine, this chunk supplies weekly practice, two private
  patrons, a mosque history and a planned Auqaf extension, and **nothing at all** about a sajjada
  nashin, a langar, land or income — in sharp contrast to Mian Wada's entry three pages earlier,
  which names its sajjada nashin and its langar manager.
- **data-darbar: the death year is under dispute and the dispute is cut off.** The text gives 465 AH
  three times over in chronograms, and the editor's footnote begins **"سال وفات میں اختلاف ہے۔ آقائے
  عبدالحی حبیبی نے کشف المحجوب کے اندرونی…"** — there is disagreement about the year of death; Mr **Abd
  al-Hai Habibi**, from the internal [evidence of] *Kashf al-Mahjub*… — and **ends there, "(باقی
  صفحہ آئندہ)"** (p. 195, folio 193 as printed, حاشیہ 1). **Do not state 465 AH as this edition's
  settled year until chunk 019's continuation is read.** Given this editor's record — 532 years on
  Shaikh Ismail, 37 on Shaikh Musa, 12 on Shah Daula, one on Ayaz — the continuation may not agree
  with 465.
- **data-darbar: the Hasani genealogy is destabilised but not replaced.** Nami's objection removes
  "Zaid **Shahid**" from the line between Husain Asghar and Imam Hasan (p. 194, حاشیہ 3) without
  supplying the right name, and the chain's transmission is traced only as far back as *Ghunya-e
  Muhammadi* via an 1267 AH[OCR?] manuscript. **Cite the pedigree only with the footnote attached.**
- **darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz: three death years are now in play and the archive's is not one of them.**
  Text **450 AH**; editor **449 AH / 1057 CE** on Ibn Athir; Firishta, as the editor reports him, "a
  few days after Maudud's death". The archive row records "**8 August 1041**, as given in the account;
  calendar era unstated", which corresponds to neither 449 nor 450 AH and falls **inside** the period
  the editor assigns to Majdud's governorship (427–433 AH). **The row's date and this book's dates
  cannot both be right; flag the row rather than amending it from here.**
- **darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz: the shrine's two best-known claims are both denied by its own edition.**
  The founding of Lahore is "later writers' conjecture", and the Mahmud–Ayaz romance is post-Seljuq
  literary growth. **An entry that repeats either must say that this 1974 edition rejects both, and
  must note that the editor offers a replacement for the first — Ayaz as commander of the
  cantonment, on Latif's own page 45[OCR?].** Neither denial is dated or sourced to a primary
  document; both rest on modern scholarship (Shirani, Hashimi Faridabadi, Latif's self-contradiction).
- **Entry no. 129's claim of Islamic priority in Lahore collides with entry no. 131 on the book's own
  figures**, and the editor's correction removes the collision. Ismail Muhaddith is the first caller
  to Islam in Lahore and dies 448 AH; Data Ganj Bakhsh arrives and dies 465 AH. Move Ismail to 980 AH
  and Data becomes the earlier figure by five centuries. **The book therefore contains, unreconciled,
  both an eleventh-century rival to Data Darbar's primacy and a footnote that abolishes him.** Record
  both; do not let an entry present either as settled.
- **Karam Shah's death year is given twice in one sentence, differently** — "سال ایک ہزار دو سو میں …
  شہید ہوا" and "۱۲۰۱ھ اس کا سال وفات ہے" (p. 190, folio 188 as printed). **1200 and 1201 AH.** The
  editor does not remark on it. His nisba is OCR-flagged ("ہنکاری [OCR?]"), as is the phrase
  describing the Sikhs ("غارت گر ملا [OCR?] قوم سکھ") — the flagged word sits inside the only
  sentence in these chunks about Sikh-era plundering, so **the characterisation itself should be
  re-read from the PDF before it is quoted.**
- **Two Jan Muhammads of Lahore, and the chunk half-distinguishes them.** Entry no. 125 dies 1082 AH
  and is buried by the **Masjid Qassab Khana** outside Lahore; entry no. 127 dies 1100 AH, is buried
  in **Mohalla Parvezabad** and then reburied beside Shaikh Ismail; the editor's footnote names
  **"میاں جان محمد قصاب پوری"** and **"میاں جان محمد پرویز آبادی"** as two distinct teachers in the
  same madrasa (p. 189). The quarter-names line up with the two graves, but **the chunk never states
  the identification**, and chunk 017 raised a third Jan Muhammad (Chishti) in the Ghore Shah
