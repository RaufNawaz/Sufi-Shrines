# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 013 — pp. 138–147

Entries 87 (conclusion) to 95: the Mujaddidi succession from Saif al-Din to Shah Abu Said, with Mirza
Mazhar Jan-e Janan and Shah Ghulam Ali Dehlvi at its centre. **No archive row takes material** (see
Doubts). The chunk is nonetheless the densest in these thirteen on **the politics of patronage and the
editor's method**: the Mujaddid's grandson is *sent to live inside Aurangzeb's fort* one generation after
his father refused the court at all; a saint is shot dead by doctrinal opponents; and the editor
overturns four death years, one burial place, one book title, one genealogical count and one claim of
imperial descent.

**The folio markers agree with the relation on every page**: folio = PDF index − 12, p. 138 → folio 126
through p. 147 → folio 135, each marker reading exactly that.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Khwaja Muin al-Din Naqshbandi Lahori** (entry no. 87 concluded, p. 138, folio 126): he settled in
  **Kashmir** and stayed "اس خانقاہ میں جو ان کے باپ کی تعمیر تھی", in the khanqah that was his father's
  construction, and spent his whole life in guidance. **"علمائے ظاہری بھی حضرت کے دل سے فرمانبردار تھے۔
  ہر ایک مقدمہ میں جو حضرت فتویٰ دیتے۔ کسی کو انکار نہ ہوتا"** — even the ulama of the outward obeyed him
  from the heart; in every case (*muqaddama*) in which the Hazrat gave a fatwa, no one demurred. Died,
  "بقول صاحب تواریخ", according to the author of the *Tawarikh*, on 2 Muharram in the year 1085 AH. No
  grave stated.
- **Shaikh Saif al-Din bin Shaikh Muhammad Masum bin the Mujaddid** (entry no. 88, pp. 138–139,
  folios 126–127): **janishin of his father**; titled **Muhi al-Sunnat**, Reviver of the Sunna, for his
  extreme scrupulousness. "جو کوئی شخص کفار یا فساق و فجار میں سے ان کے روبرو آتا تائب ہو جاتا", whoever
  of the unbelievers or profligate came before him repented; "دنیا داروں کی صحبت سے ان کو کمال نفرت
  تھی", he had utter aversion to worldly company; and "اہل محفل میں سے جب کوئی لفظ اللہ کا زبان پر لاتا
  تو شیخ پر حالت وجد طاری ہو جاتی", when anyone in the company brought the word "Allah" to his tongue, a
  state of wajd came over him. His khanqah's kitchen is under Practices. Died in the year 1098 AH; mazar
  at **Sirhind**. The editor moves the date and puts him inside the imperial fort: see Arguments and
  Practices.
- **Shaikh Sa'di Bulkhari[OCR?] Mujaddidi Lahori** (entry no. 89, pp. 139–140, folios 127–128): "لاہور
  کے بزرگان نقشبندیہ میں سے یہ بزرگ صاحب کمال مشہور ہیں", famous among the Naqshbandi elders of Lahore.
  **At eight** the longing for the Truth seized him, and through **Maulana Haji Sa'd Allah Wazirabadi** he
  came to **Shaikh Adam Banuri**, the Mujaddid's khalifa, was completed, and went with his pir to the
  **Haramain Sharifain**, where he spent some years. When Shaikh Adam died at **Medina** he came to
  **Lahore** and settled. His marvels are recorded at length in *Rauzat al-Salam*, for which "گنجائش اس
  مختصر کتاب میں نہیں ہے", there is no room in this brief book. Died 3 Rabi al-Thani, a Wednesday, in the
  year 1108; **"مزار بیرون لاہور موضع مزنگ کے پاس ہے"** — his mazar is **outside Lahore, near mauza
  Mozang** (p. 140, folio 128). The editor supplies his real name and doubts his nisba: see Arguments.
- **Maulana Haji Sa'd Allah Wazirabadi** (editor's footnote, p. 139, folio 127): **d. 1102 AH**, a great
  scholar of his time with authority to guide; "سلوک مجددیہ کے تذکروں میں آپ کا نام اسداللہ بھی لکھا
  ہوا ہے", in the tazkiras of Mujaddidi suluk his name is also written **Asad Allah** — and a letter of
  Khwaja Saif al-Din is addressed to "حاجی اسداللہ وزیر آبادی" (*Maktubat-e Saifiya* pp. 71, 122). A
  khalifa of Shaikh Adam Banuri. **"ان کا مسکن و موطن سرائے وزیر خان (وزیر آباد ضلع گجرات پاکستان)
  تھا"** — his dwelling and home was **Sarai Wazir Khan (Wazirabad, district Gujrat, Pakistan)**, and
  **"پشاور، لاہور اور وزیر آباد میں ساری زندگی تبلیغ و ارشاد میں سلسلہ مجددیہ آدمیہ کی نشر و اشاعت میں
  نمایاں کردار ادا کیا"**, in **Peshawar, Lahore and Wazirabad** he played a prominent part all his life
  in propagating the **Mujaddidiya Adamiya** silsila (Muhammad Amin Bakhshi, *Tarikh al-Harbain*[OCR?],
  ms folios 216b–222). A named sub-order and a three-city circuit, all from the editor.
- **Shaikh Adam Banuri** (editor's footnote, pp. 139–140, folios 127–128): "خلیفہ اعظم حضرت مجدد الف
  ثانیؒ", the Mujaddid's greatest khalifa, **d. 1054 AH**; he played a prominent role in propagating
  Mujaddidi suluk and had countless murids and khalifas. **"آپ کی مذہبی سرگرمیوں کی وجہ سے شاہجہان نے
  آپ کو ملک بدر کر دیا تھا۔ چنانچہ آپ عرب شریف میں رہے اور یہیں آپ کا مزار جنت البقیع میں ہے"** — because
  of his religious activities **Shah Jahan banished him from the country**; so he lived in Arabia and his
  mazar is in **Jannat al-Baqi**. Works: *Khulasat al-Maarif* (a two-volume ms owned by Muhammad Iqbal
  Mujaddidi), *Nazm al-Nikat*, *Nikat al-Asrar*, *Wuzuh al-Mazahib*. **His khalifa Maulana Muhammad Amin
  Badakhshi wrote a bulky book, *Nataij al-Haramain*, on him**, with manuscripts in the **Bhana Mari
  library, Peshawar**, the **Islamia College Library, Peshawar**, and the library of **Maulana Yusuf
  Banuri, Karachi**. See Doubts: a second footnote in the same entry dates his death to 1052 AH.
- **Sayyid Nur Muhammad Badayuni** (entry no. 90, pp. 140–141, folios 128–129): greatest khalifa of Saif
  al-Din; istighraq and intoxication dominated his temperament; utmost avoidance of worldly company; the
  Shia bai'at episode is under Legends. Died 11 Zi al-Qada in the year 1135; the text says mazar at
  Sirhind, **which the editor corrects**: see Arguments. The editor calls him "اجل عالم اور پابند شرع
  صوفی", a most eminent scholar and a shariah-bound Sufi who "ہر وقت کتاب و سنن اپنے پاس رکھتے", always
  kept the Book and the Sunna by him — and gives a striking illustration: **"ایک مرتبہ بیت الخلا میں
  داخل ہوتے ہوئے راست پاؤں پہلے رکھ دیا تو تین روز تک سیر باطنی بند رہا"**, once he entered the privy
  right foot first and his inward journeying was blocked for three days. Countless people took inward
  faiz from him, "جن میں حضرت مرزا مظہر جان جاناںؒ کا نام گرامی سرفہرست ہے۔ جن سے سارے عالم اسلام میں
  مجددی سلوک کا عام رواج ہوا", foremost Mirza Mazhar Jan-e Janan, through whom Mujaddidi suluk became
  generally current **throughout the whole Muslim world** (p. 140, folio 128, حاشیہ 3).
- **Shaikh Abd al-Ahad bin Shaikh Muhammad Saeed bin the Mujaddid** (entry no. 91, pp. 141–142,
  folios 129–130): janishin and khalifa of his own father, who sat on the **masnad of hidayat and
  irshad** after him. **"سلسلہ مجددیہ ان کی ذات بابرکات سے تمام زمانہ میں پھیل گیا۔ خلفا ان کے تمام دور
  دراز ملکوں سے مکہ مدینہ میں جا کر مصروف بہدایت خلق ہوئے"** — the Mujaddidiya spread through his blessed
  person throughout the whole age, and **his khalifas from all the far countries went to Mecca and
  Medina** and engaged in guiding people. "خوارق و کرامت کے اظہار میں حضرت کو کمال انکار تھا" — he utterly
  refused to display marvels, though they often occurred involuntarily. Died in the year 1142 AH[OCR?].
  The editor renames him and moves the year sixteen years: see Arguments.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Abid Naqshbandi Mujaddidi** (entry no. 92, pp. 142–143, folios 130–131): khalifa,
  janishin and **qaim-maqam** of Abd al-Ahad; **paternal nisbat to Abu Bakr Siddiq**; so devoted to
  worship that "سوائے عبادت کے کسی کام کی طرف رغبت نہ تھی", he inclined to nothing but worship. His daily
  office is under Practices. **"بکمال اشتیاق پا پیادہ ہند سے روانہ ہو کر حرمین الشریفین میں پہنچے"** —
  with utmost longing he set out **on foot** from Hind and reached the Haramain Sharifain for the Hajj and
  the ziyarat. Died 18 Ramadan in the year 1160 AH "القدس [OCR?]". The editor names him **Shaikh Muhammad
  Abid Hanafi Naqshbandi Sunami**, records that **Mirza Mazhar Jan-e Janan took inward benefit from him**,
  and reports a rival death year (p. 142, folio 130, حاشیہ 2).
- **Shah Shams al-Din Habib Allah, addressed as Mirza Jan-e Janan** [Mirza Mazhar Jan-e Janan]
  (entry no. 93, pp. 143–144, folios 131–132): of the **Alavi sayyids**, with a genealogy to Imam
  Muhammad bin Hanif bin Ali al-Murtaza through twenty-eight intermediaries. **"ان کے شاہان دہلی کے
  دربار میں امیر کبیر تھے۔ قرابت بھی سلاطین تیموریہ کے ساتھ رکھتے تھے"** — his forebears were great amirs
  at the court of the kings of Delhi and had **kinship with the Timurid sultans**: "امیر عبدالسبحان
  نواسہ اکبر بادشاہ کا ان کا دادا تھا", Amir Abd al-Subhan, **grandson of Emperor Akbar**, was his
  grandfather — which the editor denies: see Arguments. His father **Mirza Jan Turk**, also of the amir
  al-umara, "تارک الدنیا ہوا۔ اس نے ظاہری دولت ترک کر کے فقیری اختیار کی", renounced the world and his
  outward wealth for faqiri, and became a murid of **Shah Abd al-Rahman Qadiri**. Orphaned at sixteen,
  Mirza Jan-e Janan sought the Naqshbandiya Mujaddidiya, became a murid of **Sayyid Nur Muhammad
  Badayuni**, then took faiz from **Haji Muhammad Afzal** (d. 1146 AH, murid of Abd al-Ahad Wahdat),
  **Hafiz Sa'd Allah** (d. 11 Shawwal 1152 AH, murid of Muhammad Siddiq bin Muhammad Masum) and **Khwaja
  Muhammad Abid**, and sat on the masnad. **His assassination is under Practices.** Born the eleventh of
  Ramadan, a Friday, in the year 1111 AH; died 9 Muharram 1195 AH, **aged eighty-four**. The editor adds
  that he was "ایک بلند پایہ ادیب اور شاعر", a high-ranking man of letters and poet, whose diwan and a
  bayaz have been printed as ***Kharita-e Jawahir***, with an eleven-item bibliography including a
  manuscript in the **British Museum** and Khaliq Anjum's doctoral thesis (pp. 144–145, folios 132–133).
- **Shah Abdullah, addressed as Sayyid Ghulam Ali Shah Naqshbandi Mujaddidi Dehlvi** (entry no. 94,
  pp. 145–147, folios 133–135): true khalifa and janishin of Mirza Jan-e Janan Shahid; ancestral nisbat
  to Ali ibn Abi Talib. His father **Sayyid Abd al-Latif** lived at qasba Rotala[OCR?] and was a murid of
  **Shah Nasir al-Din Qadiri**; the son crossed to the Naqshbandiya Mujaddidiya under Mirza Jan-e Janan,
  "تمام عمر دہلی میں رہ کر بہدایت خلق مصروف رہے", lived his whole life in **Delhi**, and "وہ فیض جاری کیا
  کہ گویا اخیر زمانہ میں ہند کی ولایت ان کی ذات پر ختم ہوئی" — set flowing such faiz that, as it were,
  **the wilayat of Hind in the last age ended in his person**. Died 22 Safar in the year 1240 AH, with a
  chronogram carrying ۱۲۴۰ھ. Three karamat are under Legends. The editor's assessment is under Arguments,
  and his eight works are named: *Rasail-e Sab'a Sayyara*, the *Risala* on the Mujaddid, *Risala
  Mashghuliya*, *Risala Tariq-e Mujaddidiya*, *Durr al-Maarif*, *Jawahir-e Alawiya*, a *Majmua-e
  Malfuzat* and *Makatib* (p. 145, folio 133, حاشیہ 1).
- **Maulana Khalid Kurdi** (editor's footnote, p. 145, folio 133): Shah Ghulam Ali's khalifa, who "عرب
  [اور ترکستان] میں … سلسلہ ارشاد و تلقین شروع کر کے سارے عرب کو متاثر کیا", began a chain of guidance in
  **Arabia** — and, in a marginal addition, **Turkestan** — and affected the whole of Arabia.
- **Shah Abu Said Mujaddidi Dehlvi** (entry no. 95, opening only, p. 147, folio 135): khalifa and
  janishin of Shah Ghulam Ali, and a direct Mujaddidi descendant — "صفی القدر بن عزیز القدر بن محمد
  عیسیٰ بن محمد سیف الدین بن خواجہ محمد معصوم بن شیخ احمد مجدد الف ثانیؒ" — unrivalled in fiqh, hadith
  and tafsir, who took his outward learning from **Mufti Sharaf al-Din Dehlvi** and Maulana Rafi al-Din
  before the entry breaks off. The editor: **"حضرت شاہ ابو سعید مجددیؒ کی ذات گرامی سے نہ صرف ہندوستان
  بلکہ عرب اور ترکی میں بھی سلسلہ مجددیہ کی ترویج و اشاعت ہوئی۔ مجددی سلسلہ کی آخری فعال خانقاہوں میں
  سے آپ کی خانقاہ نے بہت سے نمایاں کام سرانجام دیے"** — through him the Mujaddidiya was propagated in
  Hindustan, **Arabia and Turkey**, and **his khanqah, among the last active khanqahs of the Mujaddidi
  silsila**, accomplished much. Three ancestors are dated: Safi al-Qadr d. 1236 AH, **buried at
  Lucknow**; Muhammad Aziz al-Qadr d. 1178 AH[OCR?]; Muhammad Isa d. 1153 AH, a scholar and poet.
- **Mufti Sharaf al-Din Rampuri Hanafi** (editor's footnote, p. 147, folio 135): among the famous
  teachers of Hindustan; **"پنجاب سے رام پور گئے تھے"**, he had gone **from the Punjab to Rampur**; great
  ulama were his students; *Siraj al-Mizan*, *Sharh al-Sullam*, *Fatawa-e Fiqh* and many risalas are
  his; died 1260 AH.
- **Pakistani places and repositories named**: **Wazirabad (Sarai Wazir Khan), district Gujrat**;
  **Peshawar** — the Bhana Mari library and Islamia College Library; **Karachi** — Maulana Yusuf Banuri's
  library and Dr. Ghulam Mustafa Khan's editions; **Rawalpindi** — the **Ganj Bakhsh library** holding
  Khwaja Dost Muhammad Qandhari's bayaz, and a manuscript owned by **Muhtarama Padshah Begum**;
  **Lahore** — the Punjab University library and Haji Muin al-Din's collection; **Mozang**, outside
  Lahore (pp. 139–146).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Four hundred dervishes fed twice a day, to order.** Of Saif al-Din's khanqah: **"ان کی خانقاہ میں ہر
  روز چار سو آدمی درویش دو وقتہ کھانا کھاتے تھے اور کھانا ہر ایک شخص کے کہنے کے بموجب تیار ہوتا تھا"** —
  every day four hundred dervishes ate two meals in his khanqah, **and the food was prepared according
  to each person's request** (p. 138, folio 126). The most precise langar in these chunks: a headcount,
  a frequency, and a kitchen that cooked to individual order rather than serving one pot.
- **A father refuses the court; the son is sent to live inside it.** Chunk 012's Muhammad Masum "کبھی اس
  کے دربار میں نہ گئے اور نہ زر نقد و جاگیر … کی طمع کی", never entered Aurangzeb's court nor coveted cash
  or a jagir (p. 135). Here: **"آپ کو حضرت خواجہ محمد معصومؒ نے اورنگ زیب کی درخواست پر شاہی دربار میں
  بھیج دیا۔ جہاں آپ بقول محمد ساقی مستعد خاں قلعہ کے اندر شاہی محل کے جوار میں رہنے لگے۔ بادشاہ اکثر
  کاروبار سلطنت سے فراغت کے بعد رات گئے۔ آپ کی خدمت میں حاضر ہو کر صحبت سے فیض یاب ہوتا"** — Muhammad
  Masum **sent** Saif al-Din to the royal court **at Aurangzeb's request**, where, on Muhammad Saqi
  Mustaid Khan's authority, he came to live **inside the fort, next to the royal palace**, and the emperor
  would often come late at night, after the business of the empire, to sit with him (*Maasir-e Alamgiri*
  44). And the correspondence survives: "آپ کے مکتوبات طبع ہو چکے ہیں۔ **ان میں اورنگ زیب کے نام آپ کے
  ۱۸ مکتوبات ہیں**", his maktubat are printed, and **eighteen of them are addressed to Aurangzeb**
  (*Maktubat-e Saifiya*, ed. Dr. Ghulam Mustafa Khan, Karachi) (p. 138, folio 126, حاشیہ 1). **One
  generation, two opposite policies toward the same emperor, both documented.**
- **A saint assassinated by doctrinal opponents, by firearm.** Of Mirza Jan-e Janan: "بتاریخ نہم ماہ
  محرم الحرام سن ایک ہزار ایک سو پچانوے ۱۱۹۵ھ **چند معاندان خاندان نقشبندیہ سے کہ عقیدہ نجدیہ [OCR?]
  رکھتے تھے بضرب [OCR?] شہادت بگلولہ [OCR?] تفنگ پائی**" — on 9 Muharram 1195 AH he attained martyrdom
  by the shot of a **musket ball** from some enemies of the Naqshbandi family **who held the
  Najdiya[OCR?] creed** (p. 144, folio 132). The killing is dated to a day, the weapon named, and the
  motive given as doctrine. Note all three of the load-bearing words are OCR-flagged.
- **A quantified daily office, the largest in these chunks.** Of Muhammad Abid: **"ہر روز بیس ہزار مرتبہ
  کلمہ طیبہ اور ہزار مرتبہ ذکر نفی اثبات بحبس دم و تلاوت یک منزل قرآن و ہزار بار درود شریف وظیفہ تھا
  اور تہجد کی نماز میں ساتھ [OCR?] مرتبہ سورۃ یٰسین پڑھے جاتے تھے"** — his daily *wazifa* was twenty
  thousand kalima tayyiba, a thousand of the negation-and-affirmation zikr **with the breath held**, one
  *manzil* of the Quran, and a thousand salutations on the Prophet; and in the Tahajjud prayer, sixty[OCR?]
  Sura Yasins (p. 143, folio 131). Read with Baqi Billah's two Quran recitations and twenty-one Yasins in
  chunk 012 (p. 128), this is the book's second numbered devotional regimen — and both are Naqshbandi.
- **A shaykh's fatwa as uncontested jurisdiction.** Of Muin al-Din in Kashmir: no one demurred at any
  fatwa he gave in any case, and even the ulama obeyed him (p. 138, folio 126) — set against chunk 010's
  Lahore ulama petitioning for a shaykh's execution.
- **Pilgrimage on foot from India to the Hijaz** (p. 143, folio 131), and khalifas dispatched to Mecca
  and Medina "تمام دور دراز ملکوں سے" (p. 141, folio 129). The Mujaddidi chapter's institutional
  geography runs to the Hijaz, Arabia, Turkey and Turkestan, where the Chishti chapter's ran to Lahore
  and Taunsa.
- **Renunciation of office as a family act, twice.** Mirza Jan Turk, an amir, "ظاہری دولت ترک کر کے
  فقیری اختیار کی" (p. 144, folio 132); and, in the editor's version, "اورنگ زیب دکن میں تھا تو انھوں نے
  ملازمت ترک کر کے سارا اثاثہ غریبوں میں تقسیم کر دیا تھا" — when Aurangzeb was in the Deccan he resigned
  his service and **distributed all his assets among the poor** (p. 144, folio 132, حاشیہ 2).
- **Succession vocabulary, again varied**: **janishin** (Saif al-Din, Abd al-Ahad, Ghulam Ali), **masnad
  of hidayat and irshad** (Abd al-Ahad, Mirza Jan-e Janan), **qaim-maqam** (Muhammad Abid) (pp. 138–145).
  All within one lineage, all in one chapter.
- **A khanqah described as one of the last active ones.** The editor on Shah Abu Said's: "مجددی سلسلہ کی
  آخری فعال خانقاہوں میں سے" (p. 147, folio 135) — a judgement about institutional decline, made in 1974
  about the early nineteenth century.
- **Print and manuscript economy**: *Maktubat-e Saifiya* and *Gulshan-e Wahdat* edited from **Karachi**
  (1966 and undated); *Rasail-e Sab'a Sayyara* from the **Matba Naqshbandi, 1282 AH** — a press named for
  the order; *Makatib-e Mirza Mazhar* and *Mirza Mazhar aur unka Kalam* from **Bombay 1960**;
  *al-Bahjat al-Saniya fi Adab al-Tariqat al-Khalidiya* from **Egypt, 1319 AH**; *Mirza Mazhar ke Khutut*,
  Delhi 1962 (pp. 138–146). The Khalidiya title is evidence in itself of the Arabian branch the editor
  claims for Ghulam Ali.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A Shia pair who asked for bai'at as a test.** Two men "جن کا مذہب شیعہ تھا" came to Nur Muhammad
  Badayuni and "امتحاناً درخواست بیعت کی", requested bai'at by way of a test; he, knowing their state "بنور
  باطنی", by the inward light, said "اول عقیدہ باطلہ شیعہ سے تائب ہو کر اپنے قلب کو صاف کرو۔ بعد ازاں
  بیعت کرنے کا اختیار ہے" — first repent of the false Shia belief and clean your heart, then you have
  leave to take bai'at. One repented sincerely and took bai'at; "دوسرے کو توفیق رفیق نہ ہوئی", the other
  was not granted the grace (p. 141, folio 129). The second sectarian episode in two chunks, after the
  Mujaddid's *Maktubat* in chunk 012.
- **A woman killed by a slap that was never struck.** Abd al-Ahad bore an abusive woman's words in
  silence, then, seeing that "غیرت الٰہی درپے انتقام ہے", the divine jealousy was bent on vengeance,
  gestured to a servant to slap her; the servant only rebuked her and hesitated, and "عورت اسی وقت گر
  پڑی اور مر گئی", she fell and died at that moment. His rebuke to the servant is the point of the story:
  **"اگر تو طمانچے لگانے میں دیر نہ کرتا تو اس عورت کی جان برباد نہ جاتی … اب منتقم حقیقی نے یہ انتقام
  لیا اور جان اس کی جاتی رہی۔ خون اس عورت کا تیری گردن پر ہے"** — had you not delayed, her life would not
  have been destroyed, for the vengeance would have come from me; now the True Avenger has taken it and
  her life is gone; **this woman's blood is on your neck** (pp. 141–142, folios 129–130). The woman's
  grievance is stated plainly: her son had taken bai'at and become a world-renouncing majzub.
- **Kebab that turned to halwa, read as a death sentence.** From the appendix to *Maqamat-e Mazhari*: a
  woman asked Shah Ghulam Ali for a sick man's recovery; he was eating bread and kebab and gave her a
  loaf and a little kebab "بطور تبرک", as *tabarruk*; at home the kebab had become halwa, "جانا کہ بیمار
  جان بر نہیں ہو گا", and it was understood that the sick man would not survive (p. 146, folio 134). A
  blessing-food that carries an answer rather than a cure.
- **A son promised to a woman of sixty.** From *Durr al-Maarif*: a murid's daughter died; Shah Ghulam Ali
  consoled the wife and said God would give her a son in the daughter's place; she answered "مجھ کو ساٹھ
  سال کی عمر میں اب لڑکا ہونا ناممکن ہے", it is impossible at sixty; he said "خداوند تعالیٰ قادر ہے"; and
  after a few days she conceived and bore a son (p. 146, folio 134).
- **A caravan plundered and one cart spared.** From the same book: a murid travelling from Delhi by cart
  saw the Hazrat coming toward him on the road, telling him "گاڑی کو دوڑا کر قافلے سے الگ ہو جاؤ", drive
  fast and separate from the caravan; a mile on, "تمام قافلہ کو رہزنوں نے لوٹ لیا اور وہ ان کی وجہ سے
  سلامت رہے", the whole caravan was plundered by highwaymen and he alone was safe (p. 147, folio 135).
- **A three-day blockage for a foot in the wrong order**, told by the editor as evidence of Nur Muhammad
  Badayuni's scrupulousness rather than as a marvel (p. 140, folio 128, حاشیہ 3).
- Of Saif al-Din, that whoever came before him repented (p. 138, folio 126); of Nur Muhammad Badayuni and
  Shah Ghulam Ali, that their marvels were "بے انتہا" and "بیشمار", without end (pp. 141, 145; folios
  129, 133).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Four death years corrected, each against the author by name.**
  - Saif al-Din: **"مفتی صاحب نے خواجہ سیف الدین کا سال وفات ۱۰۹۸ھ لکھا ہے۔ جو درست نہیں۔ صحیح سال وفات
    ۲۰ جمادی الاخری ۱۰۹۶ھ ہے"** — the Mufti wrote 1098 AH, which is not correct; the correct date is
    **20 Jumada al-Akhir 1096 AH** (*Jawahir-e Alawiya* p. 126), with his birth at Sirhind in 1049 AH
    (ibid p. 122) (p. 138, folio 126, حاشیہ 2). Note the correction adds a day and a month the text
    lacked.
  - Abd al-Ahad: **"حضرت شیخ عبدالاحد متخلص بہ وحدت معروف بہ شاہ گل کا صحیح سال وفات ۱۱۲۶ھ ہے … مفتی
    صاحب نے ۱۱۴۲ھ غلط لکھا ہے"** — the correct year is **1126 AH** (Muhammad Azam, *Tarikh-e Kashmir
    Azami*); the Mufti's 1142 AH is wrong (p. 142, folio 130, حاشیہ 1). Sixteen years, and the numeral in
    the text is itself OCR-flagged.
  - Muhammad Abid: "مفتی صاحب نے شیخ محمد عابدؒ کا سال وفات ۱۱۶۰ھ لکھا ہے۔ لیکن فقیر محمد جہلمی نے
    ۱۱۶۶ھ" — the Mufti wrote 1160 AH, but Faqir Muhammad Jhelumi 1166 AH (p. 142, folio 130, حاشیہ 2).
    The sentence has no verb and no verdict; the reader is sent to "ضمیمہ ثانی کتاب ہذا صفحہ ۲۹۷", the
    book's second appendix, p. 297.
  - Mirza Jan-e Janan's **birth**: Dr. Khaliq Anjum "نے اپنے پی ایچ ڈی کے مقالہ 'مرزا مظہر جان جاناں' میں
    مختلف شواہد کی بنا پر آپ کا سال ولادت ۱۱۱۰ھ متعین کیا ہے" — determined it as **1110 AH** on various
    evidence, against the text's 1111 AH (p. 144, folio 132, حاشیہ 5). A modern doctoral thesis admitted
    as an authority against a nineteenth-century tazkira.
- **A burial place corrected outright — the only one in these chunks.** Of Nur Muhammad Badayuni:
  **"مفتی صاحب نے لکھا ہے کہ آپ کا مزار سرہند میں ہے۔ یہ درست نہیں۔ آپ کا مزار دہلی میں جوار حضرت نظام
  الدین اولیاؑ باغ مکرم خاں میں ہے (جواہر علویہ ص ۱۳۰)"** — the Mufti wrote that his mazar is at Sirhind.
  This is not correct. **His mazar is in Delhi, beside Hazrat Nizam al-Din Auliya, in Bagh Mukarram
  Khan** (p. 141, folio 129, continued footnote). Elsewhere the editor corrects dates, names and titles;
  here he moves a grave between cities, which is the kind of correction this archive most needs.
- **A claim of imperial descent refused for want of proof.** Against the text's "امیر عبدالسبحان نواسہ
  اکبر بادشاہ کا ان کا دادا تھا": **"امیر عبدالسبحان کے والد مرزا محمد امان کے بارے میں مولانا محمد نعیم
  بہرائچی نے لکھا ہے کہ ان کی شادی اکبر کی لڑکی سے ہوئی تھی۔ لیکن اس کا کوئی تاریخی ثبوت نہیں ہے۔ مرزا
  عبدالسبحان کی شادی اسد خاں وزیر کی لڑکی یا خالہ زاد بہن سے ہوئی تھی"** — Naim Bahraichi wrote that Mirza
  Muhammad Aman married Akbar's daughter, **but there is no historical proof of this**; Abd al-Subhan's
  own marriage was to the daughter, or maternal cousin, of Asad Khan the wazir (p. 144, folio 132,
  حاشیہ 1). **The third time in four chunks that the editor dismantles a royal-marriage claim with the
  same phrase** — after Sultan Shams al-Din's daughter in chunk 010 (p. 112) — and he offers a
  substitute, unroyal marriage rather than a blank.
- **A genealogical count halved.** Of Mirza Jan-e Janan's twenty-eight intermediaries to Imam Muhammad
  bin Hanif: **"اٹھائیس واسطے غلط ہیں۔ انیس واسطے ہونے چاہئیں"** — twenty-eight is wrong; it should be
  **nineteen** (Naim Allah Bahraichi, *Mamulat-e Mazhariya* p. 11) (p. 143, folio 131, حاشیہ 1). Note
  that the Mujaddid's own pedigree in chunk 012 was also given as twenty-eight links (p. 129) and sent to
  the same appendix, p. 297.
- **A nisba emended by conjecture, and the conjecture flagged as such.** Of Shaikh Sa'di: "آپ کا نام محمد
  صادق تھا … **مگر آپ کے نام کے ساتھ مفتی صاحب نے جو بلخاری کی نسبت لکھی ہے۔ ہماری سمجھ میں نہیں آسکی۔
  ہمارے خیال میں یہ نسبت بلغاری ہے۔ افغانستان میں اس نسبت کے بعض بزرگ گزرے ہیں ممکن ہے آپ کے آباؤ اجداد
  کا تعلق بھی اسی قصبہ بلغار سے ہو**" — his name was Muhammad Sadiq; but the nisba "Bulkhari" the Mufti
  attached to him we could not understand; **in our view the nisba is "Bulghari"**; some elders of that
  nisba have lived in Afghanistan, and it is **possible** that his forebears belonged to that qasba
  Bulghar (p. 139, folio 127, حاشیہ 1). Marked twice as opinion — "ہمارے خیال میں", "ممکن ہے" — which is
  how the editor signals a guess as against his flat corrections of dates.
- **Two book titles put right.** The vaguely cited "کتاب مظہر جان جاناں" is identified as "مقامات مظہری
  … کے آخر میں ملحقہ ضمیمہ در حالات شاہ غلام علیؒ نوشتہ شاہ عبدالغنی مجددی", the appendix on Shah Ghulam
  Ali written by Shah Abd al-Ghani Mujaddidi at the end of *Maqamat-e Mazhari* (p. 146, folio 134,
  حاشیہ 1); and "دار المعارف" is corrected: **"اس کتاب کا صحیح نام درّ المعارف ہے"**, the correct name is
  *Durr al-Maarif*, a collection of Shah Ghulam Ali's malfuzat compiled by his chief khalifa Shah Rauf
  Ahmad Rafat Mujaddidi, d. 1253 AH, printed several times (p. 146, folio 134, حاشیہ 2). With chunk 011's
  *Anhar al-Asrar* and chunk 010's *Fawaid al-Asrar*, this is the third chunk in which the editor's
  bibliographic corrections outnumber his factual ones.
- **He again reports what he cannot find, and counts what he can.** *Rauzat al-Salam*, the source for
  Shaikh Sa'di's marvels, is by Shaikh Sharaf al-Din Kashmiri, d. 1205 AH, and "ہمیں اس وقت تک اس کے
  کسی خطی نسخے کا علم نہیں ہے", we know of no manuscript of it to date (p. 140, folio 128, حاشیہ 2). Of
  Abd al-Ahad's writings: "تقریباً تیس کے حوالے ملتے ہیں", references to about **thirty** are found
  (p. 142, folio 130, حاشیہ 1).
- **His two historical theses on the order's global reach.** Of Shah Ghulam Ali: **"تیرھویں صدی ہجری کے
  سب سے نامور شیخ طریقت تھے۔ آپ کی بدولت سلسلہ مجددیہ نہ صرف ہندوستان میں پھیلا۔ بلکہ سارے عالم اسلام
  میں اس کی تعلیمات پہنچ گئیں"** — the most renowned shaykh of tariqat of the thirteenth Hijri century,
  through whom the Mujaddidiya reached not only Hindustan but the whole Muslim world, by way of Maulana
  Khalid Kurdi in Arabia and Turkestan (p. 145, folio 133, حاشیہ 1). And of Nur Muhammad Badayuni's
  pupil Mirza Mazhar, "جن سے سارے عالم اسلام میں مجددی سلوک کا عام رواج ہوا" (p. 140, folio 128).
- **Controversy again documented rather than suppressed.** Abd al-Ahad's *Jannat al-Thamaniya*,
  *Shawahid al-Tajdid* and the *Sharh-e Kalimat-e Qudsi* were written "حضرت مجددؒ کی حمایت اور مجددی
  تحریک کے مخالفین کے رد میں", in the Mujaddid's defence and in refutation of the movement's opponents
  (p. 142, folio 130, حاشیہ 1) — matching the two manuscript refutations listed in chunk 012.

## Doubts

- **No archive row is the subject of, or takes material from, any entry in this chunk.** Of the nine
  saints, five are buried at Sirhind or Delhi, one at Medina and one at Lucknow. The only Pakistani
  graves named are **Shaikh Sa'di Lahori's, "بیرون لاہور موضع مزنگ کے پاس"** (p. 140, folio 128), and,
  by implication, Haji Sa'd Allah Wazirabadi's at **Wazirabad, district Gujrat** (p. 139, folio 127) —
  neither of which matches a row.
- **Mozang is an archive location but not for this saint.** The row **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri** sits
  at "Mozang Chungi, Queens Road, Lahore" with Shah Inayat Qadiri, d. c. 1728, Qadri Shattari. Shaikh
  Sa'di (Muhammad Sadiq) is Naqshbandi-Mujaddidi, d. 1108 AH ≈ 1696–97 CE, buried near mauza Mozang. Same
  quarter, different man, different order. **Do not map.** Also do not map Haji Sa'd Allah Wazirabadi to
  **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai** on the strength of the shared district of Gujrat.
- **The editor gives Shaikh Adam Banuri two death years, in two footnotes of the same entry.** Footnote 3
  says "ف ۱۰۵۴ھ" (p. 139, folio 127); footnote 1 on the next page, attached to his death at Medina, says
  "بسال ۱۰۵۲ھ (بدرالدین سرہندی : حضرات القدس دفتر دوم ص ۳۵۶)" (p. 140, folio 128). Two years apart,
  unremarked, both from the editor's own hand.
- **Shaikh Sa'di Lahori's nisba is unresolved by the editor's own admission**, and his name in the text
  ("سعدی بلخاری") differs from the name the editor gives him ("محمد صادق") (p. 139, folio 127). Cite
  the nisba as "Bulkhari (text) / Bulghari (editor's conjecture)" or not at all; the source for the real
  name is itself doubly flagged: "محمد عمر چکنی میاں [OCR?] : ظاہر [OCR?] ورق ۱۰۸".
- **Mirza Jan-e Janan's assassination rests on three OCR-flagged words.** "عقیدہ نجدیہ [OCR?]" (the
  killers' creed), "بضرب [OCR?]" (the blow) and "بگلولہ [OCR?] تفنگ" (the musket ball) (p. 144,
  folio 132). The date, 9 Muharram 1195 AH, and the fact of martyrdom are unflagged; **the identity of
  the killers and the means are not.** Do not attribute the killing to a named movement from this page.
- **His age and the editor's birth year cannot both stand.** Text: born 11 Ramadan 1111, died 9 Muharram
  1195, "چوراسی برس کی عمر پائی", aged eighty-four — which the text's own figures support. The editor
  prefers 1110 AH for the birth (p. 144, folio 132, حاشیہ 5), which gives eighty-five. One or the other;
  the thesis reference is itself OCR-flagged ("ص ۱۲ [OCR?]").
- **Abd al-Ahad's death year in the text is OCR-flagged as well as wrong by the editor's account**:
  "سال ایک ہزار ۱۱۴۲ھ [OCR?] ایک سو بیالیس ہجری" (pp. 141–142, folios 129–130), against the editor's
  1126 AH. Both the numeral and the words read 1142, so the flag is probably harmless — but the
  divergence from 1126 is not.
- **Muhammad Abid's death has no adjudicated value.** Text 18 Ramadan 1160; Faqir Muhammad Jhelumi
  1166 AH; the footnote breaks off without a verdict and points to p. 297 (p. 142, folio 130). The year
  in the text also carries an unexplained trailing word, "ہجری القدس [OCR?]" (p. 143, folio 131).
- **"ساتھ [OCR?] مرتبہ سورۃ یٰسین"** in Muhammad Abid's office (p. 143, folio 131) is almost certainly
  ساٹھ, sixty, but the numeral is flagged and the figure is load-bearing for the one quantified regimen
  in the entry. Carry it as "sixty[OCR?]".
- **Saif al-Din's khanqah is not located.** The four-hundred-dervish kitchen (p. 138, folio 126) is not
  tied to a place; he is buried at Sirhind, but the editor puts him inside the Delhi fort for part of
  his life. The kitchen could belong to either.
- **A father-and-son date problem in Shah Abu Said's pedigree.** The editor gives Muhammad Aziz al-Qadr
  d. 1178 AH[OCR?] and his son Safi al-Qadr d. 1236 AH, with Muhammad Isa (the father of Aziz al-Qadr)
  d. 1153 AH (p. 147, folio 135). Twenty-five years between Isa and Aziz al-Qadr and fifty-eight between
  Aziz al-Qadr and Safi al-Qadr — possible, but the middle figure is OCR-flagged and is the one that
  makes the sequence tight.
- **Shah Ghulam Ali's father's home is unreadable**: "قصبہ روٹالہ [OCR?]" (p. 145, folio 133). The place
  cannot be identified from this chunk.

  "محمد عمر چکنی میاں [OCR?]", "ظاہر [OCR?]", "تاریخ الحربین [OCR?]" (p. 139, folio 127); "۲۰۰ - ۲۰۷
  [OCR?]", "محمد مراد ٹنگ [OCR?]" (p. 142, folio 130); "۱۱۴۲ھ [OCR?]" (p. 141, folio 129); "القدس [OCR?]"
  and "ساتھ [OCR?]" in Muhammad Abid's entry (p. 143, folio 131); "۱ / ۳۹۱ - ۳۹۲ [OCR?]", "۳ / ۲۰۹ - ۲۱۶
  [OCR?]" in the *Maasir al-Umara* references (p. 143, folio 131); "عقیدہ نجدیہ [OCR?]", "بضرب [OCR?]",
  "بگلولہ [OCR?]", "ص ۱۲ [OCR?]" (p. 144, folio 132); "روٹالہ [OCR?]" (p. 145, folio 133); "۱۱۷۸ھ [OCR?]"
  and "آباد العلوم [OCR?]" (p. 147, folio 135).
- **Death years given in words only**, as throughout: 1085 (Muin al-Din), 1098 (Saif al-Din), 1108
  (Shaikh Sa'di), 1135 (Nur Muhammad Badayuni), 1160 (Muhammad Abid) — while Abd al-Ahad (۱۱۴۲ھ [OCR?]),

- **One cross-reference cannot be checked from this chunk**: the second appendix at "صفحہ ۲۹۷", invoked
