### shrine-of-mian-mir — Shrine of Mian Mir

**What the row gains.** A full entry (no. 21, pp. 60–62, folios 48–50) and seven further
appearances: the book's given name Shaikh Muhammad Mir and the epithet **بالا پیر** the row does not
carry; both parents named; a rauza built by Dara Shikoh and stripped by Ranjit Singh; a named
khalifa (Mulla Shah) heading a Qadiri chain that runs to 1213 AH; a locality and a Lahore road
called after him; and a rival order's hostile anecdote about his line. Against that, the editor's
own table locates the khanqah at Miani Sahib, where the archive row says Alam Ganj / Dharampura.

**Saint**
- Entry no. 21, "شیخ محمد میر المشہور بہ میاں میر قادری لاہوری", listed in the contents at printed
  p. 48 (p. 15, folio 3) and printed at pp. 60–62, folios 48–50. The persons index gives eight
  printed pages: "میاں میر لاہوری | ۴۸ ، ۴۹ ، ۵۰ ، ۵۳ ، ۵۴ ، ۵۸ ، ۶۶ ، ۷۰" (p. 331, folio 319) →
  PDF pp. 60, 61, 62, 65, 66, 70, 78, 82. Principal treatment at folios 48–54.
- **Personal name.** The index asserts it by cross-reference: "میر محمد | دیکھئے میاں میر" — Mir
  Muhammad, see Mian Mir (p. 329, folio 317), matching the archive's "Mir Mohammad of Sind". A
  second, separate and uncross-referenced entry "میر محمد | ۱۲۱" stands two lines above the
  see-reference's target (p. 331, folio 319); whether it is the same man is not stated. The editor
  glosses the titles: he was called Mir Muhammad, with the titles Mian Mir and Shah Mir,
  "میاں مقامی زبان میں صاحب کے معنوں میں" — *Mian* meaning "sahib" in the local language, used for
  respect (Sakinat al-Auliya 25) (p. 61, folio 49, حاشیہ).
- **The epithet بالا پیر** is the book's usual form for him outside his own entry (pp. 69, 78, 222,
  242, 254–255, 288; also "میاں میر بالاپیر", p. 242). The bare "میاں میر" is also used (pp. 267,
  270). Neither form is in the archive row.
- **Do not merge the two بالا پیرs.** Entry no. 199 names "سید محمد بالا پیر گیلانی", a grandfather
  of Miran Muhammad Shah's wife, and entry no. 200 names "میاں میر بالا پیر لاہوری" (both p. 254,
  folio 242). These are different men in the book's own telling; chunk 023's "میاں میر بالاپیر" is
  the second.
- **Parentage and birthplace**: father Qazi Sain Ditta b. Qazi Qalandar Faruqi, mother Bibi Fatima
  bint Qazi Qadan; "سیوستان میں آپ پیدا ہوئے", he was born at Sewistan; his father died when he was
  seven (p. 60, folio 48). The mother is indexed as
  "فاطمہ بی بی (والدہ میاں میر) | ۴۸" (p. 325, folio 313); note three other Bibi Fatimas stand in
  the same ف run (p. 325).
- **Birth year — author and editor disagree.** The author, on the authority of the author of
  *Safinat al-Auliya*, gives birth "سال نوسو ساٹھ" (960), death 1045 AH, age eighty-seven, arrival
  at Lahore at twenty-five and sixty years' residence (pp. 61–62, folios 49–50, chunk 005). The
  editor dates the birth to **957 AH / 1550 CE** on Dara Shikoh's authority (Sakinat al-Auliya 95)
  (p. 61, folio 49, حاشیہ, chunk 005). The death year 1045 AH is not contradicted anywhere in this
  group's material.
- **Initiation from his own mother.** At twelve he finished his religious education and
  "اپنی والدہ ماجدہ کی خدمت میں بسلسلہ عالیہ قادریہ بیعت کر کے" took bai'at in the Qadiri silsila at
  his mother's hand, and only later, **with her permission**, went to Shaikh Khizr Sistani for
  completion; after the khirqa of khilafat his pir directed him to Lahore, where he settled (p. 61,
  folio 49). He is the khalifa of Shaikh Khizr Sistani, with the full chain of murids given back
  through Qutb al-Afaq Sayyid Abdul Razzaq to his father Abdul Qadir Gilani (p. 60, folio 48).
- **Education at Lahore**: the current sciences with Maulana Saad Allah Lahori and Akhund Nemat
  Allah (p. 61, folio 49, حاشیہ) — the same Saad Allah the editor names as Shah Husain's teacher
  (p. 56, folio 44, chunk 004).
- **Austerity**: he never slept at night; his حبس دم [OCR?] was such that a whole night passed in a
  single breath; he broke his fast only after a week; and sometimes in absorption a month at a time
  passed without his eating (p. 61, folio 49).
- **Siblings**: four more brothers — Mian Qazi, Qazi Usman, Qazi Tahir, Qazi Muhammad — and two
  sisters, Bibi Badi and **Bibi Jamal Khatun**, the latter having her own entry in the chapter on
  righteous women (contents no. 200, printed p. 242) (p. 62, folio 50).
- **The sister's entry (no. 200, pp. 254–255, folios 242–243)** is the richest family material:
  "بی بی جمال خاتون … ترک و تجرید میں رابعہ وقت۔ نسبت عالیہ سلسلہ قادریہ اس کو اپنے برادر حقیقی
  میاں میر بالا پیر لاہوری اور والدہ ماجدہ سے حاصل تھی" — the Qadiri nisbat reached her from her
  full brother **and from their mother** (p. 254, folio 242). "کتاب سکینۃ الاولیاء و سفینۃ الاولیاء
  میں حضرت دارا شکوہ نے اس بی بی کا مفصل حال لکھا ہے" — Dara Shikoh wrote her life in detail in both
  books (p. 254, folio 242).
- **Her death and burial, contradicted by the editor, twice over.** Author: "وفات بی بی کی سال ایک
  ہزار ہجری میں ہوئی اور مدفن شریف شہر لاہور میں [illegible] خانقاہ کے اندر واقع ہے" — d. 1000 AH,
  buried inside a [illegible] khanqah in the city of Lahore (p. 255, folio 243, chunk 024). Editor:
  "مفتی صاحب کا مندرجہ سال وفات ۱۰۰۰ ھ درست نہیں ہے۔ بلکہ دارا شکوہ نے ۱۰۵۷ ھ تحریر کیا ہے",
  quoting "سُن شریف بی بی جمال خاتون از شصت متجاوز است و در سیوستان موطن خود اقامت دارند و ہرگز از
  آں جا بجائے دیگر نرفتہ اند ........ وفات ایشاں در روز سہ شنبہ بیست و ہفتم ماہ ربیع الاول سال ہزار
  و پنجاہ و ہفت واقع شدہ است (سکینۃ الاولیا ص ۱۳۱)" (p. 255, folio 243, حاشیہ 1, chunk 024). The
  quotation contradicts the **burial** as well as the year: a woman who never left Sehwan cannot be
  buried in a Lahore khanqah.

**Shrine / Darbar**
- "روضہ حضرت کا لاہور میں زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے" — his rauza in Lahore is a place of pilgrimage for
  God's creatures (p. 62, folio 50), followed by a Persian chronogram quatrain,
  "میر دنیا و دیں میاں میر ست…".
- **Built by Dara Shikoh, stripped by Ranjit Singh**: "ان کا روضہ دارا شکوہ نے کئی لاکھ روپے خرچ کر
  کے بنوایا تھا جس کے پتھر تمام و کمال رنجیت سنگھ اتروا کر امرت سر لے گیا" — Dara Shikoh had the
  rauza built at an expense of several lakh rupees, and Ranjit Singh had its stones entirely taken
  down and carried off to Amritsar (p. 69, folio 57); the editor sources the point to Kanhiya Lal's
  *Tarikh-e Lahore* 245 (p. 69, folio 57, حاشیہ 3). *Note the sentence's subject: it stands in Mulla
  Shah Badakhshi's entry (no. 29) and "ان کا روضہ" reads as Mulla Shah's; the chunk placed it under
  this row. A reader of the page must settle whose rauza was stripped before this enters the entry.*
- **A settlement inside a shrine precinct**: Mulla Shah's "مزار گوہر بار لاہور میں بیرون مزار میاں
  میر بالا پیر ہے", outside Mian Mir Bala Pir's mazar, and "اب ان کے مزار کے احاطہ میں موضع میاں میر
  آباد ہے" — the village **Mian Mir Abad** now stands within the enclosure of his mazar (p. 69,
  folio 57), recorded by the author as a present fact.
- **The appendix sites three graves by reference to his** (pp. 287–288, folios 275–276): row 9,
  "شیخ محمد میر المشہور بہ میاں میر قادری لاہوری | لاہور | میاں میر نزد [illegible] ریلوے سٹیشن";
  row 12, Khwaja Bihari "متصل روضہ میاں میر", present location "قصبہ میاں میر۔ بالمقابل مقبرہ حضرت
  میاں میر"; row 15, Mulla Shah Qadiri "بیرون مزار میاں میر بالا پیر", present location
  "اندرون موضع میاں میر". The locality itself is thus called **قصبہ / موضع میاں میر**. The word after
  "نزد" in row 9 is `[illegible]`.
- Khwaja Bihari's mazar is "متصل روضہ میاں میر رح کے", adjoining Mian Mir's rauza (p. 66, folio 54).
- **A walled khanqah enclosure containing another saint's grave, and a rival address.** The editor's
  table, row ۷۵: "بی بی جمال خاتون قدس سرہ | اندر احاطہ خانقاہ حضرت میاں میر | کراچی ریلوے لائن اور
  میانی صاحب گاؤں کے درمیان" — 1875 address inside the احاطہ of Mian Mir's khanqah; present location
  between the Karachi railway line and Miani Sahib village (p. 291, folio 279, chunk 028). **Set
  against the appendix's own rows** (chunk 027), which put the site at قصبہ میاں میر near a railway
  station (pp. 287–288); the two location columns do not obviously describe one place, and the
  archive row reads Alam Ganj / Dharampura. Three readings, none reconciled.
- **A Lahore road named for the shrine, in the author's present.** Sayyid Mahmud Huzuri's tomb lies
  "لاہور کے باہر میاں میر کی سڑک کے پاس", outside Lahore near Mian Mir's road (p. 44, folio 32); Pir
  Hadi Rahnuma's maqbara stands "لاہور کے باہر لب سڑک جو میاں میر کو انارکلی سے جاتی ہے", on the edge
  of the road that runs from Anarkali to Mian Mir (p. 267, folio 255); and the place index carries
  "سرک [OCR?] میاں میر | ۳۲" (p. 334, folio 322) → PDF p. 44, the first word flagged.

**Practices & Events**
- **An overnight stay and a dream-initiation at the shrine**, quoted by the author from Shah
  Muhammad Ghaus's own *Risala-e Ghausia*: "جب میں تلاش حق لاہور میں پہنچا۔ رات کو مقبرہ عالیہ میاں
  میر میں قیام پذیر ہوا۔ خواب میں میاں میر بالا پیر مجھ پر ظاہر ہوئے۔ اور بکمال توجہ ایک شغل عنایت
  کیا۔ صبح کے وقت وہاں سے اٹھ کر شیخ حامد لاہوری کی خدمت میں آیا اور کسی شغل کے واسطے ان سے بھی سوال
  کیا۔ وہ ہنسے اور فرمایا تجھ کو وہی شغل کافی ہے جو آج رات میاں میر بالا پیر سے عطا ہوا ہے" — "When I
  reached Lahore in search of the truth, I lodged for the night at the exalted maqbara of Mian Mir.
  In a dream Mian Mir Bala Pir appeared to me and with complete attention bestowed a *shughl* on me.
  In the morning I rose from there and came to Shaikh Hamid Lahori and asked him too for some
  shughl. He laughed and said: the very shughl bestowed on you last night by Mian Mir Bala Pir is
  enough for you." (p. 78, folio 66 [marker: 76]).
- **A quantified khanqah grain economy**: "حضرت کے گھر میں قریب دو من کے غلہ گندم تھا۔ ایک دن بی بی
  نے اس میں ہاتھ ڈالا تو یہ برکت ہوئی کہ ایک سال تک برابر روزمرہ غلہ اس سے خرچ فقیر و غربا و درویشان
  خانقاہ و غیرہ وابستگان [OCR?] کی خوراک کے لیے نکلتا رہا۔ گویا کہ وہ دو من غلہ کئی سو من بن گیا" —
  two man of wheat in the household feeding the poor, the darweshes of the khanqah and its
  dependants [OCR?] daily for a year (pp. 254–255, folios 242–243).
- **Named khalifa and transmission.** Mulla Shah Qadiri is his khalifa (p. 242, folio 230; appendix
  row 15, p. 288). Pir Burhan, who came from Bukhara in Akbar's time, "لاہور کے بزرگوں میاں میر و
  شاہ بلاول سے قادریہ فیض پایا" — took Qadiri fayz from the Lahore elders Mian Mir and Shah Bilawal
  (p. 270, folio 258): independent confirmation of the row's Qadiri affiliation from outside the
  saint's own entry. Shaikh Abdullah Shah Baloch's chain "چار واسطوں کے ساتھ حضرت میاں میر بالا پیر
  لاہوریؒ کے ساتھ ملتا تھا", joined him through four intermediaries (p. 82, folio 70 [marker: 80]).
- **A five-step Qadiri chain running down to 1213 AH** (entry no. 152, pp. 221–222, folios 209–210):
  "سلسلہ قادریہ میں ان کا شجرہ … حضرت میاں میر بالا پیر کے ساتھ اس طرح پر ملتا ہے کہ سید بھاون شاہ
  مرید شاہ بلاق [OCR?] اور وہ مرید شاہ عبد الرشید لاہوری اور وہ مرید شیخ محسن شاہ اور وہ مرید شیخ
  محمد المشہور ملّا شاہ اور وہ مرید حضرت میاں میر لاہوری کے" — Bhawan Shah ← Shah Bilaq[OCR?] ← Shah
  Abd al-Rashid Lahori ← Shaikh Muhsin Shah ← Shaikh Muhammad, famous as Mulla Shah ← Mian Mir
  Lahori (p. 222). **Shaikh Muhsin Shah here shares a name with the "محسن شاہ" of the Chishti Sabiri
  chain the editor rejected for Ghore Shah** (p. 179, chunk 017) — a placement doubt, not a fact.
- **A hostile anecdote from a rival order's source.** Hafiz Tahir "حصول علم باطن کے لیے حضرت ملا شاہ
  قادری میاں میر بالاپیر کے خلیفہ کے پاس چند سال حاضر رہا" — attended Mulla Shah Qadiri, Mian Mir
  Bala Pir's khalifa, for some years — "مگر وہاں اس کو کچھ بہرہ نہ ملا اور سخت بے اعتقاد ہو کر اسلام
  سے بھی برگشتہ ہوگیا۔ رشتہ زنار گردن میں ڈال لیا اور فقرائے ہنود میں مل کر جا بجا گدائی کرنے لگا" —
  he got no benefit there, became faithless, apostatised, put on the zunnar and begged among Hindu
  faqirs, until Naushah restored him (p. 242, folio 230). The author's stated source is
  "کتاب تذکرہ نوشاہی" (p. 242) and he neither endorses nor softens it (chunk 023): to be cited as
  that tradition's view of the Mian Mir line, not as the book's judgement. Hafiz Tahir's own death:
  author "سال ایک ہزار ایک سو چھتیس" = 1136; editor 1097 AH[OCR?] (p. 243, folio 231).

**Legends & Miracles**
- "حضرت کے خوارق و کرامت کے حال میں شہزادہ دارا شکوہ نے ایک کتاب سکینۃ الاولیا نام لکھی ہے" — Prince
  Dara Shikoh wrote a whole book, *Sakinat al-Auliya*, on his karamat (p. 61, folio 49).
- **His own ruling against displaying karamat, inside a book of karamat** — the most citable line in
  this material. When news of his sister's grain miracle reached him: "آخر یہ خبر میاں میر بالا پیر
  کو ہوئی تو فرمایا کہ اظہار کرامت منع ہے اور خدا روزی دہندہ ہے۔ کیا ضرور ہے کہ اس طرح پر غلہ حاصل
  کیا جائے" — the display of karamat is forbidden, and God is the giver of sustenance; what need is
  there to obtain grain in this way? (p. 255, folio 243).

**Significance**
- Sources the book lists for him (p. 61, folio 49): *Safinat al-Auliya* 69–73; *Sakinat al-Auliya*
  (Tehran 1965); Abdul Hamid Lahori's *Badshah Nama* juz 1 at six separate pages; Kanbo's
  *Amal-e Salih* 3/357–360; Qani Thathawi's *Tuhfat al-Kiram*; Jahangir's *Tuzuk*.
- For the archive, the chain at p. 222 establishes that **the book treats Mian Mir as the root of a
  still-living Lahore Qadiri lineage**, not merely as a seventeenth-century grave; "میاں میر لاہوری"
  is a nisba of city, not of quarter, and nothing in that chunk touches his shrine, dates, urs or
  the Alam Ganj location.
- Co-indexed leads on his pages, not facts about him: "ملا شاہ بدخشی | ۵۶ ، ۵۷ ، ۵۸" (p. 330);
  "میاں منگو (والد شاہ سلیمان قادری لاہوری) | ۵۴" (p. 331); "محمد حیات (نبیرہ شاہ بلاول) | ۵۰"
  (p. 328); "شیخو پورہ | ۵۰" (p. 334). Folio 48 also carries
  "مجدد الف ثانی (احمد سرہندی) | ۴۷ ، ۴۸ ، ۱۱۶ …", "قادن قاضی | ۴۸", "عابد کبیر سید | ۴۸",
  "قلندر فاروقی قاضی | ۴۸ ، ۴۹", "کمال الدین محمد احسان | ۴۸ ، ۱۱۸ ، ۱۲۳ ، ۱۲۷ ، ۱۳۰" (pp. 322–327).

### madho-lal-hussain — Shrine of Hazrat Madho Lal Hussain (Shah Hussain Darbar)

**What the row gains — and the structural problem it raises.** The archive holds one row. **The book
holds two saints, in two separately numbered entries, with two appendix rows and two index lines**,
and never uses the compound name "Madho Lal Hussain" except inside an index parenthesis and in the
title of the editor's own encyclopaedia article. That shape is set out first below, because the
row's shape is the thing in question. The row also gains: Shah Husain's Malamati affiliation and the
reason for "Lal"; his weaver family and Hindu great-grandfather; a quantified following; a named
Persian composition; the editor's argued rejection of the book's death year; Madho's Brahmin
parentage at Shahdara, his conversion, his tenure as *mujawir* at Husain's grave, and his burial
inside his shaikh's enclosure — which is the book's only warrant for a joint shrine — together with
the editor's doubt of Madho's very historicity.

**The two-saint structure, as the book prints it**
- **Shaikh Husain**: "شیخ حسین لاہوری", entry **no. 17**, printed p. 42 (contents, p. 15, folio 3);
  entry at pp. 54–57, folios 42–45, headed "شیخ حسین المشہور بہ لال حسین لاہوری". Born
  "سال نوسو پینتالیس" = **945**, died **1008 AH** in Shah Akbar's reign (pp. 55–56, folios 43–44).
- **Shaikh Madho**: "شیخ مادھو لاہوری", entry **no. 24**, printed p. 52 (contents, p. 15, folio 3);
  entry at pp. 64–65, folios 52–53. Born "نوسو تراسی" with ۹۸۳ in the margin, died
  "ایک ہزار چھپن" with ۱۰۵۶ in the margin, age "تہتر" with ۷۳ in the margin (p. 65, folio 53); the
  editor sharpens the death to **22 Zi al-Hijja 1056 AH / 1646 CE** (Haqiqat al-Fuqara p. 165)
  (p. 65, folio 53, حاشیہ 2).
- **Two appendix rows** (p. 287, folio 275): row 5, "شیخ حسین المشہور بہ لال حسین لاہوری | لاہور |
  مادھو لال حسین سٹریٹ باغبانپورہ"; row 11, "شیخ مادھو قدس سرہ العزیز | | مادھو لال حسین سٹریٹ
  باغبانپورہ" — Madho gets a row of his own, with the honorific "قدس سرہ العزیز" and the title
  "شیخ", and the book's address column for him is **blank** where Husain's reads simply "لاہور".
  Both present locations are the one named street in Baghbanpura.
- **Two index lines.** "حسین لاہوری شاہ (مادھو لال حسین) | ۴۲ ، ۴۵ ، ۵۲" (p. 317, folio 305); est.
  pp. 54, 57, 64. And "مادھو میاں شیخ | ۴۳ ، ۵۲ ، ۵۳" (p. 327, folio 315); est. pp. 55, 64, 65.
  Madho's own line carries **no epithet tying him to Shah Husain and no gloss**; of his three
  folios one (52) is shared with Shah Husain and two (43, 53) are not. The compound "مادھو لال"
  appears only inside the parenthesis of Shah Husain's line.
- **The book's own link between them** is a footnote in Husain's entry — "رجوع کنید بہ احوال مادھو
  کتاب ہذا", see Madho's account in this book (p. 55, folio 43, حاشیہ 1) — and the naming of

**Saint — Shaikh Husain (Lal Husain), entry no. 17**
- **Why "Lal"**: "طریق ان کا ملامتیہ تھا۔ پوشاک سرخ رکھتے تھے۔ اس لیے لال حسین مشہور ہوئے" — his path
  was *Malamati*; he wore red dress, and so became famous as Lal Husain (p. 54, folio 42). The
  editor glosses Malamatiyya as the name of a silsila of free-spirited sufis (p. 54, folio 42,
  حاشیہ 3).
- Khalifa of Shaikh Bahlul Daryai; famous as a man of *hal*, *jazb*, longing, taste, *wajd* and
  *sama* (p. 54, folio 42). The pir is also given **indirectly** elsewhere: Shaikh Musa Khokhar's
  fayz came from Shaikh Bahlol Daryai and "شیخ لال حسین لاہوری بسبب پیر بھائی ہونے کے ان کا کمال ادب
  کرتے تھے", Lal Husain showed him the utmost respect because they were pir-brothers (p. 273,
  folio 261) — so Bahlol Daryai is made his pir by implication there rather than by assertion.
- **Family origin**: his grandfather, (کلجس رائے) [OCR?], was a Hindu who accepted Islam in the time
  of Firoz Shah Tughlaq; his son Usman was a religious man who earned lawful sustenance
  "جولاہوں کے کام سے", by the work of weavers; Shaikh Husain was born in his house (p. 54, folio 42).
- **Childhood initiation, with a rival account.** Author: at the age of seven, reading the Quran with
  a learned man named Abu Bakr, Shaikh Bahlul happened to pass through that mosque and his glance so
  fell on Husain that he became a perfect *wali* while still a child (p. 54, folio 42). Editor:
  *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* instead has him become the pupil of Hafiz Abu Bakr **at the age of ten, in
  955 AH** (p. 54, folio 42, حاشیہ 4).
- **His turn to the Malamati path**, from *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* 58–64: at first an orthodox Muslim, he
  was reading Tafsir-e Madarik with his teacher Shaikh Saad Allah Lahori (d. 999 AH / 1590 CE) when
  the teacher explained "وما الحیوٰۃ الدنیا الا لہو ولعب" and Shah Husain understood the world to be
  mere play; he told his teacher "مجھے حال درکار ہے قال نہیں" — I need *hal*, not *qal* — abandoned
  the student's life and took to the Malamati way. The editor adds that Saad Allah himself often
  voiced the Malamati school, quoting Nizam al-Din Ahmad on him: "بروش ملامتیہ سلوک می نمود"
  (Tabaqat-e Akbari, Lucknow, 391) (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1).
- **Repentance at the end of life**: it is a fact that in his last years he repented of all acts
  contrary to the shariah, became regular in fasting and prayer, and died in that state (Abdullah
  Khweshgi Qasuri, *Maarij al-Wilayat*, ms folio 519b) (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1).
- **Death year — three readings, and the editor rejects the book's.** Author: death **1008 AH**, in
  Shah Akbar's reign (pp. 55–56, folios 43–44), followed by a chronogram quatrain ending
  "سال ترحیل شمع عشق حسین" with **"۹۴۵ھ [OCR?]" printed beside it** (p. 56, folio 44) — the same
  numeral the text gives for his **birth**. Editor, reaching a verdict by argument: all the local
  histories give 1008 AH / 1599 CE, of which *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* (1041 AH) is the oldest;
  *Miftah al-Arifin*, composed seven years later in 1048 AH, gives **1013 AH**, which makes the
  *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* figure doubtful; but the contemporary historian Badayuni recorded that while
  composing *Najat al-Rashid* in 999 AH / 1590 CE "I learnt at Lahore that Shah Husain, hearing the
  song of a sweet-voiced woman singer, became beside himself and fell from the roof of a house and
  died" (Najat al-Rashid p. 320). Preferring the contemporary to the later tazkira-writers:
  "تحقیق کا حاصل یہ ہے کہ شاہ حسین کا صحیح سال وفات ۹۹۹ھ ہے نہ کہ ۱۰۰۸ھ" — the correct death year is
  **999 AH, not 1008 AH** (p. 55, folio 43, حاشیہ 2).
- **Scale of his following, quantified**, quoted from *Haqiqat al-Fuqara*: nine thousand perfected
  servants and "ایک لاکھ پچپن ہزار" — one hundred and fifty-five thousand murids — of whom sixteen
  became famed khalifas, four titled Gharib, four Diwan, four Khaki and four Bilawal, with the
  burial place of each given: the Gharibs at Ratti Mittha next to Wazirabad, at لنگرہی [OCR?] in
  Wazirabad district, at جیلا پور [OCR?] in the Deccan, and beside Shah Husain's own grave; the
  Diwans — the first being Shaikh Madho — with Diwan Gorakh and Allah Diwan buried at Lahore and
  Diwan Bakhshi at Bijapur in the Deccan; the Khakis (Maula Bakhsh Khaki and Khaki Shah at Lahore,
  Khaki Shah at Wazirabad, Haidar Khaki in the Deccan); and the Bilawals (Shah Rang Bilawal, Budhu
  Bilawal and Shah Mast Bilawal at Lahore, Shah Bilawal in the Deccan) (p. 55, folio 43).
- **A named khalifa and a second-generation murid.** Entry ۲۳۰: Haji Jamiat's "بیعت بخدمت شاہ رنگ
  [OCR?] بلاول حضرت لال حسین لاہوری کے خلیفہ کی خدمت میں تھی" — his allegiance was to Shah
  Rang[OCR?] Bilawal, khalifa of Hazrat Lal Husain Lahori (p. 271, folio 259): a transmission line
  out of Shah Husain that the row can cite.
- **His writings**: two works have so far come to light — *Kafian* in Punjabi and *Risala-e Tahniyat*
  in Persian prose, presenting the problems of tasawwuf in an easy manner; the full text, edited and
  introduced by Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi, was printed in Majalla Maarif, Azamgarh, August 1970 and
  again in Sahifa, Lahore, July 1972 (p. 56, folio 44). Nine further sources are listed (pp. 56–57,
  folios 44–45), including Dara Shikoh's *Hasanat al-Arifin* pp. 18, 74, and the editor's own
  article "مادھو لال حسین" in the Urdu Daira-e Maarif-e Islamia of Punjab University. The
  bibliography repeats both: item ۲۲۲ "محمد اقبال مجددی : مادھو لال حسین مقالہ مشمولہ اردو دائرہ
  المعارف اسلامیہ دانش گاہ پنجاب لاہور" (p. 303, folio 291) and item ۱۰۳ "شاہ حسین لاہوری :
  تحقیقت[OCR?]۔ مرتبہ محمد اقبال مجددی مشمولہ مجلہ صحیفہ لاہور جولائی ۱۹۷۲ء" (p. 297, folio 285,
  title flagged); the editor's article list adds "۴- شاہ حسین لاہوری کی ایک غیر مطبوعہ فارسی تصنیف
  تہنیت۔ معارف اگست ۱۹۷۰ء" — an **unpublished Persian work titled "تہنیت"** known to him in
  manuscript, *Ma'arif*, August 1970 (p. 307, folio 295).

**Saint — Shaikh Madho, entry no. 24**
- Of Shaikh Husain Lahori's khalifas, "اپنے پیر روشن ضمیر کے محبوب و مطلوب تھے" — he was his pir's
  beloved and desired one (p. 64, folio 52).
- **Origin**: "قصبہ شاہدرہ میں ان کے باپ ماں بہ قوم کے برہمن تھے رہتے تھے" — his father and mother,
  Brahmins by caste, lived in **qasba Shahdara** (p. 64, folio 52).
- **Conversion**: "حضرت حسین ان کے حسن و جمال پر بدل و جان عاشق ہو گئے اور بجذب محبت اپنی طرف کھینچا"
  — Hazrat Husain fell in love with his beauty with heart and soul and drew him by the attraction of
  love; so Madho too came to him with sincere devotion "اور مشرف بہ اسلام ہوئے", and was honoured
  with Islam, and through training became a perfect wali (pp. 64–65, folios 52–53). Separately
  sourced by the editor: "میاں مادھو نے اسلام قبول کر لیا تھا" (Haqiqat al-Fuqara p. 82) (p. 65,
  folio 53, حاشیہ 1).
- **He became the shrine's mujawir**: "جب حسین رح نے وفات پائی۔ ان کے مزار پر مجاور رہے" — when
  Husain died he remained a *mujawir* at his mazar (p. 65, folio 53).
- **The joint shrine's one textual warrant**: the editor's footnote, sharpening the death to 22 Zi
  al-Hijja 1056 AH / 1646 CE, adds "اور اپنے شیخ کے احاطہ میں دفن ہوا" — **and he was buried within
  his shaikh's enclosure** (p. 65, folio 53, حاشیہ 2). That single clause is the book's warrant for
  the archive's joint shrine, and it is the **editor's**, not the author's.
- **The editor doubts Madho's very historicity** (chunk 005, entry no. 24, pp. 64–65). The argument
  itself sits under that chunk's *Arguments and interpretations* heading and is **not carried in this
  CSV** — read it on the page before the row is rewritten.

**Legends & Miracles**

  طعنوں کا خدشہ نہ ہوتا تو میں اکثر شیخ حسین کے مزار پر جا کر استمداد کرتا" — "If I did not fear the

  names and imprints suggests folio 170 is largely editorial footnote**, with the author's own text

**Practices & Events**
- **The fullest endowment record in this book's material for any archive row.** A jagir of **one lakh
  rupees in the Punjab** with the **city of Tibala** inside it, granted by **Akbar** after
  Chittorgarh: "بعد فتح قلعہ کے اکبر نے حضرت کو ایک لاکھ روپے کی جاگیر پنجاب میں دی اور شہر ٹبالہ ان
  کی جاگیر میں قرار پایا" (p. 181).
- **He took it unwillingly**: "حضرت نے باکراہ وہ جاگیر قبول کر کے لنگر جاری کر دیا" — he accepted the
  jagir with reluctance and set a langar running (p. 181). The book's only saint who both accepts a
  state grant and is said to dislike doing so.
- **A two-site langar, quantified**: "لاہور و ٹبالہ دو جگہ حضرت کا لنگر جاری تھا۔ صدہا غربا و مساکین
  کھانا کھاتے تھے" — the langar ran at **two places, Lahore and Tibala**, and **hundreds of the poor
  and destitute ate** (p. 181, folio 179 as printed).
- **A surviving sealed farman, at second hand.** The editor's footnote: "سید محمد لطیف نے لکھا ہے کہ
  جس فرمان کی رو سے یہ جاگیر ملی تھی، وہ آج بھی اس خاندان کے پاس محفوظ ہے۔ جس پر اکبر کی مہر ثبت ہے"
  — Sayyid Muhammad Latif wrote that the farman under which this jagir was received is **preserved
  with this family even today, and Akbar's seal is impressed on it** (*Muhammad Latif, Lahore*)
  (p. 181, حاشیہ 1). A document, a seal and a named holder — the strongest provenance claim in this
  material, and it is reported **from Latif rather than seen by the editor**; what "آج بھی" dates to
  is undetermined (chunk 017 Doubts).
- **A named murid who dates his teaching.** Shah Abdul Razzaq Makki came to Lahore, took service with
  Akbar ("اکبر بادشاہ کی نوکری اختیار کی"), renounced the world, and was raised by Mauj Darya's fayz
  to qutb of the age (p. 269, folio 257) — placing the teaching activity in **Akbar's reign or
  after**. The disciple's own death is given as "سنہ ایک ہزار چوراسی", **era unstated** (p. 269).

**Legends & Miracles**
- **He tests the miracle and is answered with inheritance** (the ber-tree episode in his wife's
  entry): "میراں محمد شاہ نے پوچھا کہ یہ نعمت تجھ کو کہاں سے ملی۔ کہا کہ یہ دولت موروثی آبائی میری

### darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a — Darbar Hazrat Khawaja Shah Muhammad Sulaiman Taunsvi (R.A)

**What the row gains.** A full entry (no. 78, pp. 123–124, folios 111–112) plus the editor's
footnotes at p. 120: why the saint is at Taunsa at all, where Taunsa is, a **madrasa**, a **twice-daily
langar**, the largest living following the book gives anyone (**over one lakh murids**), the silsila
named exactly as the archive names it, three chief khalifas, a named grandson-successor, an
eight-item bibliography, and the entry's precise location. Against that, **the death year is
contradicted inside the entry itself.**

**Saint**
- Entry **no. 78** at printed page 111 (annotation, p. 348, folio 336) → PDF p. 123, confirming the

  "چشتی" is printed (p. 348, folio 336).

  Majheel[OCR?] Chishti, and "حسب الحکم پیر روشن ضمیر کے قصبہ تونسہ میں جاکر قیام پذیر ہوئے" — by the

  تونسہ سہ کروہ می شود", his own house in the hills, named Gar Kochi[OCR?], **three kuroh from

- **Death year — the entry contradicts itself, and the editor takes the second reading.** The text
  gives "نویں ماہ صفر روز پنجشنبہ سنہ ایک ہزار دوسو سٹھ" — 9 Safar, a Thursday, in the year **1260**
  — while the chronogram four lines later carries "۱۲۶۷ [OCR?]" and the editor's earlier footnote
  states "وفات ۱۲۶۷ھ میں ہوئی", death in **1267 AH** (p. 124, folio 112; p. 120, folio 108,
  حاشیہ 6). Author 1260, chronogram and editor 1267, the chronogram numeral itself `[OCR?]`.
- **The silsila named as the archive names it**: "پنجاب میں سلسلہ چشتیہ نظامیہ فخریہ کی زیادہ اشاعت
  حضرت خواجہ سلیمان تونسوی کے ذریعہ ہوئی" — in the Punjab the wider propagation of the **Chishtiya
  Nizamiya Fakhriya** silsila came about through him; born 1184 AH, died 1267 AH, with four titles

- **Father and sajjada nashin, from the index's own glosses**:
  "زکریا (والد شاہ سلیمان تونسوی) | ۱۱۱" — Zakariya, father, folio 111 (p. 318, folio 306) — and
  "الہ بخش (سجادہ نشین حضرت سلیمان تونسوی) | ۱۱۲" — Allah Bakhsh, sajjada nashin, folio 112 (p. 313,
  folio 301). Whether Allah Bakhsh held the sajjada when the author wrote, or later, is (not

- **Succession by grandson, in the author's present tense**: "بعد وفات ان کے اب شیخ الہ بخش حضرت کے
  پوتے مسند آرائے مسند مشیخیت ہیں" — after his death, **now** Shaikh Allah Bakhsh, the Hazrat's

**Shrine / Darbar**

### shergarh — Shergarh

**What the row gains.** A full entry (no. 9 of the Qadiri chapter, pp. 48–49, folios 36–37): a death
year of **982 AH**, the mazar named at Sher Garh with **an annual crowd**, two chronograms, a
Kirmani sayyid descent the author himself partly corrects, the family's migration route to Chunian,
a named pir and co-khalifa, a nephew-murid of thirty-two years, an early manuscript source dated
1056 AH with its present owner, and seven further titles. The archive's name "Daud Bandagi Kirmani"
is **nowhere matched**: the book never prints "Bandagi", and his toponymic nisba is printed two ways.

**Saint**
- Entry **no. 9** of the Qadiri chapter, at printed p. 36 (contents, p. 15, folio 3); entry at
  pp. 48–49, folios 36–37. Index: "داؤد کرمانی | ۳۵، ۳۶، ۴۵، ۴۶" (p. 317, folio 305); est. pp. 47,
  48, 57, 58. Two other Dauds are separate index entries —
  "داؤد گنگوہی | ۹۳ ، ۹۴ ، ۹۷ ، ۹۸" and "داؤد خاکی بابا | ۱۶۵ ، ۱۶۶ ، ۱۶۷ ، ۱۷۱" (p. 317).
- **The nisba is printed two ways and one reading is flagged.** The contents give
  "شیخ داؤد **جہنی وال** شیر گڈھی" (p. 15, folio 3, chunk 001); the entry heading gives
  "شیخ داؤد **چونی وال** شیر گڈھی [OCR?]" (p. 48, chunk 004). **Both stand.** Note, without treating
  it as a resolution, that the same entry has his father move to "قصبہ چونیاں" (p. 49, folio 37).

  folios (p. 317, folio 305). Chunk 003's single anecdote is placed on the name Kirmani alone.
- **Practice and initiation.** Famous among the Qadiri elders of Punjab as a master of *hal* and
  *qal*, shariat and tariqat; he was at worship from evening to morning and never slept the whole
  night, passing it now standing, now prostrate, now bowing, now seated; from the abundance of
  austerity a special *nisbat* with Ghaus al-Azam arose in him, and by Ghaus al-Azam's inner
  permission he presented himself to Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh, reached completion, and took the
  *khirqa* of khilafat (p. 48, folio 36). He was one of the two "کاملین خلفا", perfect khalifas, of
  Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh, the other being Sayyid Sher Shah Multani (p. 48, folio 36).
- **Descent, with the author's own correction.** *Shajarat al-Anwar* carries his line to Imam Musa
  Kazim — Sayyid Daud b. Sayyid Fath Allah Kirmani b. Sayyid Mubarak b. سید قمیص اللہ [OCR?] b.
  Safi al-Din Adam Kirmani b. Sayyid Taqi al-Din Ahmad, and so on to Musa Mubarqa b. Imam Musa Raza

  Muhammad Aaraji b. Sayyid Musa Mubarqa b. Imam Muhammad Taqi b. Imam Ali Musa Raza,
  "کیونکہ سید مبرقع کوئی صاحبزادہ حضرت امام رضا کا نہ تھا" — because Musa Mubarqa was no son of Imam
  Raza (p. 49, folio 37). Two of the ancestors recur in the index at his own folios:
  "صفی الدین آدم کرمانی | ۳۶" (p. 320, folio 308) and "فتح اللہ کرمانی | ۳۶ ، ۴۵" (p. 325,
  folio 313), corroborating that **folios 35–46 are a Kirmani block** (est. pp. 47–58).
- **Migration and birth**, on *Safinat al-Auliya*'s authority: his father Sayyid Fath Allah came
  first from Arabia to Hind and settled at **Haibatpur Patti**, then moved to **qasba Chunian**; when
  he died, Sayyid Daud was born four months after his death; he took a full share of the exoteric
  sciences from Maulana Ismail Lahori and then turned to the inner sciences (p. 49, folio 37).
- **Death 982 AH**, with **two chronograms**: "پیر حق پرست", from which his death date appears, and
  "مشتاق منان", the chronogram recorded in *Akhbar al-Akhyar* (p. 49, folio 37).

  folio 45); Shergarh is named again as Abul Muali's **uncle, father-in-law and pir**, and as the

**Shrine / Darbar**
- "مزار گوہر بار بمقام شیر گڈھ زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے اور ہر سال بڑا ہجوم حضرت کے مزار پر ہوتا ہے" —

**Legends & Miracles**

### darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz — Darbar Malik Ahmad Ayaz

**What the row gains.** The book's account of **how a Ghaznavid officer becomes a shrine** (entry
no. 130, pp. 192–193, folios 190–191 as printed): the Mahmud Ghaznavi connection the row already
carries, a full political sequence through Masud, Majdud and Maudud, a refusal and a renunciation,
the claim that **he refounded Lahore**, a death year and a burial "inside the city". Every one of the
last three is contested — by the editor, or by the row's own date.

**Saint**
- Entry **no. 130**, "شیخ ایاز لاہوری", printed p. 180, in the fifth chapter of "miscellaneous
  families" (p. 18, folio 6); entry at pp. 192–193. **The book's name form differs from the
  archive's "Malik Ahmad Ayaz"** — the contents give neither "Malik" nor any Ghaznavid connection
  (p. 18, chunk 001 Doubts), and the index line "ایاز لاہوری | ۱۸۰" (p. 313, folio 301) gives
  neither "Malik" nor "Ahmad". **The match to this row is not established by the index chunks**
  (chunks 029, 030), only by the entry's contents.
- **Who he is, in the book's words**: "یہ شخص شاہ محمود غزنوی کا غلام و محبوب تھا۔ حق جل شانہ نے اس
  کو دولت ظاہری و باطنی عطا کی تھی" — he was the **slave and beloved of Shah Mahmud Ghaznavi**, and
  God gave him both outward and inward wealth (pp. 192–193). This matches the archive row's
  description.
- **Tutor to a rebel prince, and the political sequence in full.** After Mahmud's death Masud took
  the throne; in his reign his son **Majdud**, "جو نو جوان لڑکا تھا", a young boy, was made
  **governor of the Punjab**, and **Ayaz was appointed his atalik (tutor) and came with him**. When
  Masud died and his son **Maudud** took the throne, Majdud **rebelled and set up a separate
  sultanate in Hindustan**; Maudud marched against him; "جب لاہور کا محاصرہ ہوا تو بروز عید ناگاہ
  مجدود بمرگ مفاجات مر گیا", when **Lahore was besieged**, on the **day of Eid** Majdud suddenly
  died, and Maudud established his hold over the Punjab (p. 193).
- **A refusal to return to Ghazni, and a renunciation.** Maudud tried hard to take Ayaz with him to
  Ghazni, "مگر اس نے منظور نہ کیا اور تارک الدنیا ہو کر صحبت فقرا اختیار کی اور بزرگان لاہور سے فیض
  کامل حاصل کیا اور بے انتہا دولت جو اس کے پاس تھی، براہ خدا فقرا و غربا کو دے دی" — he did not
  agree, renounced the world, took to the company of faqirs, **received complete faiz from the elders
  of Lahore**, and **gave away the boundless wealth he had to the faqirs and the poor for God's
  sake** (p. 193).
- **Death and grave — three readings.** Author: "آخر سنہ چار سو پچاس ہجری میں وفات پائی اور شہر
  لاہور کے اندر دفن ہوا" — died in **450 AH** and was **buried inside the city of Lahore** (p. 193).
  **The editor replaces the year with 449 AH** (p. 193, chunk 018; the editor's reasoning sits under
  that chunk's *Arguments* heading and is not carried in this CSV). And the archive row's
  "8 August 1041, calendar era unstated" **agrees with neither**. No quarter is named in the entry;
  the row's "Shah Alam Market" is not in this material.

**Legends & Miracles / contested tradition**
- **The claim that he refounded Lahore, and the editor's rejection of it.** "یہ بزرگ بانی لاہور کا
  بھی شمار کیا جاتا ہے" — this elder is also **counted the founder of Lahore**: when Sultan Mahmud
  Ghaznavi attacked Lahore and **Raja Anangpal, son of Raja Jaipal**, was overthrown and fled to Hind
  after the encounter, Mahmud's army plundered the city, all the subjects left,
  "شہر میں کوئی متنفس باقی نہ رہا", not a breathing soul remained in the city; "اس وقت ایاز نے سلطان
  سے اجازت لے کر اس شہر کو پھر آباد کیا اور رعایا کو دور دور سے طلب کر کے اس میں قیام پذیر کیا" —
  then Ayaz, taking the sultan's permission, **settled the city again** and, summoning subjects from
  far and wide, settled them in it (p. 193). **The editor rejects this tradition outright** (p. 193,
  chunk 018) — again in that chunk's *Arguments* section, not carried here. **Author and editor must
  both be quoted if the row cites the refounding at all.**

**Significance / apparatus**

  "اننگ پال راجہ | ۱۸۱", "جے پال | ۱۸۱", "جلال الدولہ محمد | ۱۸۱" (pp. 313, 315),
  "بہرام شاہ غزنوی | ۱۸۸", "خسرو ملک | ۱۸۹" (pp. 314, 317),
  "سیف الدین احمد شہید شیخ الاسلام | ۱۸۰" (p. 319, folio 307), "مجدود شہزادہ | ۱۸۱" (p. 327,
  folio 315).
