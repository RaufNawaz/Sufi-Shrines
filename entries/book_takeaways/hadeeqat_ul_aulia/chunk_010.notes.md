# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 010 — pp. 107–116

Entries 59 to 70, the Chishti-Sabiri succession from Lahore through Gangoh, Amethah, Thanesar, Jalandhar
and Ambala. **No archive row appears in this chunk** (see Doubts for two near-misses that must not be
mapped). What the chunk does carry is exceptional: a documented attempt to have a Lahore shaykh executed
for holding sama, broken by the provincial governor's own conversion; the earliest sajjada-nashin
succession named in these chunks; and three more cases of the editor catching a death year cited to a
book that does not contain one.

**The folio markers agree with the relation throughout.** Folio = PDF index − 12 holds for every page
here: p. 107 → folio 95, p. 116 → folio 104, and each marker reads exactly that. The ten-too-high run of
chunks 007–009 is over.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Shaikh Muhammad Arif Chishti Sabri Lahori** (entry no. 59, p. 107, folio 95): janishin of Shaikh Abd
  al-Khaliq Chishti Lahori; of high station in *tajrid* and *tafrid*, and "جو کچھ زبان مبارک سے فرماتے
  ظہور میں آجاتا", whatever he said from his blessed tongue came to pass. Died 7 Zi al-Hijja in the year
  one thousand and seventy-one; **mazar in Lahore** (no further location given). The healing during sama
  is under Legends.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Siddiq Chishti Sabri Lahori** (entry no. 60, p. 107, folio 95): of the great
  Chishti mashaikh, "علومِ شریعت وطریقت میں اپنے زمانے میں فرید العہد و وحید العصر", unique of his age
  in the sciences of shariat and tariqat. "تمام روز تدریس طالبان علم میں مصروف رہتا اور رات بہر تلقین
  عارفان حق میں گذرانتا" — all day occupied in teaching students, the whole night in instructing the
  knowers of God — and **"پنجاب کے ہزاروں لوگ اس کے ذریعہ سے فیض یاب دنیا وآخرت ہوئے"**, thousands of
  people of the Punjab gained benefit through him in this world and the next. He began study at his own
  wish after the age of twelve; at twenty the true attractor drew him to Shaikh Muhammad Arif, where he
  was completed and received the khirqa. Died 1084 AH; mazar in Lahore. The editor refuses to settle
  either the year or the grave: see Arguments.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Daud bin Shaikh Muhammad Sadiq Gangohi** (entry no. 61, p. 108, folio 96): murid and
  khalifa of his own father; of high resolve and strong state. Died 5 Ramadan in the year one thousand
  and ninety-five. **Of his many khalifas four were the great ones — Shaikh Sondha, Shaikh Bilaqi,
  Sayyid Gharib Allah and Shah Abu al-Maali — "جن سے علیحدہ علیحدہ سلسلے جاری ہوئے", from whom separate
  silsilas issued** (p. 108, folio 96). The editor moves his death year twenty-two years: see Arguments.
- **Shah Abu al-Maali Chishti Sabri** (entry no. 62, p. 109, folio 97): of the great sayyids and
  mashaikh, a man of love, longing, wajd and sama. Khirqa of khilafat from Shaikh Daud, and faiz also
  from Shaikh Muhammad Sadiq Gangohi. His father **Sayyid Muhammad Ashraf** lived in **qasba Amethah,
  district Saharanpur**; Abu al-Maali was left young at his death, and "والدہ نے ان کو شیخ محمد صادق کے
  سپرد کیا", his mother handed him to Shaikh Muhammad Sadiq, who taught him and at his own death handed
  him on to Shaikh Daud — a guardianship passed twice by hand. Died in the year one thousand one hundred
  and sixteen, ۱۱۱۶ھ. Of his father the editor says only "شیخ محمد اشرف کے حالات معلوم نہیں ہو سکے",
  Shaikh Muhammad Ashraf's circumstances could not be ascertained (p. 109, folio 97, حاشیہ 1).
- **Shaikh Abd al-Rashid Jalandhari Chishti** (entry no. 63, p. 110, folio 98): of the family of the
  great sayyids of the city of **Jalandhar**; father Sayyid Ashraf. Seized young by longing for the
  Truth, he left home after the customary studies, took bai'at with Shah Abu al-Maali, and was handed
  over by him to **Miran Sayyid Bhikha** for training; some years in his service and then the khirqa.
  Died on the first (غرہ) of Rabi al-Awwal, Friday, in the year one thousand one hundred and twenty-one.
  His son **Ghulam Muhi al-Din** was also an accepted man, whose bai'at was likewise at Miran Sayyid
  Bhikha's hand — a father and son both bound to the same second shaykh rather than to each other.
- **Shaikh Sondha walad Shaikh Momin Siddiqi Chishti Sabri** (entry no. 64, pp. 110–111, folios 98–99):
  khalifa of Shaikh Daud Chishti Gangohi; genealogy to Abu Bakr Siddiq. **"باپ ان کا شیخ مومن بادشاہی
  جاگیردار امیر کبیر تھا"** — his father Shaikh Momin was a **royal jagirdar and great amir**; Sondha
  was orphaned young and on coming of age "سامان حشمت وجاہ ترک کیا", renounced the apparatus of pomp
  and rank, to become Shaikh Daud's murid. Died 24 Jumada al-Awwal 1129 AH ("ایک ہزار ایک سو انتیس
  ۱۱۲۹ھ"). Both the village urs and his own death during qawwali are under Practices and Legends. The
  editor's footnote names him **Shaikh Sondha Safidoni** (p. 111, folio 99, حاشیہ 1) — resolving the
  "سوندھا سفیدوفی[OCR?]" of chunk 009.
- **Sayyid Muhammad Saeed, called Miran Shah Bhikha Chishti Sabri** (entry no. 65, pp. 111–113,
  folios 99–101): great khalifa and janishin of Shah Abu al-Maali; without a second in zauq, shauq,
  wajd, sama, istighraq and love; "مشائخ متاخرین میں سے کسی بزرگ کو ایسے کشائش ظاہری وباطنی نصیب نہیں
  ہوئی", no elder among the later mashaikh was granted such outward and inward expansion, and his murids
  mostly reached the ranks of *aqtab* and *abdal*. **A vernacular poet sung at Sufi gatherings**: "اشعار
  ہندی و دوہڑے حضرت کے بمضامین توحید بہت مشہور ہیں۔ جو صوفیوں کی محفل میں گائے جاتے ہیں" — his Hindi
  verses and *dohras* on themes of tawhid are very famous and are sung in the assemblies of the Sufis
  (p. 111, folio 99), and the editor adds that Lutf Allah Ambalvi copied the dohras into *Thamarat
  al-Fawad* (p. 1[OCR?]) (p. 111, folio 99, حاشیہ 2). A twenty-four-link genealogy to Imam Husain
  follows (pp. 111–112, folios 99–100). Orphaned at nine, he learned the outward sciences from a scholar
  named **Farid al-Din** (*Thamarat al-Fawad* 29–32) and received the khirqa from Shah Abu al-Maali.
  Born the seventh of Rajab, a Monday, in the year one thousand and forty-six; died the fifth of Ramadan
  al-Mubarak 1131 AH, aged eighty-four; **"مزار گوہر بار قصبہ گھرام میں زیارت گاہ خلق ہے"**, his mazar
  in **qasba Gharam** is a place of pilgrimage for the people (p. 113, folio 101). A Persian chronogram
  follows carrying "۹۹۹[OCR?]" and "۱۱۳۱ھ ارتحال".
- **Sayyid Shah Zaid Salar Lashkar and the conquest of Siyana/Sewana** (in Miran Bhikha's ancestry,
  p. 112, folio 100): he came from the city of **Tirmiz** with a large army to Hind "بارادۂ جہاد", with
  the intention of jihad, and halted in the city of **Sawana**; the raja there, named **Siyana**,
  "بکمال حسد حضرت کو نماز میں شہید کر دیا", out of utter envy martyred him at prayer. His sons then
  fought the raja, won, took the city and settled there. **Sultan Shams al-Din, Shah of Delhi**, hearing
  of their nobility, gave his daughter to Sayyid Shihab al-Din, son of Zaid Salar. The editor adds that
  Sayyid Shah Sulaiman Kuffar Shikan renamed Siyana "Sewana" after the conquest (*Thamarat al-Fawad*
  p. 22), and flatly denies the marriage evidence: see Arguments.
- **Shaikh Atiq Allah Chishti Jalandhari** (entry no. 66, pp. 113–114, folios 101–102): of the sayyids
  of Jalandhar of sound lineage; his bai'at was with Shah Abu al-Maali Chishti; his whole life passed in
  worship and austerity, "زہد وتقویٰ ابدرجہ کمال". Died in the year one thousand one hundred and
  thirty-one Hijri — the same year as Miran Bhikha. No grave given.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Salim Chishti Sabri Lahori** (entry no. 67, p. 114, folio 102): of the Chishtiya
  Sabiriya mashaikh of Lahore; took the khirqa of faqr from Shaikh Muhammad Siddiq Chishti Lahori. "ان
  کی مجلس کبھی سماع سے خالی نہ ہوتی" — his assembly was never without sama. The ulama's capital petition
  against him and its outcome are under Practices; it is the fullest state-and-shrine episode in these
  chunks. Died 3 Zi al-Hijja in the year one thousand one hundred and fifty-one Hijri; mazar in Lahore.
- **Shah Bahlol Barki Chishti Sabri Jalandhari** (entry no. 68, pp. 114–115, folios 102–103): khalifa of
  Shah Bhikha Chishti, "قوم کے افغان", an Afghan by community, resident of Jalandhar; a most eminent
  scholar, versed in the rational and traditional sciences, who learned from Sayyid Abd al-Rashid,
  Sayyid Kabir and Sayyid Atiq Allah Jalandhari. **"وضع ان کی قلندرانہ تھی"** — his manner was that of a
  qalandar. After Shah Bhikha's death "یہ لاہور میں آئے اور شاہ بلاق لاہوری سے فیض پایا", he came to
  **Lahore** and took faiz from **Shah Bilaq Lahori**. He wrote *Fawaid al-Asrar*, a commentary on the
  Diwan of Khwaja Hafiz and other works, and his own diwan on themes of tawhid is popular with elite and
  common. **"مولوی جان محمد ترک کے ساتھ ہمیشہ ان کا بحث رہتا تھا"** — he was permanently in dispute with
  Maulvi Jan Muhammad Turk. His eminent students: Sayyid Alim Allah Jalandhari, "بسھاجند[OCR?]" with the
  takhallus **Nadir**, and Azmat Khan Barki, whose own diwans and works are famous too. Died 1170 AH
  ("۱۱۷۰ھ ایک ہزار ایک سو ستر ہجری"); **mazar in Jalandhar**. The editor's footnote names him **Shaikh
  Bahlol Gul bin Mirza Khan Barki thumma Jalandhari** and lists eight dated Persian manuscripts of his
  in the Sherani collection of the Punjab University library (p. 115, folio 103, حاشیہ 1) — see
  Practices for the two on sama.
- **Shah Lutf Allah Chishti (Lutf Allah Ambalvi)** (entry no. 69, pp. 115–116, folios 103–104): of the
  murids of pure conviction of Miran Sayyid Bhikha Chishti; **his residence was in the city of Ambala**.
  Raised from boyhood in his pir's presence, he took his outward and inward learning from him and "کتاب
  ثمراۃ الفواد اپنے پیر کے حال میں لکھی جو اب تک یادگار زمانہ ہے", wrote the book *Thamarat al-Fawad* on
  his pir's condition, which is to this day a memorial of the age. Died 20 Zi al-Qada in the year one
  thousand one hundred and eighty Hijri; **mazar in Jalandhar**. The editor's summary of his own
  autobiography is under Legends and Practices.
- **Maulana Fakhr al-Din Fakhr-e Jahan Shahjahanabadi Chishti** (entry no. 70, opening only, p. 116,
  folio 104): most learned of the ulama and chief among the khalifas of **Shaikh Nizam al-Din
  Aurangabadi**, who was his own father — the entry breaks off at the chunk edge.
- **Shaikh Nizam al-Din Aurangabadi** (editor's footnote, p. 116, folio 104): 1060–1142 AH /
  1649–1729 CE, a famous elder of the Chishtiya silsila; after Khwaja Gesu Daraz (d. 825 AH) no elder of
  the Chishtiya Nizamiya of such high rank "سرزمین دکن پر … قدم نہیں رکھا", set foot on the soil of the
  Deccan. "امرا کو آپ سے بڑی عقیدت تھی" — the nobles had great devotion to him — and **Nizam al-Mulk
  Asaf Jah I (dates printed "۱۷۴۸ - ۱۶۷۱ء")** was his devotee and wrote a book, "اشک گلستان ارم[OCR?]",
  on his circumstances; his teachings deeply affected both the people and the rulers of the Deccan.
- **Places named**: Amethah in Saharanpur district (p. 109, folio 97); Thanesar, where the mashaikh
  assembled (p. 109, folio 97); Jalandhar (pp. 110, 114–116); qasba Gharam, Miran Bhikha's shrine town
  (p. 113, folio 101); Ambala (pp. 115–116); mauza Naundhan[OCR?] (p. 112, folio 100); Sadhaura, in the
  maternal genealogy (p. 112, folio 100); Sawana / Siyana / Sewana (p. 112, folio 100); Maidan Zain Khan
  and Sarai Ratan Chand in Lahore (p. 107, folio 95).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **An execution demanded for holding sama, and averted by the governor's conversion.** The fullest
  state-shrine episode in these chunks, in the entry for Shaikh Muhammad Salim Lahori: "محمد شاہ بادشاہ
  کے وقت علمائے لاہور بسبب سماع دائمی ان کے دشمن ہو گئے۔ سب نے مل کر بادشاہ کی خدمت میں عرض کی کہ ایسے
  بدعتی شخص کو قتل کرنا واجب ہے۔ بادشاہ نے وہ عرضی صوبہ دار لاہور کے پاس بھیج دی۔ صوبہ دار لاہور خود ان
  کی خدمت میں آیا۔ کشش باطنی سے مرید ہو گیا۔ جب خود صوبہ دار لاہور نے بیعت کر لی تو علمائے لاہور خاموش
  ہو گئے" — in the time of **Muhammad Shah Badshah** the ulama of Lahore became his enemies on account of
  his perpetual sama; jointly they petitioned the emperor that killing such an innovator (*bid'ati*) was
  obligatory; the emperor **forwarded the petition to the subahdar of Lahore**; the subahdar came in
  person, was drawn by inner attraction and became a murid; and when the subahdar himself had taken
  bai'at, the ulama of Lahore fell silent (p. 114, folio 102). Named emperor, named office, a document
  passed down the chain of command, and a silence rather than a verdict.
- **The same controversy in writing, twice, by a shaykh of the next town.** Among Bahlol Barki's
  manuscripts: **"السیف المسلول علی من اعرض من سماع الرسول"** — *The Drawn Sword against Him who Turns
  Away from the Prophet's Sama* — dated 1138 AH, no. 4425/1374[OCR?], **two copies**; and **"عقائد علیہ
  در مذہب صوفیہ"**, *Lofty Doctrines in the Way of the Sufis*, dated 1140 AH, no. 2/1374/4254[OCR?],
  written "در جواب اعتراضات مخالفین بر رسالہ سیف المسلول", in answer to the opponents' objections to the
  *Saif al-Maslul* (p. 115, folio 103, حاشیہ 1). A defence of sama, an attack on it, and a rejoinder,
  all dated within two years of each other and within thirteen years of the Lahore petition.
- **A sajjada-nashin succession, named.** After Shah Abu al-Maali's death "ان کے دو صاحبزادے شاہ محمد
  فخر الدین اور مسعود احمد سجادہ نشین ہوئے" — his two sons Shah Muhammad Fakhr al-Din and Masud Ahmad
  became *sajjada nashin* (*Anwar al-Ashiqin*) (p. 109, folio 97, حاشیہ 3). The first explicit use of
  the term in these chunks, and it is hereditary and shared between two brothers.
- **A village urs with sama, attended by an outside shaykh, at which the landowner made his claim.**
  "ایک روز ایک گاؤں میں حضرت بتقریب ایک عرس کے تشریف لے گئے۔ جب ہنگامہ سماع گرم ہوا" — one day the
  Hazrat went to a village on the occasion of an urs, and when the tumult of sama grew warm, the young
  son of "گاؤں کے مالک", the village's owner, died; the owner carried the corpse's charpai "عین مجلس
  میں", into the very middle of the assembly, and petitioned in verse: "مردان خدا خدا نباشند / لیکن ز
  خدا جدا نباشند" — the men of God are not God, but they are not separate from God — adding "تم لوگ
  مقبولان الہی ہو", you people are the accepted of God (pp. 110–111, folios 98–99). An urs as the
  occasion on which a local landholder brings a demand to visiting saints.
- **Death staged as a sama: a shaykh who summoned the qawwals to sing him out.** On the day of his death
  Shaikh Sondha "قوالوں کو بلا کر حکم دیا" that they sing a couplet of **Hafiz Shirazi**, "صحبت غیر
  نخواہم کہ بود عین قصور / با خیال تو چرا با دگران پردازم", and when they began he entered wajd and in
  that state departed (p. 111, folio 99). Sama as a funeral rite chosen in advance.
- **A jagirdar's son who renounced the jagir.** Shaikh Sondha's father Shaikh Momin was a "بادشاہی
  جاگیردار امیر کبیر", a royal jagirdar and great amir; Sondha renounced the pomp on coming of age
  (p. 110, folio 98). Alongside Abd al-Karim Lahori's father in chunk 009, the second amir's son in two
  chunks to convert inherited office into a khanqah career.
- **Mass conversion as the measurable outcome of a miracle.** After Miran Bhikha revived the murid's
  son: "یہ کرامت دیکھ کر حاضرین مجلس سے اک شور اٹھا اور ہزاروں آدمی مرید ہوئے" — a clamour arose from
  those present and **thousands of men became murids** (p. 113, folio 101). The book's usual idiom for
  reach ("ہزاروں") is here tied to a single event.
- **A child promised, named and delivered to a pir before birth.** Lutf Allah Ambalvi's mother was
  "شاہ میراں بھیکہ کی ارشاد یافتہ", a disciple of Shah Miran Bhikha, who told her "اللہ تعالیٰ جو فرزند
  عطا کرے۔ اس کا نام لطف اللہ رکھنا", name whatever son God grants you Lutf Allah, "چنانچہ ایسا ہی
  ہوا", and so it was (p. 116, folio 104, حاشیہ 1). A woman as the disciple, and the naming as the tie.
- **A teaching day and a teaching night, and a provincial reach.** Muhammad Siddiq Lahori taught
  students all day and instructed the knowers of God all night, and "پنجاب کے ہزاروں لوگ" benefited
  through him (p. 107, folio 95) — the book's one explicitly **provincial** claim of reach rather than a
  city or a shrine.
- **Forty days in a hujra, and what was eaten there.** A murid's *chilla* in a cell containing a ber
  tree, and the claim of forty days without food (p. 108, folio 96) — the practice is recorded
  incidentally by the karamat that exposes the claim.
- **Print and manuscript economy, again recorded exactly.** *Thamarat al-Fawad* printed with an Urdu
  translation from the **Army Press, Delhi**, with two manuscripts in the Sherani collection, Punjab
  University Lahore, nos. 2229 and 1143[OCR?] (p. 109, folio 97, حاشیہ 2; repeated p. 116, folio 104);
  Bahlol Barki's eight manuscripts with shelf numbers and, for three of them, the statement that they
  are in the author's own hand (p. 115, folio 103, حاشیہ 1); *Rawaih al-Nizam*, Delhi 1344 AH, and
  manuscripts at Aligarh Muslim University and the **Karachi Museum** (p. 116, folio 104, حاشیہ 2).
- **A ruler as author of his shaykh's biography**: Nizam al-Mulk Asaf Jah I wrote a book on Nizam al-Din
  Aurangabadi's circumstances, and "اہل دکن اور والیان دکن پر آپ کی تعلیمات کے گہرے اثرات ہوئے", the
  teachings deeply affected the people and the rulers of the Deccan (p. 116, folio 104, حاشیہ 2).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A healing in the middle of wajd, to the words of the qawwali being sung.** The qawwals were singing
  "آن مسیحائے کہ جان در دست اوست / می دہد جان گر بمیرم چند بار" when a man carried in his dying son;
  "حضرت اسی وجد کی حالت میں اٹھے اور بیمار کے منہ پر ہاتھ پھیرا۔ اسی وقت اچھا ہو گیا" — the Hazrat rose
  in that very state of wajd, passed his hand over the sick boy's face, and he was well at once (p. 107,
  folio 95). The verse and the cure are told as one thing.
- **The ber tree that testified against a dervish's fast.** Told of Shaikh Muhammad Daud from *Sawati
  al-Anwar*: a murid in a cell containing a ber tree ate its leaves in a state of "مجہوک[OCR?]" and then
  claimed forty days without food; the Hazrat said "تو جھوٹ کہتا ہے", you lie, and on the man's denial
  became angry and addressed the tree, which "زبان فصیح سے بولا", spoke with an eloquent tongue: yes,
  this dervish has eaten my leaves for forty days. The dervish repented of his claim (p. 108, folio 96).
  The second speaking-object miracle used to settle a factual dispute in this book, after chunk 008's
  speaking land.
- **A buffalo killed and revived by the two halves of the kalima.** At an assembly of mashaikh in
  Thanesar, Shah Abu al-Maali said that death and life lie in the negation and affirmation of "لا الہ
  الا اللہ", and that whoever has recited it from the heart can kill with "لا الہ" spoken into a living
  ear and revive with "الا اللہ". The company asked for a test; he said "لا الہ" into the ear of a
  buffalo tied in the courtyard and it fell dead at once, then "الا اللہ" into the other ear and "فی
  الفور گاؤ میش جی اٹھی اور چارہ چرنے لگی", at once the buffalo revived and began to graze. Reported from
  *Thamarat al-Fawad* (p. 109, folio 97).
- **A boy revived at a village urs.** Shaikh Sondha lifted the sheet from the dead boy's face and said
  "اے لڑکے اس قادر قیوم کے حکم سے جو ہمیشہ زندہ ہے اور کبھی نہیں مرے گا۔ زندہ ہو جا" — O boy, by the
  command of that Powerful and Self-subsisting One who is ever living and will never die, come to life —
  and "لڑکا فی الفور زندہ ہو گیا" (p. 111, folio 99). Reported from *Sawati al-Anwar*.
- **A boy revived by a refusal to eat.** Miran Bhikha would not touch the food until his murid's son was
  brought — "لڑکا جب تک حاضر نہ ہو گا۔ کھانا ہم پر حلال نہیں ہے", until the boy is present the food is
  not lawful for us — and when the murid confessed that the boy had died two *sa'at* before his arrival
  and lay locked in a room, said "وہ مرا نہیں۔ شاید تو نے مغالط کھایا ہوگا", he is not dead, perhaps you
  were mistaken; the father found him breathing, shook him, and he sat up (pp. 112–113, folios 100–101).
  Reported from *Thamarat al-Fawad*. Note the husband's instruction to his wife to remove the signs of
  mourning and cook for the guest.
- **Shaikh Sondha's death in wajd** to a couplet of Hafiz, quoted under Practices (p. 111, folio 99).
- Of Muhammad Arif Lahori it is said in general terms that whatever he uttered came to pass (p. 107,
  folio 95); of Miran Bhikha, that his murids reached the ranks of aqtab and abdal (p. 111, folio 99).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor declines to adjudicate, once, and says why.** On Shaikh Muhammad Siddiq Lahori, whose
  death the text puts at 1084 AH: "سید محمد لطیف نے ۹۹۰ھ / ۱۵۸۲ء لکھا ہے۔ جو مفتی صاحب سے بہت متضاد
  ہے۔ دونوں مصنف بہت بعد کے ہیں۔ ہمارے پاس کوئی معاصر شہادت نہیں ہے۔ اس لیے ترجیح دینے سے قاصر ہیں" —
  Sayyid Muhammad Latif wrote 990 AH / 1582 CE, which is very contradictory to the Mufti's; **both
  authors are much later; we have no contemporary evidence; therefore we are unable to prefer either**
  (p. 107, folio 95, حاشیہ 1). Ninety-four years apart and left open. This is the clearest statement of
  the editor's criterion anywhere in these chunks: a preference requires contemporary testimony.
- **And in the same footnote he supplies a rival grave location he does not endorse.** Sayyid Muhammad
  Latif wrote of the mazar "کہ میدان زین خان میں جو مشرق کے جنوب میں سرائے رتن چند سے ریلوے اسٹیشن کو
  جاتی ہے واقع ہے (ص ۹۵)" — that it stands in **Maidan Zain Khan**, which lies to the south of the east,
  running from **Sarai Ratan Chand** to the railway station (p. 95 of Latif) (p. 107, folio 95). The book
  itself says only "مزار گوہر بار لاہور میں ہے".
- **Second instance of the pattern: a date cited to a book that has no date in it.** On Shaikh Daud
  Gangohi, whose death the text gives as 5 Ramadan 1095 AH: "مفتی صاحب نے خزینۃ الاصفیا (۱ / ۴۸۵) میں
  ۱۰۹۵ھ سواطع الانوار کے حوالہ سے لکھا ہے۔ لیکن حقیقت یہ ہے کہ سواطع الانوار (اقتباس الانوار) شیخ داؤد
  کے سال وفات سے یکسر خالی ہے (ص ۳۱۶)" — the Mufti wrote 1095 AH in *Khazinat al-Asfiya* (1/485) on the
  authority of *Sawati al-Anwar*, but the reality is that *Sawati al-Anwar* (*Iqtibas al-Anwar*) **is
  entirely devoid of Shaikh Daud's death year** (p. 316). Against it: "حدائق داؤدی میں سال وفات یک شنبہ
  رمضان ۱۰۷۳ھ درج ہے اور یہی سال محمد اسلم پسروری نے بھی لکھا ہے (فرحۃ الناظرین ص ۵۹) جو ہمارے نزدیک
  معتبر ہے" — *Hadaiq-e Daudi* records **Sunday, Ramadan 1073 AH**, and Muhammad Aslam Pasrori wrote the
  same year (*Farhat al-Nazirin* p. 59), which in our view is reliable (p. 108, folio 96, حاشیہ 1).
  **Author: 5 Ramadan 1095. Editor: Ramadan 1073, a Sunday — twenty-two years earlier, on two
  independent witnesses.**
- **Third instance, this time against a third party rather than the author.** "شیخ سوندھا سفیدونی کا
  سال وفات صاحب اقتباس الانوار نے نہیں لکھا۔ اس لیے مولانا مشتاق احمد نے انوار العاشقین میں بحوالہ
  اقتباس الانوار، شیخ سوندھا کا جو سال وفات ۱۱۱۹ھ لکھا ہے غلط ہے" — the author of *Iqtibas al-Anwar* did
  not write Shaikh Sondha Safidoni's death year, so the 1119 AH that Maulana Mushtaq Ahmad wrote in
  *Anwar al-Ashiqin* on *Iqtibas al-Anwar*'s authority **is wrong** (*Anwar al-Ashiqin* p. 104[OCR?])
  (p. 111, folio 99, حاشیہ 1). He demolishes 1119 without defending the text's 24 Jumada al-Awwal
  1129 AH — so this saint's date is left standing only on the author's own word.
- **Three men's dates now rest on the same discovery**: that *Iqtibas al-Anwar* / *Sawati al-Anwar*,
  the book the author leans on for the whole Sabiri chain, carries no death year for Muhammad Sadiq
  (chunk 009, p. 106), none for Shaikh Daud (p. 108, folio 96) and none for Shaikh Sondha (p. 111,
  folio 99). The editor found the same silence three times and each time someone had cited it as speech.
- **A fourth date left open without comment**: Shah Abu al-Maali, 1116 AH in the text, against
  "مشتاق احمد امیٹھوی نے شاہ ابوالمعالی کا سال وفات ۱۱۱۲ھ لکھا ہے" — Mushtaq Ahmad Amethvi wrote 1112 AH
  (*Anwar al-Ashiqin* 109) (p. 109, folio 97, حاشیہ 3). Stated, not adjudicated.
- **He denies a royal marriage for want of contemporary proof.** Of Sultan Shams al-Din giving his
  daughter to Zaid Salar's son: **"اس کا کوئی معاصر تاریخی ثبوت نہیں ہے۔ ثمرات الفواد میں مفصل واقعہ
  لکھا ہے"** — there is no contemporary historical proof of this; the detailed account is written in
  *Thamarat al-Fawad* (see p. 22) (p. 112, folio 100, حاشیہ 5). The same criterion as in the Muhammad
  Siddiq footnote, applied to a prestige claim rather than a date.
- **He corrects two links of the genealogy and the mother's name.** "عز الدین غلط ہے۔ صحیح رمز الدین
  ہونا چاہیے (ثمرات الفواد ص ۲۳)" — Izz al-Din is wrong, it should be Ramz al-Din (p. 112, folio 100,
  حاشیہ 1); and against the text's "بی بی سیدہ پاک دامن" for Miran Bhikha's mother, the footnote gives
  "شاہ میراں بھیکہ کی والدہ کا نام بی بی ملکوہ[OCR?]", her name was **Bibi Malikwah[OCR?]**, sister of
  Sayyid Ali Akbar, with a nine-link maternal genealogy (*Thamarat al-Fawad* p. 23) (p. 112, folio 100,
  حاشیہ 3). See Doubts — this is the one name in the chunk that could be mis-mapped to an archive row.
- **He corrects a bibliographic error of the author's own making.** Of Bahlol Barki's works: "فوائد
  الاسرار فی رفع الاستار عن عیون الاغیار در حل دیوان حافظ کی شرح کا ہی نام ہے۔ یہاں اندراج میں مفتی
  صاحب سے تسامح ہوا ہے اور فوائد الاسرار کو دیوان حافظ کی شرح سے الگ تصنیف لکھ دیا" — *Fawaid al-Asrar
  fi Raf al-Astar an Uyun al-Aghyar* **is** the name of the commentary on Hafiz's Diwan; here the Mufti
  has slipped and written *Fawaid al-Asrar* as a work separate from the commentary; the manuscript is in
  the Sherani collection (p. 114, folio 102, حاشیہ 3). One title counted twice, corrected by inspecting
  the manuscript.
- **A count of works corrected by a list of them.** "مولوی رحمان علی نے ان کی تصانیف کی تعداد نو سے[OCR?]
  لکھی ہے (تذکرہ ص ۱۳۴)" — Maulvi Rahman Ali wrote the number of his works as nine[OCR?] — set against
  the editor's own eight numbered, dated manuscripts from the Sherani catalogue (p. 115, folio 103,
  حاشیہ 1).
- **And a historiographical judgement of his own on the Deccan**: after Gesu Daraz (d. 825 AH) no
  Chishtiya Nizamiya elder of comparable rank entered the Deccan until Nizam al-Din Aurangabadi (p. 116,
  folio 104, حاشیہ 2, with Khaliq Ahmad Nizami, *Tarikh-e Mashaikh-e Chisht* 427–459).

## Doubts

- **"بی بی سیدہ پاک دامن" here is not the archive's Bibi Pak Daman.** The text names Miran Bhikha's
  mother, in a saint who died in 1131 AH, as "بی بی سیدہ پاک دامن" (p. 112, folio 100) — and the
  editor's own footnote on the same page replaces the name with **Bibi Malikwah[OCR?]**, sister of Sayyid
  Ali Akbar. The words are almost certainly descriptive ("the chaste sayyida"), not the Lahore shrine of
  the six Bibis. **Do not map this to bibi-pak-daman.** Recorded here because a name search would
  otherwise catch it.
- **"میاں شاہ عنایت" cannot be identified from this chunk.** Lutf Allah Ambalvi was moved to austerity
  by "میاں شاہ عنایت کی ریاضت" (p. 116, folio 104, حاشیہ 1), with no nisba, place or date. The archive
  holds two plausible rows — **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri** (Lahore, d. c. 1728) and
  **dargah-roza-sufi-shah-inayat-shaheed** (Jhok, d. 1718) — and the chunk supports neither. Leave
  unmapped.
- **No archive row is the subject of any entry in this chunk.** Four Lahore graves appear — Muhammad
  Arif (1071 AH), Muhammad Siddiq (1084 AH, or Maidan Zain Khan per Latif), Muhammad Salim (1151 AH) and,
  by adoption, Bahlol Barki's Lahore period — and none matches a row in the index. They belong on a
  candidate list; do not attach them to an existing Lahore entry on the strength of city and silsila.
- **Miran Bhikha's stated age does not match his stated dates.** Born the seventh of Rajab 1046, died
  the fifth of Ramadan 1131, "عمر شریف چوراسی برس کی" — noble age eighty-four (pp. 112–113, folios
  100–101). 1131 − 1046 is eighty-five Hijri years, and the death month falls after the birth month, so
  the completed age is eighty-five. Either the age or one of the years is out by one; the editor
  corrects only the birth *date* (see next), not the arithmetic.
- **Miran Bhikha's birth date is disputed within the same page opening**: the text gives the seventh of
  Rajab, and the footnote "مولانا لطف اللہ نے تاریخ ولادت نہم رجب لکھی ہے", Maulana Lutf Allah wrote the
  ninth of Rajab (*Thamarat* p. 23) (p. 113, folio 101, حاشیہ 1) — and Lutf Allah is the pupil who wrote
  the biography the editor uses throughout.
- **Shaikh Sondha's death year is left with no endorsed value.** The editor shows that the 1119 AH in
  circulation is groundless (p. 111, folio 99) but does not affirm the text's 24 Jumada al-Awwal 1129 AH.
  Carry the text's date only with this note.
- **Shaikh Daud Gangohi's day of the week and month survive the correction but the day of the month does
  not.** Text: 5 Ramadan 1095. Editor: "یک شنبہ رمضان ۱۰۷۳ھ", Sunday, Ramadan 1073, with no day number
  (p. 108, folio 96). The archive cannot state a full date either way.
- **Shah Abu al-Maali's father is a Sayyid in the text and a Shaikh in the footnote**: "سید محمد اشرف"
  (p. 109, folio 97) against "شیخ محمد اشرف کے حالات معلوم نہیں ہو سکے" (same page, حاشیہ 1). And
  entry no. 63's Abd al-Rashid Jalandhari also has a father named **Sayyid Ashraf** (p. 110, folio 98) —
  two men of the same name a generation apart, not identified with each other by the book.
- **A cross-reference in the footnotes points to the wrong entry.** "ملاحظہ ہو ترجمہ نمبر ۶۷[OCR?] کتاب
  ہذا" is given for **Sayyid Atiq Allah Jalandhari** (p. 114, folio 102, حاشیہ 2), but entry no. 67 in
  this chunk is Shaikh Muhammad Salim Lahori and Atiq Allah is entry no. **66** (pp. 113–114, folios
  101–102). The numeral is OCR-flagged; the second cross-reference, "ترجمہ نمبر ۸۷[OCR?]" for Sayyid
  Alim Allah Jalandhari, is flagged too and cannot be checked from this chunk.
- **Asaf Jah I's dates are printed in reverse order**: "نظام الملک آصف جاہ اول (۱۷۴۸ - ۱۶۷۱ء)" (p. 116,
  folio 104, حاشیہ 2). The later year comes first; treat as 1671–1748 and flag if quoted.
- **Bahlol Barki's name differs between text and footnote**: "شاہ بہلول برکی چشتی صابری جالندھری"
  (p. 114, folio 102) against "شیخ بہلول گل بن مرزا خان برکی ثم جالندھری" and the source title "احوال
  مرزا بہلول برکی" (p. 115, folio 103). Bahlol, Bahlol Gul and Mirza Bahlol for one man, unreconciled.
- `[OCR?]` flags carried over that touch facts above, by page: "مجہوک[OCR?]", the state in which the
  dervish ate the ber leaves (p. 108, folio 96); "۲۲۲۹ ۱۱۴۳[OCR?]", the two *Thamarat al-Fawad*
  shelfmarks (p. 109, folio 97); "محمد تحتہ[OCR?]", "رہبر کا کی[OCR?]", "حمیص[OCR?]", "احمیص[OCR?]",
  "احمد یحیٰ[OCR?]", "بی بی ملکوہ[OCR?]", "سید خان کلتھی[OCR?]" and "موضع نوندھن[OCR?]" — seven of the
  genealogy's links plus the mother's name and the miracle's village (p. 112, folio 100);
  "ص ۱۰۴[OCR?]" and "ص ۱[OCR?]" in the Sondha and dohra footnotes (p. 111, folio 99); "یہ بات سن کر[OCR?]"
  (p. 113, folio 101); "۹۹۹[OCR?]" and "تو کید او[OCR?]" in Miran Bhikha's chronogram (p. 113,
  folio 101); "بسھاجند[OCR?]", the student's name, and the cross-references "۶۷[OCR?]" and "۸۷[OCR?]"
  (p. 114, folio 102); every one of the eight Sherani shelfmarks for Bahlol Barki plus "نو سے[OCR?]" and
  the seven-page catalogue reference (p. 115, folio 103); "اشک گلستان ارم[OCR?]" and "مناقب
  مخمس[OCR?]" (p. 116, folio 104).
- **Death years given in words, not numerals**, for Muhammad Arif (1071), Muhammad Siddiq (1084),
  Muhammad Daud (1095), Abd al-Rashid (1121), Atiq Allah (1131), Muhammad Salim (1151) and Lutf Allah

  chronogram) and Bahlol Barki (۱۱۷۰ھ) carry a numeral beside the words.
- **One unresolved reading in the chronogram for Miran Bhikha**: "ہست نور معرفت ۹۹۹[OCR?] تو کید او[OCR?]"
  (p. 113, folio 101). A chronogram whose numeral does not obviously relate to the 1131 given beside it;
  do not treat 999 as a date.
