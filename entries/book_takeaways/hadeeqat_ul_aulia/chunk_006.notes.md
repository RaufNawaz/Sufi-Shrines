# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 006 — pp. 68–77

End of entry 28 (Shah Chiragh) through the opening of entry 37 (Shah Muhammad Ghaus Lahori), whose
eyewitness account of Sikh-period demolition breaks off at the chunk edge. Contains the chunk-series'
single most consequential finding for the archive: the editor's demolition of Shah Inayat Qadri's
death date, which he shows the author invented.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** (سید عبد الرزاق المشہور شاہ چراغ قادری لاہوری — end of entry no. 28,
  p. 68, folio 56):
  - Descent: his father was Sayyid Abdul Wahab b. Sayyid Abdul Qadir Thalith b. Muhammad Ghaus Bala
    Pir b. Zain al-Abidin b. Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani b. Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Uchi Halabi Gilani — so
    the fifth generation of the Uch Gilani family whose earlier members fill entries 2 to 8 (p. 68,
    folio 56).
  - **The name explained**: "جس روز یہ پیدا ہوئے ان کے والد بزرگوار نے فرمایا کہ یہ فرزند ہمارے گھر
    کا ایک چراغ پیدا ہوا ہے جس سے تمام خاندان روشن ہو گا۔ ان کا نام شاہ چراغ مقرر ہوا" — on the day
    he was born his father said that this son has been born as a lamp of our house, by which the whole
    family will be lit; and his name was fixed as Shah Chiragh (p. 68, folio 56).
  - He loved travel and toured many countries "بطور تجربہ", by way of experience, and also went to
    the Haramain Sharifain (p. 68, folio 56).
  - **A refused imperial marriage**: "شاہ جہان بادشاہ ان کا کامل معتقد تھا۔ اس نے ہر چند چاہا کہ ایک
    اپنی لڑکی ان کے یا ان کے فرزند سید مصطفٰی کے ساتھ منسوب کرے۔ قبول نہ کیا" — Emperor Shah Jahan was
    his complete devotee and much wished to betroth one of his daughters to him or to his son Sayyid
    Mustafa; he did not accept (p. 68, folio 56). His son's name is given here as Sayyid Mustafa.
  - **Death: "بائیسویں ذی قعدہ سن ایک ہزار اڑسٹھ ہجری"** — the twenty-second of Zi Qada, the year one
    thousand and sixty-eight Hijri — "اور روضہ حضرت کا لاہور میں ہے", and his rauza is in Lahore
    (p. 68, folio 56). 1068 AH corresponds to the archive row's 1657.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (میاں میر بالا پیر لاہوری), all via entry no. 29 on his khalifa Mulla Shah
  Badakhshi:
  - Mulla Shah's "مزار گوہر بار لاہور میں بیرون مزار میاں میر بالا پیر ہے" — his mazar is in Lahore
    outside Mian Mir Bala Pir's mazar; "اب ان کے مزار کے احاطہ میں موضع میاں میر آباد ہے", and the
    village **Mian Mir Abad** now stands within the enclosure of his mazar (p. 69, folio 57). A
    settlement grown inside a shrine precinct, recorded by the author as a present fact.
  - **The shrine's cost and its stripping**: "ان کا روضہ دارا شکوہ نے کئی لاکھ روپے خرچ کر کے بنوایا
    تھا جس کے پتھر تمام و کمال رنجیت سنگھ اتروا کر امرت سر لے گیا" — Dara Shikoh had his rauza built
    at an expense of several lakh rupees, and Ranjit Singh had its stones entirely taken down and
    carried off to Amritsar (p. 69, folio 57). The editor sources the point to Kanhiya Lal's *Tarikh-e
    Lahore* 245 (p. 69, folio 57, حاشیہ 3).
  - Khwaja Bihari's grave beside Mian Mir's rauza was recorded in the previous chunk (p. 66,
    folio 54).
- **ranmal-sharif** (شیخ حاجی محمد قادری المشہور بحضرت نوشہ گنج بخش — entry no. 30, pp. 70–72,
  folios 58–60):
  - A wali from birth and **khalifa of Shah Sulaiman Qadri** (the Bhalwal figure of entry no. 26);
    states of jazb, sukr, love and longing dominated him, and he was unequalled in asceticism, piety
    and karamat (p. 70, folio 58).
  - **The order's origin stated plainly**: "طریقہ نوشاہیہ جس کے فقیر پنجاب میں ہزاروں ہیں۔ ان سے شروع
    ہوا" — the Naushahia path, whose faqirs in Punjab number in the thousands, began with him (p. 70,
    folio 58).
  - **Parents**: his father Haji Ala al-Din was himself a great elder who performed the Hajj seven
    times in his life and was honoured with a vision of the Prophet; his mother **Bibi Jiwani** was
    also a great worshipper and ascetic (p. 70, folio 58).
  - Up to seventeen he completed the exoteric sciences, then renounced the world and stayed in the
    reed-bed of ساندل بار (Sandal Bar) in zikr, "مگر والدین نے ان کو وہاں نہ چھوڑا", but his parents
    would not leave him there; coming away, he was brought by Mulla Karim al-Din to Shah Sulaiman,
    took bai'at, and in a few years reached completion (pp. 70–71, folios 58–59). The editor corrects
    the place: "ساندل بار کی بجائے گوندل بار ہونا چاہیے" — instead of Sandal Bar it should be **Gondal
    Bar** (p. 70, folio 58, حاشیہ 2).
  - His karamat "کا انتہا نہیں ہے" and are set out in detail in *Tazkira-e Naushahi* by Muhammad
    Hayat (p. 71, folio 59).
  - **Death, and the editor's correction**: the author gives 1103 AH with the chronogram "پیر فیاض"
    (p. 71, folio 59). The editor: "حضرت نوشہ رح کا سال ولادت ۹۵۹ھ بمطابق یکم رمضان ہے… آپ کا صحیح
    سال وفات ۱۲ ربیع الاول ۱۰۶۴ھ ہے۔ ۱۱۰۳ھ غلط ہے۔ سید شرافت نوشاہی نے تحقیق سے ثابت کیا ہے" — his
    birth was 1 Ramadan 959 AH; his correct death is **12 Rabi al-Awwal 1064 AH**; 1103 AH is wrong,
    as Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi has proved by research (Sharif al-Tawarikh, three volumes cited)
    (p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 2). 1064 AH corresponds to the archive row's 1654.
  - **Where the mazar is**: "آپ کا مزار شریف اس وقت ساہنپال شریف ضلع گجرات پنجاب میں ہے" — his holy
    mazar is at present at **Sahnpal Sharif, district Gujrat, Punjab** (p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ). This
    is not the archive row's Ranmal Sharif, Phalia — see Doubts.
  - His Urdu and Punjabi verse was selected and edited by Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi as **"گنج شریف"**
    and published by Dar al-Mu'arrikhin, Lahore, in 1975 (p. 71, folio 59).
  - **Nine manuscript sources for the Naushahia line** are listed, the fullest source-list in the
    chunks so far (p. 71, folio 59): Razi al-Din Qazi Ganjahi, *Risala Tuhfa-e Razwiya*, within Mirza
    Ahmad Beg Lahori's *Risala al-Ijaz*; Ahmad Beg Mirza Lahori, *Risala al-Ijaz*, 1107 AH; Sadaqat
    Muhammad Mah Ganjahi, *Thawaqib al-Manaqib*, 1126 AH; Hafiz Muhammad Hayat, *Tazkira-e Naushahi*,
    1146 AH; Pir Kamal b. Shaikh Usman Lahori, *Sahaif-e Qudsiya*, 1186 AH; Imam Bakhsh Barqandazi
    Jalandhari, *Mirat al-Ghafuri*, 1186 AH; Muhammad Ashraf Manjri, *Kanz al-Rahmat*, 1220 AH,
    printed Lahore 1330 AH; Ghulam Muhi al-Din Faqir Lahori, *Tashrif al-Fuqara*, about 1248 AH
    [OCR?]; and Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi, *Sharif al-Tawarikh*, 1355–1394 — "سہ جلد سات ہزار صفحات",
    three volumes, seven thousand pages, in manuscript in the author's own possession. All but the
    last two are said to be owned by Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi.
  - *Tazkira-e Naushahi* is described: by Hafiz Sayyid Muhammad Hayat Rabbani Naushahi Sahnpalvi
    (d. 1173 AH), composed 1146 AH, and "اصل میں رسالہ الاعجاز مولفہ مرزا احمد بیگ لاہوری کی مشروح
    نقل ہے" — really an annotated copy of Mirza Ahmad Beg Lahori's *Risala al-Ijaz* with some
    additions; it contains only the affairs of the Naushahia hazrats; four manuscripts are in
    Sharafat Naushahi's library and two in the Punjab University Library (p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 1).
  - A Persian chronogram closes the entry: "حضرت حاجی محمد پیر نوشہ گنج بخش / آنکہ شد مشہور در اہل
    جہاں اجلال او / کرد چوں آخر عروج از فرش بر عرش بریں / پیر فیاض است ہم اہل سخاوت سال او" (p. 72,
    folio 60).
- **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri** (شاہ عنایت قادری شطاری لاہوری — entry no. 34, pp. 75–76,
  folios 63 and 74):
  - Of Shah Raza Qadri Lahori's khalifas, of great rank, comprehensive in the exoteric and esoteric
    sciences (p. 75, folio 63).
  - **He left Kasur for Lahore after a quarrel with its ruler**: "اول سکونت ان کی قصبہ قصور میں تھی۔
    پھر بسبب اس کے کہ حسین خان حاکم قصور سے ان کی رنجیدگی ہو گئی۔ لاہور میں آ کر قیام پذیر ہوئے" —
    his first residence was qasba Kasur; then, because he fell out with **Husain Khan, the ruler of
    Kasur**, he came and settled at Lahore (p. 76, folio 74).
  - "ہنگامہ مشیخت ان کی زندگی تک گرم رہا" — the contest of shaykhhood stayed hot as long as he lived
    (p. 76, folio 74).
  - **Caste and family**: "حضرت قوم کے باغبان تھے۔ اولاد اب بھی ان کی لاہور میں ہے" — he was of the
    Baghban (gardener) caste, and his descendants are in Lahore still (p. 76, folio 74).
  - Death given by the author as ۱۱۴۱ھ [OCR?], with his radiant mazar in Lahore (p. 76, folio 74).
  - **The editor destroys that date.** "شاہ عنایت کا سال وفات کسی معاصر تذکرہ نویس نے نہیں لکھا۔ پہلی
    مرتبہ مفتی صاحب نے ہی ۱۱۴۱ھ بغیر کسی سند کے لکھ دیا۔ جسے بعد کے مؤرخ نقل کرتے رہے" — no
    contemporary tazkira-writer recorded Shah Inayat's death year; the Mufti was the first to write
    1141 AH, without any authority, and later historians went on copying it. It is wrong because a
    contemporary scholar with close relations to him, **Haji Muhammad Sharif Qasuri (d. 1153 AH)**,
    in a letter addressed to Shah Inayat quoting from one of his works, wrote "سلمہ اللہ تعالیٰ" after
    his name — the formula used of a living man — and impressed his seal "العبد الفقیر محمد شریف ۱۱۴۸
    ہجری". From this it is clear that Shah Inayat was alive as late as 1148 AH and died approximately
    after ۱۱۵۰ھ / ۱۷۳۷ء [OCR?] (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74, حاشیہ 1). The archive row's "c. 1728"
    is the very figure the editor shows to be an unsourced invention.
  - **His full name and teachers**: "حافظ ابوالمعارف محمد عنایت اللہ حنفی قادری شطاری", from his own
    works *Zail al-Aghlat* and *Ghayat al-Hawashi*; father's name Pir Muhammad; he studied the
    exoteric sciences with Maulana Sayyid Abu al-Nasr alias Sayyid Ilyas and Maulvi Abdul Hadi Lahori,
    took inner benefit from Shah Ali Raza Faruqi, great-grandson of the Mujaddid Alf-e Sani
    (d. 1141 AH / 1728 CE), and from Shaikh Muhammad Sultan Bukhari, and after 1111 AH attached
    himself to Shah Raza Qadri Shattari Lahori (d. 1118 AH / 1707 CE) (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74,
    حاشیہ 1).
  - **Sixteen works of his have been traced**, of which *Ghayat al-Hawashi* is the bulkiest; and see
    Arguments for the *dar al-harb* claim (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74, حاشیہ 1). Further reading:
    Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi, *Hayat-e Shah Inayat Qadri*.
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri** (شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری): appears as the head of a documented chain of
  succession — Shaikh Muhammad Fazil Qadri Batalvi (entry no. 35) was murid and khalifa of Shaikh
  Muhammad Afzal Kalanauri, who was murid of Shaikh Abu Muhammad Lahori, who was murid of Shaikh
  Muhammad Tahir Lahori (p. 76, folio 74). Three generations from his khanqah to the Batala darbar.
- **bari-imam** (شاہ لطیف بری, indirectly): the witness the editor used against the author's date for
  Shah Latif is here given a birth date — "حضرت سید حسن پشاوری کی ولادت جمادی الآخر ۱۰۲۳ھ/۱۶۱۴ء میں
  مٹھہ میں ہوئی" (p. 73, folio 61, حاشیہ 1) — which is consistent with his meeting Shah Latif in
  Pothohar in 1077 AH [OCR?] (p. 46, folio 34, chunk 003). Note that the printing details of Shah
  Muhammad Ghaus's *Risala* differ between the two chunks — see Doubts.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Mulla Shah Badakhshi / Shaikh Shah Muhammad** (entry no. 29): kunya Akhund, laqab **Lisan Allah**,
  father Mulla Abdi, birthplace mauza **Arkasa** in Badakhshan. He left home as a child seeking truth,
  reached Agra, then Lahore, and became Mian Mir's murid, outdoing his fellow murids in renunciation:
  he kept no servant, never cooked in his house, never lit a lamp at night, needed no floor-covering
  but a sack, did his zikr always with breath-retention, never slept in his whole life, never married,
  and habitually prayed the dawn prayer with the previous evening's ablution, saying that in his whole
  life he had needed neither of the two ritual baths "کیونکہ یہ دونوں غسل نکاح اور نیند سے متعلق ہیں"
  — because both relate to marriage and to sleep, and he had done neither (pp. 68–69, folios 56–57).
  He completed his training in seven years and settled in **Kashmir** at his pir's order; "کشمیر میں
  شیعہ لوگ ان سے سخت عداوت رکھتے تھے۔ مگر جب روبرو آتے تائب ہو جاتے" — in Kashmir the Shia held
  severe enmity toward him, but repented when they came face to face. He was a poet whose Persian
  diwan is among his best works, "اور ہر ایک شعر میں سے مضامین وحدت وجودی مترشح ہیں", every verse
  exuding themes of *wahdat al-wujud* (p. 69, folio 57). The editor gives his birth as 992 AH /
  1584 CE in Badakhshan and his arrival in Hindustan as 1023 AH / 1614 CE, and lists fifteen sources
  including Jahan Ara's *Sahibiya* (۱۰۵۱ھ [OCR?], manuscript owned by Professor Muhammad Aslam,
  Lahore), Fani Muhsin's *Dabistan-e Mazahib* 319–327, a French summary by M. A. de Kremer in the
  Journal Asiatique 1869, and a Punjab University PhD thesis by Zamurrad (pp. 69–70, folios 57–58).
- **Sayyid Hasan Peshawari Qadri Gilani** (entry no. 31): murid of his father Sayyid Abdullah Gilani.
  His ancestor Sayyid Mahmud migrated from Baghdad to "مٹھہ کے ملک" and stayed some years; on his
  death his father Sayyid Abdullah came and settled at Peshawar. Full genealogy to Abdul Qadir Jilani
  given. "تمام عمر انہوں نے عبادت و ریاضت و تلقین طلبا میں صرف کی" — his whole life was spent in
  worship, austerity and instructing students; he died in the year one thousand one hundred and
  fifteen and is buried at Peshawar (pp. 72–73, folios 60–61). The editor corrects him on three
  points: it was Sayyid Abdullah, known as **Sahabi**, and not Sayyid Mahmud, who came from Baghdad to
  Mittha; Sayyid Abdullah never went to Peshawar but stayed and died at Mittha; and "یہ شجرہ نسب مفتی
  صاحب نے غلط لکھا ہے", the Mufti wrote this genealogy wrongly, the correct chain being the one Shah
  Muhammad Ghaus himself recorded (p. 72, folio 60, حواشی 1–3, citing Amir Shah Qadri Gilani's
  *Tazkira-e Abdullah Sahabi*, Peshawar 1971, and *Tazkira-e Mashaikh-e Qadiriya Hasaniya* 26).
- **Shah Raza Qadri Shattari Lahori** (entry no. 32): a Lahore elder of knowledge and practice, whose
  command in the invocation of the divine names was "الکاحکم [OCR?] قطعی" — decisive, so that whatever
  he uttered came to pass; thousands of karamat appeared from him involuntarily. His chain runs back
  through Shaikh Muhammad Fazil Lahori, Shaikh Ilah Dad Qadri Akbarabadi, Shaikh Muhammad Jalal,
  Sayyid Nur, Sayyid Zain al-Abidin Husaini, Shaikh Abdul Ghafur and Shaikh Wajih al-Din Gujrati to
  Shah Muhammad Ghaus Gwaliari. Died 12 Jumada al-Awwal 1118 AH; "مزار شہر لاہور کے اندر ہے", his
  mazar is inside the city of Lahore (pp. 73, 75; folios 61, 63). The editor adds from Shah Raza's own
  *Irshad al-Ashiqin* (composed at Akbar Nagar, 1068 AH / 1657 CE) that his full name was Muhammad
  Raza b. Shaikh Muhammad Fazil b. Shaikh Asad Allah Lahori Qadri, that Muhammad Fazil was his
  **father** as well as his pir — which the Mufti failed to say — and that "شاہ رضا بھی اپنے والد کی
  طرح لاہور کے قاضی تھے", Shah Raza too, like his father, was the **qazi of Lahore** (p. 73, folio 61,
  حاشیہ 2, with Nuzhat al-Khawatir 6/305).
- **Sayyid Ahmad Shaikh al-Hind Gilani** (entry no. 33): came from Baghdad to Hind and "متصل وزیر آباد
  کے ایک گاؤں کٹلہ نام آباد کر کے سکونت اختیار کی" — founded and settled a village named Katla
  adjoining Wazirabad. Full genealogy to Abdul Qadir Jilani. Died 1136 AH and buried at the place
  Kotla. "ان کی اولاد موضع خان پور میں سکونت رکھتی ہے اور سلسلہ پیری کا اب تک جاری ہے" — his
  descendants live in mauza Khanpur and the line of pirship continues to this day (p. 75, folio 63).
- **Shaikh Muhammad Fazil Qadri Batalvi** (entry no. 35): among the greatest ulama and fuqara; his
  whole life spent in teaching, with thousands completed through him. Died 14 Zi al-Hijja ۱۱۵۱ھ
  [OCR?]; "مزار قصبہ بٹالہ [OCR?] میں ہے", his mazar is in qasba Batala (p. 76, folio 74). See
  Practices for his khanqah's expenditure. A modern publication on the shrine is cited: Muhi al-Din,
  *Darbar-e Qadiriya Faziliya ka Qartas al-Taaruf*, Lahore 1971 (p. 76, folio 74, حاشیہ 1).
- **Pir Muhammad Sachiar Naushahi** (entry no. 36): true khalifa of Nausha Ganj Bakhsh, who came to
  him as a child; titled "Sachiar" because he had no equal in truth and rectitude; so inclined to
  *wajd* and *sama* that "سماع و وجد کی حالت میں لوگوں کو حضرت کے مر جانے کا شبہہ ہو جاتا تھا",
  people would suspect he had died. After his pir's death he settled at mauza **Nausherah**, said in
  the text to be six miles east of Gujrat, and died in the year one thousand one hundred and
  fifty-two (pp. 76–77, folios 74–75). The editor corrects both figures: Nausherah is not six but
  ۱۴ [OCR?] miles from Gujrat, and "پیر محمد سچیار نوشاہیؒ کا سال وفات ۱۱۲۰ھ صحیح ہے اور مفتی صاحب
  نے ۱۱۵۲ھ غلط لکھا ہے" — 1120 AH is the correct death year and the Mufti's 1152 AH is wrong; his
  birth was ۱۰۱۳ھ [OCR?] (p. 77, folio 75, حواشی 1–2).
- **Shah Muhammad Ghaus Gilani Qadri Lahori** (entry no. 37, beginning): beloved son of Sayyid Hasan
  Peshawari; took khilafat and the khirqa of ijazat from his father, then toured all Hindustan seeking
  God and took faiz from many awliya, among them سید میراں بھیکہ [OCR?] چشتی, Abdul Ghafur
  Naqshbandi, and the khalifas of Nausha Ganj Bakhsh (p. 77, folio 75). The editor specifies these as
  Pir Muhammad Sachiar Naushervi (d. 1120 AH) and Nausha's grandson Sayyid Shah Ismat Allah Hamza
  Pahalwan Naushahi (d. 1137 AH), to whom he went for twelve years (p. 77, folio 75, حاشیہ 3). His
  eyewitness account of the demolitions is under Practices; it breaks off mid-sentence at the chunk
  edge.
- **Sayyid Shah Ismat Allah Hamza Pahalwan Naushahi**, d. 1137 AH, Nausha's grandson (p. 77,
  folio 75).
- **Haji Muhammad Sharif Qasuri**, d. 1153 AH, whose sealed letter of 1148 AH is the instrument of the
  Shah Inayat re-dating (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Sikh-period clearance outside Lahore's walls, told as eyewitness.** Shah Muhammad Ghaus's karamat
  are mostly famous, "مگر چشم دیدہ یہ ہے" — but the one seen with the eyes is this: after Ranjit
  Singh's death, when **Kharak Singh** succeeded to the government of Lahore, his son **Nau Nihal
  Singh**, who was the empowered ruler, ordered that the ground be cleared for four thousand paces
  outside the *fasil* of Lahore and a parade ("پریٹ") be laid out on all four sides of the city;
  houses were to be pulled down and trees cut, and "دلا روس فرنگی", a European named Dila Rus, was
  appointed to the work. He began demolishing houses. "حضرت کے مزار کی چار دیواری بھی گرا دی گئی اور
  درخت تمام کاٹے گئے" — the boundary wall of the Hazrat's mazar was also thrown down and all the
  trees were cut; and when [OCR?] the inner wall, the mazar's own enclosure, was reached… (p. 77,
  folio 75). The sentence ends at the chunk boundary; the outcome is in chunk 007.
- **A shrine built for lakhs and stripped for its stone.** Dara Shikoh spent several lakh rupees on
  Mulla Shah's rauza; Ranjit Singh had its stones entirely removed to Amritsar (p. 69, folio 57).
- **A khanqah's daily expenditure and a permanent langar.** Of Shaikh Muhammad Fazil Batalvi:
  "ہزاروں روپیہ یومیہ ان کے خانقاہ میں خرچ ہوتا اور لنگر عام جاری رہتا۔ یہ سب خرچ حضرت خزانہ غیب سے
  دیتے تھے" — thousands of rupees a day were spent at his khanqah and a general langar ran
  continuously, all of which he met from the unseen treasury (p. 76, folio 74). The largest financial
  figure anywhere in the chunks so far, and the book attributes its source to the miraculous rather
  than to any endowment.
- **A saint holding state office.** Shah Raza Qadri and his father before him were qazis of Lahore
  (p. 73, folio 61, حاشیہ 2).
- **A saint's quarrel with a local governor as the cause of a shrine's location.** Shah Inayat left
  Kasur for Lahore after falling out with Husain Khan, hakim of Kasur (p. 76, folio 74) — which is why
  his shrine stands in Lahore and not in Kasur.
- **Village formation inside a shrine enclosure**: Mian Mir Abad within Mulla Shah's mazar enclosure
  (p. 69, folio 57); and Katla/Kotla founded beside Wazirabad by Sayyid Ahmad Shaikh al-Hind (p. 75,
  folio 63).
- **Pirship as a continuing family line in the author's own day**: Sayyid Ahmad Shaikh al-Hind's
  descendants at Khanpur, where "سلسلہ پیری کا اب تک جاری ہے" (p. 75, folio 63); Shah Inayat's
  descendants in Lahore (p. 76, folio 74).
- **A shrine family as a documentary archive**: seven of the nine Naushahi sources, including a
  three-volume, seven-thousand-page manuscript history, were in Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi's own
  possession when the introduction was written, and the family's sajjada line published the saint's
  verse in 1975 (p. 71, folio 59).
- **Sama and wajd as shrine practice**, in two entries: Pir Muhammad Sachiar taken for dead in the
  state (p. 77, folio 75); Shah Sulaiman the same in the previous chunk (p. 67, folio 55).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Shah Chiragh's naming: his father's words on the day of his birth (p. 68, folio 56).
- Mulla Shah Badakhshi: a lifetime without sleep, and hence without ever needing a ritual bath
  (pp. 68–69, folios 56–57); and the Shias of Kashmir repenting on coming face to face with him
  (p. 69, folio 57).
- Mulla Shah versifying his own year of death while in the throes of it (p. 69, folio 57, حاشیہ 2) —
  reported by the editor as evidence, not as marvel.
- Nausha Ganj Bakhsh: a wali from birth; his father's seven Hajjs and vision of the Prophet (p. 70,
  folio 58); karamat without end, referred to *Tazkira-e Naushahi* (p. 71, folio 59).
- Shah Raza Qadri: whatever he spoke came to pass, and thousands of marvels appeared from him
  involuntarily (p. 73, folio 61).
- Shaikh Muhammad Fazil Batalvi: thousands of rupees a day found from the unseen treasury (p. 76,
  folio 74).
- Shah Muhammad Ghaus: his mazar's inner enclosure in the path of Nau Nihal Singh's demolition — the
  karamat the author offers as eyewitness, cut off at the chunk edge (p. 77, folio 75).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **A saint credited with the first *dar al-harb* ruling on India.** "شاہ عنایت قادری سب سے پہلے
  بزرگ ہیں جنہوں نے سب سے پہلے ہندوستان کے ہندو اکثریت والے صوبوں کو دار الحرب قرار دیا" — Shah
  Inayat Qadri is the first elder to declare the Hindu-majority provinces of Hindustan to be
  *dar al-harb*; the claim is the editor's, sourced to Shah Inayat's own *Zail al-Aghlat* and
  *Ghayat al-Hawashi* (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74, حاشیہ 1).
- **The editor's method at its sharpest**, three times in this chunk, each time with a different kind
  of evidence: a dying man's own chronogram plus a dated meeting, against Mulla Shah's 1069 AH
  (p. 69, folio 57, حاشیہ 2); a family historian's research, against Nausha's 1103 AH (p. 71,
  folio 59, حاشیہ 2); and a sealed letter using a formula reserved for the living, against Shah
  Inayat's 1141 AH (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74, حاشیہ 1). In the last he also states the mechanism
  of error: the Mufti wrote a year with no authority and later historians copied it.
- On Mulla Shah's date he distinguishes the weight of witnesses: "سال وفات ۱۰۷۲ھ میں کوئی معاصر
  اختلاف نہیں ہے۔ متاخرین میں اختلاف پایا جاتا ہے جو چنداں اہم نہیں" — there is no disagreement among
  contemporaries about 1072 AH; disagreement is found among later writers, which is of no great
  importance (p. 69, folio 57, حاشیہ 2).
- He also identifies a derivative source: *Tazkira-e Naushahi*, the author's own authority for
  Nausha's karamat, is "اصل میں" an annotated copy of an earlier risala (p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 1).
- And he notes a scribal error in the printed text itself: "متن میں نادشاہ لکھا ہے جو کتابت کی غلطی
  ہے" (p. 70, folio 58, حاشیہ 1).

## Citable passages

## Doubts

- **ranmal-sharif — the place does not match the archive row.** The editor states that Nausha Ganj
  Bakhsh's mazar is "اس وقت ساہنپال شریف ضلع گجرات پنجاب میں", at present at Sahnpal Sharif in
  district Gujrat (p. 71, folio 59), and the *Tazkira-e Naushahi*'s author carries the nisba
  "ساہنپالوی" (p. 71, folio 59, حاشیہ 1). The archive row places the shrine at Ranmal Sharif, Phalia.
  The **figure** is beyond doubt — Nausha Ganj Bakhsh, founder of the Naushahia Qadiri order, whose
  corrected death year 1064 AH matches the row's 1654 — but the **site** stated here is a different
  village, and nothing in the chunk names Ranmal or Phalia. This must be resolved before the shrine
  row cites this book on location.
- **ranmal-sharif — the name and the descent.** The entry calls him "شیخ حاجی محمد قادری", Shaikh Haji
  Muhammad, where the archive row has "Syed Muhammad Noushah Ganj Bakhsh"; the chunk gives him no
  sayyid descent, and his pir Shah Sulaiman's family is claimed as Qureshi by the editor (p. 67,
  folio 55, chunk 005). "Syed" is not supported anywhere in this chunk.
- **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri — the archive's date is the one the editor rejects.** The author's
  unsourced 1141 AH [OCR?] against the editor's "alive in 1148 AH, died approximately after 1150 AH /
  1737 CE [OCR?]" (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74). Note also that the editor's own footnote uses
  1141 AH / 1728 CE for a *different* man, Shah Ali Raza Faruqi, in the same paragraph — so the two
  1141s must not be conflated.
- **The folio markers break in this chunk.** [p. 75] is marked [folio 63], as the +12 offset requires,
  but [p. 76] is marked **[folio 74]** and [p. 77] **[folio 75]**, where 64 and 65 are expected. Either
  the transcriber read ۶۴/۶۵ as ۷۴/۷۵ or the printed pagination jumps eleven pages. Page references in
  this chunk's last two pages should be cited by PDF page, and the folio treated as unverified.
- **Entry no. 34 therefore spans two differently-numbered folios**: its opening line is on p. 75
  (folio 63) and its body on p. 76 (folio 74). Do not read the gap as missing text.
- **Mulla Shah's death year: 1069 AH in the text, 1072 AH per the editor**, with a dated meeting in
  1071 AH as the hinge (p. 69, folio 57). His birth 992 AH / 1584 CE and arrival 1023 AH / 1614 CE are
  the editor's, not the author's.
- **Sayyid Hasan Peshawari's Risala is cited two ways across chunks.** "مطبوعہ پشاور ۱۲۸۴ھ" at p. 46,
