# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 015 — pp. 158–167

**The Naqshbandi chapter ends and the fourth opens: "چوتھا چمن — خاندان سُہروردیہ کے مشائخ کے تذکرہ
وغیرہ میں", the fourth garden, on the mashaikh of the Suhrawardi family** (p. 158, folio 146). Entries
100 (conclusion) to 106 — and this is the richest chunk of the eight for the archive: **five rows take
material**, four of them substantially, and three are the Multan and Uch shrines themselves. It also
contains the book's single most valuable economic passage (a seven-way partition of Baha al-Din
Zakariya's estate with a cash figure), two saints intervening in state violence, and the editor **naming
himself**.

**The folio markers agree with the relation on every page**: folio = PDF index − 12, p. 158 → folio 146
through p. 167 → folio 155, each marker reading exactly that.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (شیخ الشیوخ بہاء الدین زکریا قریشی ملتانی — entry no. 101, pp. 158–161,
  folios 146–149):
  - **Family arrival at Multan**: "پہلے ان کے جدّ بزرگوار کمال الدین علی شاہ قریشی مکہ معظمہ سے خوارزم
    میں آئے۔ وہاں سے ملتان میں آکر قیام کیا" — his grandfather **Kamal al-Din Ali Shah Qureshi came from
    Mecca to Khwarazm, and from there to Multan and settled**. His father **Shaikh Wajih al-Din** was born
    in that house and married the daughter of **Maulana Husam al-Din Tirmizi, who resided at mauza Kot
    Karor** (p. 160, folio 148).
  - **Birth, disputed**: the text says he was born in Wajih al-Din's house in the year **578 AH**
    ("پانچ سو اٹھتر"), and the chronogram gives ۵۷۸ھ twice (pp. 160–161, folios 148–149). The editor:
    **"حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانیؒ ۵۶۶ھ / ۱۱۷۰ء میں کوٹ کہروڑ (ملتان) میں پیدا ہوئے"** — born in
    **566 AH / 1170 CE at Kot Kahror (Multan)** (Nur Ahmad Faridi, *Tazkira-e Hazrat Baha al-Din Zakariya
    Multani* p. 6, on Sayyid Jalal Bukhari's authority) (p. 158, folio 146, حاشیہ 1). **Twelve years
    apart, and a birthplace the text does not give.**
  - **Education and itinerary**: Quran memorised at twelve; after his father's death, **Bukhara** for
    sarf, nahw, hadith and tafsir; then the **Haramain**, with **five years at Medina** taking faiz from
    many ulama and mashaikh; then **Bait al-Maqdis**; then **Baghdad**, where he took bai'at from Shaikh
    Shihab al-Din Umar Suhrawardi and **"اٹھارہ روز کے عرصہ میں خرقہ خلافت حاصل کیا اور ملتان کو مامور
    ہوئے"**, received the khirqa of khilafat **in eighteen days** and **was posted to Multan** (p. 160,
    folio 148). The eighteen days are given as a mark of distinction.
  - **Scale and standing at Multan**: "ملتان میں آکر **شاہان وقت** اور خاص و عام حضرت کی بیعت میں داخل
    ہوئے اور دولت ظاہری و باطنی ان کے نصیب ہوئی۔ **ہزاروں خادم حضرت کی بارگاہ فلک پایگاہ پر حاضر رہتے**"
    — the kings of the time and high and low alike entered his bai'at; outward and inward wealth was his
    portion; and **thousands of attendants were present at his sky-high court**. And "لاکھوں آدمی حضرت سے
    مستفید طریقہ سہروردیہ ہوئے", **lakhs** benefited from the Suhrawardi way through him (p. 160,
    folio 148).
  - **Office under the state, and a side taken in a civil war**: **"حضرت کے سلطان شمس الدین التمش کے ساتھ
    اچھے تعلقات تھے۔ سلطان قباچہ کے مقابلہ میں آپ نے التمش کی حمایت کی تھی اور التمش کی درخواست پر شیخ
    الاسلام کا عہدہ بھی قبول کر لیا تھا"** — he had good relations with **Sultan Shams al-Din Iltutmish**,
    **supported Iltutmish against Sultan Qabacha**, and **at Iltutmish's request accepted the office of
    Shaikh al-Islam** (Khaliq Ahmad Nizami, *Salatin-e Dehli ke Mazhabi Rujhanat* 113, 116, 205[OCR?])
    (p. 159, folio 147). Set beside Muhammad Masum's refusal of Aurangzeb's court (chunk 012) and Saif
    al-Din's residence inside his fort (chunk 013), this is the archive's clearest case of a shrine holding
    a formal state office.
  - **Death, disputed**: text and chronogram both give **666 AH** ("سال چھ سو چھیاسٹھ"; ۶۶۶ھ twice),
    "اور ملتان میں مدفون ہوئے", and he was buried at Multan (pp. 160–161, folios 148–149). The editor:
    **"حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانیؒ نے ۶۶۱ھ میں وصال فرمایا۔ سال وفات میں اختلاف ہے۔ لطائف اشرفی
    ۶۶۶ھ، اخبار الاخیار ۶۶۱ھ، گلزار ابرار ۶۶۶ھ [OCR?]، لیکن ان میں ہمارے نزدیک صحیح ۶۶۱ھ ہے"** — he
    passed away in **661 AH**; there is disagreement — *Lataif-e Ashrafi* 666, *Akhbar al-Akhyar* 661,
    *Gulzar-e Abrar* 666[OCR?] — but in our view **661 AH** is correct (p. 159, folio 147, حاشیہ 1).
    **The archive row carries 1267 CE, which corresponds to 666 AH — the reading the editor rejects.
    Record both: author 666 AH / editor 661 AH.**
  - **His writings, and an attribution settled from a dated manuscript**: one book, ***Kitab al-Awrad***,
    survives, commented on by **Maulana Ali bin Ahmad Ghori, a murid of Shah Rukn-e Alam**, as *Kanz
    al-Ibad fi Sharh al-Awrad*; a copy **written at Samarqand in 854 AH** in the hand of Shams al-Din
    Ahmad bin Maulana Sadr al-Din is in the annotator's own library. From it, **"ڈاکٹر مولوی محمد شفیع
    کے اس شبہہ کی تردید ہوتی ہے کہ یہ اوراد شیخ شہاب الدین سہروردی کے ہیں"** — Dr. Maulvi Muhammad
    Shafi's doubt, that these *awrad* are Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi's, is refuted, since the commentator
    "صاف طور پر بہاء الحق والشرع والدین لکھا ہے", plainly wrote *Baha al-Haqq wal-Shar wal-Din*. The note
    even records an interlinear gloss: "پیشِ نظر نسخہ میں 'بہار' کے نیچے 'تابندہ' لکھا ہوا ہے", in the
    copy before us "تابندہ" is written under "بہار" (pp. 158–159, folios 146–147, حاشیہ 1).
  - **His malfuzat**: ***Khulasat al-Arifin*** (Urdu translation printed Lahore 1909) and an unknown,
    unpublished *Malfuzat* which was in the library of the late Maulvi Muhammad Shams al-Din;
    "ملک لطیف مرحوم نے رسالہ صحیفہ لاہور ۱۹۷۰ء [OCR?] میں شائع کر دیا تھا"، the late Malik Latif
    published it in the journal *Sahifa*, Lahore, 1970[OCR?], and the copy is now in Malik Sahib's library
    (p. 159, folio 147, حاشیہ 1).
  - **A thirteen-item bibliography**, the richest for any figure in these chunks, and notable for its
    range: **Dara Shukoh's *Safinat al-Auliya*** 113, 115; *Fawaid al-Fawad*; *Akhbar al-Akhyar* 26, 28;
    *Lataif-e Ashrafi* 1/383–384; Sharaf al-Din Qureshi, *Jami al-Karamat* Urdu trans. Sayyid Farzand Ali,
    **Bahawalpur 1915**; Abd al-Qadir Thatthvi, *Hadiqat al-Auliya* 1016 AH ed. Sayyid Husam al-Din
    Rashidi, **Sindhi Adabi Board, Hyderabad Sindh, 1967**; Shamim Zaidi, *Ahwal wa Athar*, printed
    **Rawalpindi**; and Makhdum Muhammad Hashim Thatthvi's *Bayaz*, ms in the **Ganj Bakhsh library,
    Markaz-e Tahqiqat-e Farsi Iran wa Pakistan, Rawalpindi** (pp. 159–160, folios 147–148).
  - **His chain**, from the chapter opening: the Suhrawardiya "آغاز اس سلسلہ عالیہ کا حضرت سید الطائفہ
    جنید بغدادی سے ہے", begins with **Junaid Baghdadi**, and runs through Mamshad Alu Dinawari,
    Rudaim[OCR?], Ali Rudayari[OCR?], Ahmad Aswad Dinawari, Abu Abdullah Khafif, Abu al-Qasim Gurgani, Abu
    Bakr Nassaj, **Shaikh Ahmad Ghazali, brother of Imam Muhammad Ghazali**, Ziya al-Din Abu al-Najib
    Suhrawardi and Wajih al-Din Suhrawardi to Shihab al-Din Umar Suhrawardi, and then to him (p. 158,
    folio 146).
  - **His seven sons and the division of his estate** are under Practices — the fullest financial passage
    in these chunks, and evidence about this shrine's wealth at the founder's death.
- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** (شیخ رکن الدین ابو الفتح بن شیخ صدر الدین عارف بن شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا
  ملتانی — entry no. 105, pp. 164–166, folios 152–154):
  - **His mother named and titled**: **"حضرت کی والدہ کا نام بی بی راستی تھا جو اپنے وقت میں رابعہ وقت
    تھیں"** — his mother's name was **Bibi Rasti**, who was "the Rabia of her time" (p. 166, folio 154).
    The only woman in these chunks given a title of her own.
  - **The pregnancy prophecy**: during her pregnancy, when Bibi Rasti came into Baha al-Din Zakariya's
    presence "وہ تعظیم کو اٹھ کھڑے ہوتے", he would stand up in respect; asked why, "بخلاف عادت بزرگان",
    contrary to the custom of elders, he said **"یہ تعظیم تیرے واسطے نہیں بلکہ اس شخص کے واسطے ہے جو
    تیرے بطن عفت میں ہے کہ وہ ایک آفتاب اوج ولایت و ماہتاب برج شرافت ہو گا"** — this respect is not for
    you but for the one in your chaste womb, who will be a sun at the zenith of wilayat and a moon in the
    constellation of nobility (p. 166, folio 154).
  - **Succession and wealth**: on his father's death he sat on the masnad-e irshad, "حضرت کو دولت ظاہری
    و باطنی نصیب ہوئی۔ **سلاطین وقت حضرت کی خدمت کو اپنا فخر جانتے تھے**" — outward and inward wealth was
    his portion, and **the sultans of the time counted attendance on him their pride** (p. 166,
    folio 154). The editor gives a tenure figure from *Lataif-e Ashrafi*: **"و اقام فی سجادہ جدہ لبعد
    ابیہ اثنتین و خمسین سنۃ"** — he sat on **his grandfather's sajjada, after his father, for fifty-two
    years** (p. 165, folio 153, حاشیہ 1).
  - **His popular title**: "دنیا اور دین کا حاجت مند جو ان کی خدمت میں آتا خالی نہ جاتا۔ **اس لیے لوگ ان
    کو قبلہ حاجات کہتے تھے**" — no one needy of world or religion went away empty from him, **and therefore
    people called him *Qibla-e Hajat***, the qibla of needs (p. 166, folio 154).
  - **A barefoot intercession against a massacre**: **"ملتان میں کشلو خان نے بغاوت کی اور محمد تغلق نے
    اسے شکست دے کر ملتان میں خون کی ندیاں بہانے کا حکم دیا تو حضرت شاہ رکن عالم ننگے پاؤں بادشاہ کے پاس
    سفارش کے لیے گئے"** — when **Kishlu Khan rebelled at Multan and Muhammad Tughluq**, having defeated
    him, **ordered rivers of blood to flow in Multan, Shah Rukn-e Alam went barefoot to the emperor to
    intercede** (Isami, *Futuh al-Salatin* p. 443) (p. 165, folio 153, حاشیہ 1). The most consequential
    act by any saint in these chunks.
  - **An earlier sultan's devotion and offering**: **"سلطان علاؤ الدین خلجی آپ کا بڑا معتقد تھا۔ حضرت
    اس کی زندگی میں دہلی بھی گئے … سلطان نے نذر پیش کی"** — Sultan **Ala al-Din Khalji** was greatly
    devoted to him; the Hazrat went to Delhi in his lifetime; **the sultan presented a *nazr*** (Jamali,
    *Sair al-Arifin* part 2 p. 3[OCR?]) (p. 165, folio 153, حاشیہ 1). A recorded royal offering to this
    shrine's saint.
  - **His death, ordered and staged**: three months beforehand he shut himself in a *hujra* day and night
    "سوائے ادائے نماز پنجگانہ فریضہ کے باہر نہ آتے", emerging only for the five obligatory prayers; then,
    **after the Asr prayer on 16 Rajab 735 AH**, he called **Maulana Zahir al-Din** and "حکم دیا کہ سامان
    تجہیز و تکفین مہیا کرے", ordered him to prepare the shrouding and burial apparatus; then **led the
    Maghrib prayer as imam and died in the prostration of the last rak'a of the supererogatory prayers**
    (p. 166, folio 154). The editor's source gives a different date: see Arguments.
  - **Childless, and the sajjada passed sideways**: **"حضرت لاولد تھے۔ ان کی وفات کے بعد محمد اسماعیل ان
    کے بھائی کی اولاد سجادہ نشین ہوئی۔ روضہ منورہ حضرت کا ملتان میں زیارت گاہ خلق ہے"** — the Hazrat was
    **childless**; after his death **the progeny of Muhammad Ismail, his brother, became sajjada nashin**,
    and his illuminated rauza at **Multan** is a place of pilgrimage (p. 166, folio 154). A named
    collateral succession, and the archive's clearest statement of how this sajjada descended.
  - **Five khalifas named**: Maulana Zahir al-Din, **Sultan Hamid al-Din Hakim**, Shaikh Fakhr al-Din
    Iraqi, **Makhdum Jahaniyan Jahangasht Bukhari** and Shaikh Sama al-Din Dehlvi (p. 165, folio 153,
    حاشیہ 1).
  - **His teachings survive only at second hand**, in his khalifas' works — *Fatawa-e Sufiya* by Fazl
    Allah bin Ayyub, *Majma al-Akhbar*, and the *Kanz al-Ibad* commentary — plus "آپ کے ملفوظات کا ایک
    خطی نسخہ پروفیسر قریشی احمد حسین احمد صاحب گجرات کے کتب خانہ میں ہے", a manuscript of his malfuzat in
    Professor Qureshi Ahmad Husain Ahmad's library at **Gujrat** (p. 165, folio 153, حاشیہ 1).
  - **A thirteen-item bibliography** including Barni's *Tarikh-e Firoz Shahi* (Urdu trans. Dr. Muin
    al-Haq, eight page references), Isami's *Futuh al-Salatin* **Madras 1948**, and Nur Ahmad Khan
    Faridi's *Tazkira-e Shah Rukn-e Alam*, **Multan 1961** (p. 165, folio 153).
- **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari** (سید جلال الدین شیر شاہ المخاطب بہ میر
  سُرخ بخاری اوچی — entry no. 103, pp. 162–163, folios 150–151):
  - **He is Baha al-Din Zakariya's khalifa**, "سید صحیح النسب جامع سیادت و نجابت", a sayyid of sound
    lineage; and **"ہندوستان جنت نشان میں تمام سادات و مشائخ و امرا و سلاطین بسبب صحت و نسب ان کے ان سے
    باادب پیش آتے"** — in Hindustan all the sayyids, mashaikh, amirs and sultans treated him with respect
    on account of the soundness of his lineage (p. 162, folio 150). Precedence resting on pedigree rather
    than on office or wealth.
  - **A ten-link genealogy to Imam Muhammad Taqi** is given by name (p. 162, folio 150), and with it the
    branching of two lineages: **"سید علی اصغر، ساتواں جد حضرت کا دو صاحبزادہ سید عبد اللہ و سید اسماعیل
    رکھتا تھا اور سید عبد اللہ کی اولاد سے خاندان سادات بخاری اور سید اسماعیل کی اولاد سے خاندان سادات
    بھاکھری [OCR?] ظاہر ہوا"** — Sayyid Ali Asghar, his seventh ancestor, had two sons, Abdullah and
    Ismail; **from Abdullah's line came the Bukhari sayyids and from Ismail's the Bhakkari[OCR?] sayyids**.
  - **Five sons, with their mothers named**: on the authority of "روایات صحیحہ", sound reports — (1) Sayyid
    Ali and (2) Sayyid Jafar, both **by the daughter of the king of Bukhara**, who "اپنے نانا کے پاس
    پرورش پائی", were brought up with their maternal grandfather, Jafar settling in Bukhara and never
    coming to Hind; (3) **Sayyid Ahmad Kabir**, by **Bibi Fatima, daughter of Sayyid Badr al-Din
    Bhakkari[OCR?]**; (4) Sayyid Sadr al-Din Muhammad Ghaus; (5) **Sayyid Baha al-Din Ahmad, famous as
    Muhammad Masum**, by **Bibi Tahira Sayyida** — "یہ پانچویں فرزند حضرت کے پنج بنائے اسلام کی طرح صاحب
    مقامات عالیہ تھے", these five sons were of lofty station like the five pillars of Islam (p. 163,
    folio 151). Three named wives, a royal marriage, and a recorded branch that stayed in Central Asia.
  - **Dates and grave**: "ولادت باسعادت حضرت کی سنہ پانچ سو پچانوے اور وفات انیسویں جمادی الاول سنہ چھ سو
    نوے میں واقع ہوئی اور **مزار اوچ میں ہے**" — born in the year **595**, died **19 Jumada al-Awwal 690**,
    **mazar at Uch** (p. 163, folio 151), with a chronogram carrying ۶۹۰ھ twice. **690 AH corresponds to
    the archive row's 1291 CE**, so this row's date is corroborated and, unusually for this book, the
    editor adds no correcting footnote.
  - **The source for his early life is named**: "صاحب مظہر جلالی لکھتا ہے", the author of ***Mazhar-e
    Jalali*** writes (p. 162, folio 150) — a title the editor does not gloss. His childhood miracle is
    under Legends.
- **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht** (مخدوم جہانیاں جہاں گشت بخاری — editor's footnotes, pp. 161,
  165; folios 149, 153):
  - **He is named as one of Shah Rukn-e Alam's "نامور خلفا"**, famous khalifas, alongside Maulana Zahir
    al-Din, Sultan Hamid al-Din Hakim, Shaikh Fakhr al-Din Iraqi and Shaikh Sama al-Din Dehlvi (p. 165,
    folio 153, حاشیہ 1).
  - **He is quoted as an authority on teaching practice at the Multan khanqah**: "بقول حضرت مخدوم
    جہانیانؒ **'شیخ العارف ہر مبتدی و منتہی کو بلا کسی امتیاز کے تعلیم دیتے تھے'**" — on Makhdum
    Jahaniyan's authority, "Shaikh al-Arif taught every beginner and every advanced student **without any
    distinction**" (p. 161, folio 149, حاشیہ 1). The chunk gives nothing about his own shrine at Uch.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** (حضرت فرید پاک پٹنی — entry no. 101, p. 160, folio 148):
  - **He is shown as a guest at Multan, and gently rebuked.** "ایک روز حضرت فرید پاک پٹنی ملتان میں خواجہ
    بہاء الدین کے پاس تشریف رکھتے تھے" — one day Hazrat Farid of Pak Pattan was staying with Khwaja Baha
    al-Din at Multan; asked how far his striving had brought him, he answered that if he spoke the word
    the chairs they sat on would fly; the chairs rose, and a hand was laid on them with "یہ گفتگو بسبیل
    تذکرہ تھی نہ بسبیل ارشاد۔ تم اسی جگہ پر قائم رہو", this talk was by way of conversation, not by way of
    command; stay where you are. **Which of the two saints boasts and which restrains the chairs is not
    unambiguous in the text: see Doubts.** The episode names Baba Farid as "پاک پٹنی" — of Pak Pattan —
    which is how the book fixes him to the archive's site.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Shaikh Mahmud Shah Naqshbandi Mujaddidi Lahori** (entry no. 100 concluded, p. 158, folio 146):
  "گوشہ عبادت و ریاضت میں گوشہ نشین رہے", he lived secluded in a corner of worship and austerity;
  **"آخر سال ایک ہزار دو سو اسی ہجری میں فوت ہوئے اور متصل مقبرہ جھولن شاہ کے مدفون ہوا اور روضہ منورہ
  ان کا زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے"** — he died in the year **1280 AH** and was buried **adjoining the maqbara
  of Jhulan Shah**, and his illuminated rauza is a place of pilgrimage. A second Lahore grave fixed by
  reference to a neighbouring shrine, as Haji Ramzan's was to Tahir Bandagi's in chunk 011. See Doubts on
  the year.
- **Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif, son of Baha al-Din Zakariya** (entry no. 102, pp. 161–162, folios 149–150):
  son and janishin, who sat on the masnad-e irshad after his father and brought thousands to their
  destination. The estate division is under Practices. Died **23 Zi al-Hijja 684 AH**; **mazar in
  Multan**. The editor adds that, like his father, he combined "درس و تدریس" with guidance, that
  ***Tasrif-e Jadwali*** is his composition (*al-Durr al-Manzum* 28), and that **Shaikh Ziya al-Din
  collected his malfuzat as *Kunuz al-Fawaid*** (extracts in *Akhbar al-Akhyar* 59–61, **Meerut
  1277 AH**) (p. 161, folio 149, حاشیہ 1). Four death years are in play: see Arguments. **He has no
  archive row though his mazar is at Multan.**
- **Shaikh Ahmad Mashuq-e Ilahi** (entry no. 104, pp. 163–164, folios 151–152): khalifa of Sadr al-Din
  Arif. **"اس کا باپ میر قندھاری شہر قندھار میں دکان سوداگری کرتا"** — his father, **Mir Qandhari, kept a
  merchant's shop in the city of Qandahar**, and, finding the son unsatisfactory, "اپنے پاس سے کچھ مایہ
  دے کر نکال دیا", gave him some capital and turned him out to open a shop elsewhere; **so he came from
  Qandahar to Multan and set up a trading shop**. Sadr al-Din's gaze fell on him in the street; summoned
  to the **khanqah**, he was given "پس ماندہ شربت", the **leftover sherbet** from the Shaikh's own cup,
  and on drinking it the *nasut* and *malakut* were opened to him. He became a murid at once,
  **"دکان کا سامان اسی وقت غربا و فقرا میں تقسیم کر دیا اور سوائے ایک تہبند کے کچھ باقی نہ رکھا"** —
  distributed his shop's stock to the poor and faqirs that very hour and kept nothing but a loincloth,
  served seven years and received his title. His confrontation with the ulama is under Practices and
  Legends. Died in the year **723**[OCR?]; **mazar in Multan**, and he too has no archive row.
- **Shaikh Hamid al-Din Abu al-Hakim Qureshi Hakkari Suhrawardi** (entry no. 106, opening, p. 167,
  folio 155): khalifa of Rukn al-Din Multani, **titled Sultan al-Tarikin**, Sultan of the Renouncers; a
  genealogy of thirteen links to **Abu Sufyan bin Harith**, a Companion of the Prophet. **"ان کا جدّ
  بزرگوار قطب الدین خطہ کیچ و مقران کا بادشاہ تھا"** — his great ancestor Qutb al-Din was **king of the
  region of Kech and Makran**; and **"نانا ان کا سید احمد تختہ [OCR?] لاہور ہی مقتدائے زمانہ و پیشوائے
  اہل طریقت تھا۔ جن کا حال چھٹے باب میں تحریر ہو گا"**, his maternal grandfather **Sayyid Ahmad
  Takhta[OCR?] of Lahore** was an exemplar of the age, **whose account will be written in the sixth
  chapter**. **"شیخ حمید الدین نے سلطنت ظاہری ترک کر کے خرقہ فقر کا پہنا"** — he **renounced outward
  kingship** and put on the khirqa of faqr. His birth date, on the authority of Shaikh Shahr Allah's
  *Tazkira-e Hamidiya*, begins "بتاریخ بارہویں ربیع الاول" and the entry breaks off at the chunk edge.
- **The *Tazkira-e Hamidiya* and its transmission** (editor's footnote, p. 167, folio 155): the
  composition of **Shaikh Shahr Allah bin Rahmat Allah bin Sataji[OCR?] bin Kalu Langah Multani**,
  "جو سلطان سکندر لودھی ف ۹۲۳ھ / ۱۵۱۷ء کے عہد میں لکھی گئی", written in the era of **Sultan Sikandar
  Lodhi (d. 923 AH / 1517 CE)**; **Pir Ghulam Dastgir Nami** published its Urdu translation from Lahore
  in **1959**.
- **Bibi Rasti** (p. 166, folio 154), **Bibi Fatima** and **Bibi Tahira Sayyida** (p. 163, folio 151) —
  four named women in two entries, three of them as mothers of named saints and one with a title of her
  own. **Jhulan Shah** (p. 158, folio 146), whose Lahore maqbara serves as a landmark. **Maulana Zahir
  al-Din** (p. 166, folio 154), the khalifa who prepared Rukn-e Alam's burial.
- **Places named**: **Multan** throughout; **Kot Kahror** and **Kot Karor** (pp. 158, 160; folios 146,
  148); **Uch** (p. 163, folio 151); **Pak Pattan** (p. 160, folio 148); **Kech and Makran** (p. 167,
  folio 155); **Qandahar**, **Bukhara**, **Khwarazm**, **Samarqand**, **Mecca**, **Medina**, **Bait
  al-Maqdis**, **Baghdad**, **Delhi**, **Lahore**, **Gujrat**, **Bahawalpur**, **Hyderabad (Sindh)**,
  **Rawalpindi**, **Meerut**, **Madras** (pp. 158–167).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A shrine estate divided seven ways, with a cash figure — the most valuable economic passage in these
  chunks.** "شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانیؒ کے **سات بیٹے** تھے" — Baha al-Din Zakariya had **seven sons**,
  named as Sadr al-Din Arif, Burhan al-Din, Ziya al-Din, Ala al-Din, Shihab al-Din, Qudwat al-Din and
  Shams al-Din — and **"ان ساتوں نے بعد وفات باپ کے باپ کا ترکہ نقد و جنس آپس میں بحصص مساوی تقسیم کیا
  تو شیخ صدر الدین عارف کے حصہ میں ستر لاکھ تنکہ سرخ نقد سوائے اور اسباب پوشیدنی و زیور وغیرہ کے آیا"**
  — the seven divided their father's estate, **cash and kind, in equal shares**, and **Sadr al-Din
  Arif's share came to seventy lakh red tankas in cash**, besides clothing, jewellery and the rest
  (p. 162, folio 150). One seventh of one Suhrawardi shaykh's liquid estate, named in a named currency.
- **And divested the same day, with a stated reason.** "شیخ نے اسی روز وہ تمام نقد و جنس خدا کے واسطے
  غربا و مساکین کو دے ڈالا ایک خرمہرہ اس میں سے نہ لیا" — he gave the whole of it away to the poor and
  destitute that same day and kept not one cowrie. Reproached by well-wishers that "اس قدر دولت بے انتہا
  کو جو تم نے ایک روز میں برباد کر دیا ہے۔ اچھا نہیں کیا", he laughed and answered: **"میرے والد نے جو اس
  قدر خزانے جمع کیے اور خدا کے نام پر بھی دیا۔ وہ دنیا پر غالب تھے۔ اس قدر دولت کے جمع ہوتے نے ان کو
  کبھی خدا سے غافل نہ کیا اور میں ابھی اس رتبہ کو نہیں پہنچا۔ ڈرتا ہوں کہ یہ دنیا مجھ کو خدا سے غافل نہ
  کر دے"** — my father amassed such treasures and also gave in God's name; he was master over the world,
  and the amassing never made him heedless of God; I have not yet reached that rank, and I fear this
  world may make me heedless (p. 162, folio 150). **The book states plainly that the founder both
  accumulated and disbursed, and that his son judged accumulation a spiritual risk.**
- **A merchant's stock liquidated on conversion**, in the same chapter and the same city: Shaikh Ahmad
  gave away his whole shop and kept a single *tahband* (p. 164, folio 152). Two divestments in two
  entries, one of an inheritance and one of a business.
- **A saint in a state office, and two saints intervening in state violence.** Baha al-Din Zakariya
  accepted the post of **Shaikh al-Islam** at Iltutmish's request and backed him against Qabacha (p. 159,
  folio 147); Shah Rukn-e Alam went **barefoot** to Muhammad Tughluq to have a massacre of Multan called
  off (p. 165, folio 153); and Ala al-Din Khalji presented him a **nazr** (p. 165, folio 153). The
  Suhrawardi chapter's relation to the state is the opposite of the Naqshbandi chapter's: office accepted
  rather than refused, offerings received rather than declined.
- **A sajjada's tenure measured in years, and passed to a brother's line.** Rukn-e Alam "اقام فی سجادہ
  جدہ لبعد ابیہ اثنتین و خمسین سنۃ", sat fifty-two years on his **grandfather's** sajjada (p. 165,
  folio 153); and being childless, the seat passed to the descendants of his brother **Muhammad Ismail**
  (p. 166, folio 154). Two facts the archive can use directly: the seat is identified with the founder,
  not the incumbent, and the succession was collateral.
- **Teaching without rank at the Multan khanqah.** "شیخ العارف ہر مبتدی و منتہی کو بلا کسی امتیاز کے
  تعلیم دیتے تھے" — Shaikh al-Arif taught every beginner and advanced student without distinction
  (p. 161, folio 149) — quoted from Makhdum Jahaniyan, and the only statement in these chunks about a
  khanqah's teaching *policy* rather than its existence.
- **An eighteen-day khilafat.** Baha al-Din Zakariya received the khirqa at Baghdad "اٹھارہ روز کے عرصہ
  میں" and was posted to Multan (p. 160, folio 148) — a posting, like Nur Muhammad's to Mahar
  (chunk 011), Sulaiman's to Taunsa (chunk 011), Jan Allah's to Lahore (chunk 009) and Rauf Ahmad's to
  Bhopal (chunk 014).
- **A fatwa of *tazir* against a saint for abandoning prayer, and its collapse.** "علمائے وقت نے ترک نماز
  کے سبب سے اس سے ناراض ہو کر اس پر فتویٰ لکھا کہ **یہ واجب التعزیر ہے**" — the ulama of the time,
  displeased at his abandonment of prayer, wrote a fatwa that he was **liable to corporal punishment**;
  at the inquiry he offered to pray but not to recite Sura Fatiha, then to recite the whole sura but not
  "ایاک نعبد و ایاک نستعین"; the ulama refused both concessions; and after the wudu and the first
  attempt at the verse (see Legends) "علما نے ان کو معذور سمجھ کر معاف کر رکھا", the ulama held him
  excused and pardoned him (pp. 163–164, folios 151–152). **The third ulama-versus-shrine confrontation
  in these eight chunks** — after Shah Kaku's suppressed urs (chunk 009) and the execution petition
  against Muhammad Salim for sama (chunk 010) — and the only one where the objection is to omission
  rather than to practice.
- **A manuscript economy with named owners and one named journal.** The Samarqand copy of 854 AH and the
  *Kanz al-Ibad* in the annotator's own library; the *Malfuzat* passing from the late Maulvi Shams
  al-Din's library into Malik Latif's after publication in ***Sahifa*, Lahore, 1970**[OCR?]; Rukn-e Alam's
  malfuzat at **Gujrat**; and the **Ganj Bakhsh library, Rawalpindi**, here given its institutional name,
  the **Markaz-e Tahqiqat-e Farsi Iran wa Pakistan** (pp. 158–165, folios 146–153).
- **One modern editor behind a whole corpus.** Five of the Suhrawardi sources are "مرتبہ نامی" — edited
  by **Nami** (Pir Ghulam Dastgir Nami): *Tazkira-e Hamidiya* (Lahore 1959), *Gulzar* (1946), *Tazkira-e
  Qutbiya* (1952), *Diwan-e Qalandar Shah*, *Azkar-e Qalandari* (1957) (p. 167, folio 155). The same hand
  that produced chunk 009's *Tazkira-e Qutbiya* edition, from which the editor demolished Shah Kaku's
  death year.
- **Three shrine-town monographs by one author**: Nur Ahmad Khan Faridi's separate *tazkiras* of Baha
  al-Din Zakariya (printed), Sadr al-Din Arif (**Multan 1958**) and Shah Rukn-e Alam (**Multan 1961**)
  (pp. 159, 161, 165) — evidence of a mid-century Multan publishing programme around these three shrines.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The flying chairs at Multan.** Baba Farid, asked how far his austerities had brought him, said "ان
  کرسیوں کو جس پر ہم اور تم بیٹھے ہیں۔ اگر میں کہوں تو ابھی ہوا میں پرواز کر جائیں" — these chairs on
  which you and I are sitting, if I say the word, will fly into the air at once — and "دونوں کرسیاں زمین
  سے اٹھ کر ہوا کو پرواز کرنے لگیں", both chairs rose and began to fly; a hand was laid on them and
  "یہ گفتگو بسبیل تذکرہ تھی نہ بسبیل ارشاد۔ تم اسی جگہ پر قائم رہو", this was talk, not command; stay
  where you are (p. 160, folio 148).
- **A corpse raised at a funeral by a child, who then lived forty years.** Mir Surkh Bukhari, playing as
  a boy, came on a janaza outside the city, asked what would be done after the prayer, and on being told
  the man would be buried "جلال میں آگئے اور نعرہ اللہ اکبر مار کے مُردہ کے منہ سے پردہ اٹھایا اور
  فرمایا۔ **قُم بِاذن اللہ**" — came into *jalal*, cried Allahu Akbar, lifted the cloth from the dead
  man's face and said "Rise by God's leave"; **"مُردہ فی الفور جی اٹھا اور چالیس برس تک زندہ رہا"**, the
  dead man rose at once and lived **forty years** (pp. 162–163, folios 150–151). Reported from *Mazhar-e
  Jalali*.
- **The sherbet that opened two worlds.** Sadr al-Din Arif's leftover sherbet, given to a Multan
  shopkeeper, revealed the *nasut* and *malakut* to him on the first sip (pp. 163–164, folios 151–152).
- **Water that would not wet, a river that boiled, and a body that bled at a verse.** At Shaikh Ahmad
  Mashuq's forced wudu "بہت مشکیزے پانی کے صرف ہو گئے، مگر شیخ کے ہتھول [OCR?] پر پانی رواں نہ ہوا۔ جو
  پانی ہاتھ پر پڑتا فوراً خشک ہو جاتا" — many waterskins were used but the water would not run on his
  hands[OCR?] and dried the instant it fell; when the ulama plunged him into the river "دریا کا پانی ایسا
  اُبلا جس طرح دیگ دان پر اُبلتی ہے", the river boiled as a cauldron boils on a stove; and when he stood
  to pray and the words "ایاک نعبد و ایاک نستعین" left his tongue, **"تمام جسم پھٹ گیا اور بال بال سے
  خون جاری ہو کر کپڑے تر ہو گئے"**, his whole body split and blood ran from every hair until his clothes
  were soaked. He broke off the prayer and said **"اے بھائیو اب تو میں حائض عورت ہو گیا ہوں اور حیض والی
  عورت کو نماز معاف ہے"** — O brothers, I have become a menstruating woman, and a menstruating woman is
  excused prayer (p. 164, folio 152). Reported from *Tarikh-e Firishta*.
- **A doe called back for a crying child.** Rukn al-Din, small, wanted a fawn he had seen in the jungle;
  his father said it had gone with its mother and could not come; the child wept, and "فی الفور ہرنی مع
  بچہ جنگل سے نکل کر روبرو آکھڑی ہوئی", the doe with her fawn came out of the jungle at once and stood
  before them, and he took them home (p. 166, folio 154).
- **A death announced, prepared for and timed.** Rukn-e Alam's three months of seclusion, his order to
  Maulana Zahir al-Din for the shroud, and his dying in the last prostration of his own supererogatory
  prayers after leading Maghrib (p. 166, folio 154). Told as mastery rather than as marvel.
- **Respect paid to an unborn child** — Baha al-Din Zakariya standing for his pregnant daughter-in-law
  (p. 166, folio 154).
- Of Baha al-Din Zakariya the author says hundreds of karamat appeared "جن کی تشریح کتب اخبار میں درج
  ہے", whose exposition is in the books of reports (p. 160, folio 148); of Rukn al-Din, "اسی طرح حضرت کے
  خوارق کتابوں میں بہت لکھے ہیں" (p. 166, folio 154).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The annotator names himself.** Of the Samarqand manuscript of *Kanz al-Ibad*: it "**راقم محمد اقبال
  مجددی کے کتب خانہ کی زینت ہے**" — adorns the library of **the writer, Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi**
  (p. 159, folio 147, حاشیہ 1). "راقم", the writer of this note, is the editor's self-reference.
  **This settles the inference recorded under Doubts in chunk 014**: the same name appears there as the
  owner of four of Qasuri's manuscripts and the author of a 1973 life of him, and in chunk 012 as author
  of a 1972 monograph on the Mujaddid's opponents. The edition's apparatus is his.
- **An attribution overturned by a dated copy and an interlinear gloss.** Dr. Maulvi Muhammad Shafi held
  the *Awrad* to be Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi's; the editor answers from the **854 AH Samarqand copy** that
  the commentator "صاف طور پر بہاء الحق والشرع والدین لکھا ہے", plainly wrote Baha al-Din's honorific,
  and notes that a reader glossed hard words beneath the line — "'بہار' کے نیچے 'تابندہ'" (pp. 158–159,
  folios 146–147, حاشیہ 1). **The same evidential move as the seal argument in chunk 014: a physical
  document, closely described, against a scholar's conjecture.**
- **A death year chosen by counting witnesses, and it disagrees with the archive's date.** Of Baha al-Din
  Zakariya: three sources named, two giving 666 and one 661, and the editor **prefers the minority
  reading**: "لیکن ان میں ہمارے نزدیک صحیح ۶۶۱ھ ہے" (p. 159, folio 147, حاشیہ 1). He gives no reason
  beyond his own judgement — unlike the Muhammad Siddiq footnote in chunk 010, where the absence of
  contemporary testimony made him refuse to choose at all. Record the divergence and the absence of an
  argument.
- **Four death years for Sadr al-Din Arif, and a "family record" as the tiebreaker.** "حضرت شیخ صدر الدین
  عارف کے سال وفات میں اختلاف ہے۔ حضرت اشرف جہانگیر سمنانیؒ آپ کی ولادت س ۶۱۱ھ [OCR?] اور وفات س ۶۸۴ھ
  [OCR?] بتاتے ہیں … غوثی نے س ۶۰۹ھ [OCR?] لکھا ہے … فرشتہ نے س ۶۷۷ھ [OCR?] … لیکن **خاندانی ریکارڈ**
  میں س ۶۰۹ھ [OCR?] ہی درج ہوا ہے جس سے غوثی کے بیان کی تصدیق ہوتی ہے" — Simnani gives birth 611 and
  death 684; Ghausi 609; Firishta 677; but the **family record** enters 609, confirming Ghausi (p. 161,
  folio 149, حاشیہ 1). See Doubts: 609 cannot be a death year, and the footnote is internally confused.
  The method, though, is clear and recurs: **a shrine family's own record outranks the tazkiras**, as
  *Hadaiq-e Daudi* did in chunks 009–010.
- **Rukn-e Alam's death is moved by two months.** Text: **16 Rajab 735**, after Asr, dying in Maghrib
  nawafil (p. 166, folio 154). The editor's source: "**ثم ارتحل الی دار الوصل فی لیلۃ الجمعۃ — التاسع من
  جمادی الاول سنۃ خمس و ثلثین و سبعمائۃ**" — he departed on the **night of Friday, the ninth of Jumada
  al-Awwal, 735** (*Lataif-e Ashrafi* 1/384) (p. 165, folio 153, حاشیہ 1). Same year, different month and
  day; the editor prints the Arabic without comment, so the contradiction stands unadjudicated. The same
  passage also fixes his **birth on a Friday in 647 AH[OCR?]**, his life at **eighty-eight years** and his
  tenure at **fifty-two** — three figures that are mutually consistent (647 + 88 = 735) and that the text
  gives nowhere.
- **A genealogy declared wrong and rebuilt from a family source.** Of Hamid al-Din Hakkari's thirteen
  links: **"یہاں شجرہ نسب درست نہیں ہے۔ خاندانی ماخذ اذکار قلندری میں ہے"** — the genealogy here is not
  correct; the family source *Azkar-e Qalandari* has it thus — and the editor prints a longer chain that
  **inserts Shaikh Muhammad Musa and Abu Tahir**, adds the nisba **Hashimi**, and **extends past Abu
  Sufyan to Abd al-Muttalib, Hashim and Abd al-Manaf** (Farah Bakhsh, *Azkar-e Qalandari* ed. Nami,
  pp. 56–57) (p. 167, folio 155, حاشیہ 1). The second chain repaired in two chunks, after Imam Ali Shah's
  in chunk 014 — and again from a family document.
- **A tazkira dated by the reign it was written in.** *Tazkira-e Hamidiya* "سلطان سکندر لودھی ف ۹۲۳ھ /
  ۱۵۱۷ء کے عہد میں لکھی گئی", written in Sikandar Lodhi's era, with the author's four-generation
  patronymic given in full (p. 167, folio 155, حاشیہ 2). The editor dates sources by reign where he
  cannot date them by colophon.
- **The author's own structural principle, applied a second time.** As in the Naqshbandi chapter
  (chunk 012, p. 127), the Suhrawardi chapter opens with a full chain of predecessors and then begins the
  entries at the first figure with a Punjab grave — here Baha al-Din Zakariya, "جن کا ذکر لکھا جاتا ہے"
  (p. 158, folio 146). The rule is not restated, but the shape is identical.
- **Forward reference to a chapter not in these chunks**: Sayyid Ahmad Takhta[OCR?] of Lahore, "جن کا حال
  چھٹے باب میں تحریر ہو گا", whose account will come in the **sixth chapter** (p. 167, folio 155) — so the
  book runs to at least six chapters, of which four have now opened.

## Citable passages

- "ان ساتوں نے … باپ کا ترکہ نقد و جنس آپس میں بحصص مساوی تقسیم کیا تو شیخ صدر الدین عارف کے حصہ میں ستر
  لاکھ تنکہ سرخ نقد … آیا۔" — "The seven divided their father's estate, cash and kind, among themselves
  in equal shares, and Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif's share came to seventy lakh red tankas in cash."
  (p. 162, folio 150)
- "التمش کی درخواست پر شیخ الاسلام کا عہدہ بھی قبول کر لیا تھا۔" — "At Iltutmish's request he also
  accepted the office of Shaikh al-Islam." (p. 159, folio 147)
- "محمد تغلق نے … ملتان میں خون کی ندیاں بہانے کا حکم دیا تو حضرت شاہ رکن عالم ننگے پاؤں بادشاہ کے پاس
  سفارش کے لیے گئے۔" — "When Muhammad Tughluq ordered rivers of blood to flow in Multan, Shah Rukn-e Alam
  went barefoot to the emperor to intercede." (p. 165, folio 153)
- "حضرت لاولد تھے۔ ان کی وفات کے بعد محمد اسماعیل ان کے بھائی کی اولاد سجادہ نشین ہوئی۔" — "The Hazrat
  was childless. After his death the progeny of Muhammad Ismail, his brother, became sajjada nashin."
  (p. 166, folio 154)
- "دنیا اور دین کا حاجت مند جو ان کی خدمت میں آتا خالی نہ جاتا۔ اس لیے لوگ ان کو قبلہ حاجات کہتے تھے۔" —
  "No one needy of world or religion who came to him went away empty; therefore people called him
  Qibla-e Hajat." (p. 166, folio 154)

## Doubts

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya — the archive's date is the one the editor rejects.** Text and chronogram
  give **666 AH** (≈ 1267 CE, the row's current figure); the editor rules for **661 AH** (≈ 1262–63 CE)
  (pp. 159–161, folios 147–149). And the birth is **578 AH** in the text and chronogram against **566 AH /
  1170 CE** in the editor's footnote, with a birthplace (**Kot Kahror**) that appears only in the
  footnote. **Carry all four figures; do not silently adopt either pair.** Note that the editor's birth
  year and death year together give a life of 95 lunar years, and the text's give 88 — neither is stated
  as an age anywhere.
- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam — the death date is given two ways.** 16 Rajab 735 AH in the text
  (p. 166, folio 154); the night of Friday, 9 Jumada al-Awwal 735 AH in the Arabic the editor quotes
  (p. 165, folio 153). Both are OCR-clean; the year, 735 AH ≈ 1335 CE, matches the archive row. **The day
  and month do not survive this chunk.** His birth year, 647 AH, is OCR-flagged in the editor's Urdu but
  spelled out unflagged in the Arabic ("سنۃ سبع و اربعین و ستمایۃ") — so the Arabic is the better witness.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar — it is not certain which saint boasts and which restrains the
  chairs.** The passage uses "حضرت" for both parties: "حضرت نے پوچھا" (the host asks), "آپ نے جواب دیا"
  (the guest answers), "اتنی بات حضرت کی زبان سے نکلی" and "حضرت نے کرسیوں پر ہاتھ رکھ دیا" (p. 160,
  folio 148). The most natural reading is that **Baba Farid boasts and Baha al-Din Zakariya stills the
  chairs**, since the closing rebuke about "تذکرہ" not "ارشاد" answers the boast — but the sentence
  "اتنی بات حضرت کی زبان سے نکلی ہی تھی" can also refer to the speaker of the boast. **Do not attribute
  either speech to a named saint in an entry without checking the printed page.**
- **Sadr al-Din Arif's footnote is internally confused and cannot settle his dates.** It opens by saying
  the disagreement is about the **death** year, then treats **609 AH** as the value the "family record"
  confirms (p. 161, folio 149, حاشیہ 1). But 609 AH precedes his father's death in 661 or 666 AH, so
  609 can only be a **birth** year — in which case it competes with Simnani's 611, not with 677 or 684,
  and the death-year dispute (text 684, Firishta 677, Simnani 684) is left unresolved. All five numerals
  in the footnote are OCR-flagged. **The text's 23 Zi al-Hijja 684 AH is the only date with a day and a
  month, and it agrees with Simnani.**
- **Shaikh Mahmud Shah Lahori's death year differs between this chunk and the last.** Text here:
  **1280 AH** (p. 158, folio 146); the editor's footnote in chunk 014 gave Sayyid Muhammad Latif's
  **1866 CE / 1283 AH** (p. 157, folio 145). Three years, and neither figure is flagged.
- **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht takes only two sentences, both in footnotes, and neither is
  about his shrine.** He is named as Rukn-e Alam's khalifa and quoted on teaching at Multan (pp. 161,
  165; folios 149, 153). **Do not build an Uch entry from this chunk**, and note that the archive's
  Makhdum Jahaniyan died 1384 CE while Rukn-e Alam died 1335 — a khalifa surviving his pir by
  forty-nine years, which the chunk does not remark on.
- **Uch holds three further archive rows — tomb-of-baha-al-halim-uch-sharif, tomb-of-javindi-bibi,
  tomb-of-ustad-nuriya — and none of them appears here.** The chunk names Uch only as Mir Surkh
  Bukhari's burial place. Do not attach the Bukhari genealogy or the five sons to those rows.
- **Four Multan saints in this chunk have no archive row**: Sadr al-Din Arif (d. 684 AH), Shaikh Ahmad
  Mashuq-e Ilahi (d. 723[OCR?] AH), **Bibi Rasti**, and Sultan Hamid al-Din Hakim (Sultan al-Tarikin,
  entry no. 106). All four are buried or based at Multan and all four are attached to rows the archive
  does hold. They belong on a candidate list.
- **Shaikh Ahmad Mashuq-e Ilahi's death year is OCR-flagged and his father's description is unreadable.**
  "سال سات سو تئیس [OCR?]" (p. 164, folio 152); and the reason his father expelled him — "چونکہ یہ شخص
  دلم الحرو [OCR?] مسکو [OCR?] تھا" — is two flagged words and cannot be rendered (p. 163, folio 151).
  The whole conversion narrative turns on it.
- **Mir Surkh Bukhari's age at the funeral miracle is unreadable**: "ایک روز مکان کی عمر میں لڑکوں کے
  ساتھ کھیل رہے تھے" (pp. 162–163, folios 150–151) — "مکان کی عمر" makes no sense as printed and is not
  flagged, so this is a probable transcription fault rather than an OCR doubt. He was plainly a child.
- **The Bhakkari sayyids' name is flagged twice**, once in the lineage branching and once as Bibi
  Fatima's father's nisba: "سادات بھاکھری [OCR?]", "سید بدر الدین بھاکھری [OCR?]" (pp. 162–163, folios
  150–151). The reading governs a whole sayyid lineage's name.
- **"سید احمد تختہ [OCR?] لاہور"** (p. 167, folio 155) — a Lahore shaykh named as Hamid al-Din's maternal
  grandfather, with his nisba flagged and his account promised for the sixth chapter. He may or may not
  correspond to an archive row; **nothing in this chunk supports a mapping**, and the sixth chapter is
  outside it.
