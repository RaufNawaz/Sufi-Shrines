# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 008 — pp. 88–96

End of entry 43 (Baba Farid) through the opening of entry 48 (Amir Khusrau). Chishti chapter
throughout. Only one archive row appears — Pakpattan — but the chunk is unusually rich on
institutions: a land purchase litigated before a royal governor, Baba Farid's signed-and-sealed system
of deputing khalifas, and two accounts of langar expenditure.

**The folio markers remain ten too high**, as established in chunk 007, and this chunk supplies four
more checks against the table of contents: entry 44 is at printed p. 78 and stands on p. 90 (marker
88); entry 45 at printed p. 81 on p. 93 (marker 91); entry 47 at printed p. 82 on p. 94 (marker 92);
entry 48 at printed p. 84 on p. 96 (marker 94). Folios below are corrected, with the marker's reading
in brackets.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** (حضرت شیخ فرید الدین مسعود گنج شکر اجودھنی پاک پٹنی — end of
  entry no. 43, pp. 88–89, folios 76–77 [markers: 86–87]):
  - **Birth and upbringing**: "ولادت حضرت گنج شکر کی سال پانسو تیاسی ہجری میں بموضع کھوتوال علاقہ
    ملتان عمل میں آئی۔ پرورش بھی حضرت نے ملتان سے پائی" — Ganj Shakar's birth took place in the year
    five hundred and eighty-three Hijri at mauza Khotwal in the Multan district, and he was also
    brought up from Multan (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86]).
  - **Khilafat and residences**: he took the khirqa of khilafat from Khwaja Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar Kaki
    Aushi Dehlvi, travelled much and took inner faiz from many elders; "بعد حصول خرقہ خلافت حضرت نے
    شہر ہانسی میں سکونت اختیار کی پھر اجودھن میں آ کر قیام پذیر ہوئے" — after receiving the khirqa he
    settled in the city of Hansi, then came to Ajodhan and stayed (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86]).
  - **Family history**: after Farrukh Shah, king of Kabul, died and the kings of Ghazni took Kabul,
    sovereignty left the family though its progeny stayed on in Kabul; then when Chingiz Khan overcame
    the Sultan of Khwarazm and calamity struck the city of Kabul, "حضرت کے جد بزرگوار لڑائی میں شہید
    ہوئے اور شیخ جمال الدین سلیمان ہندوستان کو چلے آئے" — his grandfather was martyred in the fighting
    and Shaikh Jamal al-Din Sulaiman came away to Hindustan; the chain to Umar ibn al-Khattab is
    completed (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86]).
  - **The author declines the karamat and says where to find them**: thousands are recorded in books
    of history and there is no room in this brief work — "شائق کتاب خزینۃ الاصفیا میں دیکھ لے", let
    the eager reader look in Khazinat al-Asfiya — but one is given (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86]).
  - **The land dispute**, the fullest institutional narrative in these eight chunks: "حضرت نے ایک
    قطعہ زمین ایک شخص سے شمول خانقاہ کے لیے قیمت دے کر خریدا" — he bought a plot of land from a man,
    paying a price for it, **for inclusion in the khanqah**. Some months later a second claimant to the
    land appeared and "استغاثہ اس دعوے کا دیپالپور میں جا کر حاکم شاہی کے روبرو پیش کیا", presented
    his suit at **Dipalpur before the royal governor**. The Hazrat was summoned "اصالتاً یا وکالتاً",
    in person or by agent, and sent an attendant to answer. Witnesses were then demanded "جن سے زمین
    ملکیت بائع کی ثابت ہو", to prove that the land had been the seller's property. Wearied by the
    governor's repeated demands he said: "اس گردن ٹوٹے کو کہہ دو کہ خود اگر زمین سے پوچھ لے۔ اگر زمین
    گواہی نہ دے گی تو ہم گواہ بھیج دیں گے" — tell that broken-neck to ask the land himself; if the land
    will not testify, we shall send witnesses. The governor came in person, mounted, and "ہزاروں لوگ
    دیکھنے والے جمع ہوئے", thousands of onlookers gathered. The claimant asked first and the land made
    no sound; then an attendant asked on the Hazrat's behalf, and a voice came from the land: "میں اس
    کی ملکیت تھی جس نے فرید کے پاس مجھ کو فروخت کیا اور اب ملکیت خواجہ فرید کی ہوں" — I was the
    property of the man who sold me to Farid, and now I am the property of Khwaja Farid. The governor
    punished the claimant severely; and riding away after the judgement his horse stumbled, he fell,
    "اسی وقت گردن ٹوٹ گئی اور مر گیا", his neck broke at once and he died — "نعوذ بالله من غضب
    الاولیاء" (pp. 88–89, folios 76–77 [markers: 86–87]).
  - **Death**: "پانچویں ماہ محرم ۶۶۴ ہجری" — the fifth of Muharram 664 Hijri — "اور روضہ منورہ بمقام
    پاک پٹن زیارت گاہ خلق ہے", and his illuminated rauza at **Pak Pattan** is a place of pilgrimage
    (p. 89, folio 77 [marker: 87]). A Persian chronogram follows, both couplets ending "فردے یگانہ
    فرید".
  - **The editor endorses the death year and argues out the birth year**: 664 AH "ہی معتبر اور صحیح
    ہے", is the reliable and correct one (Khaliq Ahmad Nizami, *Life and Times of Baba Farid* 56–58)
    (p. 89, folio 77 [marker: 87], حاشیہ 1). On the birth he says ۵۸۲ھ [OCR?] "تو بالکل غلط ہے", is
    entirely wrong; *Sair al-Auliya* gives ۵۶۹ھ [OCR?]; *Fawaid al-Fawad* says Baba Sahib lived

    حاصل ہے گویا بابا صاحب کا سال ولادت ۵۷۱ھ درست ہے" — in our view Fawaid al-Fawad has preference
    over Sair al-Auliya, so 571 AH is correct (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86], حاشیہ 3). See Doubts: the
    text's own 583 is not among the options he considers.
  - **The malfuzat corpus attributed to him is declared fabricated**: several collections are ascribed

    all these collections are merely fabricated and falsely attributed (p. 89, folio 77 [marker: 87],
    حاشیہ 1, citing Nizami, *Baba Farid* 118–20 [OCR?], and the editor's own *Ahwal wa Athar-e
    Abdullah Khweshgi* 99–101 [OCR?]).
  - **Seventeen sources** are listed for him, the longest bibliography in these chunks (pp. 89–90,

    H-8); Abdullah Khweshgi Qasuri, *Maarij al-Wilayat* (ms, folios 21–36); Muhammad Bilaq, *Matlub
    al-Talibin* (ms, Ganj Bakhsh library, Rawalpindi); Asghar Ali, *Jawahir-e Faridi*, Lahore;
    K. A. Nizami, *Life and Times of Sh. Farid-u-Din*, Aligarh 1955; Muslim Nizami, *Anwar
    al-Faridiya*, **Pak Pattan 1965**; Muhammad Aslam, "کیا سلطان بلبن [OCR?] کی کوئی بیٹی بابا صاحب
    سے منسوب تھی؟" in *Tarikhi Maqalat* 9–31; and Wahid Ahmad Masud, *Sawanih Hazrat Baba Farid
    al-Din*, Karachi 1965.
  - **His system for deputing khalifas**, from entry no. 44: "حضرت فرید الدین گنج شکر کا یہ دستور تھا
    کہ جس خلیفہ کو کسی ملک کو روانہ کرتے۔ فرمان اپنے دستخط سے لکھ کر اس کو دیتے اور فرماتے کہ خواجہ
    جمال الدین ہانسویؒ کے پاس جا کر اس کی مہر کرا لو۔ پس وہ ہانسی جاتا اور جمال الدین کی مہر اس پر
    کرا کے مکان مامورہ کی طرف روانہ ہو جاتا" — it was Farid al-Din Ganj Shakar's rule that to whichever
    khalifa he despatched to a country he gave a *farman* written over his own signature, telling him
    to go to Khwaja Jamal al-Din Hansvi and have it sealed; so the man went to Hansi, had Jamal al-Din
    seal it, and set out for his appointed station (p. 90, folio 78 [marker: 88]). He later wrote Sabir
    a **فرمان تولیت**, a farman of custodianship, for Kaliyar with his own hand (p. 91, folio 79
    [marker: 89]). And when Jamal al-Din tore the first farman up, Farid said "پارہ کردہ جمال را فرید
    نتواند دوخت" — what Jamal has torn, Farid cannot stitch (p. 91, folio 79 [marker: 89]).
  - **He gave the title "Sabir"** to Ala al-Din Ali Ahmad for twelve years' service in the kitchen
    without eating an unpermitted morsel, "اور دہلی کا قطب بنا کر فرمان لکھ دیا", and wrote a farman
    making him the Qutb of Delhi (p. 90, folio 78 [marker: 88]).
  - **And he blessed Nizam al-Din's langar**: after eating the cowpeas Nizam al-Din had bought by
    pawning his turban, he said "ہم نے خدا سے مانگا ہے کہ تیرے مہمان خانے میں ہر روز تہتر من نمک خرچ
    ہوا کرے" — we have asked God that seventy-three maunds of salt be spent every day in your
    guest-house (p. 95, folio 83 [marker: 93]).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Khwaja Ala al-Din Ali Ahmad Sabir Kaliyari** (entry no. 44): khalifa and, the text says, sister's
  son and son-in-law of Baba Farid, and above all others in the stations of wilayat; "حضرت کی طبعیت
  میں جلال بہت تھا", there was much *jalal* in his temperament; he lived on tree leaves through twelve
  years in his pir's kitchen. The quarrel with Khwaja Jamal al-Din at Hansi, the lit finger, the torn
  farman and the mutual annulment — "تو نے میرے فرمان کو پھاڑ ڈالا ہے۔ میں نے تیری ولایت کو پھاڑ ڈالا"
  — are at pp. 90–91, folios 78–79 [markers: 88–89]. At Kaliyar the townspeople ignored him for
  months, then removed him by the arm from the qazi's prayer-place in the Jami Masjid, on which he
  said "قاضی سے بڑھ کر رتبہ قطب کا ہے اور ہم اس سرزمین کے قطب ہیں" and, finding no room to stand for
  prayer, addressed the mosque itself: see Legends. He died in the year six hundred and ninety, in the
  state of sama; Khwaja Shams al-Din, then his only attendant, buried him "اور روضہ مکلف بنوایا جو اب
  تک زیارت گاہ خلق ہے", and had an elaborate rauza built which is to this day a place of pilgrimage
  (p. 93, folio 81 [marker: 91]).
- **Khwaja Jamal al-Din Hansvi**, holder of the counter-seal on Baba Farid's farmans, who came two
  miles out of Hansi to meet Sabir, walked on foot beside his palanquin and made him imam at the
  evening prayer (pp. 90–91, folios 78–79 [markers: 88–89]).
- **Shaikh Shams al-Din Turk Panipati** (entry no. 45): khalifa of Sabir, sent by him to Panipat, where
  thousands reached nearness through him. Originally of **Turkestan**; his grandfather **Shaikh Ahmad
  Yasawi** was among the famous awliya; his genealogy joins Imam Muhammad Hanif, son of Ali
  al-Murtaza, and the family were called Alvi Qureshi. Died 715 AH, mazar at Panipat (p. 93, folio 81
  [marker: 91]).
- **Shaikh Sharaf al-Din Bu Ali Qalandar Panipati** (entry no. 46): genealogy to Imam Azam Kufi given;
  spiritual chain through Shaikh Ashiq-e Khuda, Imam al-Din Abdal and Shaikh Badr al-Din Ghaznavi to
  Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar Kaki. "حضرت کا طریق مجذوبانہ قلندرانہ اور کلام آپ کا توحیدانہ تھا" — his way
  was that of the majzub and the qalandar, and his speech was of divine unity. Died 13 Ramadan 724 AH
  (pp. 93–94, folios 81–82 [markers: 91–92]). The editor holds his affiliation unknown and one
  attributed work spurious: see Doubts.
- **Sultan al-Mashaikh Nizam al-Din Badauni Dehlvi** (entry no. 47): great khalifa of Baba Farid; real
  name Muhammad b. Ahmad Daniyal b. Ali Bukhari; titles Sultan al-Mashaikh, Sultan al-Auliya, Sultan
  al-Salatin, with the epithets Mahbub-e Ilahi and Nizam al-Milla wal-Din. His paternal grandfather
  Shaikh Ali Bukhari and maternal grandfather Shaikh Khwaja Arab both came from Bukhara and
  "چند سال لاہور میں سکونت رکھی", resided some years **in Lahore**, before going on to Badayun, where
  he was born in 634 AH; his father died when he was five and his mother **Bibi Zulaikha** brought
  him up; at twelve he completed his studies and took the turban of distinction; then through Shaikh
  Najib al-Din Mutawakkil he came to Baba Farid, and after the khirqa of khilafat left for Delhi,
  where "لاکھوں آدمی طالبان حق خدمت میں حاضر ہوئے", hundreds of thousands of seekers came. Died
  Wednesday 18 Rabi al-Akhir, the year seven hundred and twenty-five Hijri, with ۷۲۵ھ in the margin of
  the chronogram; "روضہ مبارک شہر دہلی میں زیارت گاہ خلق ہے" (pp. 94–96, folios 82–84
  [markers: 92–94]). Bibi Zulaikha has her own entry in the chapter on righteous women (contents
  no. 195, printed p. 239).
- **Khwaja Amir Khusrau, poet, Chishti of Delhi** (entry no. 48, opening): of Sultan al-Mashaikh's
  khalifas, a complete devotee, and "پیر روشن ضمیر کے روبرو اس سے زیادہ کوئی محرم اسرار و واقف راز نہ
  تھا" — before his pir no one was more a confidant of secrets; his real name was Abu al-Hasan. The
  entry breaks off at the chunk edge (p. 96, folio 84 [marker: 94]).
- **Farrukh Shah of Kabul**, of whom "کتب تاریخ خاموش ہیں", the books of history are silent; *Sair
  al-Auliya* calls him king of Kabul (p. 55), while Muhammad Hashim Kishmi made him only "اجلہ امرا و
  اعاظم وزرا سلاطین کابل", one of the great nobles and chief ministers of Kabul's sultans (Zubdat
  al-Maqamat 88) (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86], حاشیہ 1).
- **Maulana Nusrat Naushahi of Sharaqpur**, owner of the *Thamarat al-Quds* manuscript cited twice
  (pp. 89, 96; folios 77, 84 [markers: 87, 94]).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A khanqah's land bought for a price, and defended in a royal court.** The Baba Farid narrative
  above is the archive-relevant core of this chunk: a plot purchased "شمول خانقاہ کے لیے قیمت دے کر",
  for money and for inclusion in the khanqah; a rival claim entered before the **hakim-e shahi at
  Dipalpur**; summons served on the shaykh "اصالتاً یا وکالتاً"; an attendant appearing as his
  representative; and witnesses demanded to prove the vendor's title (pp. 88–89, folios 76–77
  [markers: 86–87]). Whatever one makes of the speaking land, the procedural frame is the book's
  clearest picture of how shrine land was acquired and contested.
- **Deputation by signed farman with a counter-seal.** Baba Farid's two-signature system, and the
  separate "farman of custodianship" for Kaliyar, are set out above (pp. 90–91, folios 78–79
  [markers: 88–89]). A khalifa's territory was documented, transferable and — as Jamal al-Din
  demonstrated — revocable by destroying the paper.
- **Langar expenditure, twice quantified.** Of Nizam al-Din: "ہزاروں روپیہ روزمرہ حضرت کے لنگر میں
  خرچ ہوتا اور ہزاروں روپیہ نقد خیرات کیا جاتا" — thousands of rupees a day were spent in his langar
  and thousands of rupees in cash given in charity (p. 95, folio 83 [marker: 93]). And the blessing on
  it: seventy-three maunds of salt a day in his guest-house (p. 95, folio 83 [marker: 93]).
- **Halwa and fatiha for the spirits of the Chishti pirs.** The Hazrat asks the jagirdar, if his farman
  is recovered, to give "پیران چشت کے ارواح دو آثار [OCR?] حلوا" — two *athar* [OCR?] of halwa to the
  spirits of the pirs of Chisht — and the man answers "میں فرمان کے ملنے سے پہلے ہی حلوا لا کر فاتحہ
  دلاتا ہوں", I will bring the halwa and have the fatiha recited before it is even found (p. 95,
  folio 83 [marker: 93]). A named offering with a named quantity and a named ritual.
- **A jagir farman as a fragile instrument**: one burnt in a house fire, its replacement obtained
  "بڑی محنت و مشقت سے" and then lost on the way out of the royal diwan (p. 95, folio 83
  [marker: 93]).
- **Rank asserted against the qazi inside a mosque.** Sabir's claim that the Qutb outranks the qazi,
  made over the qazi's own prayer-place, and the crowd's response of laughter and physical removal
  (p. 91, folio 79 [marker: 89]) — a direct collision between shrine authority and judicial office.
- **A city depopulated and a shrine left in a ruin.** After the mosque fell, plague and boils
  [پھوڑے OCR?] descended on Kaliyar and "وہ شہر ویران ہو گیا", the city was laid waste, and Sabir
  passed his whole life in that ruin with a single attendant (pp. 91–92, folios 79–80
  [markers: 89–90]).
- **Tomb built by the disciple who buried him**: Shams al-Din built Sabir's "روضہ مکلف", an elaborate
  rauza, still a place of pilgrimage (p. 93, folio 81 [marker: 91]).
- **A turban pawned to feed a pir** (p. 95, folio 83 [marker: 93]), and a torn pyjama replaced with the
  pir's own — the cord tied so as never to open, which is how the book explains Nizam al-Din's
  lifelong celibacy (pp. 95–96, folios 83–84 [markers: 93–94]).
- **Modern shrine publishing**: *Anwar al-Faridiya*, Pak Pattan 1965, printed at the shrine town
  itself (p. 90, folio 78 [marker: 88]).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Baba Farid and the speaking land**, quoted above, with the claimant's death by a broken neck
  immediately after judgement (pp. 88–89, folios 76–77 [markers: 86–87]).
- **Sabir's lit finger**: he blew on the big finger of his right hand and "اس پھونک سے انگلی شمع کے
  مانند روشن ہو گئی", by that breath the finger became bright as a candle, so that the farman could be
  read and sealed at night (p. 91, folio 79 [marker: 89]).
- **Sabir and the mosque**: "اے مسجد تمام لوگ سجدے کو جاتے ہیں۔ تو بھی سجدہ کر" — O mosque, all these
  people are going to prostration; do you prostrate too — "یہ بات کہتے ہی مسجد مع چھت اور دیواروں کے
  ان پر گر پڑی اور سب لوگ نیچے آ کر ہلاک ہو گئے", and as soon as he said it the mosque with roof and
  walls fell on them and everyone beneath was killed; from that day plague and boils came upon the
  city (p. 91, folio 79 [marker: 89]).
- **Sabir's annulment of another saint's wilayat** "آخر سے", from the end, and Baba Farid's refusal to
  repair it (p. 91, folio 79 [marker: 89]).
- **Nizam al-Din and the halwa wrapper**: the confectioner's paper covering the halwa turned out to be
  the lost jagir farman itself (p. 95, folio 83 [marker: 93]).
- **Nizam al-Din's celibacy**, sealed by Baba Farid's instruction that the waist-cord be tied "اس قدر
  کہ سوائے روز قیامت کے نہ کھلے۔ اگر کھلے تو حوران بہشت پر کھلے" — so tightly that it open only on the
  Day of Judgement, and if it open, let it open upon the houris of paradise (pp. 95–96, folios 83–84
  [markers: 93–94]). Narrated as "نقل ہے".
- Of Bu Ali Qalandar, Shams al-Din Turk and Nizam al-Din alike, the author says the marvels fill
  thousands of books and that this brief work has no room for them (pp. 93–95, folios 81–83
  [markers: 91–93]).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor questions whether Sabir of Kaliyar appears in any contemporary source at all.**
  "حضرت صابرؒ کا ذکر معاصر کتب میں نہیں ملتا" — mention of Hazrat Sabir is not found in contemporary
  books; *Sair al-Auliya* has only a few unclear lines, in which Amir Khurd reports from his father a
  dervish named Shaikh Ali Sabir of qasba ڈکیرکی [OCR?], a murid of Baba Farid, who asked for a
  testament and was told "جاؤ زندگی خوشی سے بسر ہو گی", go, your life will pass in happiness, and who
  was "نہایت خوش باش اور ہنس مکھ", extremely cheerful and smiling. The editor sets beside it the
  words of *Lataif-e Ashrafi* (c. 830 AH), which calls him "قطب المشائخ شیخ علی صابر" of Baba Farid's
  perfect khalifas and mentions that "اندک فتوری [OCR?] در میان واقع شدہ بود" — a slight rupture had
  occurred between them — later made good. He then quotes Abdul Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi at length, whose
  conclusion is that **the cheerful Shaikh Sabir of Sair al-Auliya is probably a different man** from
  "شیخ علی صابر … کہ داماد شیخ فرید الدین و خلیفہ او بود و قبر او در قصبہ کلیار است و سلسلہ شیخ عبد
  القدوس و غیرہ بوی منتہی می شود و ذکر او در سیر الاولیا اصلاً نکردہ" — the Shaikh Ali Sabir who was
  Farid al-Din's son-in-law and khalifa, whose grave is at Kaliyar and to whom the silsila of Shaikh
  Abdul Quddus and others goes back, and of whom Sair al-Auliya makes no mention whatever, an omission
  "خالی از غرابت نیست", not free of strangeness — ending "و اللہ اعلم" (Akhbar al-Akhyar 66) (p. 92,
  folio 80 [marker: 90], حاشیہ 1).
- **And he names the epistemology of the later literature.** Of the books written long afterwards:
  "ان کا ماخذ کشف و الہام قرار پایا" — their source turned out to be unveiling and inspiration — and
  the case became one of "چوں نزدیدند حقیقت رہ فسانہ زدند", since they did not see the reality they
  took the road of fable. His own resolution is theological rather than documentary: "حقیقت یہ ہے کہ
  حضرت صابرؒ ان اولیاء اللہ میں سے تھے جو اخفائے حال کو پسند کرتے ہیں", the truth is that Hazrat Sabir
  was of those saints who prefer to conceal their state, and that concealment persisted for a time
  after his death; his spiritual grace and the great saints of the Sabiri silsila are "شاہد عادل",
  just witnesses, to his eminence (p. 92, folio 80 [marker: 90], حاشیہ 1). **This is the one place in
  the eight chunks where the editor, having shown that a saint is undocumented, argues for him
  anyway.**
- **Method, on Baba Farid's birth year**: two sources disagree, one is preferred by name and reason,
  and a figure is derived by subtracting a recorded lifespan (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86], حاشیہ 3).
- **Method, on Nizam al-Din's education**: "بارہ برس کی عمر میں فارغ التحصیل ہونے کی روایت قیاسی ہے"
  — the tradition that he finished his studies at twelve is conjectural; at sixteen he travelled from
  Badayun to Delhi for learning and studied three or four years with the city's leading men; and he
  says of himself "من بعد دوازدہ سالہ بودم کم و بیش لغت می خواندم" (Fawaid al-Fawad 252) (p. 94,
  folio 82 [marker: 92], حاشیہ 4).
- **Method, on migration**: Amir Khurd mentions only Sayyid Ali's migration with his family from
  Bukhara, while Jamali says Nizam al-Din's father migrated from Ghazni — two routes for one family,
  set side by side without resolution (p. 94, folio 82 [marker: 92], حاشیہ 2).
- **Two whole corpora declared spurious in this chunk**: the malfuzat attributed to Baba Farid (p. 89,
  folio 77 [marker: 87]), and the *Maktubat-e Bu Ali Shah Qalandar* held in the Punjab University,
  which "محض منسوب ہے", is merely attributed (p. 94, folio 82 [marker: 92], حاشیہ 1).
- One of the listed sources is itself a piece of source criticism on a shrine claim: Muhammad Aslam's
  "کیا سلطان بلبن [OCR?] کی کوئی بیٹی بابا صاحب سے منسوب تھی؟" (p. 90, folio 78 [marker: 88]).

## Citable passages

- "حضرت نے ایک قطعہ زمین ایک شخص سے شمول خانقاہ کے لیے قیمت دے کر خریدا۔" — "The Hazrat bought a plot
  of land from a man, paying a price, for inclusion in the khanqah." (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86])
- "میں اس کی ملکیت تھی جس نے فرید کے پاس مجھ کو فروخت کیا اور اب ملکیت خواجہ فرید کی ہوں۔" — the
  land's answer: "I was the property of him who sold me to Farid, and now I am the property of Khwaja
  Farid." (p. 89, folio 77 [marker: 87])
- "جس خلیفہ کو کسی ملک کو روانہ کرتے۔ فرمان اپنے دستخط سے لکھ کر اس کو دیتے اور فرماتے کہ خواجہ جمال
  الدین ہانسویؒ کے پاس جا کر اس کی مہر کرا لو۔" — "To whichever khalifa he sent to a country he gave a
  farman written over his own signature, and told him to go to Khwaja Jamal al-Din Hansvi and have it
  sealed." (p. 90, folio 78 [marker: 88])
- "ہزاروں روپیہ روزمرہ حضرت کے لنگر میں خرچ ہوتا اور ہزاروں روپیہ نقد خیرات کیا جاتا۔" — of Nizam
  al-Din: "Thousands of rupees were spent daily in the Hazrat's langar, and thousands of rupees given
  in cash as charity." (p. 95, folio 83 [marker: 93])
- "قاضی سے بڑھ کر رتبہ قطب کا ہے اور ہم اس سرزمین کے قطب ہیں۔" — Sabir in the Jami Masjid: "The rank
  of the Qutb is higher than the qazi's, and we are the Qutb of this land." (p. 91, folio 79
  [marker: 89])

## Doubts

- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar — the birth year in the text is not even one of the candidates the
  editor weighs.** The text says 583 AH (p. 88, folio 76 [marker: 86]); the footnote on the same page
  discusses 582 [OCR?] as "entirely wrong", 569 [OCR?] from *Sair al-Auliya*, and settles on 571
  [OCR?] from *Fawaid al-Fawad*. Whether the editor was correcting a printed 582 that the transcriber
  has read as 583 in the body, or the two numbers genuinely differ, cannot be told here; note that the
  body figure is spelled out in words ("پانسو تیاسی") while all the footnote's figures are numerals
  and all are OCR-flagged. **Only the death year, 5 Muharram 664 AH, is affirmed by both the text and
  the editor.**
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar — the birthplace name.** "بموضع کھوتوال" here (p. 88, folio 76
  [marker: 86]) against "قصبہ کتہوال [OCR?]" in the previous chunk's footnote on his father's
  qaziship (p. 86, folio 74, chunk 007). Same place, two spellings, one of them flagged.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar — nothing said of the shrine itself beyond the formula.** The
  entry gives the rauza at Pak Pattan as a "زیارت گاہ خلق" and no more: no urs, no custodian, no
  building date, no visitor numbers. The archive should not read the Dipalpur land case as being about
  Pakpattan land — the text does not say where the plot was, only that it was bought for the khanqah
  and litigated at Dipalpur.
- **Nizam al-Din's birth year is contradicted, and the correction is itself internally impossible.**
  The text gives 634 AH; the footnote says Amir Khurd recorded "۵۳۶ھ [OCR?] / ۱۲۳۸ء" and that the
  Mufti's 634 is therefore not correct (p. 94, folio 82 [marker: 92], حاشیہ 3). But 536 AH is not
  1238 CE — 1238 CE falls in 636 AH — so the footnote's Hijri numeral is almost certainly a
  transcription of ۶۳۶, in which case the correction differs from the text by two years, not a
  century. Do not carry either figure without this note.
- **Sabir of Kaliyar may be two men, or none.** The editor's footnote (p. 92, folio 80 [marker: 90])
  leaves open whether the Sabir of *Sair al-Auliya* and the Sabir buried at Kaliyar are the same
  person, and records that the later literature rests on *kashf*. The mosque-collapse and
  city-destruction narrative has no contemporary source behind it in anything the chunk cites. Kaliyar
  has no archive row, but the Sabiri nisba attached to several Chishti figures in the contents (nos.
  54, 56, 59, 74) descends from this contested figure.
- **Bu Ali Qalandar's affiliation is unknown by the editor's own statement**: "آپ کی نسبت طریقت صحیح
  معلوم نہیں ہے", and Abdul Haq is quoted that neither the attribution to Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar Kaki
  nor that to Nizam al-Din Auliya "بہ صحت نہ رسیدہ است", has reached correctness (p. 93, folio 81
  [marker: 91], حاشیہ 1) — while the text prints a confident four-link chain to Bakhtiar Kaki (p. 94,
  folio 82 [marker: 92]).
- **The folio markers are ten too high on every page of this chunk**, as in chunk 007, with four fresh
  checks given in the header. Citations must be corrected before use.
- **A source citation with reversed page numbers**: "اخبار الاخیار ص ۵۲ - ۵۰" (p. 89, folio 77
  [marker: 87]).

  "ص ۹۹ تا ۱۰۱ [OCR?]" (p. 89, folio 77 [marker: 87]); "معنی [OCR?] اجمیری" and "سلطان بلبن [OCR?]"
