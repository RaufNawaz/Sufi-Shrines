## Figures and sites not in the archive

This is the book's acquisition list. *Hadiqat al-Awliya* runs to roughly 243–247 numbered
entries (the contents count **247** across seven *chamans*, p. 15–21, folios 3–9) against the
archive's 169 rows, so most of what follows has no row. Each line is one figure or site as the
book prints it, with the order, death year and burial place **only where the book gives them**;
`(not stated)` means the book does not say. `[PK]` marks a burial place in present-day
Pakistan — those are the candidate rows. Dates are kept exactly as printed, Hijri forms and
Urdu numerals included, and every `[OCR?]` / `[illegible]` flag stays on the word it was
attached to: a flagged name must be checked before it becomes a row. Where the 1875 author and
the 1974 editor (Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi) disagree, both readings are given (CLAUDE.md RULE 2).
"Appendix" = Mian Muhammad Din Kalim's table *مزارات لاہور کا موجودہ محل وقوع* (pp. 287–291),
which gives the 1974 street address for many Lahore graves.

### The Deccan

- **Shaikh Nizam al-Din Aurangabadi / شیخ نظام الدین اورنگ آبادی** — **1060–1142 AH / 1649–1729 CE**; a famous elder of the Chishtiya silsila and father and pir of Fakhr-e Jahan; after **Khwaja Gesu Daraz (d. 825 AH)** no elder of the Chishtiya Nizamiya of such rank "سرزمین دکن پر … قدم نہیں رکھا"; "امرا کو آپ سے بڑی عقیدت تھی", and **Nizam al-Mulk Asaf Jah I** (dates printed "۱۷۴۸ - ۱۶۷۱ء") was his devotee and wrote a book, "اشک گلستان ارم [OCR?]", on him; his teachings affected both the people and the rulers of the Deccan. Burial (not stated here). (pp. 116, 304, folio 104)
- **Nawab Nizam al-Mulk Ghazi al-Din Khan, Nazim of Hyderabad** — author of *Manaqib-e Fakhriya*, "بے شمار کرامتیں حضرت کی اپنی کتاب میں درج کی ہیں"; editor: "نبیرہ آصف جاہ اول", grandson of Asaf Jah I. (p. 117, folio 105)
- **Khwaja Gesu Daraz / خواجہ گیسو دراز** — **d. 825 AH**; named as the last Chishti Nizami of the first rank in the Deccan before Nizam al-Din Aurangabadi, and as Fakhr-e Jahan's maternal ancestor. (pp. 116–117)

**Count.** The section lists roughly **300 distinct figures and sites**, of which about **175 are marked [PK]** — a burial place, or a named shrine, in present-day Pakistan. Around **125 of those [PK] entries are in Lahore and its immediate environs** (Mozang, Garhi Shahu, Miani, Dholanwal, Faizpur, Khoi Miran), with the rest spread over Multan, Uch, Satghara, Hujra Shah Muqim, Jarwa, Chunian, Sharaqpur, Kasur, Gujranwala, Wazirabad, Kotla, Sialkot, Peshawar, Maharan and Taj Sarwar, Mubarakpur, Kot Lajnat, Layyah and Pakpattan. A further **35 or so are undated or unplaced** and are gathered under "Place not stated"; another **30 have a burial place now in India** (Delhi, Sirhind, Panipat, Kaithal, Gangoh, Sadhaura, Kaliyar, Jalandhar, Gharam/Kahram, Batala, Ratar Chhatar in Gurdaspur, Amritsar, Hoshiarpur, Manikpur, Amroha, Rudauli, Srinagar and the Kashmir valley) and about **10 outside the subcontinent** (Jannat al-Baqi, Medina, Mecca, Aleppo, Baghdad, and one at sea). These counts are of lines in this section, not of the book's numbered entries: the book runs to 247 entries, and a single line here sometimes covers a family enclosure holding a dozen graves.
