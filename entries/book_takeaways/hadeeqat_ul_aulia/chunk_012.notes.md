# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 012 — pp. 127–137

**The Chishti chapter ends and the third chapter opens: "تیسرا چمن — تذکرہ مشائخ خاندان نقشبندیہ", the
third garden, the memorial of the mashaikh of the Naqshbandi family** (p. 127, folio 115). Entries 82 to
87: Baqi Billah, the Mujaddid, two of the Mujaddid's sons, and the Lahore Naqshbandi Khawand Mahmud with
his son. One archive row takes material directly, but the chunk's real value is threefold: **the author
states his own principle of selection, and it is a Punjab mazar**; the editor twice overturns a death
year in a single line; and a named subahdar of Lahore orders a newly built Naqshbandi rauza demolished on
doctrinal grounds.

**The folio markers agree with the relation on every page**: folio = PDF index − 12, p. 127 → folio 115
through p. 137 → folio 125, each marker reading exactly that.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **tahir-bandagi-qadri** (حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہوری — editor's footnote, p. 129, folio 117):
  - **A death year and a disputed authorship.** Discussing the source of the Mujaddid's karamat, the
    editor writes: "تذکرہ مجددیہ کے مصنف کا نام معلوم نہیں ہے۔ محمد دین فوق نے اسے **حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر
    بندگی لاہوری (ف ۱۰۴۰ھ)** کی تصنیف بتایا ہے" — the author of *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya* is not known;
    Muhammad Din Fauq stated it to be the composition of Hazrat Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Bandagi Lahori,
    **d. 1040 AH** (Muhammad Din Fauq, *Tazkira-e Ulama wa Mashaikh-e Lahore* p. 11[OCR?]) (p. 129,
    folio 117, حاشیہ 1). The death year matches the archive row's "8 Muharram 1040 AH" exactly, from an
    independent 1974 editorial hand.
  - **Note what the editor does and does not say.** He reports Fauq's attribution while opening the
    footnote with the statement that the author's name is *not known* — so the book records the
    attribution without endorsing it. The archive should carry it as Fauq's claim, disputed.
  - The *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya*, whatever its author, is the source for the Mujaddid's Shia-convert
    karamat the text tells at pp. 129–130 (folios 117–118) — so the Lahore shrine is linked, if Fauq is
    right, to an early Naqshbandi-Mujaddidi tazkira.
  - Nothing here about the shrine, its urs, custodianship or building. Chunk 011's footnote at p. 125
    (folio 113) remains the only locational statement about it in these chunks.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **The Naqshbandi chain, twenty-two names, as the chapter's frame** (p. 127, folio 115): Abu Bakr
  Siddiq → Salman Farsi → Imam Qasim son of Abu Bakr → Imam Jafar Sadiq → Bayazid Bistami → Abu al-Hasan
  Kharqani → Shaikh Abu Ali → Khwaja Abu Yusuf Hamdani → Khwaja Abd al-Khaliq Ghujdawani → Khwaja
  Muhammad Arif → Khwaja Mahmud Khair[OCR?] Faghnawi → Khwaja Azizan Ali → Khwaja Baba Samasi → Khwaja
  Mir Kulal → Shaikh Baha al-Din Naqshband → Khwaja Ala al-Din Attar → Khwaja Yaqub Charkhi → Khwaja
  Ubaid Allah Ahrar Naqshband → Khwaja Muhammad Zahid → Khwaja Darwish → Khwaja Muhammad Khwajgi →
  Khwaja Baqi Billah Dehlvi. **"اول امام اس خاندان عالی شان کے حضرت ابوبکر صدیق رضی اللہ عنہ ہیں"** —
  the first imam of this noble family is Abu Bakr Siddiq, against the Chishti and Qadiri chains' descent
  from Ali.
- **Khwaja Muhammad Baqi, famous as Baqi Billah Naqshbandi Dehlvi** (entry no. 82, pp. 127–128,
  folios 115–116): an imam of tariqat in his time. His **outward** bai'at was at the hand of Khwaja
  Muhammad Khwajgi Amkangi[OCR?], but his **inward nisbat** was with Baha al-Din Shah Naqshband, and he
  drew much faiz from the *ruhaniyat* of Khwaja Ubaid Allah Ahrar. He travelled early to **Kabul** and
  there took "علوم فقہ و حدیث و تفسیر سے بہرہ کامل [OCR?]", a full share of fiqh, hadith and tafsir. His
  regimen is quantified: he ate little, slept little, "ضرورت کے بغیر کسی سے ہم کلام نہ ہوتے", did not
  speak without need, and **"نماز عشا کے بعد نماز تہجد تک دو ختم قرآن شریف کے کرتے۔ بعد تہجد صبح تک
  اکیس مرتبہ سورۃ یٰسین پڑھنا آپ کا روزمرہ کا ورد تھا"** — between Isha and Tahajjud he completed the
  Quran twice, and from Tahajjud to dawn recited Sura Yasin twenty-one times as his daily *wird*
  (p. 128, folio 116). Died Monday the 25th[OCR?] of Jumada al-Thani 1012 AH, **aged forty**; mazar in
  the city of Delhi, a place of pilgrimage. The editor's essay on him is under Arguments.
- **Shaikh Ahmad Mujaddid Alf-e Thani Faruqi Kabuli Sirhindi** (entry no. 83, pp. 129–130,
  folios 117–118): titled Ghaus al-Alamin, Qutb al-Aqtab, "دافع بدع و ضلالت", repeller of innovation and
  error; imam of the Silsila Aliya Naqshbandiya Mujaddidiya; paternal genealogy to Umar Faruq through
  **twenty-eight** intermediaries (the editor refers the reader to "ضمیمہ ثانی کتاب ہذا ص ۲۹۷", the
  book's second appendix, p. 297). **Bai'at in four silsilas** — see Practices. His *Maktubat* is accepted
  among the ulama and mashaikh, and "سلسلہ حضرت کا آج تک جاری ہے اور قیامت تک جاری رہے گا", his silsila
  continues to this day and will continue until the Day of Judgement. **"اگرچہ علمائے ظاہر ہی نے حضرت کی
  عداوت پر کمر بستہ رکھی، مگر خدا کی امداد اور پیران عظام کی حمایت سے فتح یاب نہ ہوئے"** — though the
  ulama of the outward kept themselves girded for enmity against him, by God's aid and the great pirs'
  support they did not prevail (p. 130, folio 118). Birth "باقوال صحیح", by correct reports, 971 AH;
  death Tuesday at dawn on the last day (سلخ) of Safar, in the year 1035; age sixty-three; **mazar at
  Sirhind**, with a chronogram carrying ۱۰۳۵. The editor overturns the death year: see Arguments.
- **Khwaja Khawand Mahmud Lahori, famous as Hazrat Ishan Naqshbandi** (entry no. 84, pp. 132–134,
  folios 120–122): born a wali, Qutb al-Irshad; descent from **Khwaja Ala al-Din Attar**, Baha al-Din
  Naqshband's greatest khalifa, through his father Mir Sayyid Sharif. Outward bai'at at the hand of
  **Khwaja Abu Ishaq Safid**[OCR?] — whom the editor identifies as **Khwaja Muhammad Ishaq Dahbidi**, of
  the descendants of Makhdum-e Azam Khwaja Ahmad Kasani (*Azami* 139) — "مگر نسبت اویسی شاہ بہاءالدین
  نقشبند کے ساتھ تھی", but his nisbat was **Uwaisi** with Shah Baha al-Din Naqshband. Born in the city of
  **Bukhara**[OCR?]; the editor gives the year as **971 AH** (*Azami* 139) (p. 133, folio 121, حاشیہ 3).
  At twenty he finished the outward sciences and went to the city of **Khash**[OCR?], then lived some
  years in **Kabul**, then some years in **Kashmir**, where "ہزارہا مخلوق کو نسبت نقشبندیہ سے سرفراز
  کیا", he honoured thousands with the Naqshbandi nisbat; then toured Hindustan — **Delhi, Akbarabad** and
  other great cities — and "لاہور میں رونق افروز ہوئے", graced **Lahore**. He meant to return home
  "مگر شاہجہان بادشاہ کی التجا سے یہاں ہی قیام کیا", but stayed here **at Emperor Shah Jahan's request**
  (p. 133, folio 121) — which the editor rewrites as an order of banishment: see Arguments. His son
  **Khwaja Muin al-Din wrote *Kitab-e Rizwani*** on him, "صدہا خوارق و کرامات حضرت کے درج کیے ہیں",
  recording hundreds of his marvels; the editor calls it "ایک اہم تذکرہ", an important tazkira, with a
  copy at the **Jammu & Kashmir Cultural Academy, Srinagar** (p. 133, folio 121, حاشیہ 5). Died
  12 Shaban 1052 AH; **mazar in Lahore** (no quarter given). The demolition order against his rauza is
  under Practices.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Saeed Naqshbandi Mujaddidi** (entry no. 85, pp. 134–135, folios 122–123): **second
  son** of the Mujaddid; thousands reached completion through him. Died 1070 AH; **mazar at Sirhind**;
  age eighty. The editor adds much: his **maktubat were published from Lahore by the late Hakim Abd
  al-Majid Saifi**; Khwaja Muhammad Hashim Kishmi mentions his *Taliqat-e Mishkat al-Masabih*; **born
  1005 AH, died 27 Jumada al-Thani 1070 AH** (*Rauzat al-Qayyumiya* 1/291–192[OCR?]); and **he had eight
  sons and five daughters**, the sons named as Shah Abdullah, Lutf Allah, Maulvi Farrukh Shah, Sa'd
  al-Din, Shaikh Abd al-Ahad Wahdat, Khalil al-Rahman, Muhammad Yaqub and Muhammad Taqi (*Hadiya-e
  Ahmadiya* 8–9) (p. 134, folio 122, حاشیہ 1). See Doubts on the age.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Masum Naqshbandi Mujaddidi Sirhindi** (entry no. 86, pp. 135–136, folios 123–124):
  **"فرزند میانگی"**, middle son of the Mujaddid, Qutb al-Mashaikh. At sixteen he finished the outward
  sciences and "دستار فضیلت کی باندھی", tied the turban of distinction, then completed inwardly under his
  father. His refusal of Aurangzeb's patronage and his three lakh murids are under Practices. He
  travelled from Hindustan to the **Haramain Sharifain** for the Hajj and ziyarat. Born in the year 1009;
  died in the year 1080. The editor: he was his father Imam Rabbani's **janishin**; "لاتعداد انسانوں کو
  آپ سے ظاہری و باطنی فیض حاصل ہوا"; his works are *Maktubat-e Masumiya* in **three volumes** and *Azkar-e
  Masumiya* (p. 135, folio 123, حاشیہ 1) — and his correct death year is 1079 AH: see Arguments.
- **Khwaja Muin al-Din, son of Khwaja Khawand Mahmud Naqshbandi Lahori** (entry no. 87, opening only,
  pp. 136–137, folios 124–125): the entry breaks off after "بعد وفات پدر بزرگوار کے", after his
  father's death. The editor's footnote is the longest in the chunk and is summarised under Arguments and
  Practices; the essentials: **Mulla Muin al-Din Kashmiri**, a leading and prolific scholar, was
  **appointed by his father to reside at the family khanqah in Kashmir** for the guidance of the people;
  he was also **a student of Shaikh Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi**; and ten of his works are listed with
  shelfmarks.
- **Khwaja Muhammad Siddiq Peshawari** (p. 136, folio 124): the narrator of the mule miracle, whom the
  editor names as one of Muhammad Masum's "محبوب ترین خلفا", most beloved khalifas, with **eighteen
  letters in volume one of the *Maktubat-e Masumiya*** addressed to him (nos. 18, 56, 59, 66, 84, 111,
  224[OCR?], 113, 118, 129, 149, 146, 129[OCR?], 133, 138, 139, 144).
- **Mulla Badr al-Din Mujaddidi** (p. 134, folio 122), narrator of the betel-leaf miracle, identified by
  the editor as "مصنف حضرات القدس اور وصال احمدی", author of *Hazarat al-Quds* and *Wisal-e Ahmadi*; and
  **Khwaja Abd al-Khaliq Sirhindi**, murid of **Shaikh Adam Banuri**, named by the editor as the author
  of the *Tazkira-e Adamiya* the text cites anonymously (p. 134, folio 122, حاشیہ 2).
- **Shah Sikandar Kaithali and Shah Kamal Kaithali** (p. 129, folio 117): the Mujaddid's Qadiri chain
  runs through them. See Doubts — **Shah Kamal** is a name the archive also carries, for Lahore.
- **Khan-e Dauran, subahdar of Lahore** (p. 134, folio 122): "جو خشک ملا تھا اور مشائخ عظام کے ساتھ اس کی
  کمال عداوت تھی", a dry mulla with utter enmity to the great mashaikh. His demolition order is under
  Practices.
- **Shaikh Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi** (pp. 128, 137; folios 116, 125): correspondent of Baqi Billah,
  author of *Kitab al-Makatib wal-Rasail* and of *Risala Tahqiq al-Ishara*, and teacher of Mulla Muin
  al-Din.
- **Places named**: **Sirhind**, burial place of the Mujaddid and of Muhammad Saeed (pp. 130, 135; folios
  118, 123); **Kabul**, where both Baqi Billah and Khawand Mahmud studied or lived (pp. 127, 133; folios
  115, 121); **Bukhara**[OCR?], **Khash**[OCR?], **Kashmir**, **Akbarabad**, **Delhi**, **Peshawar**,
  **Lahore** and the **Shalamar Bagh** (pp. 127–136); and, among library locations, **Quetta**,
  **Karachi**, **Gujrat (Pakistan)**, **Srinagar**, **Tonk**, **Patna**, **Calcutta**, the **India
  Office, London**, and **Kazan** (pp. 128–137).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The book's own rule of inclusion, stated once and decisive for the whole archive's use of it.** At
  the head of the Naqshbandi chapter, after the twenty-two-name chain: **"چونکہ بزرگان مرقوم الصدر سے
  کسی کا مزار پنجاب کی سرزمین میں نہ تھا۔ لہذا احوال مفصل ان کا قلم انداز ہوا اور خواجہ باقی باللہ سے
  آغاز کیا گیا"** — since none of the elders listed above had a mazar **in the land of the Punjab**, their
  detailed circumstances were passed over by the pen, and a beginning was made with Khwaja Baqi Billah
  (p. 127, folio 115). The selection criterion is **a grave in the Punjab**, not birth, not activity, not
  silsila. It explains the Lahore density of chunks 009–011, the Delhi and Sirhind figures who are
  nonetheless admitted, and what a search of this book can and cannot be expected to yield.
- **A demolition order against a newly built Naqshbandi rauza in Lahore, and the mujawir's answer on
  authority.** From *Kitab-e Rizwani*: "بعد وفات حضرت کے جب روضہ تعمیر ہوا تو **خان دوران صوبہ دار لاہور**
  … مجاور کو بلا کر کہا کہ **خاندان نقشبندیہ میں کسی بزرگ کا روضہ آج تک نہیں بنا۔ بلکہ شاہ نقشبند کا
  بھی روضہ نہیں ہے۔ اس کو گرا دینا چاہیے**" — after the Hazrat's death, when the rauza was built,
  Khan-e Dauran, subahdar of Lahore, summoned the **mujawir** and said that in the Naqshbandi family no
  elder's rauza had ever been built, that even Shah Naqshband has none, and that this one should be
  pulled down. The mujawir: **"مجھ کو گرانے کا اختیار نہیں ہے۔ آپ کو اختیار ہے تو گرا دو"** — I have no
  authority to demolish it; if you have the authority, demolish it. The next day the subahdar came in
  person and "حاکمانہ حکم دیا کہ روضہ گرا دیا جائے", gave a magisterial order that it be demolished; on
  his way back, riding to the **Shalamar Bagh**, his horse stumbled, he fell and broke his neck, lived
  three days and died — "نعوذ باللہ من غضب الاولیاء" (p. 134, folio 122). Four things at once: a **named
  office** acting against a **named shrine**; a **doctrinal objection to tomb-building from inside
  Naqshbandi practice**, made by a hostile official; a **mujawir** as the shrine's resident officer, whose
  answer turns on jurisdiction; and a construction date — the rauza was built just after 12 Shaban
  1052 AH. Compare the same closing formula after the Dipalpur land case in chunk 008 (p. 89).
- **A saint who refused a jagir from an emperor who was his disciple.** Of Muhammad Masum: "صحبتِ اغنیا
  سے ان کو کمال نفرت تھی۔ اگرچہ **شاہ اورنگ زیب عالمگیر حضرت کا مرید و کمال معتقد تھا، مگر حضرت کبھی اس
  کے دربار میں نہ گئے اور نہ زر نقد و جاگیر وغیرہ کی طمع کی**" — he had utter aversion to the company of
  the rich; although Aurangzeb Alamgir was his murid and utterly devoted, the Hazrat never went to his
  court and never coveted cash or a jagir or the like (pp. 135–136, folios 123–124). The clearest
  statement in these chunks of a shrine's *refusal* of state endowment, against the Chishti entries'
  langars and the jagir farmans of chunk 008.
- **Following counted at three lakh.** "مرید حضرت کے جو ان کی توجہ موجہ سے درجہٴ قرب تک پہنچے تین لاکھ
  سے زائد تھے" — his murids who reached the degree of nearness through his attention were more than
  **three lakh** (p. 136, folio 124). The largest figure in these chunks, above Taunsa's one lakh
  (chunk 011).
- **The Mujaddidiya defined as a confluence of four silsilas.** "طریقت میں ان کو چار سلاسل نقشبندیہ،
  قادریہ، چشتیہ، سہروردیہ میں بیعت تھی" — he held bai'at in four orders: **Naqshbandi** with Baqi Billah;
  **Qadiri** with Shah Sikandar Kaithali, and he with Shah Kamal Kaithali; **Chishti** through his own
  father Shaikh Abd al-Ahad, and he with Shaikh Rukn al-Din, and he with **Abd al-Quddus Gangohi** (the
  saint of chunk 009's entry no. 52); **Suhrawardi** also through Shaikh al-Ahad from Shaikh Rukn al-Din
  Gangohi. And then the definition: **"چونکہ اس سلسلہ میں چار سلسلوں کا فیض ہے۔ اس واسطے اس کو سلسلہ
  مجددیہ کہتے ہیں"** — since this silsila carries the faiz of four silsilas, it is called the Mujaddidiya
  silsila (p. 129, folio 117). This is the book's own account of a label the archive attaches to rows
  such as **ganj-e-inayat-sarkar** and **mohra-sharif-khanqah**; it is a doctrinal definition, not a claim
  about either shrine, and should be cited as such.
- **A quantified nightly devotional office** — two complete Quran recitations between Isha and Tahajjud,
  twenty-one Sura Yasins from Tahajjud to dawn (p. 128, folio 116). The only wird in these chunks given
  with numbers.
- **A khanqah staffed by appointment of a son.** Khawand Mahmud "نے انھیں کشمیر میں اپنی خانقاہ میں مقیم
  ہو کر رشد و ہدایت خلق کے لیے مقرر فرمایا تھا" — appointed Muin al-Din to reside at his own khanqah in
  Kashmir for the guidance of the people (p. 136, folio 124, حاشیہ 3). A second seat maintained after the
  shaykh's own move to Lahore.
- **A tafsir written in thanks for a change of reign, and presented at court.** Mulla Muin al-Din on his
  own *Zubdat al-Tafasir*: **"میں عرصہ دراز سے ان بدعات و اہوا کی وجہ سے پریشان تھا جو اس ملک میں رائج
  ہیں۔ بیس سال کے بعد عالمگیر کا دور آیا اور مجھے اور ملک کو اطمینان حاصل ہوا تو بطور شکریہ میں نے یہ
  تفسیر لکھ کر بادشاہ کے حضور میں پیش کی"** — I was long troubled by the innovations and caprices current
  in this country; after twenty years Alamgir's era came, and I and the country found peace, so in thanks
  I wrote this tafsir and presented it before the emperor (p. 137, folio 125, quoting the ms at the
  Saidiya Library Tonk via Qazi Muhammad Imran in *Maarif*, March 1967, p. 231). Read with the same
  footnote's statement that he "اکبر، جہانگیر اور شاہجہان کے عہد کی پیدا کردہ بدعات کے خاتمہ کے لیے
  کوشاں رہے", strove to abolish the innovations produced under Akbar, Jahangir and Shah Jahan.
- **Manuscripts in private Pakistani hands, named.** *Kalimat al-Sadiqin* "مملوکہ پروفیسر قریشی احمد
  حسین احمد گجرات پاکستان" (p. 128, folio 116); two anti-opposition risalas "مملوکہ مولانا محمد ہاشم جان
  مجددی کراچی" (p. 132, folio 120); *Risala Mahmudiya* "حال ملک اقبال بک بائنڈنگ لاہور", currently with
  Iqbal Book Binding, Lahore (p. 133, folio 121); *al-Marfaqat al-Qulub* and *Fatawa-e Naqshbandiya*
  with Haji Muin al-Din of Lahore and with Mulla Yaqub, book-dealer of **Quetta** (pp. 133, 137; folios
  121, 125). Provenance recorded at the level of a living owner's name and town.
- **Autographs treated as relics.** A **Sahih Bukhari in Mulla Muin Kashmiri's own hand**, and Abd
  al-Haq's *Risala Tahqiq al-Ishara* copied by him **in 1059 AH**, both preserved at the Saidiya Library
  Tonk (p. 137, folio 125); three of Bahlol Barki's manuscripts were similarly noted as autographs in
  chunk 010.
- **Print reach**: *Manaqib-e Ahmadiya wa Maqamat-e Saidiya*, Delhi 1282 AH[OCR?], with an **Arabic
  translation printed at Kazan in 1896**; Friedman, *Shaykh Ahmad Sirhindi*, Montreal 1971, cited in Latin
  script inside an Urdu footnote; Zawwar Husain, *Hazrat Mujaddid Alf-e Thani*, **Karachi 1974** — the
  latest imprint in these chunks and consistent with this edition's own date (pp. 130–132, folios
  118–120).
- **A manuscript facsimile is reproduced as a plate**: "مکتوب شریف امام ربانی مجدد الف ثانی", a noble
  letter of the Mujaddid, "بنام [illegible] دست مبارک ایشان [OCR?]", to an illegible addressee in his own
  blessed hand, taken "از عرفانیات باقی [OCR?] مرتبہ نظام الدین احمد کاظمی حیرت۔ مطبوعہ دہلی ۱۹۷۰ء"
  (p. 131, folio 119). The edition illustrates itself with a document; the document's text is lost to the
  transcription (see Doubts).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A Shia reader struck by Ali for throwing the Mujaddid's *Maktubat* down.** From *Tazkira-e
  Mujaddidiya*: a man of the Shia Imamiya madhhab opened the *Maktubat*, saw a page praising Abu Bakr,
  "فوراً کتاب کو ہاتھ سے پھینک دیا۔ منہ سے بھی کلمات تبرا کہے", threw the book down at once and spoke
  words of *tabarra*. In sleep he saw the Mujaddid come and say "اے مردود … تو سزا کے لائق ہے", O
  accursed one, you deserve punishment, and drag him before **Ali Murtaza**, who rose and struck a slap
  on his face; the shock woke him, "آنکھوں سے خون جاری دیکھا", he saw blood flowing from his eyes, and in
  a mirror found his face entirely black. He repented, came in the morning and became a murid, and "مرید
  ہوتے ہی چہرہ کی سیاہی جاتی رہی", the blackness left his face the moment he did (pp. 129–130,
  folios 117–118). The only sectarian karamat in these chunks, and the punishment is delivered by Ali.
- **The palas leaf that became a betel roll.** Narrated by Mulla Badr al-Din Mujaddidi of Shaikh Muhammad
  Saeed: the Hazrat ate the *pan ka bira*, retied the empty **palas leaf** and threw it to him; he picked
  it up reverently, found it empty, "حاضرین مجلس اس بات پر متبسم ہوئے اور میں نادم ہوا", the company
  smiled and he was ashamed; he tucked the leaf into his turban to save face, and at home found it had
  become a betel roll, ate it and "وہ لذت پائی کہ تمام عمر نہ بھولا", tasted such a savour as he never
  forgot all his life (pp. 134–135, folios 122–123).
- **Khan-e Dauran's broken neck**, three days after ordering the rauza pulled down (p. 134, folio 122) —
  the second death by a broken neck immediately after acting against a shrine in these chunks, after the
  Dipalpur claimant in chunk 008.
- **Muhammad Masum appears to a man dragged by a mule.** Muhammad Siddiq Peshawari, riding from Peshawar
  to Sirhind, was thrown but caught by one foot in the stirrup; "میں زندگی سے ناامید ہوا اور بحالت
  ناامیدی حضرت کو یاد کیا", despairing of life he remembered the Hazrat, who appeared, seized the mule
  and freed his foot — and was gone before he could kiss his feet (p. 136, folio 124).
- Of the Mujaddid the author says "کرامت و خوارق لاکھوں حضرت کے وجود برکت آمود سے ظاہر ہوئیں۔ جن کا
  اظہار زبان اور قلم سے نہیں ہو سکتا", lakhs of marvels appeared from him which tongue and pen cannot
  express (p. 129, folio 117); of Khawand Mahmud, that *Kitab-e Rizwani* records hundreds (p. 134,
  folio 122).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Two death years corrected in one line each, both against the text and both for the chapter's central
  figures.** Of the Mujaddid, whose death the text and chronogram both put in 1035 AH: **"حضرت مجدد کا
  صحیح سال وفات ۱۰۳۴ھ ہے"** — the Mujaddid's correct death year is **1034 AH** (p. 130, folio 118,
  حاشیہ 2). Of his son Muhammad Masum, whose death the text puts in 1080: **"خواجہ محمد معصوم کا صحیح
  سال وفات ۱۰۷۹ھ ہے (رکن چہار علویہ[OCR?])"** — the correct death year is **1079 AH** (p. 136, folio 124,
  حاشیہ 2). Neither correction argues; each is asserted, the second with a bracketed and OCR-flagged
  source. **Both are one year earlier than the text**, which is the signature of a lunar-year conversion
  or of a death falling in the last days of a Hijri year — and the Mujaddid's death is placed by the text
  on "سلخ ماہ صفر", the last day of Safar, which makes the year boundary the likely site of the error.
- **A genealogy pruned by preferring the nearest witness.** Against the text's "تاج الدین حسین" in
  Khawand Mahmud's line: **"ملا معین الدینؒ نے اپنے نسب نامہ میں کسی تاج الدین حسین کا ذکر نہیں کیا۔
  لہذا یہاں یہ نام زائد ہے"** — Mulla Muin al-Din mentioned no Taj al-Din Husain in his own genealogy, so
  the name is superfluous here; and **"خواجہ محمد اعظم نے شجرہ نسب کی جو تحقیق پیش کی ہے (اعظمی ص ۱۶۸)
  وہ ملا معین کے مقابلہ میں اہمیت نہیں رکھتی"**, the genealogical research of Khwaja Muhammad Azam has no
  weight against Mulla Muin's. He then prints Mulla Muin's own chain: Muin al-Din bin Khawand Mahmud bin
  Mir Sayyid Sharif bin Khwaja Muhammad Zia al-Din bin Khwaja Mir Muhammad bin Khwaja Ala al-Din Hamid
  Husain bin Khwaja Ala al-Din Attar (p. 133, folio 121, حاشیہ 1). **The son outranks the historian** —
  the same criterion of nearness to the subject that produced the "no contemporary testimony" refusal in
  chunk 010.
- **A request turned into a banishment.** The text says Khawand Mahmud stayed in Lahore "شاہجہان بادشاہ
  کی التجا سے", at Shah Jahan's *entreaty*. The editor: **"کشمیر میں اہل تسنن و تشیع کے اختلاف اور آپ کی
  مداخلت کی وجہ سے شاہجہان نے انھیں کشمیر سے لاہور ہجرت کرنے کا حکم دیا تھا"** — because of the
  Sunni–Shia dispute in Kashmir and his own intervention in it, Shah Jahan **ordered** him to migrate
  from Kashmir to Lahore (*Azami* 138) (p. 133, folio 121, حاشیہ 4, with eight further references
  including *Badshah Nama* 2/1/33 and *Amal-e Salih* 3/428). **The most consequential correction in this
  chunk for the archive: the shrine's presence in Lahore changes from an imperial invitation to a
  sectarian expulsion, and the editor cites two Mughal chronicles for it.**
- **He names an anonymous author, and declines to name another.** Of *Tazkira-e Adamiya*, cited by the
  text as "مولف تذکرہ آدمیہ", he supplies "خواجہ عبدالخالق سرہندی مرید حضرت شیخ آدم بنوریؒ" (p. 134,
  folio 122, حاشیہ 2). Of *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya* he says the author's name is not known and reports
  Fauq's attribution to Muhammad Tahir Bandagi Lahori without adopting it (p. 129, folio 117, حاشیہ 1).
  Two anonymous sources, two different verdicts, on adjacent pages.
- **He tells the reader which works are lost.** Of the Mujaddid's writings, seven are listed as published
  and then: "**تعلیقات عوارف المعارف** اور **رسالہ جذب و سلوک** ابھی تک دریافت نہیں ہوئے ہیں" — the
  *Taliqat-e Awarif al-Maarif* and *Risala Jazb wa Suluk* have not yet been found (p. 130, folio 118,
  حاشیہ 1). The fourth such report in four chunks.
- **His historical thesis on Baqi Billah, stated as his own.** "اگر سولھویں صدی کے آخر اور سترھویں صدی
  کے شروع کی مذہبی اور روحانی تاریخ کا غور سے مطالعہ کیا جائے تو یہ حقیقت واضح ہو جائے گی کہ **حضرت
  خواجہ باقی باللہؒ کی ذات گرامی احیائے سنت کی تمام تحریکوں کا منبع تھی**" — a careful study of the
  religious and spiritual history of the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries makes clear that
  Khwaja Baqi Billah's person was **the source of all the movements for the revival of the Sunna**; when
  the Mujaddid and Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi took up the revival of the religious sciences they held it
  necessary to draw on his lights. He then reads Abd al-Haq's reply to Baqi Billah's query about the
  *Risala Faqr-e Muhammadi* as "ہندوستان کے حالات پر ایک بصیرت افروز تبصرہ", an insight-kindling
  commentary on the state of Hindustan, in which **"پردے ہی پردے میں آپ نے عہد اکبری کے سب فتنوں کی طرف
  اشارہ کر دیا ہے اور اپنے پریشان دل کی دھڑکن کو اپنے پیر و مرشد کے کانوں تک پہنچا دیا ہے"** — under
  cover he pointed to all the fitnas of the Akbari era and carried the beat of his troubled heart to his
  pir's ears (*Kitab al-Makatib wal-Rasail* p. 19) (p. 128, folio 116, حاشیہ 1). A reading of a letter as
  veiled political testimony — the editor's most interpretive paragraph in these chunks.
- **Opposition treated as a documentable subject.** He refers the reader to a whole modern monograph for
  the Mujaddid's opponents — Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi, *Ahwal wa Athar-e Abdullah Khweshgi Qasuri*,
  Lahore 1972 (p. 130, folio 118, حاشیہ 1) — and lists two manuscript refutations of the opponents among
  his twenty sources (p. 132, folio 120). The book records the controversy rather than suppressing it.
- **A bibliography that reaches outside the tradition.** The twenty-item list for the Mujaddid includes
  a **Montreal 1971** monograph in Latin script and a **Kazan 1896** Arabic translation alongside
  manuscripts in private hands in Karachi and Gujrat (pp. 130–132, folios 118–120) — the widest source
  base the editor assembles for anyone in these chunks.

## Doubts

- **"شاہ کمال کیتھلی" must not be mapped to shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal.** The Mujaddid's Qadiri chain runs
  "شاہ سکندر کیتھلی اور ان کی بخدمت شاہ کمال کیتھلی" (p. 129, folio 117) — Shah Kamal **of Kaithal**. The
  archive's row is **Baba Shah Kamal** at Muslim Town / Samanabad near Ichhra, **Lahore**, also Qadiri,
  with no dates. The nisba here is to a different town and the chunk gives no grave, no date and no
  Lahore connection. **Leave unmapped**, and note that the shared name plus the shared order makes this a
  likely future mis-match.
- **The Mujaddid's death year has three readings in this chunk.** Text: "سلخ ماہ صفر سال ایک ہزار
  پینتیس", the last day of Safar 1035, with age sixty-three. Chronogram: ۱۰۳۵. Editor: 1034 AH (pp. 130,
  folio 118). Against a birth of 971 AH "باقوال صحیح", 1034 gives sixty-three lunar years and 1035 gives
  sixty-four — so **the age the text states supports the editor's year, not the text's own**. Record all
  three and the arithmetic.
- **Muhammad Saeed's stated age contradicts the editor's dates by fifteen years.** The text says he died
  1070 AH "اور عمر اسّی برس کی", aged eighty (p. 135, folio 123); the editor's footnote gives birth
  1005 AH and death 27 Jumada al-Thani 1070 AH (p. 134, folio 122, حاشیہ 1), which is **sixty-five**. The
  editor does not remark on the age. Do not carry "eighty" without this note.
- **Muhammad Masum's dates are internally consistent but the editor moves one.** Text: born 1009, died
  1080 (p. 136, folio 124) — seventy-one years, with no age stated. Editor: correct death year 1079 AH,
  on a bracketed and OCR-flagged authority, "(رکن چہار علویہ[OCR?])". The source cannot be identified
  from the chunk.
- **A whole illustrated document is lost to the transcription.** The plate at p. 131 (folio 119) carries
  "[illegible: 1 line]" and "[illegible: 24 lines]" — twenty-five lines of what the caption calls a
  letter of the Mujaddid in his own hand, addressed to an "[illegible]" recipient. Only the caption
  survives: "ماخوذ از عرفانیات باقی [OCR?] مرتبہ نظام الدین احمد کاظمی حیرت۔ مطبوعہ دہلی ۱۹۷۰ء". **A
  human must re-read this page from the PDF before anything is said about the letter**, including who it
  was addressed to.
- **Khawand Mahmud's arrival in Lahore has two incompatible accounts on one page** — the text's
  "التجا" (entreaty) and the editor's "حکم" (order), with a sectarian cause supplied only by the editor
  (p. 133, folio 121). Any entry using this must give both; the editor's version has two Mughal
  chronicles behind it and the text's has nothing cited.
- **Khawand Mahmud's Lahore mazar has no location.** The text says only "مزار گوہر بار لاہور میں ہے"
  (p. 134, folio 122), and the editor adds no quarter, unlike his notes on Jan Allah (Bagh Maharan
  Singh[OCR?]), Abd al-Karim (Chauburji[OCR?]) and Haji Ramzan (near Tahir Bandagi's darbar). The
  demolition story places it on a road toward the Shalamar Bagh, which is a direction of travel and not
  an address.
- **His birthplace is OCR-flagged.** "شہر بخارا [OCR?]" (p. 133, folio 121); so is the next city he moved
  to, "شہر خش [OCR?]".
- **Baqi Billah's death day is flagged**: "بروز دوشنبہ پچیسویں [OCR?] جمادی الثانی ۱۰۱۲ ہجری" (p. 128,
  folio 116). The year is unflagged and his age forty is given; 1012 − 40 would place his birth near
  972 AH, which the chunk never states.
- **No archive row is the subject of an entry in this chunk**, and three of the six saints are buried
  outside Pakistan — Delhi, Sirhind, Sirhind. Only **Khawand Mahmud** (Lahore, 1052 AH) is a Pakistani
  shrine, and he has no archive row. Candidate for a future row, not a match for an existing one; in
  particular **do not attach him to ganj-e-inayat-sarkar or mohra-sharif-khanqah** on the strength of the
  shared Naqshbandi label — those rows' figures died in 2011 and are unnamed here.
- **The four-silsila definition of the Mujaddidiya (p. 129, folio 117) is a doctrinal statement, not
  evidence about any shrine.** It is quotable for rows whose `silsila` reads Naqshbandi-Mujaddidi, but the
  chunk does not connect it to any of them.
