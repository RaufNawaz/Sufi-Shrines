# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 016 — pp. 168–177

**The Suhrawardi chapter, entries 106 (tail) to 116: the Bukhari sayyids of Uch, then their
descendants and khalifas in Lahore.** One archive row is the subject of a full entry
(**shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht**, entry no. 107, with birth, death and the Uch roza);
a second (**shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari**) appears only as the genealogical head of the
family. The chunk's three most valuable features for the archive are: **the editor calls the
author's death year for Shaikh Musa Ahangar of Lahore flatly wrong and replaces it from a
near-contemporary biography**, moving it thirty-seven years; a **named occupational group, the
dhobis, holds a saint's urs**; and a **Muslim mazar is placed in a basement inside the Badshahi
Qila at Lahore**, with a note on what the land was before Akbar built the fort.

**Folio markers: eight of ten agree with the relation, two do not.** p. 168 → folio 156 through
p. 175 → folio 163 read exactly folio = PDF index − 12. But **p. 176 reads "[folio 174]" and p. 177
reads "[folio 175]"**, where the relation gives 164 and 165. See Doubts — this looks like an
uncorrected digit slip (۱۶۴ → ۱۷۴), and it affects the folio citation for entries 114, 115 and 116.
**Confirmed by chunks 019–022:** the markers run − 2 from p. 176 to p. 197 and then revert to − 12 at
p. 198 (p. 197 → "folio 195" followed by p. 198 → "folio 186", a backward jump that is impossible in
a printed sequence), and p. 206 is marked "folio 194" as p. 196 also was. **The relation is right and
the markers for pp. 176–197 are ten too high**, so the two folios cited "as printed" below are
truly 164 and 165.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht** (سیّد جلال الدین بخاری الملقّب بمخدوم جہانیاں جہاں گرد بن
  سیّد احمد کبیر بن سیّد جلال الدین شیر شاہ میر سُرخ بخاری اوچی — entry no. 107, pp. 168–169,
  folios 156–157):
  - **Dates as printed.** Birth "چہارم شعبان المعظم شب شنبہ سنہ سات سو سات" — 4 Shaban, Saturday
    night, **707** AH; death "دہم ماہ ذی الحجہ روز عید اضحیٰ سنہ سات سو پچاسی" — 10 Zil-Hijja, the
    day of Eid al-Adha, **785** AH (p. 169, folio 157). The archive row's `figure_died` reads 1384,
    which is the CE equivalent of 785 AH; the book supplies the Hijri date, the day of the week for
    the birth and the festival day for the death, none of which the row carries.
  - **The shrine.** "روضہ بمقام اوچ زیارت گاہ خلق ہے" — the **roza is at the place Uch** and is a
    place of pilgrimage for the people (p. 169, folio 157). No quarter, no building date, no
    custodian named in the entry itself; but see Practices for the manuscript held by the sajjada
    nashin of Uch Bukhari.
  - **Four successive initiations, in order.** First bai'at at the hand of his father **Sayyid Ahmad
    Kabir**, receiving the khirqa of khilafat of the **Suhrawardi** path; then inward faiz from
    **Shaikh Sadr al-Din, famous as Muhammad Ghaus**; then, at Sadr al-Din's direction, murid of
    **Shaikh Rukn al-Din Abu al-Fath Multani**, completing and receiving the khirqa of khilafat;
    then Hajj, and at **Mecca** he stayed with **Shaikh al-Islam Shaikh Afif al-Din Abdullah Matri**
    for outward and inward benefits; then "بقدم تجرید سیر تمام روئے زمین کی کی" — he toured the whole
    face of the earth on the foot of detachment, drawing faiz from hundreds of guiding awliya
    (p. 168, folio 156).
  - **Titles as printed**: "چراغ خاندان مظہر انوار ربانی مطلع تجلیات سبحانی کاشف رموز طریقت ہادی
    طرائق حقیقت صاحب ارشاد پیشوائے اوتاد" (p. 168, folio 156).
  - **His father's death is given only approximately, by the editor**: Sayyid Ahmad Kabir was a murid
    of Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif and "۷۱۳ھ [OCR?] کے بعد انتقال کیا", died **after 713 AH [OCR?]**
    (Muhammad Ayub Qadri, *Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahan Gasht Bukhari* pp. 27, 35) (p. 168, folio 156,
    حاشیہ 1).
  - **His sajjada passed to his brother, not his son** — see entry no. 109 under Other saints and
    the succession note under Practices.
  - **The Medina sanad karamat** is under Legends. Nine further sources on him are listed by the
    editor (p. 169, folio 157); his malfuzat corpus and the editor's claim for it are under
    Arguments.
- **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari** (سیّد جلال الدین شیر شاہ میر سُرخ بخاری اوچی — p. 168,
  folio 156; again as "سید جلال الدین میر سرخ بخاری اوچی", p. 176, folio 174 as printed):
  - **Named twice, both times only as the head of the Uch Bukhari line**: as Makhdoom Jahaniyan's
    grandfather in the entry heading of no. 107, and as the ancestor from whose progeny Sayyid Haji
    Abd al-Wahhab Bukhari Dehlvi came (entry no. 114). The nisba "میر سرخ بخاری اوچی" and the
    position at the head of the Uch family match the archive row.
  - **Nothing is said in this chunk about his own dates, shrine, urs or custodianship.** He is a
    genealogical anchor here and nothing more; do not draw shrine facts from these two mentions.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **The tail of entry no. 106, subject unnamed in this chunk** (p. 168, folio 156): birth "سنہ ۵۷۷ھ
  [OCR?]"; death "بائیسویں ربیع الاول سن سات سو سینتیس ہجری" — 22 Rabi al-Awwal **737** AH; age
  **160 years**; and "بزرگان خاندان سہروردیہ سے کسی بزرگ نے اتنی عمر نہیں پائی" — no elder of the
  Suhrawardi family attained such an age. "مزار پر انوار حضرت کا موضع موعلاۃ [OCR?] ملتان میں
  زیارت گاہ خلق ہے" — his mazar is at **mauza Mu'ala'at [OCR?], Multan**, a place of pilgrimage. The
  entry heading is in chunk 015, so the name is not recoverable here: see Doubts, and do not attach
  these dates to any Multan row.
- **Sayyid Sadr al-Din, known as Shaikh Rajan Qattal, son of Sayyid Ahmad Kabir Bukhari Uchi**
  (entry no. 108, pp. 169–171, folios 157–159): **brother of Makhdoom Jahaniyan**; took the khirqa
  of khilafat "اپنے باپ اور بھائی دونوں سے", from **both his father and his brother**. Jahaniyan said
  of him: "خالق حقیقی نے ہم کو خلقت کے ساتھ مشغول کیا ہے اور صدر الدین کو اپنی ذات کے عشق میں مستغرق
  کر رکھا ہے" — the true Creator has occupied us with the creation and has drowned Sadr al-Din in
  love of His own essence (p. 170, folio 158). Death "بقول صاحب معارج الولایت بتاریخ سولہویں جمادی
  الآخر سن آٹھ سو ستائیس ہجری" — per the author of *Ma'arij al-Wilayat*, 16 Jumada al-Akhir **827**
  AH; **mazar at Uch** (p. 171, folio 159). The editor adds birth **20 Shaban 760 AH / 1359 CE**, the
  dispute over his laqab (see Arguments), his relations with Firoz Shah Tughluq and the village and
  cash he was given (see Practices), and six further sources (p. 169, folio 157, حاشیہ 2; p. 170,
  folio 158, بقیہ حاشیہ).
- **Sayyid Nasir al-Din bin Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jalal al-Din Bukhari Uchi** (entry no. 109, p. 172,
  folio 160): although the sajjada after Jahaniyan's death was Sadr al-Din Rajan Qattal, this saint
  too was engaged in guidance by his father's instruction, and "لاکھوں طالبان خدا ان کی دستگیری سے
  مقام قرب تک پہنچے", lakhs of seekers reached the station of nearness by his support. **Called "نر
  ناصر الدین"** — *Nar* Nasir al-Din — because of his many offspring: the number of his sons
  "ایک سو تک پہنچ گئی تھی", reached **one hundred**, and of all his children those who lived long
  were **twenty-one sons and two daughters**. Death **847** AH; "مزار گوہر بار اوچ میں زیارت گاہ خلق
  اللہ ہے", mazar at **Uch**, a place of pilgrimage.
- **Shaikh Abd al-Jalil, known as Qutb al-Alam Chuhar Bandagi Qureshi Harithi Hakari Lahori** (entry
  no. 110, pp. 172–173, folios 160–161): pedigree through four intermediaries to **Shaikh Hamid
  al-Din Abu al-Hakim, "بادشاہ کیچ مقران"**, king of Kech Makran — Abd al-Jalil bin Abu al-Fath bin
  Abd al-Aziz bin Shihab al-Din bin Nur al-Din bin Sultan al-Tarikin Hamid al-Din Abu al-Hakim.
  First bai'at with his own father; then travelled far countries and drew faiz from hundreds of
  elders; "آخر لاہور میں آکر سکونت اختیار کی", finally settled in **Lahore** and engaged in guidance;
  "پنجاب کے رہنے والوں نے خدمت حضرت کو اپنا فخر سمجھا", the people of the **Punjab** counted service
  to him their pride. The editor dates his arrival: he came "۸۸۰ھ / ۱۴۷۵ء کے حدود میں", **around
  880 AH / 1475 CE** (*Nami, Tarikh-e Jalila* 150) (p. 172, folio 160, حاشیہ 2). His death, burial in
  his own khanqah and the emperor at his ghusl are under Practices; the meaning of "چوہڑ" is under
  Arguments.
- **Shaikh Jamal al-Din Abu Bakr bin Abu al-Fath** (p. 172, folio 160, حاشیہ 3 and بقیہ): **full
  brother of Abd al-Jalil**; by his murshid's order made **Agra** his home and began preaching and
  guidance; died **1545 CE** in the era of Sher Shah Suri; "مزار جوگی لودر [OCR?] آگرہ میں ہے"
  (*Ghausi Mandwi, Azkar-e Abrar*, translation of *Gulzar-e Abrar* 226). He wrote broad commentaries
  on the *Wasaya* of Imam Muhammad and the *Usul* of Bazdawi, and **a large book on his brother's
  life and stations, composed between 947–952 AH / 1540–1545 CE**, which the editor names
  *Tazkira-e Qutbiya* — "یہ تذکرہ نہایت اہم ہے اور لاہور کی ثقافتی و مذہبی تاریخ کا ایک قابل قدر
  ماخذ ہے", this tazkira is extremely important and a valuable source for the cultural and religious
  history of Lahore — published from **Lahore in 1952** by Nami (pp. 172–173, folios 160–161).
- **Sayyid Usman, famous as Shah Jhula Bukhari Lahori** (entry no. 111, pp. 173–174, folios 161–162):
  came **from Uch to Lahore**; a master of kashf and karamat, "بہت سے لوگ ان کے مرید تھے اور ہنگامہ
  مشیخت گرم ہوا", many were his murids and the tumult of shaykhhood grew warm. Pedigree to Makhdoom
  Jahaniyan in nine steps: Usman bin Mahmud bin Baha al-Din bin Hamid bin Muhammad bin Rukn al-Din
  bin Hamid Bukhari "المخاطب بہ نوبہار صاحب دستار بند" bin Nasir al-Din bin Jalal al-Din Makhdoom
  Jahaniyan. **The laqab explained by a disease**: he had a tremor (رعشہ) in his left arm, "اس واسطے
  بخطاب جھولہ مخاطب ہوئے کہ جھولا پنجابی زبان میں رعشہ کو کہتے ہیں" — he was addressed as *Jhula*
  because *jhula* is the **Punjabi** word for tremor. Death 8 Rabi al-Awwal **912** AH; his mazar is
  under Practices.
- **Shaikh Ilm al-Din Chuniwal** (entry no. 112, p. 174, folio 162): khalifa of Abd al-Jalil Chuhar
  Lahori; "پیر روشن ضمیر کے حکم سے ان کو خدمت کپڑے دھونے کی سپرد تھی" — by his pir's order **the
  service of washing clothes** was entrusted to him; utterly devout, ascetic and secluded; after
  completion he was posted by his murshid's order to **qasba Chunian** and spent his whole life there
  guiding people. Died **916** AH; "روضہ حضرت کا اسی علاقہ میں ہے", his rauza is in that same area.
  **"دھوبی لوگ حضرت کا عرس کرتے ہیں"** — the **dhobis (washermen) perform his urs**. The editor
  reports a different affiliation for him: see Doubts.
- **Shaikh Musa Ahangar Lahori** (entry no. 113, pp. 174–175, folios 162–163): "آہنگر", the
  blacksmith; among Abd al-Jalil Chuhar Bandagi's khalifas, greatly devout and ascetic and a man of
  ishq, jazb, sukr and karamat. **First a murid of Shaikh Shahr Allah Multani**, who at his last hour
  gave him leave to go to Lahore and complete under Shaikh Chuhar, which he did. Death in the text
  "سنہ نو سو پچیس ۹۲۵ھ" — **925 AH** — which the editor rejects outright (see Arguments). **Mazar in
  Lahore, "متصل قلعہ گوجر سنگھ المشہور بہ گنبد سبز"**, adjoining **Qila Gujjar Singh**, famous as
  the **Gumbad Sabz** (Green Dome), a place of pilgrimage; "روضہ خرد اس عورت نو مسلمہ کا پاس حضرت
  کے ہے" — the **small rauza of the newly-Muslim woman** of the spindle miracle stands beside his
  (p. 175, folio 163). The editor adds his genealogy up to Imam Husain, birth in the environs of
  **Multan** on 27 Rajab **818 AH [OCR?]** (*Manaqib-e Musawi* p. 15), and his chief khalifas: Mir
  Hashim Bukhari, Haji Ishaq Sindhi, Shaikh Mohri[OCR?], Makhdoom Ilm al-Din bin Abd al-Salam
  Khokhar, Shah Nur, Shah Rizq Allah Niazi (*Manaqib-e Musawi* 70–84) (p. 175, folio 163, حاشیہ 3).
- **Shaikh Shahr Allah Multani** (p. 175, folio 163, حاشیہ 2): a scholar and gnostic of his time, **d.
  23 Zil-Hijja 920 AH**; three sons — Baha al-Din, Shaikh Muhammad Ismail, Muhammad Sadr al-Din; "آپ
  ملتان میں اپنے مشائخ کے پہلو میں مدفون ہیں", **buried at Multan beside his own mashaikh**. Also
  named as author of *Tazkira-e Hamidiya*, translated by Nami, Lahore 1959 (pp. 169, 173; folios 157,
  161).
- **Shaikh Sayyid Haji Abd al-Wahhab Bukhari Dehlvi** (entry no. 114, p. 176, folio 174 as printed):
  of the progeny of Sayyid Jalal al-Din Mir Surkh Bukhari Uchi; his paternal nisbat meets **Sayyid
  Muhammad Ghaus bin Sayyid Jalal al-Din**; "سید صدر الدین بخاری سے ان کی بیعت تھی", his bai'at was
  with Sayyid Sadr al-Din Bukhari. First lived in **Multan**, then Hajj and the Haramain, then
  settled in **Delhi**. **"سلطان سکندر لودھی حضرت کا مرید تھا"** — Sultan Sikandar Lodhi was his
  murid. Among his writings, **a tafsir of the whole Quran read as praise of the Prophet**: "ہر ایک
  آیت کے ایسے معنی بیان کیے ہیں کہ ان سے پیغمبر خدا … کی مدح ظاہر ہوتی ہے", he gave every verse such
  meanings that the Prophet's praise appears from them. Death **932** AH, "اور شیخ حاجی مادہ تاریخ
  ہے" — "Shaikh Haji" is the **chronogram** of the year. The editor adds: born **869 AH**; mother
  Fatima bint Qutb al-Din bin Kabir al-Din bin Ismail bin Mahmud Chishti Bukhari; **after Multan he
  went to Delhi and drew faiz from Shaikh Abdullah bin Yusuf Qurashi Multani**; his tafsir **begun
  Rabi al-Thani 915 AH and completed 17 Shawwal 915 AH**; also *Risala Shamail al-Nabawi* and qasidas
  in the Prophet's praise (p. 176, folio 174 as printed, حاشیہ 1). See Doubts on the two pirs.
- **Sayyid Jamal al-Din Suhrawardi Dehlvi** (entry no. 115, p. 177, folio 175 as printed): "برادر
  حقیقی و مرید و خلیفہ", **full brother, murid and khalifa** of Abd al-Wahhab Bukhari Dehlvi; by an
  inward sign he went from Hind to **Kashmir**, where thousands were honoured by his bai'at; a book
  ***Jamal-e Irfan*** was written on his marvels; after touring Kashmir he returned to Hindustan and
  died **948** AH. The editor: his chain of bai'at reaches **Makhdoom Jahaniyan through six
  intermediaries**; he worked in Kashmir "آخری سلاطین کشمیر کے عہد میں", in the era of the last
  sultans of Kashmir, then returned to Delhi; **"حضرت مخدوم حمزہ کشمیری کی تربیت آپ کی توجہ کا نتیجہ
  ہے"**, Makhdoom Hamza Kashmiri's training was the fruit of his attention; died 948 AH (*Muhammad
  Azam, Tarikh-e Kashmir Azami* p. 82) (p. 177, folio 175 as printed, حاشیہ 1).
- **Makhdoom Shaikh Hamza Kashmiri** (entry no. 116, opening only, p. 177, folio 175 as printed): the

- **Nawahon, Hindu royal agent (عامل شاہی) resident at Uch** (pp. 170–171, folios 158–159): the

- **Qazi Abd al-Muqtadir and his son Muhammad** (p. 171, folio 159): the Delhi scholar whose

- **Shaikh Nur Qasuri and Shaikh Atu[OCR?] Baghban** (p. 175, folio 163, حاشیہ 1): both murids of

- **Makhdoom Nau Bahar Shah, sajjada nashin of Uch Bukhari** (p. 169, folio 157, حاشیہ 1): the living

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A named occupational group holds a saint's urs.** Of Shaikh Ilm al-Din Chuniwal, whose service
  under his pir had been the washing of clothes: **"دھوبی لوگ حضرت کا عرس کرتے ہیں"** — the *dhobi*
  people perform the Hazrat's urs (p. 174, folio 162). The service assigned in life and the group
  that keeps the urs are the same trade, and the book states the connection by placing the two
  sentences together without comment. The only urs in this chunk given a named sponsoring community.
- **A mazar inside the Lahore Fort, with a note on the land's earlier status.** Of Shah Jhula
  Bukhari: **"مزار پُر انوار بادشاہی قلعہ لاہور کے اندر تہ خانہ میں ہے کہ تعمیر قلعہ اکبری سے اول وہ
  زمین متعلق شہر کے تھی"** — his mazar is in a **basement (تہ خانہ) inside the Badshahi Qila,
  Lahore**, for **before the building of Akbar's fort that land belonged to the city** (p. 174,
  folio 162). A precise, checkable statement about a grave inside a monument and about the fort's
  encroachment on urban ground, dated by implication to after 912 AH (his death) and before Akbar's
  construction.
- **Burial inside a khanqah, with the sultan present at the washing.** Of Abd al-Jalil Chuhar
  Bandagi: on 1 Rajab ("غرہ رجب") **910 AH** he was in assembly when his state changed and he died
  with his head in prostration; **"غسل کے وقت سلطان سکندر لودھی بادشاہ حاضر ہوا"** — Sultan Sikandar
  Lodhi was present at the ghusl; after the washing the name of the Divine Essence issued three times
  from his tongue and all heard it, so the bier was held back an hour until all were certain, "تو
  جنازہ اٹھا کر خانقاہ کے اندر لاہور میں دفن کیا کہ اب تک مزار گوہر بار زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے" —
  then the bier was raised and he was **buried inside the khanqah in Lahore**, and to this day the
  mazar is a place of pilgrimage (p. 173, folio 161). A khanqah converted into a shrine by the burial
  of its founder, with a reigning sultan as witness.
- **A land grant and a cash grant to a saint, with the figure.** Of Rajan Qattal: he had good
  relations with **Firoz Shah Tughluq** and "آپ اس کے لشکر میں بھی رہے", he even stayed with his
  army; **"بادشاہ نے آپ کو ایک گاؤں اور دو ہزار تنکے نقد پیش کیے"** — the king presented him **one
  village and two thousand tankas in cash** (*al-Durr al-Manzum* p. 25 [OCR?]) (p. 170, folio 158,
  بقیہ حاشیہ). The most exactly quantified endowment in these chunks, and it comes from the editor,
  not the author.
- **A protocol of royal reception, stated as this family's standing rule.** When Rajan Qattal came to
  Delhi, **"حسب قاعدہ اس خاندان کے کہ بادشاہ تین میل استقبال کو آتا تھا بادشاہ سوار ہوا"** — by the
  rule of this family the king came **three miles** to receive them, so the king mounted (p. 171,
  folio 159). An institutionalised ceremonial claim of the Uch Bukhari house on the Delhi sultanate.
- **A capital case over a conversion, tried between a shrine and a court.** Nawahon, a **Hindu royal
  agent (عامل شاہی) resident at Uch**, came to Makhdoom Jahaniyan's deathbed and made a speech
  declaring him the seal of the awliya as the Prophet was the seal of the messengers; Jahaniyan held
  that the speech proved confession of *tawhid* and *risalat*, told Rajan Qattal "اب یہ مسلمان ہو
  چکا ہے۔ اگر پھر مرتد ہو گیا تو واجب القتل ہے", he is now a Muslim, and if he apostatises he is
  liable to death. Nawahon fled by night to **Delhi** and petitioned the king; **Firoz Shah** himself
  urged Islam on him and he refused; after Jahaniyan's burial Rajan Qattal travelled to Delhi "اسی
  مقدمہ کے فیصلہ کے لیے", for the decision of that very case; the king assembled ulama to find a
  shar'i argument to save the man; the argument failed (see Legends); "پھر نواہون کو بلایا اور
  بادشاہ نے قبول اسلام کے لیے بہت کچھ کہا، مگر اس نے نہ مانا اور گردن مارا گیا" — Nawahon was
  summoned, the king pressed him hard to accept Islam, he refused, **and he was beheaded**
  (pp. 170–171, folios 158–159). A shrine's ruling, a sultan trying to evade it, and an execution.
- **Succession recorded as passing sideways, with a co-active son.** "اگرچہ بعد وفات مخدوم جہانیاں
  صاحب سجادہ سید صدر الدین راجن قتال تھے، مگر یہ بزرگ بھی اپنے والد کے ارشاد کے بموجب ہدایت و ارشاد
  میں مصروف تھے" — although after Jahaniyan's death the **sajjada holder** was his brother Sayyid
  Sadr al-Din Rajan Qattal, his son Nasir al-Din too was engaged in guidance by his father's
  instruction (p. 172, folio 160). The book distinguishes the seat from the authority to guide.
- **A hundred sons, twenty-one surviving, as a recorded fact about a lineage** (p. 172, folio 160) —
  the demographic base of the Uch Bukhari sayyid families, stated as the reason for the epithet *Nar*
  Nasir al-Din.
- **A manuscript in the hands of a named living sajjada nashin.** "مظہر جلالی کا خطی نسخہ مخدوم نو
  بہار شاہ سجادہ نشین اوچ بخاری کے پاس ہے" — the manuscript of *Mazhar-e Jalali* is with **Makhdoom
  Nau Bahar Shah, sajjada nashin of Uch Bukhari** (Muhammad Ayub Qadri, *Makhdoom Jahaniyan* 256)
  (p. 169, folio 157, حاشیہ 1). The custodianship of the shrine and the custodianship of its archive
  are the same office.
- **Other manuscripts located by owner and shelfmark**: *Fawaid al-Mukhlisin*, compiled by Sayyid
  Muhammad Jafar Tumasi, "خطی نسخہ مخزونہ کتب خانہ دانش گاہ پنجاب نمبر ۱/۱۲۹۳/۴۳۴۶" — **Panjab
  University Library no. 1/1293/4346** (p. 168, folio 156, حاشیہ); *Manaqib al-Wilayat*, "قلمی
  مملوکہ خلیفہ الہٰ داد خان صاحب اوچ", owned by **Khalifa Ilah Dad Khan of Uch** (p. 170, folio 158);
  *Siraj al-Hidaya* "قلمی مملوکہ محمد اقبال مجددی لاہور" (p. 169, folio 157); *Nur Nama* of Baba
  Nasib al-Din Kashmiri, "قلمی ذخیرہ شیرانی کتب خانہ دانش گاہ پنجاب نمبر ۶۳۸/۳۶۵۹" — **Sherani
  collection, Panjab University no. 638/3659** (p. 177, folio 175 as printed).
- **A shrine composed of two graves, one of a woman convert.** Beside Shaikh Musa Ahangar's mazar
  stands "روضہ خرد اس عورت نو مسلمہ کا", the small rauza of the newly-Muslim woman of the spindle
  miracle (p. 175, folio 163). The only instance in this chunk of a subsidiary grave named as part of
  a shrine, and the only woman's grave named at all.
- **Print history of the tradition's own literature, as the editor records it**: *Latai'f-e Ashrafi*,
  Delhi 1299 AH; *al-Durr al-Manzum*, translation of the *Malfuz al-Makhdoom*, Delhi 1309 AH;
  *Tazkira-e Hamidiya*, Nami's translation, Lahore 1959; *Sab'-e Sanabil*, Kanpur 1299 AH;
  *Tazkira-e Makhdoom Jahaniyan*, Deccan 1962; Muhammad Ayub Qadri's *Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahan
  Gasht*, **Karachi 1963**; *Tazkira-e Qutbiya*, Nami's edition, Lahore 1952; *Tarikh-e Jalila*,
  Lahore 1960; *Azkar-e Qalandari*, Nami's edition, Lahore 1957; *Manaqib-e Musawi* (1152 AH; also
  given as ۱۱۵۷ھ [OCR?]), Urdu translation by Muhammad Ali, Lahore 1961; *Wird al-Muridin*, Lahore
  1894; *Tarikh-e Kashmir Azami*, Kashmir 1355 AH (pp. 169–177). **Ghulam Dastgir "Nami" is the
  editor of four of these** — the single busiest modern hand behind the Lahore Suhrawardi record as
  this book uses it.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The Prophet answers from the tomb and settles a claim of sayyid descent.** From *Mazhar-e Jalali*:
  when Makhdoom Jahaniyan reached **Medina**, the sharifs of Medina demanded the **sanad of his
  sayyid-hood**; he went to the Prophet's roza and said "السلام علیک یا رسول اللہ و ابی و جدی", and
  from within a loud voice came, "علیک السلام یا ولدی یا قرۃ عینی" — peace be upon you, O my son, O
  coolness of my eye; seeing this karamat all accepted his sayyid descent "اور بزرگی آپ کی سب پر
  ثابت ہو گئی" (p. 169, folio 157). A miracle whose function is documentary: it supplies a missing
  certificate.
- **A saint's glance kills an eloquent lawyer in mid-argument.** The qazi-zada's plan was that the
  king should ask Rajan Qattal whether he had come for the case of "نواہون کافر", Nawahon the
  unbeliever, so that a yes would concede the man's unbelief; Rajan Qattal answered "نہیں ہم نواہون
  مسلمان کے مقدمہ کے لیے آئے ہیں", no, we have come for the case of Nawahon the *Muslim*, with
  reliable witnesses to his Islam in company. When the qazi-zada pressed that sincerity of heart is
  required for conversion, the saint came into *jalal*: **"تیری تقریر سے ہم کو دیانت کی بُو نہیں آتی
  اور تیری اجل کا وقت اگر نزدیک نہ ہوتا تو ہم تجھ سے تقریر کرتے"** — from your speech we catch no
  scent of honesty, and were the hour of your death not near we would argue with you. At the words he
  was seized with a pain in the side and thrashed on the ground "مرغ نیم بسمل کی طرح", like a
  half-slaughtered fowl; the king sent him to his father at once, and before Qazi Abd al-Muqtadir's
  plea for pardon was finished a man ran from the house with news that he was dead (p. 171,
  folio 159).
- **A compensating prophecy, verified by counting.** Refusing the father's plea, Rajan Qattal said the
  son had already departed, told him to see to the burial, and that God would give a better recompense
  because "عورت اس کی حاملہ ہے", his wife was pregnant, and the child would be "عالم و فاضل و ولی",
  a scholar and a wali; the qazi went home and **found his wife two months pregnant** (p. 171,
  folio 159).
- **The blacksmith, the spindle and the gold.** From the tazkira (which the editor renames — see
  Arguments): a beautiful young woman brought her *takla*, the spindle women spin with, to Shaikh
  Musa Ahangar's shop to be straightened; he took the fee, put it in the furnace, worked the bellows
  with one hand and held the spindle with tongs in the other, and gazed at her face without lifting
  his eyes for a full hour. She grew angry: "تو عجب سفید ریش بزرگ آدمی ہے کہ بیگانی عورتوں کی طرف
  شہوت کی نظر سے دیکھتا ہے" — you are a strange white-bearded elder, to look at other men's women
  with the eye of lust. He answered **"میں تجھ کو نہیں دیکھتا بلکہ تیرے مصور کو دیکھتا ہوں"**, I do
  not look at you but at your Painter, drew the red-hot spindle from the fire and **drew it across his
  eyes like kohl**, praying: "الٰہی اگر میں نے اس عورت کو بد نظر سے دیکھا ہے تو میری آنکھیں جلا دے۔
  ورنہ اس لوہے کے سُرمچو کو اپنی محبت و عشق کی پارس سے سونا بنا دے" — O God, if I looked at this
  woman with an evil eye, burn out my eyes; else turn this iron kohl-stick to gold by the touchstone
  of Your love. The iron *surmachu* turned to gold at once; the Hindu woman was astounded, renounced
  the world that same day, sat in a corner of worship and rose to high degrees by service to him
  (p. 175, folio 163). Her grave beside his is under Practices.
- **The name of God on a washed corpse's tongue.** After Abd al-Jalil's ghusl the Divine Name issued
  three times from his tongue and all present heard it, so that people thought him still alive and the
  bier was delayed an hour (p. 173, folio 161).
- **Marvels counted rather than told.** Of Makhdoom Jahaniyan: "ہزارہا خوارق و کرامت ان سے سرزد
  ہوئے۔ جن کی تشریح کتب سیر میں لکھی ہے", thousands issued from him, expounded in the books of *siyar*
  (p. 168, folio 156); of Rajan Qattal, his marvels in books are countless "مگر لاکھوں میں سے ایک
  لکھے جاتے ہیں", but one out of lakhs is written here (p. 170, folio 158); of Abd al-Jalil, his
  brother's book records thousands (p. 173, folio 161); of Sayyid Jamal al-Din, *Jamal-e Irfan*
  records innumerable ones (p. 177, folio 175 as printed).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor declares the author's death year wrong, and replaces it from a near-contemporary
  life.** Of Shaikh Musa Ahangar, whose death the text puts in 925 AH: **"قریب العہد سوانح شیخ
  موسیٰ، مناقب موسوی مذکورہ میں آپ کا سال وصال بروز پنجشنبہ ۱۸ صفر ۹۶۲ھ درج ہوا ہے (ص ۶۳) اس لیے
  مفتی صاحب کا مندرجہ سنہ وفات ۹۲۵ھ غلط ہے"** — in the near-contemporary biography of Shaikh Musa,
  the *Manaqib-e Musawi* mentioned above, his year of passing is entered as **Thursday 18 Safar
  962 AH** (p. 63), **therefore the Mufti Sahib's given death year of 925 AH is wrong** (p. 175,
  folio 163, حاشیہ 2). Note three things: the editor names the author as "مفتی صاحب" and says
  *ghalat*, not merely "the correct year is"; the correction is **thirty-seven years**, the largest in
  these chunks; and the criterion is explicitly **nearness in time** ("قریب العہد"), the same standard
  that governed the genealogical rulings of chunk 012. Read with the editor's birth date of 27 Rajab
  **818 AH [OCR?]** in the adjacent footnote, 962 AH gives a life of about 144 lunar years and 925 AH
  about 107 — the editor does not remark on this: see Doubts.
- **A book's title corrected twice, in the same chunk, against the author.** The text calls Abu Bakr
  "صاحب تذکرہ جلیلیہ" and cites "تذکرہ جلیلیہ" for the spindle miracle. The editor: the brother's
  book is *Tazkira-e Qutbiya*, composed 947–952 AH / 1540–1545 CE and published by Nami from Lahore
  in 1952 (p. 172, folio 160, حاشیہ 3 بقیہ), and again, flatly, **"اس کتاب کا نام تذکرہ قطبیہ ہے،
  تذکرہ جلیلیہ نہیں"** — the name of this book is *Tazkira-e Qutbiya*, not *Tazkira-e Jaliliya*
  (p. 175, folio 163, حاشیہ 3). **The source of the chunk's central Lahore miracle is therefore
  misnamed in the text**; anything cited from "Tazkira-e Jaliliya" must be re-cited as
  *Tazkira-e Qutbiya*. Note that the editor separately lists a *Tarikh-e Jalila* by Nami (Lahore
  1960) as a distinct work, so the two titles are not interchangeable.
- **A laqab contested on philological grounds.** Of Rajan Qattal: "حضرت مخدومؒ کے لقب راجو قتال کے
  بارے میں مناقب الولایت میں تحریر ہے کہ یہ لفظ راجن کتال ہے۔ 'قتال نہیں' یہ سریانی زبان کا لفظ ہے۔
  جس کے معنی 'بزرگ' اور راجن کے معنی 'ہیبت ناک' ہیں۔ لیکن دوسرے تذکروں میں ان کے لقب کی وجہ رعب و
  جلال بتائی گئی ہے اور اسی طرح آپ معروف ہوئے" — *Manaqib al-Wilayat* holds the word to be **"راجن
  کتال"**, a **Syriac** word meaning "elder", with *rajan* meaning "awe-inspiring", and **not
  *qattal*** ("slayer"); but other tazkiras give his awe and majesty as the reason for the laqab, and
  that is how he became known (p. 169, folio 157, حاشیہ 2). The editor reports both and adopts
  neither, while the book's own heading keeps "قتّال". A case where a shrine's name carries a
  contested etymology.
- **A laqab explained from Hindi, in the book's own voice.** Of Abd al-Jalil: "چوہڑ کے معنی ہندی
  زبان میں شکار کو تدبیر سے قابو میں لانے کے ہیں۔ چونکہ حضرت نے اپنے نفس کو مجاہدہ و ضبط سے خدا کی
  بندگی کے لیے رام کر لیا تھا۔ اس لیے چوہڑ بندگی کے لقب سے ملقب ہوئے" — *chuhar* in **Hindi** means
  bringing game under control by stratagem, and because he had tamed his own *nafs* to God's service
  by struggle and restraint he was titled *Chuhar Bandagi* (*Nami, Tarikh-e Jalila* 143) (p. 172,
  folio 160, حاشیہ 1). With the Punjabi etymology of "جھولہ" for tremor (p. 174, folio 162), the
  chunk gives two saints' titles vernacular derivations and a third a Syriac one.
- **Malfuzat claimed as primary historical sources, and politics as the saint's field.** The editor on
  Makhdoom Jahaniyan: **"حضرت مخدوم جہانیاں جہاں گشتؒ کے اپنے معاصر سلاطین سے گہرے روابط تھے۔ آپ نے
  سیاست کو جس طریقہ سے آلودگیوں سے پاک کرنے کی کوشش کی۔ اس کی مثال بہت کم ملے گی۔ آپ کے ملفوظات کے
  مجموعے سیاسی، ثقافتی اور مذہبی تاریخ کے ماخذ کی حیثیت رکھتے ہیں"** — he had deep relations with the
  sultans of his own time; few examples will be found of the manner in which he tried to purify
  politics of its pollutions; and **his collections of malfuzat have the standing of sources for
  political, cultural and religious history**. He then lists the collections traced so far:
  *al-Durr al-Manzum*, *Siraj al-Hidaya*, *Muqarrar Nama* (maktubat and hidayat), *Khazana-e Jalali*,
  *Jawahir-e Jalali*, *Mazhar-e Jalali*, *Manaqib-e Makhdoom*, and *Fawaid al-Mukhlisin* compiled by
  Sayyid Muhammad Jafar Tumasi (p. 168, folio 156, حاشیہ). **The clearest statement in these chunks of
  the editor's own historiographical programme**: a shrine's devotional literature read as an archive
  of political history.
- **A tazkira named as a source for a city's history.** Of *Tazkira-e Qutbiya*: "یہ تذکرہ نہایت اہم
  ہے اور لاہور کی ثقافتی و مذہبی تاریخ کا ایک قابل قدر ماخذ ہے" — extremely important, and a
  valuable source for the **cultural and religious history of Lahore** (p. 172, folio 160). The same
  move as above, applied to Lahore rather than to the sultanate.
- **A different pir supplied for Shaikh Ilm al-Din, without adjudication.** The text makes him Abd
  al-Jalil's khalifa; the editor's footnote opens "شیخ علم الدین یا علیم الدین بقول صاحب اذکار
  قلندری، شیخ موسیٰؒ کے مرید تھے" — Shaikh Ilm al-Din **or Alim al-Din**, according to the author of
  *Azkar-e Qalandari*, was a murid of **Shaikh Musa** (p. 175, folio 163, حاشیہ 1). Both the name and
  the affiliation are left standing in two versions.
- **The editor points to a source for the shrine's present condition rather than describing it.** For
  Ilm al-Din: "مزار کی موجود حالت و محل وقوع کے لیے ملاحظہ ہو: نامی: تاریخ جلیلہ ۳۵۷-۳۵۸" — for the
  **present condition and location of the mazar**, see Nami, *Tarikh-e Jalila* 357–358 (p. 175,
  folio 163). A 1974 edition treating a shrine's current state as a documented question with a page
  reference, and the one place in this chunk where the edition acknowledges that a mazar has a
  present.
- **Opposition recorded, not suppressed.** Of Makhdoom Hamza Kashmiri: "اہل تشیع کشمیر آپ کے سخت
  مخالف تھے", the Shia of Kashmir were his strong opponents (p. 177, folio 175 as printed) — stated
  by the editor as a plain fact about a saint's reception, as the Mujaddid's opponents were in
  chunk 012.

## Doubts

- **Two folio markers contradict the folio = PDF − 12 relation.** p. 176 is marked "[folio 174]" and
  p. 177 "[folio 175]", where the relation gives **164** and **165**; the eight preceding markers
  (pp. 168–175 → folios 156–163) are all exact. The pattern is a single-digit slip (۱۶ → ۱۷) repeated
  on two consecutive pages, so the relation is almost certainly right and the markers wrong — but
  **every folio citation for entries 114, 115 and 116 in these notes is written "folio 174/175 as
  printed" and must be resolved by a human against the PDF** before it enters an entry.
- **The tail of entry no. 106 cannot be attributed.** p. 168 (folio 156) opens mid-entry with a birth
  of "۵۷۷ھ [OCR?]", a death of 22 Rabi al-Awwal 737 AH, an age of 160 years and a mazar at "موضع
  موعلاۃ [OCR?] ملتان". **The heading naming this saint falls in chunk 015.** The archive carries six
  Multan rows and several Suhrawardi ones; **none may be matched on these figures from this chunk
  alone**. Note also that the stated arithmetic is internally consistent (577 + 160 = 737) but that
  160 lunar years is extraordinary, and the book itself says no Suhrawardi elder attained such an age
  — which is an assertion of the claim, not a defence of it. The place name "موعلاۃ" is OCR-flagged
  and unresolved.
- **Shaikh Musa Ahangar now has two death years thirty-seven years apart, and neither fits the
  editor's own birth date comfortably.** Text: 925 AH (p. 175, folio 163). Editor: Thursday 18 Safar
  962 AH from *Manaqib-e Musawi* p. 63, with the text's year called *ghalat* (same page, حاشیہ 2).
  Editor's birth, in the very next footnote: 27 Rajab **818 AH [OCR?]** from *Manaqib-e Musawi* p. 15.
  818 → 962 is **about 144 lunar years**; 818 → 925 is about 107. **The editor corrects the death year
  from the same book that supplies the birth year and does not remark that the result is a
  144-year life.** Record all three figures, the OCR flag on the birth year, and this arithmetic; do
  not present 962 AH as settled.
- **Abd al-Wahhab Bukhari Dehlvi has two pirs on one page.** The text: "سید صدر الدین بخاری سے ان کی
  بیعت تھی", his bai'at was with Sayyid Sadr al-Din Bukhari. The editor's footnote: after his stay in
  Multan he went to Delhi and "شیخ عبداللہ بن یوسف قرشی ملتانی سے فیض حاصل کیا", drew faiz from
  Shaikh Abdullah bin Yusuf Qurashi Multani (p. 176, folio 174 as printed). *Bai'at* and *faiz* are
  not the same relation and the two may both stand, but the editor does not say so.
- **Shaikh Ilm al-Din Chuniwal's name and pir are both unsettled** — "شیخ علم الدین یا علیم الدین",
  khalifa of Abd al-Jalil per the text and murid of Shaikh Musa per *Azkar-e Qalandari* (p. 174,
  folio 162; p. 175, folio 163, حاشیہ 1). His rauza is placed only as "اسی علاقہ میں", in that same
  area, i.e. the Chunian region, with no site given; the editor refers the reader elsewhere for its
  location rather than stating it. **Do not place this shrine.**
- **Rajan Qattal's dates come from two hands and the laqab from two etymologies.** Death 16 Jumada
  al-Akhir 827 AH is given "بقول صاحب معارج الولایت", on the authority of *Ma'arij al-Wilayat*, not as
  the author's own (p. 171, folio 159); birth 20 Shaban 760 AH / 1359 CE is the editor's (p. 169,
  folio 157). 760 → 827 is 67 lunar years, which the chunk does not state. The name is "راجن قتّال"
  in the heading, "راجو قتال" and "راجن کتال" in the footnote.
- **The two archive rows this chunk touches are unequal in what they yield.** Entry no. 107 gives
  **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht** dates and the Uch roza and nothing about urs, building,
  custodianship or economy. **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari** gets **no facts at all** here
