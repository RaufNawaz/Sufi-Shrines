# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 023 — pp. 236–245

**The book's sixth "chaman" opens here — "چھٹا چمن / مجانین و مجاذیب کے ذکر میں", on the madmen
and the God-absorbed (p. 236, folio 224) — and runs entries 171 to 188, the last cut off
mid-sentence, from a majzub of Hansi in Baba Farid's day to a Lahore majzub summoned before a
British district officer.** Four archive rows take material: **ranmal-sharif**, whose saint
"حاجی محمد نوشاہ گنج بخش" is pir to four of these majzubs (entries 181–184) and whose disciples'
death years the editor contradicts on family authority in every case; **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**,
where Ranjit Singh's deathbed is told from a Lahore faqir's side; **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar**,
with Farid's movements between Hansi and Delhi and a grandson receiving a Hindu convert; and
**shrine-of-mian-mir**, named "میاں میر بالاپیر" with his khalifa Mulla Shah Qadiri. The three most
substantial features: **the editor's four flat corrections of the author's Naushahi dates**
("مفتی صاحب کا مندرجہ سال وفات ۱۱۳۱ ھ غلط ہے"); **a ruler of Kashmir refusing the ulama's execution
fatwa on a majzub who said "ہمہ اوست"** — "بحکم المجنون معذور"; and **the end of Sikh rule foretold
by a Lahore majzub** — "نو برس اور یہ سلطنت رہے گی پھر پنجاب کے مالک فرنگی ہو جائیں گے".

**Folio markers: all ten run folio = PDF index − 12**, p. 236 → folio 224 through p. 245 →
folio 233. No page breaks the relation. No `[illegible]` in this chunk; `[OCR?]` flags are listed
under Doubts.

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (میاں میر بالاپیر — within entry no. 184, p. 242, folio 230):
  - **The epithet and a khalifa named.** Hafiz Tahir, "حصول علم باطن کے لیے **حضرت ملا شاہ قادری میاں
    میر بالاپیر کے خلیفہ** کے پاس چند سال حاضر رہا" — attended for some years Mulla Shah Qadiri, the
    khalifa of Mian Mir Bala Pir (p. 242, folio 230).
  - **A hostile anecdote from a rival order's source.** "مگر **وہاں اس کو کچھ بہرہ نہ ملا** اور سخت بے
    اعتقاد ہو کر **اسلام سے بھی برگشتہ ہوگیا**۔ رشتہ زنار گردن میں ڈال لیا اور فقرائے ہنود میں مل کر جا
    بجا گدائی کرنے لگا" — he got no benefit there, became faithless, apostatised, put on the zunnar and
    begged among Hindu faqirs, until Naushah restored him (p. 242, folio 230). The author's stated
    source is "کتاب تذکرہ نوشاہی" (p. 242).
  - **What the row gains**: the book's name-form "میاں میر بالاپیر" and Mulla Shah Qadiri as his khalifa;
    and a Naushahi tazkira's claim that a seeker left Mulla Shah's circle empty-handed — to be cited
    as that tradition's view, not as the book's judgement on Mian Mir. **Nothing on Mian Mir's shrine,
    Lahore, or dates in this chunk.**

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **An urs kept in a private house, with bread baked all day.** Of Abu al-Ghaith Bukhari: "ایک روز
  **بتقریب عرس ان کے گھر میں تمام روز توا گرم رہا اور روٹیاں پکتی رہیں**" — on the occasion of an urs in
  their house the griddle stayed hot all day and breads kept baking (p. 238, folio 226). **Whose urs is
  (not stated)**; the family is that of Sayyid Haji Abd al-Wahhab Bukhari. A domestic langar-scale urs,
  the only urs in this chunk.
- **Grain distribution to mendicants at a saint's residence, regardless of religion.** At "حضرت نوشاہ کے
  دولت خانے": "حضرت نے حکم دیا کہ **ہر ایک شخص کو غلہ گندم دے دو**۔ چنانچہ سب کو مل گیا … **غلہ ختم ہو چکا
  تھا**" — the recipients were a party of "فقرائے ہنود" (p. 242, folio 230). Wheat, given by the head;
  the supply finite.
- **A ruler refuses the ulama's death fatwa on a majzub.** "**علمائے کشمیر نے کئی مرتبہ اس کے قتل کا فتویٰ
  دیا۔ مگر حاکم کشمیر نے بحکم المجنون معذور اس فتویٰ پر عمل نہ کیا**" — the ulama of Kashmir several times
  gave a fatwa for Badi Shah's execution, but the ruler of Kashmir, by the rule "the madman is excused",
  did not act on it (p. 240, folio 228). The offence: "کلمہ **ہمہ اوست** بر ملا کہتا". **State and
  ulama at odds over a saint, and the state's ground is a legal maxim, not devotion.** Neither ruler nor
  date is named.
- **A city seeks a majzub's prayer against plague.** "جب **سال ایک ہزار چھپیس** میں کشمیر میں **وبا** آئی اور
  ہزاروں لوگ مر گئے تو **شہر کے لوگ جمع ہو کر بطلب دعا دفع وبا کے اس کے پاس گئے**" (p. 240, folio 228). A
  collective civic recourse to a majzub, dated 1026.
- **A majzub's offerings redistributed on the spot.** Of Nizam Shah: "**جو روپیہ لوگ دیتے حاضرین وقت کو
  تقسیم کر دیتا**" — whatever money people gave he divided among those present (p. 245, folio 233).
- **A house built inside the Miani graveyard, with a punitive stocks.** "اپنی مرگ سے چند سال پہلے اس نے
  اپنے رہنے کے لیے **ایک مکان قبرستان میانی میں بنایا**۔ وہاں **ایک کاٹھ چوبی جس میں حکام مجرموں کے پاؤں ڈال
  کر قفل لگا دیتے تھے**۔ تیار رکھا تھا۔ جس پر غصہ ہوتا۔ اس کو پکڑ کر اس میں قید کر دیتا جب ایک دو ساعت گذرتی
  پھر چھوڑ دیتا" (p. 245, folio 233). **A living majzub resident in the graveyard where the archive
  locates four rows** (see Doubts), exercising an informal jurisdiction with the state's own instrument.
- **The informal jurisdiction meets the colonial one.** A "**قوم جوگی مسلمان**" man whose water-pot
  ("گلی کوزہ") Nizam Shah had broken over was put in the stocks for a ghari; "اس شخص نے ناراض ہو کر **استغاثہ**
  اس بات کا **بحضور میجر میگریگر [OCR?] صاحب حاکم ضلع لاہور** پیش کیا۔ وہاں سے **خدا بخش کوتوال** کے نام حکم
  جاری ہوا کہ **مدعا علیہ کو مع کاٹھ کے حاضر کرے**۔ چونکہ اس روز **شنبہ** کا دن تھا۔ دوسرے روز **یک شنبہ کی
  تعطیل** ہو گئی۔ چونکہ **خدا بخش کوتوال بھی اس کا معتقد تھا**۔ دوسرے روز خود اس کے پاس گیا" (p. 245,
  folio 233). **A complaint, a summons naming the stocks as an exhibit, the Sunday holiday, and a kotwal
  who is himself a devotee** — the fullest procedural picture of a saint before British administration
  in these chunks; the outcome is in the next chunk.
- **A Muslim majzub at the Sikh court.** "**مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ نے بحالت بیماری و آخری وقت کے ان کو بلایا** اور
  اپنی صحت کے لیے عرض کی … جب تاج شاہ **قلعے** سے نکلا" (p. 245, folio 233). The saint is summoned into the
  fort; he does not go of himself.
- **A mosque's furnishing changed for a feast.** Nizam Shah at "**مسجد محلہ سادھواں**": "امام مسجد کی طرف
  مخاطب ہو کر کہا کہ آج عید اضحیٰ کا روز ہے۔ **پرانے بوریے لپیٹ کر نئے بوریے بچھا دو**" — roll up the old mats

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The book's own taxonomy: a separate "garden" for the God-absorbed.** The heading "چھٹا چمن / مجانین و
  مجاذیب کے ذکر میں" (p. 236, folio 224), and the cross-reference "جن کا ذکر **خاندان سہروردیہ** میں تحریر
  ہو چکا ہے" (p. 238, folio 226), show the earlier chamans organised by order (khandan) and this one by
  state. **Into it the author puts a Hindu convert (no. 172), a Quran-hafiz who apostatised and returned
  (no. 184), a drinker "شراب کے نشے میں ہر وقت مستغرق" (no. 188), a man under a death fatwa for "ہمہ اوست"
  (no. 179) and two who went naked (nos. 175, 184) — and gives every one "قدس سرّہ".** For the archive,
  this is the author's explicit position that majzubs are saints, whatever their orthodoxy or order.
- **The author reports the ulama's fatwa and the ruler's refusal without taking a side, but his epithets
  do.** He calls Badi Shah "یہ بزرگ" and his grave "مزار پُر انوار" (pp. 239–240). **The book sides with the
  majzub against the ulama by praise, not by argument.**
- **The editor's method against the author: family manuscripts over the tazkira, and the correction
  stated bluntly.** Four times in three pages: "غلط ہے" (p. 241, حاشیہ 1), "مفتی صاحب کا مندرجہ سال وفات
  ۱۱۳۱ ھ غلط ہے" (p. 241, حاشیہ 2), "یہ دونوں سنین غلط ہیں … جو ہمارے نزدیک معتبر ہے" (p. 242, حاشیہ 2),
  "بھی ۱۱۳۶ ھ غلط ہے … ہونا چاہیے" (p. 243, حاشیہ 1). His authority each time is "خاندانی روایات / خاندانی
  مآخذ" as carried in **Sharafat Naushahi's *Sharif al-Tawarikh* (manuscript)** and *Tazkira Naushahi*
  (manuscript in Sharafat Naushahi's possession). **The edition here prefers a living family's own
  record to both the nineteenth-century author and to *Khazinat al-Asfiya*** (p. 242). Any archive use of
  Naushahi dates from this book must say which of the two traditions it follows.
- **The author's four Naushahi dates sit a near-constant interval later than the editor's.** 1115 against
  1076; 1131 against 1092; 1135 (or 1132) against 1094[OCR?]; 1136 against 1097[OCR?] (pp. 241–243).
  **Three of the four differ by exactly 39 years and the fourth by 41 (or 38).** This is arithmetic on the
  chunk's figures, not something either author or editor says; but a uniform offset suggests a systematic
  cause rather than four independent slips, and a human should look for it before adopting either set.
- **The editor corrects a name as well as dates.** "یہاں سہو کتابت معلوم ہوتا ہے۔ خواجہ محمد اعظم نے یہ نام
  **غیبی** تحریر کیا ہے" (p. 239, حاشیہ 1) — the entry's head-name "جیتی شاہ" is, for the editor, a
  copyist's error for "غیبی". **The edition's practice of printing the source's Persian in full** (Akhbar
  al-Akhyar on pp. 237–238, Azami on pp. 239–240) lets the reader check him; note that the author's own
  citation is only "بقول صاحب تواریخ اعظمی".
- **Regime change is registered through saints' predictions and audiences.** The end of Sikh rule is "سکھوں
  کی سلطنت کی خرابی", foretold in years and attributed to "فرنگی" (p. 244); Ranjit Singh's death follows a
  majzub's departure from the fort (p. 245); Hira Singh's killing is announced as Eid (p. 245); and the
  British appear as "میجر میگریگر [OCR?] صاحب حاکم ضلع لاہور" with a kotwal who is the saint's devotee
  (p. 245). **With chunk 022's "عملداری انگریزوں کی" and Durrani–Sikh–British sequence, the book's
  consistent stance is that the saints saw the transitions coming and stood outside them.** The author
  offers no evaluation of Sikh or British rule in his own voice.
- **The author's evidentiary habits in this section.** He names his written sources in the text
  (*Akhbar al-Akhyar*, *Tawarikh-e Azami*, *Tazkira Naushahi*), quotes an oral tradition ("شیخ حاجی محمد
  کا قول ہے", p. 238), and for a Lahore majzub dead in 1221 produces **his own friend as eyewitness** —
  "شیخ وہاب الدین جو راقم الحروف کا ایک دوست تھا۔ **اپنی آنکھ کا دیکھا ہوا حال** اس طرح بیان کرتا تھا"
  (p. 243, folio 231). **The chronogram is used once** ("مجذوب صادق" = 946, p. 237).
- **A dating clue for the composition.** "چنانچہ وہ لڑکا پیدا ہوا اور **اب تیس برس کی عمر کو پہنچ چکا ہے**"
  (p. 245, folio 233): the boy Loda was promised by Taj Shah, who died 1261 AH, so was born no later than
  that, and is thirty as the author writes. **If the birth fell near the saint's death, the author's
  "now" is near 1291 AH; if earlier, earlier.** This is arithmetic, not stated, and it is an upper bound
  consistent with the 1290–1292 AH range chunk 022 derived from other entries.
- **A rival order's slight preserved.** The book, following *Tazkira Naushahi*, has a seeker spend
  "چند سال" with Mulla Shah Qadiri, Mian Mir's khalifa, and get "کچھ بہرہ نہ" before apostatising (p. 242).
  **The author neither endorses nor softens it.** For **shrine-of-mian-mir** this is a Naushahi
  tradition's view of the Mian Mir line and should be cited as such.

## Doubts

- **ranmal-sharif: the chunk gives two death-year traditions for the saint's disciples and none for the
  saint.** The archive row carries figure_died 1654. The chunk dates Naushah's death only relatively —
  "ایک روز ان کی وفات میں باقی تھا" when Shah Abdullah, "منصبدار ہفت [OCR?] ہزاری دربار عالمگیری", took bai'at
  (p. 241, folio 229) — and then gives Shah Abdullah's own death as 1131 (author) or 1092 (editor). **A
  human must decide which tradition the row follows before any of the four disciples' dates enter it, and
  should check the row's 1654 against the "Alamgiri court" placement of the deathbed bai'at.** The chunk
  itself does not date the court.
- **ranmal-sharif: the editor's preferred years are themselves flagged in two of four cases** — "۱۰۹۴ ھ
  [OCR?]" (p. 242) and "۱۰۹۷ ھ [OCR?]" (p. 243) — and his spelling of the first disciple differs from the
  author's heading ("شیخ مطا [OCR?]" against "شیخ مٹھا", pp. 240–241). The reference "خزینۃ الاصفیا ۲/۴۲
  [OCR?]" and "شریف التواریخ نمبر [OCR?]" are flagged too (p. 242). **Nanu's burial village is flagged**:
  "موضع کلاسکے [OCR?] چیمہ ضلع گجرات" (p. 242). Only "ضلع گجرات پاکستان" is secure.
- **ranmal-sharif vs data-darbar: two "Ganj Bakhsh".** The pir here is "حاجی محمد نوشاہ **گنج بخش**"
  (pp. 240, 242). The archive's **data-darbar** row is for "Data Ganj Bakhsh" (Ali Hujwiri). **Nothing in
  this chunk concerns Hujwiri; do not let the epithet pull these entries toward Lahore.**
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh: no date for the death, and the key verb is flagged.** "مرنا [OCR?] سب
  کے واسطے ہے" (p. 245). The narrative's other elements — the summons "بحالت بیماری و آخری وقت", the fort,
  the sandalwood, the death on the faqir's exit — are unflagged. **The "nine years" prophecy is the
