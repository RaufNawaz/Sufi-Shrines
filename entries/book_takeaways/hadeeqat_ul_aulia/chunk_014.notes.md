# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 014 — pp. 148–157

Entries 95 (conclusion) to 100. **No archive row takes material** (see Doubts for four near-misses), but
this is the most valuable chunk of the eight for two reasons. First, **the editor dates a document by
arguing against the seal it carries** — the Bukhari *sanad* of Ghulam Muhi al-Din Qasuri bears Shah Abd
al-Aziz's own seal reading 1189 AH, thirteen years before Qasuri was born, and the editor rules that the
Shah reused an old seal. Second, **the author twice writes in the present tense about men he has met**,
which dates the original composition from inside the text: Abd al-Rasul of Kasur (d. 1295 AH per the
editor) is alive and blessed, and Sadiq Ali Shah's young son died "this year".

**The folio markers agree with the relation on every page**: folio = PDF index − 12, p. 148 → folio 136
through p. 157 → folio 145, each marker reading exactly that.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Shah Abu Said Mujaddidi Dehlvi** (entry no. 95 concluded, p. 148, folio 136): he took his outward
  learning from Maulana Rafi al-Din, "صاحب محدث فرزند شاہ ولی اللہ محدث دہلوی سے", son of Shah Wali
  Allah Muhaddith Dehlvi, then was completed under Shah Ghulam Ali. The river-crossing miracle is under
  Legends. In his last years he made the Hajj — **the editor dates the journey to 1249 AH** — and died on
  his way home: **"جب بمقام ٹونک پہنچے۔ بروز عید سنہ ایک ہزار دو سو پچاس منگل کے روز وفات کی"**, when he
  reached **Tonk** he died on the day of Eid, a Tuesday, in the year 1250, aged fifty-five. **"شاہ عبد
  الغنی حضرت کے صاحبزادے ان کی نعش کو دہلی میں لائے اور شاہ غلام علیؒ و مرزا جان جاناں کے مزار کی قربت
  میں دفن کیا"** — his son Shah Abd al-Ghani **brought the corpse to Delhi and buried it beside the mazars
  of Shah Ghulam Ali and Mirza Jan-e Janan**. The editor dates that son 1235–1296 AH and calls him "عالم
  اور معروف مدرس", a scholar and well-known teacher from whom a great number of ulama took faiz (p. 148,
  folio 136, حاشیہ 2).
- **Shah Rauf Ahmad Naqshbandi Mujaddidi** (entry no. 96, p. 149, folio 137): murid and khalifa of Shah
  Ghulam Ali and **maternal cousin** ("برادر خالہ زاد") of Shah Abu Said. His famous works are *Tafsir-e
  Raufi* and *Diwan-e Rafat*, and he compiled *Durr al-Maarif*, Shah Ghulam Ali's malfuzat. **"حضرت شاہ
  غلام علی نے بعد عطائے خرقہ خلافت ان کو شہر بھوپال کو مامور کیا"** — after granting the khirqa Shah
  Ghulam Ali **posted him to the city of Bhopal**, where he brought hundreds to the station of nearness.
  He died at sea: "جب جہاز دریائے محیط میں پہنچا۔ پیک اجل آیا" — when the ship reached the encircling
  ocean the courier of death came, on the Hajj voyage; **1253 is his death year**, and "شاہ ابو سعید کی
  وفات کے بعد صرف اڑھائی برس حضرت زندہ رہے", he survived Shah Abu Said by only two and a half years. The
  editor: takhallus **Rafat**, a sixth-generation descendant of the Mujaddid, **born at Rampur in 1201 AH
  / 1786 CE**, first taught suluk by **Shah Dargahi**, and **"اپنے خود نوشت حالات جواہر علویہ کے خاتمہ
  پر لکھے ہیں (۲۹۴۔ ۳۰۹)"** — he wrote his own autobiography at the end of *Jawahir-e Alawiya*, pp.
  294–309. Eleven works are listed, with manuscripts at the Punjab University library and the **Rampur
  State library** (p. 149, folio 137, حاشیہ 1).
- **Hazrat Ghulam Muhi al-Din Qasuri Mujaddidi** (entry no. 97, pp. 150–154, folios 138–142), the longest
  entry in these eight chunks: a gatherer of outward and inward perfections and "عاشق صادق رسول کریم", a
  true lover of the Prophet, with utmost love for the Companions; **"مذہب آپ کا حنفی اعظمی تھا"**, his
  madhhab was Hanafi Azami; and he held utmost conviction toward **both** Ghaus al-Thaqalain [Abd al-Qadir
  Jilani] **and** the Mujaddid. He was first the murid of his paternal uncle **Hafiz Shaikh Muhammad**,
  with whom "مکتوبات حضرت مجدد الف ثانی سبقاً سبقاً پڑھے تھے", he read the Mujaddid's *Maktubat* lesson by
  lesson (p. 150, folio 138, حاشیہ 2). On his uncle's death he went to **Delhi** and took bai'at with Shah
  Ghulam Ali — the investiture is under Practices. **Born in the year 1202, died in the year 1270, aged
  sixty-eight**, with a chronogram of unusual elaboration (see Doubts). His father was **Hafiz Shaikh
  Murtaza** in the text and **Shaikh Mustafa** by the editor's correction; his grandfather **Shaikh Ghulam
  Murtaza**; his great ancestor **Haji Hafiz Qari Abd al-Malik**, who "ملک سندھ سے آکر قصور میں سکونت
  پذیر ہوئے", came **from the country of Sindh and settled at Kasur**, and was foremost of the age in the
  science of *qirat* (p. 151, folio 139). **"ان سب بزرگوں کے مزار قصور میں ہیں"** — the mazars of all
  these elders are at **Kasur** (p. 152, folio 140), with a standing miracle of the platform under
  Legends. His son **Shaikh Abd al-Rasul** is described as living: see Practices and Doubts.
- **Shaikh Ghulam Murtaza Qasuri** (editor's footnote, p. 152, folio 140): "مفصل حالات نہیں ملتے",
  detailed circumstances are not found. **"آپ نے پنجاب میں سکھ گردی سے تنگ آکر پشاور ہجرت فرمائی"** — he
  migrated to **Peshawar**, wearied by the **Sikh gardi** in the Punjab. He was a contemporary of **Ahmad
  Shah Abdali**, and when Abdali came into the Punjab and put a question to the ulama here, "احمد شاہ
  ابدالی کو جن علماء نے جواب دیا۔ ان میں حضرت حافظ غلام مرتضیٰ کا نام بھی آتا ہے", Hafiz Ghulam Murtaza's
  name is among those who answered (*Risala Masail-e Fiqh*, ms owned by Haji[OCR?] Muin al-Din, Lahore).
  **"آپ پشاور میں فوت ہوئے۔ اور وہاں سے آپ کی نعش قصور لا کر دفن کی گئی"** — he died at Peshawar and his
  corpse was brought from there to Kasur and buried. The text separately gives his death as the year 1202
  and says he had the ijazat of all four orders, as a murid of the khalifa of **Hazrat Haji Fath Ali
  Muttaqi Sialkoti** (p. 152, folio 140).
- **Shah Ahmad Saeed Mujaddidi** (entry no. 98, pp. 154–155, folios 142–143): son, khalifa and janishin of
  Shah Abu Said, trained by Shah Ghulam Ali. After his father's death he stayed some years in Delhi;
  **"آخر جب شورش فوج انگریزی ۱۸۵۷ء میں ہوا اور تمام شہر تہ و بالا ہو گیا تو حضرت بھی مع عیال و اطفال وطن
  چھوڑ کر بیت اللہ کو چلے گئے اور وہاں ہی سکونت رکھی"** — finally, when the tumult of the English army
  came in **1857** and the whole city was turned upside down, the Hazrat too left his homeland with wife
  and children for the **House of God** and settled there, dying there in **1277 AH**. His two surviving
  sons, **Hafiz Abd al-Ghani and Abd al-Mughni**, "اب تک وطن واپس تشریف نہیں لائے اور وہاں ہی قیام پذیر
  ہیں", **have not to this day returned home and reside there** (p. 155, folio 143). The editor: born at
  **Rampur in 1217 AH**, buried in **Jannat al-Baqi**; eight works, including ***Haq al-Mubin fi Radd
  al-Wahhabin***, and *Maktubat* addressed to **Maulana Dost Muhammad Qandhari** (pp. 154–155, folios
  142–143, حاشیہ 1).
- **Muhammad Mazhar, son of Shah Ahmad Saeed** (editor's footnote, p. 155, folio 143): **d. 1301 AH**, who
  **"مدینہ منورہ میں ایک خانقاہ 'رباط مظہری' بنائی تھی، جو اب تک قائم ہے"** — built a khanqah at Medina
  named **Ribat-e Mazhari, which stands to this day**.
- **Sayyid Imam Ali Shah Samari al-Hasani al-Husaini Naqshbandi Mujaddidi** (entry no. 99, pp. 155–157,
  folios 143–145): "آخری زمانہ میں یہ بزرگ گویا قطب وقت و چشمہ فیض تھے", in the last age as it were the
  Qutb of the time and a fountain of faiz. A chain of **six** intermediaries to the Mujaddid is given —
  Imam Ali ← Miran Shah Husain ← Shaikh Haji Ahmad Mujaddidi ← Khwaja Haji Muhammad Zaman ← Khwaja Zaki ←
  Khwaja Abd al-Ahad ← Shaikh Muhammad Masum ← the Mujaddid — which the editor repairs: see Arguments.
  After Miran Shah Husain's death "مسند ارشاد پر متمکن ہو کر قبول عظیم حاصل کیا", he was seated on the
  masnad-e irshad and attained great acceptance; **"خلیفے ان کے اقالیم دور دراز میں پہنچ کر ہدایت خلق
  مصروف ہوئے"**, his khalifas reached far countries. His langar and his reputation as a healer are under
  Practices. **Born in the year 1212; died 13 Shawwal 1282 AH; "روضہ منورہ موضع رٹڑ چھتر ضلع گورداسپور
  میں ہے"**, his illuminated rauza is at mauza **Ratar Chhatar, district Gurdaspur** (p. 156, folio 144).
  His son **Sayyid Sadiq Ali Shah** took the masnad after him: see Practices.
- **Sayyid Shah Husain, known as Bhohre Wala** (editor's footnote, p. 156, folio 144): lineage to the
  Prophet; "آپ نے مروجہ علوم کی تحصیل پشاور میں کی تھی", he studied the current sciences **at Peshawar**;
  he took the khirqa of khilafat from **Hazrat Haji Ahmad, resident of Qalat**, and "پنجاب کو فیض یاب
  کیا", gave faiz to the Punjab. Died **1224 AH**, a year Imam Ali Shah himself derived from the phrase
  "مرشد محذو دمی" [OCR?] (*Mirat al-Muhaqqiqin*).
- **Shaikh Mahmud Shah Naqshbandi Mujaddidi Lahori** (entry no. 100, opening, p. 157, folio 145): "یہ
  بزرگ خدا پرست عابد اور زاہد لاہور میں سکونت پذیر تھا", a God-worshipping, worshipful and ascetic elder
  resident in **Lahore**. In the **Tariq-e Ahmadiya Mujaddidiya** he received faiz from **Shaikh Abd
  al-Karim Mujaddidi**, who had the khirqa of ijazat from Shah Ghulam Ali. "لاہور کے رہنے والے طالبان خدا
  اکثر ان کی خدمت میں جا کر فائدہ حاصل کرتے تھے", the seekers of God resident in Lahore often went to him
  and profited, and he lived his whole life "مجردانہ", in celibacy — the entry breaks off there. His tomb
  and his death year are under Practices and Arguments.
- **Named places and repositories**: **Kasur**, the Qasuri family's burial ground and the home of a
  sahibzada holding an autograph (pp. 150–154); **Rampur**, birthplace of both Rauf Ahmad and Ahmad
  Saeed, and a state library (pp. 148–155); **Tonk**, **Sambhal**, **Bhopal**, **Delhi**, **Peshawar**,
  **Sialkot**, **Qalat**, **Medina**, **Muradabad**, **Amritsar**, **Madras**, **Lyallpur**, and the
  **Ganj Bakhsh library, Rawalpindi**, which holds three of Qasuri's manuscripts (pp. 148–157).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **An investiture described step by step — the fullest initiation in these chunks.** At Qasuri's bai'at,
  Shah Ghulam Ali said "آج ایک امر عظیم نے ظہور کیا ہے کہ ایک فاضل عمدہ نے آکر ہم سے اخذ طریقہ کیا ہے",
  today a great matter has appeared, that an excellent scholar has come and taken the tariqa from us;
  then, taking both his hands and turning his face to the sky, "الہٰی جو فیض حضرت غوث الاعظمؒ کو آبائے
  کرام سے ملا تھا۔ ان کے نصیب کر", O God, allot to him the faiz that Ghaus al-Azam had from his noble
  forefathers; then raising his hand in the air, "تمہارا ہاتھ ہم نے حضرت غوث الثقلین کے ہاتھ میں دیا کہ
  ہر ایک کام دین و دنیا میں تمہارے ممد و معاون رہیں", we have placed your hand in the hand of Ghaus
  al-Thaqalain; **"پھر اپنے فرق مبارک سے کلاہ اٹھا کر حضرت کے سر پر رکھی اور اجازت سلاسل اربعہ یعنی
  قادریہ چشتیہ، نقشبندیہ و سہروردیہ عطا فرمائی"** — then he lifted the **cap from his own head and placed
  it on his**, and granted the **ijazat of the four orders: Qadiriya, Chishtiya, Naqshbandiya and
  Suhrawardiya** (p. 151, folio 139). A Naqshbandi shaykh commending a Naqshbandi initiate into the hand
  of the Qadiri founder, and conferring four-order authority in one act.
- **A saint who sold his house to build his own tomb.** Of Mahmud Shah Lahori, on Nur Ahmad Chishti's
  authority: **"آپ نے اپنا رہائشی مکان فروخت کر کے اپنا مقبرہ بنوایا تھا"** — he sold his dwelling house
  and had his own maqbara built (*Tahqiqat-e Chishti* p. 383) (p. 157, folio 145, حاشیہ 1). A shrine
  financed by the liquidation of the saint's only property, in his own lifetime, in Lahore.
- **A village renamed after the shrine in it, with a date.** **"۱۲۷۰ھ میں رٹڑ چھترہ (ضلع گورداسپور،
  مشرقی پنجاب) کا نام تبدیل کر کے مکان شریف رکھا گیا"** — in **1270 AH** the name of Ratar Chhatra
  (district Gurdaspur, **East Punjab**) was changed to **Makan Sharif** (Qaim al-Din, *Zikr-e Mubarak*
  part 1, p. 93) (p. 156, folio 144, حاشیہ 3). The change happened twelve years before the saint's death,
  so during his own tenure.
- **A langar counted in hundreds, of bread, twice daily.** Of Imam Ali Shah: **"لنگر آپ کا اس قدر جاری
  ہوا کہ صدہا مسافر و غریب و محتاج دو وقتہ روٹی حضرت کے لنگر سے کھاتے تھے"** — his langar ran to such an
  extent that **hundreds of travellers, poor and needy ate bread twice a day** from it (p. 156,
  folio 144). With Saif al-Din's four hundred (chunk 013) and Taunsa's thousands (chunk 011), this is the
  third quantified langar, and the only one that names the food.
- **A shrine as a petition office.** "حضرت کی ذات بابرکات شفائے بیماراں و حاجت روائے حاجت منداں تھی جو
  سائل و طالب کسی مطلب کا حضرت کے در دولت پر آیا۔ خالی نہ گیا" — his blessed person was the healing of
  the sick and the fulfiller of the needy's needs, and no petitioner of any object who came to his "در
  دولت", door of fortune, went away empty (p. 156, folio 144). The book's plainest statement of what a
  living shrine was for.
- **Succession, and its finances.** **Sayyid Sadiq Ali Shah** took the masnad-e irshad after his father
  and is "کمال خلیق و خوش خلق و مہمان نواز و **صاحب دولت و جاہ**", of the utmost good nature, hospitable,
  and **a man of wealth and rank** — "حق سبحانہٗ تعالیٰ نے **جامہٴ فقر میں ان کو بادشاہت بخشی ہے**", God
  has bestowed **kingship on him in the garb of faqr** (p. 157, folio 145). The book states a
  sajjada-nashin's wealth as a fact and reconciles it theologically in the same sentence.
- **A line continued through daughters.** Of Qasuri's son and janishin Abd al-Rasul (1235–1295 AH):
  **"آپ کی نرینہ اولاد نہیں تھی۔ صرف دو صاحبزادیاں تھیں۔ جن کی اولاد اب تک قصور اور پنجاب کے مختلف مقام
  میں آباد ہے"** — he had no male issue, only two daughters, **whose descendants are settled to this day
  in Kasur and various places in the Punjab** (p. 152, folio 140, حاشیہ 2). The editor writing in 1974
  about a still-identifiable descent group.
- **A saint's autograph still in family custody at the shrine town.** Among Qasuri's seventeen listed
  works, the *Risala Ilm-e Mirath* of 1227 AH is "بخط مصنف قلمی **مملوکہ صاحبزادہ ارشاد حسین شاہ قصور**"
  — in the author's own hand, owned by **Sahibzada Irshad Husain Shah, Kasur** (p. 150, folio 138,
  حاشیہ 1). Three more of his manuscripts are in the **Ganj Bakhsh library, Rawalpindi**, two with
  Dr. Maulvi Muhammad Shafi of Lahore, and four with **Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi**.
- **A Punjabi-language work by a Naqshbandi alim**: ***Zad al-Hajj* (پنجابی نظم)**, Punjabi verse, ms
  Sherani collection no. 467 (p. 150, folio 138, حاشیہ 1) — the only vernacular Punjabi composition named
  in these chunks, beside Miran Bhikha's Hindi *dohras* in chunk 010.
- **Preaching as a described practice.** Of Abd al-Rasul: "حضرت کا وعظ ایسا پُر ملاق [OCR?] و پُر تاثیر
  ہے کہ سننے والے کے دل پر اس کے مضامین نقش ہو جاتے ہیں اور جب تک حضرت وعظ میں مصروف رہتے ہیں آنکھوں سے
  آنسو نہیں تھمتے" — his *waz* is so affecting that its themes are engraved on the hearer's heart, and
  the tears do not stop as long as he preaches (p. 154, folio 142). The only account of a sermon in these
  chunks, and it is of a living man.
- **A living assembly's liturgy, recorded by an eyewitness.** In Sadiq Ali Shah's majlis "درود شریف کا
  ذکر اکثر اوقات ان کی مجلس میں ہوتا ہے اور ذکر نفی و اثبات دائمی ورد ہے" — the *durud sharif* is often
  recited and the **zikr of negation and affirmation is the constant wird** (p. 157, folio 145).
- **Two corpses moved for burial.** Shah Abu Said's from **Tonk to Delhi**, to lie beside his two
  predecessors (p. 148, folio 136); Ghulam Murtaza Qasuri's from **Peshawar to Kasur** (p. 152,
  folio 140). In both cases the destination is a family or lineage ground, not the place of death.
- **A family's flight in 1857, and its permanence.** The Mujaddidi line left Delhi for the Hijaz in 1857
  and had still not returned when the book was written (p. 155, folio 143); their descendant built the
  **Ribat-e Mazhari** at Medina, standing in 1974 (p. 155, folio 143). A Punjab-and-Delhi order's
  endowment relocated to Arabia by a colonial event.
- **A shrine's platform as its own relic.** "ان کے مزار کا چبوترہ ایسے حال میں کہ تابستان کی گرم دھوپ
  پڑتی ہو، **چبوترہ چونہ کچ** ہمیشہ سرد رہتا ہے۔ کبھی گرم نہیں ہوتا" — the *chabutra* of their mazar, of
  **lime and unbaked brick**, always stays cool under the hot summer sun and never becomes hot (p. 152,
  folio 140). The only building material named anywhere in these chunks.
- **A Punjab shrine's press and a Punjab shrine's biography.** *Khazina-e Marifat* by Muhammad Ibrahim
  Qasuri was published at **Sharaqpur**, pp. 79–88 on Imam Ali Shah (p. 156, folio 144); *Zikr-e Mubarak*
  on the notables of Makan Sharif at **Amritsar 1940**; *Anwar-e Muhi al-Din*, a life of Qasuri, at
  **Lyallpur 1966**; and *Hayat-e Maulana Ghulam Muhi al-Din Qasuri* by **Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi**,
  **1973** (p. 154, folio 142).
- **A Hindu driver's conversion as the payoff of a miracle** (p. 148, folio 136) — see Legends; the
  occupational detail, "گاڑی بان", the cart-driver, is given twice and his religion once.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A carriage driven across a river.** Shah Abu Said, travelling from Rampur to Sambhal, found no boat
  or boatman at the river after dark and told the driver to put the *bahli* into the water. The driver
  refused — "دریا میں گاڑی ڈال دینا موجب بربادی جان و مال ہے" — and the Hazrat answered "آخر میں بھی اسی
  گاڑی میں بیٹھا ہوں تو کچھ اندیشہ نہ کر", after all I too am sitting in this cart, so fear nothing.
  **"گاڑی بان اگرچہ ہندو تھا۔ لیکن توکل بخدا کر کے گاڑی دریا میں ڈال دی"** — although the driver was a
  Hindu, he trusted in God and drove in, and the cart went over the water as it had come over the land;
  and on the far bank **"گاڑی بان فوراً مسلمان ہو گیا اور مرید ہو کر جامہ درویشی پہنا"**, the driver at
  once became a Muslim and, becoming a murid, put on the garb of a dervish (p. 148, folio 136).
- **The platform that never grows hot**, at the Qasuri family's mazar in Kasur, called "دائمی کرامت", a
  **permanent** karamat, as against the one-off marvels elsewhere in the book (p. 152, folio 140).
- **A possessed woman cured a kos before she arrived.** In Lahore "ایک سنار کی عورت کو سخت آسیب تھا",
  **a goldsmith's wife was severely afflicted by an *asib***: at night she left the house unaware of her
  household, and by day was found "کبھی کسی چاہ [OCR?] میں پڑی ہوئی اور کبھی کسی قبر کی مغاک میں چھپی
  ہوئی", sometimes fallen in a well[OCR?] and sometimes hidden in the hollow of a grave; often the spirit
  threw down the walls of their house, and sometimes the roof fell of itself. They brought her to Imam
  Ali Shah, and **"جب مسکن مبارک سے ایک کوس کا فاصلہ رہ گیا۔ عورت ہوش میں آگئی اور آسیب از خود دفع ہو
  گیا"** — when one kos remained from the blessed dwelling, the woman came to her senses and the spirit
  was repelled of itself. The Hazrat, "بے دریافت حال", without inquiring, said the enemy was already gone
  and sent them home, and they returned to Lahore (p. 157, folio 145). A Lahore family's journey to a
  Gurdaspur shrine for an exorcism, and a named trade for the petitioner.
- **A young son's death borne without complaint**, told of the living Sadiq Ali Shah as a virtue rather
  than a marvel — "اس سال میں حضرت کا جوان لڑکا فوت ہو گیا۔ حضرت نے کمال صبر کیا اور کسی طرح کی شکایت
  زبان پر نہ لائے" (p. 157, folio 145).
- **A death at sea on the way to the Hajj**, told plainly: "پیک اجل آیا", the courier of death came
  (p. 149, folio 137).
- Of Qasuri the author says only that his qualities are those of a lover of the Prophet, and reserves the
  marvel-language for the mazar's platform; of Shah Abu Said, "خوارق و کرامت بہت سے ان کی ذات بابرکات سے
  سرزد ہوئے" (p. 148, folio 136).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The headline: a document dated against its own seal.** In the footnote that accompanies the facsimile
  of Qasuri's Bukhari *sanad*: **"یہاں حضرت قصوریؒ کی سند بخاری کا عکس دیا جا رہا ہے۔ یہ سند آپ نے حضرت
  شاہ عبد العزیز محدث دہلویؒ سے حاصل کی تھی۔ یہ سند خود شاہ عبد العزیز کے دست مبارک کی لکھی ہوئی ہے جس
  پر شاہ صاحب کی مہر بھی ثبت ہے۔ جس میں ۱۱۸۹ھ تحریر ہے۔ جس سے عام قاری یہی اندازہ کرے گا کہ آپ نے ۱۱۸۹ھ
  میں یہ سند حاصل کی۔ یہ قیاس درست نہیں ہے، بلکہ اس سند پر شاہ صاحب نے پرانی مہر ثبت کر دی ہے۔ حضرت
  قصوری کا سال ولادت ۱۲۰۲ھ ہے اور خود حضرت قصوری کا ۱۲۳۷ھ [OCR?] میں دہلی میں موجود ہونا آپ کی یاد داشت
  (بر خاتمہ تحفہ اثنائے عشریہ) سے ثابت ہے۔"** — the sanad is in Shah Abd al-Aziz's own hand and **bears
  his seal, which reads 1189 AH**; the ordinary reader will infer that Qasuri received it in 1189 AH;
  **"یہ قیاس درست نہیں ہے"**, that inference is not correct — **the Shah affixed an old seal on it**.
  Qasuri's birth year is 1202 AH, and his presence in Delhi in 1237 AH[OCR?] is proved from his own
  memorandum at the end of *Tuhfa-e Ithna Ashariya* (p. 151, folio 139, حاشیہ 4). **The seal's date
  precedes the recipient's birth by thirteen years; the editor keeps the document, rejects its date, and
  substitutes a date from the subject's own hand.** This is the clearest instance in these chunks of the
  editorial method the edition is valuable for.
- **A period of discipleship cut from a year to three or four months, from the subject's own book.**
  Against the text's statement that Shah Ghulam Ali kept Qasuri a year: **"آپ کا ایک سال تک شاہ غلام علی
  دہلویؒ کی خدمت میں رہنے کا کوئی ثبوت نہیں ہے بلکہ خود آپ نے ملفوظات چہل روزہ میں لکھا ہے کہ شاہ صاحب
  نے مجھے تین چار ماہ میں باطنی علوم سے نواز دیا"** — there is no proof of a year; he himself wrote in the
  *Malfuzat-e Chihil Roza* that the Shah bestowed the inward sciences on him in **three or four months**
  (p. 151, folio 139, حاشیہ 3). Note that the same footnote block identifies the *Malfuzat-e Chihil Roza*
  as **Qasuri's own compilation** of Shah Ghulam Ali's forty days of sayings — so the correction is the
  disciple against his own biographer.
- **A father's name corrected from the son's own treatise.** "آپ کے والد کا نام شیخ مرتضیٰ درست نہیں۔
  بلکہ جیسا کہ خود آپ نے **شیخ مصطفیٰ** لکھا ہے (رسالہ علم میراث مذکورہ)" — the father's name is not
  Shaikh Murtaza but **Shaikh Mustafa**, as he himself wrote in the *Risala Ilm-e Mirath* (p. 152,
  folio 140, حاشیہ 1); and the same footnote block quotes his genealogy from that treatise: "غلام محی
  الدین بن مصطفیٰ بن الشیخ حافظ المرتضیٰ" (p. 151, folio 139, حاشیہ 4). **The facsimile on p. 153
  (folio 141) carries this out**: its colophon reads "غلام محي الدين ابن حضرت صاحب … حسب الشيخ ابراهيم
  المصطفى [OCR?] … الشيخ الحافظ المرتضى" — Mustafa as father, al-Hafiz al-Murtaza as the elder. The
  plate is the evidence for the correction.
- **A spiritual chain repaired by inserting three missing links.** Of Imam Ali Shah's six-step chain:
  **"یہاں سے کچھ نام رہ گئے ہیں۔ حاجی محمد زمان مرید شیخ محمد و ہو مرید خواجہ محمد حنیف و ہو مرید خواجہ
  محمد سعید و ہو مرید شیخ عبد الاحد"** — **some names have been left out here**: Haji Muhammad Zaman was
  the murid of Shaikh Muhammad, he of Khwaja Muhammad Hanif, he of Khwaja Muhammad Saeed, he of Shaikh
  Abd al-Ahad (Qaim al-Din, *Zikr-e Mubarak* 73, part 1) (p. 156, folio 144, حاشیہ 2). The text's chain
  also runs "خواجہ زکی" where the editor's has none. See Doubts: the text's own version contradicts
  chunk 013's entry no. 91.
- **The editor's estimate of Qasuri, and his own book about him.** "حضرت مولانا غلام محی الدین قصوریؒ
  تیرھویں صدی ہجری کے جید [OCR?] عالم، **قطب پنجاب** اور شاعر باکمال تھے۔ حدیث کی سند حضرت شاہ عبد
  العزیز محدث دہلویؒ سے اور سلوک باطنی کا فیض حضرت شاہ غلام علی دہلوی سے حاصل کیا" — a leading scholar of
  the thirteenth Hijri century, **the Qutb of the Punjab**, and an accomplished poet, with his hadith
  sanad from Shah Abd al-Aziz and his suluk from Shah Ghulam Ali; followed by **seventeen numbered works
  with owners and shelfmarks** (p. 150, folio 138, حاشیہ 1). The bibliography closes with "محمد اقبال
  مجددی: حیات مولانا غلام محی الدین قصوری ۱۹۷۳ء" (p. 154, folio 142) — a monograph published one year
  before this edition, by the man named as the owner of four of the seventeen manuscripts.
- **A death year supplied where the text gives none.** Of Mahmud Shah Lahori, whose entry breaks off
  before any date: "سید محمد لطیف نے آپ کا سال وصال ۱۸۶۶ء / ۱۲۸۳ھ لکھا ہے ص ۱۵۸" — Sayyid Muhammad Latif
  wrote 1866 CE / 1283 AH (p. 157, folio 145, حاشیہ 1), with Kanhiya Lal's *Tarikh-e Lahore* (1884) and
  Nur Ahmad Chishti's *Tahqiqat-e Chishti* also cited. Latif and Nur Ahmad Chishti are the same two
  nineteenth-century Lahore topographers the editor used against the text in chunks 010 and 011 — he
  treats them as the standing authority on Lahore graves.
- **A death year derived from a phrase, reported as the subject's own method.** Imam Ali Shah fixed his
  pir's death year (1224 AH) "مرشد محذو دمی [OCR?] سے … نظر کیا", from a chronogram phrase (p. 156,
  folio 144, حاشیہ 1) — the book recording not just a date but how a date was made.
- **The author speaks in his own voice, twice, about living men.** Of Abd al-Rasul of Kasur: "حضرت کی ذات
  بابرکات ایسے زمانہ میں کہ مردان خدا عنقا ہو گئے ہیں، مغتنمات وقت سے ہے۔ **مؤلف کتاب بھی ان کے دیدار
  پر انوار سے مشرف ہوا ہے** … **اللہ ان کو دیر تک سلامت رکھے**" — in such a time as this, when the men of
  God have become as rare as the phoenix, his person is one of the prizes of the age; **the author of the
  book too has been honoured with the sight of him**; may God keep him safe long (p. 154, folio 142). And
  of Sadiq Ali Shah: "**سید صادق علی شاہ کی زیارت سے مؤلف کتاب بھی مستفید ہوا ہے** … **اس سال میں** حضرت
  کا جوان لڑکا فوت ہو گیا" — the author has benefited from his sight too, and **"in this year"** the
  Hazrat's young son died (p. 157, folio 145). See Doubts for what this dates.

## Citable passages

- "یہ سند خود شاہ عبد العزیز کے دست مبارک کی لکھی ہوئی ہے جس پر شاہ صاحب کی مہر بھی ثبت ہے۔ جس میں ۱۱۸۹ھ
  تحریر ہے … یہ قیاس درست نہیں ہے، بلکہ اس سند پر شاہ صاحب نے پرانی مہر ثبت کر دی ہے۔" — "This sanad is
  written in Shah Abd al-Aziz's own blessed hand and bears the Shah's seal too, in which 1189 AH is

- "آپ نے اپنا رہائشی مکان فروخت کر کے اپنا مقبرہ بنوایا تھا۔" — of Mahmud Shah Lahori: "He sold his
  dwelling house and had his own tomb built." (p. 157, folio 145)
- "لنگر آپ کا اس قدر جاری ہوا کہ صدہا مسافر و غریب و محتاج دو وقتہ روٹی حضرت کے لنگر سے کھاتے تھے۔" —
  "His langar ran to such an extent that hundreds of travellers, poor and needy ate bread twice a day
  from the Hazrat's langar." (p. 156, folio 144)
- "۱۲۷۰ھ میں رٹڑ چھترہ (ضلع گورداسپور، مشرقی پنجاب) کا نام تبدیل کر کے مکان شریف رکھا گیا۔" — "In
  1270 AH the name of Ratar Chhatra (district Gurdaspur, East Punjab) was changed to Makan Sharif."
  (p. 156, folio 144)
- "ان کے مزار کا چبوترہ … چونہ کچ ہمیشہ سرد رہتا ہے۔ کبھی گرم نہیں ہوتا۔" — of the Kasur graves: "The

## Doubts

- **No archive row is the subject of, or takes material from, any entry in this chunk, and four
  near-misses must be resisted.**
  - **Kasur** is the location of **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** (Bulleh Shah, d. 1757, Qadiri). The Qasuri
    family graves at Kasur (pp. 151–152, folios 139–140) belong to a Naqshbandi-Mujaddidi ulama lineage
    from Sindh, dying 1202, 1270 and 1295 AH. **Different shrine; do not map.**
  - **Sharaqpur** appears only as the imprint of *Khazina-e Marifat* (p. 156, folio 144). The archive's
    **shrine-of-pir-sher-muhammad** row is at Sharaqpur, but the chunk does not connect the imprint to
    that shrine or its press. **Do not infer a shrine press from an imprint.**
  - **Sialkot** appears only in the nisba "حاجی فتح علی متقی سیالکوٹی" (p. 152, folio 140), with no
    grave, no date and no link to **shrine-of-imam-ali-ul-haq**.
  - **Qalat** appears only as the residence of "حاجی احمد ساکن قلات" (p. 156, folio 144); the archive's
    **kalat-kali-temple** is a Hindu temple there and wholly unrelated.
  - Imam Ali Shah's own shrine, **Makan Sharif (Ratar Chhatar), district Gurdaspur**, is in **East
    Punjab** by the editor's own note and so falls outside this archive's geography.
- **The author's own date of composition can be bracketed from this chunk, and should be checked against
  the manifest.** He writes of **Abd al-Rasul of Kasur** as living and prays for his long life (p. 154,
  folio 142), and the editor gives Abd al-Rasul's death as **1295 AH** (p. 152, folio 140); he writes of
  **Sadiq Ali Shah** as living and of a bereavement "اس سال میں", in this year (p. 157, folio 145), Sadiq
  Ali Shah having succeeded after **1282 AH** (p. 156, folio 144). Both statements together place the
  writing **between 1282 and 1295 AH (c. 1866–1878 CE)**. Also consistent: Mahmud Shah Lahori's entry
  (d. 1283 AH per Latif) is the last in the chunk. **This is an inference from three dates in one chunk,
  not a statement the book makes; a human should confirm it against the title page before it is used.**
- **The editor's own identity is strongly implied but never stated.** "محمد اقبال مجددی" appears as the
  owner of four of Qasuri's manuscripts, as the compiler of a fifth, as the owner of the *Majma
  al-Tawarikh* and of Qasuri's memorandum, and as the author of a 1973 life of Qasuri (pp. 150–154,
  folios 138–142) — and chunk 012 cites his *Ahwal wa Athar-e Abdullah Khweshgi Qasuri*, Lahore 1972,
  which chunk 008's notes treat as the editor's own book. **Treat the identification as unconfirmed
  within these chunks**; the manifest or title page must settle it.
- **The facsimile on p. 153 (folio 141) does not obviously match what the footnote says it is.** The
  footnote announces "حضرت قصوریؒ کی سند بخاری کا عکس", a facsimile of the **Bukhari sanad** (p. 151,
  folio 139), but the plate's Arabic text is a **fiqh passage on apostates and inheritance**, closing
  "هو المختصر وعليه المفتى", with a colophon naming Qasuri as the copyist and a marginal "۱۲۳۷ [OCR?]".
  That reads as a page of his *Risala Ilm-e Mirath* (listed as 1227 AH in his own hand), not as a hadith
  sanad. Either the edition carries two plates, or the caption and the plate are mismatched, or the
  transcription has merged them. **A human must re-read pp. 151–153 from the PDF before anything is said
  about either document.** Twenty-two lines of the plate's margins are marked "[illegible]".
- **The Delhi visit has two years, from the same piece of evidence.** Footnote 1 on p. 151 (folio 139)
  gives "۱۲۳۴ھ [OCR?]" and footnote 4 on the same page gives "۱۲۳۷ھ [OCR?]", both citing the memorandum
  at the end of *Tuhfa-e Ithna Ashariya*, ms Ganj Bakhsh library Rawalpindi. Both numerals are
  OCR-flagged. The facsimile's margin reads "۱۲۳۷ [OCR?]". The seal argument rests on this date being
  after 1202 AH, which all readings satisfy; the exact year does not survive this chunk.
- **The Risala Ilm-e Mirath is dated two ways**: 1227 AH in the work-list (p. 150, folio 138) against the
  facsimile margin's 1237[OCR?] (p. 153, folio 141). One digit, and both instances are flagged or
  inferred.
- **Imam Ali Shah's chain contradicts chunk 013 and is incomplete by the editor's own account.** The text
  makes **Abd al-Ahad the murid of Shaikh Muhammad Masum** (p. 156, folio 144); chunk 013's entry no. 91
  makes Abd al-Ahad the son, khalifa and janishin of **Shaikh Muhammad Saeed** (p. 141, folio 129), and
  chunk 012 lists him among Muhammad Saeed's eight sons (p. 134, folio 122). The editor's repaired chain
  here has "خواجہ محمد سعید" in the position the text gives to "خواجہ زکی". **Do not carry this chain
  without the editor's footnote and without the chunk 012–013 readings beside it.**
- **Ghulam Murtaza Qasuri's death year and his son's birth year are the same**: the text gives Ghulam
  Murtaza's death as "سن ایک ہزار دو سو دو" and Ghulam Muhi al-Din's birth as "سنہ ایک ہزار دو سو دو" —
  both 1202 AH, on facing pages (pp. 152, folio 140). Possible, but the text also makes Ghulam Murtaza
  the **grandfather** and Shaikh Mustafa/Murtaza the father, so the coincidence is between a grandfather's
  death and a grandson's birth. Worth verifying rather than quoting as a fact about either.
- **Qasuri's chronogram is the most elaborate in these chunks and should not be mined for dates
  carelessly.** It carries **three** phrases yielding ۱۲۰۲ھ ("بخشش", "مرشد حق متقی", "شیخ الکرام") and
  **six** yielding ۱۲۷۰ھ ("ہست خورشید معلّا", "ذات حقانی", "فیض فقر", "شیخ رفیع", "منظور رجال", "ہم سجان
  [OCR?] ابر سخاوت") (p. 154, folio 142). The prose gives the same two years, so the chronogram adds no
  independent evidence — and the last phrase is OCR-flagged.
- **Shah Abu Said's age is given without a birth year**: "پچون سال حضرت کی عمر تھی", fifty-five, against a
  death in 1250 AH, which implies c. 1195 AH — a year the chunk never states (p. 148, folio 136).
- **Shah Rauf Ahmad's interval does not quite close.** He is said to have outlived Shah Abu Said by "صرف
  اڑھائی برس", only two and a half years, with his own death in 1253 and Abu Said's on Eid 1250 (pp. 148,
  149; folios 136, 137). Workable only if the Eid is Eid al-Fitr and the death falls early in 1253; do
  not compute a month from these two statements.
- **A word is missing from the Bhopal posting**: "انھوں نے [illegible] جا کر قیام کیا" (p. 149,
  folio 137). The destination is given in the preceding clause, so the loss is probably harmless, but the
  sentence cannot be quoted whole.
- `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags carried over that touch facts above, by page: "شام کے لبعد [OCR?]" and
  "عبد المعادر [OCR?] رام پوری" (p. 148, folio 136); "[illegible]" in the Bhopal sentence (p. 149,
  folio 137); "جید [OCR?]", "تجویز اسمار [OCR?]", "قریشی احمد حسین [OCR?]" (p. 150, folio 138);
  "۱۲۳۴ھ [OCR?]", "رسے المحاج [OCR?]", "۱۲۳۷ھ [OCR?]" (p. 151, folio 139); "حجی [OCR?] معین الدین" and
  "غلام محی الدین کنجاہی [OCR?]" (p. 152, folio 140); twenty-two illegible margin lines plus nine flagged
  phrases in the facsimile's Arabic, including "المحقق [OCR?]", "صاحب السنته [OCR?]", "ابراهيم المصطفى
  [OCR?]", "بالرضى [OCR?]", "غوث الطالبين [OCR?]", "وارضاه [OCR?]", "۱۲۳۷ [OCR?]" (p. 153, folio 141);
  "پُر ملاق [OCR?]", "ہم سجان [OCR?]", "محمد صالح کنجاہی [OCR?]", "محمد حسن علمی [OCR?]", "ص ۴۸۱ [OCR?]",
  "ص ۴۹۲ [OCR?]" (p. 154, folio 142); "۱۲۸۲ھ [OCR?]" (p. 155, folio 143); "مرشد محذو دمی [OCR?]",
  "۴۵ [OCR?]", "ص ۷ [OCR?]" (p. 156, folio 144); "کسی چاہ [OCR?]" in the possession story and "ص ۲۶۳
  [OCR?]" (p. 157, folio 145).
- **Death years given in words only**: 1250 (Shah Abu Said), 1253 (Rauf Ahmad), 1202 and 1270 (the two
  Qasuris), 1282 (Imam Ali Shah), and the birth years 1202 and 1212 (pp. 148–156). Only Shah Ahmad
  Saeed's ۱۲۷۷ھ carries a numeral beside the words, and Mahmud Shah Lahori's entry gives no date at all
  in the text.
