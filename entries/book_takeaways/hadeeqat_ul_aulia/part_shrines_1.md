### shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya — Shrine of Bahauddin Zakariya

**What the row gains.** A full biographical entry (no. 101, folios 146–149) with family
migration, education, a formal state office, death and burial at Multan — and two date pairs the
book refuses to settle: birth 578 AH (author) against 566 AH (editor), death 666 AH (author, twice
by chronogram) against 661 AH (editor). The archive row's **1267 CE is exactly 666 AH, the reading
the editor rejects.** Plus eighteen citable sources, including a Samarqand manuscript of 854 AH.
Nothing anywhere in this group describes the present shrine, its urs or its custodianship.

**Saint**
- Opens the Suhrawardi chapter as entry no. 101, printed p. 146 (p. 17, folio 5); entry at
  pp. 158–161, folios 146–149.
- The book's names for him vary: "شیخ الشیوخ خواجہ بہار الدین زکریا" and "شیخ بہار الدین زکریا ملتانیؒ"
  — printed **"Bahar al-Din"** for Bahauddin (pp. 17, 24); "شیخ الشیوخ بہاء الدین زکریا قریشی ملتانی"
  (p. 158); "شیخ بہاؤالدین بن زکریا ملتانی" (p. 278); "بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی" in the index (p. 314).
  The editor records an interlinear gloss in his manuscript: "پیشِ نظر نسخہ میں 'بہار' کے نیچے 'تابندہ'
  لکھا ہوا ہے" (pp. 158–159, folios 146–147, حاشیہ 1).
- Family arrival: "پہلے ان کے جدّ بزرگوار کمال الدین علی شاہ قریشی مکہ معظمہ سے خوارزم میں آئے۔ وہاں سے
  ملتان میں آکر قیام کیا" — grandfather **Kamal al-Din Ali Shah Qureshi** came from Mecca to
  Khwarazm and thence to Multan. His father **Shaikh Wajih al-Din** was born in that house and
  married the daughter of **Maulana Husam al-Din Tirmizi, resident at mauza Kot Karor**
  (p. 160, folio 148).
- **Birth — two readings, side by side (chunk 015).** *Author:* born in Wajih al-Din's house in
  **578 AH** ("پانچ سو اٹھتر"), with ۵۷۸ھ given twice by chronogram (pp. 160–161, folios 148–149).
  *Editor:* "حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانیؒ ۵۶۶ھ / ۱۱۷۰ء میں کوٹ کہروڑ (ملتان) میں پیدا ہوئے" — born
  **566 AH / 1170 CE at Kot Kahror (Multan)**, on Nur Ahmad Faridi, *Tazkira-e Hazrat Baha al-Din
  Zakariya Multani* p. 6, on Sayyid Jalal Bukhari's authority (p. 158, folio 146, حاشیہ 1).
  Twelve years apart, **and a birthplace the author's text does not give at all.**
- Education and itinerary: Quran memorised at twelve; after his father's death **Bukhara** for
  sarf, nahw, hadith and tafsir; the **Haramain**, with **five years at Medina**; **Bait
  al-Maqdis**; then **Baghdad**, bai'at from Shaikh Shihab al-Din Umar Suhrawardi, and "اٹھارہ روز
  کے عرصہ میں خرقہ خلافت حاصل کیا اور ملتان کو مامور ہوئے" — the khirqa of khilafat **in eighteen
  days**, then posted to Multan (p. 160, folio 148). The eighteen days are given as a mark of
  distinction.
- Chain as the chapter opens: "آغاز اس سلسلہ عالیہ کا حضرت سید الطائفہ جنید بغدادی سے ہے" — the
  Suhrawardiya begins with **Junaid Baghdadi**, running through Mamshad Alu Dinawari,
  Rudaim[OCR?], Ali Rudayari[OCR?], Ahmad Aswad Dinawari, Abu Abdullah Khafif, Abu al-Qasim
  Gurgani, Abu Bakr Nassaj, **Shaikh Ahmad Ghazali, brother of Imam Muhammad Ghazali**, Ziya
  al-Din Abu al-Najib Suhrawardi and Wajih al-Din Suhrawardi to Shihab al-Din Umar Suhrawardi,
  and then to him (p. 158, folio 146).
- **Office under the state, and a side taken in a civil war:** "حضرت کے سلطان شمس الدین التمش کے
  ساتھ اچھے تعلقات تھے۔ سلطان قباچہ کے مقابلہ میں آپ نے التمش کی حمایت کی تھی اور التمش کی درخواست پر
  شیخ الاسلام کا عہدہ بھی قبول کر لیا تھا" — good relations with **Sultan Shams al-Din Iltutmish**,
  support for him **against Sultan Qabacha**, and at Iltutmish's request acceptance of the office
  of **Shaikh al-Islam** (Khaliq Ahmad Nizami, *Salatin-e Dehli ke Mazhabi Rujhanat* 113, 116,
  205[OCR?]) (p. 159, folio 147). Chunk 015 calls this the archive's clearest case of a shrine
  figure holding formal state office.
- **Death — two readings, side by side (chunk 015).** *Author:* text and chronogram both give
  **666 AH** ("سال چھ سو چھیاسٹھ"; ۶۶۶ھ twice), and "اور ملتان میں مدفون ہوئے", buried at Multan
  (pp. 160–161, folios 148–149). *Editor:* "حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانیؒ نے ۶۶۱ھ میں وصال
  فرمایا۔ سال وفات میں اختلاف ہے۔ لطائف اشرفی ۶۶۶ھ، اخبار الاخیار ۶۶۱ھ، گلزار ابرار ۶۶۶ھ [OCR?]، لیکن
  ان میں ہمارے نزدیک صحیح ۶۶۱ھ ہے" — died **661 AH**; the sources disagree (*Lataif-e Ashrafi* 666,
  *Akhbar al-Akhyar* 661, *Gulzar-e Abrar* 666[OCR?]) "but in our view 661 AH is correct"
  (p. 159, folio 147, حاشیہ 1). **The archive row's 1267 CE = 666 AH, i.e. the author's figure,
  which this edition's editor rejects.**
- A third witness to the same pair, **not the book's text**: the publisher's catalogue prints
  "الاوراد | مُصنف: بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی (۵۶۶۔ ۶۶۱)" — **"۵۶۶۔ ۶۶۱" as printed, with no era
  marker** (p. 353), i.e. the editor's dates, not the author's (chunk 032).
- Writings: one book, ***Kitab al-Awrad***, survives, commented on by **Maulana Ali bin Ahmad
  Ghori, a murid of Shah Rukn-e Alam**, as *Kanz al-Ibad fi Sharh al-Awrad*; a copy **written at
  Samarqand in 854 AH** in the hand of Shams al-Din Ahmad bin Maulana Sadr al-Din is in the
  annotator's own library. From it, "ڈاکٹر مولوی محمد شفیع کے اس شبہہ کی تردید ہوتی ہے کہ یہ اوراد شیخ
  شہاب الدین سہروردی کے ہیں" — Dr Maulvi Muhammad Shafi's doubt, that these *awrad* are Shihab
  al-Din Suhrawardi's, is refuted, the commentator having "صاف طور پر بہاء الحق والشرع والدین لکھا
  ہے" (pp. 158–159, folios 146–147, حاشیہ 1).
- Malfuzat: ***Khulasat al-Arifin*** (Urdu translation printed **Lahore 1909**) and an unknown,
  unpublished *Malfuzat* once in the library of the late Maulvi Muhammad Shams al-Din; "ملک لطیف
  مرحوم نے رسالہ صحیفہ لاہور ۱۹۷۰ء [OCR?] میں شائع کر دیا تھا" — the late Malik Latif published it in
  *Sahifa*, Lahore, **1970[OCR?]**, the copy now in Malik Sahib's library (p. 159, folio 147,
  حاشیہ 1). **The bibliography dates the same article differently:** item ۲۵۰ "لطیف ملک : ملفوظات
  حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی صحیفہ لاہور جنوری ۱۹۷۱ء" — **January 1971** (p. 305, folio 293).
  Both as printed; the 1970 is flagged `[OCR?]`, the 1971 is not.
- Son and daughter-in-law: **شیخ صدر الدین عارف** named as his son, and Bibi [Rasti] as "زوجہ شیخ
  صدر الدین عارف بن شیخ بہاؤ الدین زکریا ملتانی" who "نسبت ارادت اپنے خسر بہاؤ الدین زکریا کے ساتھ
  رکھتی تھی" — held her bond of discipleship to her father-in-law personally (p. 252, folio 240).
  Her death: "سنہ چھ سو پچانوے ہجری" — **695 AH** (p. 252). Editor's sources for that entry:
  "(۱) جمالی : سیر العارفین (۲) نور احمد فریدی : تذکرہ حضرت صدر الدین عارف ص ۲۶ - ۳۴" (p. 252,
  folio 240, حاشیہ 3). His own dates, shrine and Multan are **(not stated)** in that entry.
- The chunk records that he had **seven sons and that his estate was divided**, calling it the
  fullest financial passage in these chunks and evidence about the shrine's wealth at the
  founder's death; the passage itself sits under that chunk's Practices heading and is **not
  reproduced in these findings** (chunk 015, entry no. 101, pp. 158–161).

**Practices & Events**
- Scale and standing at Multan: "ملتان میں آکر **شاہان وقت** اور خاص و عام حضرت کی بیعت میں داخل ہوئے
  اور دولت ظاہری و باطنی ان کے نصیب ہوئی۔ **ہزاروں خادم حضرت کی بارگاہ فلک پایگاہ پر حاضر رہتے**" — the
  kings of the time and high and low alike entered his bai'at, outward and inward wealth was his
  portion, and **thousands of attendants** were present at his sky-high court; and "لاکھوں آدمی
  حضرت سے مستفید طریقہ سہروردیہ ہوئے", **lakhs** benefited from the Suhrawardi way through him
  (p. 160, folio 148).
- **A women's line inside the Multan household:** through his daughter-in-law, "بہت سی عورات کو
  فیض نسبت سہروردیہ اس کی ذات با برکات سے پہنچا" — the Suhrawardi nisbat reached many women through
  her (p. 252, folio 240).
- **The lineage's dispersal and economy.** Entry ۲۲۹: "یہ بزرگ **حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی کی
  اولاد سے ملتان سے لاہور میں آیا**" — Shah Rahmatullah Qureshi came from Multan to Lahore from among
  his descendants (p. 271, folio 259); the family was living in the author's present in Lahore and
  mauza Dholanwal and subsisting on murids' *nazr*, one of them, **بہادر شاہ**, personally known to
  the author (p. 271, folio 259).

**Significance**
- **Terminus of five spiritual chains in this book.** Head of both the **Shah Jamal** chain
  (p. 185) and the **Shah Daula** chain (p. 184), at eight and thirteen steps respectively
  (chunk 017); terminus of **Mian Wada's** thirteen-step silsila (p. 188) and of Muhammad Aqil
  Lahori's *isnad* (chunk 018 — the note breaks off mid-sentence); and terminus of a nine-step
  Suhrawardi chain from the Lahore scholar Shaikh Hamid Qari Suhrawardi: "مولوی (۱) تیمور … شیخ
  عبدالکریم … مخدوم طیب … مخدوم برہان … **مخدوم چنن [OCR?]** … **شیخ میلو [OCR?]** … حسام الدین متقی …
  شیخ صدرالدین … **شیخ بہاؤالدین بن زکریا ملتانی**" (p. 278, folio 266).

  *Chunk 002, p. 24, folio 12, concluded p. 25, folio 13, "sixteen generations as printed":*

  Muhammad Taqi b. Mufti Muhammad Taqi** b. **Maulana Kamal al-Din Khurd** b. Mufti Abdul Sami

### tahir-bandagi-qadri — Darbar Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri

**What the row gains.** The archive's death date (8 Muharram 1040 AH) confirmed to the day and
to the hour, and independently repeated by the editor; the Miani Sahib location attested four
separate times, including by the book's own appendix; **four named khalifas**; a walled enclosure
(چار دیواری) used for burying other saints, with a dated occupant; **two named sajjada nashins a
century apart**, the second inside a footnote that rewrites the custodial genealogy; and a
rebuilding of the tomb. Against that: the book names his order **four different ways**.

**Saint**
- Entry no. 20, printed p. 47 (p. 15, folio 3). The contents call him "شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری قادری";
  the entry heading itself gives **both orders**, "شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری **قادری و نقشبندی**"
  (pp. 59–60, folios 47–48).
- Murid and khalifa of **Shah Sikandar b. Pir Kamal Kaithali**; he first completed his training
  under Shah Sikandar Kaithali, then attended "امام ربانی مجدد الف ثانی پیر احمد فاروقی سرہندی",
  gained great benefits, and came to Lahore **at the Mujaddid's direction** to guide the people,
  where thousands reached high stations through him (p. 59, folio 47). Separately named as a
  khalifa of the Mujaddid, "extremely strict in shariah", **died 1040 AH** (p. 56, folio 44,
  حاشیہ 1).
- "سلسلہ قادریہ میں … اپنے عہد کے قطب وقت" — Qutb of his age in the Qadiri silsila; and "کوئی سائل
  دین و دنیا کا جو ان کے دروازے پر آیا خالی نہ گیا" (p. 59, folio 47).
- **Death, and the one date this book does not dispute:** "بروز پنجشنبہ وقت چاشت آٹھویں محرم سال ایک
  ہزار چالیس ہجری" — **Thursday, at the forenoon hour, 8 Muharram 1040 AH** — "اور عمر چھپن برس کی
  پائی", aged **fifty-six** (p. 59, folio 47). **The archive row's "8 Muharram 1040 AH" agrees to
  the day.** The editor repeats 1040 AH independently, at p. 56 (folio 44, حاشیہ 1) and as
  "حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہوری **(ف ۱۰۴۰ھ)**" (p. 129, folio 117, حاشیہ 1).
- Livelihood: "کتب احادیث و تفاسیر کی کتابت حضرت کرتے اور ہدیہ کر کے گزارہ اوقات کرتے" — he copied
  out books of hadith and tafsir and subsisted by presenting them, his nights passing in
  instructing his attendants and in worship (p. 59, folio 47).
- A refusal of patronage: "یہ حضرت تمام عمر کسی دولت مند کے پاس نہ گئے اور نہ ان کو اپنے دربار میں بار
  دیا" — all his life he went to no rich man, nor admitted them to his own darbar (p. 59,
  folio 47).
- **Adam Banuri came to him on foot.** "پیادہ بنور سے لاہور میں آیا" (p. 59, folio 47); the editor
  quotes Adam Banuri's own Persian account of receiving the *nisbat-e jazba* from Shaikh Tahir
  Lahori, "کہ خلفائی کبار حضرت پیر است" — who is among the great khalifas of Hazrat Pir — and of
  the nisbat having reached Tahir through Shah Iskandar b. Pira Shah Kamal Kaithali (*Khulasat
  al-Maarif* vol. 2, ms folio 259b) (p. 59, folio 47, حاشیہ 1).
- **Four khalifas named**, in entry no. 246: "**شیخ طاہر کے چار خلیفہ تھے**۔ ایک **ابو محمد قادری**،
  دوسرے **سید صوفی**، تیسرے **شیخ آدم بنوری**، چوتھے **شاہ گھلن سرمست**۔ جن کا مزار لاہور میں ہے" — Abu
  Muhammad Qadiri, Sayyid Sufi, Shaikh Adam Banuri and Shah Ghulan Sarmast, the last's own mazar
  "**موری دروازے کے باہر سرکاری باغ کے اندر**", outside Mori Gate inside the government garden
  (p. 279, folio 267).
- A documented three-generation chain out of his khanqah: Shaikh Muhammad Fazil Qadri Batalvi
  (entry no. 35) was murid and khalifa of Shaikh Muhammad Afzal Kalanauri, who was murid of
  Shaikh Abu Muhammad Lahori, who was murid of Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori (p. 76, folio 74).
- **A disputed authorship, reported without endorsement.** "تذکرہ مجددیہ کے مصنف کا نام معلوم نہیں
  ہے۔ محمد دین فوق نے اسے **حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہوری (ف ۱۰۴۰ھ)** کی تصنیف بتایا ہے" — the author
  of *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya* is not known; **Muhammad Din Fauq** stated it to be his composition
  (Fauq, *Tazkira-e Ulama wa Mashaikh-e Lahore* p. 11[OCR?]) (p. 129, folio 117, حاشیہ 1). The
  editor opens the footnote by saying the author is *not known*, then reports Fauq's attribution
  — so the archive should carry it as **Fauq's claim, disputed**. That *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya*,
  whatever its author, is the source for the Mujaddid's Shia-convert karamat told at pp. 129–130
  (folios 117–118) — so the Lahore shrine is linked, **if Fauq is right**, to an early
  Naqshbandi-Mujaddidi tazkira.
- **The order is given four ways.** "قادری" in the contents (p. 15) and in entry no. 246 (p. 279,
  folio 267); "**قادری و نقشبندی**" in the entry heading (p. 59, folio 47); "**شیخ محمد طاہر قادری
  نقشبندی**" in appendix row 8 (p. 288, folio 276); "**حضرت طاہر بندگی قادری**" in appendix row 61
  (p. 290, folio 278), with "شیخ طاہر بندگی" unqualified in row 27 (p. 288). The archive row
  already notes a discrepancy in how his order is named; this adds a **Naqshbandi** element.
- **The name, and the "Bandagi" element.** The contents omit it (p. 15, folio 3); the persons
  index treats the full form as canonical — "محمد طاہر بندگی لاہوری | ۴۴ ، ۴۷ ، ۵۲ ، ۶۴ ، ۱۱۳ ، ۱۱۷"
  (p. 328, folio 316), i.e. PDF pp. 56, 59, 64, 76, 125, 129 on the observed offset, confirming
  that the shortened "شیخ طاہر لاہوری" of the later entries and the fully named saint are **one
  indexed person** (chunk 031). But chunk 030 reports, from the ط section of the same index,
  **four Tahirs, none of them "بندگی"** (p. 321). Both readings stand. Note too the separate
  entry "شیخ عبدالجلیل چوٹھر **بندگی** لاہوری", no. 110, printed p. 160 (p. 18, folio 6), which does
  carry "Bandagi" (chunk 001).

**Shrine / Darbar**
- **Location, attested four times.** Entry no. 23: Shah Muhammad Muqim, travelling to Lahore,
  "جب گورستان میانی میں بجوار مزار شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری پہنچے" — when he reached the **Miani
  graveyard, in the vicinity of the mazar of Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori** (p. 64, folio 52),
  the editor calling the place "میانی شریف" (p. 64, حاشیہ 1). Editor's footnote at p. 125,
  folio 113: "حاجی شیخ رمضان لاہوری کا مزار **دربار حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہور میانی صاحب** کے قریب
  ہے" (citing *Khazinat al-Asfiya* 1/515 and Imam Bakhsh, *Hadiqat al-Asrar* p. 13). And appendix
  row 8: "**شیخ محمد طاہر قادری نقشبندیؒ** | | **قبرستان میانی**" — no address "according to Hadiqat
  al-Awliya" is given, and the present location is the Miani graveyard (p. 288, folio 276).
  **This matches the archive row's Miani Sahib graveyard**, and the footnote is an independent
  1974 confirmation that the darbar stood there and was well enough known to fix another grave by.
- **The tomb was rebuilt, and the old one copied the Mujaddid's.** "موجودہ مزار جناب پیر
  [illegible] گیلانی مدظلہ کا تعمیر کردہ ہے" — the present mazar was built by Pir [illegible]
  Gilani, who says that when the mazar was excavated for the new construction, "قدیم مزار بالکل
  مزار حضرت مجدد الف ثانی رح کی طرز پر بنا ہوا تھا" — the old mazar had been built exactly in the
  style of the Mujaddid Alf-e Sani's mazar (pp. 59–60, folios 47–48, حاشیہ 2).
- **A walled enclosure used for others' burials, with a dated occupant.** Of Sayyid Munawwar Ali
  Shah Naqshbandi Suhrawardi Lahori: "وفات حضرت کی **ایک ہزار دو سو چونسٹھ** ہجری میں واقع ہوئی اور
  **مرقد چار دیواری شیخ طاہر لاہوری کے اندر ہے**" — died **1264 AH**, his tomb inside the
  *chahar-diwari* of Shaikh Tahir Lahori (pp. 230–231, folios 218–219). Chunk 022 left that
  enclosure unlocated; **appendix row 61 resolves it** — "سید منور علی شاہ نقشبندی سہروردی لاہوری
  قدس سرہ | لاہور | **قبرستان میانی نزد مقبرہ حضرت طاہر بندگی قادری**" (p. 290, folio 278). The
  persons index points to that entry's opening, "منور علی شاہ نقشبندی | ۲۱۸" (p. 330, folio 318)
  — consistent with chunk 022's burial sentence one folio later.
- A second neighbour fixed by his tomb: row 27, "شیخ حاجی رمضان لاہوری چشتی قدس سرہ | | **نزد مقبرہ
  شیخ طاہر بندگی میانی**" (p. 288, folio 276). **A Chishti and a Naqshbandi-Suhrawardi buried beside
  him fixes the precinct as a multi-order burial ground.**
- The book's word for the site differs by voice: "**مزار**" in the author's text and "**درگاہ**" in
  the editor's footnote (p. 274, folio 262).

**Practices & Events**
- **Two named custodians, and a genealogy the editor rewrites.** *Author,* in the Gilani genealogy
  of entry ۲۳۶: "اس کا بیٹا سید شاہ حسین اس کا بیٹا سید زندہ علی اس کا بیٹا **سید شہسوار جو اب [OCR?]
  سجادہ نشین مزار شیخ طاہر بندگی کا ہے**" — **Sayyid Shahsawar** is now[OCR?] sajjada nashin (p. 274,
  folio 262). *Editor:* "**یہاں شجرہ نسب درست نہیں ہے۔ بلکہ یوں ہونا چاہیے**۔ شیخ عبدالقادر ثانی بن سید
  حاجی ابوبکر بن سید شیخ محمد بن سید سعید بن سید حمید بن سید عبدالقادر بن شاہ حسین بن سید زندہ علی بن سید
  شاہ سوار بن سید مبارک علی شاہ مرحوم بن **پیر شیر حسین درسی [OCR?] طاہری مد ظلہ اس وقت سجادہ نشین
  درگاہ حضرت شیخ طاہر بندگی ہیں**" — the genealogy here is not correct, it should run thus, and
  **Pir Sher Husain Darsi[OCR?] Tahiri**, may his shadow endure, is at this time sajjada nashin
  (p. 274, folio 262, حاشیہ ۲). **Keep both lines.** The nisba **طاہری** is taken from the shrine
  itself, and "مد ظلہ" means the second custodian was living when the footnote was written.
- The author's own uncorrected line runs back to **سید عبدالقادر لاہوری**, son of Sayyid Jalal
  al-Din Baghdadi, who settled at Lahore in Akbar's reign and married a daughter of **نواب میر
  کفایت خاں**; and an occupation in the family is recorded — "**بہادر بن چراغ شاہ جو مہر کنی کا کام
  کرتا ہے**", a seal-engraver (p. 274, folio 262).
- **The promise that explains the burials around him:** "حضرت فرماتے تھے کہ میری وفات کے بعد جو شخص
  میرے احاطہ مزار میں مدفون ہو گا۔ میں نے خدا سے مانگا ہے کہ وہ جنتی ہو گا" — whoever is buried within
  the enclosure of his mazar, he has asked God that they shall be of paradise (p. 59, folio 47).
- **Miani Sahib context, deliberately not attached to this row:** the graveyard was in use for a
  venerated majzub in 1269, and a majzub had built a house inside it (p. 245). Chunks 023 and 024
  both insist the house is tied to none of the rows sharing the graveyard and that **the graveyard
  is not itself an archive row** — record it against the graveyard, not against this shrine.

**Legends & Miracles**
- His one recorded act in the p. 56 footnote is a **wish to visit Shah Husain's mazar for
  *istimdad*** (p. 56, folio 44, حاشیہ 1).

**Significance**
- Nine further sources are listed, including *Zubdat al-Maqamat* 340/346, *Hazarat al-Quds*
  daftar 2 288/296, *Rauzat al-Qayyumiya* rukn 1 326/328 and *Tahqiqat-e Chishti* 163/171
  (p. 60, folio 48). A chronogram quatrain closes the entry: "شیخ طاہر پاک باز و پاک دین / اوج
  عرفان پر تھے مثل آفتاب…" (p. 60, folio 48).
- A page-map for the saint across the whole book, from the index: four separate stretches, folios
  44–52, 64, 113–117 (p. 328, folio 316) — targetable directly by a later editor.

### darbar-abul-muali-qadri — Darbar Abul Muali Qadri

**What the row gains.** A full entry (no. 19) with a two-step patrilineal pedigree, the Kirmani
nisba, the epithet "عاشق لاابالی", birth 960 AH and **death 1024 AH agreeing with the archive**, six
works and a takhallus, a dated act of patronage in 1023 AH, a street in Gowalmandi named for him,
and infrastructure he built on the Kasur–Lahore road. Against that: **the name خیر الدین appears
three ways** — as part of his own name, as his father's, and (glossed identically) as the name of a
different Abul Muali's father — and the index keeps **three Abul Mualis** apart.

**Saint**
- Entry no. 19 of the Qadiri chapter, printed p. 45 (p. 15, folio 3). **A second, separate entry
  "شاہ ابوالمعالی چشتی" stands at no. 62, printed p. 97, in the Chishti chapter** (p. 16, folio 4).
  The archive row is Qadri and in Lahore, which is why no. 19 is mapped, **but a reader must check
  both entries before citing** (chunk 001).
- Full name as the entry and the appendix head it: "**عاشق لا ابالی شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی لاہوری
  بن سید رحمۃ اللہ بن سید فتح اللہ کرمانی قادری**" (p. 57, folio 45; appendix row 7, p. 287,
  folio 275) — a two-step patrilineal pedigree (son of Sayyid Rahmat Allah, son of Sayyid Fath
  Allah Kirmani), the **Kirmani** nisba and **Qadiri** order that the archive's survey-derived
  row gives only as descent and affiliation, and the epithet "عاشق لاابالی", the heedless lover.
- Kinship and discipleship: "یہ بزرگ برادر زادہ حقیقی شیخ داؤد کرمانی شیر گڈھی کے ہیں اور انہیں کے
  مرید تھے" — the true brother's son (nephew) of **Shaikh Daud Kirmani Shergarhi** and his murid
  (p. 57, folio 45); his own line makes Daud's father his grandfather, consistent with the
  relationship as stated. The editor adds that he was Daud's **nephew, son-in-law (داماد) and
  khalifa**, and made continuous and tireless efforts for the spread of the Qadiri silsila
  (p. 58, folio 46, حاشیہ 1).
- He attended on his pir for **thirty-two years** before reaching completion, and after the grant
  of the khirqa of khilafat "لاہور کو مامور ہوئے", was deputed to Lahore (p. 57, folio 45).
- **Birth and death:** "سال نوسو ساٹھ" with **۹۶۰** in the margin; "سال ایک ہزار چوبیس" with **۱۰۲۴**
  in the margin (p. 58, folio 46). **The archive row's 1024 AH agrees.** No editorial rejection of
  either year appears anywhere in this group's findings.
- **The father problem, three readings kept side by side.**
  1. *Author's entry heading and the appendix* make **خیر الدین part of his own name** — "شاہ **خیر
     الدین** ابوالمعالی لاہوری بن سید رحمۃ اللہ" (pp. 57, 58, 287; chunks 004, 005, 027).
  2. *The index* makes **Khair al-Din the father**: "**خیر الدین (والد شاہ ابوالمعالی) | ۴۵**"
     (p. 317, folio 305; chunk 029) — folio 45 being the Lahori's own folio.
  3. *The index again*, with the identical unqualified gloss: "**محمد اشرف امبیٹھوی (والد شاہ ابو
     المعالی) | ۹۷**" (p. 327, folio 315; chunk 030) — but folio 97 belongs to the **third** Abul
     Muali, "ابوالمعالی امبیٹھوی | ۹۶، ۹۷، ۱۰۰، ۱۰۱، ۱۰۶", not to "ابوالمعالی شاہ، لاہوری | ۳۳، ۳۷،
     ۴۵، ۴۶", and the father's nisba there is likewise Ambethwi. **So the index uses the same gloss
     "(والد شاہ ابوالمعالی)" for the fathers of two different men**, and the gloss cannot be read
     without its folio.
  The archive row names the saint himself "Hazrat Syed Muhammad Khair ul Deen, known as Shah Abul
  Muali Qadri". **This does not resolve whether the row conflates father and son; record all three
  readings.**
- **The index keeps three Abul Mualis apart:** "ابوالمعالی سید | ۳۶، ۵۱", "ابوالمعالی شاہ، لاہوری |
  ۳۳، ۳۷، ۴۵، ۴۶", "ابوالمعالی امبیٹھوی | ۹۶، ۹۷، ۱۰۰، ۱۰۱، ۱۰۶" (all p. 312, folio 300). **Only the
  second is "Lahori"**; its four locators are est. pp. 45, 49, 57, 58.
- Works, per the author: *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya*, on Ghaus al-Azam's khawariq and karamat, is famous,
  "اور دیوان اشعار ان کی اولاد کے پاس موجود ہے" — a diwan of his verses is in the possession of his
  descendants (p. 58, folio 46). Per the editor: *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya*; *Naghmat-e Daudi*, on the
  conditions of his own murshid, mentioned by Badayuni (*Muntakhab al-Tawarikh* 288); *Munis-e
  Jan*; *Zafran Zar*; *Guldasta-e Bagh-e Iram* (ms owned by Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi); and a
  *Sharh-e Yazdah Bai'at* of Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani (same owner); **he wrote verse under the
  takhallus "غریبی"** (p. 58, folio 46, حاشیہ 1). *Naghmat-e Daudi* is also the first-listed source
  on Shaikh Daud Kirmani (p. 49, folio 37, حاشیہ).
- Cited as an authority in his own right: in *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya* he wrote that Shaikh Jamal
  Allah, of the progeny of Shaikh Abdul Razzaq b. Abdul Qadir Jilani, was in that era living in
  the jungles of Bastam (p. 45, folio 33, حاشیہ 2) — the passage the editor uses against the
  sobriquet "Hayat al-Mir" (see bari-imam below).

**Shrine / Darbar**
- Appendix row 7: "**عاشق لاابالی شاہ خیر الدین ابوالمعالی لاہوری بن سید رحمت اللہ بن سید فتح اللہ کرمانی
  قادری** | | **شاہ ابوالمعالی سٹریٹ گوالمنڈی**" — **no address given "according to Hadiqat al-Awliya"**;
  present location **Shah Abul Muali Street, Gowalmandi** (p. 287, folio 275).

**Practices & Events**
- **Infrastructure on the road to Lahore:** "راستے میں جس جس مقام پر یہ منزل گزیں ہوئے۔ چاہ و باغیچہ و
  تالاب بجنہ [OCR?] بنوائے" — at every place where he halted he had a **well, a small garden and a
  tank** built (p. 57, folio 45), "جو اب تک جو کھائی [OCR?] **عبد المعالی** مشہور ہیں" — which to this
  day are famous by the name of Abdul Muali (p. 58, folio 46). **Note the book prints "عبد المعالی"
  there, not "ابوالمعالی".**
- At Lahore, "انہوں نے قبول عظیم حاصل کیا اور ہزاروں مرید و خادم ہوئے" — he found great acceptance and
  had thousands of murids and attendants (p. 58, folio 46).
- **The state of the family in the author's own day:** "اولاد ان کی لاہور میں سکونت پذیر ہے مگر افسوس
  کہ ایسے عالم و فاضل و ولی کی اولاد علم سے بے بہرہ ہے" — his descendants reside in Lahore, but alas
  the offspring of such a learned and saintly man are destitute of learning (p. 58, folio 46).

**Legends & Miracles**
- "ادنیٰ کرامت ان کی یہ تھی کہ مریدان کا بیعت کے روز ہی حضرت غوث الاعظم کی زیارت سے مستفید ہو جاتا تھا"
  — his **least** karamat was that a murid would be granted a vision of Ghaus al-Azam on the very
  day of his bai'at; he had a special *nisbat* with Ghaus al-Azam and a devotion to him beyond
  bounds (p. 58, folio 46).

**Significance**
- **A dated act of patronage.** Of Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi's *Sharh Futuh al-Ghaib*: "**فتوح
  الغیب** حضرت عبد القادر جیلانیؒ کی تصنیف ہے۔ جس کی شرح **حضرت شاہ ابو المعالیؒ کے حکم سے ۱۰۲۳ھ میں مکمل
  کی**۔ **مفتاح فتوح** تاریخی نام ہے۔ **لاہور ۱۲۸۳ھ** اور **لکھنؤ ۱۲۹۰ھ** میں شائع ہو چکی ہے" — completed
  **in 1023 AH by his order**; "Miftah-e Futuh" is its chronogrammatic name; published at Lahore
  1283 AH and Lucknow 1290 AH (p. 207, folio 195, حاشیہ 6). The editor elsewhere credits him with

### shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri — Shrine of Shah Inayat Qadiri

**What the row gains.** The archive's "Qadri Shattari" confirmed in the book's own wording, his
full name and five teachers from his own works, his caste, a flight from Kasur to Lahore after a
quarrel with its ruler, sixteen traced works, and an appendix address. And **the archive's death
year is destroyed**: the editor says no contemporary tazkira gives one at all, that the Mufti
invented 1141 AH, and that a sealed letter proves the saint alive in 1148 AH.

**Saint**
- Entry no. 34, printed p. 63 (p. 16, folio 4), as "شاہ عنایت قادری شطاری" — **the book's silsila
  wording matches the archive's "Qadri Shattari" exactly**. Entry at pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74,
  as "شاہ عنایت قادری شطاری لاہوری".
- One of **Shah Raza Qadri Lahori's** khalifas, of great rank, comprehensive in the exoteric and
  esoteric sciences (p. 75, folio 63).
- **Full name and teachers**, from the editor: "**حافظ ابوالمعارف محمد عنایت اللہ حنفی قادری شطاری**",
  taken from his own works *Zail al-Aghlat* and *Ghayat al-Hawashi*; father's name **Pir
  Muhammad**; exoteric sciences with **Maulana Sayyid Abu al-Nasr alias Sayyid Ilyas** and
  **Maulvi Abdul Hadi Lahori**; inner benefit from **Shah Ali Raza Faruqi, great-grandson of the
  Mujaddid Alf-e Sani (d. 1141 AH / 1728 CE)**, and from **Shaikh Muhammad Sultan Bukhari**; and
  after 1111 AH he attached himself to **Shah Raza Qadri Shattari Lahori (d. 1118 AH / 1707 CE)**
  (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74, حاشیہ 1).
- **Death — the sharpest contradiction in this group.** *Author:* ۱۱۴۱ھ **[OCR?]**, with his radiant
  mazar in Lahore (p. 76, folio 74). *Editor:* "شاہ عنایت کا سال وفات کسی معاصر تذکرہ نویس نے نہیں

  contemporary tazkira-writer recorded Shah Inayat's death year; the Mufti was the first to write
  1141 AH, without any authority, and later historians went on copying it.** It is wrong because
  a contemporary scholar with close relations to him, **Haji Muhammad Sharif Qasuri (d. 1153 AH)**,
  in a letter addressed to Shah Inayat quoting from one of his works, wrote "سلمہ اللہ تعالیٰ"
  after his name — the formula used of a living man — and impressed his seal "**العبد الفقیر محمد
  شریف ۱۱۴۸ ہجری**". From this it is clear that Shah Inayat was **alive as late as 1148 AH and died
  approximately after ۱۱۵۰ھ / ۱۷۳۷ء [OCR?]** (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74, حاشیہ 1).
  **The archive row's "c. 1728" is the very figure the editor shows to be an unsourced
  invention.** The editor's own book title repeats his dating: "حیات شاہ عنایت قادری قصوری ؒ **(ف
  بعد ۱۷۳۷ء)**" (p. 307, folio 295).
- Worth a human's eye, as printed: the same year, **1141 AH / 1728 CE**, is given in that footnote
  as the death year of **Shah Ali Raza Faruqi**, his own pir in the inner sciences (pp. 75–76,
  حاشیہ 1).
- **Caste and family:** "حضرت قوم کے باغبان تھے۔ اولاد اب بھی ان کی لاہور میں ہے" — he was of the
  **Baghban (gardener)** caste, and his descendants are in Lahore still (p. 76, folio 74).
- **Sixteen works of his have been traced**, of which *Ghayat al-Hawashi* is the bulkiest, and
  chunk 006 refers a *dar al-harb* claim to its Arguments section (pp. 75–76, folios 63 and 74,
  حاشیہ 1). Further reading: Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi, *Hayat-e Shah Inayat Qadri*, described as
  "**زیر طبع**", in press (p. 82, folio 70 [marker: 80]); and book-list item ۶۱ "**ایضاً : حیات شاہ
  عنایت قادری قصوری ثم لاہوری زیر تکمیل ۱۹۷۴ء**" — **"under completion" in 1974** (p. 295, folio 283),
  where the saint is described as "**of Kasur, then of Lahore**". Item ۲۰۸ "محمد شفیع مولوی ڈاکٹر :
  اولیائے قصور۔ لاہور ۱۹۶۲ء" (p. 303, folio 291) may bear on the Kasur phase but names no one.

**Shrine / Darbar**

### mazar-of-bulleh-shah — Mazar of Bulleh Shah

**What the row gains.** His pir, his residence at Kasur, his *sama* practice, **his Punjabi
poetry still being sung by qawwals when the book was written in 1875**, the mazar at Kasur as a
public pilgrimage place — and a death year the editor overturns from a dated seal, the same seal
that settles the spelling of his name.

**Saint**
- Entry no. 39, printed p. 69 (p. 16, folio 4), "سید بلھے شاہ قادری شطاری"; entry at pp. 81–82,
  folios 69–70 [markers: 79–80], as "سید بلہی **[OCR?]** شاہ قادری شطاری قصوری".

  Inayat Qadri Lahori, resident in qasba Kasur (p. 81, folio 69 [marker: 79]).
- **Death — two readings, side by side.** *Author:* "وفات ان کی **۱۱۸۰ھ** میں ہوئی۔ اور مزار گوہر بار
  قصور میں زیارت گاہ خلق ہے" (p. 82, folio 70 [marker: 80]). *Editor, rejecting it on documentary
  evidence of a seal:* it is wrong according to Dr Maulvi Muhammad Shafi's research, because Shafi
  happened to see a manuscript of *awrad* in the possession of Abdul Aziz Khan at Ferozepur

  تک بقید حیات تھے" — from which it appears that Baba Sahib was **alive up to 1181 AH** (p. 82,

  ہے۔ آپ کی مہر 'بلھا شاہ قادری ۱۱۸۱ھ' سے بھی موجودہ تلفظ 'بلھا' کی تائید ہوتی ہے" — the present
  pronunciation is Bulhe or Bulha, and the seal supports **Bulha** (p. 81, folio 69 [marker: 79],

- **The book calls him a sayyid** — "**سید** بلھے شاہ" (p. 219, folio 207).
- **He is refused by one shaikh and referred to another.** In entry no. 150, Khwaja Abd al-Khaliq
  went "**باتفاق سید بلھے شاہ اور گلشیر محمد اپنے بھائی کے** بارادہ بیعت بخدمت **شیخ عبد الحکیم قادری** کے
  بمقام **تلمنہ [OCR?]**" — with **Sayyid Bulleh Shah** and his own brother **Gul Sher Muhammad**,
  intending bai'at, to Shaikh **Abd al-Hakim Qadiri** at **Talamba[OCR?]**. After muraqaba the
  shaikh took Gul Sher Muhammad as his murid, "اور **سید بلھے شاہ کو کہا کہ تیرے نصیب کا بجزہ [OCR?]
  شاہ عنایت قادری کے پاس ہے تو بمقام قصور جائے گا تو حصہ پائے گا**" — and told him the portion of his
  lot lies with Shah Inayat Qadiri, and that if he goes to **Qasur** he will receive his share

  referral**, and he is placed in company with the founder of the Uwaisiya. **No dates, no shrine,

**Practices & Events**

### shrine-of-miran-hussain-zanjani-zanjani-sahib — Shrine of Miran Hussain Zanjani (Zanjani Sahib)

**What the row gains.** A short entry (no. 133) with a Junaid chain, an arrival at Lahore in
company, a death year of 600 AH, and the **Chah Miran** burial confirming the archive's location
twice. And the edition's own argument that **the archive's `figure_died` of 1042 belongs to a
different, earlier man of the same name** — with the famous Data Ganj Bakhsh bier story belonging
to that earlier namesake.

**Saint**
- Entry no. 133, printed p. 186 (p. 18, folio 6), "سید حسین زنجانی لاہوری"; entry at p. 198,
  folio 186, with the editor's footnote running to p. 199.
- **The entry itself is four sentences.** Among the ancient elders, a man of guidance, austerity,
  piety, nobility and sayyid descent; "**شجرہ ان کا حضرت جنیدؒ بغدادی کے ساتھ ملتا ہے**", his chain
  meets **Junaid of Baghdad**; "**سید یعقوب زنجانی کے ساتھ یہ لاہور میں آئے**" — he came to Lahore
  **together with Sayyid Yaqub Zanjani** (entry no. 135) and set the tumult of shaykhhood warm;
  he spent his whole life guiding the people; "**آخر سال چھ سو ہجری میں وفات کی**" — died in the year
  **600 AH** (p. 198).
- **Two men of one name — the editor's argument, and the row's date problem.** "**شیخ حسین زنجانی
  لاہوریؒ کے حالات کے سلسلہ میں مفتی صاحب خاصے الجھے ہوئے معلوم ہوتے ہیں**" — the Mufti appears **quite
  tangled**. In *Khazinat al-Asfiya* (2/232), on the authority of *Fawaid al-Fawad*, the Mufti
  writes that Husain Zanjani was **Data Ganj Bakhsh's pir-brother**, and tells the story: ordered
  to Lahore, Data objected "وہاں تو میرے برادر طریقت حسین زنجانی موجود ہیں۔ میری کیا ضرورت ہے"; the
  murshid repeated the order; and on reaching Lahore Data **saw Husain Zanjani's bier going past**
  and understood his murshid's far-seeing eye. "حضرت علی ہجویری کا سال وفات با ختلاف روایت بعد ۴۰۰ھ
  ہے۔ گویا اس مندرجہ روایت کے مطابق شیخ حسین زنجانی کا انتقال ۴۰۰ھ سے بہت پہلے ہو چکا تھا" — on that
  report **Husain Zanjani had died long before 400 AH**. "اب مفتی صاحب خود ہی خزینۃ الاصفیا (۲۵۰/۲)
  اور **حدیقۃ الاولیاء** میں شیخ حسین زنجانی کا سال وفات **بغیر کسی حوالے کے ۶۰۰ھ** لکھ دیتے ہیں" — yet in
  *Khazinat al-Asfiya* 2/250 **and in this very book** he writes 600 AH **without any reference at
  all**. Then the resolution: "**حقیقت یہ ہے کہ زیر بحث شخصیت فوائد الفواد میں مذکور شیخ حسین زنجانی سے
  مختلف ہے**" — the personality under discussion **is a different man** from the Shaikh Husain
  Zanjani of *Fawaid al-Fawad* (p. 198, حاشیہ 1). **For the archive: the Chah Miran shrine belongs
  to the later Husain Zanjani, d. 600 AH, and the Data-bier story to an earlier namesake. The
  row's 1042 (≈ 434 AH) fits the earlier man; the row's site fits the later.**
- **The author's date is then defended from a dated document — the editor's only such defence in
  these chunks.** From *Waqai-e Sialkot* (1071 AH, by Muhammad Muqim, pp. 8, 5) he quotes a
  Persian passage mentioning, in **758 AH**, **Khizr Khan**, grandson (نواسہ) of Sayyid Husain,
  "برادر … سید یعقوب صدر شاہ زنجانی کہ مرقد مقدس ایشاں در لاہور گذر **سنجا [OCR?]** زیارت گاہ خاص و عام
  است", Khizr Khan being "در مقربان عالی شان و امیران … فیروز شاہی", among the high intimates and
  amirs of Firoz Shah. Then: "**اس اقتباس سے تصریح ہوتا ہے کہ ۷۵۸ھ میں شیخ حسین زنجانی کے نواسے بقید
  حیات تھے۔ اس لیے شیخ حسین زنجانی کا مذکورہ سال وفات ۶۰۰ھ قرین صحت ہے**" — in 758 AH his grandson was
  alive, **therefore the stated 600 AH is close to correct** (p. 199, بقیہ حاشیہ 1). A shrine's
  date defended by a grandson's dated appearance in a seventeenth-century local chronicle.
- **A meeting with Khwaja Muin al-Din Chishti.** "حضرت خواجہ معین الدین چشتی اجمیریؒ جب قریباً ۵۸۵ھ
  (تاریخ السلف ۷، ۹) میں لاہور تشریف لائے تو شیخ حسین زنجانی سے **ملاقات و صحبت** رہی" — when Muin al-Din
  came to Lahore around **585 AH** he had **meeting and company** with him (*Ain-e Akbari* 3/207;
  Jamali's *Siyar al-Arifin*; Ghausi's *Gulzar-e Abrar* 25–26; Muhammad Salih Kanbo's *Amal-e
  Salih* 1/50; Dara Shikoh's *Safinat al-Awliya* 93) (p. 198, حاشیہ 1). **Five sources for one
  meeting**, and it is the editor's supporting argument that this Husain Zanjani lived around
  585–600 AH, not before 400 AH.

### bari-imam — Bari Imam

**What the row gains.** An entry (no. 6) of high praise, a named pir and a second pir, and an
eight-item bibliography — set against **a 123-year gap on the death year**, the editor rejecting
the author's ۹۹۴ھ[OCR?] for a family record of 1117 AH / 1706 CE on the evidence of two dated
meetings. The editor also argues the pir himself is unhistorical. **The book never names the
shrine's place**, and the name "Bari Imam" appears only inside the title of a modern book.

**Saint**
- Entry no. 6 of the Qadiri chapter, at printed p. 33 (p. 15, folio 3), "شاہ لطیف بری قادری".
- "**بزرگان پنجاب سے یہ حضرت بڑے بزرگ مشہور ہیں**" — famous as a great elder among the elders of
  Punjab; thousands of *khawariq* and *karamat* famous of him; a great worshipper, ascetic,
  recluse, *mast* and *majzub*; thousands of murids reached the stages of completion (p. 45,
  folio 33).
- He received his inner blessing (نعمت باطنی) from **Hazrat Hayat al-Mir Zinda Pir**, "who is among
  the grandsons of Ghaus al-Azam and is eternally living (**زندہ جاوید**)" (p. 45, folio 33).
- **A second pir**, from Shaikh Bahlul Qadri's entry (no. 10): "کہ شاہ لطیف نے سوائے حیات المیر
  زندہ پیر کے **شیخ نصیر الدین قرشی ملتانی** سے بھی فیض حاصل کیا تھا" — besides Hayat al-Mir Zinda Pir,
  Shah Latif had also taken faiz from **Shaikh Nasir al-Din Qurashi Multani** (p. 50, folio 38).
- **Death — two readings, 123 years apart.** *Author:* **۹۹۴ھ [OCR?]**, and his holy *rauza* is
  famous, **without naming its place** (p. 45, folio 33). *Editor, rejecting it outright:* "شاہ
  لطیف بری کا یہ سال وفات بہت **[OCR?]** متنازع فیہ ہے" — this death year is much disputed. The family
  record now existing gives **1117 AH / 1706 CE**, "which fits the circumstantial evidence",
  because **Sayyid Hasan Peshawari met Shah Latif in Pothohar in ۱۰۷۷ھ [OCR?]**, and **Shah
  Muhammad Ghaus Lahori b. Sayyid Hasan Peshawari met him in 1116 AH** and wrote the meeting up
  himself (p. 46, folio 34, حاشیہ 3).
- Hasan Peshawari's account, quoted in Persian: "چوں در ملک پوٹھوہار رسیدم۔ سید شاہ لطیف مجذوب دیدم
  کہ خیلے صاحب نظر آثار بودند و با من بسلوک تمام ملاقات کردند۔ یک دو روزی گذرانید و رخصت شدم" — "When
  I reached the country of Pothohar I saw Sayyid Shah Latif Majzub, who bore the marks of great
  insight, and he met me with complete courtesy; a day or two passed and I took leave" — sourced
  to Mir Ghulam Kashmiri, *Khawariq al-Adat*, 1189 AH, manuscript owned by Maulana Sayyid Amir
  Shah Qadri of Peshawar, folio **۸ [OCR?]**b (p. 46, folio 34, حاشیہ 3).
- **The witness is dated**, which supports the editor's chronology: "حضرت سید حسن پشاوری کی ولادت
  جمادی الآخر **۱۰۲۳ھ/۱۶۱۴ء** میں **مٹھہ** میں ہوئی" (p. 73, folio 61, حاشیہ 1) — consistent with his
  meeting Shah Latif in Pothohar in 1077 AH **[OCR?]**.
- **The editor also attacks the pir.** Hayat al-Mir "is not mentioned in contemporary books of
  history or in the genealogies". Shah Abul Muali Lahori wrote that of the sons of Shaikh Abdul
  Razzaq b. Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani, Shaikh Jamal Allah was in that era present in the jungles
  of Bastam, **but did not give him the sobriquet Hayat al-Mir**; the sobriquet was first applied
  to Shaikh Jamal Allah by Ahmad Ali Astarabadi in *Tazkira-e Muqimi*, **۱۱۷۲ھ [OCR?]**, p. 2,
  "**جس کا کوئی تاریخی ثبوت نہیں ہے**" — for which there is no historical proof (p. 45, folio 33,
  حاشیہ 2).

**Shrine / Darbar**
- The author says only that his **holy rauza is famous**, and **does not name its place** (p. 45,
  folio 33). **No finding in this group gives a location for this shrine.**

**Significance**
- **An eight-item bibliography** (p. 46, folio 34): Shah Muhammad Ghaus Lahori, *Risala dar
  Kasb-e Suluk wa Bayan-e Marifat*, **Peshawar 1284 AH**; Mir Ghulam Kashmiri, *Khawariq
  al-Adat*, **1189 AH**; Muqim Muhkam al-Din Hajrawi, *Durr al-Ajaib*, translation, Lahore; Gul
  Hasan Shah, *Tazkira-e Ghausia*, Lahore (n.d.); Muhammad Qasim Rajori, *Hayat-e Bari Imam*,
  Rawalpindi; Manzur al-Haq Siddiqi, *Shah Latif Bari*, Lahore 1970; and Iqbal Mujaddidi's own
  article "بری شاہ لطیف" in the *Danishnama-e Shibh-e Qarra*, Tehran. **The title *Hayat-e Bari
  Imam* is the only place in chunk 003 where the name "Bari Imam" itself appears.** Chunk 006
  records that **the printing details of Shah Muhammad Ghaus's *Risala* differ between chunks 003
  and 006** — a review point.
- The edition's book list gives two of them again: item ۲۰۶ "**محمد قاسم راجوروی : حیات بری امام۔
  راولپنڈی (س۔ن)**" — **undated ("س۔ن")** (p. 303, folio 291); and item ۲۲۱ "**منظور الحق صدیقی : شاہ
  لطیف بری۔ لاہور ۱۹۷۰ء**" (p. 303, folio 291), **probably the same saint** given the archive's
  "Sayyid Abdul Latif Kazmi (Bari Imam)", **but the entry does not say so**. So: one certain and
  one probable monograph.
- **An index caution, not a fact about him.** Chunk 030 weighs an index line against **bari-imam**
  and **bhit-bhit-shah** (p. 322, folio 310) — the line itself is not preserved in the surviving
  fragment — and records "عبد الکریم حافظ | ۲۰۹" beside "عبد الکریم لاہوری | ۸۷ ، ۹۱ ، ۹۲ ، ۱۷۶" and
  "عبد الکریم مجددی لاہوری | ۱۴۵", **none with a place tying it to** either of two further rows
  (p. 322).

### shrine-of-imam-ali-ul-haq — Shrine of Imam Ali-ul-Haq

  "فرحت فرح بخش | ۱۵۵ ، ۱۶۱ ، ۱۷۸ ، ۱۸۷" (p. 325).

- **Sialkot otherwise appears only in a nisba:** "حاجی فتح علی متقی سیالکوٹی" (p. 152, folio 140),
  with no grave, no date and no link to this row (chunk 014).

**Significance**
- Book-list item ۱۴۱ "**محمد مقیم : وقائع سیالکوٹ ۱۰۷۱ھ (احوال شہادت امام علی الحق سیالکوٹی) مرتبہ ڈاکٹر
  عبداللہ چغتائی۔ لاہور ۱۹۷۷ء**" — a *Waqa'i Sialkot* of **1071 AH** on the **martyrdom (شہادت)** of
  Imam Ali al-Haq, edited by Dr Abdullah Chaghatai, Lahore **1977** (p. 299, folio 287).
- Note for whoever cites it: **the same *Waqai-e Sialkot* of 1071 AH by Muhammad Muqim is the

  archive rows.
- **The edition's date is itself contradictory, and this item is one of the three reasons.** The
  colophon is dated **12 December 1974**, but three book-list entries carry later imprints: item
  ۱۰۴ "راولپنڈی ۱۹۷۵ء" (p. 298, folio 286); item ۵۶ "دانش گاہ طہران ۱۹۷۷ء" (p. 295, folio 283); and
  item ۱۴۱ "لاہور ۱۹۷۷ء" (p. 299, folio 287). **None of the three is flagged by the transcriber.**
  Either the printed edition is later than its colophon (a reprint with an updated list), or the
  three numerals are misprints or mis-OCR; the chunk cannot tell. Chunk 028's advice is to date
  the edition "**colophon 12 December 1974; printed no earlier than 1977 if the bibliography is
  original**".
