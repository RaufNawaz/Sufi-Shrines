# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 017 — pp. 178–186

**The Suhrawardi chapter continues, entries 116 (tail) to 125, and this is the richest chunk so far
for the archive.** Four rows are the subjects of full entries — **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari**
(no. 120), **shah-jamal** (no. 123), **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai** (no. 124) — and a fourth,
**bibi-pak-daman**, acquires a dated, named subsidiary grave inside its precinct (no. 121). Its three
most substantial features: **an Akbari jagir of one lakh rupees, a named city, a two-site langar, and
a farman with Akbar's seal reported still in the family's hands**; **the editor overturns Shah
Daula's death year and states his method for doing so** — a contemporary source preferred over later
reports, landing within a year of the archive's own figure; and **a seven-storey building at Ichhra
reduced to three, with a Mughal princess's complaint as the cause**.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari** (میران محمد شاہ المشہور سید موج دریا بخاری لاہوری — entry no. 120,
  pp. 181–182, folios 179–180 as printed [−12: 169–170]):
  - **Descent.** Of the progeny of Sayyid Jamal al-Din Mir Surkh Bukhari Uchi; the chain as printed:
    Miran Muhammad Shah bin Sayyid Safi al-Din bin Sayyid Nizam al-Din bin Sayyid **Ilm al-Din
    Thani** bin Jalal al-Din bin Sayyid **Ilm al-Din Awwal** bin Sayyid [نہ OCR?] Nasir al-Din bin
    Sayyid Jalal al-Din Makhdoom Jahaniyan bin Sayyid Ahmad Kabir al-Din bin Sayyid Jalal al-Din Mir
    Surkh Bukhari (p. 181). "ولایت و شرافت و کرامت موروثی ان کو حاصل تھی" — wilayat, nobility and
    karamat were **hereditary** in him.
  - **He came to the Punjab from Uch by an emperor's summons, for a siege.** "پہلے سکونت ان کی اوچ
    میں تھی۔ وہاں سے اکبر بادشاہ نے ان کو مقام چتوڑ گڈھ طلب کیا اور فتح قلعہ کے لیے دعا چاہی" — he
    first lived at **Uch**; from there **Akbar summoned him to Chittorgarh and asked his prayer for
    the conquest of the fort** (p. 181).
  - **The grant, with a figure and a named city.** "بعد فتح قلعہ کے اکبر نے حضرت کو ایک لاکھ روپے کی
    جاگیر پنجاب میں دی اور شہر ٹبالہ ان کی جاگیر میں قرار پایا" — after the fort's conquest Akbar
    gave him **a jagir of one lakh rupees in the Punjab**, and the **city of Tibala** was fixed within
    his jagir (p. 181). See Practices for the langar and the farman.
  - **He took it unwillingly.** "حضرت نے باکراہ وہ جاگیر قبول کر کے لنگر جاری کر دیا" — he accepted
    the jagir **with reluctance** and set a langar running (p. 181). The book's only saint who both
    accepts a state grant and is said to dislike doing so.
  - **Residence and the shrine.** "حضرت کی سکونت اکثر لاہور میں تھی اور یہاں ہی رہ کر حضرت نے ہنگامہ
    مشیت گرم رکھا" — he lived mostly in **Lahore** and kept the tumult of shaykhhood warm there.
    Death "سنہ ایک ہزار چودہ ۱۰۱۴ھ ہجری" — **1014 AH** — "اور روضہ عالیہ لاہور میں زیارت گاہ خلق ہے",
    and his lofty roza in **Lahore** is a place of pilgrimage (p. 182). The archive row's
    `figure_died` reads 1604, which is the CE equivalent of 1014 AH. **No quarter of Lahore is
    given**; the row's "Edward Road, Anarkali" is not in this chunk.
  - **Chronogram, printed twice with the numeral.** "چوں محمد شاہ ملک عاقبت / در مقام قرب حقانی نشست
    // از حضور ۱۰۱۴ھ آمد عیان تاریخ او / نیز سرور گفت مرد حق پرست ۱۰۱۴ھ" (p. 182).
  - **Three sons named, with their mothers, and the family's split between two towns.** Sayyid **Safi
    al-Din**, named for his grandfather, and Sayyid **Baha al-Din**, both "سیدہ پاک آمنہ المشہور بی
    بی کلاں بنت سید عبدالقادر ثالث کے بطن پاک سے" — from Sayyida Pak Amina, famous as **Bibi Kalan**,
    daughter of Sayyid Abd al-Qadir III; third, **Shah Shihab al-Din** [المعنی OCR?] "بہ مہر بی بی
    فورنگ [OCR?]", from another wife. Shihab al-Din settled at **Tibala** and his descendants are
    there; **Safi al-Din's descendants live in Lahore** (p. 182). A living descent line attached to
    the Lahore shrine, stated in the present tense of 1974's text.
  - **A fourth son, supplied by the editor and absent from the text**, and a partition-era note on
    his shrine — see Other saints (Fath Allah Shah) and Practices.
  - **The wooden-pot karamat** is under Legends; his brother's grave at Bibi Pak Daman is under that
    row.
- **shah-jamal** (حضرت شاہ جمال سہروردی لاہوری — entry no. 123, p. 185, folio 183 as printed [−12:
  173]):
  - **The shrine is named from its building.** "مزار پر انوار لاہور میں متصل موضع اچھرہ کے ہے۔ جس کو
    شاہ جمال کا دمدمہ کہتے ہیں" — his mazar is in **Lahore adjoining mauza Ichhra**, and it is called
    **Shah Jamal's damdama** (p. 185). The archive row's location, "Shah Jamal/Ichhra, Lahore",
    matches; the book supplies the *reason* the site carries that name.
  - **Death** "چودھویں ماہ ربیع الثانی سنہ ایک ہزار پچاس" — **14 Rabi al-Thani 1050 AH** (p. 185).
    The archive row reads 1671, which is not 1050 AH: see Doubts.
  - **His chain runs to Multan, not to Bukhara.** "حضرت شاہ جمال مرید شیخ گدا بیگ کے اور وہ مرید شاہ
    شرف کے وہ مرید شاہ معروف کے وہ مرید جعفر الدین کے وہ مرید ضیاء الدین کے وہ مرید شیخ جمال کے وہ
    مرید شیخ عارف صدر الدین کے وہ مرید اپنے والد شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی کے" — Shah Jamal murid
    of Shaikh Gada Beg, he of Shah Sharaf, he of Shah Maruf, he of Jafar al-Din, he of Zia al-Din, he
    of Shaikh Jamal, he of Shaikh Arif Sadr al-Din, he of his own father **Shaikh Baha al-Din Zakariya
    Multani** (p. 185). **Eight steps from Baha al-Din Zakariya**, i.e. a Multani Suhrawardi line, not
    a Bukhari one.
  - **"اور یہ حضرت سید حسینی تھے اور اولاد ان کی اب تک سیالکوٹ میں سکونت رکھتی ہے"** — he was a
    **Sayyid Husaini**, and his descendants **live to this day in Sialkot** (p. 185). Both statements
    conflict with the archive row's "Naqvi Bukhari": see Doubts. Note also that the descent line is
    placed at **Sialkot**, not at the Lahore shrine — so the archive should not assume a resident
    sajjada family from this book.
  - **A brother: "ان کا دوسرا بھائی شاہ کمال بھی عابد و زاہد صاحب جذب و شغل تھا۔ ان دونوں بزرگوں کے
    خوارق و کرامت اکثر لوگوں میں مشہور ہیں"** — his second brother **Shah Kamal** was also devout,
    ascetic, a man of jazb and shughl, and the marvels of **both** these elders are famous among most
    people (p. 185). No grave, no date and no location for Shah Kamal: see Doubts on
    shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal.
  - **The building, the night shift, and the four sunken storeys** are under Practices and Legends —
    the most detailed architectural account in these chunks.
  - He is also the pir who reformed a Lahore grain merchant's weights: see entry no. 119 under Other
    saints, and the note on the thirty-eight-year gap under Doubts.
- **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai** (شاہ دولا دریائی گجراتی — entry no. 124, p. 186 and p. 184,
  folios 184 and 182 as printed [−12: 174 and 172]; **the entry's text is dislocated across these two
  pages — see Doubts**):
  - **"پنجاب کے مشہور بزرگوں میں سے"** — among the **famous elders of the Punjab** (p. 186); a man of
    lofty stations, marvels, jazb, ishq, zuhd and riyazat, "جامع فتوحات ظاہری و باطنی" (p. 184).
  - **Death: the text and the editor disagree, and the editor states his method.** Text: "وفات حضرت
    کی سال ایک ہزار پچھتر میں واقع ہوئی اور مادہ تاریخ لفظ خدا دوست ہے" — death in the year
    **1075** AH, the chronogram being the word **"خدا دوست"**; "مزار گوہر بار شہر پنجاب گجرات میں
    زیارت گاہ خلق ہے", his mazar in the **city of Gujrat, Punjab**, is a place of pilgrimage
    (p. 186). Editor: **"۱۰۸۷ھ [OCR?] / ۱۶۷۶ع [OCR?] میں انتقال کیا"** — he died in **1087 AH [OCR?]
    / 1676 CE [OCR?]** (Mushtaq Ram Gujrati, *Karamat Nama*, qalmi folio 5 [OCR?], owned by Sayyid

    1675 is within a year of the editor's figure and twelve years from the author's.**
  - **A court like a king's.** "ان کی سرکار بادشاہوں کی سرکار کے مانند تھی۔ ہزاروں نوکر چاکر گھوڑا
    پالکی دروازہ پر حاضر رہتے تھے" — his establishment was like the establishment of kings; thousands
    of servants, a horse and a palanquin stood at his door (p. 184). "اہل حاجت کا ہجوم ہر وقت رہتا

  - **The vow of a child, the "chuha", and the shrine as a foundling institution** — the fullest
    account of a shrine's economy and social function in these chunks; see Practices.
  - **His chain, thirteen steps to Baha al-Din Multani, through Rajan Qattal of Uch.** Shah Daula
    murid and khalifa of **Shaikh Sina Sarmast**, he of Shah Monga, he of Shah Kabir, he of Shaikh
    **Shahr Allah [OCR?]**, he of Shaikh Yusuf, he of Pir Burhan, he of Shaikh Sadr al-Din, he of
    Shaikh Badr al-Din, he of Shaikh Ismail Qureshi, he of **Shaikh Sadr al-Din Rajan Qattal Uchi**
    (chunk 016, entry no. 108), he of **Shaikh Rukn al-Din Abu al-Fath Multani**, he of Shaikh Sadr
    al-Din Arif, he of **Shaikh Baha al-Din Multani** (p. 184). "خاندان چشت اہل بہشت سے بھی ان کو
    فیض کامل حاصل ہوا" — he also received complete faiz from the **Chishti** family.
- **bibi-pak-daman** (بی بی پاک دامناں — within entry no. 121, p. 183, folio 181 as printed [−12:
  171]):
  - **A dated, named grave built against the shrine's enclosure wall, with its own door into the
    khanqah.** Of Sayyid Sultan Jalal al-Din Haidar bin Safi al-Din Bukhari, brother of Mauj Darya:
    **"آخر سال ایک ہزار سولہ میں فوت ہو کر بدیوار حریم مزار بی بی پاک دامناں مدفون ہوئے۔ وہ روضہ اب
    تک موجود ہے اور دروازہ اس کا خانقاہ پاک دامناں کے اندر ہے اور لوگ اس کو مزار استاد بی بی پاک
    دامناں کہتے ہیں"** — he died in the year **1016 AH** and was buried **at the wall of the harim of
    the mazar of Bibi Pak Damanan**; that rauza **still exists**, its **door is inside the khanqah of
    Pak Damanan**, and **people call it "the mazar of the ustad (teacher) of Bibi Pak Damanan"**
    (p. 183).
  - **Three separable facts for the row**: the precinct contained, by 1016 AH, a subsidiary rauza
    against its enclosure wall; that rauza was still standing when the book was written; and the
    popular name attached to it — *ustad* of the Bibis — is a **misattribution the book implicitly
    corrects by naming the actual occupant**, a Bukhari sayyid who died in 1016 AH, roughly a thousand
    years after the Bibis' traditional era. The book does not say the name is wrong; it simply reports
    both. **Cite it as the book does: the grave of Jalal al-Din Haidar, called by people the ustad's
    grave.**
  - The chunk says nothing else about Bibi Pak Daman itself — no urs, no custodian, no building date.
    The words "حریم" (enclosure/sanctum) and "خانقاہ پاک دامناں" are the only architectural terms.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Makhdoom Shaikh Hamza Kashmiri** (entry no. 116, tail, p. 178, folio 176 as printed): so absorbed
  that he met no worldly person; **"پہاڑ کے ایک غار میں ان کا مقام تھا"**, his station was in **a cave
  in the mountain**; he formed such an **Uwaisi** nisbat with the Prophet that he reached the rank of
  *fana fi'l-rasul*. "چونکہ مرشد ظاہری ان کا کوئی نہ تھا" — since he had **no outward murshid**, he
  sought leave in the matter from the Prophet's *ruhaniyat* and was told **"تیرا مرشد خود تیرے پاس
  آئے گا"**, your murshid will come to you himself; some months later Sayyid Jamal al-Din Suhrawardi
  came from **Delhi to Kashmir** by divine inspiration, "گویا تشریف لانا ان کا کشمیر میں ان کی تربیت
  و تکمیل کے لیے تھا", as though his coming to Kashmir were for this man's training. Died **984** AH;
  "مزار ملک کشمیر میں ہے", mazar in the land of Kashmir. With the editor's birth year of 900 AH
  (chunk 016), a life of 84 lunar years.
- **Baba Daud Khaki Kashmiri Suhrawardi** (entry no. 117, pp. 178–179, folios 176–177 as printed):
  took to learning in his youth, then bai'at with Shaikh Hamza; "شیخ حمزہ کو جس قدر محبت ان کے ساتھ
  تھی اور کسی مرید کے ساتھ نہ تھی", Hamza loved him more than any other murid. **The Chak sultans of
  Kashmir, "جن کا مذہب شیعہ تھا"**, had become kings and "بہ تعصب مذہبی انہوں نے قاضی موسیٰ کشمیری
  کو شہید کر دیا تھا", out of sectarian bigotry had **martyred Qazi Musa Kashmiri**; Baba Daud
  therefore left the country for Hind and declared **"جب تک کشمیر سے سلاطین چک کی سلطنت دور نہ ہو
  گی۔ ہم کشمیر میں نہ آئیں گے"**, we will not come to Kashmir while the Chak sultanate stands.
  "آخر جب قاسم خان بحری اکبر بادشاہ کی فوج لے کر کشمیر کو گیا" — when **Qasim Khan Bahri** took
  Akbar's army to Kashmir and Timurid possession was established and the Chak sultanate was wiped
  out, he went to Kashmir, "مگر اسی سال یعنی نو سو چورانوے میں وفات پائی اور شہر سری نگر میں مدفون
  ہوئے" — but died that **same year, 994** AH, and was **buried in the city of Srinagar**. The editor
  confirms and dates the conquest: Akbar attacked in **994 AH / 1585 CE** to free Kashmir's people
  from this family's oppression and ended it (Muhibb al-Hasan, *Kashmir Salatin ke Ahd mein*
  pp. 257[OCR?], 284[OCR?]); "حضرت بابا داؤد خاکی اکبر بادشاہ کے لشکر کے ہمراہ کشمیر پہنچے اور اسی
  سال انتقال کیا", **he reached Kashmir with Akbar's army and died that year** (*Tarikh-e Kashmir
  Azami* p. 108). His works: *Wird al-Muridin*, *Dastur al-Salikin* (its commentary), *Qasida-e
  Jalaliya*, *Risala-e Ghusliya* (p. 178, حاشیہ 1).
- **Sayyid Jhulan Shah, famous as Ghore Shah Lahori** (entry no. 118, p. 179, folio 177 as printed):
  beloved son of Sayyid Shah Muhammad bin Sayyid Usman Bukhari — i.e. **grandson of Shah Jhula
  Bukhari of chunk 016's entry no. 111** — "مجذوب [OCR?] مادر زاد ولی تھے", a majzub and a wali from
  birth; **real name Baha al-Din**. At five he loved riding horses, "اور وہ شوق اس قدر بڑھا کہ جو
  کوئی مٹی کا بنا ہوا گھوڑا ان کی خدمت میں لے آتا جو مراد مانگتا حاصل ہو جاتی" — whoever brought him
  **a horse made of clay** had whatever wish he asked granted. When this became famous and the needy
  came in droves, his father grew angry: "الہی یہ خورد سال لڑکا کہ موجب انکشاف اسرار الہی ہوتا ہے۔
  زندہ رہنے کے قابل نہیں ہے" — O God, this small boy, who becomes the cause of the disclosure of
  divine secrets, is not fit to live; **at the mere speaking of these words Jhulan Shah died**. Death
  **11 Rabi al-Awwal 1003 AH**; "مزار لاہور کے باہر زیارت گاہ خلق ہے", mazar **outside Lahore**. The
  votive clay horses and the two later burials are under Practices; the editor's correction of his
  name and silsila is under Arguments.
- **Shaikh Hasan Kunjadgar, famous as Hassu Teli Lahori** (entry no. 119, pp. 180–181, folios 178–179
  as printed): **a grain seller** ("اول غلہ فروشی کا کام کرتا تھا") in straits, who came to **Shah
  Jamal Lahori** asking for expansion of livelihood and was told **"کم تولنا چھوڑ دو"**, give up
  weighing short; "اس روز سے اس نے کم تولنا چھوڑ دیا اور چند سال میں دولت مند بن گیا", from that day
  he did, and within a few years grew rich. Returning with money as a thank-offering, "حضرت نے نہ
  لیا", the Hazrat would not take it, and said that if he now gave up love of this wealth he would be
  richer still; he renounced at once and "مال و دولت براہ خدا، غربا و فقرا کو تقسیم کر دیا",
  distributed his goods to the poor and the destitute for God's sake, spending the rest of his life in
  his murshid's service. Died **1012** AH. The editor's footnote, from Surat Singh, is much fuller:
  he was from **Makiwal, on the bank of the Chenab**; father Shaikh Chindu[OCR?], mother
  Meli[OCR?]; **died 3 Shawwal 1011 AH at Lahore, aged 120**; he had ties with contemporary nobles —
  **Abd al-Rahim Khan-e Khanan asked his prayer for the conquest of Thatta**; his relations with
  **Makhdoom al-Mulk Abdullah Sultanpuri** were strained; Shaikh Farid Bakhsh asked his prayer for
  offspring; **his will had a part in Prince Salim's rebellion and then in his reconciliation with
  Akbar**; and **in days of famine Akbar asked him to pray for rain, and the prayer proved
  effective** (p. 180, folio 178 as printed, حاشیہ 1).
- **Baba Nasib al-Din Suhrawardi Kashmiri** (entry no. 122, p. 183, folio 181 as printed): "خلیفہ
  اعظم شیخ داؤد کشمیری کے", the greatest khalifa of Shaikh Daud Kashmiri; spent his whole life in
  renunciation, never ate any delicious food or fruit, "سوائے نان خشک کے کوئی چیز آپ کی غذا نہ تھی",
  nothing but dry bread was his food, "اور نہ کبھی دیدہ حق بین خواب سے آشنا ہوئے", nor did his eye
  ever know sleep. Death **13 Muharram 1047 AH**; "مزار سرزمین کشمیر میں ہے"; the chronogram **"وہو
  خیر الصالحین" = 1047**. The editor's account of his *Nur Nama* is under Arguments.
- **Shaikh Jan Muhammad Suhrawardi Lahori** (entry no. 125, opening only, p. 186, folio 184 as
  printed): among the khalifas of **Shaikh Ismail, famous as Bara Mian Lahori**; people came from far
  to him. A man complained of his poverty and was told to recite the *kalima-e tamjid* a hundred
  times a day and report in a week; after a week he said "اب میں مالدار ہو گیا ہوں۔ اب کوئی حاجت
  میری باقی نہیں رہی", I have become wealthy and have no need left; told to continue another week, he
  returned saying "میری طبیعت مال دنیا سے بیزار ہے۔ مجھ کو اپنا مرید کرو", my nature is sick of
  worldly goods, make me your murid — and renounced the world. The entry breaks off at "وفات". The
  editor: Maulana Jan Muhammad Lahori was a foremost scholar of his time and a *sahib-e irshad*; his
  manuscript *Namaz-e Chahar Gana Ba'd az Juma* is **"راقم محمد اقبال مجددی کے کتب خانہ میں"**, in
  the library of the writer, **Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi** (p. 186, حاشیہ 1). See Arguments on what
  this tells us about the edition.
- **Fath Allah Shah, famous as Fath Shah Bukhari — a fourth son of Mauj Darya, and an Amritsar
  shrine's fate** (p. 182, folio 180 as printed, حاشیہ 1): "حضرت موج دریا کے چوتھے فرزند کا ذکر نامی
  صاحب نے ایک نامعلوم الاسم قلمی کتاب کے حوالہ سے فتح اللہ شاہ مشہور بہ فتح شاہ بخاری مدفون بیرون
  دروازہ لوہ گڈھ دلاہوری [OCR?] امرتسر کا ذکر کیا ہے" — Nami, on the authority of an **anonymous
  manuscript**, mentions a **fourth son** of Mauj Darya, Fath Allah Shah, famous as Fath Shah
  Bukhari, buried **outside the Loh Garh gate [دلاہوری OCR?], Amritsar** (*Nami, Bazurgan-e Lahore*
  no. 136). And then: **"تقسیم پاک و ہند تک امرتسر میں یہ مزار سب سے زیادہ مرجع خلائق تھا۔ اب بھی
  شکستہ صورت میں موجود ہے"** — until the **partition of Pakistan and India** this mazar was the most
  frequented in Amritsar; **it still exists, in a broken state**. The only statement in these chunks
  about a shrine's condition after 1947, and about a shrine now across the border.
- **Qazi Musa Kashmiri** (pp. 178–179): martyred by the Chak sultans, the event that empties Kashmir
  of Baba Daud.
- **Princess Sultan Begum, sister of Emperor Akbar** (p. 185, folio 183 as printed): owner of the
  garden adjoining Shah Jamal's damdama and the complainant against its height; see Practices.
- **Rulers and nobles acting in this chunk**: **Akbar** (Chittorgarh and the jagir; the Kashmir
  conquest of 994 AH; the famine prayer; the reconciliation with Salim), **Prince Salim**, **Qasim
  Khan Bahri**, **Abd al-Rahim Khan-e Khanan**, the **Chak sultans** of Kashmir, and **Shaikh Farid
  Bakhsh**.
- **Named subsidiary burials beside Ghore Shah** (p. 179, folio 177 as printed, بقیہ حاشیہ):
  **Sayyid Shahbaz bin Imadi al-Mulk**, 7 Rajab **1041 AH**, and **Sayyid Kamwi[OCR?] Shah bin Arif
  Shah bin Imadi al-Mulk**, 22 Rajab **1050**, both buried "بہ پہلوئے مزار جھولن شاہ", at the side of
  Jhulan Shah's mazar. **Sayyid Imadi al-Mulk**, real brother of Jhulan Shah, resident in Lahore, and
  **Sayyid Hakim Shah and Muhammad Shah**, his descendants, are named as the holders of the
  genealogical register the dates come from.
- **Places named**: **Lahore** (Mauj Darya's roza, Ghore Shah's mazar "در زمین حاجی نالہ بیرون
  لاہور", Shah Jamal's damdama at Ichhra, Hassu Teli's death, Jan Muhammad, Bibi Pak Daman's
  khanqah); **Tibala** (jagir city, langar, a branch of the family); **Ichhra**; **Gujrat**; **Uch**;
  **Srinagar** and Kashmir; **Amritsar**; **Sialkot**; **Chittorgarh**; **Makiwal on the Chenab**;
  **Multan**; **Thatta**; and, among library locations, **Karachi Museum**, **Asiatic Society
  Calcutta**, **Edinburgh**, **India Office London**, **Punjab Public Library Lahore**, **Punjab
  University (Sherani collection)**, **British Museum**, **Aligarh Muslim University**, **Peshawar**
  and **Sahnpal** (pp. 178–186).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A state jagir with a figure, a named city, a two-site langar, and a surviving sealed farman.** The
  fullest endowment record in these chunks, for **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari**: a jagir of **one
  lakh rupees in the Punjab** with the **city of Tibala** inside it, granted by **Akbar** after
  Chittorgarh; accepted "باکراہ", reluctantly; and turned into a langar — **"لاہور و ٹبالہ دو جگہ
  حضرت کا لنگر جاری تھا۔ صدہا غربا و مساکین کھانا کھاتے تھے"**, the Hazrat's langar ran at **two
  places, Lahore and Tibala**, and **hundreds of the poor and destitute ate** (p. 181, folio 179 as
  printed). Then the editor's footnote: **"سید محمد لطیف نے لکھا ہے کہ جس فرمان کی رو سے یہ جاگیر
  ملی تھی، وہ آج بھی اس خاندان کے پاس محفوظ ہے۔ جس پر اکبر کی مہر ثبت ہے"** — Sayyid Muhammad Latif
  wrote that **the farman under which this jagir was received is preserved with this family even
  today, and Akbar's seal is impressed on it** (*Muhammad Latif, Lahore*) (p. 181, حاشیہ 1). **A
  document, a seal, and a named holder — the strongest provenance claim in these chunks, and it is
  reported at second hand from Latif rather than seen by the editor.** See Doubts on what "آج بھی"
  dates to.
- **A city's founding date, from a shrine's own tazkira, with a second source disagreeing.** "شہر
  ٹبالہ حضرت شیخ عبدالجلیل چوہڑ بندگی لاہوری کے حکم سے ۹۱۰ھ [OCR?] آباد کیا گیا" — the city of
  **Tibala was settled by the order of Shaikh Abd al-Jalil Chuhar Bandagi Lahori in 910 AH [OCR?]**
  (*Tazkira-e Qutbiya* 63–64); "وقائع سیالکوٹ میں ہے کہ ٹبالہ [illegible] میں آباد ہوا تھا", the
  *Waqai-e Sialkot* says Tibala was settled in [illegible] (*Muhammad Muqim, Waqai-e Sialkot* 38
  [OCR?]) (p. 181, حاشیہ 2). Note that 910 AH is the **year of Abd al-Jalil's death** as chunk 016
  gives it — see Doubts. A saint founds the town that a later saint of another lineage is granted as
  jagir.
- **Votive clay horses accumulated at a grave, offered on fulfilment.** Of Ghore Shah: **"اب بھی کئی
  انبار گلی گھوڑوں کے مزار پر موجود ہیں اور اہل حاجت جن کی حاجت بر آتی ہے چڑھا جاتے ہیں"** — even
  now **several heaps of clay horses** are present at the mazar, and the needy whose need is met **go
  on offering them** (p. 179, folio 177 as printed). A named, material, still-current votive practice
  tied to the saint's childhood, and the object of the vow is the same object that worked the miracle.
- **A shrine that raises abandoned children, and the vow that supplies them.** Of Shah Daula: the
  childless came asking prayer for offspring and were told "مجھے تمام اولاد کے جوان کو حاصل ہو گی۔
  ایک لڑکا ہمارے نذر کر دینا", make over one boy as an offering to us; those who agreed had children,
  and the child vowed to the Hazrat "مستانہ و مجذوب ہو جاتا۔ سر اس کا بہت چھوٹا ہوتا۔ اسی واسطے اس
  کو **شاہ دولا کا چوہا** کہتے" — became mad and majzub with a very small head, and for that reason
  was called **Shah Daula's chuha**. "چنانچہ اب تک یہ کرامت جاری ہے۔ دو چار چوہے ہمیشہ خانقاہ پر
  پرورش پاتے ہیں" — this karamat continues to this day, and **two or four chuhas are always being
  reared at the khanqah**; the parent "اس کو حضرت کے مزار پر چھوڑ جاتا ہے و متعلقان مزار اس کو پرورش
  کرتے ہیں", leaves the child at the Hazrat's mazar and **the shrine's dependants rear it** (p. 186,
  folio 184 as printed). The book reports all of this in the present tense and as a *karamat*, not a
  practice to be judged. **"متعلقان مزار"** — the people attached to the mazar — is the chunk's term
  for the staff that does the rearing.
- **A shrine establishment described as a royal one.** Shah Daula's *sarkar* like a king's, with
  thousands of servants and a horse and palanquin at the door, and a permanent crowd of petitioners
  (p. 184, folio 182 as printed). Read against Muhammad Masum's refusal of Aurangzeb's jagir in
  chunk 012, this is the opposite pole of the same question.
- **A seven-storey building, night labour hired off a royal worksite, and its reduction to three.** Of
  Shah Jamal: he intended to build "ایک دمدمہ بلند اپنے قیام کے واسطے", a lofty **damdama** for his
  own residence, but **"بسبب اس کے کہ بادشاہی عمارتیں جا بجا تعمیر ہو رہی تھیں معمار دستیاب نہیں
  ہوتے تھے"** — because royal buildings were going up everywhere, **masons were not to be had**; so
  "حضرت نے حکم دیا کہ معمار دن کو بادشاہی عمارت پر کام کریں اور رات کو ہمارے دمدمہ کی تعمیر میں
  مصروف رہیں", he ordered that the masons work on the royal building by day and on his damdama by
  night. When it stood **seven storeys** ("دمدمہ ہفت منزلہ"), Akbar's sister **Princess Sultan
  Begum**, whose garden adjoined it, sent word that it was too high and overlooked her palace; he
  kept silent; some days later, in ecstasy at a **sama' assembly** ("مجلس سماع گرم تھی"), he rose and
  danced, **"تو چار منزلیں دمدمہ کی زمین کے اندر غرق ہو گئیں اور تین باقی رہیں جو اب تک موجود ہیں"**
  — four storeys sank into the ground and **three remained, which exist to this day** (p. 185,
  folio 183 as printed). For the archive this is at once a building account, a labour-market note, a
  neighbour dispute with the imperial family, evidence of **sama'** at the site, and the origin of the
  name "Shah Jamal's damdama". **Three storeys standing when the book was written** is a checkable
  claim about the present structure.
- **Short weight as the obstacle to a merchant's livelihood.** Shah Jamal's whole prescription for a
  grain seller's poverty is **"کم تولنا چھوڑ دو"** (p. 180, folio 178 as printed) — the shrine
  intervening in market practice, and the wealth that follows then renounced and distributed to the
  poor.
- **A saint's prayer solicited for a siege, a conquest, offspring and rain.** Akbar at **Chittorgarh**
  (Mauj Darya, p. 181); **Khan-e Khanan for the conquest of Thatta** and **Akbar for rain in famine**,
  "جو موثر ثابت ہوئی", which proved effective (Hassu Teli, p. 180); Shaikh Farid Bakhsh for offspring
  (same); the childless at Gujrat (Shah Daula, p. 186). Four kinds of petition, two of them from the
  state.
- **Dated subsidiary burials beside a Lahore grave, from a family genealogical register.** Beside
  Ghore Shah: Sayyid Shahbaz bin Imadi al-Mulk on **7 Rajab 1041 AH**, and Sayyid Kamwi[OCR?] Shah
  bin Arif Shah bin Imadi al-Mulk on **22 Rajab 1050**; the source is "شجرہ نسبیہ سید حاکم شاہ و
  محمد شاہ", the **genealogical register of Sayyid Hakim Shah and Muhammad Shah**, descendants of
  Jhulan Shah's brother Imadi al-Mulk, resident in Lahore (p. 179, folio 177 as printed, بقیہ
  حاشیہ). The shrine grows into a family burial ground across half a century, and the evidence is the
  family's own document.
- **A grave's location given as a land parcel**: Ghore Shah's mazar "در زمین حاجی نالہ بیرون لاہور" —
  in the **land of Haji Nala, outside Lahore** — and "از اشہر تہین [OCR?] مزارات", among the most
  famous of mazars (p. 179, folio 177 as printed). Precise enough to be searched for.
- **A hereditary line split between two towns, named and dated.** Mauj Darya's descendants: Shihab
  al-Din's at **Tibala**, Safi al-Din's at **Lahore** (p. 182, folio 180 as printed). The only
  statement in these chunks of where a Lahore shrine's descent line actually lives.
- **A manuscript in the editor's own library**, and others with named owners: *Karamat Nama* with
  **Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi** (p. 184); *Sahibiya* of Jahan Ara with **Professor Muhammad Aslam,
  Lahore**, and *Muftah al-Arifin* in the Sherani collection (p. 184); *Namaz-e Chahar Gana* and
  *Tuhfat al-Muslimin* with **Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi** (p. 186); Surat Singh's *Tazkirat al-Shaikh
  wal-Khadam* at **Aligarh Muslim University**, History Department, no. 196[OCR?] (p. 180).
- **A book identified as unpublished, with six manuscript locations.** Of Baba Nasib al-Din's *Nur
  Nama* / *Rishi Nama*: **"یہ گراں بہا کتاب اب تک شائع نہیں ہوئی ہے"**, this precious book has not
  been published to this day; copies at **Karachi Museum** (Maulvi Shams al-Din's, written 1134 AH
  [OCR?]), **Asiatic Society Calcutta**, **Edinburgh**, **India Office London**, **Punjab Public
  Library Lahore** and **Punjab University Sherani collection no. 638/3659** (Storey 1/1/958[OCR?])
  (p. 183, folio 181 as printed, حاشیہ 1).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A wooden pot on a saint's feet, answering a sectarian slur with a Punjabi proverb.** Someone said
  before Mauj Darya that "سید کبھی سنی نہیں ہوتا" — a sayyid is never Sunni, because sayyids have no
  love for the three companions, and quoted the Punjabi saying **"سید سنی نہیں کاٹھ دی کنٹی [OCR?]
  نہیں"**, glossed in the text as "سید سنی نہیں ہے اور لکڑی کی ہنڈیا نہیں ہے" — there is no Sunni
  sayyid as there is no wooden pot. Mauj Darya called for **a wooden pot**, made a stove of his two
  feet, lit a fire in it, put rice in the pot and held it on his feet until the rice was cooked; "خدا
  کی قدرت سے نہ تو پاؤں جلے اور نہ ہنڈیا لکڑی کی جلی", by God's power neither his feet nor the wooden
  pot burned; then: **"دیکھو سید بھی سنی ہے اور ہنڈیا بھی لکڑی کی"** — see, there is a Sunni sayyid
  and a wooden pot too (p. 182, folio 180 as printed). A karamat whose entire point is a **sectarian
  claim about the Bukhari sayyids' Sunni allegiance**, argued through a proverb.
- **Four storeys sunk by a dance** — Shah Jamal's damdama, under Practices (p. 185, folio 183 as
  printed).
- **Water burning as oil in the lamps** at midnight on that same building site (p. 185, folio 183 as
  printed).
- **A father's word kills his five-year-old son.** Ghore Shah dies "بمجرد فرمانے ان کلمات کے", at the
  mere speaking of his father's words, because the boy's wish-granting was disclosing divine secrets
  (p. 179, folio 177 as printed). The karamat is the father's, and the cost is the child's life; the
  shrine is the child's.
- **Clay horses that grant wishes**, at five years of age (p. 179, folio 177 as printed).
- **Poverty cured by an honest scale, and wealth cured by renunciation** — the two-stage prescription
  to Hassu Teli, in which the miracle is moral and the second instruction undoes the first result
  (pp. 180–181, folios 178–179 as printed).
- **Wealth in one week, revulsion in two** — the same structure again in Jan Muhammad's entry, with
  the *kalima-e tamjid* a hundred times a day (p. 186, folio 184 as printed).
- **A murshid who arrives because the Prophet said he would**, months after the promise, travelling
  from Delhi to a cave in Kashmir (p. 178, folio 176 as printed).
- **The chuhas of Shah Daula** — reported as a karamat still in operation, under Practices (p. 186,
  folio 184 as printed).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor overturns Shah Daula's death year and states the rule he is applying.** Text: 1075 AH.
  Editor: **1087 AH [OCR?] / 1676 CE [OCR?]**, and then, in his own words: **"شاہ دولہ کے سال وفات
  میں اختلاف ہے۔ ہم نے معاصر ماخذ کرامت نامہ کو اس سلسلہ میں دیگر روایات پر ترجیح دی ہے"** — there is
  **disagreement** about Shah Daula's year of death; **we have preferred the contemporary source, the
  *Karamat Nama*, over the other reports** in this matter (p. 184, folio 182 as printed, حاشیہ 1).
  This is the clearest statement of editorial method in these chunks: the criterion is **contemporaneity
  of the source**, the disagreement is acknowledged rather than hidden, and the choice is marked as
  the editor's own ("ہم نے … ترجیح دی ہے"). He also notes that the author of *Ma'arij al-Wilayat* met
  Shah Daula while travelling to Hasan Abdal. The same criterion produced the Shaikh Musa correction
  in chunk 016 ("قریب العہد") and the genealogical rulings of chunk 012.
- **He calls another author's identification "utterly wrong", and prosecutes it with the author's own
  other book.** Of Ghore Shah: **"نور احمد چشتی نے سید جھولن شاہ (گھوڑے شاہ) کا نام شاہ محمد حفیظ
  اور ان کی نسبت سلسلہ چشتیہ صابریہ سے کی ہے جو درست نہیں"** — Nur Ahmad Chishti gave Jhulan Shah's
  name as Shah Muhammad Hafiz and affiliated him to the **Chishti Sabiri** silsila, **which is not
  correct**; and then, quoting **"مفتی صاحب"** — the author of this very book — from his *Khazinat
  al-Asfiya*, in Persian: "آنکہ صاحب تحقیقات چشتی می فرماید کہ نام حضرت جھولن شاہ، محمد حفیظ است و
  ارادت بسلسلہ چشتیہ صابریہ بخدمت محسن شاہ و وہی بخدمت جان محمد چشتی لاہوری داشت **محض غلط است۔
  اعوذ باللہ من اظہار الاخبار الکذب**" — what the author of *Tahqiqat-e Chishti* says … is **utterly
  false; I seek refuge in God from the publishing of lying reports** (*Khazinat al-Asfiya* 2/89–90)
  (p. 179, folio 177 as printed, حاشیہ 1 and بقیہ). **Three things for the archive**: a saint's
  *name* and *silsila* are both contested in print; the evidence preferred is a **family genealogical
  register** naming its living holders; and the editor's technique here is to let the author refute
  the third party, rather than to correct the author — the opposite of his Shah Daula and Shaikh Musa
  footnotes. The rejected affiliation runs through "جان محمد چشتی لاہوری", a name two entries away
  from this chunk's own Suhrawardi Jan Muhammad: see Doubts.
- **The edition's editor names himself, and locates a manuscript in his own library.** "راقم محمد
  اقبال مجددی کے کتب خانہ میں ہے" — it is in the library of **the writer, Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi**
  (p. 186, folio 184 as printed, حاشیہ 1), and *Tuhfat al-Muslimin* is "قلمی مملوکہ محمد اقبال
  مجددی" on the same page. Chunk 016 cited *Siraj al-Hidaya* as his property and chunk 012 cited his
  1972 Lahore monograph as a source. **So the apparatus of this edition is the work of a named
  collector citing his own holdings** — a provenance fact worth stating in any entry that leans on
  the footnotes, and a reason those citations cannot be independently checked from print alone.
- **A vernacular proverb quoted, glossed and then refuted by a miracle** (p. 182, folio 180 as
  printed) — the book's method for a sectarian objection: it does not argue, it stages.
- **An unpublished manuscript treated as the authority on a whole class of Kashmiri holy men.** The
  editor's account of *Nur Nama* / *Rishi Nama* lists its contents saint by saint — Baba Nur al-Din
  Wali Rishi in detail, then Baba Bam al-Din, Baba Zain al-Din, Lutf al-Din, Nasir al-Din, Rajab
  al-Din, Shukr al-Din, Latif al-Din with khalifas, Baba Nauroz Rishi, and Shaikh Hamza with his
  khalifas and malfuzat — calls it "گراں بہا", precious, notes that it is still unpublished, and
  lists six manuscript locations on three continents (p. 183, folio 181 as printed, حاشیہ 1). The
  same programme as chunk 016's claim for Makhdoom Jahaniyan's malfuzat: devotional compilations
  treated as the primary record.
- **A shrine's history written from a non-Muslim author's tazkira.** Hassu Teli's fullest biography in
  this chunk — birthplace, parents' names, exact death date, age, and five separate dealings with
  Mughal power — comes entirely from **Surat Singh's *Tazkirat al-Shaikh wal-Khadam***, an Aligarh
  manuscript, mediated through **Professor Muhammad Aslam's article in *al-Ma'arif*, Lahore,
  October 1973** (p. 180, folio 178 as printed, حاشیہ 1). The most recent citation in these chunks —
  one year before this edition — and the editor takes a saint's life from it wholesale.
- **A shrine's post-partition condition recorded as a historical fact** (p. 182, folio 180 as
  printed): the Amritsar mazar of Fath Shah Bukhari, "سب سے زیادہ مرجع خلائق" until partition, now
  "شکستہ صورت میں موجود". The editor's only sentence about 1947 in these chunks, and it is about loss.

## Citable passages

- "بعد فتح قلعہ کے اکبر نے حضرت کو ایک لاکھ روپے کی جاگیر پنجاب میں دی اور شہر ٹبالہ ان کی جاگیر میں
  قرار پایا۔" — "After the conquest of the fort, Akbar gave the Hazrat a jagir of one lakh rupees in

- "جس فرمان کی رو سے یہ جاگیر ملی تھی، وہ آج بھی اس خاندان کے پاس محفوظ ہے۔ جس پر اکبر کی مہر ثبت
  ہے۔" — "The farman under which this jagir was received is preserved with this family even today,
  and Akbar's seal is impressed on it." (p. 181, folio 179 as printed)
- "شاہ دولہ کے سال وفات میں اختلاف ہے۔ ہم نے معاصر ماخذ کرامت نامہ کو اس سلسلہ میں دیگر روایات پر
  ترجیح دی ہے۔" — the editor's method: "There is disagreement about Shah Daula's year of death; we

## Doubts

- **The folio offset changes mid-book and the stated relation fails here.** All nine markers in this
  chunk give **folio = PDF − 2**; chunk 016's first eight gave **− 12**. The break is at p. 176. Until
  a human reads the printed page numbers at pp. 174–178 from the PDF, **no folio in these two chunks
  should be published**, and **chunk 016's folio paragraph, which read the p. 176–177 markers as a
  two-page digit slip, is wrong and should be corrected to record the systematic change.** Either the
  transcriber's markers are right and the book's pagination jumps ten, or the markers are wrong from
  p. 176 on; this chunk cannot decide it.
- **Entry no. 124's text is dislocated across two pages and must be re-read from the PDF.** As
  transcribed, p. 186 carries the heading and the words "پنجاب کے مشہور بزرگوں میں سے یہ بزرگ صاحب
  مقامات بلند و مدارج", and then, immediately, "ان کو اولاد حاصل ہو۔ ارشاد ہوتا کہ…". p. 184 begins
  "ارجمند و خوارق و کرامت…" and ends "…استدعائے دعا کرتے کہ خدا کی جناب سے". **The joins are exact at
  both ends** — "مدارج" + "ارجمند" and "خدا کی جناب سے" + "ان کو اولاد حاصل ہو" — so the reading order
  is **p. 186 (first line) → p. 184 → p. 186 (remainder)**, and p. 184's un-headed text is the middle
  of entry no. 124. It also means entry no. 124's body stands *before* entry no. 123 in the page
  sequence. Every page reference to Shah Daula in these notes is therefore split between pp. 184 and
  186 as transcribed, and a human must establish the true pagination before any of it is cited.
- **shah-jamal: the archive's death year and the book's are thirty-one years apart, and the nisba
  conflicts.** Book: **14 Rabi al-Thani 1050 AH** (≈ 1640 CE), a **Sayyid Husaini** in a chain from
  **Baha al-Din Zakariya Multani**, descendants at **Sialkot** (p. 185, folio 183 as printed).
  Archive row: died **1671**, figure "Syed Shah Jamal Uddin Naqvi Bukhari", silsila Suhrawardi. The
  silsila and the site (Ichhra, Lahore, the damdama) agree and the mapping is confident, but **the
  date and the lineage do not, and the book offers no alternative reading of either**. Record both.
  Note that the book gives no *urs* date and no custodian for this shrine.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal must not be mapped from this chunk, although the case is tempting.**
  The chunk names **Shah Kamal as Shah Jamal's second brother**, devout and a man of jazb, with
  marvels famous among the people (p. 185, folio 183 as printed) — and the archive's Baba Shah Kamal
  is at **Muslim Town / Samanabad near Ichhra, Lahore**, the same quarter as Shah Jamal's damdama.
  Against the match: the chunk gives **no grave, no date, no location and no silsila** for Shah Kamal;
  his family here is **Suhrawardi** from Baha al-Din Zakariya, while the archive row reads **Qadiri**;
  and he is a **Sayyid Husaini** here where the row is silent. Chunk 012 already flagged a third Shah
  Kamal (of Kaithal) in the Mujaddid's Qadiri chain. **Three Shah Kamals now stand in these notes.
  Leave unmapped and resolve on external evidence.**
- **Hassu Teli's death year differs between text and editor, and his relation to Shah Jamal strains
  the chronology.** Text: died **1012 AH**, murid of **Shah Jamal Lahori** (pp. 180–181). Editor:
  died **3 Shawwal 1011 AH**, aged **120** (p. 180, حاشیہ 1) — so born about 891 AH. But Shah Jamal
  died in **1050 AH** (p. 185), thirty-eight years *after* his murid. Not impossible, but the book
  neither notices nor explains it, and **it is possible that entry no. 119's "شاہ جمال لاہوری" is not
  entry no. 123's Shah Jamal**. Do not cite Hassu Teli as evidence about the Ichhra shrine without
  this caveat. The editor does not mark the 1012/1011 difference as a correction.
- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari: what is undated in the jagir story.** The chunk gives no year for
  the Chittorgarh summons, none for the grant, none for the start of the langar, and no indication
  whether the langar was still running when the book was written — the present-tense verbs belong to
  the descendants' residence, not to the langar. The farman is "آج بھی … محفوظ", preserved **even
  today**, but "today" is **Sayyid Muhammad Latif's**, not the 1974 editor's, and no page of Latif's
  *Lahore* is cited. **The seal is therefore a nineteenth-century report of a document, relayed in
  1974.** Do not write that the farman survives.
- **Tibala's founding date sits oddly with its founder's death.** The editor dates the city's
  settlement to **910 AH [OCR?]** by Abd al-Jalil Chuhar Bandagi's order (p. 181, حاشیہ 2), and
  chunk 016 gives Abd al-Jalil's death as **1 Rajab 910 AH** (p. 173). Possible — an order in the
  year of death — but the year is OCR-flagged and the rival *Waqai-e Sialkot* date is **[illegible]**.
  **Both the date and its alternative are unusable as transcribed.**
- **bibi-pak-daman: the book does not say the popular name is wrong.** It reports that the occupant
  is Jalal al-Din Haidar, d. 1016 AH, *and* that people call the grave the ustad's. An entry must not
  turn that juxtaposition into a correction the book did not make, and must not date Bibi Pak Daman
  itself from the 1016 AH burial — the burial dates only the subsidiary rauza. "بدیوار حریم" is
  rendered here as "at the wall of the enclosure"; whether it means built into the wall or against it
  is not determinable from the chunk.
- **Two men named Jan Muhammad Lahori stand three pages apart, of different orders.** The editor
  rejects a Chishti Sabiri chain for Ghore Shah running "بخدمت محسن شاہ و وہی بخدمت **جان محمد چشتی
  لاہوری**" (p. 179), and entry no. 125 is **شیخ جان محمد سہروردی لاہوری** (p. 186), whom the editor
  calls Maulana Jan Muhammad Lahori. **The chunk gives no basis for identifying or separating them.**
- **Mauj Darya's sons are incompletely and unclearly recorded.** The text names three; the editor adds
  a fourth from an **anonymous manuscript** via Nami (p. 182, حاشیہ 1). The third son's name carries
  two OCR flags — "شاہ شہاب الدین المعنی [OCR?] طلب بہ مہر بی بی فورنگ [OCR?]" — and the phrase
  naming his mother is not recoverable. "بی بی کلاں بنت سید عبدالقادر ثالث" is legible; the second
  wife's name is not.
- **The pedigree of Mauj Darya contains an OCR artefact inside a name**: "سید نہ [OCR?] ناصر الدین"
  (p. 181) — most likely the "نر ناصر الدین" epithet of chunk 016's entry no. 109, but **the chunk
  does not say so** and the syllable is flagged.
- **Shah Daula's death year now has three values and one flag.** Text 1075 AH with the chronogram
  "خدا دوست"; editor 1087 AH [OCR?] / 1676 CE [OCR?]; archive row 1675. **Both of the editor's
  figures are OCR-flagged**, and his authority, *Karamat Nama* folio 5, is flagged too. The archive's
  agreement with the editor is therefore agreement with a flagged reading, and a human must confirm
  1087 AH from the printed footnote.
- **Entry no. 125 breaks off at the word "وفات"**, so Shaikh Jan Muhammad Suhrawardi Lahori has **no
  death year and no grave** in this chunk. Continues in chunk 018.
