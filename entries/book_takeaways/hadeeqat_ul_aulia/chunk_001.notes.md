## Practices, institutions, economy

- The book's seventh section and its appendices are effectively a shrine survey of Lahore:
  "زیارات عالیات موجودہ شہر لاہور" (the exalted shrines presently in the city of Lahore) at printed
  p. 244; "تفصیل زیارات موجودہ قلعہ لاہور" (detail of the shrines presently in the Fort of Lahore)
  at printed p. 246; and "تفصیل زیارات حصہ دوم جو فقیر صاحبوں کے خاندان میں موجود ہیں" — shrines
  held within the families of the *faqir sahibs* — at printed p. 248 (p. 20, folio 8). The last is a
  direct statement of family custodianship as an organising category.
- The editor's Appendix 1 is "ضمیمہ اول مزارات لاہور کا موجودہ حدود اربعہ", the present-day
  boundaries (*hudud-e arba*) of the mazars of Lahore, at printed p. 275 (p. 21, folio 9) — a modern
  topographical locating of Lahore's shrines, and the single most directly usable section for the
  archive's Lahore rows.
- Also in the apparatus: a list of the illustrations included in Hadiqat al-Auliya at printed p. 272;
  sources of the introduction and footnotes at printed p. 280; a list of Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi's
  books and articles at printed p. 295; Appendix 2 at printed p. 297; and an index in two parts,
  *rijal* (persons) at printed p. 299 and *amakin* (places) at printed p. 321 (p. 21, folio 9).
- Chapter structure, all six *silsila* chapters called a چمن ("garden"): first chaman, shaykhs of the
  Qadiriyya, from printed p. 27; second, Chishtiyya, p. 73; third, Naqshbandiyya, p. 115; fourth,

  (the mad and the ecstatics), p. 224; seventh, "عورات صالحات" (righteous women), p. 235

- Imprint and economics of this edition: publisher Abu Najib Haji Muhammad Arshad Qureshi, founder of

## Arguments and interpretations

- The editor opens by arguing that most historians of Pakistan and India were influenced by the
  Iranian theory of history, so histories of the period record only the doings of kings and military
  campaigns and give "no glimpse at all" of the life of the common people and their problems
  (p. 22, folio 10).
- Tazkiras, *maktubat* and *malfuzat* of the shaykhs make up this painful deficiency to a degree:
  they illuminate not only the sufis' lives and thought but the mental atmosphere, economic
  conditions, literary movements and social tendencies of the period, preserving the people's inner
  feelings, hidden longings, defeats and victories, disappointments and anxieties (p. 22, folio 10).
- People brought the smallest and the largest problems of daily life to these shaykhs, and the
  *malfuzat* record such incidents everywhere (p. 22, folio 10).
- The religious inclinations of the sultans can be gauged from this literature alone (p. 23,
  folio 11); and the sufis' part in the growth of the various languages is "in no way less" than the
  poets', because they insisted on knowing the popular languages, without which their movement could
  not have reached the people (p. 23, folio 11).
- Claim, footnoted: no social history of a country, especially Pakistan and India, can be complete
  until sufi tazkiras and *malfuzat* are fully used (p. 23, folio 11). The footnote attributes the
  whole passage: "خلیق احمد نظامی، 'ملفوظات کی تاریخی اہمیت' مشمولہ نذر عرشی ص ۳۵م [OCR?] — ۴۴۷" and
  adds that all this information is taken from that article in summary with slight alteration
  (p. 23, folio 11, حاشیہ 1).
- On Punjab specifically: it is "سب سے محروم القسمت خطہ", the most ill-fated region, because no
  regular researched history of it has yet been written; those who have written on Punjab's history
  have disposed of it under a few formal chapters, and sufi tazkiras have not merely been denied the
  status of primary sources but "have not even been touched" (p. 23, folio 11).
- Editorial history of the edition, in the editor's own account: in early 1974, as a student in the
  History Department of Punjab University Lahore, he decided while writing his MA thesis that Mufti
  Ghulam Sarwar's short book should be re-edited with footnotes; he had known the Mufti's work since
  buying his Khazinat al-Asfiya as a seventh-class pupil and compiling an index of its contents
  [OCR?]; he completed the edition during the MA and it was first printed in 1976 after he became a
  lecturer, selling out quickly; asked for a second edition, he would not reissue it without
  revision, and after 23 years absorbed in other research could add nothing but a single appendix
  (p. 13). Signed Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi, 6 November 1999, Lahore (p. 14, folio 2).
- The text's transmission is stated plainly: this is a second impression, with new footnotes and
  annotations added, of the Lahore 1976 annotated edition, which was itself made from the Lucknow
  1906 printing of Mufti Ghulam Sarwar Lahori's Hadiqat al-Auliya (p. 14, folio 2).
- The editor's other credentials, given in the same preface: research travels to Egypt, Iran,
  Afghanistan, India, England and Germany; editions of Maqamat-e Mazhari, Ma'mulat-e Mazhariya,
  Bisharat-e Mazhariya and Kamalat-e Mazhariya from the silsila of Hazrat Mirza Mazhar Jan Janan
  Shaheed (p. 13); Hasanat al-Haramain, the *malfuzat* of Khwaja Muhammad Masum Sirhindi (d. 1079 AH),
  and a manuscript Maqamat-e Masumi, for which he wrote one volume of annotations and a separate
  introductory volume that "became a picture of the social and ideological history of
  seventeenth-century Pakistan and India"; work for the Oxford University project "Socio-Cultural and
  Intellectual Atlas of the Muslims of South Asia"; and about a thousand articles for Pakistani and
  world journals and for the Danishnama-e Shibh-e Qarra, Tehran (p. 14, folio 2).

## Doubts

- **shergarh**: the book's "شیخ داؤد جہنی وال شیر گڈھی" carries the Shergarh attribution and stands
  in the Qadiri chapter (p. 15, folio 3), but this chunk nowhere gives the name "Bandagi Kirmani"
  that the archive row uses. Confirm from the entry itself at printed p. 36.
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri**: mapped on "شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری قادری" (p. 15, folio 3) — Lahore plus
  Qadiri — but without the "Bandagi" element. Note also the separate "شیخ عبدالجلیل چوٹھر بندگی
  لاہوری", entry no. 110, printed p. 160 (p. 18, folio 6), which does carry "Bandagi".
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri**: two Abul Muali entries exist — no. 19 "شاہ ابوالمعالی لاہوری" in the
  Qadiri chapter (printed p. 45) and no. 62 "شاہ ابوالمعالی چشتی" in the Chishti chapter (printed
  p. 97). The archive row is Qadri and in Lahore, which is why no. 19 is mapped, but a reader must
  check both entries before citing.
- **madho-lal-hussain**: the contents give "شیخ حسین لاہوری" (no. 17, printed p. 42) and "شیخ مادھو
  لاہوری" (no. 24, printed p. 52) as two independent entries and never join them as "Madho Lal
  Hussain". Whether either or both belong to the archive's row cannot be settled from a contents list.
- **darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz**: "شیخ ایاز لاہوری" (no. 130, printed p. 180) is mapped on the Ayaz name
  and the Lahore attribution only; the contents give neither "Malik" nor any Ghaznavid connection.
- **shah-jamal**: mapped on "حضرت شاہ جمال سہروردی" (no. 123) because the book places him among the
  Suhrawardi shaykhs, matching the archive's row; but the contents give no place, and a second
  Suhrawardi Jamal — "سید جمال الدین سہروردی", no. 115, printed p. 165 — stands eight entries
  earlier (p. 18, folio 6).
- **Unplaced, probable, not mapped**: "سید عبدالرزاق قادری" (no. 28, printed p. 55) may be the Shah
  Chiragh of *shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh*, but the contents also carry a separate "مزار چراغ شاہ"
  (no. 238, printed p. 263), so the two cannot be told apart here (pp. 15, 21; folios 3, 9).
- **Unplaced, probable, not mapped**: "شاہ سلیمان قادری" (no. 26, printed p. 54) could be Shah
  Sulaiman Nuri of *purana-bhalwal* (Qadiri) or, less likely given the chapter, the Sulaiman of
  *darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a*; and "خواجہ محمد سلیمان چشتی" (no. 78,
  printed p. 111) sits in the Chishti chapter where Taunsvi would belong but is not named Taunsvi
  (pp. 15, 17; folios 3, 5).
- **Unplaced, probable, not mapped**: contents entry no. 191 groups six women in a single line —
  "بی بی حاج۔ بی بی تاج۔ بی بی نور۔ بی بی حُور۔ بی بی گوہر۔ بی بی شہباز", printed p. 235 (p. 20,
  folio 8). Six bibis in one entry invites comparison with *bibi-pak-daman* ("the six Bibis"), but
  the chunk never names Pak Daman and the identification must not be made from the contents alone.
