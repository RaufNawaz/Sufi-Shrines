# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 021 — pp. 214–224

**Entries 147 (tail) to 156: the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries — Ahmad Shah Durrani's
sack of Lahore, the founding of the Uwaisi order near Bahawalpur, a Sikh raid turned back, and the
author's own grandfather.** Three archive rows take material: **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** and
**shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri**, both named in a single sentence that sends Bulleh Shah to his pir,
and **shrine-of-mian-mir**, the head of a five-step Lahore Qadiri chain. The three most substantial
features: **a saint's two Lahore quarters exempted from plunder by Ahmad Shah Durrani's own order,
with royal horsemen posted to guard them**; **Bulleh Shah told by a third shaikh that his portion lies
with Shah Inayat Qadiri**; and **a man's ecstatic unconsciousness broken only by singers**, in the
entry of the founder of the Uwaisiya.

**Folio markers: all eleven run folio = PDF index − 12**, p. 214 → folio 202 through p. 224 →
folio 212. The relation holds, as in chunk 020.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** (سید بلھے شاہ — within entry no. 150, p. 219, folio 207):
  - **He appears as one of three men travelling together for bai'at, and is redirected.** Khwaja Abd
    al-Khaliq went "**باتفاق سید بلھے شاہ اور گلشیر محمد اپنے بھائی کے** بارادہ بیعت بخدمت **شیخ عبد
    الحکیم قادری** کے بمقام **تلمنہ [OCR?]**" — together with **Sayyid Bulleh Shah** and his own
    brother **Gul Sher Muhammad**, intending bai'at, to Shaikh **Abd al-Hakim Qadiri** at the place
    **Talamba[OCR?]**. After muraqaba the shaikh took Gul Sher Muhammad as his murid, "اور **سید بلھے
    شاہ کو کہا کہ تیرے نصیب کا بجزہ [OCR?] شاہ عنایت قادری کے پاس ہے تو بمقام قصور جائے گا تو حصہ
    پائے گا**" — and **told Sayyid Bulleh Shah that the portion of his lot lies with Shah Inayat
    Qadiri, and that if he goes to Qasur he will receive his share** (p. 219).
  - **What the row gains**: the book names him "**سید** بلھے شاہ", a sayyid; puts him in company with
    the founder of the Uwaisiya and with Gul Sher Muhammad; makes his affiliation to Shah Inayat the
    result of **another shaikh's refusal and referral**; and attaches **Qasur** to the transaction —
    the town the archive gives as his own shrine's location. **No dates, no shrine, no urs and no
    poetry are mentioned**; he is a figure in someone else's entry.
  - **"قصور" here is where he is sent, and the chunk does not say it is where Shah Inayat was.** See
    Doubts.
- **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri** (شاہ عنایت قادری — within entry no. 150, p. 219, folio 207):
  - **Named once, as the holder of Bulleh Shah's portion**: "تیرے نصیب کا بجزہ [OCR?] **شاہ عنایت
    قادری** کے پاس ہے" (p. 219). The name, the order (**Qadiri**) and the role — Bulleh Shah's
    destined pir — all match the archive row.
  - **What the row gains**: a second, independent witness inside this book to the Bulleh Shah
    connection, and the detail that the referral came from **Shaikh Abd al-Hakim Qadiri of
    Talamba[OCR?]**, a figure the archive does not carry. **Nothing about his Lahore shrine, his dates
    or his Shattari affiliation** — and note that chunk 020's Shattari chain (Shah Gada's) is the only
    thing in these chunks that speaks to the row's "Qadri Shattari" label, and it does not name him.
  - **The chunk's geography is ambiguous for this row: see Doubts.**
- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (حضرت میاں میر بالا پیر / حضرت میاں میر لاہوری — within entry no. 152,
  pp. 221–222, folios 209–210):
  - **He heads a five-step Qadiri chain running down to 1213 AH.** "سلسلہ قادریہ میں ان کا شجرہ
    **حضرت میاں میر بالا پیر** کے ساتھ اس طرح پر ملتا ہے کہ سید بھاون شاہ مرید **شاہ بلاق [OCR?]** اور
    وہ مرید **شاہ عبد الرشید لاہوری** اور وہ مرید **شیخ محسن شاہ** اور وہ مرید **شیخ محمد المشہور ملّا
    شاہ** اور وہ مرید **حضرت میاں میر لاہوری** کے" — in the Qadiri silsila Sayyid Bhawan Shah's chain
    meets Hazrat Mian Mir Bala Pir thus: Bhawan Shah ← Shah Bilaq[OCR?] ← Shah Abd al-Rashid Lahori ←
    Shaikh Muhsin Shah ← Shaikh Muhammad, famous as **Mulla Shah** ← **Hazrat Mian Mir Lahori**
    (p. 222).
  - **Two things the row does not have**: the epithet **"بالا پیر"**, and a named line of successors
    running through **Mulla Shah** to a saint who died in **1213 AH** and whose murids were a whole
    tribe. **For the archive this establishes that the book treats Mian Mir as the root of a
    still-living Lahore Qadiri lineage**, not merely as a seventeenth-century grave.
  - **Nothing here about his shrine, dates, urs or Alam Ganj location.** "میاں میر لاہوری" is a nisba
    of city, not of quarter.
  - Note that **Shaikh Muhsin Shah** in this chain shares a name with the "محسن شاہ" of the Chishti
    Sabiri chain the editor rejected for Ghore Shah in chunk 017 (p. 179). See Doubts.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Two Lahore quarters exempted from a sack by royal order, with a guard detail posted.** When
  **Ahmad Badshah Durrani** came "پہلی مرتبہ لاہور کی تسخیر کو" and the subah of Lahore was beaten,
  "لاہور کے لوگ بخوف غارت بھاگ گئے"; the residents of **"محلہ لکھی و عبد اللہ واڑ ہی"**, where Haji
  Muhammad Saeed lived, came to him saying all Lahore had fled and "ہم اب تک اپنے گھر دل [OCR?] میں
  **حضرت کی حمایت کے بھروسے پر** بیٹھے ہیں", we are still sitting in our homes **trusting in the
  Hazrat's protection**. He answered: **"ہاں ہم نے خدا سے مانگا ہے کہ ہماری سکونت کا محلہ غارت سے بچ
  جائے۔ تم کھلے دروازے اپنے گھروں میں بیٹھے رہو"** — yes, we have asked God that our quarter of
  residence be spared the plunder; **sit in your houses with your doors open**. "آخر جب شہر فتح ہوا
  تو فوج نے **کل شہر لوٹ لیا۔ سوائے محلہ لکھی و عبد اللہ واڑ ہی کے**" — when the city fell the army
  plundered the whole of it **except those two quarters**. And the mechanism: when the king alighted
  at **Shahdara** he asked "کہ اس شہر میں **بزرگ صاحب طریقت کون ہے**", who in this city is an elder
  of the tariqat; people named him; the king came at once "باخلاص دل", with a sincere heart, and after
  **ziyarat** "حکم دیا کہ **یہ دونوں محلے غارت نہ ہوں** اور **چند سوار بادشاہی حفاظت کے لیے مقرر کر
  دیے**" — ordered that these two quarters not be plundered and **appointed some royal horsemen for
  their protection** (p. 214, folio 202). **Four things for the archive: a dated event (the editor
  gives 1161 AH / 1748 CE), two named quarters, a formal exemption and a military guard — the most
  concrete instance in these chunks of a shrine's standing translated into state protection.** Read
  with the editor's note that **Ahmad Shah had corresponded with him before the invasion** (see
  Arguments), the sequence is not a chance encounter.
- **A named Lahore madrasa that hosted Shah Wali Allah, and an ijazat given there.** **"حضرت شاہ ولی
  اللہ محدث دہلوی نے حج پر جاتے ہوئے لاہور میں آپ کے مدرسہ میں قیام فرمایا تھا اور جواہر خمسہ کے
  اعمال کی اجازت حاجی صاحب سے حاصل کی تھی۔ جس کا شاہ صاحب نے خود ذکر کیا ہے (انتباہ)"** — Shah Wali
  Allah, on his way to the Hajj, **stayed in Haji Muhammad Saeed's madrasa in Lahore** and **obtained
  from him the ijazat for the practices of the *Jawahir-e Khamsa***, which Shah Wali Allah **himself
  mentions** in *al-Intibah* (p. 215, folio 203, حاشیہ 2), and the editor dates the visit to
  **1143 AH** on the outward Hajj journey (p. 217, folio 205, حاشیہ 3). **A Lahore shrine-madrasa
  documented from the visitor's own writing** — the strongest cross-attested institutional fact in
  these chunks.
- **Two offices divided between two grandsons at a named khanqah.** **"شیخ عبد الرحیم اور مولوی عبد
  الرحمان حضرت حاجی محمد سعید لاہوری کے دو نواسے تھے جو آپ کے جانشین تھے۔ شیخ عبد الرحیم کے ذمہ
  تلقین و ارشاد اور مولوی عبد الرحمان خانقاہ حاجی سعید میں درس و تدریس کے فرائض انجام دیتے تھے"** —
  Shaikh Abd al-Rahim and Maulvi Abd al-Rahman were Haji Muhammad Saeed's **two grandsons and his
  janishins**: **Abd al-Rahim's charge was talqin and irshad**, while **Abd al-Rahman discharged the
  duties of teaching in the khanqah of Haji Saeed** (p. 216, folio 204, حاشیہ 1). **A formal split of
  a shrine's spiritual and educational functions between two heirs, each named** — the clearest
  statement of shrine administration in these chunks after Mian Wada's composite office (chunk 018).
  The editor also shows that **Abd al-Rahim prayed at his grandfather's mazar for his own inward
  attention to be effective**, which is his proof that the succession happened after Haji Sahib's
  death: see Arguments.
- **A saint's own fort outside Lahore.** Mir Muhammad Yaqub "باہر لاہور کے متصل محلہ پیر عزیز مزنگ
  **اپنا قلعہ علیحدہ بنوایا ہوا تھا**" (p. 216, folio 204) — a separate fortification built by a
  shaikh as his residence at Mazang. **The only fortified shrine residence in these chunks**, and it
  sits in the same suburb where Pir Bhawan Shah first settled two entries later.
- **Healing of snake and rabid-dog bites as a shrine's routine service.** "ادنیٰ فیض اس کا یہ تھا کہ
  جس شخص کو سانپ یا سگ دیوانہ وغیرہ **زہر ملیا** جانور کاٹتا۔ **آپ کے دہن کے تھلے [OCR?] سے** اچھا ہو
  جاتا" — his **least** faiz was that anyone bitten by a snake, a **mad dog** or other venomous
  creature was cured by [the flagged phrase, apparently something from his mouth] (p. 216,
  folio 204). **A specific, repeatable therapeutic function, described as the lowest of his
  powers** — and the means is OCR-flagged: see Doubts.
- **A tribe converted wholesale into a shrine's following.** "قوم کھکہ [OCR?] تمام و کمال حضرت کے
  مرید ہوئے" (p. 222, folio 210) — the Khakha[OCR?] tribe entire as Pir Bhawan Shah's murids. The only
  statement in these chunks of a whole named community as a single shrine's constituency.
- **A dated burial in the Miani graveyard, Lahore.** Maulvi Ghulam Farid Lahori, d. **1216 AH**,
  "مزار پر انوار **گورستان میانی** میں ہے" (p. 222, folio 210). **For the archive this dates the
  burial ground's use to at least 1216 AH / c. 1801 CE** — useful context for the four archive rows
  that stand in Miani Sahib graveyard, **though it says nothing about any of them: see Doubts.**
- **Twelve years in a jungle, then a settled qasba** — Pir Bhawan Shah's three residences, ending at
  the place of his grave (p. 222, folio 210). A shrine sited by a deliberate final migration "دامن
  کوہ", to the foot of the mountain.
- **A saint's family living in deliberate poverty beside a trading brother** — the author's
  grandfather refusing to join Hafiz Muhammadi's business (p. 222, folio 210). **The book's own
  family's economy, stated by the author about his own grandfather**, and set against the langars,
  jagirs and grants of the earlier entries.
- **A hereditary order in a scholarly family**: "طریق ان کا **موروثی سہروردیہ** تھا" (p. 223,
  folio 211) — the Suhrawardi path transmitted by descent and taught to students, in the family that
  wrote this book.
- **An order founded in the Punjab in the twelfth Hijri century, with two shrines near Bahawalpur.**
  The **Uwaisiya**: founded by Abd al-Khaliq (d. 1185 AH, Mubarakpur near Bahawalpur), continued by
  his nephew Muhkam al-Din (d. 1197 or 1188 AH, Kot Lajnat[OCR?] near Bahawalpur), with **nine named
  khalifas** and a printed Persian manual (*Talqin-e Ludanni*, Agra), a Saraiki account (*Chahar
  Bagh*), and a 1386 AH Bahawalpur biography of both founders (pp. 219–221). **The archive carries no
  Uwaisi row; this is a documented eighteenth-century Punjabi order with two locatable shrines.**
- **Manuscripts and collections named**: the **Khuda Bakhsh Library, Patna** (Shah Wali Allah's
  ijazat-nama); the **Ganj Bakhsh Library, Rawalpindi**, **Maulana Ghulam Muhyi al-Din Qasuri
  collection** (*Lata'if-e Nafisiya*, twice); *Qiran al-Sa'dain*, *Ahwal-e Mashaikh-e Kibar* and
  *Jami al-Fawaid* with **Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi**; *Khulasat al-Nawafil* and the Persian qasidas
  with **Chaudhry Abd al-Wahid, Lahore**; the *Maktubat-e Saifiya* compiled by **Maulana Muhammad
  Azam, Karachi**; *Qawl al-Jali wa Asrar al-Khafi* "مخزونہ **خانقاہ کاکوری**" (pp. 214–221).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A girl brought back from Kabul with her shopping still in her hands.** After the plunder of the
  Punjab, when the king had gone to Kabul, a Lahore man told Haji Muhammad Saeed that the Afghans had
  looted his house and carried off a daughter dear to him — "اب مجھ کو گھر کے لُٹ جانے کا تو غم کوئی
  نہیں، مگر **لڑکی کی جدائی نہایت شاق** ہے". The Hazrat said close your eyes; a moment later, open
  them; and **the girl stood before him, "ایک ہاتھ میں اس کے چار فلوس تھے اور ایک ہاتھ میں تیل کا
  برتن تھا"**, with **four fulus in one hand and an oil vessel in the other**. Asked, she said: taken
  to Kabul, the man who took her **sold her to another, who made her his slave-girl**; at that moment
  her master had given her **four paisas and an oil vessel** and sent her to the bazaar for oil; in
  the bazaar "یہ حضرت جو موجود ہیں۔ مجھ کو مل گئے" — this Hazrat who is present met her, told her to
  shut her eyes, and when she opened them she found herself here (pp. 214–215, folios 202–203). **The
  detail that authenticates the miracle is the domestic errand — and it documents the sale of Punjabi
  captives into slavery at Kabul after the sack.**
- **Uwais Qarni comes in person, three nights running.** Abd al-Khaliq, alone in his hujra reciting
  darud, saw "ایک شخص **ماہ طلعت بزرگ صورت**" appear and greet him; at the sight of his face he
  fainted, and stayed beside himself "تمام رات اور تمام روز", coming to at sunset; the second night
  the same, "بدستور **آٹھ پہر** بے ہوش رہے"; the third night he seized the visitor's feet and asked
  his name: **"ہمارا نام اویس قرنی عاشق رسول ہے۔ ہم تیری تکمیل و تربیت کے لیے خدا کے حکم سے آئے
  ہیں"** — our name is Uwais Qarni, lover of the Prophet; we have come by God's command for your
  completion and training. He sat, **took his bai'at** and turned his attention on him, whereupon he
  was unconscious three nights and three days (p. 219, folio 207). **A whole silsila founded on a
  bai'at given by a long-dead saint in bodily form.**
- **Music as the only thing that brings him back — twice stated.** "چوتھے روز ایک اتفاقاً **گانے والا
  شخص** اس راہ سے گذرا۔ جب **سرود** کی آواز حضرت کے کان میں پڑی۔ بدن کو جنبش ہوئی۔ **متعلقین نے اس
  کو بلا لیا**۔ **راگ** کے سننے سے حضرت وجد میں آئے۔ بعد وجد ہوش میں آ گئے اور اپنے آپ کو **کامل و
  مکمل** پایا" — on the fourth day a **singer** chanced to pass; when the sound of *sarud* reached his
  ear his body stirred; **the attendants called the man in**; on hearing the *rag* he came into
  ecstasy, and after the ecstasy came to himself and **found himself perfect and complete** (p. 219).
  And from *Lata'if-e Nafisiya*: he would faint at "Allahu Akbar" and "**جب تک گانے والے نہ گاتے ہوش
  میں نہ آتے**", would not revive until the singers sang (p. 220, folio 208). **For an archive
  tracking sama' this is unusually explicit: professional singers kept or summoned as a functional
  necessity, and the completion of a saint's training accomplished by a rag.**
- **A child flies away in a sama' assembly and is never seen again.** Khwaja Qutb al-Din, "خرد سالی
  کی عمر میں ایک روز **سماع** میں بیٹھا تھا۔ جب حالت وجد طاری ہوئی تو **اللہ اکبر کہہ کر آسمان کو
  اڑ گیا** اور ایک لمحہ میں ایسا بلند گیا کہ نظر سے غائب ہو گیا۔ **پھر ان کا نشان روئے زمین پر ظاہر
  نہ ہوا**" — sitting one day in a sama' as a small child, when ecstasy came upon him he said "Allahu
  Akbar" and **flew up to the sky**, rising so high in a moment that he vanished from sight, **and his
  trace never again appeared on the face of the earth** (p. 220, folio 208). **A saint with no grave,
  explicitly — the only one in these chunks.**
- **A purse dug from the ground, lost to greed, and dug up again elsewhere.** A beggar asked Muhkam
  al-Din for five hundred rupees to marry his unmarried daughters; "چونکہ حضرت کا وقت اس وقت خوش
  تھا"، he **dug the ground and gave him a purse of five hundred rupees** and walked on. Greed seized
  the man; he set the purse aside and dug on, "وہاں سے ایک اور **حبہ** نہ نکلا", not another grain came
  out; repenting, he turned for the purse and **found it gone too**; he ran weeping after the shaikh,
  who **laughed** and said **"وہاں کوئی خزانہ مدفون نہ تھا۔ یہ تیرے طمع کی تاثیر تھی کہ وہ تھیلی بھی
  جاتی رہی"** — there was no treasure buried there; it was the effect of your greed that the purse
  too was lost — and then dug the ground where he was standing and produced the purse again
  (pp. 220–221, folios 208–209).
- **A raiding army converted at a village boundary.** "ایک مرتبہ **صاحب سنگھ بیدی** ان کے گاؤں کے
  لوٹنے کے ارادہ پر آیا لوگ بھاگنے لگے حضرت نے سب کو منع کیا کہ **کوئی گاؤں سے نہ نکلے**۔ آخر **جس
  قدر لشکر اس کا گاؤں میں داخل ہوا۔ سب کے سب مسلمان ہو گئے**۔ باقی ماندہ مارے خوف کے گاؤں میں نہ
  آئے" — once **Sahib Singh Bedi** came intending to plunder his village; people began to flee and the
  Hazrat forbade anyone to leave; in the end **as much of his army as entered the village, every one
  of them became Muslim**, and the rest, out of fear, did not come in (p. 222, folio 210). **The same
  structure as Haji Muhammad Saeed's protected quarters — a saint forbids flight and the village is
  spared — with conversion in place of a royal order.**
- **A village burnt by a fistful of grass, cursed to stay uninhabited.** Thieves took two of Nur
  Husain's cows; when his attendants were abused, "حضرت غضب میں آئے اور **ایک مٹھی گھاس خشک کی ہاتھ
  میں لے کر اس میں پھونکا تو اس کو آگ لگ اٹھی**", he took a fistful of dry grass and blew on it and
  it caught fire, threw it toward their village and said **"ہم نے چوروں کے گاؤں کو جلا دیا ایسا کہ
  پھر قیامت تک آباد نہ ہو"** — we have burnt the thieves' village so that it shall never be inhabited
  again until the Day of Judgement; "اسی وقت … تمام و کمال جل گیا" (p. 223, folio 211). **The
  mirror-image of the two preceding miracles: the same power, used to destroy a village rather than
  save one.**

## Arguments and interpretations

- **A death year moved by a contemporary source, in one line.** Of Haji Muhammad Saeed, whose death
  the text puts at 1166 AH: **"معاصر ماخذ قران السعدین میں آپ کا سال وفات ۱۱۶۲ھ درج ہے"** — in the
  **contemporary source** *Qiran al-Sa'dain* his death year is entered as **1162 AH** (p. 215,
  folio 203, حاشیہ 1). The same criterion — "معاصر ماخذ" — as Shah Daula (chunk 017) and Shaikh
  Musa (chunk 016). Note that *Qiran al-Sa'dain* is the malfuzat of this very saint, compiled by his
  own disciple, and is the editor's manuscript.
- **A death year shown to belong to a different man, from the author's own other book.** **"یہاں مفتی
  صاحب سے سہو ہوا ہے۔ ۱۱۶۰ھ میر یعقوب گیلانی کا سال وفات نہیں ہے۔ بلکہ یہ سال وفات قدوۃ [OCR?] فضل
  علی یکدل [OCR?] لاہوری کا ہے۔ جیسا کہ خود مفتی صاحب (خزینہ ۲/۳۷۴ [OCR?]) میں لکھ چکے ہیں۔ میر
  یعقوب کا سال وفات مفتی صاحب نے خزینہ (۲/۳۷۴) ۹ صفر ۱۱۷۹ھ [OCR?] درج کیا ہے"** — here the Mufti has
  slipped: **1160 AH is not Mir Yaqub Gilani's death year but Fazl Ali Yakdil[OCR?] Lahori's** — that
  is, **his own pir's** — as the Mufti himself wrote in *Khazinat* 2/374, where he gives Mir Yaqub's
  death as **9 Safar 1179 AH** (p. 216, folio 204, حاشیہ 2). **A date transposed from pir to murid,
  caught by collating the author against himself** — and it means **both** years in the entry are
  wrong for the man they are attached to.
- **A succession restored, against the author, from a contemporary tazkira.** The Mufti wrote
  (*Khazina* 1/676[OCR?]) that both of Haji Muhammad Saeed's grandsons **predeceased** him, **"جو
  درست نہیں ہے"**, which is not correct; "بلکہ معاصر تذکرہ قران السعدین میں واضح طور پر تحریر ہے کہ
  صاحبزادہ عبد الرحیم نے **اپنی باطنی توجہ مؤثر ہونے کی دعا حضرت حاجی صاحب کے مزار پر جا کر کی
  تھی**۔ جس سے ثابت ہوتا ہے کہ صاحبزادہ حاجی صاحب کے وصال کے بعد جانشین ہوئے اور **دیر تک بقید حیات
  بھی رہے**" — rather the contemporary tazkira states plainly that Abd al-Rahim **went to Haji
  Sahib's mazar and prayed there** for his own inward attention to be effective, **which proves** that
  he became janishin after Haji Sahib's passing and remained alive a long time (p. 216, folio 204,
  حاشیہ 1). **The evidence is an act of shrine practice used as chronological proof** — a disciple
  praying at a grave can only have done so after the burial. **The archive should note the method:
  ritual behaviour dated as documentary evidence.**
- **A work's genre corrected in five words, and a famous death year with it.** Of *Fath al-Rahman*:
  **"یہ تفسیر نہیں ہے بلکہ فقط ترجمہ ہے"** — this is not a tafsir but **merely a translation**
  (p. 217, folio 205, حاشیہ 1). And: **"شاہ ولی اللہ کا مسلمہ سال وفات ۱۱۷۶ھ / ۱۷۶۲ء [OCR?] ہے۔
  یہاں مفتی صاحب ۱۱۸۰ھ درج کرنے میں غلطی ہوئی ہے"** — Shah Wali Allah's **accepted** death year is
  1176 AH / 1762 CE; the Mufti has erred in entering 1180 AH (حاشیہ 2). **"مسلمہ", accepted, is a new
  formula in these chunks** — not a nearer source, not the editor's own research, but the settled
  consensus.
- **A correspondence that reframes a karamat as diplomacy.** **"احمد شاہ ابدالی کے تسخیر ہند سے
  پیشتر ہی حاجی محمد سعید سے تعلقات تھے۔ چنانچہ اس نے حملے سے پیشتر جن مشائخ ہند کے ساتھ خط و کتابت
  کی تھی۔ ان میں حاجی سعید کا اسم گرامی بھی شامل ہے"** — Ahmad Shah Abdali **had relations with Haji
  Muhammad Saeed even before the conquest of Hind**; among the mashaikh of Hind with whom he
  corresponded **before the attack**, Haji Saeed's name is included (*Aziz al-Din Popalzai, Taimur
  Shah Durrani* 2/68) (p. 214, folio 202, حاشیہ 2), with the invasion dated to **1161 AH / 1748 CE**
  (حاشیہ 1). **The text tells a story of a king who asked who the holy man was and spared his
  quarters; the editor shows they were already in contact. The exemption becomes a political
  arrangement without ceasing to be a karamat, and the archive can cite both.**
- **The author names his own grandfather and his family's order.** Entry no. 154 is the author's
  paternal grandfather, "جدّ بزرگوار بندہ غلام سرور مؤلف کتاب", and states **"طریق ان کا موروثی
  سہروردیہ تھا"** (pp. 222–223, folios 210–211). **With chunk 020's entry no. 145 — his family's
  shaikh Khwaja Ayub — this is the second entry in two chunks devoted to the author's own line, and
  together they establish that Hadiqat al-Awliya is written from inside a hereditary Suhrawardi
  family of Lahore muftis.** An entry citing this book on Suhrawardi shrines should say so.
- **A dating clue in the present tense.** Of Nur Husain's khalifa Shaikh Rasul Shah: **"جو اس سال میں
  فوت ہوئے ہیں"** — who has died **in this year** (p. 223, folio 211). **The author is writing close
  enough to a death to call it "this year", and the entry immediately before it ends in 1236 AH.**
  Read with chunk 018's "827 years since 465 AH" (≈ 1292 AH) and the editor's "سوا نو سو سال"
  (chunk 019), the book's composition is being written across a span the text itself dates three
  different ways. **No single year is stated anywhere in these chunks; all three figures should be
  carried to consolidation together.**
- **A source's author identified, and its manuscript found without one.** Of *Lata'if-e Nafisiya*: it
  "**شیخ احمد بن محمود** کی تصنیف ہے" on the authority of *Khazinat al-Asfiya* 2/346[OCR?]; and "کتب
  خانہ گنج بخش … میں بھی اس نام اور موضوع کا ایک مخطوط موجود ہے۔ **لیکن اس کے دیباچہ میں مؤلف کا نام
  مذکور نہیں ہے**" — a manuscript of this name and subject also exists in the Ganj Bakhsh Library,
  **but its preface does not name the compiler** (p. 219, folio 207, حاشیہ 1). **The editor's habitual
  care: he reports the attribution and then reports that the copy he has seen does not confirm it** —
  the same posture as the *Tazkira-e Mujaddidiya* footnote in chunk 012.
- **A saint's death year contested between a printed tradition and a family's own.** Muhkam al-Din:
  5 Rabi al-Thani **1197 AH** in the text, **6 Rabi al-Akhir 1188 AH** "خاندانی روایت کے مطابق",
  according to **family tradition**, from a book by a family member (p. 221, folio 209, حاشیہ 1).
  **The editor reports it without adjudicating** — unlike his Shah Daula and Shaikh Musa footnotes,
  and note that here the "contemporary source" criterion would not decide it.
- **The editor prints two documents he has authenticated himself.** The Shah Wali Allah ijazat-nama
  "کتب خانہ خدا بخش (پٹنہ)" (p. 218, folio 206) and the Bukhari sanad "**بتحقیق محمد اقبال مجددی**"
  (p. 224, folio 212). **This is the third plate in these chunks after the Mujaddid's letter
  (chunk 012), and the pattern is consistent: the edition illustrates itself with manuscript
  documents whose text the transcription cannot recover.**

## Doubts

- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah and shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri: the geography of the referral is
  ambiguous.** The sentence reads "تیرے نصیب کا … شاہ عنایت قادری کے پاس ہے **تو بمقام قصور جائے گا
  تو حصہ پائے گا**" (p. 219). It can mean that **Shah Inayat is at Qasur**, or that **Bulleh Shah must
  go to Qasur** (his own town, per the archive) and will receive his portion there. The archive places
  **Shah Inayat's shrine at Mozang Chungi, Lahore** and **Bulleh Shah's at Kasur**, so the reading
  matters. **The chunk does not settle it and neither reading should be published as the book's.**
  One word in the clause is OCR-flagged ("بجزہ [OCR?]"), which is exactly where the sense of
  "portion" sits.
- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah: the chunk gives him no dates, no grave and no poetry**, and he appears only
  inside another saint's entry. The single new fact for the row is the **referral by Shaikh Abd
  al-Hakim Qadiri of Talamba[OCR?]**, and the place-name is flagged. **Do not build a biography from
  this.**
- **shrine-of-mian-mir: the chain is a chain, not shrine evidence.** "میاں میر بالا پیر" and "میاں
  میر لاہوری" are the only forms given; there is no date, no quarter, no urs. The second link,
  **"شاہ بلاق [OCR?]"**, is OCR-flagged, and he is the subject's own father — so the chain's first
  step is unverified as transcribed.
- **"شیخ محسن شاہ" appears in two chains of different orders.** Here he is a Qadiri link between Shah
  Abd al-Rashid Lahori and Mulla Shah (p. 222); in chunk 017 (p. 179) "محسن شاہ" stands in the
  **Chishti Sabiri** chain that the editor rejected for Ghore Shah. **The chunk gives no basis for
  identifying or separating them; leave both as printed.**
- **mithankot-kot-mithan must not take anything from entry no. 153.** "مولوی غلام فرید لاہوری" died
  **1216 AH** and is buried in the **Miani graveyard, Lahore** (p. 222). The archive's row is **Khwaja
  Ghulam Farid** of **Mithankot**, d. **1901 CE**, Chishti — a different man by a century, a province
  and an order. **Leave unmapped**, and note the shared name as a likely mis-match. Likewise
  **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** takes nothing from this entry.
- **The four Miani Sahib rows take nothing from entry no. 153 either.** The archive's
  **ghazi-ilm-din-shaheed**, **khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz**, **tahir-bandagi-qadri** and
  **wasif-ali-wasif** all stand in that graveyard, and **none of them is this man.** The citable fact
  is about the **site**: a scholar of Lahore was buried there in 1216 AH. **An entry may use it as
  context for the burial ground; no row may claim it as its own.**
- **Mir Muhammad Yaqub now has three candidate death years and none is secure.** Text: 4 Muharram
  **1160 AH**; editor: 1160 belongs to **Fazl Ali Lahori**, and the author elsewhere gives Mir
  Yaqub's death as **9 Safar 1179 AH [OCR?]** (p. 216). **Two of the three figures in the editor's
  correction are OCR-flagged**, as is the name "فضل علی یکدل [OCR?]" and the *Khazina* volume
  reference. **A human must read this footnote from the PDF before either year is used**, and note
  that the correction also silently supplies **Fazl Ali Lahori's** death year, which the archive does
  not carry.
- **The healing method is unreadable at the decisive word.** "آپ کے **دہن کے تھلے [OCR?]** سے اچھا ہو
  جاتا" (p. 216) — the phrase naming what came from his mouth (saliva? breath? a formula?) is flagged.
  **The practice may be recorded; the means may not.**
- **Both manuscript plates lose their documents.** p. 218 (folio 206) carries "[illegible: 6 lines]"
  under a caption identifying an **ijazat-nama in Shah Wali Allah's own hand** from the **Khuda Bakhsh
  Library, Patna** — with two OCR flags in the caption itself; p. 224 (folio 212) gives a partly
  legible Arabic Bukhari sanad with **four "[illegible]" gaps** and three OCR flags, including on its
  own heading. **A human must re-read both pages from the PDF before anything is said about either
  document**, in particular the grantee's name.
- **The Bukhari sanad's grantee may or may not be the collector cited throughout these chunks.** The
  plate names "**مولوي غلام محي الدين صاحب** [OCR?]" (p. 224); chunks 018, 019 and 021 repeatedly cite
  the manuscript collection of "**مولانا غلام محی الدین قصوری**" at the Ganj Bakhsh Library. **The
  chunk does not connect them and the name on the plate is flagged.** If they are the same man it
  would explain the collection's contents, but that is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- **The author's own family pedigree has two versions across two chunks.** Chunk 020, in the author's
  voice: Ghulam Sarwar ← Ghulam Muhammad ← Hafiz Rahim Allah ← Hafiz Rahmat Allah ← **Mufti Hafiz
  Muhammad Taqi**. Here, in the editor's voice: Hafiz Rahmat Allah ← **Mufti Muhammad Naqi [OCR?]** ←
  Mufti Muhammad Taqi[OCR?] (p. 222, folio 210) — **an extra generation, and both names flagged.**
  Chunk 020 also carried a **Mufti Muhammad Taqi bin Mufti Muhammad Taqi, d. 1196 AH**, so the family
  has repeating names. **Do not reconstruct the line from these two passages; the editor refers the
  reader to the edition's introduction and to *Zikr-e Jamil*.**
- **Pir Bhawan Shah's three places and his tribe are all OCR-flagged**: "پٹھو پور [OCR?]",
  "میرلوپر [OCR?]" twice — including as his **burial place** — and "قوم کھکہ [OCR?]" (p. 222). **His
  shrine cannot be located from this chunk**, and the tribe cannot be identified. His father's name
  "شاہ بلاق [OCR?]" is flagged too, and "بارہ کے سادات" (the Barha sayyids) is given without a source.
- **Muhkam al-Din's burial place is flagged**: "بمقام **کوٹ لجنٹ [OCR?]** متصل بہاول پور" (p. 221).
  Abd al-Khaliq's **Mubarakpur near Bahawalpur** is not flagged, so the founder's shrine is locatable
  and the successor's is not.
- **Nur Husain's village-burning curse names no village.** "فلاں گاؤں کے زمیندار" — the zamindars of
  such-and-such village (p. 223). **The most drastic karamat in these chunks is geographically
