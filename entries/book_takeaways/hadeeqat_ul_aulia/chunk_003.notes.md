# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 003 — pp. 37–47

The author's own voice begins here: closing devotional verses, his statement of why he wrote the book,
a chronogram on its title, then the first *chaman* — the Qadiri chain from Abdul Qadir Jilani, the
Gilani family of Uch, and entries 1 to 7. The editor's حاشیہ notes run alongside and repeatedly
correct the author.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **bari-imam** (شاہ لطیف بری قادری, "Shah Latif Bari Qadri" — entry no. 6):
  - "بزرگان پنجاب سے یہ حضرت بڑے بزرگ مشہور ہیں" — famous as a great elder among the elders of
    Punjab; thousands of *khawariq* and *karamat* famous of him; a great worshipper, ascetic,
    recluse, *mast* and *majzub*; thousands of murids reached the stages of completion (p. 45,
    folio 33).
  - He received his inner blessing (نعمت باطنی) from Hazrat Hayat al-Mir Zinda Pir, "who is among the
    grandsons of Ghaus al-Azam and is eternally living (زندہ جاوید)" (p. 45, folio 33).
  - The author gives his death as ۹۹۴ھ [OCR?] and says his holy *rauza* is famous, without naming its
    place (p. 45, folio 33).
  - **The editor rejects that date outright**: "شاہ لطیف بری کا یہ سال وفات بہت [OCR?] متنازع فیہ
    ہے" — this death year is much disputed. The family record now existing gives 1117 AH / 1706 CE,
    "which fits the circumstantial evidence", because Sayyid Hasan Peshawari met Shah Latif in
    Pothohar in ۱۰۷۷ھ [OCR?], and Shah Muhammad Ghaus Lahori b. Sayyid Hasan Peshawari met him in
    1116 AH and wrote the meeting up himself (p. 46, folio 34, حاشیہ 3).
  - Hasan Peshawari's account of the meeting is quoted in Persian: "چوں در ملک پوٹھوہار رسیدم۔ سید
    شاہ لطیف مجذوب دیدم کہ خیلے صاحب نظر آثار بودند و با من بسلوک تمام ملاقات کردند۔ یک دو روزی
    گذرانید و رخصت شدم" — "When I reached the country of Pothohar I saw Sayyid Shah Latif Majzub, who
    bore the marks of great insight, and he met me with complete courtesy; a day or two passed and I
    took leave" — sourced to Mir Ghulam Kashmiri, *Khawariq al-Adat*, 1189 AH, manuscript owned by
    Maulana Sayyid Amir Shah Qadri of Peshawar, folio ۸ [OCR?]b (p. 46, folio 34, حاشیہ 3).
  - A bibliography of seven further sources on him is given (p. 46, folio 34): Shah Muhammad Ghaus
    Lahori, *Risala dar Kasb-e Suluk wa Bayan-e Marifat*, Peshawar 1284 AH; Mir Ghulam Kashmiri,
    *Khawariq al-Adat*, 1189 AH; Muqim Muhkam al-Din Hajrawi, *Durr al-Ajaib*, translation, Lahore;
    Gul Hasan Shah, *Tazkira-e Ghausia*, Lahore (n.d.); Muhammad Qasim Rajori, *Hayat-e Bari Imam*,
    Rawalpindi; Manzur al-Haq Siddiqi, *Shah Latif Bari*, Lahore 1970; and Iqbal Mujaddidi's own
    article "بری شاہ لطیف" in the Danishnama-e Shibh-e Qarra, Tehran. The title *Hayat-e Bari Imam*
    is the only place in this chunk where the name "Bari Imam" itself appears.
  - The editor also attacks the pir: Hayat al-Mir "is not mentioned in contemporary books of history
    or in the genealogies". Shah Abul Muali Lahori wrote that of the sons of Shaikh Abdul Razzaq b.
    Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani, Shaikh Jamal Allah was in that era present in the jungles of Bastam,
    but did not give him the sobriquet Hayat al-Mir; the sobriquet was first applied to Shaikh Jamal
    Allah by Ahmad Ali Astarabadi in *Tazkira-e Muqimi*, ۱۱۷۲ھ [OCR?], p. 2, "جس کا کوئی تاریخی ثبوت
    نہیں ہے" — for which there is no historical proof (p. 45, folio 33, حاشیہ 2).
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** (حضرت شاہ ابوالمعالی لاہوریؒ): cited as an authority in his own right
  and credited with the work *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya*, in which he wrote that Shaikh Jamal Allah, of the
  progeny of Shaikh Abdul Razzaq b. Abdul Qadir Jilani, was in that era living in the jungles of
  Bastam (p. 45, folio 33, حاشیہ 2). The chunk gives nothing about his shrine.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (میاں میر): used as a Lahore landmark rather than described — Sayyid Mahmud
  Huzuri's tomb is placed "لاہور کے باہر میاں میر کی سڑک کے پاس", outside Lahore near Mian Mir's
  road (p. 44, folio 32). The road already bore his name in the author's time.
- **shergarh** (حضرت داؤد کرمانی, "Hazrat Daud Kirmani"): one anecdote, in a footnote sourcing the
  Hujra family — *Maqamat-e Daudi* by عبدالباقی حجی [OCR?], a manuscript owned by Muhammad Iqbal
  Mujaddidi, records that during Hazrat Daud Kirmani's period of *i'tikaf* Hazrat Baha al-Din Hajrawi
  came to meet him, and because of the i'tikaf the meeting could not take place (p. 47, folio 35,
  حاشیہ 4). Placement rests on the name Kirmani alone — see Doubts.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani** (حضرت غوث الاعظم): the chapter's founder. Spiritual chain given from
  Imam Ali b. Musa Raza, the eighth Imam, through Maruf Karkhi, Sari Saqti, Junaid Baghdadi, Abu Bakr
  Shibli, Abdul Wahid Tamimi, Abul Faraj Tartusi, Abul Hasan Qureshi Hakari and Abu Said Mubarak
  Makhzumi to him; both his paternal and maternal genealogies are set out in full; he was
  "صحیح الطرفین حسنی و حسینی" — a sayyid on both sides; his madhhab was Hanbali; his khawariq and
  karamat "as many as the stars in the sky" (pp. 39–40, folios 27–28). He is called Jili and Jilani
  from Jilan, where he lived (p. 39, folio 27).
- **Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Uchi Jili Gilani**: "اول پنجاب میں آئے" — the first of Abdul Qadir Jilani's
  line to come to Punjab. His forebear Sayyid Abul Abbas Ahmad b. Sayyid Sufi left Baghdad with his
  younger brother Sayyid Abu Sulaiman at the time of the sack of Baghdad under Hulagu Khan, went to
  Rum, and settled at Aleppo when the disorder ended; Sayyid Muhammad was born there, travelled
  distant countries in his youth, performed Hajj several times, came to Lahore and stayed some years,
  returned to Aleppo, and after his father's death came by way of Khurasan to Multan and settled at
  Uch. His descendants are spread widely in Hindustan (p. 40, folio 28). He died 923 AH and was buried
  at Uch; he had four sons — Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani, Sayyid Abdullah Rabbani, Mubarak Haqqani and
  Sayyid Muhammad Nurani — "from each of whom a separate faiz flowed" (p. 41, folio 29). He was also
  a scholar and poet with the *takhallus* "Qadri", whose diwan survives in the Siraj al-Din Azar
  collection of the Punjab University library, no. ۸۱۴ [OCR?], and who wrote *Miftah al-Ikhlas*, an
  abridgement of *Khulasat al-Mafakhir* (p. 41, folio 29, حاشیہ 1).
- **Mir Sayyid Shah Firoz** (entry no. 1): a Gilani sayyid and pir-e-tariqat; his grandfather Shah
  Alam came from Baghdad to Hind and settled at Lahore after touring; on his grandfather's death
  Shah Firoz "دادا کی مسند پر بیٹھے" — sat on his grandfather's masnad — and carried on instruction
  and teaching, spending his whole life guiding people. His pir-chain is given back to Ghaus al-Azam.
  He died 933 AH and his mazar is in Lahore, "ٹنڈی گراں کے تکیے میں", in the takya of Tandi Garan
  (pp. 41–42, folios 29–30), which the editor sources to Khazina 1/118 (p. 42, folio 30, حاشیہ 5).
- **Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani b. Sayyid Muhammad Halabi Uchi Gilani** (entry no. 2): taught the outer
  and inner sciences by his father; thousands of karamat recorded in books; received the title
  "Abdul Qadir Sani" from Ghaus al-Azam in the world of the unseen. Died 17 Rabi al-Awwal 940 AH /
  1533 CE, mazar at Uch (pp. 42–43, folios 30–31). See Practices for his refusal of the Delhi
  court's grants.
- **Sayyid Mahmud Huzuri Lahori** (entry no. 3): a Musawi Ghauri sayyid; his father Khwaja Shams
  al-Din, known as Shams al-Arifin, was from the country of Ghur; after his father's death he toured
  Hind and settled in Lahore in Muhalla Haji Sawai. Died ۹۷۲ [OCR?] AH (pp. 43–44, folios 31–32).
- **Miran Sayyid Mubarak Haqqani b. Sayyid Muhammad Uchi Halabi Gilani** (entry no. 4): left
  habitation for the jungle; no human could come before him, and whoever did became *mast* and
  *majzub*; he remained twelve years in a state of intoxication; only one man, Maruf Chishti, received
  khilafat from him. He then came to Lahore and died there in "نو سو چھبیس سال ہجری" (926 AH); his
  body was carried to Uch and buried (p. 44, folio 32).
- **Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Bala Pir b. Sayyid Zain al-Abidin b. Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani Uchi Gilani**
  (entry no. 5): murid of his grandfather, who also raised him, because his own father had been
  martyred at the hands of infidels while young, in his father's presence. When his grandfather died
  he fell out with his uncle Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh, left Uch, and settled at **Satghara**,
  "a famous qasba in Punjab", where he died 5 Shawwal 959 AH and where his *rauza* also stands
  (p. 45, folio 33).
- **Sayyid Baha al-Din Gilani, known as Bahawal Sher Qalandar Hujrawi** (entry no. 7): descendant of
  Sayyid Abdul Razzaq b. Abdul Qadir Jilani through the full chain given; born at Baghdad; came to
  Hindustan with his father and his paternal aunt and settled at Badayun; his father died there and he
  was trained by his aunt, "جو رابعہ عصر تھی" — who was the Rabia of the age. Died 18 Shawwal 973 AH,
  buried at Hujra; his death date is derivable from the chronogram phrase "عبدالقادر ثانی" (pp. 46–47,
  folios 34–35). See Legends for the founding of Hujra.
- **Sayyid Hasan Peshawari** and his son **Shah Muhammad Ghaus Lahori**: witnesses to Shah Latif Bari's
  dates, meeting him in 1077 AH [OCR?] and 1116 AH respectively (p. 46, folio 34, حاشیہ 3).
- **Imam al-Din Hakim**, "مدح خوان رسول کریم" — the friend at whose insistence the author undertook
  this book (p. 37, folio 25).
- **Sultan Husain Mirza, ruler of Sind, and Sultan Sikandar Lodhi, king of Hind** — both became murids
  of Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus after he settled at Uch, and "لاکھوں آدمیوں" (hundreds of thousands of
  men) took benefit from him (p. 40, folio 28).
- **Uch** as a burial site: Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus (d. 923 AH), Mubarak Haqqani (d. 926 AH, body carried
  there from Lahore) and Abdul Qadir Sani (d. 940 AH) are all placed there (pp. 41–44,
  folios 29–32) — a Qadiri Gilani cluster distinct from the archive's Suhrawardi Bukhari rows at Uch
  Sharif.
- **The fall of Baghdad** is dated in a footnote: "۶۵۶ھ / ۱۲۵۸ء میں ہلاکو کے ہاتھوں بغداد کا سقوط
  ہوا" (p. 40, folio 28, حاشیہ 1).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The book's own definition of Punjab**, and therefore of its shrine geography: the author was
  asked for a short Urdu collection of the awliya of the country of Punjab, "یعنی دہلی سے پشاور تک
  جس قدر علاقہ اس وقت پنجاب کے ساتھ متعلق ہے اور مشہور اولیاء کے مزار اس میں ہیں" — that is, from
  Delhi to Peshawar, whatever territory is at this time attached to Punjab and in which the mazars of
  famous awliya stand; the necessary account of all of them was to be written (p. 37, folio 25).
- **A sajjada nashin refuses state endowment.** When Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani became sajjada nashin on
  his father's death he gave up the company of kings and nobles; the Shah of Delhi took offence and
  wished his brother to hold the sajjada instead; before that could be acted on, Abdul Qadir Sani
  sent back to the king the فرامین [OCR?] of *jagir*, *a'imma* and *wazaif* (stipends) "جو متعلق
  خانقاہ فقرا تھے" — which pertained to the khanqah of the faqirs — writing that he had no need of
  royal jagir or a'imma and the king should give them to whomever he made sajjada nashin; thereafter
  his expenditure was "غیبی خزانہ سے", from the unseen treasury. Summoned again with a thousand
  entreaties, he refused and answered in two Persian couplets (pp. 42–43, folios 30–31). This is the
  clearest statement in the chunk of shrine endowment as a royal instrument and of succession
  disputes reaching the court.
- **Destruction of a Lahore muhalla under the Sikhs.** Muhalla Haji Sawai, where Sayyid Mahmud Huzuri
  settled, "لاہور قدیم کے بیرون شہر تھا۔ سکھوں نے اسے تباہ کر دیا تھا" — lay outside the old city of
  Lahore and was destroyed by the Sikhs; the editor quotes the author's own Persian: "محلہ حاجی
  سوائی بیرونی شہر کہ حال از دست سکھاں جاہل رُو بہ ویرانی آوردہ است" (Khazina 1/122) (pp. 43–44,
  folios 31–32, حاشیہ 2).
- **Lahore topography recorded by the author**: the takya of Tandi Garan, holding Shah Firoz's mazar
  (p. 42, folio 30); Muhalla Haji Sawai, outside the walled city (p. 43, folio 31); and the road of
  Mian Mir, outside Lahore, beside which Sayyid Mahmud Huzuri's maqbara stands (p. 44, folio 32).
- **Succession by masnad and by khilafat**: Shah Firoz succeeded to his grandfather's masnad (p. 42,
  folio 30); Mubarak Haqqani gave khilafat to exactly one man (p. 44, folio 32); Sayyid Muhammad
  Ghaus's four sons each carried a separate line of *faiz* (p. 41, folio 29).
- **The founding of a settlement by a saint**, with the zamindars converted: see Legends, Hujra.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Of Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani: "مشہور کرامت ان کی یہ تھی کہ جو کوئی کفار یا فساق و فجار میں سے ان کی
  خدمت میں حاضر ہوتا مسلمان اور تائب ہو جاتا" — his famous karamat was that any unbeliever or
  reprobate who came into his presence became a Muslim and repented (p. 42, folio 30).
- Of Sayyid Mahmud Huzuri: the day anyone took bai'at at his hand, that same night he was honoured
  with a vision of the Prophet — which is why he was given the title "Huzuri" (pp. 43–44,
  folios 31–32).
- Of Mubarak Haqqani: whoever came before him became *mast* and *majzub*; he remained twelve years
  intoxicated (p. 44, folio 32).
- Of Bahawal Sher Hujrawi: he lived 250 years, an age no Qadiri shaykh has reached; he sat three
  separate twelve-year seclusions, worshipping without food or sleep; sitting forty years in one spot
  in a cave, his back stuck to the stone he used as a pillow and the skin stayed on the stone when he
  rose; his beard only began to grow at the age of one hundred (p. 47, folio 35).
- Of Bahawal Sher Hujrawi, the founding of Hujra: driven twice off land belonging to the zamindars of
  the قوم دھول [OCR?] where he had built a صفہ [OCR?] on the riverbank, he came into *jalal* and
  ordered the river to move and vacate the place; the water went several miles off at once, a high
  ridge emerged from the river, and he settled on it; the zamindars, seeing the karamat, all became
  his murids and habitation grew there — the present qasba of Hujra (p. 47, folio 35). Narrated by
  the author as "نقل ہے" (it is related).
- Of Shah Latif Bari: thousands of khawariq and karamat are famous, none specified (p. 45, folio 33);
  and his pir Hayat al-Mir Zinda Pir is said to be "زندہ جاوید", eternally living (p. 45, folio 33).
- Of Abdul Qadir Jilani: karamat "as many as the stars in the sky" (p. 40, folio 28).

## Arguments and interpretations

- The author's own account of the book's occasion: he had already compiled *Khazinat al-Asfiya* in
  Persian, "جو دو مرتبہ لاہور و دہلی میں چھپ کر" — printed twice, at Lahore and Delhi — covering
  every silsila and family from its beginning to his own time; friends then pressed him for a short
  Urdu collection confined to Punjab; "ایک دوست کے فرمانے اور دلی محبت نے جو قدیم سے مجھ کو اولیائے
  اللہ کے ساتھ ہے۔ اس کام پر مجھ کو آمادہ کیا" — a friend's word and his own long-standing love for
  the friends of God made him willing; and he hopes the book will remain as his memorial in this
  perishing world after his death (p. 37, folio 25).
- He restates the seven-*chaman* plan in his own words (p. 38, folio 26), matching the editor's
  account in the previous chunk.
- The editor corrects the author on genealogy three times in this chunk alone: the father's line of
  Abdul Qadir Jilani per *Bahjat al-Asrar* (p. 39, folio 27, حاشیہ 1); "یہاں مفتی صاحب سے شجرہ نسب
  درج کرنے میں غلطی ہوئی ہے" — here the Mufti has erred in recording the genealogy — against
  Abdul Haq Dehlvi's *Akhbar al-Akhyar* p. 189 (p. 40, folio 28, حاشیہ 3); and, on Shah Firoz's
  pir-chain, that the name of Shaikh Ahmad has dropped out and that Sayyid Mir should stand after
  Sayyid Ahmad (p. 42, folio 30, حواشی 1 and 4).
- The editor also corrects the author's bibliography: Mufti Ghulam Sarwar gave the author of
  *Shajarat al-Anwar* as Asghar Ali, which is wrong — it is Sayyid Ali Asghar Gilani, whose work on
  the conditions and genealogies of this same Uch family was completed in 1193 AH, and whose own
  descent from Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Uchi is given (pp. 41–42, folios 29–30, حاشیہ 2).
- The editor supplies a manuscript bibliography for the Uch family: *Akhbar al-Akhyar*;
  Ali Asghar Gilani, *Shajarat al-Anwar* (ms, Punjab University library); Abdullah Marwari Uchi,
  *Manaqib al-Asfiya*, a contemporary of Sayyid Hamid Ganj Bakhsh (ms owned by Sayyid Nur Muhammad of
  Gujrat); Muhammad Idris Uchi, *Nataij al-Akhbar* (ms owned by Maulvi Ghulam Ahmad Akhtar of Uch);
  and Sharafat Naushahi, *Sharafat al-Tawarikh* vol. 1 p. ۸۰۹ [OCR?] (ms of the author) (p. 41,
  folio 29).
- Two chronograms are recorded for Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Uchi's death, from two different books:
  "محمد مخدوم عالم" in *Khazinat al-Asfiya* and "محمد حسنی پاک رفت" in *Shajarat al-Anwar* (p. 41,
  folio 29) — the author preserving a disagreement rather than resolving it.

## Doubts

- **bari-imam — the central date problem of this chunk.** The author gives ۹۹۴ھ [OCR?] (p. 45,
  folio 33); the editor gives 1117 AH / 1706 CE from the surviving family record and argues it from
  two dated meetings (p. 46, folio 34). The two differ by more than 120 years and cannot both stand.
  The author's figure additionally carries an `[OCR?]` flag, as does the 1077 AH of the first
  meeting; only 1116 AH and 1117 AH / 1706 CE are unflagged. Nothing in the chunk places Shah Latif's
  shrine geographically, so the identification with the archive's Bari Imam rests on the editor's
  citation of a book titled *Hayat-e Bari Imam* (Rawalpindi) and on the Pothohar meeting, not on any
  statement in the text.
- **shergalh/shergarh**: the footnote's "حضرت داؤد کرمانی" (p. 47, folio 35, حاشیہ 4) is placed
  against the archive's Daud Bandagi Kirmani on the shared name only. The footnote gives no place,
  no date and no silsila, and the book's own contents call the Shergarh figure
  "شیخ داؤد جہنی وال شیر گڈھی". Confirm at the entry itself (printed p. 36) before using this
  anecdote.
- **Contradiction on where Khazinat al-Asfiya was printed.** The author says it was printed twice,
  "لاہور و دہلی" — at Lahore and Delhi (p. 37, folio 25). The editor's own print history in the
  previous chunk names Lahore 1284 AH, Lucknow ۱۳۰۴ء [OCR?], Kanpur 1312 AH and 1902 CE, and no Delhi
  printing at all (p. 30, folio 18, chunk 002).
- **Internal chronological impossibility, unresolved by the book.** Bahawal Sher Hujrawi is said to
  have lived 250 years, to have been born at Baghdad, and to have died 18 Shawwal 973 AH (pp. 46–47,
  folios 34–35). On that arithmetic he would have been born around 723 AH, and the genealogy makes
  him the twelfth generation from Abdul Qadir Jilani (d. 6th century AH). The author states the age
  as a mark of distinction and draws no inference from it; neither does the editor correct it.
- **Sayyid Abdul Qadir Sani's family is given two irreconcilable versions in one footnote**: two sons
  (Shaikh Abdul Razzaq, d. 5 Jumada al-Akhir ۹۴۲ھ [OCR?], and Sayyid Zain al-Abidin) per *Akhbar
  al-Akhyar* p. 193, against seven sons per Sharafat Naushahi's *Hadaiq al-Anwar*, ms p. ۳۵ [OCR?]
  (p. 43, folio 31, حاشیہ 1). His age at death is given as ۸۷ [OCR?] years.
- **Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Uchi's sons are given as four by the author and three by his source.** The
  text names four — Abdul Qadir Sani, Abdullah Rabbani, Mubarak Haqqani, Muhammad Nurani (p. 41,
  folio 29) — while the footnote quotes Abdul Haq Dehlvi for three, Abdul Qadir (called مخدوم [OCR?]
  Sani), Sayyid Abdullah and Sayyid Mubarak, with descendants known as Mir Miran resident at Lahore
  (p. 41, folio 29, حاشیہ 1).
- **Date of his arrival at Uch is disputed in the apparatus**: the author narrates the migration
  without a year, while the footnote gives 887 AH on Ali Asghar Gilani's authority, via Khazinat
