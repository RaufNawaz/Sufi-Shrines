# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 007 — pp. 78–87

End of entry 37 through the opening of entry 43 (Baba Farid): the close of the Qadiri chaman, then the
second chaman on the Chishtis. Contains an eyewitness account of the 1840 Lahore demolitions, the
entries on Bulleh Shah and on Abdul Rahman Pak Naushahi with its description of a living sama ritual,
and the start of Baba Farid.

**Folio markers in this chunk are wrong by ten, and the error is provable.** Every `[folio N]` from
p. 76 onward reads ten too high (a ۶/۷ misreading). Checked against the table of contents in chunk
001: entry 38 is at printed p. 68 and stands on p. 80 (marker 78); entry 39 at printed p. 69 stands on
p. 81 (marker 79); entry 40 at printed p. 70 on p. 82 (marker 80); entry 41 at printed p. 71 on p. 83
(marker 81); entry 42 at printed p. 73 on p. 85 (marker 83); entry 43 at printed p. 74 on p. 86
(marker 84). The offset PDF = printed page + 12 therefore holds unbroken from p. 14 to p. 87. Folios
below are given corrected, with the marker's own reading in brackets.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (میاں میر بالا پیر):
  - **A first-person account of an overnight stay and a dream-initiation at the shrine**, quoted by the
    author from Shah Muhammad Ghaus's own *Risala-e Ghausia*: "جب میں تلاش حق لاہور میں پہنچا۔ رات کو
    مقبرہ عالیہ میاں میر میں قیام پذیر ہوا۔ خواب میں میاں میر بالا پیر مجھ پر ظاہر ہوئے۔ اور بکمال
    توجہ ایک شغل عنایت کیا۔ صبح کے وقت وہاں سے اٹھ کر شیخ حامد لاہوری کی خدمت میں آیا اور کسی شغل کے
    واسطے ان سے بھی سوال کیا۔ وہ ہنسے اور فرمایا تجھ کو وہی شغل کافی ہے جو آج رات میاں میر بالا پیر
    سے عطا ہوا ہے" — "When I reached Lahore in search of the truth, I lodged for the night at the
    exalted maqbara of Mian Mir. In a dream Mian Mir Bala Pir appeared to me and with complete
    attention bestowed a *shughl* on me. In the morning I rose from there and came to Shaikh Hamid
    Lahori and asked him too for some shughl. He laughed and said: the very shughl bestowed on you
    last night by Mian Mir Bala Pir is enough for you." (p. 78, folio 66 [marker: 76])
  - The shrine as a lineage anchor: Shaikh Abdullah Shah Baloch's chain "چار واسطوں کے ساتھ حضرت میاں
    میر بالا پیر لاہوریؒ کے ساتھ ملتا تھا", joined Mian Mir Bala Pir through four intermediaries
    (p. 82, folio 70 [marker: 80]).
- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** (سید بلہی [OCR?] شاہ قادری شطاری قصوری — entry no. 39, pp. 81–82,
  folios 69–70 [markers: 79–80]):

  - "سماع و وجد کا شغل دن رات مد نظر تھا۔ اکثر اوقات خوارق و کرامت بے اختیار ان سے سرزد ہوتی" — the
    practice of sama and wajd was before his eyes day and night, and marvels often issued from him
    involuntarily (p. 81, folio 69 [marker: 79]).
  - **His poetry still being sung when the book was written**: "پنجابی زبان میں ابیات و کافی ہزاروں
    ان کی تصنیف کی ہوئی مضامین توحید اب تک قوال مجالس فقراء میں گاتے ہیں" — thousands of *abyat* and
    *kafis* of his composition in the Punjabi language, on themes of *tauhid*, are to this day sung by

  - "وفات ان کی ۱۱۸۰ھ میں ہوئی۔ اور مزار گوہر بار قصور میں زیارت گاہ خلق ہے" — he died 1180 AH and
    his pearl-scattering mazar at Kasur is a place of pilgrimage (p. 82, folio 70 [marker: 80]).

  - **The name's pronunciation, settled from the same seal**: "اس وقت اس لفظ کا تلفظ 'بلھے' یا 'بلھا'
    ہے۔ آپ کی مہر 'بلھا شاہ قادری ۱۱۸۱ھ' سے بھی موجودہ تلفظ 'بلھا' کی تائید ہوتی ہے" — the present
    pronunciation is Bulhe or Bulha, and his seal "Bulha Shah Qadri 1181 AH" supports the current

  - Sources listed: *Kulliyat-e Bulhe Shah* ed. Faqir Muhammad Faqir, Lahore; Ahmad Husain Ahmad
    Qureshi, *Sawanih-e Baba Bulhe Shah*, **in press with the Department of Auqaf, Lahore**;
    Dr Muhammad Shafi, *Auliya-e Kasur* 197–199; and two works of the editor's own in preparation,
    *Tarikh-e Kasur* and *Hayat-e Shah Inayat Qadri* (p. 82, folio 70 [marker: 80]).
- **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri** (شاہ عنایت قادری لاہوری): named here as Bulleh Shah's pir (p. 81,

- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** (حضرت شیخ فرید الدین مسعود گنج شکر اجودھنی پاک پٹنی — entry
  no. 43, beginning, pp. 86–87, folios 74–75 [markers: 84–85]):
  - The heading itself places him at **Ajodhan / Pakpattan**, matching the archive row (p. 86,
    folio 74 [marker: 84]).
  - "ترک و تجرید و زہد و ریاضت و علم و عمل و کشف و کرامت میں ہر ایک سے سبقت لے گئے تھے" — in
    renunciation, detachment, austerity, knowledge, practice, unveiling and miracle he outstripped
    everyone of the Chishti family; he had perfect occupation with *zauq*, *shauq*, love, *wajd* and
    *sama* (p. 86, folio 74 [marker: 84]).
  - "زہد و ریاضت راہ خدا میں جس قدر انہوں نے کیا ہے کسی نے متقدمین اولیا میں سے بھی نہیں کیا۔ روزہ
    آپ کا دائمی تھا جب افطار کرتے تو درختوں کی پتی ان کی خوراک تھی" — no one, not even among the
    earlier awliya, performed as much austerity in God's way as he did; his fast was perpetual, and
    when he broke it the leaves of trees were his food. His days passed in instructing the people and
    his nights in worship (p. 86, folio 74 [marker: 84]).
  - **Family**: his father was Jamal al-Din Sulaiman, said in the text to be a nephew of Sultan Mahmud
    Ghaznavi, who came from Kabul to Lahore in the time of Sultan Shihab al-Din Ghori, resided چندے
    [OCR?] in **qasba Kasur**, then went to Multan and married **Qursum Khatun**, daughter of Mulla
    Wajih al-Din Khujandi. Three sons were born of her: Shaikh Azz al-Din Mahmud, Farid al-Din Masud,
    and Najib al-Din Mutawakkil, "کہ یہ تینوں بھائی اپنے زمانے کے یگانہ تھے" — all three brothers
    unique in their time (pp. 86–87, folios 74–75 [markers: 84–85]). Qursum Khatun has her own entry
    in the chapter on righteous women (contents no. 194, printed p. 238).
  - The ancestral chain is said to reach Sultan Ibrahim b. Adham through seventeen links and Umar ibn
    al-Khattab through thirty, and is printed in full (p. 87, folio 75 [marker: 85]).
  - **The editor rejects almost all of this**: the migration story, transmitted in *Sair al-Auliya*,
    *Mirat al-Asrar*, *Maarij al-Wilayat* and *Sair al-Arifin*, "درست نہیں ہے" — is not correct; Qazi
    Shuaib came to Hindustan before 1175 CE and about forty years before the Mongol occupation, indeed
    at the time of the غز [OCR?] invaders in **552 AH / 1157 CE** (p. 87, folio 75 [marker: 85],
    حاشیہ 1, citing Khaliq Ahmad Nizami, *Shaikh Farid al-Din*, p. 1). Mulla Wajih al-Din Khujandi
    "کا کسی معاصر تذکرہ نویس نے ذکر نہیں کیا" — is mentioned by no contemporary tazkira-writer, his
    name appearing only in four later books (p. 87, folio 75 [marker: 85], حاشیہ 2). And of the
    genealogy: "سیر الاقطاب پہلا تذکرہ ہے جس میں حضرت بابا صاحب کا شجرہ نسب درج ہوا ہے۔ ورنہ اس سے
    پہلے کی کتابیں شجرہ نسب سے خالی ہیں۔ سیر الاقطاب سے تقابل کے بعد مفتی صاحب کا منقولہ شجرہ غلط
    ثابت ہو جاتا ہے" — *Sair al-Aqtab* is the first tazkira in which Baba Sahib's genealogy is
    recorded, earlier books being devoid of one, and on comparison with it the Mufti's transmitted
    genealogy is proved wrong; the corrected chain is then printed from *Sair al-Aqtab* p. 163
    (p. 87, folio 75 [marker: 85], حاشیہ 3).
  - The editor also fixes his grandfather's office and grave: after Qazi Shuaib's death, Shaikh Jamal
    al-Din Sulaiman was appointed qazi of qasba کتہوال [OCR?], between Multan and Ajodhan — "اس وقت
    ایک معمولی قصبہ ہے جسے مشائخ کی جھاولی [OCR?] کہتے ہیں", now an ordinary qasba called Mashaikh ki
    Jhawali — and was buried there (pp. 86–87, folios 74–75 [markers: 84–85], حاشیہ 2, citing
    *Sair al-Arifin* p. 37 and Nizami's *Life and Times of Shaikh Farid al-Din* p. 11).
- **ranmal-sharif** (the Naushahia order), via entry no. 38 on Abdul Rahman Pak Naushahi, "بڑے خلیفہ

  - **A described sama ritual of the order, in the present tense of the 1870s**: "پوشیدہ نہ رہے کہ
    فرقہ نوشاہیہ عالیہ میں سب لوگ صاحب وجد و سماع و شوق و ذوق و مستی ہیں۔ مگر فقرائے سلسلہ پاک رحمان
    کے سماع کے وقت سب سے زیادہ مست ہو جاتے ہیں۔ جب تک ان کے پاؤں میں رسہ ڈال کر الٹا نہ لٹکائیں اور
    وہ ساعت دو ساعت اسی حالت میں رقص نہ کریں سرد نہیں ہوتے اور اگر اس عمل سے ہوش میں نہ آئیں تو اسی
    حالت میں ان کو زمین پر کھینچتے ہیں۔ جب تک وہ ہوش میں آ جائیں رسن ان کے پاؤں سے کھولا نہیں جاتا" —
    Let it not stay hidden that in the exalted Naushahia sect all are possessed of wajd, sama, longing
    and intoxication; but the faqirs of the Pak Rahman line become the most intoxicated at the time of
    sama. They do not cool until a rope is put round their feet and they are hung upside down and
    dance an hour or two in that state; and if they do not come to their senses by this operation they
    are dragged along the ground in it; and the rope is not untied from their feet until they come to
    themselves (p. 81, folio 69 [marker: 79]).
  - "مزار پر انوار موضع بھڑی [OCR?] زیارت گاہ خلق ہے" — his radiant mazar at mauza Bharri [OCR?] is a

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Shah Muhammad Ghaus Gilani Qadri Lahori** (end of entry no. 37): died 1152 AH, "مزار لاہور میں
  ہے", his mazar is in Lahore, with an Urdu-Persian chronogram carrying ۱۱۵۲ in the margin (p. 78,
  folio 66 [marker: 76]). Eleven sources are listed, one of them dismissed in the listing itself:
  Payam Shahjahanpuri's *Tazkira-e Shah Muhammad Ghaus* — "یہ کتاب بغیر کسی تاریخی شعور کے مدون کر لی
  گئی ہے۔ اکثر تحقیقات بے سرو پا ہیں", this book has been compiled without any historical sense and
  most of its investigations are baseless. Five works of his own are listed, including *Sharh-e
  Ghausia* ۱۱۳۱ھ [OCR?], a *Sharh-e Bukhari Sharif* in manuscript, a *Risala Zikr-e Jahr*, a Persian
  translation of the *Qasida Ghausia* published by Pir Abdul Ghaffar Lahori in 1910 under the alternate
  title *Sharh-e Khamira*, and an Arabic *Asrar al-Tauhid*; two manuscript treatises of his on
  *wahdat al-wujud* had recently reached Maulvi Sayyid Muhammad Amir Shah (pp. 78, 80; folios 66, 68
  [markers: 76, 78]).
- **Shaikh Hamid Lahori**, who declined to add to what Mian Mir had given in the dream (p. 78,
  folio 66 [marker: 76]).
- **Shaikh Abdul Rahman, known as Pak Rahman Naushahi** (entry no. 38): the attributes of *samadiyat*
  so overcame him "کہ کھانا، پینا، سونا ان کا بالکل موقوف ہو گیا تھا", that his eating, drinking and
  sleeping stopped entirely; at the time of wajd people were certain he had died; all night he did
  silent zikr with breath-retention, sometimes hung head-down in a well worshipping through the night,
  and in seclusion "زمین میں قبر کھود کر بیٹھ جاتے اور اوپر سے بند کرا دیتے", would dig a grave in the
  ground and sit in it and have it closed over, being taken out in a wretched state when it was opened
  after forty days (p. 80, folio 68 [marker: 78]).
- **Shaikh Shadi**, Abdul Rahman Pak's servant: see Legends (pp. 80–81, folios 68–69
  [markers: 78–79]).
- **Shaikh Abdullah Shah Baloch Lahori Qadri** (entry no. 40): murid and khalifa of Shaikh Sharaf
  al-Din Panipati. "یہ بزرگ ذات کے بلوچ تھے اور ساربانی کے پیشے سے بہت مال جمع کیا تھا اور موضع مزنگ
  میں متصل لاہور ایک کوٹ اپنے نام کا آباد کیا۔ اس کا نام کوٹ عبد اللہ رکھا" — he was a Baloch by
  caste, amassed much wealth by the trade of camel-driving, and founded a *kot* in his own name at
  mauza Mozang adjoining Lahore, calling it **Kot Abdullah**; then, drawn by the true Attractor, he
  renounced worldly wealth and became a faqir. Died 8 Jumada al-Awwal 1212 AH, "مزار بیرون شہر موضع
  مزنگ میں ہے", his mazar being outside the city in mauza Mozang, with "غریب" as the death chronogram
  (pp. 82–83, folios 70–71 [markers: 80–81]). A modern book is cited: Muhammad Din Kalim,
  *Abdullah Shah Baloch*, Lahore 1971. **He is not the archive's Abdullah Shah Ghazi** — see Doubts.
  - His great khalifas included **Hafiz Ghulam Muhammad, known as Imam Gamun, imam of the Masjid
    Wazir Khan**, and **Shaikh Faiz Bakhsh Qureshi Lahori**, پدر [OCR?] of Shaikh Imam Bakhsh, "جد
    مادری مؤلف کتاب" — the author's own maternal grandfather (p. 82, folio 70 [marker: 80]). The
    alchemy anecdote is narrated by Shaikh Murad Bakhsh, son of Shaikh Faiz.
- **Shaikh Ghulam Husain of Walian Wali** (entry no. 41): extremely devout; "آخری زمانے میں اگر ان کو
  قطب وقت لکھا جائے تو بجا ہے", if he were called the Qutb of the age in the last era it would be apt;
  thousands of seekers reached their desire, and among worldly petitioners too "جو اہل حاجت حضرت کے
  دروازے پر حاضر ہوا۔ خالی نہ گیا"; his unveilings and marvels are so current on people's tongues
  that a separate book would be needed to record them. Died 1260 AH; his son **Shaikh Qaisar Shah**
  benefited many after him (p. 83, folio 71 [marker: 81]). The editor gives his full descent — b.
  Bahram Khan b. Zar Bakhsh b. Rasida, of the قوم تور [OCR?] alias داوال [OCR?] — and locates the

  communications; and corrects the son: "اصل نام کبیر شاہ تھا۔ سال وفات ۱۲۸۱ھ", his real name was
  Kabir Shah, died 1281 AH, born through the prayer of Sayyid Ilahi Bakhsh Naushahi Sahnpalvi
  (d. 1253 AH) (p. 83, folio 71 [marker: 81], حواشی 2–3).

- **Khwaja Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar Kaki Aushi Dehlvi** (entry no. 42): of the sadat of **Aush**, a qasba

  Sadiq given; his father died in his childhood and "والدہ ماجدہ نے ان کو پڑھایا اور تربیت کی", his

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Sikh-period demolition outside Lahore's walls, and its reversal — the author as eyewitness.** The
  clearance ordered by Nau Nihal Singh (p. 77, folio 65, chunk 006) ends thus: "تو قدرت الہی سے اس روز
  کھڑک سنگھ مر گیا اور نونہال سنگھ جب اس کی نعش جلا کر آیا تو سلامی کی توپوں کے زلزلے سے قلعہ کی
  دیوار سے ایک پتھر جدا ہو کر نونہال سنگھ کے سر پر آ پڑا اور وہ جوان جوانمرگ ہوا۔ اس کی والدہ چند کور
  نہایت ڈری اور حکم دیا کہ مزار حضرت کا نہ گرایا جائے۔ چنانچہ گرا ہوا مکان اسی وقت تعمیر ہو گیا" — by
  divine power Kharak Singh died that very day, and when Nau Nihal Singh returned from burning his
  corpse a stone was shaken loose from the fort wall by the tremor of the salute guns and fell on his
  head, and that young man died young. His mother **Chand Kaur** was greatly frightened and ordered
  that the Hazrat's mazar not be pulled down; accordingly the fallen building was rebuilt at that very
  time (p. 78, folio 66 [marker: 76]).
- **Offerings, and a saint forbidding payment for a miracle.** A woman whose child had been restored
  "ایک جوڑہ زنانہ کپڑوں کا شادی کے نذر کیا", offered a suit of women's clothes as *nazr* to Shaikh
  Shadi; he took them home and dressed the Hazrat's daughter in them; and Abdul Rahman Pak, hearing
  of it, summoned him in wrath: "تو اس نعمت کے لائق نہیں کہ اس بڑھیا کے بچہ کو زندہ کر کے تو نے اجرت
  لے لی" — you are not worthy of this gift, for you have taken a wage for bringing that old woman's
  child to life; "اسی روز سے وہ شادی سے کرامت جاتی رہی", and from that day the karamat left Shadi
  (pp. 80–81, folios 68–69 [markers: 78–79]).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The deaths of Kharak Singh and Nau Nihal Singh presented as the shrine's karamat**, and offered by
  the author as the one marvel he can vouch for with his own eyes (p. 78, folio 66 [marker: 76]; the
  "چشم دیدہ" claim is at p. 77, folio 65, chunk 006).
- Mian Mir bestowing a *shughl* in a dream on a man sleeping at his tomb (p. 78, folio 66
  [marker: 76]).
- Abdul Rahman Pak's three-fold gift to Shaikh Shadi — that any sick person he looked at be healed,
  any dead person he turned to come alive, and any sinner he looked at become a wali — and the
  restoration of the dead child on the road, narrated by the author as "نقل ہے" (p. 80, folio 68
  [marker: 78]).
- Abdul Rahman Pak's own forty-day burials in a closed grave, and the nightly inverted vigils in a
  well (p. 80, folio 68 [marker: 78]).
- Abdullah Shah Baloch and the alchemy: told the boy who had ruined his coins with arsenic and sulphur
  that if he wanted the operation of alchemy he should come and serve as his father had, "اور ہم تم کو
  ایسی کیمیا بخشیں گے کہ یہ کیمیا تمہاری نظر میں خاک نظر آئے گی" — and we will bestow on you such an
  alchemy that this alchemy will look like dust in your eyes (pp. 82–83, folios 70–71
  [markers: 80–81]).
- Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar Kaki producing two hot *kak*, that is *kulchas*, from his armpit at each sama
  until the whole assembly was fed — "اسی واسطے حضرت کا خطاب کاکی مشہور ہے", which is why his title
  Kaki is famous (p. 85, folio 73 [marker: 83]). And his death in the middle of Ahmad-e Jam's ghazal,
  at the verse "کشتگان خنجر تسلیم را / ہر زمان از غیب جانے دیگر ست", when "حضرت کا حال برگشتہ ہو گیا
  اور اسی حالت میں جان دے دی" (p. 86, folio 74 [marker: 84]).
- Baba Farid's perpetual fast broken on tree leaves (p. 86, folio 74 [marker: 84]).
- Shaikh Ghulam Husain's marvels, said to need a book of their own and therefore not given (p. 83,
  folio 71 [marker: 81]).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **A documented dispute over the Chishti chain**, set out by the editor: Shah Wali Allah Muhaddith
  Dehlvi held in his *Intibah fi Salasil al-Auliya* that the Chishti silsila does not reach Hazrat Ali
  through Khwaja Hasan Basri, "اس لیے کہ خواجہ بصری اس وقت خرد سال تھے اور خلیفہ نہیں ہو سکتے تھے"
  — because Hasan Basri was a child at the time and could not have been a khalifa; his contemporary
  Shah Fakhr al-Din Dehlvi wrote a risala *Fakhr al-Hasan* in refutation; and Maulana Hasan al-Zaman
  Hyderabadi (d. 1328 AH) wrote a bulky Arabic commentary on it, *Qaul al-Mustahsan fi Fakhr
  al-Hasan*, printed at Hyderabad in 1317 AH (p. 84, folio 72 [marker: 82], حاشیہ). The chain the
  author prints at pp. 84–85 is the one Shah Wali Allah rejected.
- **Two places called Chisht, and which one the Khwajas belong to**: *Shajarat al-Anwar* holds that one
  Chisht is in Khurasan near Herat and the other a qasba in Hindustan between Uch and Multan, and
  "خواجگان چشت خراسان والے چشت سے تعلق رکھتے ہیں", the Khwajas of Chisht belong to the Khurasan one
  (p. 84, folio 72 [marker: 82], حاشیہ 1, citing *Tarikh-e Mashaikh-e Chisht* 36–135 [OCR?]). A
  corrected sixteen-name order of the chain is then printed from the same work, pp. 139–140.
- **The editor's method extended to a seal.** The Bulleh Shah re-dating rests on a physical object — a
  seal impressed on a manuscript with a year in it — rather than on a text (p. 82, folio 70
  [marker: 80]), the same species of evidence he used for Shah Inayat's 1148 AH letter (pp. 75–76,
  chunk 006). Two of the Qadiri chapter's best-known dates are overturned by seals.
- **He also pronounces on the quality of modern secondary work**, dismissing Payam Shahjahanpuri's
  tazkira as compiled "بغیر کسی تاریخی شعور کے" (p. 78, folio 66 [marker: 76]) — one of the few places
  he judges a twentieth-century book rather than a manuscript.
- **On Baba Farid he applies the rule of the earliest source and finds the genealogy worthless**, and
  states the general principle behind it: books earlier than *Sair al-Aqtab* carry no genealogy at all,
  so anything in the later chain is unsupported (p. 87, folio 75 [marker: 85], حاشیہ 3).
- The author himself signals what he will not write: Shaikh Ghulam Husain's karamat "کے اندراج کے لیے
  ایک کتاب علیٰحدہ لکھی جائے" (p. 83, folio 71 [marker: 81]), the same compression he applied to Shah
  Husain's marvels at p. 55, folio 43 (chunk 004).

## Doubts

- **The folio markers on every page of this chunk are ten too high.** The correction is set out in the
  header above with six independent checks against the table of contents. Any citation taken from this
  chunk's markers as printed will point ten printed pages too far into the book. The same error begins
  at p. 76 in chunk 006.
- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah — three different death years are now in play.** The author's 1180 AH
  (p. 82, folio 70 [marker: 80]); the editor's finding that he was still alive in 1181 AH (same
  page, حاشیہ 1); and the archive row's 1757, which is 1170–71 AH and is supported by nothing in this
  chunk. The row's date needs a source outside this book or it needs changing.
- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah — the name as printed.** The entry heading reads "سید بلہی [OCR?] شاہ", with
  the transcriber's flag on the crucial word, and the editor's own note treats the spelling as an open
  question resolved only by the seal (p. 81, folio 69 [marker: 79]). The archive row's "Abdullah Shah
  Qadri" appears nowhere in the chunk; the book gives him no personal name beyond Bulha/Bulhe and the
  attribution "Qadri Shattari Qasuri".
- **shrine-of-abdullah-shah-ghazi is NOT entry no. 40.** Entry no. 40's Shaikh Abdullah Shah Baloch
  Lahori Qadri was a Baloch camel-driver of Mozang, Lahore, khalifa of Sharaf al-Din Panipati, who
  died 8 Jumada al-Awwal 1212 AH (pp. 82–83, folios 70–71 [markers: 80–81]). The archive's Abdullah
  Shah Ghazi is at Clifton, Karachi, and died 773. The names are close enough that the two must be
  kept apart deliberately; nothing in this book connects entry 40 to Karachi.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar — the whole of the author's family history is rejected by his own
  editor.** The Ghaznavid kinship, the arrival "from Kabul to Lahore in the time of Shihab al-Din
  Ghori", the residence at Kasur, the marriage to Wajih al-Din Khujandi's daughter and the
  thirty-link chain to Umar are each contradicted or declared unsupported in the three footnotes at
  pp. 86–87 (folios 74–75 [markers: 84–85]). The mother's name Qursum Khatun rests on a man
  "mentioned by no contemporary tazkira-writer". Nothing in this part of the entry should enter an
  archive entry without the footnote attached.
- **ranmal-sharif — Abdul Rahman Pak's date differs by 38 years** between the text (1153 AH) and the
  editor's family sources (4 Muharram ۱۱۱۵ھ [OCR?], itself OCR-flagged) (p. 81, folio 69
  [marker: 79]). The place, mauza بھڑی [OCR?], is also OCR-flagged.
