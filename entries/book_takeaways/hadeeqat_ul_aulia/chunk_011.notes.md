# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 011 — pp. 117–126

Entries 70 (conclusion) to 81. The Chishti chapter reaches the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries and
crosses into Pakistani Punjab in earnest: **Taunsa, Multan, Kot Mithan, Sharaqpur, Mahar and Lahore all
appear**, and four archive rows take material. The chunk also contains the single richest practice
passage in these eleven chunks — a Lahore silk-worker's calendar of **seventeen annual urs**, each with a
food distribution — and the author's own present tense, which dates his writing: a grandson is "now"
on the masnad at Taunsa, and murids of the dead are "موجود ہیں", still living.

**The folio markers agree with the relation on every page.** Folio = PDF index − 12 throughout:
p. 117 → folio 105 through p. 126 → folio 114, each marker reading exactly that.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a** (خواجہ محمد سلیمان چشتی — entry no. 78,
  pp. 123–124, folios 111–112; plus the editor's footnote at p. 120, folio 108):
  - **Placed at Taunsa by his pir's order**: he was of the renowned khalifas of Khwaja Nur Muhammad
    Majheel[OCR?] Chishti, and "حسب الحکم پیر روشن ضمیر کے قصبہ تونسہ میں جاکر قیام پذیر ہوئے" — by the
    command of his pir he went and took up residence at qasba Taunsa (p. 123, folio 111).
  - **Origins and education**: "اصل وطن ان کا پہاڑی ملک میں موضع گڑ گوجھی[OCR?] تھا", his original home
    was mauza Gar Gojhi[OCR?] in the hill country; he first acquired outward learning **at qasba Kot
    Mithan in the service of Qazi Muhammad Aqil**, and only then came to Nur Muhammad (p. 123,
    folio 111). The editor gives the lineage and birth: "شاہ سلیمان تونسوی بن زکریا بن عبدالوہاب بن عمر
    خاں کی ولادت ۱۱۸۴ھ بمقام گڑ گوجھی[OCR?] میں ہوئی" — Shah Sulaiman Taunsvi son of Zakariya son of
    Abd al-Wahhab son of Umar Khan, born 1184 AH at Gar Gojhi[OCR?] — quoting *Nafi al-Salikin* p. 11:
    "خانہ خود کہ در کوہ است واسم آں گڑ کوچی[OCR?] است کہ مسافت از تونسہ سہ کروہ می شود", his own house
    which is in the hills, named Gar Kochi[OCR?], **three kuroh from Taunsa** (p. 123, folio 111,
    حاشیہ 1).
  - **Where Taunsa is, and a madrasa founded there**: "تونسہ ڈیرہ غازی خاں سے ۴۰ کوس کے فاصلے پر ایک
    گاؤں ہے۔ آپ نے اپنا شہر چھوڑ کر بحکم مرشد تونسہ میں سلسلہ ارشاد وتلقین کا آغاز کیا۔ **یہاں ایک
    مدرسہ قائم کیا۔** آپ کو قرآن حدیث اور فقہ پر عبور حاصل تھا" — Taunsa is a village 40 kos from Dera
    Ghazi Khan; he left his own town and by his murshid's command began the silsila of guidance and
    instruction at Taunsa; **he established a madrasa here**; and he had mastery of Quran, hadith and
    fiqh (p. 123, folio 111, حاشیہ 2). The editor, not the author, records the madrasa.
  - **Langar, twice daily, for travellers and the poor**: "ہزاروں طالبان خدا وہاں حضرت کی خدمت میں مشرف
    ہوئے اور ہزاروں مسافر ومسکین وغربا دو وقتہ حضرت کے لنگر سے کھانا کھاتے" — thousands of seekers were
    honoured in his service there, and **thousands of travellers, destitute and poor ate two meals a day
    from the Hazrat's langar** (p. 123, folio 111).
  - **Scale of following, quantified**: "غرض وہ قبول حاصل ہوا کہ اس زمانہ میں کسی کو حاصل نہ تھا", he
    attained such acceptance as no one in that age had; "ایک لاکھ سے زیادہ حضرت کے مرید ہوئے اور
    سینکڑوں کفار وفجار نے توبہ کی" — **more than one lakh became his murids**, and hundreds of
    unbelievers and profligates repented (p. 123, folio 111). The largest figure the book gives for any
    living following.
  - **Three chief khalifas named**: "شیخ محمد یار و حافظ محمد علی و مولوی محمد علی ثانی تین خلیفے حضرت
    کے مقرب و محبوب و محرم اسرار تھے" — Shaikh Muhammad Yar, Hafiz Muhammad Ali and Maulvi Muhammad Ali
    Thani were his intimate, beloved and secret-sharing khalifas, out of countless others (p. 124,
    folio 112). Of the second the editor adds: **Hafiz Muhammad Ali, known as Muharram Ali Khairabadi
    (1192–1266)**, was among Taunsvi's earliest khalifas, and "خیرآباد میں ان کی خانقاہ علم و فضل کا
    مرکز تھی", his khanqah at Khairabad was a centre of learning (p. 124, folio 112, حاشیہ 1).
  - **Death date, and a contradiction inside the entry**: the text gives "نویں ماہ صفر روز پنجشنبہ سنہ
    ایک ہزار دوسو سٹھ" — 9 Safar, a Thursday, in the year **1260** — while the chronogram four lines
    later carries "۱۲۶۷ [OCR?]" and the editor's earlier footnote states "وفات ۱۲۶۷ھ میں ہوئی", death in
    **1267 AH** (p. 124, folio 112; p. 120, folio 108, حاشیہ 6). See Doubts.
  - **Succession by grandson, in the author's present tense**: "بعد وفات ان کے اب شیخ الہ بخش حضرت کے
    پوتے مسند آرائے مسند مشیخیت ہیں" — after his death, **now** Shaikh Allah Bakhsh, the Hazrat's
    grandson, adorns the masnad of shaykh-hood (p. 124, folio 112). The editor confirms and dates him:
    "اپنے دادا کی طرح خواجہ الہ بخش تونسویؒ روحانی اصلاح و تربیت کے کام میں سرگرم رہے۔ حقیقت یہ ہے کہ
    انیسویں صدی میں چشتیہ سلسلہ کی رونق ان کے دم قدم سے تھی" — like his grandfather he was active in
    spiritual reform and training, and in truth the splendour of the Chishtiya silsila in the nineteenth
    century was owing to him; born 1231 AH, died 1319 AH (p. 124, folio 112, حاشیہ 2).
  - **The silsila named as the archive names it**: "پنجاب میں سلسلہ چشتیہ نظامیہ فخریہ کی زیادہ اشاعت
    حضرت خواجہ سلیمان تونسوی کے ذریعہ ہوئی" — in the Punjab the wider propagation of the **Chishtiya
    Nizamiya Fakhriya** silsila came about through Khwaja Sulaiman Taunsvi; born 1184 AH, died 1267 AH,
    with four titles named as sources — *Nafi al-Salikin*, *Khatam-e Sulaimani*, *Intikhab-e Manaqib-e
    Sulaimani*, *Manaqib al-Mahbubin* (p. 120, folio 108, حاشیہ 6).
  - **An eight-item bibliography** with imprints: *Nafi al-Salikin*, Lahore 1285 AH; *Khatam-e
    Sulaimani*, Lahore 1335 AH; Muhammad al-Din Sufi, *Zikr-e Habib* Beti[OCR?] Baha al-Din 1334;
    *Manaqib-e Hafiziya*; Amir Bakhsh, *Anwar-e Shamsiya*, Lahore 1335 AH; Din Muhammad, *Ahtiram
    al-Asfiya* / *Nishat-e Hafiziya*, Aligarh 1931; *Nafa'at[OCR?] al-Mahbub fi Ihya al-Qulub*; Khaliq
    Ahmad Nizami, *Tarikh-e Mashaikh-e Chisht* 608–667 (p. 124, folio 112).
  - **Two of his khalifas have their own entries here**: Shaikh Haji Ramzan Lahori (entry no. 80) and,
    through Haidar Shah and Khair Shah, the Lahore line of entry no. 81 — so the Taunsa shrine's reach
    into Lahore is documented in the book itself (pp. 125–126, folios 113–114).
- **shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani** (خواجہ محمد جمال / حافظ محمد جمال ملتانی — entry no. 72 and
  the editor's footnote, p. 120, folio 108):
  - He is the **third of the four chief khalifas** of Khwaja Nur Muhammad Maharvi named "صاحب قرب", of
    nearness (p. 120, folio 108).
  - The editor's judgement of his significance: **"ملتان میں سلسلہ چشتیہ کو حضرت حافظ محمد جمال ملتانی
    ف ۱۲۲۶ھ کی بدولت فروغ ہوا"** — in **Multan** the Chishtiya silsila flourished thanks to Hazrat Hafiz
    Muhammad Jamal Multani, **d. 1226 AH** (p. 120, folio 108, حاشیہ 5).
  - **Three collections of his malfuzat are named**: *Gulzar-e Jamaliya* by Allama Abd al-Aziz Parharvi;
    *Anwar-e Jamaliya* by Ghulam Hasan; *Asrar al-Kamaliya* by Zahid Shah — "آپ کے ملفوظات کے مجموعے
    ہیں", these are collections of his malfuzat (with *Manaqib al-Mahbubin* 121–140 and Khaliq Ahmad
    Nizami, *Tarikh-e Mashaikh-e Chisht* 590–607) (p. 120, folio 108, حاشیہ 5). A citable corpus for the
    Multan entry, named on one page.
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri** (دربار حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہور، میانی صاحب — editor's footnote, p. 125,
  folio 113):
  - **The darbar is used as a topographic landmark.** Of Shaikh Haji Ramzan Lahori (entry no. 80) the
    editor writes: "حاجی شیخ رمضان لاہوری کا مزار دربار حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہور میانی صاحب کے
    قریب ہے" — Haji Shaikh Ramzan Lahori's mazar is **near the darbar of Hazrat Shaikh Muhammad Tahir
    Bandagi, Lahore, Miani Sahib** (p. 125, folio 113, حاشیہ 1, citing *Khazinat al-Asfiya* 1/515 and
    Imam Bakhsh, *Hadiqat al-Asrar* p. 13). The archive's row places this shrine in Miani Sahib
    graveyard; this is an independent 1974 confirmation that the darbar stood there and was well enough
    known to fix another grave by. Note the book gives the name as **Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Bandagi**, not
    "Tahir Bandagi Qadri", and says nothing else about it.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** (خواجہ فرید الدین — entry no. 81, p. 125, folio 113):
  - **His annual observance was kept in Lahore, by a silk-worker, as one of seventeen.** Shaikh Faiz
    Bakhsh Lahori "ہر سال میں سترہ عرس" performed seventeen urs a year, the list including "خواجہ فرید
    الدین" between Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar and Ala al-Din Ali Ahmad Sabir, and at each "طعام وافر پکا غربا
    و مساکین کو تقسیم کرتے", cooked abundant food and distributed it to the poor and destitute (p. 125,
    folio 113). Evidence that Baba Farid's *salina* was observed away from Pakpattan, inside a domestic
    ritual calendar, in nineteenth-century Lahore.
- **mithankot-kot-mithan** (قصبہ کوٹ مٹھن — entry no. 78, p. 123, folio 111):
  - **The town appears as a place of Chishti teaching a century before the archive's saint.** Sulaiman
    Taunsvi "ابتدائے حال میں قصبہ کوٹ مٹھن میں قاضی محمد عاقل کی خدمت میں حاضر ہو کر علم ظاہری حاصل
    کیا" — in his early days attended Qazi Muhammad Aqil at qasba Kot Mithan and acquired the outward
    sciences. The editor identifies that teacher: Khwaja Muhammad Aqil was of the **Faruqi** family, his
    father Muhammad Sharif, his family title **Koreja**, and he died 14 Rajab 1229 AH (p. 120, folio 108,
    حاشیہ 4). **The chunk says nothing about the shrine at Kot Mithan or about Khwaja Ghulam Farid** —
    see Doubts on how far this may be used.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Maulana Fakhr al-Din Fakhr-e Jahan Shahjahanabadi Chishti** (entry no. 70 concluded, pp. 117–118,
  folios 105–106): a scholar of the shariat sciences and knower of tariqat and haqiqat; **paternal
  genealogy to Shaikh Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi, maternal to Sayyid Muhammad Gesu Daraz Chishti**; God
  created him "خاندان چشت اہل بہشت میں … زمانہ اخیر کا قطب", the Qutb of the last age in the family of
  Chisht, and "لاکھوں خوارق و کرامتیں ان سے سرزد ہوئیں", lakhs of marvels issued from him. Born in the
  year 1126; died the seventh of Jumada al-Thani in the year "ایک ہزار ایک صد نونوے[OCR?]"; and **"مزار
  گوہر بار دہلی میں اندرون احاطہ مزار خواجہ قطب الدین بختیار کاکی کے ہے"** — his mazar is in Delhi
  inside the enclosure of Khwaja Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar Kaki's mazar (p. 118, folio 106). A chronogram by
  the author follows. The editor corrects the day and lists his books: see Arguments.
- **Nawab Nizam al-Mulk Ghazi al-Din Khan, Nazim of Hyderabad** (p. 117, folio 105): author of *Manaqib-e
  Fakhriya*, in which he "بے شمار کرامتیں حضرت کی اپنی کتاب میں درج کی ہیں", recorded countless karamat
  of Fakhr-e Jahan. The editor identifies him as **"نبیرہ آصف جاہ اول"**, grandson of Asaf Jah I, says
  the Persian text was printed by Matba Mujtabai Delhi, and that **Mir Dard Kakorvi published an Urdu
  translation from Karachi in 1961** (p. 117, folio 105, حاشیہ 1).
- **Shaikh Sayyid Alim Allah bin Sayyid Atiq Allah Chishti Jalandhari** (entry no. 71, pp. 118–119,
  folios 106–107): sayyids of sound descent on both sides from qasba Jalandhar; genealogy to Zaid bin
  Imam Hasan; first bai'at with Shah Abu al-Maali, then training, completion and khirqa from Miran
  Sayyid Bhikha. "علوم ظاہری میں بھی یہ صاحب تدریس تھے" — he was also a teacher of the outward sciences.
  Works: *Anhar al-Asrar*, *Sharh Bostan* of Sa'di, *Risala Nuzhat al-Salikin*, *Sharh Akhlaq-e Nasiri*,
  *Zubdat al-Riwayat* (fiqh) and *Nathr al-Jawahir*, a Persian translation of the Arabic *Nazm al-Durar
  wal-Marjan*. His Persian verses are famous beyond count. **His khalifa Shaikh Abdullah wrote a book
  *Asrar al-Alim* on his circumstances**, recording many karamat. Born 22 Jumada al-Thani in the year
  1109; died 16 Safar in the year 1202; and **"روضہ عالیہ جالندھر میں زیارت گاہ خلق ہے"**, his lofty
  rauza at Jalandhar is a place of pilgrimage (p. 119, folio 107), with a chronogram carrying "(۱۲۰۲)".
  The editor's corrections and his catalogue of the works are under Arguments; note that *Nathr
  al-Jawahir* translates a work "مؤلفہ مرزا خان برکی", compiled by **Mirza Khan Barki** — the father of
  chunk 010's Bahlol Barki (p. 119, folio 107, حاشیہ 2).
- **Shaikh Nur Muhammad Chishti, famous as Nur Muhammad Majheel[OCR?] Maharvi** (entry no. 72,
  pp. 119–120, folios 107–108): **greatest khalifa** of Fakhr-e Jahan. After memorising the Quran he went
  **from the Punjab to Delhi**, took bai'at with Fakhr al-Din, and after some years of service received
  the khirqa; then "پنجاب میں آکر قصبہ مہاراں علاقہ نواب بہاول خاں میں سکونت اختیار کی", came to the
  Punjab and settled at **qasba Maharan in the territory of Nawab Bahawal Khan**. Thousands reached
  nearness through him. **Four chief khalifas**: Khwaja Thani titled **Khalifa Sahib**, Maulana Qazi
  Muhammad Aqil, Khwaja Muhammad Jamal and Khwaja Sulaiman, "اور خلیفے بھی آپ کے بہت ہیں۔ جن سے فیض اب
  تک جاری ہے", with many more from whom faiz continues to this day. Died 1205 AH; **"مزار پر انوار قریہ
  تاج سرور متصل قصبہ مہاراں کے ہے"** — his mazar is at the village **Taj Sarwar adjoining qasba Maharan**
  (p. 120, folio 108). The editor adds a great deal: **"حضرت خواجہ نور محمد مہاروی کا آبائی نام
  مجھیل[OCR?] تھا۔ حضرت شاہ فخر الدین نے اسے نور محمد سے بدل دیا"** — his ancestral name was
  Majheel[OCR?] and Shah Fakhr al-Din changed it to Nur Muhammad; a ten-link paternal genealogy from
  Hindal bin Tatar; **"آپ کا تعلق قوم کھرل سے تھا"**, he belonged to the **Kharral** tribe; **born
  14 Ramadan 1142 AH at qasba Chotala**, east of Mahar towards Bahawalpur (*Manaqib al-Mahbubin* 44–45);
  bai'at with Shah Fakhr in **1165 AH**; three sons, Nur al-Samad Shahid, Nur Ahmad and Nur Hasan; and
  **"جن میں سے تیس کے نام محفوظ ہیں"**, of his many khalifas thirty names are preserved (p. 120,
  folio 108, حاشیہ and حاشیہ 1–2).
- **Nur Muhammad Thani Naruwala** (editor's footnote, p. 120, folio 108): the "خلیفہ صاحب" of the text is
  identified as him, "آپ نے ۱۲۰۴ھ میں وصال فرمایا", he passed away in 1204 AH — a year before his own
  pir, by the book's dates.
- **Sayyid Ali Shah Chishti Sabri Jalandhari** (entry no. 73, p. 121, folio 109): khalifa of Sayyid Alim
  Allah Jalandhari; after his pir's death "مسند ارشاد پر متمکن ہوئے", he was seated on the **masnad-e
  irshad**. Died in the year 1213; mazar at qasba Jalandhar, "زیارت گاہ خلق".
- **Shaikh Muhammad Saeed Chishti Sabri Sharaqpuri** (entry no. 74, pp. 121–122, folios 109–110):
  intoxicated with divine love; "قصبہ شرقپور متعلقہ ضلع لاہور میں سکونت رکھتے تھے", he resided at
  **qasba Sharaqpur attached to Lahore district**. **"چونکہ یہ بزرگ قوم کے خوجہ تھے۔ پہلے اپنے ہم قوم
  خوجوں کے ساتھ محنت مزدوری سے گذارہ کرتے تھے"** — since this elder was a **Khoja** by community, he
  first made his living by manual labour along with his fellow Khojas, and only when divine longing
  seized him turned to worship. An eight-link spiritual chain is given by name from him through **Shah
  Murad Multani**, Shaikh Jiwi[OCR?] Shah Gujrati, Shaikh Zakariya, Shaikh Haji Qutb, **Shaikh Dargahi
  Lahori** and Haji Abu Said Hanafi Gangohi to Shaikh Nizam al-Din Balkhi. Died, "باقوال صحیح" (by
  correct reports), in the year 1214 AH; **mazar in Sharaqpur**. The grain-cart miracle is under
  Legends. See Doubts: Sharaqpur holds an archive row for a different saint.
- **Shaikh Mahmud Saeed Chishti Jalandhari** (entry no. 75, p. 122, folio 110): of Sayyid Alim Allah
  Jalandhari's khalifas; "ہزاروں آدمیوں نے ان کی ذات سے بہرۂ کامل تلقین وتعلیم سے پایا", thousands
  received a full share of instruction and teaching from him, and "تمام عمر انھوں نے طلبہ کی تکمیل
  وتربیت میں صرف کی", he spent his whole life on the completion and training of **students**. Died
  1220 AH ("۱۲۲۰ھ"). No grave given.
- **Shaikh Khair al-Din, known as Khair Shah Chishti Lahori** (entry no. 76, p. 122, folio 110): khalifa
  of Shaikh Salim Chishti Lahori; "اخیر زمانہ میں انھوں نے علم مشیخت کا بلند کیا", in the latter age he
  raised the banner of shaykh-hood. Perfect inclination to wajd, tawajud and sama. **"لنگر بھی ان کا
  فقرا اور درویشوں کے واسطے جاری رہتا"** — his langar too kept running for the fuqara and dervishes. Died
  19 Zi al-Hijja in the year 1228; mazar in Lahore. Three rival death years: see Arguments.
- **Hafiz Musa Chishti Manikpuri** (entry no. 77, pp. 122–123, folios 110–111): of Shah Bhikha's silsila,
  murid and khalifa of Shaikh Sayyid Azam Chishti Rupri. **"ابتدائے حال میں مقفلی گری کے کام میں مشغول
  رہتے اور دو زوجہ ان کی تھیں۔ جب جاذب حقیقی نے ان کو اپنی طرف کھینچا۔ دونوں عورتوں کو طلاق دے دی"** — at
  first he worked as a **lock-maker** and had **two wives**; when the true attractor drew him he
  **divorced both women**, left worldly connection and turned wholly to worship. His jazb was such that
  whoever met his gaze in wajd became a *majzub*: "کئی شخص مثل کریم شاہ ومحمد شاہ وغیرہ مجذوب ہو گئے",
  several men such as Karim Shah and Muhammad Shah were made majzub. Died 16 Ramadan, a Sunday, in the
  year 1245; **mazar at qasba Manikpur**. Six khalifas named — Amanat Ali Amrohi, Ghulam Muin al-Din
  Shah Khamosh, Khwaja Abdullah Amrohi, Mir Amanat Ali Thani, Muhammad Bakhsh, Pir Shah.
- **Mian Amir Bakhsh the bookbinder** (p. 124, folio 112): the author adds that Taunsvi's murids of zauq
  "بے شمار تھے اور موجود بھی ہیں", were countless and are still living, and that "لاہور کے مشائخ میں سے
  میاں امیر بخش جلد ساز ایک مرد صاحب ذوق و عبادت تھا جو سنہ بارہ سو اکیانوے میں خلد معلی میں قیام پذیر
  ہوا" — among the mashaikh of Lahore, Mian Amir Bakhsh the **jild-saz** (bookbinder) was a man of taste
  and worship, who in the year 1291 took up residence in paradise. A named trade for a named Lahore
  shaykh, and the second craftsman-shaykh in this chunk.
- **Maulvi Amanat Ali Chishti Sabri** (entry no. 79, pp. 124–125, folios 112–113): of Hafiz Musa
  Manikpuri's khalifas, "تارک الدنیا"; after his pir's death he **went to Kashmir** and took faiz also
  from Shaikh Ahmad Shah Kashmiri, then returned and **settled at Amroha**. Died in the year 1280. Three
  of his murids — Shaikh Abd al-Rahim, Abd al-Rahman and Shaikh Sami Allah — "موجود ہیں", are living.
- **Shaikh Haji Ramzan Lahori Chishti** (entry no. 80, p. 125, folio 113): khalifa of **Khwaja
  Sulaiman** [Taunsvi]; resident in **Lahore**; lived in tajrid and tafrid, and "سماع کے حالت میں کمال
  اضطراب و بیقراری ان پر عائد ہوتی", in sama perfect agitation came upon him. **He went to the Haramain
  Sharifain** and benefited from the Hajj and the ziyarat of the Prophet's rauza; his prayers were mostly
  accepted. The author's own lament on his age: "ایسے ناپرسان وقت میں کہ ذات مردان خدا حکم عنقا رکھتی
  ہے۔ ان کی ذات مبارکات مغتنمات میں سے تھی" — in such an unregarding time, when the persons of the men of
  God have the status of the phoenix, his person was among the blessed to be prized. Born in the month of
  Ramadan in the year 1202; died 3 Ramadan 1282 AH; "عمر شریف اسی برس کی تھی", noble age eighty years —
  which the two dates support exactly.
- **Shaikh Faiz Bakhsh Lahori Sabri Chishti** (entry no. 81, pp. 125–126, folios 113–114): khalifa of
  **Shaikh Haidar Shah**, who had taken inward faiz from Khair Shah; a man of hal, qal, wajd, sama,
  zauq, tajrid and tafrid. **"گذارہ ان کا ریشم سازی کے کام پر تھا"** — his livelihood was the work of
  **silk-making**. His seventeen annual urs are under Practices, the locust miracle and his death in wajd
  under Legends. Austerities: the whole night in wakefulness, three baths in one night, and "بنظر ترک
  لذت حلوا [OCR?] میں نمک مرچ لگا کر تناول فرماتے", eating halwa[OCR?] with salt and chilli on it so as
  to renounce pleasure. Died of "تپ محرق", a burning fever, on 9 Rajab 1286 AH ("۱۲۸۶ھ [OCR?]")
  (p. 126, folio 114; Imam Bakhsh, *Hadiqat al-Asrar* 13; Nami, *Buzurgan-e Lahore* 179–180).
- **Hafiz Qadir Bakhsh, madh-khwan** (p. 126, folio 114): the named singer called to Faiz Bakhsh's
  deathbed. **Mahan Singh's garden** in Lahore, where Faiz Bakhsh worked his silk (p. 126, folio 114).
  **Shahpur** and **the old dome of the Madrasa Aliya** on the Sharaqpur–Lahore road (p. 121, folio 109).
  **Khairabad**, site of Hafiz Muhammad Ali's khanqah (p. 124, folio 112). **Manikpur**, **Amroha**,
  **Rupar**, **Jalandhar**, **Chotala**, **Maharan**, **Taj Sarwar**, **Gar Gojhi[OCR?]** (pp. 120–125).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Seventeen urs a year, kept by a silk-worker, each with a food distribution.** The fullest ritual
  calendar in these chunks: "ہر سال میں سترہ عرس مثل سالینہ حضرت پیغمبر صلی اللہ علیہ وسلم علی المرتضیٰ
  و فاطمۃ الزہرا و ختم سالینہ امامین و غوث الثقلین و خواجہ معین الدین حسن سنجری و خواجہ قطب الدین
  بختیار و خواجہ فرید الدین و خواجہ علاء الدین علی احمد صابر وغیرہ پیران عظام کیا کرتے اور ہر ایک
  سالینہ میں طعام وافر پکا غربا و مساکین کو تقسیم کرتے" — every year he performed **seventeen urs**: the
  *salina* of the Prophet, of Ali al-Murtaza, of Fatima al-Zahra, the *khatm salina* of the two Imams, of
  Ghaus al-Thaqalain, of Khwaja Muin al-Din Hasan Sanjari, of Khwaja Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar, of Khwaja
  Farid al-Din, of Khwaja Ala al-Din Ali Ahmad Sabir and other great pirs — and **at each annual he
  cooked abundant food and distributed it to the poor and destitute** (p. 125, folio 113). Nine
  observances named out of seventeen; a private householder's calendar, funded from silk-work, honouring
  shrines he did not own — three of them at Ajmer, Delhi, Pakpattan and Kaliyar respectively.
- **Two langars named as continuing institutions.** Taunsa: thousands of travellers, poor and destitute
  eating **twice a day** (p. 123, folio 111). Lahore: Khair Shah's langar "فقرا اور درویشوں کے واسطے
  جاری رہتا", kept running for the fuqara and dervishes (p. 122, folio 110). Note the different
  clientele — travellers and the poor at Taunsa, dervishes at Lahore.
- **A madrasa at Taunsa, founded by the saint, recorded only by the editor** (p. 123, folio 111,
  حاشیہ 2) — the third teaching institution attached to a shrine in these chunks, after Shah Kaku's
  madrasa in Landa Bazar (chunk 009) and Hafiz Muhammad Ali's khanqah at Khairabad (p. 124, folio 112).
- **Succession: masnad, sajjada and grandson.** "مسند ارشاد پر متمکن ہوئے" of Ali Shah Jalandhari
  (p. 121, folio 109); "مسند آرائے مسند مشیخیت ہیں" of Allah Bakhsh at Taunsa, a **grandson** taking the
  seat (p. 124, folio 112). With chunk 010's two brothers as sajjada nashin, the book records three
  different idioms for the same office.
- **Trades and livelihoods, named without embarrassment.** Muhammad Saeed Sharaqpuri a **Khoja** doing
  "محنت مزدوری", manual labour, and carting grain by bullock to Lahore (pp. 121–122, folios 109–110);
  Hafiz Musa a **lock-maker** (p. 123, folio 111); Faiz Bakhsh a **silk-worker** (p. 125, folio 113);
  Mian Amir Bakhsh a **bookbinder** (p. 124, folio 112). Four working saints in one chunk, against the
  amirs' sons of chunks 009–010.
- **A garden let out for its fruit.** In the locust story the man who weeps is "باغبان جس نے باغ کا میوہ
  خریدا تھا", the gardener **who had bought the garden's fruit** (p. 126, folio 114) — a fruit-crop
  purchase inside a named Lahore garden, incidental to the miracle and more informative than it.
- **A following counted, and conversions counted**: over one lakh murids and hundreds of repentances at
  Taunsa (p. 123, folio 111); thirty khalifas' names preserved for Nur Muhammad Maharvi (p. 120,
  folio 108); "چالیس"-style totals give way here to record-keeping.
- **Naming as an act of the pir**: "حضرت شاہ فخر الدین نے اسے نور محمد سے بدل دیا", Shah Fakhr al-Din
  replaced the tribal name Majheel[OCR?] with Nur Muhammad (p. 120, folio 108) — read with chunk 010's
  Lutf Allah, named before birth by his mother's pir.
- **Tribal and caste identities recorded**: **Kharral** for Nur Muhammad Maharvi, **Faruqi** with the
  family title **Koreja** for Muhammad Aqil (p. 120, folio 108), **Khoja** for Muhammad Saeed Sharaqpuri
  (p. 121, folio 109).
- **A shrine in the gift of a princely territory**: Maharan is located "علاقہ نواب بہاول خاں", in the
  territory of Nawab Bahawal Khan, and Chotala "از مہار سرکردہ سمت مشرق بہاول پور" (p. 120, folio 108).
  The book locates Chishti settlement by reference to the Bahawalpur state.
- **Sama as the medium of death, twice more.** Faiz Bakhsh called the qawwals in his final illness and
  died during a naat (p. 126, folio 114); Shaikh Sondha did the same in chunk 010. Here the singer is
  named and the text of the naat is given.
- **Print and translation economy**: *Manaqib-e Fakhriya* in Persian from Matba Mujtabai Delhi with an
  **Urdu translation published at Karachi in 1961** (p. 117, folio 105); *al-Qaul al-Mustahsan fi Fakhr
  al-Hasan*, a commentary of about **eight hundred pages**, printed Hyderabad 1312 AH (p. 118,
  folio 106); *Anhar al-Asrar* printed Lahore 1281 AH from a manuscript copied 1134 AH (p. 119,
  folio 107); *Nuzhat al-Salikin* translated into Urdu as *Tuhfat al-Salihin*, Lahore (p. 119,
  folio 107); *Nafi al-Salikin* Lahore 1285 AH and two Lahore imprints of 1335 AH (p. 124, folio 112).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A boy dead in a sama assembly, revived by resuming the qawwali.** From *Manaqib-e Fakhriya*: at
  Sultan al-Mashaikh Nizam al-Din's khanqah the sama was warm and a young boy among the intoxicated;
  a change of qawwals interrupted the singing, and when the company looked at him "سانس کا آنا جانا
  مسدود اور بدن سرد ہے", breath stopped and body cold. His father said "یہی ایک میرا فرزند تھا … میں
  بھی اپنی زندگی نہیں چاہتا", this was my only son, now I do not want my own life. Fakhr-e Jahan said be
  patient, "شاید ابھی یہ زندہ ہو", perhaps he is alive even now, and ordered the qawwals to sing "یک لب
  لعل تو صد جان می دہد …"; the company came into fervour, after one *sa'at* the youth stirred and rolled
  on the carpet, and a little later came to his senses (p. 117, folio 105). **The cure is the music
  itself, not a touch or a prayer** — and the death is attributed to the music stopping.
- **Ali al-Murtaza as a veiled horseman on the Sharaqpur–Lahore road.** Muhammad Saeed's bullock fell
  and broke its leg beside the old dome of the Madrasa Aliya; his companions went on and left him;
  at midnight "ایک سوار برقع پوش آیا اور اپنے نیزہ کی نوک کے ساتھ بیل کو اشارہ کیا", a veiled rider came
  and gestured at the bullock with his spear-point; the bullock stood, the grain-sack was replaced by
  the same gesture, and the rider said your bullock is sound, go on to Lahore. Asked his name he said
  "ہمارا نام علی المرتضیٰ ہے اور حق سے تیری امداد کے لیے مامور ہوئے ہیں", our name is Ali al-Murtaza and
  we have been deputed by the Truth to help you; asked for inward help he answered **"تیرا حصہ شاہ مراد
  ملتانی کے پاس ہے۔ وہاں جاکر لے لے"**, your portion is with Shah Murad Multani, go and take it there
  (pp. 121–122, folios 109–110). A vision that assigns a man to a named living pir.
- **A line of water drawn round a garden against locusts.** Faiz Bakhsh made a fine hole in the bottom of
  a water pot ("ٹنڈ") and told the weeping gardener to walk the garden's four sides with it; "ٹیڈی باہر
  کے تمام درختوں کو کھا گئی مگر باغ کے اندر ایک پرند بھی نہ گیا", the locusts ate every tree outside but
  not one flier went inside (p. 126, folio 114). The swarm is described as hiding the sun.
- **Death in wajd to a named naat.** Hafiz Qadir Bakhsh sang "منم خاک در کوئے محمد / اسیر حلقہٴ موئے
  محمد"; on hearing it "حضرت وجد میں آئے اور لرزہ جسم پر ظاہر ہوا۔ تمام بدن پر عرق ہو گیا آخر اسی جوش و
  خروش میں وفات پائی" — trembling appeared on his body, sweat covered him, and he died in that fervour
  (p. 126, folio 114).
- **A gaze that made majzubs**: Hafiz Musa's look in wajd left Karim Shah, Muhammad Shah and others
  permanently *majzub* (p. 123, folio 111).
- Of Fakhr-e Jahan the author says lakhs of marvels issued from him (p. 117, folio 105); of Faiz Bakhsh
  that his murids relate marvels without end and "جس کی گنجائش اس مختصر میں نہیں ہے", there is no room
  for them here, "مگر ہزاروں میں سے ایک تحریر ہوتی ہے", but one out of thousands is written (p. 126,
  folio 114); of Alim Allah Jalandhari that his khalifa's book records many (p. 119, folio 107).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor corrects a death day by ten and cites the shrine's own biography.** Of Fakhr-e Jahan,
  whose death the text puts on the seventh of Jumada al-Thani: **"۷ جمادی الآخری غلط ہے بلکہ ۲۷ جمادی
  الآخری ہونا چاہیے"** — 7 Jumada al-Akhir is wrong; it should be **27** Jumada al-Akhir (Khaliq Ahmad
  Nizami, *Tarikh-e Mashaikh-e Chisht*, citing *Fakhr al-Talibin* p. 53[OCR?]) (p. 117, folio 105,
  حاشیہ 2). A day-level correction, sourced through a modern historian to a shrine's own tazkira.
- **A book counted twice, corrected — and the error's transmission traced.** Of Alim Allah Jalandhari's
  works the text lists *Anhar al-Asrar* and *Sharh Bostan* separately. The editor: "یہاں مفتی صاحب سے
  تسامح ہوا ہے۔ شرح بوستان ہی کا نام انہار الاسرار ہے … انہار الاسرار اور شرح بوستان دو کتابیں نہیں
  ہیں۔ **مولانا عبدالحی (نزہۃ ۷ / ۲۳۷) نے اسی غلطی کا اعادہ کیا ہے**" — *Anhar al-Asrar* is the very
  name of the *Sharh Bostan*; they are not two books; **and Maulana Abd al-Hai repeated the same mistake
  in *Nuzhat al-Khawatir* 7/237** (pp. 118–119, folios 106–107, حاشیہ 2). Then he proves it
  bibliographically: "انہار الاسرار شرح بوستان سعدی شیرازی بسال ۱۱۳۰ھ مکتوبہ ۱۱۳۴ھ قلمی ملکہ محمد
  اقبال مجددی ومطبوعہ لاہور ۱۲۸۱ھ", composed 1130 AH, copied 1134 AH, the manuscript in his own
  colleague's possession and printed at Lahore in 1281 AH (p. 119, folio 107, حاشیہ 2). **This is the
  same error type he caught in chunk 010 on Bahlol Barki's *Fawaid al-Asrar*, and here he shows it
  propagating into a standard reference work.**
- **He reports a work he cannot find.** "اسرار العلیم کے کسی مطبوعہ یا خطی نسخہ کا ہمیں علم نہیں ہے" —
  we know of no printed or manuscript copy of *Asrar al-Alim*, the biography the text says Shaikh
  Abdullah wrote of his pir (p. 119, folio 107, حاشیہ 1). The third such report in three chunks, after
  Abd al-Karim Lahori's two books (chunk 009).
- **Three death years for one Lahore shaykh, and no ruling.** Of Khair Shah Lahori the text gives
  19 Zi al-Hijja 1228; the footnote: "شیخ خیر شاہ لاہوری کا سال وفات نور احمد چشتی نے ۱۲۴۶ھ / ۱۸۳۰ء
  لکھا ہے (تحقیقات چشتی ۳۰۹) اور امام بخش نے ۱۲۲۰ھ درج کیا ہے (حدیقۃ الاسرار ص ۱۲۹)" — **Nur Ahmad
  Chishti wrote 1246 AH / 1830 CE and Imam Bakhsh recorded 1220 AH** (p. 122, folio 110, حاشیہ 1, also
  citing Kanhiya Lal, *Tarikh-e Lahore* p. 9[OCR?]). Twenty-six years of spread and the editor takes no
  side — consistent with his stated rule in chunk 010 that a preference needs contemporary testimony,
  and all three witnesses here are late.
- **The editor's own verdicts on regional history.** Two are given as flat historical claims rather than
  hagiography: **"ملتان میں سلسلہ چشتیہ کو حضرت حافظ محمد جمال ملتانی ف ۱۲۲۶ھ کی بدولت فروغ ہوا"**, the
  Chishtiya flourished in Multan thanks to Hafiz Muhammad Jamal (p. 120, folio 108, حاشیہ 5); and
  **"پنجاب میں سلسلہ چشتیہ نظامیہ فخریہ کی زیادہ اشاعت حضرت خواجہ سلیمان تونسوی کے ذریعہ ہوئی"**, the
  wider propagation of the Chishtiya Nizamiya Fakhriya in the Punjab came through Sulaiman Taunsvi
  (p. 120, folio 108, حاشیہ 6). A third follows on Allah Bakhsh Taunsvi and the nineteenth century
  (p. 124, folio 112, حاشیہ 2).
- **A tribal name behind a saint's name.** The editor's claim that "نور محمد" replaced the ancestral
  **Majheel[OCR?]** of a **Kharral** family, with the paternal line given as Hindal bin Tatar bin Fath
  Muhammad and so on (p. 120, folio 108) — a genealogical argument that places a major Chishti shrine
  in a local lineage rather than a sayyid one, against the sayyid pedigrees the book supplies elsewhere.
- **A theological controversy documented by its refutation's size.** Fakhr-e Jahan's *Fakhr al-Hasan*
  argued for Hasan Basri's bai'at to Ali; Maulana Hasan al-Zaman Hyderabadi's commentary on it, *al-Qaul
  al-Mustahsan fi Fakhr al-Hasan*, runs to about eight hundred pages and was printed at Hyderabad in
  1312 AH (p. 118, folio 106, حاشیہ 1). The editor reports both without adjudicating the question.
- **The author's own judgement on his age**, quoted because it is his voice: the men of God have become
  as rare as the phoenix "ایسے ناپرسان وقت میں", in such an unregarding time (p. 125, folio 113).

## Citable passages

- "ہزاروں مسافر ومسکین وغربا دو وقتہ حضرت کے لنگر سے کھانا کھاتے۔" — of Taunsa: "Thousands of travellers,
  destitute and poor ate two meals a day from the Hazrat's langar." (p. 123, folio 111)
- "ایک لاکھ سے زیادہ حضرت کے مرید ہوئے اور سینکڑوں کفار وفجار نے توبہ کی۔" — "More than one lakh became
  the Hazrat's murids, and hundreds of unbelievers and profligates repented." (p. 123, folio 111)
- "ہر سال میں سترہ عرس … کیا کرتے اور ہر ایک سالینہ میں طعام وافر پکا غربا و مساکین کو تقسیم کرتے۔" —
  "Every year he performed seventeen urs … and at each annual he cooked abundant food and distributed it
  to the poor and destitute." (p. 125, folio 113)
- "بعد وفات ان کے اب شیخ الہ بخش حضرت کے پوتے مسند آرائے مسند مشیخیت ہیں۔" — "After his death, now
  Shaikh Allah Bakhsh, the Hazrat's grandson, adorns the masnad of shaykh-hood." (p. 124, folio 112)
- "حاجی شیخ رمضان لاہوری کا مزار دربار حضرت شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی لاہور میانی صاحب کے قریب ہے۔" — "Haji
  Shaikh Ramzan Lahori's mazar is near the darbar of Hazrat Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Bandagi, Lahore, Miani
  Sahib." (p. 125, folio 113)

## Doubts

- **Sulaiman Taunsvi's death year is given two ways inside one entry.** The prose says 9 Safar, a
  Thursday, in the year **1260** ("ایک ہزار دوسو سٹھ"); the chronogram immediately below reads
  "۱۲۶۷ [OCR?]", and the editor's footnote in the previous entry states 1267 AH (p. 124, folio 112;
  p. 120, folio 108). Two of the three readings agree on 1267, and one of those is OCR-flagged. The
  archive row for Taunsa carries 1850 CE, which corresponds to 1267 AH, not 1260 — so **the entry's
  prose figure is the outlier**, but nothing in this chunk adjudicates it explicitly. Record both.
- **mithankot-kot-mithan — the mention is of the town, not of the shrine.** "قصبہ کوٹ مٹھن" appears once,
  as the place where Qazi Muhammad Aqil taught Sulaiman Taunsvi before 1184–1205 AH (p. 123, folio 111,
  with the editor's note on Muhammad Aqil at p. 120, folio 108). The book says nothing about a shrine
  there, nothing about Khwaja Ghulam Farid, and does not spell the name in a way that excludes another
  Kot Mithan. Use only as evidence that the town was a seat of Chishti teaching in the late eighteenth
  century, and say so.
- **Sharaqpur holds an archive row for a different saint.** Entry no. 74's **Shaikh Muhammad Saeed
  Chishti Sabri Sharaqpuri**, d. 1214 AH, mazar in Sharaqpur (pp. 121–122, folios 109–110), is not the
  **shrine-of-pir-sher-muhammad** row's Mian Pir Sher Muhammad Sharaqpuri (d. 1928, Qadiri). Same qasba,
  different man, different silsila, 114 years apart. **Do not map.** Note that Sharaqpur also appears in
  chunk 008 as the home of Maulana Nusrat Naushahi, owner of a *Thamarat al-Quds* manuscript — the town
  recurs in this book without the archive's saint ever appearing.
- **"شیخ محمد طاہر بندگی" is named without the archive's nisba.** The footnote at p. 125, folio 113 gives
  no order, no dates and no description beyond the location. The mapping to **tahir-bandagi-qadri** rests
  on the name plus "لاہور میانی صاحب", which matches the row's location exactly; it is confident but it
  is all this chunk supports.
- **"باغ مہان سنگھ" here and "باغ مہاران سنگھ[OCR?]" in chunk 009 may be one Lahore garden.** Faiz Bakhsh
  worked his silk in "مہان سنگھ کے باغ" (p. 126, folio 114); chunk 009's footnote buried Shaikh Jan Allah
  in "باغ مہاران سنگھ[OCR?]" (p. 103, folio 91). The flagged form is the earlier one. If they are the
  same place, a Lahore garden held both a grave and a workshop — but the chunks do not say so.
- **"قوم خوجہ" here and "قوم خوجگان [OCR?]" in chunk 009 are probably the same community.** Muhammad
  Saeed Sharaqpuri is "قوم کے خوجہ" who laboured with "اپنے ہم قوم خوجوں" (p. 121, folio 109); chunk 009
  named "قوم خوجگان [OCR?]" as the murids who kept Shah Kaku's urs in Lahore (p. 100). The unflagged
  reading here supports the flagged one there. Still an inference, not a statement of the book's.
- **Fakhr-e Jahan's death year is OCR-flagged in words, not numerals**: "ایک ہزار ایک صد نونوے[OCR?]"
  (p. 118, folio 106), and the chronogram gives no numeral. The editor corrects only the day, to 27
  Jumada al-Akhir, which leaves the year resting on one flagged reading.
- **A khalifa predeceases his pir by the book's own dates.** Nur Muhammad Thani Naruwala is said to have
  died in 1204 AH (p. 120, folio 108, حاشیہ 3) while his pir Nur Muhammad Maharvi died 1205 AH (p. 120,
  folio 108) — possible, but the text calls him the first of the four khalifas "صاحب قرب" without noting
  it.
- **The father's name repeats across two entries again**: Alim Allah Jalandhari's father is **Sayyid Atiq
  Allah** (entry no. 71, p. 118), who is entry no. 66's saint from chunk 010 — consistent; but
  entry no. 71's genealogy runs to Zaid bin Imam Hasan while chunk 010's entry no. 66 says only "سادات
  صحیح النسب جالندھری". Not contradictory, but the two entries carry different amounts of pedigree for
  the same family.
