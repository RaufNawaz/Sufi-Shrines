# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 020 — pp. 205–213

**Entries 140 (tail) to 147 of the fifth chapter: Kashmir, Delhi, Sialkot and a run of Lahore
Shattari and Qadiri shaikhs.** Two archive rows take material — **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak**, named as
the pir who gave Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi his bai'at, and **darbar-abul-muali-qadri**, named as
the man who ordered a commentary written in 1023 AH. The chunk's three most substantial features:
**the author names himself and his own five-generation pedigree** ("مفتی غلام سرور مؤلف کتاب بن مفتی
غلام محمد…"), which settles the book's authorship from inside; **the editor says he does not know
whether *Tashrif al-Shurafa* exists**, the book that supplied Sakhi Sarwar's whole pedigree in
chunk 019; and **eighty thousand rupees are taken from Aurangzeb to build a madrasa in Lahore**.

**Folio markers: all nine run folio = PDF index − 12**, p. 205 → folio 193 through p. 213 → folio 201.
This confirms the relation stated in the job and **exposes the pp. 176–197 markers as errors**: chunk
019's p. 206 equivalent is marked "folio 194" here, and chunk 019 also gave "folio 194" for **p. 196**
— the same printed folio claimed for two pages ten apart. **Folio = PDF − 12 throughout; the notes
for chunks 016–019 need their folios for pp. 176–197 reduced by ten.**

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak** (سید جمال الدین ابو الحسن موسیٰ پاک شہید گیلانی ملتانی — within entry
  no. 141, p. 206, folio 194):
  - **Named as the pir who gave bai'at to Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi**, with his own father named:
    "بیعت خدمت **سید جمال الدین ابو الحسن موسیٰ پاک شہید گیلانی ملتانی** خلف الصدق قدوہ سادات کرام
    زبدہ مشائخ عظام جامع سیادات و نجابت **شیخ سید حامد گیلانی** تھے" — his bai'at was at the service
    of Sayyid Jamal al-Din Abu al-Hasan Musa Pak Shahid Gilani Multani, **the true son of** Shaikh
    Sayyid Hamid Gilani, "قدوہ سادات کرام، زبدہ مشائخ عظام", exemplar of the noble sayyids and cream
    of the great mashaikh (p. 206). **The archive row gives only "Syed Musa Pak Shaheed" and
    "Qadiri"; this chunk supplies the fuller name — Sayyid Jamal al-Din Abu al-Hasan — the nisbas
    Gilani and Multani, and the father's name.**
  - **A terminus for his death, by inference from the chunk's own statements.** The text: "ان کی وفات
    کے بعد پھر یہ شیخ عبد الوہاب خلیفہ شیخ علی متقی کی خدمت میں حاضر ہوئے" — **after his death**,
    Abd al-Haq went on to Shaikh Abd al-Wahhab, khalifa of Shaikh Ali Muttaqi, and received faiz and
    a *khirqa-e tabarruk* (p. 206). The editor's footnote dates that next stage: "شیخ عبد الحق محدث
    دہلویؒ **۹۹۶ھ** میں حجاز مقدس پہنچے اور **۹۹۹ھ** تک وہیں قیام کیا اور تقریباً سارا وقت شیخ عبد
    الوہاب کی خدمت میں گزارا" — he reached the Hijaz in **996 AH** and stayed until **999 AH**,
    passing almost the whole time with Shaikh Abd al-Wahhab (p. 206, حاشیہ 1). **Taken together the
    two statements place Musa Pak's death before 996 AH** — which the archive row, whose `figure_died`
    is empty, has nothing to set against. **This is an inference from two sentences on one page, not
    a statement of the book's, and must be labelled as such.**
  - Nothing here about the Multan shrine, its urs, custodianship or fabric, and no death date, no
    martyrdom account and no silsila label. **The row gains a famous murid and a father, not a
    shrine.** Note that "شہید" stands in his name without explanation.
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** (حضرت شاہ ابو المعالیؒ — within entry no. 141's footnotes, p. 207,
  folio 195):
  - **He commissioned a commentary, and the commission is dated.** Of Abd al-Haq's *Sharh Futuh
    al-Ghaib*: "**فتوح الغیب** حضرت عبد القادر جیلانیؒ کی تصنیف ہے۔ جس کی شرح **حضرت شاہ ابو
    المعالیؒ کے حکم سے ۱۰۲۳ھ میں مکمل کی**۔ **مفتاح فتوح** تاریخی نام ہے۔ **لاہور ۱۲۸۳ھ** اور
    **لکھنؤ ۱۲۹۰ھ** میں شائع ہو چکی ہے" — *Futuh al-Ghaib* is Abd al-Qadir Jilani's composition, and
    its commentary was **completed in 1023 AH by the order of Hazrat Shah Abu al-Ma'ali**; "Miftah-e
    Futuh" is its chronogrammatic name; it has been published at **Lahore in 1283 AH** and **Lucknow
    in 1290 AH** (p. 207, folio 195, حاشیہ 6).
  - **Why this is the archive's row, and on what it rests.** The archive's Shah Abul Muali Qadri of
    Lahore died **16 Rabi ul Awal 1024 AH**; this commission falls in **1023 AH**, the year before,
    and its subject is a work of **Abd al-Qadir Jilani** — a Qadiri shaikh of exactly that standing
    and moment. **The identification is confident on the date and the subject; the chunk gives no
    location and no silsila for him, so see Doubts.**
  - **What the row gains**: a dated act of patronage — a leading Qadiri of Lahore commissioning the
    principal hadith scholar of Mughal India to comment on the founder of his own order — and a
    publication history for the resulting book. **Nothing about the Lahore shrine itself.**
- **Chain and label mentions worth recording, not mapped:**
  - **Abd al-Qadir Jilani (غوث الاعظم محبوب سبحانی)** stands at the head of **three** chains in this
    chunk — Abd al-Haq's Uwaisi nisbat (p. 206), Shah Gada's fourteen-step Shattari silsila (p. 210)
    and Haji Muhammad Saeed's Qadiri chain (p. 213) — and is the author of two of the books discussed
    (*Futuh al-Ghaib*, *Ghunyat al-Talibin*). He is not an archive row; the structural point is that
    **this book roots the Lahore Shattari and Qadiri lineages alike in Baghdad.**
  - **The Shattari chain as this book gives it** (entry no. 144, p. 210): Sayyid Abu Turab ← Shaikh
    Wajih al-Din Gujarati ← Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus Gwaliari ← Shaikh Safur[OCR?] Jami ← Shaikh Abu
    al-Fath, famous as Hidayat Allah Sarmast ← Shaikh Qazan[OCR?] ← Shaikh Abd al-Wahhab ← Shaikh Abd
    al-Rauf ← Shaikh Mahmud ← Shaikh Abd al-Ghaffar ← Shaikh Muhammad ← Abd al-Rahim ← Sayyid Abu Bakr
    Taj al-Din ← **his own father Abd al-Qadir Jilani**. **This is the doctrinal content of the
    compound label "Qadri Shattari" that the archive attaches to
    shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri** — a Shattari line whose root is Qadiri. **But the chunk never names
    Shah Inayat, so nothing enters that row except, if wanted, this chain as the order's own account
    of itself.** See Doubts.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Shaikh Nur al-Din Wali Kashmiri** (entry no. 140, tail, p. 205, folio 193): the entry broken off
  in chunk 019 concludes. **Bibi Lal Ded** (بی بی لل ودی), "جو ایک عورت عارفہ کشمیر میں تھی", who
  was a gnostic woman in Kashmir, also came to his mother and **gave news that God would soon give
  her a son who would be the qutb of the age**. Birth **757**, death **842** AH; "روضہ منورہ ملک
  کشمیر میں زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے". His **six khalifas** who became guides after him: **Baba Zain
  al-Din, Bam al-Din, Latif al-Din, Baba Nasib al-Din, Baba Nasir al-Din and Qiyam al-Din**.
  Chronogram: "چو نور الدین والی والی کشمیر / ز دنیا گشت اندر پردہ مستور // نوشتم طرفہ سال ارتحالش /
  ولی با صفا نور علیٰ نور **۸۴۲**". **Three of the six khalifas are named converts with their
  pre-Islamic names** — see Practices — and one of the six is a chronological impossibility the editor
  catches: see Arguments.
- **Shaikh Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi** (entry no. 141, pp. 205–207, folios 193–195): "یہ بزرگ
  علمائے متبحر دہلوی سے تھے۔ **ان کے ذکر خیر سے کتابیں بھری ہوئی ہیں**", the books are full of good
  mention of him. **An Uwaisi nisbat with Ghaus al-Azam**; bai'at with **Musa Pak Shahid Gilani
  Multani** (see above); after his death, faiz and a khirqa from **Shaikh Abd al-Wahhab**, khalifa of
  **Shaikh Ali Muttaqi**. "خصوصاً علم حدیث و تفسیر میں وہ کمال تھا کہ اپنے وقت میں ثانی نہیں رکھتے
  تھے." Works named in the text: the Arabic and Persian **Sharh Mishkat**, *Sirat al-Mustaqim*,
  ***Akhbar al-Akhyar***, *Sharh Futuh al-Ghaib* and *Jazb al-Qulub ila Diyar al-Mahbub*, "محبوب و
  مقبول خاص و عام ہے". Death **1051 AH** in the text; mazar in **Delhi**. **The editor corrects the
  year and locates and dates the tomb: see Arguments and Practices.** He also dates each work — see
  Arguments — and adds the Hijaz journey of 996–999 AH and the message that sent him home: Shaikh Abd
  al-Wahhab told him **"دہلی واپس جانا چاہیے کیونکہ دہلی تمہاری جدائی میں نالاں ہے"**, you should go
  back to Delhi, for Delhi is lamenting your absence, and picked out students to go with him, all
  **"پاک و ہند کی کایا پلٹنے کے لیے"**, in order to transform Pakistan and India (p. 206, حاشیہ 1,
  citing *Zad al-Muttaqin*, *Akhbar al-Akhyar*, and Khaliq Ahmad Nizami's *Hayat-e Shaikh Abd al-Haq*
  102, 111).
- **Shaikh Abd al-Wahhab Muttaqi** (p. 206, folio 194, حاشیہ 1): "پاک و ہند کے ان عدیم المثال علما بر
  حدیث [OCR?] میں سے تھے۔ جنہوں نے **مکہ معظمہ کے ایک گوشہ میں بیٹھ کر ساری علمی دنیا کو اپنی طرف
  متوجہ کر لیا تھا** اور اپنے علمی تبحر کا سکہ **حجاز، یمن، مصر اور شام** سے منوایا تھا" — one of
  those unmatched hadith scholars of Pakistan and India who, sitting in a corner of Mecca, drew the
  whole learned world to himself and made his mastery current in the Hijaz, Yemen, Egypt and Syria.
- **Shaikh Ali Muttaqi** (p. 206, folio 194, حاشیہ 2): "بن حسام الدین بن عبد الملک بن قاضی خان قادری
  شاذلی مدنی چشتی"; born at **Burhanpur in 885 AH**, died aged **ninety in 975 AH**; "شیخ علی متقی کو
  تصنیف و تالیف سے عشق تھا", he loved composition and kept at it to the end; **Abd al-Haq put the
  number of his works at a hundred** (*Zad al-Muttaqin* folios 8–9). Sources include **Muhammad
  Iqbal Mujaddidi's own article "شیخ علی متقی", in *Risala Sarhad*, March 1974** — **the latest
  imprint in these chunks and the same year as this edition.**
- **Shaikh Abd al-Hakim Sialkoti** (entry no. 142, p. 208, folio 196): "یہ بزرگ **اکابر علما و
  اعاظم فضلائے پنجاب** سے تھا۔ علوم ظاہری و باطنی میں فرید الدہر و وحید العصر"; unrivalled in hadith,
  tafsir and fiqh. Works: **Hashiya Tafsir Baidawi**, "کتاب مشہور و تحشیہ و تکملہ و حاشیہ عبد
  الغفور"; and **he translated Abd al-Qadir Jilani's *Ghunyat al-Talibin* into Persian "حسب الایمائے
  حضرت شاہ بلاول لاہوری کے"**, at the indication of Hazrat **Shah Bilawal Lahori** (see Doubts).
  **"اس بزرگ کو شاہان جغتائی کے دربار میں بڑی توقیر حاصل تھی اور بادشاہ کی اجازت سے اس نے لاہور میں
  درس جاری کیا۔ اس کا لکھا ہوا فتوٰی کل علمائے ہند کو منظور ہوتا تھا"** — he held great honour at the
  court of the **Chaghtai kings**, and **by the emperor's permission he set a teaching circle running
  in Lahore**; his written fatwa was accepted by all the ulama of Hind. **Shaikh Ahmad Mujaddid Alf-e
  Thani** was very kind to him and **titled him "آفتاب پنجاب", Sun of the Punjab**. Death "باقوال
  صحیح", by correct reports, **1068 AH** — and **no grave is given**. The editor adds: he wrote a
  treatise **Dala'il al-Tajdid** in the Mujaddid's support, and Muhammad Hashim Kishmi records their
  **correspondence** (*Zubdat al-Maqamat*); "عہد مغلیہ میں جن چند علما کو **'علامی'** کے لقب سے
  سرفراز کیا گیا۔ ان میں آپ کا نام نامی بھی شامل ہے" — he is among the few ulama honoured in the
  Mughal era with the title *Allami*; and his sons were **Maulvi Abdullah Lahori** ("بڑے نامور علما
  میں سے"), **Maulvi Rahmat Allah** and **Maulvi Rahman Quli** (pp. 208–209, حاشیہ 2–3). Sources
  include a **1969 Panjab University PhD thesis** on him (p. 209) and Muhammad Din Fauq's *Malik
  al-Ulama Sialkoti*, Lahore **1924**.
- **Hakim Sarmad Dehlvi Maqtul** (entry no. 143, p. 209, folio 197): "صاحب جذب و سکر و مستی و
  استغراق". **"پہلے یہودی مشرب تھا۔ کتاب توریت کمال شوق سے پڑھا کرتا۔ من بعد مشرف با اسلام ہوا"** —
  he was **first of the Jewish persuasion** and used to read the Torah with the utmost eagerness;
  afterwards he was honoured with Islam and studied the outward sciences, gaining fame in **Delhi
  [OCR?]** for learning and skill. Then "اچانک حضرت عشق اس کے حال پر متوجہ ہوئے **اور یہ ایک ہندو
  بچہ پر عاشق ہوا**", suddenly Love turned upon him and he fell in love with a **Hindu boy**, and
  remained long in that snare; afterwards, "بحکم **المجاز قنطرۃ الحقیقت**", by the rule that the
  metaphorical is the bridge to the real, he was so absorbed in love of the real Beloved that no room
  for duality remained between lover and beloved; and "یہ بے خود بے ہوش **سر و پا برہنہ مکشوف
  العورت** کبھی بازاروں میں پھرا کرتا اور کبھی ویرانہ جنگل کو نکل جاتا" — beside himself, unconscious,
  **bare of head and foot with his nakedness uncovered**, he would wander the bazaars and sometimes
  go out to the wilderness. **His execution is under Practices.** Killed **1070 AH**; grave in Delhi.
  **The book's only Jewish convert and its only saint executed by a court.**
- **Sayyid Abu Turab, known as Shah Gada Husaini Shattari Lahori** (entry no. 144, pp. 209–210,
  folios 197–198): first lived at **Shiraz**; came to Hindustan "بطلب حق" and completed at **Gujarat**
  under **Shaikh Wajih al-Din Gujarati**; "جب وجیہہ الدین فوت ہو گئے تو **لاہور میں آکر سکونت اختیار
  کی**". A thirty-seven-step pedigree to **Imam Musa Kazim** through "عبد العزیز شیرازی"; and the
  Shattari chain given above. Death **14 Shawwal 1071 AH** — **no grave is given**. **"چھ خلیفہ کامل
  شاہ گدا کے تھے۔ اول قاضی محمد لاہوری دوم شیخ فاضل، سوم شاہ جمال، چہارم لعل گدا، پنجم احمد گدا، ششم
  شہباز گدا"** — Shah Gada's six perfect khalifas: Qazi Muhammad Lahori, Shaikh Fazil, **Shah Jamal**,
  Lal Gada, Ahmad Gada, Shahbaz Gada. **Three of the six carry "Gada" as a shared name-element, which
  is itself a lineage marker.** On Shah Jamal, see Doubts.
- **Khwaja Ayub Qureshi Lahori** (entry no. 145, pp. 210–212, folios 198–200): a man of kashf,
  tasarruf and karamat; works, "**مثنوی مخزن عشق**" and a commentary on **Rumi's Masnavi** "جس کو
  **شرح ایوبی** کہتے ہیں۔ اس کتاب میں حضرت نے حق شرح ادا کر دیا ہے". **He is the author's own
  relation by marriage: see Arguments for the pedigree.** Two reports: a student who could not grasp a
  couplet was told by **Rumi himself in a dream**, "خواجہ ایوب کو **اویسی ہماری روحانیت سے** پہنچا
  ہے۔ جو وہ فرماتے ہیں۔ بیت میں وہی منشا ہمارا ہے" — Khwaja Ayub has reached us **Uwaisi-fashion**
  through our spirituality, and what he says is our own intent in the couplet; and, when he resolved
  to write, he took the *Masnavi* in hand, sought Rumi's permission, opened it and **found at the head
  of the page** the verses "مثنوی ای ضیا را [OCR?] الحق حسام الدین بیا … **مثنوی را شرح با مشروح دہ
  / صورت امثال او را رُوح دہ**", give the Masnavi a commentary along with the commented, give the form
  of its likenesses a spirit — "جب یہ اجازت مولانا سے حاصل ہوئی تو کمر ہمت تحریر شرح پر باندھ لی اور
  **کئی سال میں ختم کی**". A completion chronogram follows: "یافت شرح مثنوی معنوی مولوی / خلعت اتمام
  از لطف خدا // گفت تاریخش بگوش دل شنو / طرفہ شرح مثنوی جان فزا". Death **Thursday 21 Jumada
  al-Thani 1155 AH**; **mazar in Lahore** (no quarter). The editor has seen manuscripts of *Sharh-e
  Ayubi* at the **Panjab University Library** and with **Maulana Sayyid Muhammad Tayyib Hamdani of
  Qasur**, notes that **Mulla Abd al-Khaliq's *Sharh-e Masnavi* (1155 AH) drew heavily on it** in its
  marginalia and quotes Abd al-Khaliq's own deferential note to Khwaja Ayub in Persian (daftar 5,
  folio 30b, ms "حال ملک مولانا عبد الرشید لاہور"), and gives the completion year as **1120 AH**
  against a rival 1111 AH: see Arguments (pp. 210–211, حاشیہ 1, 5).
- **The author's own family** (p. 211, folio 199, and حاشیہ 2–4): **Mufti Ghulam Sarwar**, the author;
  his father **Mufti Ghulam Muhammad**; **Hafiz Rahim Allah**; **Hafiz Rahmat Allah**; **Mufti Hafiz
  Muhammad Taqi** (the fifth ancestor, **d. 1131 AH**, son of **Mufti Kamal al-Din**, "خود لاہور کے
  جید علما میں سے"); **Mufti Muhammad Taqi bin Mufti Muhammad Taqi, d. 1196 AH**, who "اپنی آبائی
  **مسجد مفتیاں** میں قرآن، حدیث و فقہ کا درس دیتے تھے", taught Quran, hadith and fiqh in the
  family's ancestral **Masjid Muftiyan**; and **Mufti Mahmud Alam**, "نبیرۂ مفتی غلام سرور", the
  author's grandson, who wrote the family history ***Zikr-e Jamil* (احوال خاندان مفتی غلام سرور
  لاہوری), Lahore 1968**.
- **Shaikh Fath Shah Shattari Lahori** (entry no. 146, p. 212, folio 200): of **Shah Latif
  Burhanpuri's** khalifas; chain: Fath Shah ← Shah Latif Burhanpuri ← Shaikh Burhan Tarlami[OCR?] ←
  Shaikh Isa Zandol[OCR?] ← Shaikh Wajih al-Din Gujarati ← Shaikh Muhammad Ghaus Gwaliari. **"سات
  برس کی عمر میں پدر عالی قدر نے اس کو شاہ لطیف کی خدمت میں حاضر کیا"** — at **seven years old** his
  father presented him to Shah Latif, and he was trained from childhood; "بسبب غایت جذب و استغراق
  بخطاب **فتح شاہ سرمست** مخاطب ہوا". **"بعد تکمیل لاہور کو مامور ہوا"** — after completion he was
  **posted to Lahore** and spent his whole life there in guidance. **The Ravi flood is under
  Legends.** Death **1150 AH**; mazar in Lahore.
- **Shaikh Haji Muhammad Saeed Lahori** (entry no. 147, pp. 212–213, folios 200–201, breaking off):
  among the elders of Lahore. On the authority of **the author of *Tashrif al-Shurafa***: he received
  the **Qadiri khilafat** from **Sayyid Mahmud bin Sayyid Ali Husaini Kurdawi** and **took bai'at
  from him at Medina**; through **Shaikh Muhammad Ashraf Lahori** his chain meets **Shah Muhammad
  Ghaus Gwaliari**; and **"اجازت سلسلہ نقشبندیہ کی ان کو حافظ سعد اللہ مجددی سے حاصل تھی"**, he held
  the **Naqshbandi ijazat from Hafiz Sa'd Allah Mujaddidi**. His Qadiri chain to Abd al-Qadir Jilani
  runs Haji Muhammad Saeed ← Sayyid Mahmud ← Sayyid Jalal al-Din ← Sayyid Shihab al-Din ← Sayyid Jamal
  al-Din ← Shams al-Din Abu al-Wafa Qadiri ← Sayyid Shihab al-Din Ahmad ← Sayyid Qasim ← Sayyid Abd
  al-Basit ← Sayyid Baha al-Din al-Abbas… — **the entry breaks off here**, and the editor corrects the
  chain twice: see Arguments. **Three orders in one man: Qadiri by khilafat, Shattari by chain,
  Naqshbandi by ijazat.**
- **Shaikh Muhammad Ashraf Lahori** (p. 213, folio 201, حاشیہ 1): son of Shaikh Yunus; murid of
  Shaikh Farid Thani, he of his own father Shaikh Bayazid Thani, he of Shaikh Wajih al-Din Gujarati,
  he of Shah Muhammad Ghaus Gwaliari. "آپ لاہور کے نامور علماء میں سے تھے." **The madrasa grant is
  under Practices.** Works: *Jami al-Fawaid*, "در مسائل تصوف بطور مقدمہ اپنے حالات بھی لکھے ہیں",
  which includes his own life as an introduction, and his malfuzat, "احوال مشائخ کبار", compiled by
  **Sulaiman bin Shaikh Sa'd Allah** — both manuscripts **owned by Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi**. Died
  **1104 AH**.
- **Hafiz Sa'd Allah Wazirabadi** (p. 213, folio 201, حاشیہ 2): **d. 1102 AH [OCR?]**; "کا نام **اسد
  اللہ** بھی معروف ہے۔ معاصر کتب میں **اسد اللہ ہی تحریر ہوا ہے**" — his name is also known as **Asad
  Allah**, and **in contemporary books it is Asad Allah that is written**; murid and khalifa of
  **Shaikh Adam Banuri**; it was he who took **Shaikh Saadi Lahori (d. 1100 AH [OCR?])** to Shaikh
  Adam Banuri; and he had devotion to and correspondence with **Khwaja Saif al-Din bin Khwaja
  Muhammad Masum Sirhindi** (a letter of Khwaja Saif al-Din to Shaikh Asad Allah in *Maktubat-e
  Saifiya*, p. 129). **A direct link back to chunk 012's entry no. 86 (Muhammad Masum).**
- **The six Kashmiri khalifas of Nur al-Din Wali, with three pre-Islamic names** (p. 205, folio 193,
  حاشیہ 1–6): **Baba Zain al-Din Kashmiri**, originally from **Kishtwar**, "**زیا سنگ [OCR?]** نام
  تھا", named Zain al-Din on becoming Muslim, **d. 850 AH**; **Bam al-Din**, whose name before Islam
  was "**لوہر سادی [OCR?]**" and who "**طبقہ برہمن سے نسبی تعلق تھا**", had a lineal connection with
  the **Brahmin class**, repenting at the end of his life, buried in Kashmir; **Baba Latif**, whose
  pre-Islamic name was "**لدی رینہ [OCR?]**", buried in Kashmir; **Baba Nasir al-Din**, whose "صحیح
  نام **نصر الدین** تھا", who attached himself in childhood and was buried **"اپنے مرشد کے جوار
  میں"**, in his murshid's vicinity; and **Baba Qiyam al-Din**, who "بحکم مرشد **پرگنہ دیلہ [OCR?]**
  میں سکونت اختیار کی" and was buried in Kashmir. **Baba Nasib al-Din** is the misplaced sixth: see
  Arguments.
- **Rulers and patrons named**: **Aurangzeb Alamgir** (Sarmad's execution; the 80,000-rupee madrasa

- **Places named**: **Lahore** (Abd al-Hakim's teaching, Shah Gada, Khwaja Ayub, Fath Shah, Haji

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Eighty thousand rupees from an emperor to build a madrasa in Lahore.** Of Shaikh Muhammad Ashraf
  Lahori: **"اورنگ زیب عالمگیر سے اسی ہزار روپے لاہور میں مدرسہ کی تعمیر کے لیے قبول فرما کر بے مثل
  مدرسہ بنایا تھا"** — he **accepted eighty thousand rupees from Aurangzeb Alamgir for the
  construction of a madrasa in Lahore** and built a **peerless madrasa** (*Muhammad Rafi, Qiran
  al-Sa'dain*, qalmi folio 100b, 101r[OCR?]) (p. 213, folio 201, حاشیہ 1). **The largest cash sum in
  these chunks, and the only one attached to a building rather than to income** — against Mauj
  Darya's one-lakh jagir (chunk 017) and Firoz Shah's two thousand tankas to Rajan Qattal (chunk 016).
  The verb is notable: "قبول فرما کر", *having accepted* — the same idiom of reluctant acceptance the
  book uses of Mauj Darya's jagir.
- **A tomb built for a living saint by a named noble, and located.** Of Abd al-Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi:
  **"مزار مبارک دہلی میں حوض شمسی کے کنارے ہے۔ جسے نواب مہابت خاں نے شیخ محدث کی حین حیات بنوایا
  تھا"** — the blessed mazar is in Delhi **on the bank of the Hauz-e Shamsi**, and **Nawab Mahabat
  Khan had it built during the Shaikh Muhaddith's own lifetime** (*Hayat-e Shaikh Abd al-Haq* 151–152)
  (p. 207, folio 195, حاشیہ 3). **A dated patron and a pre-mortem construction — the clearest
  instance in these chunks of a shrine built before its occupant died.**
- **An execution by fatwa, with the emperor's permission sought and given.** Of Sarmad: when the
  ulama of the time learned he was saying **"من خدایم"** openly, **"سب نے باتفاق اس کے قتل کا فتوٰے
  لکھا اور اورنگ زیب عالمگیر کی خدمت میں حاضر ہو کر اس کے قتل کی اجازت چاہی۔ چنانچہ یہ بادشاہ کے حکم
  سے سنہ ایک ہزار ستر ہجری میں قتل ہوا"** — all of them **unanimously wrote a fatwa for his killing**
  and went to Aurangzeb Alamgir to **seek permission** for it; and so **by the emperor's order he was
  killed in 1070 AH** (p. 209, folio 197). **The full procedure — collective fatwa, imperial
  sanction, execution — set down in one sentence, and the book still calls him "یہ بزرگ" and "قدس
  سرہ".** Compare the killing of Nawahon in chunk 016, where a shrine's ruling and an emperor's
  reluctance run the other way.
- **A teaching circle licensed by the emperor.** Abd al-Hakim Sialkoti "بادشاہ کی اجازت سے … لاہور
  میں درس جاری کیا" (p. 208, folio 196) — the state authorising a madrasa rather than funding it, two
  entries before the state funds one.
- **A madrasa inside the author's own family mosque, with its teachers dated.** The **Masjid
  Muftiyan** of Lahore, where Mufti Muhammad Taqi (d. 1196 AH) taught Quran, hadith and fiqh, "اپنی
  آبائی مسجد" (p. 211, folio 199, حاشیہ 3). **This is the institution the book itself comes out of**,
  and the archive should record it as such.
- **A title conferred by a saint on a scholar**: "آفتاب پنجاب", Sun of the Punjab, given to Abd
  al-Hakim Sialkoti by the Mujaddid (p. 208); and an imperial one, **"علامی"**, granted in the Mughal
  era to only a few ulama (p. 208, حاشیہ 3). Two registers of honour, one Sufi and one official, for
  the same man.
- **A river turned back at a governor's petition.** "ایک مرتبہ **دریائے راوی** میں اتنی طغیانی ہوئی
  کہ **شہر کے اندر پانی آگیا**۔ **صوبہ لاہور** نے حضرت کی خدمت میں آکر دعا چاہی" — once there was
  such a flood in the **Ravi** that **water came inside the city**, and the **subah of Lahore** came
  to seek the Hazrat's prayer (p. 212, folio 200). **A dated-by-reign flood reaching inside Lahore,
  and the provincial governor going to a shrine about it.** The prayer's form is under Legends.
- **Three named Hindu converts among one Kashmiri saint's khalifas**, with their pre-Islamic names and
  one with a stated caste — Zia Sing[OCR?] of Kishtwar, Lohar Sadi[OCR?] "طبقہ برہمن سے", and Ladi
  Rena[OCR?] (p. 205, folio 193). **For an archive covering several traditions this is unusually
  direct evidence of conversion inside a shrine lineage**, and it is the editor's material, not the
  author's.
- **Burial in a murshid's vicinity as a recorded practice**: Nasr al-Din "اپنے مرشد کے جوار میں دفن
  ہوئے" (p. 205) — the fourth instance in these chunks of a disciple's grave placed by the pir's,
  after Jan Muhammad's reburial (chunk 018) and the woman convert's rauza beside Shaikh Musa's
  (chunk 016).
- **A print and publication record for one author's works, with presses named**: the Arabic *Lam'at
  al-Tanqih* completed **4 Rajab 1025 AH**, "حافظ عبد الرحمان سلفی نے **لاہور** سے شائع کر دیا ہے";
  the Persian *Ash'at al-Lam'at* completed **1019–1025 AH** "چھ سال کی محنت سے", with six years'
  labour, and "**منشی نولکشور** نے اپنے مطبع سے **چار جلدوں** میں شائع کیا تھا"; *Sharh Futuh
  al-Ghaib* at **Lahore 1283 AH** and **Lucknow 1290 AH**; *Jazb al-Qulub*, a **history of Medina in
  Persian**, begun at Medina **998 AH** and completed at Delhi **1001 AH**, "متعدد مرتبہ چھپ چکی ہے";
  *Akhbar al-Akhyar* completed **999 AH**, with a *takmila* carrying later material, "متعدد مرتبہ
  چھپ چکی ہے" (p. 207, folio 195, حاشیہ 3–6, and حاشیہ 1). **The most detailed bibliographical
  apparatus in these chunks.**
- **Manuscripts located by owner**, several the editor's own: *Zad al-Muttaqin* with **Maulana Ata
  Allah Hanif Bhojiani, Lahore**; *Kalimat al-Sadiqin* with **Professor Qureshi Ahmad Husain,
  Gujrat** (as in chunk 012); *Tazkira-e Naushahi* and *Karamat Nama* with **Sayyid Sharafat
  Naushahi**; *Sharh-e Ayubi* at the **Panjab University Library** and with **Maulana Sayyid Muhammad
  Tayyib Hamdani, Qasur**; Mulla Abd al-Khaliq's *Sharh-e Masnavi* with **Maulana Abd al-Rashid,
  Lahore**; *Jami al-Fawaid*, the *Ahwal-e Mashaikh-e Kibar* and *Zawahir* with **Muhammad Iqbal
  Mujaddidi**; *Nur Nama* in **Maulvi Shams al-Din's collection at the Karachi Museum** (pp. 205–213).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Rumi authorises his own commentator, twice.** In a student's dream: "خواجہ ایوب کو اویسی ہماری
  روحانیت سے پہنچا ہے" — Khwaja Ayub has received from our spirituality **Uwaisi-fashion**, and what
  he says **is our own intent** in the couplet; and by *bibliomancy*, the verse "مثنوی را شرح با
  مشروح دہ" found at the head of the opened page when he asked leave to write (p. 211, folio 199).
  **A commentary's authority established by dream and by opening the book at random** — and the book
  in question is the *Masnavi*.
- **A river ordered out of a city.** Fath Shah Sarmast sent a servant to the Ravi: **"دریا کو جا کر
  کہہ دو کہ جدھر سے آیا ہے۔ چلا جا۔ ورنہ قیامت تک خشک کر دیا جائے گا"** — go and tell the river to go
  back the way it came, or it will be dried up till the Day of Judgement; "جب یہ پیام دریا کو پہنچا۔
  فی الفور شہر سے دور چلا گیا", when the message reached the river it went far from the city at once
  (p. 212, folio 200). **The threat, not the prayer, is the mechanism.**
- **A gnostic woman and the rijal al-ghaib both announce a birth.** Bibi Lal Ded tells Nur al-Din's
  mother "خداوند تعالیٰ عنقریب تیرے گھر ایک ایسا فرزند پیدا کرے گا **جو قطب وقت ہو گا**" — God will
  soon give you a son who will be the **qutb of the age** (p. 205, folio 193).
- **A love of a Hindu boy read as a bridge to God.** Sarmad's passage from "ایک ہندو بچہ پر عاشق
  ہوا" to "معشوق حقیقی کے عشق" under the maxim **"المجاز قنطرۃ الحقیقت"** (p. 209, folio 197). **The
  book states the theological justification in Arabic and does not argue it** — and the outcome is
  still an execution.
- **A saint titled for his absorption** — Fath Shah **Sarmast**, "بسبب غایت جذب و استغراق" (p. 212) —
  and another for his poverty, Shah **Gada** (p. 209), whose name three of his six khalifas inherit.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author names himself, and his own line, inside an entry — the book's self-identification.**
  Of Khwaja Ayub Qureshi: **"سلسلہ عالیہ سہروردیہ میں خواجہ ایوب مرید و شاگرد مفتی حافظ محمد تقی اور
  داماد مفتی حافظ محمد تقی کے تھے اور مفتی محمد تقی پانچویں جد مؤلف کتاب ہذا کے ہیں۔ اس طرح پر کہ
  مفتی غلام سرور مؤلف کتاب بن مفتی غلام محمد بن حافظ رحیم اللہ بن حافظ رحمت اللہ بن مفتی حافظ محمد
  تقی"** — in the exalted Suhrawardi silsila Khwaja Ayub was **murid, student and son-in-law** of
  Mufti Hafiz Muhammad Taqi, **and Mufti Muhammad Taqi is the fifth ancestor of the author of this
  book: namely Mufti Ghulam Sarwar, author of the book, son of Mufti Ghulam Muhammad, son of Hafiz
  Rahim Allah, son of Hafiz Rahmat Allah, son of Mufti Hafiz Muhammad Taqi** (p. 211, folio 199).
  **This settles from inside the text what the editor's "مفتی صاحب" and his *Khazinat al-Asfiya*
  citations have implied throughout chunks 016–019: the author is Mufti Ghulam Sarwar (Lahori), of a
  Lahore family of muftis attached to the Masjid Muftiyan, writing in the Suhrawardi line.** The
  editor adds "اس شجرۂ نسب کی تفصیل کے لیے ملاحظہ ہو **مقدمہ کتاب ہذا**", referring the reader to
  the edition's own introduction (حاشیہ 4). **It also means entry no. 145 is an entry about the
  author's own family shaikh**, which an archive citing it should say.
- **The editor corrects the author's grandson, using the author.** **"مفتی محمود عالم مرحوم نبیرۂ
  مفتی غلام سرور نے خواجہ ایوب قریشی کو مفتی محمد تقی کا فرزند لکھا ہے (ذکر جمیل ۵۵) حالانکہ خود
  مفتی غلام سرور نے انہیں صرف شاگرد و داماد لکھا ہے۔ فرزندی کا کوئی ذکر نہیں (خزینۃ ۳۷۰/۲)"** — Mufti
  Mahmud Alam, **the author's grandson**, wrote Khwaja Ayub as Mufti Muhammad Taqi's **son**, whereas
  Mufti Ghulam Sarwar himself called him only **student and son-in-law**, with no mention of sonship
  (p. 211, حاشیہ 1). And again on the commentary's date: **"شرح ایوبی کا سال تکمیل ۱۱۲۰ھ ہے (خزینۃ
  ۳۷۱/۲) مفتی محمود عالم نے … ۱۱۱۱ھ لکھا ہے (ذکر جمیل ۵۶) جو درست نہیں ہے"** — the completion year is
  **1120 AH**; Mahmud Alam's **1111 AH** is not correct (حاشیہ 5). **Two corrections in one footnote
  block, both defending the author against his own descendant's 1968 family history** — the mirror
  image of the editor's usual posture, and a useful calibration: **he is not simply hostile to the
  author, he is hostile to unsupported statements.**
- **The editor says he does not know whether a source exists — and that source carried Sakhi Sarwar's
  pedigree.** **"کتاب تشریف الشرفا کے مصنف اور اس کے وجود کے بارے میں اس وقت تک ہمیں علم نہیں ہے"** —
  as to the author of *Tashrif al-Shurafa*, **and its existence**, we have no knowledge as yet
  (p. 212, folio 200, حاشیہ 1). In this chunk the book is the authority for Haji Muhammad Saeed's
  Qadiri khilafat and Medina bai'at; **in chunk 019 it was the sole authority for the twenty-one-step
  pedigree of Sakhi Sarwar** ("شجرہ آبائی ان کا **بقول صاحب تشریف الشرفا**", p. 197). **The archive
  must therefore treat Sakhi Sarwar's pedigree in this book as resting on a source the 1974 editor
  could not locate**, exactly as it must treat Shaikh Ismail Muhaddith's dates as resting on the
  *Tuhfat al-Wasilin* the same editor called fabricated and non-extant (chunks 018–019). **Two of the
  book's named sources are, on the edition's own showing, unverifiable — and this is the second.**
- **"یہاں مفتی صاحب سے تسامح ہوا ہے" — the editor's standard formula, used twice here.** Of Abd
  al-Haq's death: **"شیخ محدث کا انتقال ۲۱ ربیع الاول ۱۰۵۲ھ کو ہوا"** — he died **21 Rabi al-Awwal
  1052 AH** (*Hayat-e Shaikh Abd al-Haq* 150–152), against the text's 1051 (p. 207, حاشیہ 2). And of
  Nur al-Din Wali's khalifas: **"بابا نصیب الدین کشمیری ف ۱۰۴۷ھ مصنف نور نامہ، بابا نور الدین ولی
  کشمیری ف ۸۴۲ھ سے بہت بعد کی شخصیت ہیں (ملاحظہ ہو کتاب ہذا کا چمن سلسلہ سہروردیہ)"** — Baba Nasib
  al-Din Kashmiri, **d. 1047 AH**, author of the *Nur Nama*, is **a personality much later than** Baba
  Nur al-Din Wali, **d. 842 AH** — see this book's own **Suhrawardi chapter** (p. 205, حاشیہ 4). **A
  205-year anachronism in the author's own list of khalifas, caught by cross-referencing the book
  against itself** — the Suhrawardi chapter's entry no. 122, which is in chunk 017 (p. 183), where
  Baba Nasib al-Din's death is given as 13 Muharram 1047 AH. **The editor's cross-reference is exact
  and checkable.** Note also that it is the *Nur Nama* — the book the editor relies on for all six
  khalifas' biographies — whose author the text has mistakenly placed among them.
- **Two names restored to a chain and one replaced.** Of Haji Muhammad Saeed's Qadiri silsila: "یہاں
  نقل کرنے میں **مفتی صاحب سے دو نام رہ گئے ہیں**۔ سید محمود بن سید محمد مرید **شیخ عبد الرزاق** و ہو
  مرید **شیخ شرف الدین** و ہو مرید سید جلال الدین" — in copying, **two names have been dropped**; and
  "**یہ نام شیخ شہاب الدین ابی العباس ہے نہ کہ بہاء الدین**" — this name is Shaikh **Shihab al-Din
  Abi al-Abbas**, not Baha al-Din (p. 213, folio 201, حاشیہ 3–4; both from *Qiran al-Sa'dain* folio
  32b). **A silsila corrected from a single manuscript chronicle, twice on one page** — as with
  Khawand Mahmud's pedigree in chunk 012 and Tokhta Tirmizi's in chunk 019, the method is to prefer a
  named source over the author's copy.
- **A name preferred on the authority of contemporary usage.** Of Hafiz Sa'd Allah: "کا نام **اسد
  اللہ** بھی معروف ہے۔ **معاصر کتب میں اسد اللہ ہی تحریر ہوا ہے**" — his name is also known as Asad
  Allah, and **in contemporary books it is Asad Allah that is written** (p. 213, حاشیہ 2). The same
  criterion of contemporaneity that moved Shaikh Musa's death year by thirty-seven years (chunk 016)
  and Shah Daula's by twelve (chunk 017), applied here to spelling.
- **An identification rejected.** "مولانا عبد الحی نے شیخ بہاؤ الدینؒ کو خواجہ بہاؤ الدین نقشبند
  بخاری لکھا ہے … جو درست نہیں ہے" — carried over from chunk 019's p. 204 footnote and still in play
  for the Kashmir khalifas here.
- **A work's existence acknowledged as unknown.** "**صراط المستقیم** کے بارے میں کوئی علم نہیں ہے" —
  nothing is known about *Sirat al-Mustaqim* (p. 207, حاشیہ 4). One of five works the text ascribes to
  Abd al-Haq; the editor documents four in detail and marks the fifth unverifiable. **The pattern
  across these chunks is consistent: the edition's value is that it tells you which of its own
  citations cannot be checked.**
- **A tazkira called the most authoritative of its kind, with its scope and date.** Of *Akhbar
  al-Akhyar*: **"پاک و ہند کے علما و مشائخ کا نہایت ہی مستند تذکرہ ہے"** — a most authoritative
  tazkira of the ulama and mashaikh of Pakistan and India; it covers **from Khwaja Muin al-Din Chishti
  to the mashaikh of his own time**; "ابتدائے حضرت شیخ عبد القادر جیلانیؒ کا ذکر **عقیدتاً** کیا گیا
  ہے", the opening mention of Abd al-Qadir Jilani is made **out of devotion** rather than scope;
  completed **999 AH**, with a *takmila* for later material (p. 207, حاشیہ 5). **The editor
  distinguishing a tazkira's devotional frame from its documentary content** — the same distinction
  the archive needs when citing this book.

## Doubts

- **The folio relation is now confirmed and the earlier notes must be amended.** Nine consecutive
  markers here give folio = PDF − 12, and **"folio 194" is claimed both for p. 196 (chunk 019) and
  for p. 206 (here)** — two pages cannot share a printed folio. **The pp. 176–197 markers are ten too
  high; chunks 016, 017, 018 and 019 all record inflated folios for pages in that range, and
  chunk 017's paragraph asserting a genuine ten-page pagination jump should be struck.**
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri: the identification is mine, not the book's.** The chunk names only
  "حضرت شاہ ابو المعالیؒ" and the year 1023 AH; **it gives no city, no silsila, no dates and no
  shrine**. The match to the archive's Lahore Shah Abul Muali Qadri rests on (a) the year falling
  immediately before the row's death date of 16 Rabi ul Awal 1024 AH and (b) the commissioned work
  being a commentary on Abd al-Qadir Jilani. **That is strong but circumstantial, and a human should
  confirm it before the fact enters the row.** If confirmed it is the row's only dated act besides
  the death.
- **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak: the pre-996 AH death is an inference, and the chunk offers nothing
  else.** The two sentences that yield it are in different registers — the text's "ان کی وفات کے
  بعد" and the editor's dated Hijaz journey — and the text does not say the two events were
  consecutive. **Record the inference as an inference.** Note also that the row's `figure_died` is
  empty and this book does not fill it.
- **"شاہ بلاول لاہوری" must not be mapped to shah-noorani-shrine-syed-bilawal-shah-noorani.** The
  chunk names Shah Bilawal **Lahori** as the man at whose indication Abd al-Hakim Sialkoti translated
  *Ghunyat al-Talibin* (p. 208, folio 196). The archive's row is **Syed Bilawal Shah Noorani** at
  **Lahoot Lamakan, Khuzdar, Balochistan** — a different nisba and a different province, and the
  chunk gives this Shah Bilawal no grave, no dates and no order. **Leave unmapped**, and note the
  shared given name as a likely future mis-match.
- **A fourth Shah Jamal now stands in these notes, and he is Shattari.** Shah Gada's third khalifa is
  "شاہ جمال" (p. 210, folio 198), in a Shattari line, with a pir who died in **1071 AH**. Chunk 017's
  **shah-jamal** is Suhrawardi, of Baha al-Din Zakariya's line, and died **1050 AH**. **Do not attach
  this khalifa to the Ichhra row.** With the Shah Kamal of Kaithal (chunk 012) and the Shah Kamal who
  is Shah Jamal's brother (chunk 017), the book now supports four distinct Jamal/Kamal identifications
  in Lahore and beyond; **all four should be listed together at consolidation.**
- **"محمد عمر چمکنی" may be the figure of shrine-of-mian-umar-baba-chamkani, and the chunk cannot
  decide it.** He is cited as the author of a manuscript *Zawahir* on figures who died around
  1100–1102 AH (p. 213, folio 201, حاشیہ 2). The archive's row is **Mian Muhammad Umar Chamkani**,
  Chamkani, Peshawar, **d. 1776 CE (≈ 1190 AH)**, Naqshbandi — chronologically possible for a writer
  on that period, but **the chunk gives him no dates, no location and no order**, and the folio
  reference is left blank in the transcription ("ظواہر قلمی ورق"). **Leave unmapped and flag as a
  candidate**: if confirmed, it would make an archive shrine's saint one of this edition's sources.
- **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri: the Shattari chain is the order's, not the shrine's.** Shah Gada's
  fourteen-step line (p. 210) supplies the doctrinal content of the archive's "Qadri Shattari" label,
  but **Shah Inayat is nowhere in this chunk** and Shah Gada's own grave is not given. **Nothing may
  enter that row from here except the chain, cited as this book's account of the Shattari silsila.**
- **Four names in the Shattari chain are OCR-flagged or unresolved**: "شیخ صفور [OCR?] جامی", "شیخ
  قاذن [OCR?]" (p. 210); "شیخ برہان طرلمی [OCR?]", "شیخ عیسیٰ زندول [OCR?]" in Fath Shah's chain
  (p. 212). **The chain cannot be published as transcribed.**
- **Abd al-Hakim Sialkoti has no grave in this chunk, and Shah Gada none either.** Two substantial
  Punjab figures with death years (1068 AH, 14 Shawwal 1071 AH) and **no burial place** — and the
  editor supplies none, despite four footnotes on Abd al-Hakim. Worth recording as an absence rather
  than searching elsewhere for it.
- **Sarmad's entry is undated apart from the execution and unsourced throughout.** No footnote, no
  citation, no birth year, and "دہلی [OCR?]" is flagged at the one place his city is named. The
  account of his Jewish origin, the Hindu boy and the naked wandering is **the book's alone** in these
  chunks, and an entry using it must attribute it to the book and note the absence of any editorial
  apparatus — unusual for an execution the editor could easily have dated from a chronicle.
- **Khwaja Ayub's completion date has two values and his relation to the author's ancestor has two
  versions.** 1120 AH (*Khazinat* 2/371, preferred by the editor) against 1111 AH (*Zikr-e Jamil* 56);
  son-in-law and student (the author) against son (the grandson). **Both disputes are between members
  of one family's own literature**, which is worth saying plainly: the book's account of its own
  founder-figure is contested inside the family.
- **The verse quoted as found at the head of the opened page is corrupt.** "مثنوی ای ضیا را [OCR?]
  الحق حسام الدین بیا" (p. 211, folio 199) — the flagged words fall exactly where the *Masnavi*'s own
  opening invocation would stand. **Do not quote the couplet; quote only the second verse and the
  narrative.**
- **Nur al-Din Wali's dates give an unusually long life and the chunk does not remark on it.** Birth
  **757**, death **842** AH (p. 205) — eighty-five lunar years, of which twenty-six were the fast
  described in chunk 019. Consistent, but note that the same page's khalifa list is the one the editor
  finds impossible, so **the entry's internal reliability is already in question.**
- **Three converts' pre-Islamic names are all OCR-flagged**: "زیا سنگ [OCR?]", "لوہر سادی [OCR?]",

  in the footnote is the least legible**, and these names would be the key to placing the men.
- **Hafiz Sa'd Allah's death year and Shaikh Saadi Lahori's are both flagged** — "ف ۱۱۰۲ھ [OCR?]" and
