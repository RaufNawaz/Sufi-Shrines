# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 005 — pp. 58–67

End of entry 19 (Shah Abul Muali) through the opening line of entry 28 (Shah Chiragh). The densest
archive chunk so far: Abul Muali, Tahir Bandagi, Mian Mir, Madho, Shah Chiragh and — via the editor's
correction of a place name — Purana Bhalwal all appear, several with exact dates that match the
archive's rows.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** (عاشق لا ابالی شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی لاہوری — end of entry no. 19,
  p. 58, folio 46):
  - The wells, gardens and tanks he built on the road to Lahore "جو اب تک جو کھائی [OCR?] عبد المعالی
    مشہور ہیں" — which to this day are famous by the name of Abdul Muali (p. 58, folio 46).
  - At Lahore "انہوں نے قبول عظیم حاصل کیا اور ہزاروں مرید و خادم ہوئے" — he found great acceptance
    and had thousands of murids and attendants (p. 58, folio 46).
  - "ادنیٰ کرامت ان کی یہ تھی کہ مریدان کا بیعت کے روز ہی حضرت غوث الاعظم کی زیارت سے مستفید ہو جاتا
    تھا" — his *least* karamat was that a murid would be granted a vision of Ghaus al-Azam on the very
    day of his bai'at; he had a special *nisbat* with Ghaus al-Azam and a devotion to him beyond
    bounds (p. 58, folio 46).
  - **Birth "سال نوسو ساٹھ" with ۹۶۰ in the margin; death "سال ایک ہزار چوبیس" with ۱۰۲۴ in the
    margin** (p. 58, folio 46). The archive row's 1024 AH agrees.
  - His book *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya*, on Ghaus al-Azam's khawariq and karamat, is famous, "اور دیوان
    اشعار ان کی اولاد کے پاس موجود ہے" — and a diwan of his verses is in the possession of his
    descendants (p. 58, folio 46).
  - **The state of the family in the author's own day**: "اولاد ان کی لاہور میں سکونت پذیر ہے مگر
    افسوس کہ ایسے عالم و فاضل و ولی کی اولاد علم سے بے بہرہ ہے" — his descendants reside in Lahore,
    but alas the offspring of such a learned and saintly man are destitute of learning (p. 58,
    folio 46).
  - The editor adds that he was Shaikh Daud Kirmani Shergarhi's **nephew, son-in-law (داماد) and
    khalifa**, and that he made continuous and tireless efforts for the spread of the Qadiri silsila.
    His works are listed as *Tuhfat al-Qadiriya*; *Naghmat-e Daudi*, on the conditions of his own
    murshid, mentioned by Badayuni (Muntakhab al-Tawarikh 288); *Munis-e Jan*; *Zafran Zar*;
    *Guldasta-e Bagh-e Iram* (manuscript owned by Muhammad Iqbal Mujaddidi); and a *Sharh-e Yazdah
    Bai'at* of Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani (same owner). He wrote verse under the *takhallus* "غریبی"
    (p. 58, folio 46, حاشیہ 1).
  - The editor also credits him with prompting Shaikh Abdul Haq Muhaddith Dehlvi's work: "فتوح الغیب
    اور مشکوٰۃ کی شرح انہیں ہی کے اصرار کا نتیجہ ہیں" — the commentaries on Futuh al-Ghaib and on the
    Mishkat are the result of his insistence (p. 58, folio 46, حاشیہ). Nine further sources on him are
    listed, including Nuzhat al-Khawatir 5/32–34 and Zahur al-Din Ahmad's article "شاہ ابو المعالی
    (شاعر)" in Nazr-e Rahman, Lahore 1966.
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri** (شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری قادری و نقشبندی — entry no. 20, pp. 59–60,
  folios 47–48):
  - The entry heading itself gives him **both orders**: "قادری و نقشبندی". Murid and khalifa of Shah
    Sikandar b. Pir Kamal Kaithali; he first completed his training under Shah Sikandar Kaithali, then
    attended "امام ربانی مجدد الف ثانی پیر احمد فاروقی سرہندی", gained great benefits, and came to
    Lahore **at the Mujaddid's direction** to guide the people, where thousands reached high stations
    through him (p. 59, folio 47).
  - "یہ حضرت تمام عمر کسی دولت مند کے پاس نہ گئے اور نہ ان کو اپنے دربار میں بار دیا" — all his life
    he went to no rich man, nor admitted them to his own darbar (p. 59, folio 47).
  - **His livelihood**: "کتب احادیث و تفاسیر کی کتابت حضرت کرتے اور ہدیہ کر کے گزارہ اوقات کرتے" — he
    copied out books of hadith and tafsir and subsisted by presenting them; his nights passed in
    instructing his attendants and in worship (p. 59, folio 47).
  - He was "سلسلہ قادریہ میں … اپنے عہد کے قطب وقت", the Qutb of his age in the Qadiri silsila, and
    "کوئی سائل دین و دنیا کا جو ان کے دروازے پر آیا خالی نہ گیا" — no petitioner of religion or of
    the world who came to his door went away empty (p. 59, folio 47).
  - Sayyid Adam Banuri Mujaddidi Naqshbandi, hearing of his greatness, "پیادہ بنور سے لاہور میں آیا"
    — came on foot from Banur to Lahore and was blessed (p. 59, folio 47). The editor quotes Adam
    Banuri's own Persian account of receiving the *nisbat-e jazba* from Shaikh Tahir Lahori, "کہ
    خلفائی کبار حضرت پیر است" — who is among the great khalifas of Hazrat Pir — and of the nisbat
    having reached Tahir through Shah Iskandar b. Pira Shah Kamal Kaithali (Khulasat al-Maarif vol. 2,
    ms folio 259b) (p. 59, folio 47, حاشیہ 1).
  - **Death: "بروز پنجشنبہ وقت چاشت آٹھویں محرم سال ایک ہزار چالیس ہجری"** — on Thursday, at the
    forenoon hour, the eighth of Muharram, the year one thousand and forty Hijri — "اور عمر چھپن برس
    کی پائی", aged fifty-six (p. 59, folio 47). The archive row's "8 Muharram 1040 AH" agrees to the
    day.
  - **The promise that explains the burials around him**: "حضرت فرماتے تھے کہ میری وفات کے بعد جو شخص
    میرے احاطہ مزار میں مدفون ہو گا۔ میں نے خدا سے مانگا ہے کہ وہ جنتی ہو گا" — he used to say that
    after his death, whoever is buried within the enclosure of his mazar, he has asked God that they
    shall be of paradise (p. 59, folio 47).
  - **Architecture, modern and old**: "موجودہ مزار جناب پیر [illegible] گیلانی مدظلہ کا تعمیر کردہ
    ہے" — the present mazar was built by Pir [illegible] Gilani, who says that when the mazar was
    excavated for the new construction, "قدیم مزار بالکل مزار حضرت مجدد الف ثانی رح کی طرز پر بنا ہوا
    تھا" — the old mazar had been built exactly in the style of the Mujaddid Alf-e Sani's mazar
    (pp. 59–60, folios 47–48, حاشیہ 2).
  - **Location, from entry no. 23**: Shah Muhammad Muqim, travelling to Lahore, "جب گورستان میانی میں
    بجوار مزار شیخ محمد طاہر لاہوری پہنچے" — when he reached the **Miani graveyard, in the vicinity
    of the mazar of Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori** — found Hayat al-Mir in a cell there (p. 64,
    folio 52). The editor calls the place "میانی شریف" (p. 64, folio 52, حاشیہ 1). This matches the
    archive row's Miani Sahib graveyard.
  - Nine further sources are listed, including Zubdat al-Maqamat 340/346, Hazarat al-Quds daftar 2
    288/296, Rauzat al-Qayyumiya rukn 1 326/328 and Tahqiqat-e Chishti 163/171 (p. 60, folio 48). A
    chronogram quatrain closes the entry: "شیخ طاہر پاک باز و پاک دین / اوج عرفان پر تھے مثل آفتاب…"
    (p. 60, folio 48).
- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (شیخ محمد میر المشہور بہ میاں میر قادری لاہوری — entry no. 21, pp. 60–62,
  folios 48–50):
  - Khalifa of Shaikh Khizr Sistani, with the full chain of murids given back through Qutb al-Afaq
    Sayyid Abdul Razzaq to his father Abdul Qadir Gilani (p. 60, folio 48).
  - **Parentage and birthplace**: father Qazi Sain Ditta b. Qazi Qalandar Faruqi, mother Bibi Fatima
    bint Qazi Qadan; "سیوستان میں آپ پیدا ہوئے" — he was born at Sewistan; his father died when he
    was seven (p. 60, folio 48). The editor dates the birth to **957 AH / 1550 CE** on Dara Shikoh's
    authority (Sakinat al-Auliya 95) and glosses the name: he was called Mir Muhammad, with the
    titles Mian Mir and Shah Mir, "میاں مقامی زبان میں صاحب کے معنوں میں", *Mian* meaning "sahib" in
    the local language and used for respect (Sakinat al-Auliya 25) (p. 61, folio 49, حاشیہ).
  - **He took bai'at from his own mother**: at twelve he finished his religious education and "اپنی
    والدہ ماجدہ کی خدمت میں بسلسلہ عالیہ قادریہ بیعت کر کے" took bai'at in the Qadiri silsila at his
    mother's hand, and only later, **with her permission**, went to Shaikh Khizr Sistani for
    completion; after the khirqa of khilafat his pir directed him to Lahore, where he settled (p. 61,
    folio 49).
  - **Austerity**: he never slept at night; his حبس دم [OCR?] was such that a whole night passed in a
    single breath; he broke his fast only after a week; and sometimes in absorption a month at a time
    would pass without his eating (p. 61, folio 49).
  - "حضرت کے خوارق و کرامت کے حال میں شہزادہ دارا شکوہ نے ایک کتاب سکینۃ الاولیا نام لکھی ہے" —
    Prince Dara Shikoh wrote a whole book, *Sakinat al-Auliya*, on his karamat (p. 61, folio 49).
  - **Dates and residence**: birth, on the authority of the author of *Safinat al-Auliya*, "سال نوسو
    ساٹھ" (960); death 1045 AH; age eighty-seven; he came to Lahore at twenty-five and stayed sixty
    years (pp. 61–62, folios 49–50).
  - **Siblings**: four more brothers — Mian Qazi, Qazi Usman, Qazi Tahir, Qazi Muhammad — and two
    sisters, Bibi Badi and **Bibi Jamal Khatun** (p. 62, folio 50), the latter having her own entry in
    the chapter on righteous women (contents no. 200, printed p. 242).
  - "روضہ حضرت کا لاہور میں زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے" — his rauza in Lahore is a place of pilgrimage for
    God's creatures (p. 62, folio 50), followed by a Persian chronogram quatrain, "میر دنیا و دیں
    میاں میر ست…".
  - In Lahore he studied the current sciences with Maulana Saad Allah Lahori and Akhund Nemat Allah
    (p. 61, folio 49, حاشیہ) — the same Saad Allah the editor names as Shah Husain's teacher (p. 56,
    folio 44, chunk 004).
  - Sources listed include Safinat al-Auliya 69–73, Sakinat al-Auliya (Tehran 1965), Abdul Hamid
    Lahori's *Badshah Nama* juz 1 at six separate pages, Kanbo's *Amal-e Salih* 3/357–360, Qani
    Thathawi's *Tuhfat al-Kiram* and Jahangir's *Tuzuk* (p. 61, folio 49).
  - **A neighbouring grave**: Khwaja Bihari's mazar is "متصل روضہ میاں میر رح کے", adjoining Mian
    Mir's rauza (p. 66, folio 54).
- **madho-lal-hussain** (شیخ مادھو لاہوری — entry no. 24, pp. 64–65, folios 52–53):
  - Of Shaikh Husain Lahori's khalifas, "اپنے پیر روشن ضمیر کے محبوب و مطلوب تھے" — he was his pir's
    beloved and desired one (p. 64, folio 52).
  - **Origin**: "قصبہ شاہدرہ میں ان کے باپ ماں بہ قوم کے برہمن تھے رہتے تھے" — his father and mother,
    Brahmins by caste, lived in **qasba Shahdara** (p. 64, folio 52).
  - "حضرت حسین ان کے حسن و جمال پر بدل و جان عاشق ہو گئے اور بجذب محبت اپنی طرف کھینچا" — Hazrat
    Husain fell in love with his beauty with heart and soul and drew him by the attraction of love; so
    Madho too came to him with sincere devotion "اور مشرف بہ اسلام ہوئے", and was honoured with Islam,
    and through training became a perfect wali (pp. 64–65, folios 52–53).
  - **He became the shrine's mujawir**: "جب حسین رح نے وفات پائی۔ ان کے مزار پر مجاور رہے" — when
    Husain died he remained a *mujawir* at his mazar (p. 65, folio 53).
  - **Dates**: birth "نوسو تراسی" with ۹۸۳ in the margin; death "ایک ہزار چھپن" with ۱۰۵۶ in the
    margin; age "تہتر" with ۷۳ in the margin (p. 65, folio 53). The editor sharpens the death to
    **22 Zi al-Hijja 1056 AH / 1646 CE** (Haqiqat al-Fuqara p. 165) "اور اپنے شیخ کے احاطہ میں دفن
    ہوا" — **and he was buried within his shaikh's enclosure** (p. 65, folio 53, حاشیہ 2). That single
    clause is the book's warrant for the archive's joint shrine.
  - The conversion is separately sourced: "میاں مادھو نے اسلام قبول کر لیا تھا" (Haqiqat al-Fuqara
    p. 82) (p. 65, folio 53, حاشیہ 1).
  - **The editor doubts Madho's very historicity**: see Arguments.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** (سید عبد الرزاق المشہور شاہ چراغ قادری لاہوری — entry no. 28,
  p. 66, folio 54): only the heading and the first line fall inside this chunk — "مشائخ قادریہ میں سے
  یہ بزرگ صاحب عبادت و ریاضت و زہد و تقویٰ جامع علوم ظاہری و…", of the Qadiri mashaikh, a man of
  worship, austerity, abstinence and piety, comprehensive in the outer sciences and… The entry
  continues past the chunk. **This settles the identification left open in chunk 001**: the contents'
  "سید عبدالرزاق قادری" at no. 28 is indeed Shah Chiragh.
- **purana-bhalwal** (شاہ سلیمان قادری — entry no. 26, pp. 66–67, folios 54–55):
  - Khalifa of Shah Maruf Chishti. "ان کا باپ منگو قوم موچی موضع بھیلووال [OCR?] میں رہتا تھا" — his
    father Mangu, of the Mochi caste, lived in mauza Bhelowal; **the editor corrects the place name:
    "بھیلووال کی بجائے بھلوال ہونا چاہیے"**, instead of Bhelowal it should be **Bhalwal** (pp. 66–67,
    folios 54–55, and حاشیہ 3).
  - He listened to *sama* and in *wajd* fell so senseless "کہ مرگ کی حالت تک پہنچ جاتے" — that he
    reached the condition of death; thousands entered his bai'at and reached high degrees; his whole
    life was spent guiding students (p. 67, folio 55).
  - Death given by the author as "سال ایک ہزار پینسٹھ" with ۱۰۶۵ in the margin; **the editor rejects
    it**: "آپ ۲۷ رمضان ۱۰۱۲ھ میں فوت ہوئے (عمر بخش: مناقبات نوشاہی قلمی)، ۱۰۶۵ھ غلط ہے" — he died
    27 Ramadan 1012 AH; 1065 AH is wrong (p. 67, folio 55, حاشیہ 1). 1012 AH is the year the archive
    row's 1604 falls in.
  - **The editor also rejects the caste**: Shah Sulaiman Qadri's father was Shaikh Abdullah, known as
    Mian Mangu, of the **Qureshi** caste — "آپ موچی نہیں تھے", he was not a Mochi — with a Qureshi
    genealogy cited to Sharafat Naushahi's *Sharif al-Tawarikh* vol. 1 p. 865 and Umar Bakhsh
    Rasulnagri's *Manaqibat-e Naushahi* (pp. 66–67, folios 54–55, حاشیہ 2).
  - See Legends for Shah Maruf's designation of him as a child.
- **shergarh** (شیخ داؤد کرمانی شیر گڈھی): named again as Shah Abul Muali's uncle, father-in-law and
  pir, and as the subject of Abul Muali's *Naghmat-e Daudi* (p. 58, folio 46, حاشیہ 1).
- **ranmal-sharif** (سلسلہ نوشاہیہ): the two manuscript authorities the editor uses to correct Shah
  Sulaiman's caste and death date are both Naushahi works — Sharafat Naushahi's *Sharif al-Tawarikh*
  and Umar Bakhsh Rasulnagri's *Manaqibat-e Naushahi*, the latter owned by Sayyid Sharafat Naushahi
  (pp. 66–67, folios 54–55). Relevant only as evidence that the Naushahi family's own records were the
  living archive for this line of Shah Maruf's khalifas.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Sayyid Shah Bilawal b. Sayyid Usman b. Sayyid Isa Qadri Lahori** (entry no. 22): took the khirqa of
  *faqr* from Khwaja Shams al-Din Lahori; a wali from birth per *Mahbub al-Wasilin*; exoteric
  education from Maulana Abul Fath Lahori. Died 28 Shaban 1044 AH, aged seventy; "مزار گوہر بار
  دروازہ دہلی شہر لاہور کے باہر ہے" — his mazar is outside **Delhi Gate** of the city of Lahore
  (pp. 62–63, folios 50–51). The editor adds that Shah Jahan and Dara Shikoh used to attend on him,
  and that a risala on him was written while his grandson Shaikh Abdul Ghani b. Shaikh Muhammad Hayat
  was alive, the author reporting some traditions from Abdul Ghani's own mouth (p. 62, folio 50,
  حاشیہ 1). See Practices for the jagir and the langar.
- **Sayyid Muhammad Muqim Muhkam al-Din b. Shah Abul Muali b. Sayyid Muhammad Nur b. Sayyid Bahawal
  Sher Gilani Jarwi** (entry no. 23): murid and khalifa of Sayyid Jamal Allah Hayat al-Mir Zinda Pir;
  left young by his father's death; many karamat. Death given as "سال ایک ہزار پچپن ہجری" (1055) with
  "مزار پر انوار بمقام جرہ زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہے", his radiant mazar at the place **Jarra** a place of
  pilgrimage (p. 64, folio 52). The editor prefers 9 Shawwal 1050 AH from the near-contemporary
  *Tazkira-e Muqimi* (ms p. 23), notes that his work *Durr al-Ajaib* on marifat and tariqat has been
  published "سجادہ نشینان جروہ" — by the sajjada nashins of Jarwa — and that Maulana Abdul Hakim
  Sialkoti came to him to resolve certain passages of the Fusus al-Hikam (Tazkira-e Muqimi ms p. 22)
  (p. 64, folio 52, حاشیہ 3). **This entry's father is a different Shah Abul Muali** — see Doubts.
- **Khwaja Bihari Qadri Lahori** (entry no. 25): khalifa of Mian Mir; after Mian Mir's death
  "انہوں نے ہنگامہ مشیخت گرم کیا" — he made the contest for the shaykhhood hot — and thousands
  reached the ranks of wilayat with him. Died in the year one thousand and sixty Hijri; buried
  adjoining Mian Mir's rauza (pp. 65–66, folios 53–54). The editor places his origin at Hajipur,
  Patna, his early study with Shaikh Jalal Auliya at qasba کودہ پور [OCR?], and his Lahore studies
  with Mulla Fazil, in whose madrasa he first lodged before moving elsewhere (Sakinat al-Auliya 205,
  208) (p. 66, folio 54, حاشیہ 1).
- **Sayyid Jan Muhammad Huzuri b. Shah Nur b. Sayyid Mahmud Huzuri Lahori** (entry no. 27): of
  Lahore's elders; his ancestor Sayyid Mahmud came from Koh-e Ghur and settled at Lahore; trained by
  his father Sayyid Nur and his grandfather, and after their death "مسند ارشاد پر بیٹھے", sat on the
  masnad of guidance; thousands entered his circle. "لاہور میں متصل موضع گڈھی شاہو میں ان کا مزار
  ہے" — his mazar is in Lahore adjoining mauza **Garhi Shahu** — and he died in 1065 AH (p. 67,
  folio 55). See Practices for what became of his family.
- **Shaikh Adam Banuri**, who walked from Banur to Lahore for Shaikh Tahir (p. 59, folio 47).
- **Shah Sikandar b. Pir Kamal Kaithali**, Shaikh Tahir's first pir (p. 59, folio 47), named
  "شاہ اسکندر بن پیرہ شاہ کمال کیتھلی" in the footnote's Persian.
- **Shaikh Khizr Sistani**, Mian Mir's pir (p. 60, folio 48).
- **Hayat al-Mir Zinda Pir / Sayyid Jamal Allah**, found living in a cell at the Miani graveyard,
  Lahore (p. 64, folio 52) — the same figure whose very existence the editor questioned at p. 45,
  folio 33 (chunk 003).
- **Nawab Ghazi Khan**, at whose winter banquet Khwaja Bihari sat in the fire (p. 66, folio 54).
- **Mulla Fazil** and his madrasa at Lahore (p. 66, folio 54).
- **Qasba Sheikhupura**, **Shahdara**, **Garhi Shahu**, **Jarra/Jarwa**, **Bhalwal** and the **Delhi
  Gate** of Lahore all appear as located places, with a named grave or a grant attached to each
  (pp. 62–67, folios 50–55).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A jagir given to a saintly family by an emperor.** Shah Bilawal's forebears "ہمایوں بادشاہ کے
  ساتھ ہرات سے ہند میں آئے۔ بادشاہ نے قصبہ شیخو پورہ ان کی جاگیر میں دیا" — came from Herat to Hind
  with Emperor Humayun, and the emperor gave the qasba of Sheikhupura in their jagir, where they
  settled; Shah Bilawal himself was born there (p. 62, folio 50).
- **A general langar, quantified.** "حضرت نے لنگر عام جاری کیا ہزاروں مسافر، غربا مساکین دو وقتہ
  کھانا آپ کے لنگر سے کھاتے تھے" — Shah Bilawal started a general langar; thousands of travellers,
  poor and needy ate two meals a day from it (p. 63, folio 51). The chunk's only langar.
- **Sajjada nashini in the author's own time, and what the custodians lived on.** Of Sayyid Jan
  Muhammad Huzuri's family: "ان کی اولاد بھی لاہور میں رہتی ہے۔ مگر علم دینی و دنیوی سے بے بہرہ ہے۔
  جلد سازی وغیرہ کاموں پر گذارہ کرتے ہیں۔ مزار پر سجادہ نشین وہی ہیں" — his descendants also live in
  Lahore, but are destitute of religious and worldly learning and subsist on bookbinding and such
  work; they are the sajjada nashins at the mazar (p. 67, folio 55). The same judgement is passed on
  Shah Abul Muali's descendants at p. 58, folio 46. Taken together these are the author's two direct
  nineteenth-century observations of shrine families.
- **Sajjada nashins as publishers.** The sajjada nashins of Jarwa published Sayyid Muhammad Muqim's
  *Durr al-Ajaib* (p. 64, folio 52, حاشیہ 3) — a twentieth-century act of custodianship recorded in
  the apparatus.
- **A saint's livelihood by copying manuscripts.** Shaikh Muhammad Tahir Lahori copied books of hadith
  and tafsir and lived on what came from presenting them, refusing all contact with the wealthy
  (p. 59, folio 47).
- **A promise of paradise attached to burial in a shrine enclosure**, Shaikh Tahir's (p. 59,
  folio 47) — the mechanism by which a graveyard grows around a grave; Madho was likewise buried
  "اپنے شیخ کے احاطہ میں", within his shaikh's enclosure (p. 65, folio 53).
- **Mujawiri** as an office: Madho remained mujawir at Shah Husain's mazar after his death (p. 65,
  folio 53).
- **Contest over succession.** Khwaja Bihari "ہنگامہ مشیخت گرم کیا" after Mian Mir's death (p. 65,
  folio 53) — the third succession struggle recorded so far in this chapter.
- **Shrine rebuilding.** Shaikh Tahir's present tomb is a modern construction by a named Gilani pir,
  over an older tomb found on excavation to have been built in the style of the Mujaddid's mazar at
  Sirhind (pp. 59–60, folios 47–48).
- **Royal attendance.** Shah Jahan and Dara Shikoh attended on Shah Bilawal (p. 62, folio 50); Dara
  Shikoh wrote two books that are the principal sources for Mian Mir and Khwaja Bihari (pp. 61, 66;
  folios 49, 54); and in 1051 AH / 1641 CE Dara asked Khwaja Bihari to pray for deliverance when the
  Shah of Iran meant to attack Qandahar, "چنانچہ ایسا ہی ہوا کہ اسے زہر دے کر مار دیا گیا" — and so
  it came about that he was killed by poison (p. 66, folio 54, حاشیہ 1).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Shah Bilawal, aged seven, raised a neighbour's dead boy of eight: standing by the cot he said
  "اے دوست بے وقت سونا کیا ضروری ہے۔ اٹھ بیٹھ کہ آپس میں کھیلیں" — "O friend, is it needful to sleep
  out of season? Get up and sit, that we may play together" — and "لڑکا فی الفور زندہ ہو گیا اور اٹھ
  کر ان کے ساتھ روانہ ہوا", the boy came alive at once, rose, and went off with him (p. 63, folio 51).
  Attributed by the author to the book *Mahbub al-Wasilin*.
- Khwaja Bihari sat in a lit fire for the space of an hour during a dispute about *tauhid* at Nawab
  Ghazi Khan's house and "ایک بال ان کا نہ جلا", not one hair of his was burnt; he then said that
  tauhid requires "حال درکار ہے نہ قال", a state and not talk, and that a man overcome by state cannot
  be harmed by fire, water, air, earth, lion or snake, all creation being obedient to him.
  Attributed by the author to Dara Shikoh's *Safinat al-Auliya* (p. 66, folio 54).
- Shah Muhammad Muqim's dream: sleeping nightly at his ancestor Bahawal Sher's rauza after zikr, he
  saw him in a dream saying "اے نور العین۔ تیرا حصہ ہمارے پاس نہیں بلکہ حیات المیر زندہ پیر کے پاس
  ہے۔ تم لاہور جاؤ" — "O light of my eye, your portion is not with us but with Hayat al-Mir Zinda Pir;
  go to Lahore" — which sent him to the Miani graveyard (pp. 63–64, folios 51–52).
- Shah Maruf, a guest at Mangu's house, took the child Shah Sulaiman in his arms and told the father
  "یہ لڑکا ہماری امانت ہے۔ اس کی پرورش بخوبی کرنا" — this boy is our trust, bring him up well
  (p. 67, folio 55).
- Shah Abul Muali's "least" karamat: a vision of Ghaus al-Azam granted to every murid on the day of
  his bai'at (p. 58, folio 46).
- Mian Mir's austerities, reported as marvels: a night passed in one breath, a weekly fast-breaking, a
  month at a time without food (p. 61, folio 49).
- Shah Sulaiman's *wajd* reaching the very condition of death (p. 67, folio 55).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor argues that Shaikh Madho may be a late invention.** "میاں مادھو کا ذکر ہمیں فقط حقیقت
  الفقرا ہی میں ملتا ہے" — mention of Mian Madho is found by us only in *Haqiqat al-Fuqara*; it is
  astonishing that in the books composed between 999 AH / 1590 CE and 1096 AH / 1688 CE which do
  mention Shah Husain, "ان میں مادھو کا نام تک درج نہیں ہوا", Madho's name is not even recorded. He
  names five: Badayuni's *Najat al-Rashid* (999 AH), Dara Shikoh's *Hasanat al-Arifin* (1062 AH),
  Abdi's *Akhbar al-Auliya* (1077 AH), Abdul Fattah's *Miftah al-Arifin* (1048 AH) and Abdi's
  *Maarij al-Wilayat* (1096 AH). Dara Shikoh in particular wrote *Hasanat al-Arifin* from exactly the
  point of view that would have made Shah Husain's tale of profane love its choicest *shath*. "لیکن
  پھر اچانک ۱۰۷۱ھ/۱۶۶۰ء میں ایک غیر محتاط اور عقیدت کیش مصنف پیر محمد حقیقت الفقرا کے نام سے شاہ
  حسین کے سوانح کے موضوع پر تمام رطب و یابس جمع کرتا ہے اور اسی کے ذریعہ مادھو کی داستان عشق کو ہوا
  ملتی ہے" — but then suddenly, in 1071 AH / 1660 CE, a careless and devotion-driven author, Pir
  Muhammad, gathers all the sound and the rotten into *Haqiqat al-Fuqara* on Shah Husain's life, and
  it is through that book that Madho's love story catches the wind (p. 65, folio 53, حاشیہ 2). Every
  fact the archive holds about the Madho half of this shrine runs through the one book the editor is
  attacking here.
- **The editor twice overrules the author's dates using a near-contemporary manuscript**: 9 Shawwal
  1050 AH for Muhammad Muqim against the text's 1055 (p. 64, folio 52, حاشیہ 3), and 27 Ramadan
  1012 AH for Shah Sulaiman against the text's 1065 (p. 67, folio 55, حاشیہ 1). The stated ground in
  each case is that *Tazkira-e Muqimi* / *Manaqibat-e Naushahi* is "قریب العہد", near in time.
- **He records a source admitting its own ignorance** — an unusually careful note: Kanbo's *Amal-e
  Salih* 3/370 gives Khwaja Bihari's death year as 1000 AH, but "مطبوعہ نسخوں میں سال وفات کے بعد
  سوالیہ نشان ڈال دیا گیا ہے اور پھر خطی نسخوں میں بھی یک ہزار کے بعد بیاض ہے جس کا مطلب ہے کہ مصنف
  کو صحیح سال وفات کا علم نہیں ہو سکا تھا" — in the printed copies a question mark has been placed
  after the year, and in the manuscript copies there is a blank after "one thousand", which means the
  author could not learn the correct year (p. 66, folio 54, حاشیہ 4).
- **He tests the author's topography against the source and draws a dated inference**: *Tazkira-e
  Muqimi* records the visit to Miani Sharif but says nothing of Shaikh Muhammad Tahir's mazar;
  "اگر مفتی صاحب کی یہ روایت درست ہے تو اس کا یہ مطلب ہو گا کہ شیخ محمد طاہر کی وفات ۱۰۴۰ھ کے بعد
  شاہ جمال اللہ سے بیعت ہوئے تھے" — if the Mufti's report is right, it means the bai'at with Shah
  Jamal Allah took place after Shaikh Muhammad Tahir's death in 1040 AH (p. 64, folio 52, حاشیہ 1).
- The author himself twice records that a saint's descendants had fallen into ignorance (pp. 58, 67;
  folios 46, 55) — an editorial judgement of his own, and the nearest thing in the chunk to
  contemporary social observation.

## Doubts

- **Two different Shah Abul Mualis inside the Qadiri chapter.** Entry no. 19 is Shah Khair al-Din
  Abul Muali Lahori b. Sayyid Rahmat Allah b. Sayyid Fath Allah Kirmani, nephew and son-in-law of
  Daud Kirmani, d. 1024 (pp. 57–58, folios 45–46). Entry no. 23's subject is "سید محمد مقیم محکم
  الدین بن شاہ ابو المعالی بن سید محمد نور بن سید بہاول شیر گیلانی جروی" — son of a Shah Abul Muali
  who belongs to the Hujra/Jarwa Gilani line, not the Kirmani line (p. 63, folio 51). Add to these
  the contents' third Abul Muali, "شاہ ابوالمعالی چشتی" at no. 62 (p. 16, folio 4, chunk 001). The
  archive's *darbar-abul-muali-qadri* row matches entry no. 19 on the name Khair al-Din, the Kirmani
  descent, the Daud Bandagi affiliation and the year 1024 — but the name alone can no longer be used
  to place a citation anywhere in this book.
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri — the silsila question from chunk 004 is answered by the entry heading, not
  resolved.** "قادری و نقشبندی" (p. 59, folio 47) makes him both, and the entry says he took his first
  training from a Qadiri pir and his later benefit from the Mujaddid, calling him "Qutb of his age in
  the Qadiri silsila". A reader must not carry either half alone. "Bandagi" still never appears.
- **madho-lal-hussain — Madho's dates and the evidential problem.** Text: birth 983, death 1056, age
  73 — which does not compute (1056 − 983 = 73 in Hijri years, so the arithmetic is internally
  consistent, but it sits against the editor's finding that Shah Husain died in 999, which would leave
  Madho as mujawir for 57 years). The editor's 22 Zi al-Hijja 1056 AH / 1646 CE and the burial in
  Shah Husain's enclosure both come from *Haqiqat al-Fuqara*, the book he elsewhere calls careless and
  uncritical. Nothing about Madho in this chunk has any source outside it.
- **Contradiction on the date of *Haqiqat al-Fuqara*.** Given as 1041 AH, in Persian verse, in chunk
  004 (p. 54, folio 42, حاشیہ 5, and p. 57, folio 45), and as "۱۰۷۱ھ/۱۶۶۰ء" here (p. 65, folio 53,
  حاشیہ 2). The editor's own argument about the silence of the sources up to 1096 AH depends on which
  date is right.
- **Contradiction on the date of *Tazkira-e Muqimi*.** 1172 AH at p. 45, folio 33 and p. 47, folio 35
  (chunk 003); **1177 AH** here, twice (pp. 63–64, folios 51–52). Both are the same Rawalpindi
  Ganj Bakhsh manuscript by Ahmad Ali Astarabadi.
- **Mian Mir's birth year is given three ways in three lines.** The text has "سال نوسو ساٹھ" (960) on
  the authority of *Safinat al-Auliya*, with death 1045 AH and age 87 — which requires a birth in
  958 — while the editor's footnote gives 957 AH / 1550 CE from *Sakinat al-Auliya* (pp. 61–62,
  folios 49–50). Sixty years at Lahore from the age of twenty-five also implies 85, not 87.
- **Mian Mir's siblings are named differently by text and footnote**: the text's brother "میاں قاضی"
  against the footnote's "قاضی بولن", and the text's sister "بی بی بادی" against the footnote's
  "بی بی باجی" (pp. 61–62, folios 49–50).
- **purana-bhalwal — mapped on the editor's emendation.** The text's place name is بھیلووال [OCR?]
  and only the editor's footnote makes it Bhalwal (p. 67, folio 55, حاشیہ 3). The name "Nuri" and the
  title "Sakhi Badshah" that the archive row carries appear nowhere; the identification rests on
  place + Qadiri + the editor's 1012 AH against the archive's 1604. The author's own 1065 AH, which
  the editor calls wrong, would put him sixty years later.
- **A caste attribution the editor denies.** The author makes Shah Sulaiman's father a Mochi; the
  editor makes him a Qureshi and says flatly that he was not a Mochi (pp. 66–67, folios 54–55). Both
  readings are in the book; neither should be tidied away.
- **Shah Bilawal Lahori is again not Bilawal Shah Noorani.** His mazar is outside Delhi Gate, Lahore,
  his family's jagir was Sheikhupura, and he died 1044 AH (pp. 62–63, folios 50–51). Nothing places
  him in Balochistan.

  only to a Gilani pir living at the time of the 1974 introduction.
