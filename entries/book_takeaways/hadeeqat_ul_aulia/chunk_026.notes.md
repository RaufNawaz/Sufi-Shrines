# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 026 — pp. 266–276

**Thirty short entries of undated and lightly dated Punjab saints, overwhelmingly Lahore, plus the
editor's two sharpest date disputes in the book so far.** The chunk opens on the tail of the Sultan
Bahu footnote carried over from chunk 025 — "(بقیہ حاشیہ)" (p. 266, folio 254) — and then runs
entries **۲۱۴–۲۴۳** continuously, the last one broken off mid-sentence. The material is dominated by
**Lahore graves keyed to city landmarks** (Anarkali bazar, Mochi Gate, Bhati Gate, Delhi Gate,
Roshnai Gate, Wazir Khan mosque's south wall, "ریل کے پڑاؤ", "بدھو کے پڑاوے", mauza Mozang), and by
three recurring institutional themes the archive can use directly: **Sikh-era demolition followed by
murid-funded rebuilding** (three separate cases, pp. 267, 271), **nazr as a named household income**
(Raja Dina Nath's thousands to a majzub's son; a Multani family "مریدوں سے نذر لے کر گذارہ کرتے ہیں"),
and **hereditary office** (a Wazir Khan mosque imamate across three named generations; two competing
statements of who holds the sajjada of Shaikh Tahir Bandagi). Eleven archive rows take material,
none of them as a subject entry — every one appears as a **pir in someone else's chain, a landmark in
someone else's address, or a footnote's source**. The most substantial single items: **two editor's
footnotes that reject the author's dates outright**, one of them by name against the author's own
earlier book; **a Sunday healing ritual at a named Lahore well, running "اس روز سے آج تک"**; **a
double-burial tomb with real graves in a crypt and imitation graves above, stripped of its stone by
Ranjit Singh**; and **a Qadam Rasul whose inscription the author copied himself and whose present
keepers he accuses of "کمال بے ادبی"**.

**Folio markers: all eleven pages run folio = PDF index − 12**, p. 266 → folio 254 through p. 276 →
folio 264; **no break**. The chunk **begins mid-footnote** (the continuation of chunk 025's Sultan
Bahu source list) but cleanly at entry heading ۲۱۴, and **ends mid-entry and mid-sentence** at ۲۴۳,
"صاحب شریعت و طریقت و علم و فضل" (p. 276, folio 264) — Mufti Shaikh Muhammad Mukarram Qureshi
continues into chunk 027.

    (p. 266, folio 254) — an Urdu translation of *Manaqib Sultani* printed at Lahore, and Bilal
    Zubairi, *Tazkira Auliya-e-Jhang*, pp. 114–126. Publication years: (not stated).
  - **What the row gains**: **two further modern citations for Sultan Bahu**, completing the
    three-item list the editor began at (۱) *Sultan Hamid: Manaqib Sultani* (chunk 025, p. 265) — a
    printed Urdu translation and a page range in a twentieth-century Jhang tazkira. Nothing new about

- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** (رنجیت سنگھ — one act of despoliation, p. 267, folio 255):
  - **He had the stone stripped from a Lahore tomb.** Of Pir Hadi Rahnuma's maqbara: "پہلے اس مقبرہ کی
    عمارت **سنگین** تھی۔ **سکھوں کے وقت میں رنجیت سنگھ نے اس کا پتھر اکھڑوالیا**" — formerly the
    building was of stone; in the Sikhs' time Ranjit Singh had its stone torn out (p. 267, folio 255).
    Year: (not stated).
  - **What the row gains**: **nothing about the samadhi, Hazuri Bagh or his death** — one more
    datum for the book's portrait of him as a despoiler of Muslim funerary buildings, this time a
    named tomb outside Lahore on the Anarkali–Mian Mir road, and the reason its fabric changed. It
    pairs with chunk 025's relic-confiscation material. His successors **کھڑک سنگھ و نونہال سنگھ**
    appear separately (p. 271) — see Practices and Legends.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (میاں میر — as a road's destination and as a Qadiri pir, pp. 267 and 270):
  - **A Lahore road named for the shrine.** Pir Hadi Rahnuma's maqbara stands "لاہور کے باہر **لب سڑک
    جو میاں میر کو انارکلی سے جاتی ہے**" — outside Lahore, on the edge of the road that goes from
    Anarkali to Mian Mir (p. 267, folio 255). **The shrine is used as the terminus by which another
    grave is located**, with no description of the shrine itself.
  - **Named as a Qadiri initiator of Lahore.** Pir Burhan, who came from Bukhara in Akbar's time,
    "لاہور کے بزرگوں **میاں میر و شاہ بلاول سے قادریہ فیض پایا**" — obtained Qadiri fayz from the
    saints of Lahore, Mian Mir and Shah Bilawal (p. 270, folio 258).
  - **What the row gains**: (1) the book's **testimony that the shrine gave its name to a Lahore road
    running from Anarkali** in the author's present, which is a locational and urban-history claim the
    row can cite; (2) **independent confirmation of the row's Qadiri affiliation** from outside the
    saint's own entry — he is one of two Lahore figures a Bukharan immigrant took Qadiri fayz from,
    paired with "شاہ بلاول"; (3) the form of the name the book uses is the bare "میاں میر", not the
    row's "Mir Mohammad of Sind". **Nothing on his death year, tomb fabric, urs or custodians.** On
    "شاہ بلاول" see Doubts.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** (حضرت شاہ چراغ گیلانی لاہوری — as a pir, p. 267, folio 255):
  - **A named murid.** Entry ۲۲۰: "یہ بزرگ **حضرت شاہ چراغ گیلانی لاہوری کے مرید** صاحب کشف و کرامات

  - **What the row gains**: the row's saint named **"گیلانی لاہوری"** — a nisba pair the row's own
    fields do not carry — and **a named disciple, Shah Dargahi Qadri**, whose grave the book locates
    beside that of Shah Ismail Muhaddith and whose well-cure became a standing Lahore ritual (see
    Practices). It establishes Shah Chiragh as a pir with a transmitting line in Lahore, not only as a
    grave. **Death year, tomb and urs: (not stated) here.** **A name collision with entry ۲۳۸'s
    "چراغ شاہ" outside Roshnai Gate, and with entry ۲۲۵'s "شاہ عبدالرزاق" — see Doubts.**
- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari** (حضرت میراں محمد شاہ موج دریا سہروردی لاہوری — as a pir, p. 269,

  - **What the row gains**: **exact corroboration of the row's principal figure and silsila in the

    year, tomb, urs, custodians: (not stated) here. The disciple's own death year is given as "**سنہ
    ایک ہزار چوراسی**" (p. 269, folio 257) — era unstated; see Doubts.
- **data-darbar** (علی مخدوم گنج بخش ہجویری — as the city's locational anchor, p. 270, folio 258):
  - **A grave sited by reference to it.** Entry ۲۲۷: "**علی مخدوم گنج بخش ہجویری کے پرلے طرف** یہ مزار
    **بلند چبوترے پر** ہے" — beyond Ali Makhdum Ganj Bakhsh Hujwiri, this mazar stands on a high
    plinth (p. 270, folio 258). The figure so sited is **پیر غازی المشہور بہ پیر از غیب**.
  - **A Mughal princess building in the vicinity.** "مشہور اس طرح پر ہے کہ **زیب النسا، شہزادی** یہاں
    کوئی عمارت بناتی تھی" — it is said that the princess Zeb al-Nisa was putting up some building here
    (p. 270, folio 258); the wall that kept collapsing is under Legends.
  - **What the row gains**: (1) **the form of the name the book uses — "علی مخدوم گنج بخش ہجویری"** —
    without "Data Darbar", which the chunk does not use; (2) the shrine functioning as the reference
    point from which another Lahore grave is placed, i.e. **evidence of its standing as the city's
    known landmark in 1875**; (3) a report of **Zeb al-Nisa building in its immediate surroundings**,

    in the author's own framing. **Nothing on Data Darbar's own fabric, urs, langar or custodians.**

    [OCR?] بلاول حضرت لال حسین لاہوری کے خلیفہ** کی خدمت میں تھی" — his allegiance was to Shah
    Rang[OCR?] Bilawal, khalifa of Hazrat Lal Husain Lahori (p. 271, folio 259).
  - **A pir-brother relationship, with the shared pir named.** Entry ۲۳۳, of Shaikh Musa Khokhar:

    ادب کرتے تھے**" — fayz reached him from Shaikh Bahlol Daryai; Shaikh Lal Husain Lahori showed him
    the utmost respect because they were pir-brothers (p. 273, folio 261).
  - **What the row gains**: (1) **the book's names for him — "حضرت لال حسین لاہوری" and "شیخ لال حسین

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی — as an ancestor, p. 271,

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A well, a saint's prescription, and a Sunday ritual still running at the time of writing.** At Shah
  Dargahi Qadri's mazar: "ایک چاہ جس کو لوگ **پانی والیاں والا چاہ** کہتے ہیں حضرت کے مزار کے **جنوب**
  کی طرف ہے۔ **اس چاہ کے زمیندار حضرت کے مرید تھے**"; the zamindar's son broke out in boils "جس کو
  **زبان پنجابی کی اصطلاح میں پانی واستے [OCR?]** کہتے ہیں"; the saint said "**اس مرض کے واسطے تیرے چاہ
  کا پانی دوا ہے**۔ اس کو اس چاہ کے پانی سے نہلا دو"; and then "**اس روز سے آج تک اتوار کے روز لوگ اپنے
  بچوں کو جن کو یہ مرض ہوتا ہے اس چاہ پر لے جا کر نہلاتے ہیں**۔ **خدا کے حکم سے شفا ہو جاتی ہے**۔ **بعض
  اس چاہ کے حوض سے ٹھیکری لے جاتے ہیں اور گھس کر پھوڑے پر لگاتے ہیں**" (p. 268, folio 256). **A dated
  weekday, a named well, a named Punjabi disease term, a specific ailment, two techniques (bathing and
  grinding a potsherd from the tank), and an unbroken continuity claim — the fullest single healing
  practice in the chunk, and the well is not at the shrine but south of it, on land whose owners were
  murids.**
- **A pebble taken and returned, with misri distributed by weight.** At Pir az Ghaib's mazar: "**جس کے
  گلے میں ورم پڑ جائے**۔ وہ یہاں سے **ایک سنگریزہ اٹھا لے جاتا ہے** اور اس ورم پر **پھیرتا ہے**۔ **جب
  شفا ہو جاتی ہے تو اس [OCR?] ٹھیکرے کے برابر مصری وزن کر کے بانٹ دیتا ہے اور ڈھیلا پھر یہاں ہی چھوڑ
  جاتا ہے**" — for a swelling of the throat one carries off a pebble and passes it over the swelling;
  on recovery one weighs out an equal weight of *misri* and distributes it, and leaves the lump back
  here (p. 270, folio 258). **An offering calibrated to the weight of the object borrowed, and the
  object returned to circulation — a closed-loop votive economy stated in one sentence.**
- **A double-grave tomb with a crypt, and its stone stripped.** Pir Hadi Rahnuma's maqbara: "**بہت
  پرانا پختہ مسقف** بنا ہوا ہے جس میں **ایک تو ان کی قبر اور دوسری اور تیسری محسن شاہ اور عبداللہ شاہ ان

  سکھوں کے وقت میں **رنجیت سنگھ نے اس کا پتھر اکھڑوالیا** اور **قبریں دوہری ہیں یعنی اصلی قبریں تو تہ
  خانے میں ہیں اور اوپر نقلی قبریں بنائی ہوئی ہیں**" (p. 267, folio 255). **A stated construction era,
  a stated change of fabric with a named agent, three brothers in one chamber, and an explicit
  real-grave/imitation-grave arrangement with the real burials in a crypt.**

  بیٹے** نے چاہا کہ **لاہور کے باہر دور دور مکانات صاف کر کے میدان بناویں**۔ اس وقت **یہ مکان بھی گرا
  دیا گیا**"; after their deaths "**وہ تجویز موقوف رہی تو اعتقاد مند لوگوں نے پھر یہ مزار تعمیر کر

  two pages.**
- **Three abandoned or unfrequented sites in the author's present.** The Qadam Rasul enclosure "**غیر
  آباد پڑا ہے**" (p. 271, folio 259); Shaikh Muhtaram's rauza "**اب غیر آباد ہے**" (p. 273, folio 261);
  Muhammad Fazil's mosque "**اگرچہ قائم نہیں مگر نشان باقی ہیں**" (p. 267, folio 255). **The book records
  decay as plainly as devotion.**
- **A precinct of many graves on shared platforms.** The Takiya Ambli Wala enclosure: two chahar-diwaris
  and three "**چبوترے پختہ چونہ گچ**", with named occupants distributed across them and one figure in
  "**ایک علیحدہ مکان**" (pp. 274–275, folios 262–263). **A family necropolis rather than a shrine, and
  the book's own cross-reference sends the reader to an earlier chaman for the ancestor.**
- **Graves keyed to colonial-era infrastructure.** "**ریل کے پڑاؤ کے شمال**" (p. 271, folio 259),
  "**بدھو کے پڑاوے کے غرب**" (p. 273, folio 261), "**لاٹ صاحب کی کوٹھی کے جنوب**" (p. 267, folio 255),
  "**محکمہ جمی [OCR?] کے روبرو**" (p. 273, folio 261). **The railway halt and the Lieutenant-Governor's
  house are now the landmarks by which Lahore's saints' graves are located.**
- **A chronogram inside a dome as the only record of a death date.** Shaikh Muhtaram: "**اندر گنبد کے
  … قطعہ تاریخ وفات لکھا ہے**" (p. 273, folio 261), and the qita is printed but not resolved to a year.
- **A seal-engraver in a custodial sayyid family.** "**بہادر بن چراغ شاہ جو مہر کنی کا کام کرتا ہے**"
  (p. 274, folio 262) — a trade recorded for a descendant of the line holding the Tahir Bandagi sajjada.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A pebble that cures a swollen throat**, at Pir az Ghaib's mazar, told as the shrine's known
  karamat: "**کرامت اس کی یہ مشہور ہے** کہ جس کے گلے میں ورم پڑ جائے …" (p. 270, folio 258).
- **A wall that would not stand until an old grave was found beneath it.** Of Pir az Ghaib: "**مشہور اس
  طرح پر ہے** کہ زیب النسا، شہزادی یہاں کوئی عمارت بناتی تھی۔ **خدا کی قدرت سے اس مقام کی دیوار گر جاتی
  تھی**۔ جب زمین کھدوائی تو **ایک پرانی قبر وہاں سے نکلی** اس نے اس کو **بلند کر کے بنا دیا**" — by
  God's power the wall kept falling; when the ground was dug an old grave came out, and she had it
  raised up and built (p. 270, folio 258). **The shrine's origin story is a construction failure.**
- **Well water prescribed, and an instant cure.** Shah Dargahi Qadri: "چنانچہ اس نے نہلایا تو **لڑکا فی
  الفور اچھا ہو گیا**" (p. 268, folio 256), as reported by the author without a source.
- **A father and son dying on one day after a demolition.** Pir Burhan: "**بعد گرانے کے خدا کی قدرت سے
  کھڑک سنگھ و نونہال سنگھ باپ بیٹے ایک روز مر گئے** اور وہ تجویز موقوف رہی" — after the demolition, by
  God's power Kharak Singh and Nau Nihal Singh, father and son, died on one day, and the scheme lapsed
  (p. 271, folio 259). **A political death told as retribution, in the author's own voice — the chunk's
  sharpest instance of that.**
- **A shrine built in one night, and attributed to angels.** Shah Rahmatullah Qureshi: "**یہ مزار
  فرشتوں کا مزار کہلاتا ہے**۔ اس سبب سے کہ **اس گھر کے معمار مرید بہت تھے**۔ جب شاہ رحمۃ اللہ فوت ہو
  گئے تو **دن کو ان کی کار بسرکار شاہی سے فراغت نہیں ہوتی تھی**۔ **رات کو انہوں نے جمع ہو کر ایک رات میں
  مزار کو تعمیر کر دیا** اور **مشہور ہوا کہ رات کو فرشتے بنا گئے ہیں**" — the masons were many of them
  murids, and by day their royal-service work left them no leisure, so they gathered at night and built
  the mazar in one night, and it became famous that angels had built it by night (p. 271, folio 259).
  **The author gives the miracle and its mundane explanation in the same sentence, and keeps both.**
- **Hands emerging from a grave to shake hands with visitors, until stopped by an act of disrespect.**
  Shah Gardez Multani: "**بعد وفات اظہر کرامت ان کی یہ تھی** کہ جو **طالب زیارت** مزار فیض آثار پر حاضر
  ہوتا **حضرت کے دونوں دست مبارک** اس راستے سے **جو حسب الوصیت قبر کے تعویذ میں رکھا گیا تھا**۔ **قبر سے
  باہر آتے اور طالب کے ساتھ مصافحہ ہوتا**۔ **یہ کرامت مدت مدید تک جاری رہی**۔ آخر **ایک شخص نالائق نے جو
  کوئی ایسی بے ادبی کی تو دست مبارک کا باہر آنا موقوف رہا**" (p. 276, folio 264). **A miracle with a
  built mechanism, a long duration, and a recorded end — the second time in the chunk a practice ends
  through "بے ادبی" (cf. the Qadam Rasul).**
- **Kashf and karamat on the common tongue.** Shaikh Fath Shah Amritsari: "**کشف و کرامات اکثر خلقت کے
  زبان زد ہیں**" (p. 266, folio 254) — attributed to popular report, with no incident given.
- **A pull felt without warning.** Shah Farid Naushahi: "**ناگاہ جاذب حقیقی نے اس کو اپنی طرف کھینچا**"
  (p. 274, folio 262).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor declares one of the author's dates simply wrong, and names who to believe instead.** "**شاہ
  کنٹھہ نوشاہی کا سال وفات ۱۲۱۹ھ غلط ہے۔ نور احمد چشتی نے ۱۱۱۹ھ دیا ہے جو سید شرافت صاحب کے نزدیک درست
  ہے**۔ ملاحظہ ہو" — the death year 1219 AH is wrong; Nur Ahmad Chishti gave 1119 AH, which Sayyid
  Sharafat holds correct — followed by four citations: "**(۱) نور احمد چشتی ، تحقیقات چشتی ص ۳۴۰**",
  "**(۲) فوق لاہوری ، یاد رفتگاں ص ۹۳**", "**(۳) عبدالحی صدیقی ، تاریخ احسن یا تذکرۃ الصحابہ ص ۲۷**",
  "**(۴) شرافت نوشاہی : شریف التواریخ جلد سوم حصہ اول ۱۱۳/۴۱۶ [OCR?] قلمی**" (p. 273, folio 261,
  حاشیہ). **A hundred-year correction — 1219 → 1119 — supported by four named works, one of them a
  manuscript ("قلمی"). For the archive, the printed year in the book's own text is not usable without
  this footnote beside it.**
- **The editor turns the author's own earlier book against him, and then undercuts that too.** "**مفتی
  [OCR?] صاحب نے شاہ فرید لاہوری کا سال وفات خزینۃ الاصفیا ۱/۲۰۵ میں بحوالہ تذکرہ نوشاہی ۱۱۵۸ھ لکھا
  ہے**۔ **بقول صاحب شریف التواریخ تذکرہ نوشاہی میں شاہ فرید نوشاہی کا نام تک نہیں آیا**۔ **چہ جائیکہ سال
  وفات مذکور ہو**۔ ملاحظہ ہو : **شرافت نوشاہی : شریف التواریخ جلد سوم حصہ دوم ۳۱۵ - ۳۲۲ قلمی**" — the
  Mufti[OCR?] wrote the death year 1158 AH in *Khazinat al-Asfiya* 1/205 on the authority of *Tazkira
  Naushahi*; but according to the author of *Sharif al-Tawarikh*, *Tazkira Naushahi* does not even
  contain Shah Farid Naushahi's name, let alone his death year (p. 274, folio 262, حاشیہ ۱). **Three
  layers: the author's other book, the source it claimed, and a third scholar denying the source says
  any such thing. Note that this footnote's year, 1158 AH, is supplied for a saint the author's text
  here leaves undated.**
- **The editor rejects a printed genealogy outright and substitutes his own.** "**یہاں شجرہ نسب درست
  نہیں ہے۔ بلکہ یوں ہونا چاہیے**" (p. 274, folio 262, حاشیہ ۲), followed by an eleven-name chain that
  **reverses the direction of several of the author's links** — the author has سید حمید → سید سعید →
  سید شاہ حسین → سید زندہ علی → سید شہسوار, the editor **بن سید سعید بن سید حمید … بن شاہ حسین بن سید
  زندہ علی بن سید شاہ سوار**. **A named descent line is contested in the archive's own source, which
  matters because the line ends in a shrine's custodianship.**
- **The author repeatedly records that he does not know.** "**اس بزرگ کا اصلی حال کچھ ظاہر نہیں ہوتا**"
  (Pir az Ghaib, p. 270, folio 258); "**معلوم نہیں ہوتا کہ کس خاندان کا یہ بزرگ تھا**" (Shaikh
  Muhtaram, p. 273, folio 261); "**اور ان حضرت حال معلوم نہیں ہوا**" (Marg Naini, p. 275, folio 263).
  **Three flat admissions of ignorance in a section whose whole premise is unproven dates, consistent
  with the section heading quoted in chunk 025.**
- **The author distinguishes hearsay from statement.** "**مشہور اس طرح پر ہے**" for the Zeb al-Nisa
  story (p. 270, folio 258); "**مشہور ہوا کہ رات کو فرشتے بنا گئے ہیں**" with the masons' explanation
  given first (p. 271, folio 259); "**کرامت اس کی یہ مشہور ہے**" (p. 270). **Where he can name a human
  cause he names it and still records the rumour.**
- **The author writes as a first-hand witness three times in eleven pages.** He met the Delhi sajjada
  nashin — "**جن کی زیارت سے مؤلف کتاب بھی بہرہ یاب ہوا ہے**" (p. 269, folio 257); he knows a
  descendant of Shah Rahmatullah — "**بہادر شاہ نام مؤلف کتاب سے بھی واقفیت رکھتا ہے**" (p. 271, folio
  259); and he copied the Qadam Rasul inscription himself — "**مؤلف کتاب ہذا نے خود اس کی نقل اپنے قلم
  سے کی تھی**" (p. 271, folio 259). **The third is the strongest provenance claim in the chunk: a text
  transcribed by the author from a surface whose lettering was already partly lost ("حروف اڑ گئے
  تھے").**
- **The author dates his own writing.** Of Murad Shah: "**اس سال ۱۲۹۲ھ میں فوت ہوا ہے**" — he died this
  year, 1292 AH (p. 276, folio 264). **The one internal anchor for when this part of the book was being
  written, in the author's own words and in figures.** Fazl Shah's "**پچیس برس کا عرصہ ہوا ہے**" (p.
  272, folio 260) is relative to the same present.
- **The author's editorial anger is reserved for neglect, not for demolition.** The Sikh demolitions are
  reported flatly; the whitewashing of the Qadam Rasul dome draws "**مگر افسوس**" and "**کمال بے
  ادبی**" (p. 272, folio 260). **His complaint is against unnamed present-day custodians, not against
  the Sikh state.**
- **Two cross-references to an earlier chaman.** "**جن کا ذکر خیر پہلے چمن میں مذکور ہو چکا ہے**" (p.
  274, folio 262) and "**جن کا ذکر خیر پہلے چمن میں تحریر ہو چکا ہے**" (p. 275, folio 263). **The book
  expects the reader to hold the Gilani sayyids of Uch and Shah Qamis Qadri Sawhawi[OCR?] from earlier
  chapters — both are entry-points for consolidation across chunks.**

## Doubts

- **Shah Gardez Multani has no given name in the book.** Entry ۲۴۲ calls him only "**حضرت شاہ گردیز
  ملتانی**" (p. 276, folio 264). The archive row `shrine-of-shah-yusaf-gardez` names its figure Shah
  Yusuf Gardez of Multan, and no other Gardez row exists, so the placement above is the only one
  available — but **the identification rests on the nisba and the city, not on a name the chunk
  prints**. The row's death year (1150) is (not stated) here, and nothing in the chunk confirms or
  contradicts it.
- **"پیر سید کمال المشہور پیر جہانیاں" of Chunian is not placed against
  `shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht`.** Both are "جہانیاں" and both are Bukhari sayyids ("سادات
  بخاری", p. 266, folio 254), but the archive row is at Uch Sharif and its figure is Sayyid Jalaluddin
  (Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht), while this entry gives the personal name **کمال**, the site **قصبہ
  چونیاں**, and living descendants there. **A human must decide whether this is a distinct saint with a
  shared sobriquet or a second site claimed for the Uch figure; the chunk supports neither reading.**
- **"شاہ بلاول" of Lahore versus the archive's Bilawal Shah Noorani.** The chunk twice names a Shah
  Bilawal — as one of "**لاہور کے بزرگوں میاں میر و شاہ بلاول**" (p. 270, folio 258) and as "**شاہ رنگ
  [OCR?] بلاول حضرت لال حسین لاہوری کے خلیفہ**" (p. 271, folio 259). The archive's
  `shah-noorani-shrine-syed-bilawal-shah-noorani` is in the Lahoot Lamakan valley, Khuzdar District,
  Balochistan. **The chunk's figure is explicitly of Lahore, so no match is made; whether the two
  mentions are even the same Shah Bilawal is also unclear, since the second carries an unresolved
  "رنگ [OCR?]" before the name.**
- **Three overlapping "Chiragh"/"Abdul Razzaq" figures.** (1) "**حضرت شاہ چراغ گیلانی لاہوری**", pir of
  Shah Dargahi (entry ۲۲۰, p. 267, folio 255) — placed above against
  `shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh`, whose figure the index names Syed Abdul Razzaq (Shah Chiragh) of
  Lahore. (2) "**شاہ عبدالرزاق مکی**", murid of Mauj Darya, tomb in Anarkali bazar, died "ایک ہزار
  چوراسی" (entry ۲۲۵, p. 269, folio 257) — **the same personal name as that row's figure, in the same
  quarter of Lahore as the row's location (The Mall, near Lahore High Court), but a different pir and a
  different year.** (3) "**مزار چراغ شاہ**" outside Roshnai Gate, successor of Sayyid Ali Gilani who
  died "ایک ہزار دو سو ستائیس" (entry ۲۳۸, p. 275, folio 263) — **the same sobriquet, a different
  century and a different gate.** The book treats all three as distinct people; **a human must rule on
  whether the archive's row is (1) or (2) before any of this material enters the entry.** Note also a
  fourth, unrelated "چراغ شاہ" as the father of a seal-engraver (p. 274, folio 262).
- **"شاہ شمس الدین تبریزی" is not placed against `shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari`.** Pir Hadi
  Rahnuma is called "**شمسی سید اولاد شاہ شمس الدین تبریزی** سے" (p. 267, folio 255). The archive row's
  figure is Shah Shams-ud-Din **Sabzwari** of Multan. **The nisba printed here is Tabrizi, not
  Sabzwari, and the chunk says nothing about Multan or about that shrine, so no match is asserted.**
- **"شیخ طاہر بندگی" versus the archive's Daud Bandagi.** The archive carries `shergarh` (Daud Bandagi
  Kirmani) and a "Syed Dawood Bandgi Kirmani" affiliation under `darbar-abul-muali-qadri`. The chunk's
  two mentions read **طاہر** in both the author's text and the editor's footnote (p. 274, folio 262),
  which matches `tahir-bandagi-qadri` and not the Daud rows — **but the shared "بندگی" is worth a
  reviewer's eye, since the two figures are one letter apart in transcription.**
- **"شاہ فرید لاہوری / شاہ فرید نوشاہی" is not Baba Farid of Pakpattan.** He is a Naushahi Qadiri
  murid of Pir Muhammad Sachyar, buried three miles south of Lahore near mauza Dholanwal (pp. 272, 274,
  folios 260, 262). **No material here belongs to `shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar`**; the name is
  noted only so the two are not merged downstream.
- **An internal contradiction of about 250 years in entry ۲۱۸.** The tomb "**بابر شاہ کے عہد میں تعمیر
  ہوا**" but the saint died "**سنہ چھ سو اکیاسی**" (p. 267, folio 255). **Either the year is wrong, or
  the era is not the one the reader assumes, or the tomb is much later than the burial. The chunk offers
  no reconciliation and no footnote corrects it.** Nothing here may be used as a death date.
- **Seven death years are printed in words with no era named.** "سنہ ایک ہزار ایک سو" (۲۱۷, p. 267),
  "سنہ چھ سو اکیاسی" (۲۱۸, p. 267), "سنہ ایک ہزار ایک سو چھتیس" (۲۲۲, p. 268), "سنہ ایک ہزار ایک سو
  بیاسی" (۲۲۳, p. 269), "سنہ ایک ہزار چوراسی" (۲۲۵, p. 269), "سال ایک ہزار ایک سو چالیس" (۲۲۶, p. 270),
  "سال ایک ہزار دو سو ستائیس" (۲۳۸, p. 275). **Only the two figures-form years carry "ھ" — ۱۲۱۹ھ (p.
  273) and ۱۲۹۲ھ (p. 276). Hijri is the natural reading for all of them and the chunk does not say so;
  treat every word-form year as era-unstated.**
- **A name divergence inside entry ۲۳۰.** The heading reads "مزار **حاجی جمعیت** مرحوم" and the body
  "**اصلی نام اس بزرگ کا حاجی جمیل** تھا", with "حاجی جمعیت" used again for the builder and "حاجی
  جمیل" in the Arabic inscription's last link (pp. 271–272, folios 259–260). **Two forms of one name in
  one entry; the inscription supports "جمیل".**
- **A name divergence inside entry ۲۴۱**: heading "پیر **ڈہل**", text "**ڈھل** محلہ" (p. 276, folio
  264).
- **Transcriber flags that bear on facts above.** "سادات گیلانی صاحبان **[OCR?]** اوچ" (p. 266);
  "ان کی نسبت **[OCR?]** ہے" (p. 266) — the Chishti affiliation clause of Pir Muhammad Shirazi; "سادات
  **ناروال [OCR?]**" (p. 267) — the place the Shia descendants are from; "پرانے **ہزار دول [OCR?]**"
  and the Lat Sahib's kothi (p. 267) — the whole location of Sharaf Shah Lahori's tomb; "مزار حضرت شاہ
  اسمعیل محدث کے **دری [OCR?]** طرف" (p. 267) — the direction of Shah Dargahi's grave; "پانی **واستے
  [OCR?]**" (p. 268) — the Punjabi disease name the whole Sunday ritual is keyed to; "شہر **سبز دار
  [OCR?]**" (p. 269) — Shah Abdul Razzaq Makki's origin; "مقبرہ **برنگ [OCR?] نیلگوں**" (p. 269) — the
  tomb's colour; "شیخ سعدی **بلخاری [OCR?]** لاہوری" and "آخر **کبندست [OCR?]**" (p. 270) — Pir Zuhdi's
  first pir and the clause introducing his Chishti khirqa; "اس **[OCR?]** ٹھیکرے کے برابر مصری" (p. 270)
  — the object whose weight the misri offering matches; "شاہ **رنگ [OCR?]** بلاول" (p. 271); "گنبد
  **کانسی [OCR?]** کا" (p. 271, and again p. 272) — the dome's original finish, central to the
  desecration charge; "اتنی **پشت [OCR?]**" (p. 272) — the number of generations the footprint stayed in
  the family, which is therefore **not recoverable**; "شاہ **کنٹھہ [OCR?]** نوشاہی" (pp. 272, 273, and
