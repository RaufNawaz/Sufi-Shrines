# hadeeqat_ul_aulia — chunk 009 — pp. 97–106

End of entry 48 (Amir Khusrau) through entry 58 (Shaikh Abd al-Khaliq Lahori). Still the Chishti
chapter, and from entry 51 onward it becomes a Chishti-Sabiri chapter **of Lahore**: five of the last
eight entries are Lahore men with Lahore graves. No archive row is the subject of an entry, but the
chunk is the densest yet on the editor's method — he corrects the author's death year three times, twice
against the author's *own other book*, and once shows the author citing a source for a date that the
source explicitly refuses to give.

**The folio markers are ten too high on pp. 97–100 and correct from p. 101 onward.** Taking
folio = PDF index − 12 (the relation verified for this book), p. 97 is folio 85 but the marker reads 95;
pp. 98, 99, 100 read 96, 97, 98 for 86, 87, 88. At p. 101 the marker sequence drops back to 89, which is
correct, and pp. 101–106 agree with the relation throughout (89–94). So the ten-too-high run that began
in chunk 007 **ends inside this chunk**. Folios below are the corrected values, with the marker's own
reading in brackets where it differs.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Khwaja Amir Khusrau** (end of entry no. 48, p. 97, folio 85 [marker: 95]): takhallus in verse was
  Khusrau; real name given in the previous chunk. Father **Amir Saif al-Din**, an amir of rank, wealth,
  learning and forbearance, devoted to Sultan al-Mashaikh's service, "ستر سال کی عمر میں کفار کے جہاد
  میں شہید ہوا", martyred at seventy in jihad against the infidels. Maternal grandfather **Amir Imad
  al-Mulk**, a man of good and blessing and knower of outer and inner mysteries, in whose shade Khusrau
  was raised, being left nine years old at his father's death. A brother, **Aʿazz al-Din Ali**, greatly
  beloved of Sultan al-Mashaikh. Output: "تصانیف ان کی ایک کم سو مشہور ہیں" — ninety-nine (one less than
  a hundred) of his works are famous — and his Persian verses "پانچ لاکھ سے کم اور چار لاکھ سے زیادہ",
  fewer than five lakh and more than four lakh, with countless Hindi verses besides; he finished *Matla
  al-Anwar* in answer to Nizami's *Makhzan al-Asrar* in two weeks. Died in the year seven hundred and
  twenty-five, six months after Sultan al-Mashaikh, "بدھ کے روز اٹھارہویں ماہ شوال", Wednesday 18
  Shawwal; "مزار گوہر بار سلطان المشائخ کے روضہ کے پاس ہے", his mazar is beside Sultan al-Mashaikh's
  rauza. He was absent at his pir's death, away with the king on the **Bengal campaign**; on hearing he
  came at once to Delhi, put on black and sat by the grave "بے خور و خواب … با سینہ بریاں و چشم گریاں",
  without food or sleep, breast burning and eyes weeping, for the six months until he died. A Persian
  chronogram follows.
- **Shaikh Nasir al-Din Mahmud Audhi, called Chiragh-e Dehli** (entry no. 49, pp. 97–98, folios 85–86
  [markers: 95–96]): great friend and true khalifa of Sultan al-Mashaikh Nizam al-Din; lineage to Imam
  Hasan; grandfather Sayyid Abd al-Latif, father Sayyid Yahya. "بعد وفات سلطان المشائخ کے ولایت خطہ
  دہلی کی ان کو ملی" — after Sultan al-Mashaikh's death the *wilayat of the region of Delhi* fell to
  him. Patience, contentment and submission were his mark; the books are full of his marvels, for which
  this brief work has no room; thousands of seekers reached nearness. Died 18 Ramadan, Friday, in the
  year seven hundred and fifty-seven Hijri; mazar in the city of Delhi.
- **Shaikh Jalal al-Din Mahmud Panipati Gazruni**, called **Kabir al-Auliya** in the editor's footnote
  (entry no. 50, pp. 98–99, folios 86–87 [markers: 96–97]): murid of Shaikh Shams al-Din Turk Panipati
  and khirqa-holder from him; real name Muhammad bin Mahmud; genealogy to Amir al-Muminin Uthman.
  *Jazb* and *istighraq* so overcame him that he was unconscious at all times, and at prayer his
  attendants called "حق حق حق" loudly in his ear to bring him round. **Forty perfect awliya were his
  khalifas, from whom separate silsilas issued**; *Zad al-Abrar* is among his fine works. Died in the
  year seven hundred and sixty-five Hijri. (For his kitchen and his offerings see Practices.)
- **Shah Kaku Chishti Lahori** (entry no. 51, pp. 99–100, folios 87–88 [markers: 97–98]): descendant of
  Baba Farid; a great elder of worship, austerity, asceticism, piety and trust; took the khirqa of
  khilafat from **Shaikh Pir Muhammad Chishti Lahori** and spent his whole life guiding people; outward
  and inward wealth were his portion; his karamat are very famous. Died in the year eight hundred and
  eighty Hijri; **mazar in Lahore outside Delhi Gate**. The editor's footnote gives him a different pir,
  a madrasa in present-day Landa Bazar, a famous scholar son, and no certain death year at all: see
  Arguments and Doubts.
- **Shaikh Abd al-Quddus Gangohi** (entry no. 52, pp. 100–101, folios 88–89 [markers: 98, —]): great
  khalifa of Shaikh Muhammad bin Shaikh Arif bin Shaikh Ahmad Abd al-Haq Rudaulvi, and so joined to
  Shaikh Jalal al-Din Panipati "تین واسطوں سے", through three intermediaries, since Ahmad Abd al-Haq
  took the khirqa from Jalal al-Din. Of the "خاندان چشت اہل بہشت سلسلہ صابریہ". His *Anwar al-Uyun*
  (4)[OCR?] in tasawwuf is popular with elite and common. Died in the year nine hundred and forty-five
  Hijri; mazar at Gangoh(5)[OCR?]. The editor's long footnote adds: he was among the best-known mashaikh
  of the Sabiriya, his era politically "بڑا ہوش ربا", and "آپ کو مجبوراً سیاست میں حصہ لینا پڑا", he was
  compelled to take part in politics — a long letter (maktub 33[OCR?]) to **Sikandar Lodhi**, and another
  to **Babur** when his dominion came, both giving the kings counsel; thirteen of his works are listed,
  with editions (p. 101–102, folios 89–90).
- **Shaikh Ahmad Abd al-Haq Rudaulvi** (editor's footnote, p. 101, folio 89): d. 836 AH[OCR?] /
  1433/34[OCR?] or 1434 CE; **he founded the Chishtiya khanqah at Rudauli**, and "آپ کے نفسِ گرم سے
  سلسلہ صابریہ کو بڑا عروج حاصل ہوا", through his warm breath the Sabiriya silsila attained great
  ascendancy.
- **Shaikh Jalal al-Din Thanesari** (entry no. 53, p. 102, folio 90): khalifa and janishin of Abd
  al-Quddus Gangohi; genealogy to Umar ibn al-Khattab; father Mahmud also a learned man; born a wali,
  "ولی مادر زاد"; memorised the Quran at seven and finished sarf, nahw, mantiq, maani, hadith and tafsir
  at seventeen. Died in the year nine hundred and eighty-nine Hijri. The editor notes his *Tahqiq-e
  Arazi-e Hindi* (printed Karachi) and *Irshad al-Talibin* (printed Amritsar) as current, and that Mulla
  Abd al-Qadir Badayuni met him twice and recorded his impressions in *Muntakhab al-Tawarikh* 248–249.
- **Shaikh Jan Allah Chishti Sabri Lahori** (entry no. 54, p. 103, folio 91): khalifa of Shaikh Nizam
  al-Din Balkhi, the great janishin of Jalal al-Din Thanesari; first a teacher of the outward sciences
  for some years, then completed inwardly under Balkhi and "لاہور میں بعد حصول خرقۂ خلافت مامور ہوئے",
  was **posted to Lahore** after receiving the khirqa. Died in the year one thousand and twenty-nine;
  mazar in Lahore. The editor places the grave precisely: "شیخ جان اللہ باغ مہاران سنگھ[OCR?] لاہور میں
  مدفون ہیں", buried in Bagh Maharan Singh[OCR?], Lahore, and names three pir-brothers who were also in
  Lahore — Sayyid Allah Bakhsh Bhakkari, Shaikh Allah Bakhsh Lahori, Shaikh Dost Muhammad Lahori
  (Khazinat al-Asfiya; Nami, *Buzurgan-e Lahore* 167–169) (p. 103, folio 91).
- **Shaikh Nizam al-Din Balkhi** (editor's footnote, p. 103, folio 91): Nizam al-Din Balkhi bin Abd
  al-Shukur Faruqi Thanesari, d. 1036 AH, a well-known scholar and shaikh; ten or more works listed,
  with manuscripts in the Jawahir collection of the Etawah Museum (now Muslim University Aligarh) and in
  Dr. Maulvi Muhammad Shafi's possession in Lahore.
- **Shaikh Haji Abd al-Karim Chishti Lahori** (entry no. 55, pp. 103–104, folios 91–92): his father
  "عماد الملک(۲)[OCR?] عبداللہ انصاری" was a great amir in the royal service; he renounced outward
  wealth, became murid of Nizam al-Din Balkhi and was completed. When the king "اکبر بادشاہ(۱)"
  was displeased with his pir and expelled him from India to the Haramain Sharifain, Abd al-Karim went
  with him; returning, he settled in Lahore for the rest of his life. Works: a Persian *Sharh Fusus
  al-Hikam* and *Risala Asrar-e Ajiba*(2) on the zikr and shughl of the pirs of Chisht. Died 23 Rajab
  in the year one thousand and forty-five Hijri, "مزار گوہر بار لاہور میں متصل باغ زیب النسا بیگم
  المشہور چوبرجی[OCR?] کے پہلو[OCR?]" — mazar in Lahore adjoining the garden of **Zeb al-Nisa Begum,
  famous as Chauburji**[OCR?], at its side[OCR?] (p. 104, folio 92; the sentence breaks off unfinished at
  the page edge). The editor corrects both the father's title and the king's name: see Arguments.
- **Shaikh Abu Said Chishti Sabri Gangohi** (entry no. 56, pp. 104–105, folios 92–93): son of Shaikh Nur
  al-Din(3) bin Abd al-Quddus Gangohi; trained and given the khirqa by Nizam al-Din Balkhi. Died in the
  year one thousand and forty-nine[OCR?]; mazar at Gangoh. The editor corrects the descent and the date:
  see Arguments.
- **Shaikh Muhammad Sadiq bin Fath Allah Gangohi Hanafi Chishti Sabri** (entry no. 57, pp. 105–106,

- **Shaikh Abd al-Khaliq Lahori Chishti Sabri** (entry no. 58, p. 106, folio 94): khalifa of Shaikh Jan
  Allah Lahori, unrivalled in the outer and inner sciences. In wajd and sama "لوگوں کو ان کے مر جانے کا

  Died in the year one thousand and sixty-eight Hijri; mazar in Lahore. His langar is under Practices.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A kitchen for a thousand, topped up by invitation and by the unseen.** Of Shaikh Jalal al-Din
  Panipati: "ان کے مطبخ میں ایک ہزار آدمی سے کم کھانا نہیں کھاتے تھے۔ جب کم ہوتے۔ شہر سے آدمی بلائے
  جاتے اور جب کبھی سفر میں ہوتے تو ایک ہزار آدمی کا کھانا غیب سے نمودار ہوتا" — no fewer than a thousand
  men ate in his kitchen; when they were fewer, men were summoned from the city; and when he was
  travelling, food for a thousand appeared from the unseen (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97]). The
  thousand is treated as a floor to be maintained, not a count of who happened to come.
- **Offerings named as nazr and futuh.** The editor quotes *Maarij al-Wilayat* on the same shaikh:
  "مردمان از ہر جانب روئے بآں ولی آوردند و نذر و فتوح بے شمار آوردند" — people from every side turned
  their faces to that wali and brought countless *nazr* and *futuh* (Abdullah Khweshgi, *Maarij
  al-Wilayat*, ms folio 194b) (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97], حاشیہ, continued from p. 98).
- **A langar open to the poor, twice daily, in Lahore.** Of Shaikh Abd al-Khaliq Lahori: "لنگر ان کا
  غربا ومساکین پر عام تھا۔ ہزاروں لوگ دو وقت کھانا ان کے باورچی خانے سے کھاتے تھے" — his langar was
  general to the poor and destitute; thousands ate two meals a day from his kitchen (p. 106, folio 94).
- **An urs suppressed and restored under Sikh rule, and the community that performed it.** At Shah
  Kaku's mazar the Sikhs — "سکھان شہید بونگیوں [OCR?]" — closed the annual urs "جو قوم خوجگان [OCR?] کی
  مرید کرتے ہیں", which the murids of the Khojgan[OCR?] community used to perform, and interrogated the
  murids as to why they held it; the murids answered that the elder was a wali, a worshipper and an
  ascetic, "اس واسطے ان کا عرس ہوتا ہے". After the desecration attempt and its outcome (see Legends),
  "اس روز سے پھر سکھ مانع عرس نہ ہوئے", from that day the Sikhs no longer obstructed the urs (p. 100,
  folio 88 [marker: 98]). A named hereditary constituency for an urs, a named suppressing power, and a
  restoration — the clearest state-vs-shrine episode in these chunks.
- **A ber tree standing on the grave** — "اس بیر کے درخت پر جو حضرت کے قبر پر ہے", the ber tree that is
  on the Hazrat's grave, at Shah Kaku's mazar in Lahore (p. 100, folio 88 [marker: 98]). A physical
  feature of a Lahore shrine, recorded incidentally.
- **A madrasa built in Lahore and a mohalla named after its builder.** The editor: "آپ نے موجودہ لنڈا
  بازار متصل ریلوے سٹیشن لاہور میں اپنا مدرسہ تعمیر کروایا تھا", Shah Kaku had his madrasa built in the
  present Landa Bazar adjoining Lahore Railway Station, and his mohalla is mentioned by Bayazid Bayat
  (*Tazkira-e Humayun wa Akbar* p. 30). His son Shaikh Ishaq was a noted scholar and teacher, and Shaikh
  Sa'd Allah bin Israil and Shaikh Munawwar Lahori his famous students (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97],
  حاشیہ 1). A shrine, a teaching institution and a quarter of the city as one complex.
- **Spiritual territory as an assignable jurisdiction.** "ولایت خطہ دہلی کی ان کو ملی" — the wilayat of
  the Delhi region *fell to* Chiragh-e Dehli after Nizam al-Din's death (p. 98, folio 86 [marker: 96]),
  and the editor glosses it administratively: he "چشتیہ سلسلہ کے مرکزی نظام کو … سنبھالا", took over the
  central organisation of the Chishti silsila (p. 98, folio 86 [marker: 96], حاشیہ 1). Read with chunk
  008's signed-and-sealed farmans of deputation, the book treats a khalifa's territory as a post.
- **Forty khalifas, forty silsilas.** Of Jalal al-Din Panipati: "چالیس اولیائے کامل ان کے خلیفہ تھے۔ جن
  سے علیحدہ علیحدہ سلسلے جاری ہوئے" (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97]); the editor repeats the forty and
  singles out Ahmad Abd al-Haq Rudaulvi (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97], حاشیہ 2).
- **Shaikhs writing to kings, and a shaikh expelled by one.** Abd al-Quddus Gangohi's letters of counsel
  to Sikandar Lodhi and to Babur (p. 101, folio 89, حاشیہ 5); and Nizam al-Din Balkhi expelled from
  India to the Haramain by a displeased emperor, his murid Abd al-Karim accompanying him into exile and
  settling at Lahore on return (p. 104, folio 92).
- **Manuscript and print provenance, recorded throughout.** *Anwar al-Uyun* in five editions — Matba
  Gulzar Muhammadi Lucknow 1295 AH, Aligarh 1905, Lucknow 1909, a **Khanqah edition** of 1318 AH, and
  *al-Durr al-Maknun* (translation), Matba Mujtabai Delhi (p. 101, folio 89, حاشیہ 4); *Iqtibas
  al-Anwar* published Matba Islamia Lahore 1895 (p. 105, folio 93, حاشیہ 1); manuscripts in the Sherani
  collection, the Punjab University library (no. pc/17/42), and the Rashidiya Library, Lahore (pp. 102,
  104, 105–106; folios 90, 92, 93–94). A khanqah publishing its own founder's malfuzat is an
  institution's economy as much as a bibliography.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Amir Khusrau's shirt burnt at the heart.** On the authority of the author of *Safinat al-Auliya*:
  such fire of love was kindled in his "دل [OCR?] محبت منزل" that his shirt was always burnt away at the
  place of the heart, and a new shirt burnt at the heart's place the moment he put it on (p. 97, folio 85
  [marker: 95]).
- **The Sikh who urinated on Shah Kaku's grave.** A Sikh said "میں اس کا امتحان کرتا ہوں", I will test
  this, climbed the ber tree over the grave and began to urinate on it; "اسی وقت پاؤں پھسل کر نیچے آ
  پڑا اور ٹانگ ٹوٹ گئی", at that very moment his foot slipped, he fell and his leg broke — after which
  the urs was never again obstructed (p. 100, folio 88 [marker: 98]).
- **"حق حق حق" shouted into a shaikh's ear** to rouse him from istighraq for the prayer (p. 99, folio 87
  [marker: 97]) — reported as his settled practice, not as a marvel.
- **Food for a thousand from the unseen** while travelling (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97]).
- **Abd al-Karim Lahori takes a murid to the Hajj and back in an afternoon.** Told to shut his eyes and
  keep a hand on the shaikh's shoulder half a kos out of Lahore, the murid opened them at **Arafat**,
  performed the Hajj with joy, stayed "دوسرے[OCR?] روز" there as well, and returned to Lahore the same
  way (p. 104, folio 92).
- **Three blows of a staff open three worlds.** To a man who wanted God without the strength for
  austerity, Shaikh Abu Said said "ہم اس عصا کی تین ضرب سے طالب کو خدا تک پہنچا دیتے ہیں"; the first
  blow opened the *alam-e malakut*, the second *jabarut*, the third *alam-e mashhud*; he lay unconscious
  three days and became a sincere murid on waking. Reported from *Sawati al-Anwar* (p. 105, folio 93).
- **The idol of Jagannath speaks, and is answered from the shariah.** Absorbed before a beautiful stone
  idol in the bazaar, Shaikh Muhammad Sadiq heard it say by God's command "اَنَا المعبود لا تعبد
  سوائی", I am the worshipped, worship none but me; though overpowered in state he prostrated toward the
  Kaaba "برعایت شرعِ مقدس"; the idol then said "اینما تولو فثم وجہ اللہ", wherever you turn there is the
  face of God, and the Shaikh replied that this is true "مگر رعایت شرعِ رسول فرض ہے", but regard for
  the Prophet's shariah is obligatory. Reported from *Sawati al-Anwar* (pp. 105–106, folios 93–94).
- Of Chiragh-e Dehli the author again says the books are full of his marvels and "جن کی گنجائش اس مختصر
  میں نہیں ہے", there is no room for them in this brief work (p. 98, folio 86 [marker: 96]); the same of
  Shah Kaku, whose karamat are "بہت مشہور" and of whom only the Sikh episode is told (p. 100, folio 88
  [marker: 98]).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The editor takes Shah Kaku's death year away altogether, and names the author as the source of the
  confusion.** The text says he died in the year eight hundred and eighty Hijri (p. 100, folio 88
  [marker: 98]). The footnote answers: the Mufti himself wrote in *Khazinat al-Asfiya* (1/400) that
  Qutb al-Alam Shah Kaku died in ۸۸۲ھ; but "حقیقت یہ ہے کہ سارے تذکرے میں کہیں شاہ کاکوؒ کے سال وفات کا
  ذکر تک نہیں ہے" — the reality is that in the whole of the *tazkira* meant (Jamal al-Din Abu Bakr's
  *Tazkira-e Qutbiya*) there is not even a mention of Shah Kaku's death year. All it records is that
  when Shaikh Abd al-Jalil came to Lahore in the Lodhi period, Shah Kaku "بہت مسن اور ضعیف ہو چکے تھے",
  had already become very aged and weak; from which Nami inferred that Abd al-Jalil came around 882 AH
  and Shah Kaku must have died that year. The editor's verdict: "گویا آپ کا حتمی سال وفات معلوم نہیں" —
  his definite death year is not known (pp. 99–101, folios 87–89, حاشیہ 1 continued over three pages).
  **Author: 880 AH. Editor: neither 880 nor his own 882 — unknown, and 882 is a conjecture from an
  aged man's appearance.**
- **And he catches the father and son 114 years apart.** Shaikh Ishaq, Shah Kaku's son, died 994 AH /
  1586 CE; "باپ اور بیٹے کے سال وفات میں ۱۱۴ سال کا فرق ہے یہ فرق مفتی صاحب نے پیدا کیا ہے" — there is
  a gap of 114 years between the death years of father and son, and this gap was created by the Mufti
  (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97], حاشیہ 1). The arithmetic is the argument: 994 − 880 = 114.
- **He replaces the emperor in the expulsion story.** The text has "اکبر بادشاہ" displeased with Nizam
  al-Din Balkhi and expelling him from India. The footnote: "یہاں مفتی صاحب سے تسامح ہوا ہے" — here the
  Mufti has slipped — since he himself wrote in *Khazinat al-Asfiya* that it was **Jahangir**, who became
  suspicious of him for consoling and praying for his rebel son **Prince Khusrau**, and banished him
  (*Khazinat al-Asfiya* 1/463–364[OCR?]) (p. 104, folio 92, حاشیہ 1). The correction is again the author
  against himself.
- **He corrects a genealogy by one generation.** The text calls Shaikh Abu Said the son of Shaikh Nur
  al-Din bin Abd al-Quddus Gangohi. The footnote: "شیخ نورالدین، شیخ عبدالقدوس گنگوہی کے صاحبزادے نہیں
  تھے، بلکہ پوتے تھے۔ یعنی شیخ نورالدین بن شیخ علی بن شیخ عبدالقدوس" — Nur al-Din was not Abd
  al-Quddus's son but his grandson, Nur al-Din bin Shaikh Ali bin Abd al-Quddus (Ghulam Abd al-Quddus,
  *Hadaiq-e Daudi*, ms Sherani collection, rauza 2) (p. 104, folio 92, حاشیہ 3).
- **He corrects a title in one line**: of the father called "عماد الملک" — "مخدوم الملک ہونا چاہیئے", it
  should be Makhdum al-Mulk (p. 103, folio 91, حاشیہ 2).
- **The strongest case in the chunk: the author cites a source for a date that the source says it does
  not know.** On Shaikh Muhammad Sadiq Gangohi the text gives 18 Muharram 1058 AH (p. 106, folio 94).
  The editor: "شیخ محمد صادق گنگوہی کے سال وفات میں اختلاف ہے", there is disagreement; in the **first**
  edition of *Khazinat al-Asfiya* the Mufti versified ۱۰۳۹ھ[OCR?], and then "یکایک دوسرے ایڈیشن میں
  بحوالہ سواطع الانوار (اقتباس الانوار) ۱۰۵۸ھ لکھ دیا ہے", suddenly in the second edition wrote 1058 AH
  on the authority of *Sawati al-Anwar*. But *Iqtibas al-Anwar* states plainly "سنہ وفاتش تا حال معلوم
  این احقر نہ شد کہ نوشتہ آید" (p. 303) — the year of his death has not till now become known to this
  humble one, that it might be written. Against this the editor sets "ایک خاندانی دستاویز یعنی حدائق
  داؤدی", a family document, *Hadaiq-e Daudi*, which gives the date **with isnad** as 19 Muharram
  1051 AH, and concludes "شیخ محمد صادق کا سال وفات ۱۰۵۱ھ صحیح ہے", 1051 AH is the correct year (p. 106,
  folio 94, حاشیہ 1, with five references). **Author: 18 Muharram 1058 AH, citing Sawati al-Anwar.
  Editor: 19 Muharram 1051 AH, from a family document with a chain — and the cited authority disclaims
  the date entirely.**
- **A fourth date correction, briefly.** Of Shaikh Abu Said, whose death the text puts in 1049[OCR?]:
  "شیخ ابوسعید کا سال وفات حدائق داؤدی میں ۱۰۳۹ھ درج ہے" — his death year is recorded in *Hadaiq-e
  Daudi* as 1039 AH (p. 105, folio 93, حاشیہ 2). Stated, not argued.
- **A different pir for Shah Kaku than the text gives.** Text: he took the khirqa of khilafat from
  Shaikh Pir Muhammad Chishti Lahori (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97]). Footnote: he "شیخ علاء الحق و
  الدین بن اسعد اللہ لاہوری کے مرید تھے" and afterwards "شیخ عبد الکریم لاہوری سے بھی فیض حاصل کیا",
  also took faiz from Shaikh Abd al-Karim Lahori (same page, حاشیہ 1). Two incompatible affiliations on
  one page, neither reconciled; and see Doubts on the chronology of the second.
- **The editor's political reading of Chiragh-e Dehli's Delhi**, quoted because it is his own voice
  rather than a source's: "اب دہلی علاؤ الدین خلجی کی دہلی نہ تھی۔ بلکہ اب یہ بد قسمت شہر ایک مطلق
  العنان بادشاہ کے بدلتے ہوئے افکار و تصورات کا بازیچہ بنا ہوا تھا" — Delhi was no longer Ala al-Din
  Khalji's Delhi; the unfortunate city had become the plaything of an absolute monarch's shifting
  notions. Muhammad bin Tughluq harassed him in many ways "لیکن آپ نے سلسلہ ارشاد و تلقین سے سر موجنبش
  نہ کی", but he did not move a hair from guidance and instruction (Khaliq Ahmad Nizami, *Salatin-e
  Dehli ke Mazhabi Rujhanat* 362–368) (p. 98, folio 86 [marker: 96], حاشیہ 1).
- **He also defends a saint against a modern academic.** Of Professor Muhammad Aslam's treatment of Abd
  al-Quddus Gangohi in *Din-e Ilahi aur us ka Pas Manzar*: his "مؤثر نگاریاں[OCR?]" are "تصوف اور
  اصطلاحات تصوف سے عدم واقفیت کا نتیجہ ہیں", the result of unfamiliarity with tasawwuf and its
  terminology (p. 101, folio 89, حاشیہ 5).
- **Source criticism of the tazkira tradition itself**: *Sawati al-Anwar* is identified as also being
  *Iqtibas al-Anwar*, by Shaikh Muhammad Akram Barasvi (d. 1159 AH), completed 1132 AH/1719 CE, and is
  granted "معاصر دستاویز کی حیثیت", the status of a contemporary document, **but only for five named
  shaikhs** — Muhammad Sadiq, Daud, Sondha Safidwifi[OCR?], Allah Bakhsh and Muhammad Ali (p. 105,
  folio 93, حاشیہ 1). A dated, bounded grant of authority to a source the author uses throughout.

- "ان کے مطبخ میں ایک ہزار آدمی سے کم کھانا نہیں کھاتے تھے۔ جب کم ہوتے۔ شہر سے آدمی بلائے جاتے۔" — "No

## Doubts

- **The folio markers change behaviour inside this chunk.** On pp. 97–100 they read ten too high
  (95–98 for folios 85–88), continuing the run recorded in chunks 007–008; at p. 101 the marker reads 89
  and from there to p. 106 every marker matches folio = PDF − 12. The relation is the thing to trust;
  any citation taken from a marker on pp. 97–100 must be lowered by ten.
- **Shah Kaku's death year should not be carried at all.** The text's 880 AH, the author's own 882 AH
  elsewhere, and the editor's demonstration that the underlying tazkira gives no year — with 882 traced
  to Nami's inference from a description of an aged man — leave the archive with no usable date
  (pp. 99–101, folios 87–89). Only the burial place, Lahore outside Delhi Gate, is uncontested here.
- **Shah Kaku's pir is given two different ways on one page**, and the second creates a chronological
  impossibility. The footnote's "شیخ عبد الکریم لاہوری", from whom Shah Kaku is said to have taken faiz
  (p. 99, folio 87 [marker: 97]), shares a name with the Shaikh Haji Abd al-Karim Chishti Lahori of
  entry no. 55, who died in 1045 AH (p. 104, folio 92) — some 165 years after Shah Kaku's 880. Either
  two men of the same name and nisba, or an error; the chunk does not distinguish them.
- **Muhammad Sadiq Gangohi's death has three candidate dates in this chunk alone**: 18 Muharram 1058 AH
  (text, p. 106), 1039 AH[OCR?] (the author's own first edition, per the footnote), and 19 Muharram
  1051 AH (the editor's conclusion from *Hadaiq-e Daudi*). The editor prefers 1051; note that the same
  footnote's "۱۰۳۹ھ[OCR?]" is OCR-flagged and is also the year *Hadaiq-e Daudi* gives for **Shaikh Abu
  Said** (p. 105, folio 93, حاشیہ 2), so a transcription slip between the two footnotes cannot be ruled
  out from inside this chunk.
- **Abu Said's death year is OCR-flagged in the text**: "سنہ ایک ہزار انچاس[OCR?]" (1049) against the
  editor's 1039 AH (pp. 105, folio 93). Ten years, and the flagged numeral is the author's.
- **"Sair al-Auliya" is attributed to Amir Khusrau in one footnote**: "(۲) امیر خسرو [OCR?] : سیر
  الاولیاء ۲۳۷" (p. 98, folio 86 [marker: 96]). Chunk 008's bibliography for Baba Farid gives the same
  work to **Amir Khurd**, and the name here carries an `[OCR?]` flag. Almost certainly a misreading of
  Khurd, but do not cite it either way from this page.
- **The Sikh group's name and the urs community's name are both flagged**: "سکھان شہید بونگیوں [OCR?]"
  and "قوم خوجگان [OCR?]" (p. 100, folio 88 [marker: 98]). Both are load-bearing for the urs episode —
  who suppressed it and who performed it — and neither can be resolved here.
- **Shaikh Abd al-Karim Lahori's grave location is incomplete and flagged.** "متصل باغ زیب النسا بیگم
  المشہور چوبرجی[OCR?] کے پہلو[OCR?]" breaks off at the foot of p. 104 (folio 92) with no verb and two
  flags; the next chunk should be checked for the continuation before the Chauburji location is used.
- **Shaikh Jan Allah's burial place rests on one flagged word**: "باغ مہاران سنگھ[OCR?]" (p. 103,
  folio 91). The text itself says only "مزار لاہور میں ہے".
- **The editor doubts that two of Abd al-Karim Lahori's listed works exist**: "ان کتابوں کے وجود کے
  بارے میں ہمیں ہنوز کوئی اطلاع نہیں ہے" (p. 104, folio 92, حاشیہ 2) — against the text's statement
  that they are "مقبول خاص وعام", popular with elite and common (p. 104, folio 92). A third instance in
  two chunks of an attributed corpus being questioned.
- **A reversed or truncated page range in a citation**: "بایزید بیات : تذکرہ ہمایوں و اکبر کلکتہ ۳۷۴ -
  ۳۰ [OCR?]" (p. 100, folio 88 [marker: 98]), where the same work is cited as "ص ۳۰" a page earlier.
- `[OCR?]` flags carried over that touch facts above, by page: "دل [OCR?]" in the burning-shirt report
  and "امیر خسرو [OCR?]" as author of *Sair al-Auliya* (pp. 97–98, folios 85–86 [markers: 95–96]);
  "سکھان شہید بونگیوں [OCR?]", "قوم خوجگان [OCR?]", "۳۷۴ - ۳۰ [OCR?]" (p. 100, folio 88 [marker: 98]);
  "انوارالعیون(۴)[OCR?]", "گنگوہ(۵)[OCR?]", "الہدیہ[OCR?]", "۸۳۶ھ[OCR?]", "۱۴۳۳/۳۴[OCR?]", "محمد اکرم
  برلسوی[OCR?]", "مکتوب ۳۳[OCR?]", "مؤثر نگاریاں[OCR?]" (p. 101, folio 89); "بحرالانتشاب[OCR?]",
  "حاشیہ شرح مصحائف[OCR?]" (p. 102, folio 90); "عماد الملک(۲)[OCR?]", "۴۳ - ۴۸[OCR?]", "تجلیات

  "چوبرجی[OCR?]", "پہلو[OCR?]", "دوسرے[OCR?] روز", "۱ / ۴۶۳ - ۳۶۴[OCR?]", "مسلک حدیث[OCR?]", "۲ / ۲۸۴ -
  ۲۸۵[OCR?]" (p. 104, folio 92); "ایک ہزار انچاس[OCR?]", "سوندھا سفیدوفی[OCR?]" (p. 105, folio 93);
  "۱۰۳۹ھ[OCR?]" (p. 106, folio 94).
- **Death and birth years mostly given in words, not numerals**, as throughout this book: 757 for
  Chiragh-e Dehli, 765 for Jalal al-Din Panipati, 880 for Shah Kaku, 945 for Abd al-Quddus, 989 for
  Jalal al-Din Thanesari, 1029 for Jan Allah, 1045 for Abd al-Karim, 1049[OCR?] for Abu Said, 1068 for

  alongside the words (p. 106, folio 94).
- **No archive row is the subject of any entry in this chunk.** Several entries are Lahore shrines with

  on a candidate list rather than in an existing entry; in particular **do not attach any of them to
  shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri, shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal or shrine-of-mian-mir** on the strength of a
  shared city and a Chishti-Sabiri nisba.
