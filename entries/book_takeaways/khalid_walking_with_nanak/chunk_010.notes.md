# khalid_walking_with_nanak — chunk 010 — pp. 218–238

*Chunk spans the end of the chapter "NOT HINDU ENOUGH" (Karachi, Clifton, Port Grand, Manora; pp. 218–230), all of chapter 15 "GURU HARGOBIND" (pp. 231–235) and the opening of chapter 16 "THE SHIVA OF MY IMAGINATION" (Katas Raj; pp. 236–238). The chunk gives no date for the Karachi/Manora visits (not stated). Present-day companions named: Humayun, Sadia, Ali ("Sadia's friend"); Iqbal Qaiser is consulted by telephone (p. 224).*

- **gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-chitti-gatti**, **gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-jhalian-jhalian-dhilwan**, **gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-mozang**, **gurdwara-chowmala-sahib**, **gurdwara-panjvi-chati-patshahi**, **gurdwara-patshahi-chhevin-hadiara-lahore** (Guru Hargobind, person-level; chapter 15):
  - "On the 24 July 1606", after Guru Arjan appointed him successor, Hargobind "decided to give darshan"; "the Aka/ [OCR?] [Akal] Takht, translated as the Eternal Throne, had also been prepared by then" (p. 231, folio 205).
  - Dressed "as a royal": "churidar pyjama, a saffron gown", turban "with an aigrette", necklace, "two swords" (p. 231, folio 205).
  - He "refused to take up the religious symbols of Guruhood" of earlier Gurus; reported saying "My rosary shall be the sword belt and on my turban, I shall wear the emblem of royalty" (p. 231, folio 205).
  - Introduced "miri-piri": one sword "temporal power", the other "spiritual prowess" (p. 231, folio 205).
  - Began a saffron flag facing the Akal Takht, "to eventually become Nishan cael [OCR?] [Nishan Sahib?] a symbol of the Khalsa"; kept a large drum "nagar" [OCR?] and "a hawk as a pet" (p. 232, folio 206).
  - P. S. Grewal, "Nanak's Doctrine and Feudalization of Sikh Gurudom": militarisation began under "Guru Asjan [OCR?] [Arjan]" but gained impetus under Hargobind (p. 232, folio 206).
  - "fifty-two bodyguards"; an army titled "Sant Sipahi or saint soldiers", "five hundred soldiers to begin with", "not paid a salary but… exempt from paying the required tribute" (p. 232, folio 206).
  - "Mohsin Fani, a Persian historian from the seventeenth [century]… says that the number of Sant Sipahi was seven hundred cavaliers and sixty artillery men" (p. 232, folio 206).
  - Retained "Afghan mercenaries whom Cunningham identifies as criminal elements"; an "akhara" for training in warfare (p. 232, folio 206).
  - A fragment reads "In 1660 [OCR?], the Guru ¢…" with the rest lost (p. 232, folio 206).
  - Through his "Masands" he instructed devotees to offer "arms and horses" [instead] of money; reference to "the rule of Dasawandh (one-tenth)" (p. 233, folio 207; heavily damaged).
  - His sword passed to "his SON, Guru Tegh Bahadur", then to Guru Gobind Singh (p. 233, folio 207).
  - Critics: militarism "a departure from the teachings of the early Gurus"; "Hargobind did not even compose a single hymn" (p. 233, folio 207).
  - Some claims assert he joined Jahangir's "employment, serving him in the Punjab"; he "assisted the Emperor to overcome Tara Chand of Nalagarh" (pp. 234–235, folios 208–209).
  - Imprisoned "in Gwalior jail on the orders of the Emperor"; one explanation: he "had retained the money that [was] given him by the Court to be disbursed to his troops"; Sikh version: the Emperor "felt threatened" (p. 235, folio 209).
  - Under Shahjahan, "Three battles… all of them were won by the Sikhs… Amritsar, Lahira and Kartarpur"; after the last he "decided to settle at Kartarpur" (p. 235, folio 209).
  - "Gury [OCR?] [Guru] Hargobind remains one of the most controversial Sikh Gurus"; "He laid the foundations of the contemporary face of Sikhism" (p. 235, folio 209).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)
- Manora, not Clifton, better fits the Sikh record that Nanak took a boat to the sea-god temple; "We will never know for sure" (p. 229).
- Local Hindus and Sikhs in Karachi uniformly deny Nanak visited the southern coast (pp. 224, 227, 228).
- The author projects himself onto Nanak; his depiction "is not an accurate version" (pp. 229–230).
- Khalsa identity is absorbing the independent Nanak-followers and erasing distinctions among the Gurus (p. 230).
- Hargobind's militarisation: critics see departure from early Gurus; the author says it "would be unfair to critic[ise]" without context of Arjan's martyrdom (pp. 233–234).
- "The reality… is much more complex": Hargobind reconciled with Jahangir; the Sikh version "is quiet about the politics of the Guru" (p. 234, folio 208).

**Mapping doubts**
- Guru Nanak 18-row bullet, Hargobind 6, Arjan 3, Gobind Singh, Mardana, Valmiki 3, Jhule Lal 3, Krishna 2, Rama, Kali 3, Durga, Shiva 8, Varuna: attachment is person-level, not site-specific. Kali/Durga/Rama/Krishna/Valmiki rest on bare mentions.
- Lahore (35), Karachi (11), Clifton (2), Shikarpur (3), Nankana Sahib (7): attachment is place-level. Shikarpur rests on one biographical aside about Humayun.
- Talwandi mapped to the Nankana Sahib rows only because the context is Nanak's home/youth; the chunk does not itself equate Talwandi with Nankana Sahib.
- The Manora conical temple is not named "Varun Dev" in the chunk; it goes to **shri-varun-dev-mandir** on place (Manora Island) and on the author's Varuna/sea-god argument. The sea-god cave temple of the reconstructed scene is placed by "later Sikh writers" at Clifton and by the author possibly at Manora — unresolved.
- Karachi's "Pehli Patshah" gurdwara is NOT **gurdwara-pehli-patshahi-jind-pir-sukkur** (Sukkur); kept at Karachi place-level only.
- The Port Grand Jhule Lal temple is not identified with **darya-lal-mandir…** or **jhollay-lal-mandir**; it takes them only via Karachi place-level and Jhule Lal person-level.
- Kartarpur (Hargobind's battle and settlement) — the book does not say which Kartarpur; not mapped to **gurdwara-darbar-sahib-kartarpur**.
- The Panja Sahib and Wali Gandhari items are a poster in Manora; Hasan Abdal is not named.
- "Angad … Das and then Arjan" (p. 233) is broken; Ram Das is not printed, so **gurdwara-guru-ram-das-ji** is not mapped.
- "Tilig lo" (p. 238) might be a site of ascetics but is unreadable; not mapped to **tilla-jogian**.
- "Pandavs" are not King Pandu; **panj-tirath** not mapped.

**Internal contradictions**
- The book calls Tegh Bahadur Hargobind's "SON" (p. 233) — recorded as printed.
- Katas Raj is said to be "about fifty kos from Talwindi" and pilgrims come "from hundreds of kilometres away" — not a contradiction, different frames; noted only.
