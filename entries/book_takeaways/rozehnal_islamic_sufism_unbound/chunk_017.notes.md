## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author judges the modern order's doctrinal writing thinner than its predecessors'**: "On the
  whole, however, the writings of contemporary Chishti Sabiri shaykhs regarding the lata'if provide
  far less nuance and detail than those of their premodern predecessors" (p. 258, folio 251).
- **The author checks his primary sources' own citation practice and finds it wanting**: "Wahid Bakhsh
  provides no reference for this hadith, though it is also found in Hajji Imdad Allah's book Ziya'
  al-qulub" (p. 258, folio 251).
- **A stated gap in the scholarship**, after listing Riddell, Woodward, Drewes and Brakel, Lawrence
  and Ricklefs: "None of these sources, however, documents the history of Pulau Besar or the life of
  Shaykh Isma'il 'Abd al-Qadir Thani" (p. 260, folio 253).
- **The theoretical apparatus the back matter reveals**: Foucault on heterotopia (p. 260, folio 253;
  p. 268, folio 262) and on technologies of the self (p. 269, folio 263); Victor Turner on "The Center
  Out There: The Pilgrim's Goal" and on liminality, "On liminality and the transformative power of
  ritual" (p. 261, folio 254); Bourdieu, Asad, Bhabha, Chakrabarty, Appadurai, Spivak and Anderson in
  the Secondary Sources (pp. 265–266, folios 259–260; p. 273, folio 267). The book reads shrine
  practice through ritual theory and postcolonial theory, not through area studies alone.
