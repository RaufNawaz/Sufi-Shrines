## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Attributed to "the Jewish tradition", via `A. Altmann, "The Ladder of Ascension"`: that the Jews "took Buraq to be an ass and nothing more", so that the story of Buraq climbing Jacob's ladder "was utterly fantastic and an assertion of the impossible"; hence, Schimmel suggests, the saying "If an ass can ascend a ladder, knowledge can be found among launderers" and the Hebrew colophon formula "Until the ass ascends the ladder / About which our Father Jacob dreamt" (p. 275, folio 257).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **On the limits of language.** The cheetah/lion contrast is read as Schimmel's own gloss: "Words are as much inferior to the real sense as is a tame cheetah to a roaring lion. Another way to point to man's incapability of expressing his experience in words is, for example, D 1926/20274" (p. 259, folio 241).
- **On nakedness of the soul.** "Among the Muslim mystics Rumi, following his father's example, uses the imagery of 'becoming naked' more frequently than any other poet" (p. 259, folio 241), continuing on p. 260 (folio 242) with Mechtild of Magdeburg as the medieval Christian parallel and `Heiler, Erscheinungsfonnen [text-layer?: Erscheinungsformen], p. 221`.
- **On Rumi's technical skill.** "For Rumi's technical skill, which enabled him to use complicated rhetorical devices in his spontaneous verse see J. Christoph Burge! [text-layer?: Burgel], "Lautsymbolik und funktionelles Wortspiel bei Riimi [text-layer?: Rūmī]."" (p. 260, folio 242).
- **On whether material art shaped Rumi's imagery.** "It would be interesting to find out whether Rumi's choice of images was influenced by the objects of art that surrounded him", and "it may be that reliefs representing the Tree-of-Life or dragons on Seljukid architecture have affected his poetical imagination, since he was able to transform everything into a poetical image" (p. 267, folio 249). Stated as a question, not a finding.
- **On self-effacement as a classical Sufi ideal.** "the classical ideal of the Sufis that everything human has to diminish so that God's presence can increase. In the language of love mysticism, this idea was expressed by stressing the contrasting qualities of lover and beloved… for the lovers are all petition, niytiz, the beloved all coquettish pride, ntiz [text-layer?: niyāz … nāz]. All these ideas work together to give the imagery of self-effacement a prominent place in Persian poetry in general, and in Rumi's verse in particular. But he can also tum [text-layer?: turn] the image" (p. 272, folio 254).
- **An attribution Schimmel declines to make.** Of `parda-i <Jraqi`: "H. H. Schaeder once mentioned during a seminar the slim possibility that the parda-i <Jraqi in D 2395 might perhaps point to < Iraqi's poetry; the poem is indeed quite close in Stimmung to verses of Rurni's [text-layer?: Rumi's] younger contemporary" (p. 273, folio 255).
- **An admitted gap in her own explanation.** Of Baghdad in Rumi's imagery: "several times this Baghdad is contrasted, for reasons unknown to me, with Hamadhan" (p. 265, folio 247).
- **A dating problem raised and left open.** On the Kanz al-ʿummal: "although the mention of the Kanz in a poem from the first half of the sixteenth century is strange; one would have to find out when this collection was actually introduced in theological courses in India in order to date the verse properly" (p. 279, folio 261).
- **On the Sufi poets' professed aversion to writing.** "The alleged aversion to writing is common also with Sufi poets in the classical tradition, who were prolific writers and yet felt the constant tension between the impossibility of expressing their experience and the urge to convey their wisdom to their disciples" (p. 279, folio 261), illustrated from Sana'i.
- **A textual-critical note on a Sindhi edition.** `Qa41 Qtidan jo kaltim gives the seven verses which were hitherto known only in the appendix because they are not mentioned in the Hariyana manuscript on which this edition relies` (p. 279, folio 261).

## Doubts

**Archive mapping.**

- **All seven archive bullets rest on author-only or literary-reference mentions, and every one of them is a bare name in an endnote citation** — no tomb, shrine, date, place or silsila is attached to any of the seven anywhere in this chunk. This is the 18 September ruling working as intended, but the consolidator should know the evidence is thin in each case: `bhit-bhit-shah` (four citations of the Risālō, one of them carrying a real claim — verbatim translation of a Mathnawi verse into Sindhi, p. 276, folio 258), `shrine-of-sachal-sarmast` (one citation, p. 279, folio 261), `mazar-of-bulleh-shah` (one citation, and *at second hand*, through a commentary on his kāfīs rather than an edition of them, p. 279, folio 261), `garh-maharaja-shorkot` (one citation, p. 280, folio 262), `mazar-e-iqbal` (one sentence, p. 274, folio 256), `data-darbar` (one citation of the Nicholson Kashf al-maḥjūb, p. 276, folio 258).
- **`rahman-baba-mausoleum-rehman-baba-shrine` is the weakest of the seven.** "Rahman Baba" appears only *inside a book title* — `Jens Enevoldsen, Selections from Rahman Baba` (p. 278, folio 260) — in a list of three anthologies of Afghan/Pashto poetry. The book says nothing about him. I take the id because the 18 September ruling covers a literary reference and an endnote citation is such a mention, but a consolidator may reasonably weigh a name-in-a-title lower than a name in prose.
- **The book spells two of the archive's principal figures differently from the archive.** "Hz. Bullhe Shah" (p. 279, folio 261) against the row's "Bulleh Shah (Abdullah Shah Qadri)"; "Sacha! Sarmast [text-layer?: Sachal Sarmast]" (p. 279, folio 261) against the row's "Sachal Sarmast (Abdul Wahab Faruqi)". Both are recorded as printed; neither is silently normalised.
- **No archive TOPONYM appears in this chunk. Explicit negative, checked against `toponym_map.txt` line by line and against `shrine_index.tsv`'s `location_short` column for the 49 truncated rows.** Not one of the map's thirty-one places (Lahore, Peshawar, Karachi, Multan / Multan City, Nankana Sahib, Rawalpindi, Sialkot, Uch Sharif, Bahawalpur, Islamabad / Islamabad Capital Territory, Miani Sahib Graveyard, Dadu, Eminabad, Jhal Magsi, Shikarpur, Sukkur, Anarkali, Gujranwala, Jamshoro, Khairpur, Margalla Hills, Matiari, Nagarparkar, Nowshera, Okara, Quetta, Saidpur Village, Tharparkar, Walled City) is named on any of these twenty-two pages. **Ruling (a) therefore yields nothing here, and no place-level bullet is written.**
