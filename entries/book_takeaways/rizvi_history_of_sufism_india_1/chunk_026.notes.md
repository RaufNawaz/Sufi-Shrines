# rizvi_history_of_sufism_india_1 — chunk 026 — pp. 442–462

**The folio regime is printed on twenty of the twenty-one pages, and the brief's statement about the Index rectos is wrong in a way worth correcting.** All fifteen bibliography pages carry the folio in the running header exactly as the brief predicts: recto `Bibliography 419` (p. 443), verso `418 A History of Sufism in India` (p. 442), so pp. 442–456 print folios 418–432 with no gap. **The Index is the correction.** p. 457 prints only the word `Index` and no number — genuinely `folio not stated`. But pp. 459 and 461 print `Index` at the head of column one **and the folio at the foot of the OCR's column-two flow, `435` and `437`** (pp. 459, 461), because the OCR reads this two-column page as column 1 → the `Google` footer artifact → column 2, splitting the running header across the whole page. The versos do the same: p. 458 prints `434` at the top and `A History of Sufism in India` after the artifact, p. 460 prints `436`, p. 462 prints `438`. **So of the six Index pages only p. 457 lacks a printed folio, not three of them.** By the pattern of pp. 459 and 461 a folio `433` should stand on p. 457, and I do not cite it — see Doubts.

**The brief's second structural claim is also wrong and the error matters for the next chunk's boundary.** The brief says p. 456 (folio 432) "ends on `Yusuf Husain`". In fact `Yusuf Husain` is the **first** entry on that page — "Yusuf Husain, L''Inde mystique au moyen age, Paris, 1929." (p. 456, folio 432) — and the page then runs on through Zaehner, Zolondek, Zubayd Ahmad and Zwemer to a final ruled section headed `Periodical literature`, whose last and closing entry is Pearson's *Index Islamicus* (p. 456, folio 432). The bibliography therefore ends on **Pearson**, not Yusuf Husain.

**What this chunk is.** Fifteen pages of bibliography in three parts, then six pages of index broken off mid-letter B. The bibliography's first part — an alphabetical list of Arabic and Persian sources, its heading before this chunk's opening — runs from `Hafiz Abru` (p. 442, folio 418) to `Ziya'u'd-Din Barani` and four `Anonymous` entries (p. 448, folio 424); a second part headed `PRIMARY AND SECONDARY SOURCES / Sanskrit, Hindi and Urdu` runs pp. 448–449 (folios 424–425); a third headed `SECONDARY SOURCES / English and European Languages` runs pp. 450–456 (folios 426–432). **The single largest yield here is the manuscript apparatus: I count forty-one entries that name a place of deposit, and roughly forty-two more that give only a catalogue siglum and number** — all inventoried under Practices, institutions, economy, because manuscript custody is the one institution this chunk documents. **One of the forty-one is held at `Mashhad, Astana-i 'Aliyya`** (p. 445, folio 421), the only repository in the whole bibliography named as an *astana*, and the lead the project asked for; the qualifications are under Doubts and they are real. **Six archive ids are mapped**, four from the bibliography and two from the index.

## Arguments and interpretations

A bibliography argues by its arrangement, and this one makes four statements a consolidator should have.

- **Urdu is classified with Sanskrit and Hindi, not with Persian — and that group alone is labelled both primary and secondary.** The heading reads "PRIMARY AND SECONDARY SOURCES / Sanskrit, Hindi and Urdu" (p. 448, folio 424), against "SECONDARY SOURCES / English and European Languages" (p. 450, folio 426) and an Arabic–Persian list whose heading falls before this chunk. **The author thus treats Urdu scholarship as partly source material and partly commentary, and groups it by script-region rather than by the language of Sufism.**
- **A stated exclusion, printed as a broken sentence.** "The articles published in journals are not listed. The titles and the authors of the articles in the following work:" followed by the Pearson entry (p. 456, folio 432). **The second sentence has no verb and no completion — either the book prints it so or a line is lost; the effect is an editorial policy stated in a sentence that does not finish.** It also contradicts the list itself, which carries at least a dozen journal articles in full — Ritter in *Oriens* three times and in *Orientalische Studien* (pp. 446, folio 422), Macdonald in *JRAS* (p. 453, folio 429), Makdisi in *BSOAS* (p. 453, folio 429), Margoliouth in *Islamic Culture* (p. 453, folio 429), Meier in *Der Islam* and *Eranos-Jahrbuch* (p. 453, folio 429), Molé twice (pp. 453–454, folios 429–430), Browne twice in *JRAS* (p. 451, folio 427), Nazim in *JRAS* (p. 454, folio 430), Yusuf Husain in "F. Asiat" [OCR?] [J. Asiat.] (p. 456, folio 432).
- **The author cites himself nine times, across two of the three sections.** Seven Hindi and Urdu titles — "Adi turk kalin Bharat, Aligarh, 1956", "Khalji kalin Bharat, Aligarh, 1955", "Tughlug kalin Bharat [OCR?] [Tughluq], Aligarh, 1956-57", "Uttar timur kalin Bharat, Aligarh, 1957", "Mughal kalin Bharat, Babur, Aligarh, 1960", "Mughal kalin Bharat, Humayun, Aligarh, 1961-2", and with J.R. Zaidi "Alakh bani, Aligarh, 1971" (p. 449, folio 425) — and two English, "Muslim revivalist movements in northern India in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, Agra, 1965." and "Religious and intellectual history of the Muslims in Akbar's Reign, Delhi, 1975." (p. 454, folio 430). **The first of the two English titles is the unattributed footnote source chunk 023 could not resolve; the author is Rizvi himself.** "Alakh bani" is matched by the index heading "Alakhbani, 336-44" (p. 459, folio 435).
- **Chunk 023's unsourceable Macauliffe is resolved here.** The footnotes at chunk 023's pp. 399–401 cite "Macauliffe, VI, p. 249" and eight further volumes-and-pages with no title; this chunk supplies it: "Macauliffe, M.A., The Sikh religion, Oxford, 1909." (p. 453, folio 429). **Every Kabir verse quoted in chunk 023 therefore comes from a six-volume work on the Sikh religion, which a consolidator should weigh.** Two further chunk-023 sigla are also resolved: "'Literary history of Persia" is "Browne, E.G., A literary history of Persia, 4 vols., London, 1902-24, reprint, Cambridge, 1964." (p. 451, folio 427), and "Ethé, nos. 1276-77" belongs to the Ethé siglum discussed above; **"Rieu, I, pp. 545-46" is the British Museum catalogue** (p. 454, folio 430). **"Descriptive catalogue of Bengali manuscripts", cited four times in chunk 023 with no compiler, is NOT in this bibliography** — I checked the B, C and D runs of all three sections and it is absent.

## Doubts

**The source carries no transcriber apparatus — no `[OCR?]`, no `[illegible]`, no `[folio N]` lines — so every inline flag above is mine, and the inventory below is the damage class list for pp. 442–462, per interim convention (b), applied in both forms. A bibliography is the worst place in a book for tesseract damage and this one bears it out: the corruption sits precisely on shelfmarks, AH dates and transliterated titles.**

**Archive-mapping doubts, most consequential first:**

1. **`bhit-bhit-shah` is the closest call in the chunk and I have not mapped it.** The bibliography prints "Shah 'Abdu'l-Latif, Silsila-i 'Aliya-i Chishtiyya-i Nizamiya-i Fakhriyya-i Sulaimaniyya-i Latifiyya, Tippera, 1917." (p. 447, folio 423). The archive row's principal figure is "Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai", d. 1752, and the 18 September ruling would give him the id on an author-only mention. **Three things in the printed entry argue the other way and none of them is general knowledge: the imprint is Tippera 1917, the work is a Chishti-Nizami-Fakhri-Sulaimani silsila chain, and the chain's last element "Latifiyya" makes the author its eponym — i.e. this Shah 'Abdu'l-Latif is the founder of a twentieth-century Chishti sub-line, not a Sindhi poet of the eighteenth.** Against that: the book gives no dates, no place of origin and no other work, so the identification cannot be closed either way from this chunk. **I have withheld rather than mapped, and a human should settle it; if it reverses, the id is `bhit-bhit-shah` and the evidence is one bibliography line.**
2. **"Guru Nanak" appears seven times as a book's subject and is unmapped for a multi-row collision, exactly as in chunk 023 — and the two chunks are not quite alike, which is worth saying.** Chunk 023 withheld a bare name in a sentence; here he is the titled subject of seven monographs (pp. 451, 452, 453, 454, 455; folios 427, 428, 429, 430, 431), which is stronger evidence. **I have followed chunk 023 for consistency, not because the evidence is the same.** The eighteen rows are: `gurdwara-babay-de-ber`, `gurdwara-balila-sahib-bal-lila-sahib`, `gurdwara-chakki-sahib`, `gurdwara-choa-sahib`, `gurdwara-darbar-sahib-kartarpur`, `gurdwara-khoohi-bhai-lalo-bhai-lalo-di-khooi`, `gurdwara-malji-sahib`, `gurdwara-panja-sahib`, `gurdwara-patti-sahib`, `gurdwara-pehli-patshahi-jind-pir-sukkur`, `gurdwara-rori-sahib`, `gurdwara-sach-khand-sahib`, `gurdwara-sacha-sauda`, `gurdwara-sahib-saidpur-guru-nanak-dev-ji`, `gurdwara-sri-kiara-sahib`, `gurdwara-sri-tilganji-sahib`, `gurdwara-tambo-sahib`, `gurudwara-janam-asthan-nankana-sahib`. **Note that `gurdwara-rori-sahib` is already mapped in this chunk through Bhai Mardana, so a reversal would add seventeen, not eighteen.**
3. **"Gorakhnath" is unmapped for the same two-row collision chunk 023 recorded, but one half of chunk 023's doubt is now closed.** Chunk 023 noted that the book printed only "Gorakh", never "Gorakhnath", so even the orthographic identification was an inference. **This chunk prints "Gorakhnath" twice in full** — "Raghaw, S., Gorakhnath aur unka yug, Delhi, 1963." (p. 449, folio 425) and "Banerjea, A.K., Philosophy of Gorakhnath, Gorakhpur, n.d." (p. 450, folio 426) — **so the spelling question is settled and only the two-row question remains.** The rows are `tilla-jogian` ("Guru Gorakhnath (Nath yogis)") and `gorakhnath-goraknath-temple` ("Guru Gorakhnath"). **These are bibliography entries about him, not by him, so the 18 September ruling applies only by extension.**
4. **`gurdwara-rori-sahib` is mapped on an index line of three words and two folio numbers.** "Bhai Mardana, 384-85" (p. 462, folio 438) gives no Eminabad, no Guru Nanak and no gurdwara. **My reason for mapping rather than withholding is that the archive names him as a principal figure of exactly one row, so this is not a collision — but the consolidator must treat this bullet as evidence that the book discusses him at folios 384-85, not as evidence about the gurdwara.**
5. **"Amnabad, 385" (p. 462, folio 438) may be the archive's Eminabad and I have not treated it as such.** The printed form is "Amnabad"; an m/i confusion would give "Aminabad", which is the Eminabad of three archive rows. **Two things make it tempting and neither is decisive: the folio 385 it lists is one of the two folios listed against "Bhai Mardana, 384-85", and "Babur, Emperor (1526-30), 311, 345, 370, 385-89" (p. 461, folio 437) lists the same folio.** But it is a bare toponym either way, so under interim convention (a) it takes no id. **On a reversal of (a) combined with the OCR reading, the rows are `gurdwara-chakki-sahib`, `gurdwara-khoohi-bhai-lalo-bhai-lalo-di-khooi` and `gurdwara-rori-sahib`. I have not made the reading and the printed string stands as "Amnabad".**
6. **Three near-collisions resolved against the archive, recorded so nobody reopens them.** (i) **`dargah-pir-ratan-nath-jee` — NOT mapped.** "Baba Ratan, Hajji, 320, 354" (p. 461, folio 437) is styled *Hajji*, is listed at the same folio 320 as "Bhatinda" (p. 462, folio 438), and carries no Nath, no Peshawar and no Jhanda Bazar; the archive's figure is "Baba Pir Ratan Nath".
