# shackle_risalo_shah_abdul_latif — chunk 007 — pp. 234–274

## What this chunk is

**Substantive Risalo text, not apparatus.** Seven numbered and titled sections of the poem, printed
as English verse only: the tail of **18 Ramakali** (from mid-verse 59 to verse 118V, pp. 234–244),
then **19 Khahori** complete (vv. 1–32V, pp. 245–250), **20 Purab** complete (vv. 1–20V,
pp. 251–254), **21 Karayal** complete (vv. 1–20V, pp. 255–257), **22 Sarang** complete (vv. 1–43V,
pp. 258–266), **23 Rip** complete (vv. 1–29V, pp. 267–271), and **24 Barvo Sindhi** from verse 1 to
verse 17 at the chunk boundary (pp. 272–274). The running head alternates `risalo` with the section
head — `18 || ramakali`, `19 || khahori`, `20 || purab`, `21 || karayal`, `22 || sarang`, `23 || rip`,
`24 || barvo sindhi` (pp. 234–274). As in chunk 006, **the chunk carries no translator's prose at
all**: no headnote to any of the seven sections and no footnote texts, only superscript markers.
Printed page numbers run 207 to 247 (printed page = PDF page − 27). **One archive row is touched —
`bhit-bhit-shah` — and only as a place-name in a rain catalogue.**

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The section titles as printed.** `19 Khahori` (p. 245), `20 Purab` (p. 251), `21 Karayal`
  (p. 255), `22 Sarang` (p. 258), `23 Rip` (p. 267), `24 Barvo Sindhi` (p. 272). **The chunk nowhere
  states what any of these titles denotes** — no headnote, no gloss. Record the names; do not infer.
  "Barvo Sindhi" is the only title in this range carrying a regional or linguistic word, and it is
  unexplained (p. 272).
- **The horn as the yogis' instrument, and the closing vai devoted to it.** "There are still yogis
  who are worthy of their horns. They are sitting and playing, if you would hear them, oh sannyasi"
  (p. 241); "Realize that that the horn you hold disgraces you.37" (p. 242 — the doubled word is as
  printed; see Doubts). Verse 118V measures it against a list of other instruments: "It is not like
  the pipe the camel men play. It wipes out the fute, and the gourd40 is not its equal. It is not

  severed Diyach's head.42… It far surpasses thousands of other instruments. Large bells charm wild

- The earth's salutation: "the very earth says bismillāh and kisses the path on which he goes. The

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

*The translator adds nothing in this range — no headnote, no visible footnote text. What follows is
the poet's or the speaker's own judgement, as printed.*

- **Two consecutive verses state opposite verdicts on the Hinglaj pilgrimage, and the chunk reconciles
  neither.** Verse 104: "The yogis did not look at the place where the abode of the lord is. The
  yogis with misguided faith traveled on a long journey. He is here, but they went in vain to
  Hinglaj" (p. 241). Verse 105: "The yogis did look34 at the place where the abode of the lord is.
  The yogis with rightly guided faith traveled on a long journey. He is right here, and they found
  him in Hinglaj" (p. 242). The distinction the verses turn on is stated as "misguided" against
  "rightly guided" faith, nothing more. **This pair is the most consequential thing in the chunk for
  any argument about pilgrimage, and a footnote marker (34) sits on the pivot word.**
- **A parallel inversion on the quilt, also in consecutive verses**: "Quilts38 inside and silk
  outside—yogis of this sort are slaves of donkeys. Silk inside and quilts outside—yogis of this sort
  are slaves of God" (p. 243). The test is placed on what is hidden, not on what is worn.
- **Being worshipped is named as the yogi's characteristic fault**, twice in succession: "Do not let
  yourself be worshiped, oh yogi, curses on people's devotion… keep your mouth from pretense"
  (p. 237); "Do not let yourself be worshiped, oh yogi, but maintain your yoga. Oh master, to make
  people your servants is a great fault" (p. 237). **A judgement against the shrine relation itself,
  in the poet's voice.**
- **Yoga set outside obligatory worship and outside the law.** "They do not bow down to the lord, nor
  does the lord make them bow down. They do not make yoga an object of pilgrimage or worship"
  (pp. 234–235); "Why should they ask about the religious law when the court is within them?22"
  (p. 236); against which "It was not the naked ones who bowed to their lord, but their love that
  made them bow before him" (p. 234).
- **Renunciation tested by hunger rather than by observance**: "Those who have not killed their lower
  self fnd no favor with the lord" (p. 235); "God is still far away from the sannyasi who is
  concerned about clothing and food" (p. 240); "The yogis have flled their bags with hunger and
  rejoice. They have no desire for food, they pour out thirst and drink it" (p. 239).
- **Lineage and caste explicitly denied of the ascetics**: "They have no ancestry or lineage, no
  mother or father" (p. 237). Beside it, the speaker of section 19's closing vai declares the
  opposite of herself: "I am of low caste. Recognize yourself, beloved… If they graciously call me
  their slave girl, then I am fortunate" (p. 250).
- **Being lost preferred to being guided, as a stated paradox**: "Those who lost their way look and
  search in the mountains. Learning from the lost path, they do not proceed along the right track.
  Abandoning both worlds, they do not ask about the correct route" (p. 249); "In being guided there
  are many snakes; being lost is like honey. Awareness comes to the one who wanders away from them
  both" (p. 249); "Wherever men wander lost is where the path to the beloved lies. Only one person in
  a million fnds a trace of that country" (pp. 248–249).
- **Search conditioned on a lamp, stated twice in reversed order**: "Searching is very difcult, do
  not search without a lamp" (p. 241); "Do not search without a lamp, searching is very difcult.
  Hundreds and thousands and millions have been blinded by this darkness.33" (p. 241); then
  undercut — "What you thought was a lamp is the brilliance of the sun. For the blind it is still
  dark when night passes and turns into day" (p. 241).
- **Effort measured against ordinary appetite**: "If people looked for the spiritual path in the way
  that they look for bread, they would have crawled and found the way, says Latif, and their pain
  would be removed" (p. 240).
- **Divine immanence stated as identity with the whole scene**: "He is the bird, the cage, the lake,
  and the wild goose. When I looked within myself, I realized that the hunter whom the body fears3
  prowls about inside me" (p. 256); "Recognize him as the one in everything" (p. 243); "The clouds
  gather in the heart, even though there is no cloud outside" (p. 266); "What shall I do with clouds?
  It is inside me that it rains" (p. 268).
- **Suffering revalued as honour and as method**: "Those who are in pain fnd honor in traveling along
  the path" (p. 267); "Seeking adversity, they arise and keep company with sufering" (p. 235);
  "People think it is hunger that makes ascetics thin, but it is actually the pains of love"
  (p. 253); "Sufering has no hands or feet, the pain of love operates internally" (p. 267).
- **Concealment of pain enjoined**: "Open your heart to those who know its secrets, do not reveal
  them by weeping" (p. 267); "Weep in secret, do not reveal your pain while you are apart from the
  beloved" (p. 267) — consistent with the kiln verses (p. 270).
- **A prayer for Sindh and for the world, closing the long rain-catalogue**: "Oh my God, make Sindh
  fourish forever. Dear friend and sweet beloved, make the whole world prosperous" (p. 265).
- **Hoarders and oppressors cursed together**, an economic judgement in the poet's mouth: "May all
  hoarders and all oppressors perish. The farmers have again spoken of plentiful rain. Everyone is
  supported by your favor, says Shah" (p. 266).
- **Death urged as immediate rather than prospective**: "You sit thinking of last year; do something
  now about going. Die today, oh yogi; everyone will die tomorrow" (p. 240).
- **The rains identified with love and with the beloved, stated as an equivalence and not a simile**:
  "The rains are synonymous with love.9" (p. 262); "Today my beloved has taken on the form of the
  rain" (p. 258); "Today my beloved has appeared as the rains" (p. 266).

## Doubts

- **The `bhit-bhit-shah` mapping rests on a place-name in a rain catalogue and nothing else.** Bhit
  is named twice (both p. 261), inside a run of verses that also rain on Kirar, Variyasu, Makani,
  Mount Pab, the Garang channel, Hadakut and Padham lake. **The chunk does not connect Bhit to a
  shrine, a grave, the poet's residence, or any building or practice.** That the book is Shah Abdul
  Latif's Risalo and the archive row's principal figure is Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai is knowledge from
  outside this chunk and is deliberately not used here. A human must decide whether a bare
  place-name in the poem is enough to carry into the entry.
- **Hinglaj (pp. 238, 241, 242, 254) may correspond to an archive row, and that row cannot be
  cited.** As recorded in chunk 006, `shrine_index.tsv` carries a row named *Shaktipeeth Shri Hinglaj
  Mata Mandir* (Hingol Balochistan National Park, Road، Asha Pura, Pakistan) whose **id column is
  empty** — the only such row in the index. With no id there is no valid mapping, so the Hinglaj
  material sits under "Other saints, sites and events". **This chunk makes the problem more urgent
  than chunk 006 did: verses 104 and 105 (pp. 241–242) are the book's explicit argument about
  Hinglaj, and they cannot be filed to a row until that row gets an id.**
- **"Gorakhnath" (p. 237) matches the principal figure of two archive rows and is not assigned to
  either.** `gorakhnath-goraknath-temple` (Gorakhnath Temple, Gor Khatri, Peshawar — principal figure
  Guru Gorakhnath) and `tilla-jogian` (Tilla Jogian, Salt Range — principal figure "Guru Gorakhnath
  (Nath yogis)"). The chunk names the figure only, with no place, so nothing distinguishes the two
  rows. **Do not pick one.**
- **"Kalat" (p. 251) must not be mapped to `kalat-kali-temple`.** The chunk names Kalat only as
  somewhere the beloved might have gone; no temple, deity or building is mentioned there. The shared
  place-name is the whole of the resemblance.
- **"Jhok" (p. 264) must not be mapped to `dargah-roza-sufi-shah-inayat-shaheed` on this chunk's
  evidence.** That row's location field reads "Jhok Sharif (Jhok), Sindh, Pakistan", but the row's
  subject is the dargah and its figure, and the chunk says only "the beloved thunders near Jhok.14"
  — a rain-line with a footnote whose text is not in this chunk. **Flagged rather than mapped; a
  human should read footnote 14 in the PDF before deciding.**
- **"Umarkot" (p. 265) again must not be mapped to `umarkot-amarkot-shiv-mandir`**, for the reason
  given in chunk 006: it appears only as one name in a list of places rained on.
- **"Shiv" and "Ram" (pp. 234–236, 240) name deities and no site.** Shiva (Mahadev) is the principal
  figure of several archive rows and Ram of `ram-mandir-saidpur-ram-kund-mandir`, but the chunk
  attaches no temple, place or practice-at-a-site to either name, so no row is informed. Do not map a
  deity-name to a row.
- **No footnote text is in this chunk, and the markers are dense.** Section 18 continues 22–43
  (pp. 236–244); section 19 runs 1–3; 20 runs 1–2; 21 runs 1–5; 22 runs 1–17; 23 runs 1–2; 24 runs
  1–6. Everything that hangs on a note is unreadable from here, including the glosses for **Hinglaj,
  Gorakhnath, Mount Ganjo, Junagarh, Diyach, Suhini, Jhok, Khambhat, Kachchh, Variyasu, Savan, Kalat,
  "He is one", "Take counsel with the truth and take counsel with patience", "bismillāh", "the call
  to prayer that preceded Islam", "the feld where Ali fought", and the "measuring stick"**. Note 34
  (p. 242) sits on the verb that reverses verse 104 into verse 105 and is the single most important
  missing note in this chunk.
- **The "V" suffix remains unexplained, now across six further sections.** Verses **118V** (p. 243),
  **32V** (p. 250), **20V** (p. 254), **20V** (p. 257), **43V** (p. 266) and **29V** (p. 270) are
  each the last of their section and each printed as a run of short lines rather than as a block —
  the same pattern chunk 006 recorded for 108V, 27V and 13V. **The chunk still nowhere says what "V"
  marks.** Six more instances make it a systematic feature of the edition, not an accident.
- **"Ali says" (p. 271) is an attribution formula found nowhere else in this range.** In the closing
  vai of section 23: "In our difculties, Ali says, the beloved answers our call." Everywhere else the
  formula is "says Latif", "says Shah" or "says Abdul Latif". Whether this is the poet using a
  different name for himself, a quotation of another speaker, or a text-layer or typesetting error
  **cannot be determined from this chunk** and must be checked against the PDF.
- **"Realize that that the horn you hold disgraces you.37" (p. 242) prints "that" twice.** Either a
  typesetting error in the book or an extraction artefact; do not quote the line without checking.
- **Systematic text-layer damage: the fi/fl ligatures and the doubled f are lost throughout, and per
  instruction the damaged words above are quoted as they stand, without inline marks.** This is a
  deliberate divergence from chunk 006, which inserted `[text-layer?]` after each damaged word —
  **Pass 2 must not read the absence of marks here as an absence of damage.** The forms occurring in
  this chunk are: "sufer"/"sufering"/"suferings", "fll"/"flled"/"flling"/"flls"/"fulflled",
  "fnd"/"fnds"/"fnished", "fre"/"fred"/"bonfre", "fame", "fowers"/"fowerlike", "fy"/"few"/"fies"/
  "fown"/"fashes", "food"/"foods", "fute", "fddles", "fail", "fattery", "fesh", "ferce", "fne",
  "frmly", "fxes"/"fx", "fourishing"/"fourish"/"fourishingly", "overfows", "difcult"/"difculties",
  "afair", "afects", "afair", "efect", "efort", "ofered"/"ofering", "sacrifce", "safron",
  "infnity", "stafs", "bufalo"/"bufaloes", "fve"/"ffteen", and "of" for "off" ("set of on their
  travels", p. 236; "fnished of their bodies", p. 240; "seeing him is still a long way of", p. 273).
  **None of these are corrected here.**
- **Four damaged words where the reading changes the sense and the PDF must be consulted.** "the
  stone fail that beats the corn" (p. 274) — *flail* is a reading, not what is printed; "It is for
  just a little while, this fattery" (p. 257) — *flattery*; "my eyes food with tears" (p. 253) and
  "cause most wonderful foods" (p. 265) — *flood/floods* in both, but *foods* is grammatically
  possible at p. 265 and would change the verse's economy. Do not quote any of these four without
  checking the page.
- **No Sindhi original text appears in this chunk except one transliterated Arabic word.** The only
  non-English form preserved with diacritics is **bismillāh** (p. 274). Otherwise the retained
  non-English forms are the section titles (**Ramakali, Khahori, Purab, Karayal, Sarang, Rip, Barvo
  Sindhi**) and the terms **Khahori(s)**, **sannyasi(s)**, **faqirs**/**fakirs**, **rebab**,
  **Savan**, **Ganjo**, **Kirar**, **Variyasu**, **Makani**, **Hadakut**, **Garang**, **Padham**.
  Printed page numbers run 207–247 across PDF pp. 234–274, so no facing original page falls in this
  range. **Note the inconsistent spelling "faqirs" (p. 240) against "fakirs" (p. 236) within the same
  section** — as printed, not corrected.
- **Six place-names in section 22 could not be placed at all from this chunk**: Kirar, Variyasu,
  Makani, Hadakut, Garang and Padham (pp. 261–262). Each carries or sits beside a footnote marker
  whose text is missing. Kirar matters most, since it is the name the chunk twice pairs with Bhit.
- **Section 18 opens mid-sentence at the chunk boundary**, completing verse 59 from chunk 006: that
  chunk ended "By day their bodies are in" (p. 233) and this one begins "pain, and they sufer all
  night long" (p. 234). The two chunks must be read together for that verse.
- **Section 24 Barvo Sindhi breaks at the end of the chunk**, after verse 17 (p. 274). Its remaining
  verses and its vai are in the next chunk, and whether the section ends with a "V" verse cannot be
  confirmed here.
- **Speaker attribution is again unreliable.** Direct speech is opened and closed inconsistently in
  the text layer — e.g. the quotation at p. 252 verse 11 opens "My darling, some strong reason has
  delayed you" and closes on the next page (p. 253), and verse 13 (p. 253) and verse 27 (p. 249)
  carry quotation marks with no clear speaker. Which runs are the poet's narration and which are a
  speaker's words should not be asserted from this text.
