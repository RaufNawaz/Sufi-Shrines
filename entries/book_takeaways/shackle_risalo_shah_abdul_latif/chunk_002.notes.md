# shackle_risalo_shah_abdul_latif — chunk 002 — pp. 41–80

Continuous translated verse, no editorial apparatus. The chunk runs from the middle of **Sur 2,
Yaman Kalyan** (it opens at verse 7 on p. 41 and closes at verse 123V on p. 58), through **Sur 3,
Asa** complete (vv. 1–58V, pp. 59–68), **Sur 4, Khambhat** complete (vv. 1–40V, pp. 69–75), and
into **Sur 5, Sirirag**, which is cut mid-sur at verse 29 (pp. 76–80). Sur names are given here
exactly as the running heads and sur headings print them: `2 || yaman kalyan`, `3 Asa` /
`3 || asa`, `4 Khambhat` / `4 || khambhat`, `5 Sirirag` / `5 || sirirag`. **There is no
translator's introduction, no headnote, no gloss and no facing original text anywhere in this
range** — nothing but numbered translated verse and superscript note-markers whose notes fall
outside the chunk. **No shrine, place, date, order or biographical fact of any kind appears in
these forty pages.**

  the *takhallus* appears as **"says Latif"** (p. 45; also pp. 56, 74, 77), **"says Shah"**
  (p. 48; also pp. 49, 76, 80), **"Abdul Latif says"** (p. 58) and **"says Abdul Latif"** (pp. 68,
  77). These are the only occurrences of the poet's own name in the chunk, and they occur only

  title and on the running head **"risalo"** (pp. 41, 43, 45, 47 and throughout), not on any
  statement made in the chunk; see Doubts.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Each sur is titled with a musical mode and the verse numbering restarts at 1 within it.**
  Yaman Kalyan reaches 123 (p. 58), Asa 58 (p. 67), Khambhat 40 (p. 75), Sirirag at least 29
  (p. 80). The **final verse of each sur carries the suffix "V"** and is set as a run of long
  lines rather than a numbered stanza: **123V** (p. 58), **58V** (pp. 67–68), **40V** (p. 75).
  The chunk nowhere explains the suffix.
- **Music and musicians.** "For those whose body is a rosary, whose mind is a bead, and whose
  heart is the lute, the strings of seeking resound with the mystery of unity. *He is one and he
  has no partner* is the tune their veins play" (v. 57, p. 67) — the **lute** is the only
  instrument named in the chunk. A **singer** is named once, and set above the poet: "Oh poet, you
  have acquired this desire from the singer.1" (v. 45, p. 46).
- **Veneration of the remains of the devout — the one passage in the chunk that touches anything
  like shrine practice.** "The ashes of those who spend the whole night awake in remembrance of
  God fnd [text-layer?] honor, says Abdul Latif. Thousands come before them and ofer [text-layer?]
  their respects" (v. 12, p. 77).
- **Attendance at a door as the lover's discipline** — the chunk's dominant social image, and the
  nearest thing in it to shrine-going, though the chunk never calls it that: "Every day they go to
  the beloved's door and weep. In no other way can they fnd [text-layer?] acceptance with him"
  (v. 100, p. 55); "Oh lover, keep sitting in the beloved's street. Do not lose heart and quit his
  door" (v. 105, p. 55); "keep sitting in the beloved's shop. Bow down before him with humbly
  covered head" (v. 106, p. 56); "keep sitting in the beloved's passageway" (v. 107, p. 56);
  "It is foolishness to go the beloved's door all the time. Do not hurry to his neighborhood"
  (v. 108, p. 56); and the closing vai of Sur 4: "Oh God, although you chase me away, I will not
  leave your door. … I have seen many other doors, but you are the only one for me" (v. 40V,
  p. 75). Also "At the beloved's door there are many lovers like me" (v. 123V, p. 58).
- **Dust of the feet as kohl.** "Realize that special dust trodden by his feet is perfect kohl for
  the eyes" (v. 123V, p. 58).
- **The forty-day retreat (a "forty-day vigil"), twice rejected.** "A forty-day vigil14 is not
  equal to a sight of the beloved" (v. 85, p. 52); "The body is a mosque and the mind is a cell;
  do not keep a forty-day vigil. Why do you not worship God the unseen twenty-four hours a day?"
  (v. 86, p. 52).
- **Recitation practice.** A "spoken formula" recited all day (v. 69, p. 50); a "closing formula.3"
  that the Sufi will not discuss — "For him it is a sin to reveal this" (v. 63, p. 49); silent
  interior recitation — "They recite the holy name of God in their hearts" (v. 74, p. 51); "Those
  who have learned the formula of pain, recite the lesson of sufering [text-layer?]. Holding the
  slate of contemplation, they study in silence" (v. 75, p. 51).
- **Rosary and bead** as bodily metaphor (v. 57, p. 67).
- **Canonical prayer, prostration, fasting, Hajj and the profession of faith, each measured
  against interior sight.** "So long as you can see yourself, your prayer is of no use… then say
  'God is great'" (v. 5, p. 59), repeated for prostration (v. 6, p. 59); "There is no standing or
  sitting in prayer for them, nor do they perform prostrations" (v. 8, p. 60); "Take out your eyes
  before you break your fast… Seeing the beloved's face is equal to eating seventy dishes" (v. 10,
  p. 60); "Entering his presence is as if one's being, body, and soul have performed the Hajj"
  (v. 11, p. 60); "Faith does not come about by claiming to recite the profession of faith, when
  the heart is flled [text-layer?] with deceit, polytheism, and the devil" (v. 36, p. 64).
- **Sufi dress — the tall cap.** "It is unftting [text-layer?] for a Suf [text-layer?] to call
  himself one and yet to be full of desire. Cut up your tall Suf [text-layer?] cap and throw it
  into the fre [text-layer?]" (v. 67, p. 50); "If you wear a Suf [text-layer?] cap, then be a
  proper Suf [text-layer?]" (v. 68, p. 50).
- **Yogis and ascetic practice.** "Be aware, oh deserving one, and like the yogis keep your
  loincloth tight" (v. 53, p. 66).
- **The four stations, in the translator's English.** "Make the Way your support, recognize the
  Law. Get your heart used to Reality and know the place of Gnosis.11" (v. 52, p. 66).
- **Spiritual masters.** "The power of helping us see is vested in our spiritual masters" (v. 31,
  p. 63); and the failure to become one: "They died deprived. They did not become masters before
  their death" (v. 32, p. 63).
- **The poor in their huts, and eating with them.** "There is something about these old huts we
  see. Although no one notices those who live there, they are accepted at his door. / They are
  considered strangers, but they eat with the beloved" (v. 58V, p. 67), continuing "Leave your own
  ideas on the ground, and follow what they think. / Serve them courteously, humbly covering your
  head. / Sisters, says Abdul Latif, it is here that you will fnd [text-layer?] something"
  (p. 68).
- **Medicine as a trade, in detail** (Sur 2, vv. 12–25, pp. 41–43): prescribed diet and its
  breach (v. 16, p. 42); cataracts (v. 15, p. 42); bandages, plasters and self-dressing of wounds
  (vv. 7–8, p. 41); cutting and **branding** — "Bad doctors cut me and did not put me together.
  The ignorant branded me, and gave my body pain" (v. 22, p. 43); **pulse-taking** — "He checks my
  pulse and gives me the medicine of his mercy" (v. 24, p. 43); doctors driving the wounded from
  their house (v. 18, p. 42); patients refusing treatment (vv. 13–14, p. 42).
- **Blacksmithing and steel** (vv. 35–42, pp. 45–46): "They heat ore and produce steel. Only expert
  blacksmiths know about them" (v. 35, p. 45); the **stoker** heaping coals, and "the unsmelted
  steel breaks into pieces" (v. 36, p. 45); anvil and blows (vv. 38–39, pp. 45–46); the
  **whetstone** whetted with water to sharpen swords, and removing rust to "reveal the steel"
  (vv. 40–42, p. 46).
- **Wine selling and distilling as a named low-status trade** (vv. 47–61, pp. 47–49): a **wine
  seller** with a shop, jars and a courtyard; a **cupbearer**; a **tavern**; **stills** and a
  **distiller**. "The wine seller is no gentleman, but comes from a low caste. He destroys the
  drinkers by pouring cups of wine down their throats" (v. 53, p. 47). Travellers are the
  clientele: "Give the travelers who walk past plenty to drink. Oh wine seller, that will make your
  shop popular" (v. 47, p. 47); "see how they will give lakhs for every drop you give them"
  (v. 48, p. 47); "Sprinkle the dew of the tavern on the travelers… when the morning drinkers have
  come into your courtyard" (v. 51, p. 47). Burial at the workplace: "Those who were overcome by
  what he said lie buried beside his stills" (v. 58, p. 48). Payment in the head: "Give your head
  and somehow make a deal with the distiller… a cup costs more than that" (v. 59, p. 48); "They
  come right into his shop, ready to sacrifce [text-layer?] their heads" (v. 60, pp. 48–49).
- **Thieves, chains and the gallows as a model of secrecy.** "learn from the way that thieves
  behave. They celebrate by keeping awake and taking no rest all night long… When they are chained
  together and put on the gallows, they say nothing. Although they are cut with knives, they reveal
  nothing" (v. 17, p. 42).
- **Literacy, the scribe and the law court.** "Why did you study letters, you wretch, and become a
  qazi?" (v. 78, p. 51); "Oh scribe, just as you write lām joined to alif,13 so does the beloved
  remain joined to our soul" (v. 84, p. 52); "Oh scribe, why do you pile pages on pages?" (v. 85,
  p. 52); "the line that begins with alif.7" (vv. 76–77, p. 51); "Read the letter alif, forget all
  the other pages" (v. 82, p. 52); "Keep a legal adviser19 within you, so that you will not be
  helpless before the judge" (v. 119, pp. 57–58).
- **Camel-herding and the pastoral economy** (Sur 4, vv. 16–39, pp. 71–75). Named feeds and
  plants, as printed with the book's diacritics: **sandal** (and "buds of sandal", "sandalwood",
  "sandal juice"), **cardamoms**, "bended branches of **henna**", the **lāṇī** bush (marked note
  1, first at v. 20, p. 72), the **ak**2 plant (v. 32, p. 74), the **khaṭaṇahāru**4 plant, whose
  white flowers the camel refuses (v. 36, p. 74), and the **sirkhanḍu**5 (v. 36, p. 75). Gear:
  hobbles on "both forefeet and on both rear legs and chains of steel around its chest" (v. 34,
  p. 74), hobble rope and twisted ropes (vv. 30, 33, pp. 73–74), nosebag (v. 28, p. 73), "a silken
  saddlecloth" and neck ornaments of rubies and pearls (v. 24, p. 72), "a leading rein of gold"
  (v. 25, p. 73), blinkers (v. 35, p. 74). **Prices, as printed:** "Two branches cost millions, and
  leaves cost half a million a quarter. That is what my camel eats every day" (v. 38, p. 75);
  "Give millions to drive my precious camel" (v. 39, p. 75).
- **Field ownership and watchmen.** "The camel greedily thrust its mouth into the creepers. The
  owners of the feld [text-layer?] became aware of this, and the watchmen threatened it" (v. 29,
  p. 73).
- **The oil press and press-bullocks.** "You have blinkers on your eyes, and your feet are chafed
  by the oil press.3 Have you forgotten your herd that was bound and used to turn the press?"
  (v. 35, p. 74).
- **A woman's lack of a mount.** "I cannot get there on foot, and my father will not give me a
  camel to mount early in the morning and ride toward my beloved" (v. 16, p. 71); "I have no camel
  or horse to take me to my beloved by night, and I cannot get there on foot. I am helpless and sit

  (vv. 11 p. 41; 22 p. 72; 27 p. 73; 34 p. 74) and **"Sisters"** (p. 68).
- **Seafaring, pilotage and maritime trade** (Sur 5, pp. 76–80). Vessel and gear: sail washed and
  whitened "with soap" (v. 2, p. 76), mast, rigging, rudder blade, timbers, planks that leak,
  holes stopped and timbers oiled daily before launch (v. 29, p. 80), an old boat with a loose
  rigging kept going "so long as the steersman is strong" (v. 28, p. 80), a **telescope** (v. 4,

  (v. 15, p. 78); and a limit on their knowledge — "Pilots gather and bring reports on waters close

  **Wind:** "The wind blows from the north, the wine seller has opened his jars" (v. 50, p. 47) and
  "Trusting in the north wind, yesterday they unfurled their sails from the mast. … The traders
  have loaded their craft with merchandise" (v. 24, p. 80).
- **Pearl diving.** "Divers know the way to explore the ocean. They have entered the depths and
  gathered gems. They have brought up handfuls of diamonds and rubies and given them away" (v. 17,
  p. 78); and the divers' equipment — "Those who went into the sea with protective glass over their
  faces1 entered the depths and sought out shells" (v. 19, p. 79).
- **Commodity trade and its grades.** "I dealt in glass, I did not deal in pearls. I did business
  in lead, says Shah" (v. 5, p. 76); "I traded in worthless glass and broke my contract with God"
  (v. 6, p. 76); "Trade in the merchandise that does not become old by being stored, and that does
  not incur the slightest loss when sold abroad" (v. 25, p. 80). And the lover's currency: "He
  carries no money, but uses his head to trade with" (v. 101, p. 55).
- **Cosmetics as social marking.** "Apply the mascara stick of union to your eyes" (v. 39, p. 64);
  "The blackness of mascara is suitable for women. As a man, do not apply blackness with a stick.
  Put the redness of the beloved on your eyes" (v. 40, p. 65); red mascara and "the splendor of a
  scarlet wedding outft [text-layer?]" (v. 41, p. 65); white mascara (v. 42, p. 65).
- **Marriage imagery as reward.** "Enter the beloved's brightness and become his bridegroom"
  (v. 31, p. 44); "They who please the beloved are the ones who will enjoy their marriage
  beds… they are chosen and are adorned as bridegrooms" (v. 55, p. 67).
- **Non-Muslim markers named from outside.** "You are certainly no Hindu, nor are you worthy of the
  sacred thread. The forehead mark is properly put on those who are true to polytheism" (v. 37,
  p. 64).
- **Domestic economy, in passing.** "Love is not created in a grain jar, meat is not cooked in
  husks" (v. 48, p. 66); cloth dyeing — "Dyed without alum, they delight in the company of the
  beloved" (v. 12, p. 60); "Let me roast on burning sticks of thorn and acacia" (v. 27, p. 44).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The blind men and the elephant**, told as a parable and turned to the authority of the
  master: "The blind men quarreled about the dead elephant.5 They felt with their hands, being
  unable to see with their eyes. Actually,6 only the sighted can recognize the elephant. The power
  of helping us see is vested in our spiritual masters" (v. 31, p. 63). Notes 5 and 6 are outside
  the chunk.
- **Azazil damned by an excess of love** — asserted, not narrated: "He became accursed because of
  his abundant love.10" (v. 79, p. 51).
- **Rescue from drowning named as a miracle.** "It is the miracle of the lord that he rescues
  people from the deep whirlpool. It is the power of the lord that he takes the drowning out of the
  deep water" (v. 22, p. 79).
- **The moths and the flame**, the chunk's one sustained exemplum (vv. 29–32, pp. 44–45): "Ask the
  moths what burning is like. They hurl themselves into the fre [text-layer?], and their hearts
  are pierced by the lances of love" (v. 29, p. 44); "they were burned in the fre [text-layer?] of
  truth. Many of the poor creatures were consumed" (v. 32, p. 45).
- No karamat of any saint, and no miracle at any site, is recorded anywhere in this chunk.

## Arguments and interpretations

*The chunk contains no translator's voice at all — no headnote, no introduction, no editorial
comment. What follows is the poet's own doctrine as the verse states it.*

- **Duality is the obstacle, stated as a formula and repeated.** "One cup and two people—this is
  not how love works. How will counting get anyone near the beloved? See how their sense of
  separate existence has deprived them of union" (v. 43, p. 46), repeated at vv. 44 and 45
  (p. 46); "No one ever made it across with the 'I.' God is an odd number, and that is what he
  loves, so get rid of duality" (v. 2, p. 59); "A curse on duality! Beloved, stop me from the self"
  (v. 3, p. 59); "The self is a veil over yourself… It is existence that stands in the way of
  union" (v. 45, p. 65); "Get rid of double vision, and enjoy the state of gnosis" (v. 39, p. 64).
- **Annihilation as the condition of union.** "Through adopting nonexistence, creatures were
  exalted" (v. 7, p. 60); "Those who have destroyed their existence are efaced [text-layer?] in
  God.4 … While nonexistent, they are joined with existence" (v. 8, p. 60); "True Sufs
  [text-layer?] are those who take nonexistence with them" (v. 64, p. 49); "The Suf [text-layer?]
  has washed clean the page of his existence. Afterward, while still alive, he gets to see the
  beloved" (v. 66, p. 49).
- **Everything is the divine reality — with the accusation of shirk turned on the doubter.** "If
  you can see properly, you will say that everything is divine reality. Oh blind polytheist, do not
  doubt this truth" (v. 34, p. 64); "Get rid of doubt, of the polytheism that gets in the way of
  afrming [text-layer?] God's existence. There is nothing like the denial in which that existence
  is afrmed [text-layer?]" (v. 35, p. 64).
- **Secrecy as a discipline, not a preference — the chunk's most insistent argument.** "Complete
  all the stages of your spiritual journey in secrecy. Never reveal your passion, says Latif.
  Otherwise people may fnd [text-layer?] out, and an obstacle may be created in the way of your
  union" (v. 33, p. 45); "He does not say anything about the closing formula. For him it is a sin
  to reveal this" (v. 63, p. 49); "Those who have seen this place do not speak of it" (v. 71,
  p. 50); "Do not be naïve and tell passersby about it… Share your love in secret talk between
  yourselves" (v. 108, p. 56); "What is unsaid does not become apparent, what is said no one
  understands" (vv. 111–112, p. 56); "May I burn inside, without any smoke escaping" (v. 44,
  p. 65). Hiding is a physical practice too: "Hiding away from people, they bandage their wounds
  themselves" (v. 7, p. 41); tears betray the secret — "My color was my foe, and revealed my
  secret" (v. 43, p. 65).
- **Performed grief is rejected.** "Mother, I do not believe those who shed tears and show people
  how their eyes water. Those who truly think of the beloved do not weep or say anything" (v. 11,
  p. 41).
- **Book-learning attacked, letters defended only as the single letter alif.** "The learned keep
  reading but do not sufer [text-layer?] in their hearts. The faster they turn the pages, the
  higher their sins mount up" (v. 81, p. 52); "As you turn the pages, the more sins you see. What
  use is talking about him if the beloved is not present?" (v. 83, p. 52); "Read the letter alif,
  forget all the other pages" (v. 82, p. 52); "You may turn twenty pages, but the letter15 is the
  same" (v. 85, p. 52). The qazi is the named target (v. 78, p. 51).
- **A malamati position — outward disrepute against inward truth.** "On the outside they are
  fornicators, but they are lost in contemplation. The arrow of true teaching has pierced their
  inner being" (v. 74, p. 51); "The Suf [text-layer?] is no Kuf [text-layer?],4 no one understands
  him. His struggle takes place within, leaving no external mark. He is on the side of those who
  are at odds with him" (v. 65, p. 49); and the counter-test, that the Sufi cap must be earned or
  burnt (vv. 67–68, p. 50).
- **Interiority set against building and rite.** "The body is a mosque and the mind is a cell"
  (v. 86, p. 52); "Pitch the secret tent of almighty God in your body.5" (v. 69, p. 50); "Examine
  yourself and see him before you in everyone. The beloved is inside my mind" (v. 86, p. 52); "He
  stands before everyone. There is no place without him" (v. 87, p. 53); "If you remove the veil
  from your heart, you will behold him within" (v. 73, p. 50).
- **Suffering is the method, and medicine is refused as an interference with it.** "Do not give me
  medicine, doctor, in case I get well. Perhaps my beloved will come sometime to ask after me"
  (v. 20, p. 43); "It is the one who inficted [text-layer?] my wounds who became my doctor"
  (v. 19, p. 43); "Those who have sufered [text-layer?] pain were granted health. For lovers
  misery is sweet" (v. 109, p. 56); "Desire and death18 both begin with the same letter" (v. 103,
  p. 55).
- **An ethic of patience and non-retaliation, sustained over ten verses** (vv. 113–122,
  pp. 57–58): "Be patient, for those who are patient succeed, while those who quarrel lose"
  (v. 115, p. 57); "There is peace in the homes of the patient, but talkers do not prosper"
  (v. 116, p. 57); "The one who makes the frst [text-layer?] move sufers [text-layer?]" (v. 117,
  p. 57); "If the bow is drawn too hard, the bowstring snaps" (v. 118, p. 57); "With your head upon
  your knees, live in lowliness" (v. 119, p. 57); and a rule about company — "Sitting with some
  people brings you much sufering [text-layer?]. Avoid their company and gain a thousand
  benefts [text-layer?]" (v. 121, p. 58).
- **Rumi named as the authority for the doctrine of creation as a search for God** (vv. 71–73,
  p. 50) — the only named human authority in the chunk besides the poet himself.

## Doubts

- **No archive row is informed by this chunk in the ordinary sense.** Across pp. 41–80 there is no
  shrine, no place in Pakistan or India, no year, no *silsila*, no death or birth, no custodian and
  no institution. Nothing here can enter a data field of any entry.
- **The mapping to `bhit-bhit-shah` is inferential and should be treated as such.** The chunk
