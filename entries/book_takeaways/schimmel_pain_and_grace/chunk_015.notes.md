# schimmel_pain_and_grace — chunk 015 — pp. 276–300

**This chunk is the book's back matter and contains no running prose.** It holds, in order: the tail
of the German verse **APPENDIX** for Khwaja Mir Dard (p. 276, folio 276) with its locator list; the
whole verse appendix for **SHAH ABDUL LATIF** (pp. 277–290, folios 277–290); the **BIBLIOGRAPHY**
in two sections, `a) SOURCES IN EUROPEAN LANGUAGES` (pp. 291–294) and `b) SOURCES IN ORIENTAL
LANGUAGES` (pp. 294–296); the **INDEX OF QURANIC QUOTATIONS AND HADITH** (pp. 297–299); and the
opening page, letter A only, of the **INDEX OF PROPER NAMES AND TECHNICAL TERMS** (p. 300, folio not
stated), which runs past the end of the chunk.

**THE ABBREVIATIONS KEY FOR DARD'S SIGLA IS NOT IN THIS CHUNK.** There is no list of abbreviations,
no key and no siglum-by-siglum expansion anywhere on pp. 276–300. Worse, the bibliography
**explicitly defers to the missing page**: the Dard entry in the oriental-languages section ends
"—, For his other works see the list of abbreviations." (p. 295, folio 295). The Dard verse appendix
on p. 276 then cites its sources by bare unexpanded sigla — only `U` and `P` appear in that list. So
this chunk confirms that the Abbreviations page is the sole locus of the mapping and supplies none of
it. Two partial leads are recorded under `## Bibliography and apparatus` and `## Doubts`; neither is
an identification and neither may be used as one.

**Folio regime: offset 0, confirmed on every page in the chunk that prints a number.** Verso headers
print the number first (`276 APPENDIX`, `278 APPENDIX`, `292 BIBLIOGRAPHY`, `294 BIBLIOGRAPHY`,
`296 BIBLIOGRAPHY`, `298 INDEX OF QURANIC QUOTATIONS AND HADITH`); recto headers print it last

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A prayer for cheap grain, inside the saint's rain-verse.** "Wolke! Um Gottes willen, gewahre dem
  Durstenden Schutz! Mach billiger das Getreide, die trockenen Tiimpel[OCR?] hilf fiillen[OCR?], Das
  Land lass fruchtbar schwellen, dass sich der Landmann freut." ("Cloud! For God's sake, grant the
  thirsty shelter! Make the grain cheaper, help fill the dry pools, let the land swell fruitful, so
  that the farmer rejoices.") (p. 286, folio 286). **A grain price, irrigation tanks and the farmer's
  welfare, asked for in a devotional poem — the most directly economic passage in the chunk.**
- **The agrarian calendar as the setting of devotion.** "Auch heute weht's von Norden, der Kuckuck
  singt sein Lied, Der Bauer spannt den Pflug an, der Hirte froh die Herden sieht" ("today too it
  blows from the north, the cuckoo sings its song, the farmer yokes the plough, the herdsman sees his
  herds gladly") (p. 285, folio 285); and "Es grasen Kiihe[OCR?], fett, im Land und kehren heim zur
  Hirde." ("cows graze, fat, in the land, and return home to the fold") (p. 286, folio 286). The same
  stanza-run has "nun loscht das Vieh des Durstes Brand" ("now the cattle quench their thirst's
  burning") and "Die Teiche fiille[OCR?] du, dass sich der Landmann freut" ("fill Thou the ponds, so
  that the farmer rejoices") (p. 286, folio 286).
- **Renunciation of property as the entry condition to the ascetic village.** Sur Khahori: "Sie lassen
  Felder und Tennen, suchend Gott nahe zu sein" ("they leave fields and threshing-floors, seeking to

- **Dervishes named as a collective.** "Wer koénnt'[OCR?] es wohl entziinden[OCR?], wenn nicht der

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

The chunk reports no karamat and makes no miracle claim. What it carries is the Risalo's romance
material in translation, plus two cosmological narratives, all presented as the poet's verse.

- **The pre-eternal covenant (alastu), told as the heroine's own memory.** "Als Er die Seelen fragte:
  ‘Bin ich nicht euer Herr?’ Ward mir im Herzen zum Omen urewiges Ja, das ich sagte" ("when He asked
  the souls, 'Am I not your Lord?', the pre-eternal Yes that I said became an omen in my heart")
  (p. 281, folio 281). The Quran index anchors it — "v. 171, alastu 209, 243" under "Stra?[OCR?] 7"
  (p. 297, folio not stated).
- **Sohni in the flood, with the clay jar and the crocodiles.** "In der furchtbaren Flut des Flusses,
  wo der Schrei der Schrecken schlug … wohin ging der tonerne Krug!" ("in the terrible flood of the
  river, where the cry of terrors struck … where did the clay jar go!"); "die machtigen Krokodile,
  Gewaltige Alligatoren im Strome, unzahlbar viele" ("the mighty crocodiles, huge alligators in the
  stream, countless many") (p. 281, folio 281). The addressee is "O Sahar, Herrscher du klug" and
  "Furst[OCR?] Sahar" (p. 281, folio 281).
- **The love that precedes creation.** "Die Schonheit des Liebsten bestand, bevor die Geschicke
  geschrieben, Noch war kein ‘Es werde!’ … Die Engel waren noch nicht, als Sohnis Klage entbrannt"
  ("the Beloved's beauty existed before the destinies were written; there was as yet no 'Let it be!'
  … the angels were not yet, when Sohni's lament caught fire") — and the verse names its author:
  "die Liebe — so sagte Latif." ("love — so said Latif") (p. 281, folio 281). **This is the one place
  in the appendix where the poet's own name appears inside the translated verse.**
- **The pearl born of the shell's affliction, given as a lesson for girls.** "So hat sie die Perle
  getroffen, da Drangsal in dunkelster Tiefe sie litt." ("so she met with the pearl, since she
  suffered affliction in the darkest depth"); "So lernet alle, ihr Madchen, von Muscheln, wie Tugend
  tut" ("so learn, all you girls, from mussels, how virtue acts") (pp. 284–285, folios 284–285).
- **The written destiny that could have been washed off.** "Hatt' ich die Schrift des Geschicks von
  urew'ger Tafel gespilt[OCR?]" ("had I washed the writing of fate from the pre-eternal tablet")
  (p. 283, folio 283).

## Arguments and interpretations

The chunk contains no argumentative prose. Three places, and three only, carry an authorial
judgement, and all three are in the apparatus:

- **An attribution is rejected.** Under Muhsin Fani: "— (wrongly ascribed to), Dabistan-i madhahib,
  transl. D. Shea and A. Troyer, London 1901." (p. 295, folio 295). **The book declines the standard
  ascription of the Dabistan and says so in the bibliography.**
- **An edition is singled out for its introduction.** Under Dard: "—, Diwan, ed. Muhammad Habibur
  Rahman Shirwani, Delhi s.d. (with an important introduction)." (p. 295, folio 295).
- **A source's scope is characterised.** Under Quddasi: "Tadhkira-yi sifiya-yi[OCR?] Sindh, Karachi
  1959 (the same author has collected biographies of the saints from the various regions of Pakistan:
  Panjab, Frontier, Bengal)." (p. 296, folio 296). **A pointer to a four-region hagiographical series,
  of which only the Sindh volume is cited.**
- **The selection itself is the argument.** The appendix translates the Risalo's Sur cycles into German
  verse rather than prose, keeps the Sur names in Sindhi, and gives each block a bare numeric locator;
  the bibliography then lists Schimmel's own "Sah © Abdul Latif's[OCR?] Beschreibung des wahren Sufi"
  (p. 293, folio 293) — the study behind the Sufi passage translated at pp. 278–279. **Recorded as an
  editorial correspondence, not as a claim the chunk makes.**
