# schimmel_pain_and_grace — chunk 016 — pp. 301–310

**This chunk is apparatus, not text.** It is the book's `INDEX OF PROPER NAMES AND TECHNICAL TERMS`
and nothing else: ten pages of two-column headwords and page figures, running to the last line of the
file (`Zulaikha 173`, p. 310, folio 310). **There is no prose, no argument, no quotable sentence and
no statement of fact anywhere in it.** Everything below is therefore a finding-aid record: what the
index covers, which archive-relevant headwords it carries and with which page figures, which it does
not carry, and how far its numerals can be trusted. Per the deviation carried over from the sorley
run, the apparatus is recorded under `## Bibliography and apparatus`, which stands where
`## Arguments and interpretations` would fall (that heading is omitted as empty, as are
`## Legends and miracles (karamat)` and `## Citable passages`).

**The index does not begin in this chunk.** PDF p. 301 opens mid-letter-A, with a detached run of page
figures (`58 n, 60, 68, 65-69, 76, 78-92, 102, 109, 115, 125, 128, 130, 131, 134, 136, 173, 265`)
belonging to an entry whose headword is on a page not in this chunk, and only then `Anwari 207`
(p. 301, folio 301). The A entries — including any headword for `ʿAbdul Latif, Shah` — begin on
p. 300 or earlier. **A human must read the preceding page(s) to close the index.**

**FOLIO: every one of the ten pages prints its own number, and folio = PDF throughout.** The measured
statement that this run found no printed number beyond PDF p. 305 does not hold on these pages: pp.
306–310 each show their number in the running-head position, so I use what the page shows and say so
here. The pattern is a clean recto/verso alternation — on odd pages the number ends the header
(`INDEX OF PROPER NAMES AND TECHNICAL TERMS 301`, p. 301; likewise 303, 305, 307, 309), on even pages
it begins it (`302 INDEX OF PROPER NAMES AND TECHNICAL TERMS`, p. 302; likewise 304, 306, 308, 310).

## Bibliography and apparatus

**What the index is.** One single alphabetical sequence titled `INDEX OF PROPER NAMES AND TECHNICAL
TERMS` (p. 301, folio 301), running from mid-A on p. 301 to "Zulaikha 173" on p. 310 (folio 310).
**Proper names and technical terms are merged in one A–Z run** — "Pir" sits between
"Pilgrim’s Progress (Bunyan)" and "Plassey" (p. 307, folio 307) — so there is no separate subject
index, no separate glossary, no index of works and no index of places. **This is the book's only
closing apparatus: no errata, no addenda, no list of illustrations, nothing after the last headword**
(p. 310, folio 310).

**Extent: it indexes both Parts and the front matter.** The two subjects' own ranges are printed in
their headwords — **"Dard, Khwaja Mir IX, X, 2, 14-17, 19, 20, 24, 25, 27, 31-147, 151, 152, 186, 187,
191, 194, 208, 212, 250, 261, 263-269"** (p. 302, folio 302) and **"Risalé[OCR?] (Shah jo) 25, 152-262,
267"** (p. 308, folio 308) — and roman figures IX and X are indexed throughout, so the preface is
covered. **"Sindhi X, 8, 13, 20, 22-24, 26, 77, 151-259, 267"** (p. 308, folio 308) is the widest
language run in the index.

**Structure and sorting conventions, which a user must know:** `Ch` and `Sh` are treated as separate
letters and sorted **after** all of C and of S — "Chanésar, Jam" follows "Corbin, Henri" (p. 302,
folio 302), and the whole Sh sequence from "ash-Shadhili" to "shukr" follows "Syria" (p. 309,
folio 309). The Arabic article and the ʿayn are ignored in sorting ("al-Ash‘ari" under A,
"ash-Shadhili" under Sh, "ʿurs" under U). Cross-references use "s." for see — "Bairagi s. Muhammad
Muʿin" (p. 301, folio 301), "ʿAzazil s. Satan" (p. 301), "Todi s. Sohni" (p. 309, folio 309),
"Mansi[OCR?] s. Hallaj" (p. 306, folio 306) — and "s." is **German *siehe*, not English "see":** the
index also carries untranslated German editorial words, "Marui (auch Sur)" (p. 306, folio 306),
"Sassui (auch Sur)" (p. 308, folio 308), "Sohni (auch Sur)" (p. 308, folio 308). Footnotes are cited
"n.", continuations "ff." and "f." ("219 ff.", "252 ff.", "258 f.", pp. 307–308).

## Doubts

**RELIABILITY OF THE PAGE NUMERALS — the answer the queue needs.** Two separate questions, with
different answers.

1. **The digits themselves are mostly sound.** The overwhelming majority of runs are monotonically
   increasing, which is an internal check the OCR passes. But the corruption is real and it is
   demonstrable: **"58 n, 60, 68, 65-69, …"** (p. 301, folio 301) and **"42, 48,47, 54"** in "Firaq
   Nasir Nadhir" (p. 302, folio 302) are **out of sequence, so at least one numeral in each is wrong**;
   **"Golconda 9, I1, 261"** (p. 303, folio 303) prints a letter for 11; **"Iqbal, Sir Muhammad 1X"**
   (p. 304, folio 304) prints 1X for the roman IX; **"Satan 78, {05, 185"** (p. 308, folio 308) prints
   `{05` for 105; **"Tahir, Shaikh 24, 25)"** (p. 309, folio 309) carries a stray bracket;
   **"Qut al-‘ dshigin (Makhdim Muh. Hashim) 28, 253"** (p. 305, folio 305) reads 28 where the same
   author's other works read 22–23 on pp. 305 and 309; **"Turkey IX, 7, 49, 203, 206, 214, 247. 250"**
   (p. 309, folio 309) and **"Sind … ; Lower 5, 157"** (p. 308, folio 308) have lost punctuation or a
   sub-entry label into the numerals; **"tauhid 3, 50, 75, 83, 117, 212,"** (p. 309, folio 309) ends on
   a comma with a figure possibly lost. **Verdict: no single page figure from this OCR should be cited
   without checking it against the printed page.**
2. **The attachment of runs to headwords is broken on every one of the ten pages, and that is the
   fatal defect.** The two-column layout has collapsed: tesseract has read across columns and dumped
   orphaned number runs at the foot of the page or in the middle of other entries. **At least twenty
   headwords in this chunk stand with no page run at all, and at least thirty number runs stand with
   no headword.** Every page is affected; p. 302 is the worst (eight numberless headwords, ten orphan
   fragments). **The archive-relevant casualties are: "Hinglaj", "Burton, Sir Richard",
   "Ba[OCR?] Ali Qalandar", "Hyderabad/Sind", "Hir Ranjha (Warith Shah)", "Ripa, Sur",
   "Omar of Omarkot", "Mehar, Mehanval, also Sahar" and "Laghari, Nawwab Hajji Noor Ali".**
   **These headwords are unusable from this text and must be read off the printed page.**
3. **Net answer: the index is usable as a coarse finding aid for the majority of entries — those whose
   headword and run sit on one line and whose run is monotonic — and is not usable as authority for
   any individual citation, nor at all for the roughly twenty entries whose runs are detached.** It is
   good enough to tell you *that* a topic is in the book and roughly where; it is not good enough to
   cite. A single check of the printed index against this transcription would fix most of it, because
   the damage is layout damage, not illegibility.

**RECONSTRUCTION FLAGGED FOR A HUMAN — Hinglaj.** On p. 303 (folio 303) exactly three headwords stand
without numbers — "Hinglaj", "Husamuddin Chelebi", "Hyderabad/Sind" — and exactly three number runs
stand without headwords, in this order: "25, 159, 178, 221, 223, 234", "118, 136, 265",
"151, 152 n, 159". **By OCR line order they would attach in that sequence, giving Hinglaj 25, 159,
178, 221, 223, 234.** This is a reconstruction from the OCR's line order, **not** something the page
states, and it is recorded as a hypothesis only. **Do not enter these figures for Hinglaj until the
printed page is checked.** The archive's own defect stands regardless: the row
`Shaktipeeth Shri Hinglaj Mata Mandir` has an empty id cell in shrine_index.tsv, so the headword is
left unmapped and no id is invented.

**WHY THIRTEEN ROWS WERE MAPPED AND THE REST WERE NOT.** An index headword gives a name and page
figures, never a claim, so mapping needs identifying detail from the headword itself. Mapped where the
headword supplies a place ("of Jhok", "of Hala", "Lanwari", "Multani"), a work ("Kashf al-mahjub
(Hujwiri)", "Hir Ranjha (Warith Shah)", "(Shah ʿAbdul Latif)"), or a full distinctive name with no
rival in the archive ("Lal Shahbaz Qalandar", "Sachal Sarmast", "Bullhé Shah", "Sultan Bahu",
"Fariduddin Ganj-i Shakar", "Iqbal, Sir Muhammad"). Not mapped where the headword is a bare place
name whose saint is absent from the index ("Bulrri", "Multan", "Lahore", "Sehwan", "Thatta", "Makli
Hill", "Khairpur"), where the archive's equation of two headwords is the archive's and not the book's
("Lal Udér6" with "Tahir, Shaikh"), where the figure carries no distinguishing detail ("ʿInat, Miyan
Shah"), or where the archive id belongs to a monument the index never mentions. **Two mappings rest on
a name form alone and are the weakest: bhit-bhit-shah, on the place headword "Bhit Shah" plus the
poet's name inside the Sur parentheses; and mazar-e-iqbal, where only the man is indexed.**

**THE POET OF BHIT HAS NO HEADWORD IN THIS CHUNK, and this is the single most useful caution for a
user of the index.** There is no "Shah ʿAbdul Latif", no "ʿAbdul Latif", no "Latif" and no "Shah
Karim" in the L, S or Sh sequences: the L run goes "la … lamakan, Laghari, lahtit, Lahore, Laila,
Lal Qila, Lal Shahbaz Qalandar, Lal Udér6, lam, landay, Las Bela, latafat, Lila, Lucknow, Lutfullah"
(p. 305, folio 305) and the Sh run goes "ash-Shadhili, Shadhiliyya, ash-Shafi‘i, Shafiq, Shah Jahan,
Shahjahanabad, shahada, Shaibani Khan, shaikh …" (p. 309, folio 309). **Dard has a full headword with
a thirty-two-figure run (p. 302, folio 302); his co-subject has none in this range.** Either the poet
is entered under "ʿAbdul Latif" in the A sequence, which falls on a page before p. 301 and outside
this chunk, or he is not separately indexed at all. **A human must read the index's opening page(s) to
settle this**, and until then the only routes to Part II through the index are the ~28 Sur headwords
and "Risalé (Shah jo) 25, 152-262, 267".

**CROSS-REFERENCE HAZARDS — headwords that would silently merge or mislead if followed.**
- **"ʿInat, Miyan Shah 162, 207, 213, 214"** (p. 304, folio 304) and **"(Inayat Shahid of Jhok 21, 22,
  183, 228"** (p. 304, folio 304): **two distinct figures, kept properly apart by the index with
  non-overlapping runs and no cross-reference between them.** That is good news, but a reader
  searching one spelling will never be sent to the other, and a reader merging them would fuse a
  Sindhi poet with the martyr of Jhok. **"Nasrpur 162"** (p. 306, folio 306) shares p. 162 with ʿInat;

  Thana°?ullah[OCR?] Khan 59"** (p. 302, folio 302) — two Firaqs; the first's run is also the one with
