# sorley_shah_abdul_latif_of_bhit — chunk 024 — pp. 427–442

**This chunk is NOT prose. It is the end matter of the book**, and it is used here under the
convention for apparatus: an added heading `## Bibliography and apparatus` is placed after
`## Arguments and interpretations` and carries the index and the glossary. Four distinct pieces of
apparatus follow one another with no connecting text:

1. **pp. 427–429 (folios 418–420): the tail of the verse-translation appendix**, sections numbered
   `VII` and `VIII`, both headed `Marui.`, with one interposed speaker-heading `Sayid.` (p. 429,
   folio 420) and a third short `Marui` section closing the appendix on the same page. The appendix
   ends mid-page at p. 429 (folio 420) with no closing rubric.
2. **pp. 430–431 (folios 421–422): `GLOSSARY OF UNCOMMON WORDS FOUND IN THE POEMS`** — 78 headwords
   from `Ades is` to `Yazid`, recorded term by term below because a large minority of them are
   shrine, ascetic and Sufi vocabulary.
3. **pp. 432–441 (folios 423–432 where printed): `INDEX` / `(to Parts I and II)`** — ten pages,
   double-column. **Not transcribed entry by entry.** Recorded as the protocol for a last chunk
   requires: which archive-relevant saints, shrines, places, practices and institutions the index
   proves the book discusses, and the printed page numbers it gives for each.
4. **p. 442: a single line, `ISBN 0 19 636029`.** That is the entire page and the entire imprint
   matter of the book as transcribed.

**THE −9 OFFSET HOLDS TO THE END OF THE BOOK, ON EVERY PAGE THAT PRINTS A NUMBER, AND THE LAST PAGE
ON WHICH IT HOLDS IS PDF p. 441 → printed `432`.** Verified page by page, as this chunk was
specifically asked to do: PDF p. 427 → `4 18`[text-layer?] (418, the digits split by a space);
p. 428 → `419`; p. 429 → `420`; p. 430 → `.42r`[text-layer?] (421); p. 431 → `422`; p. 432 → `423`;
p. 439 → `430`; p. 440 → `43r`[text-layer?] (431); p. 441 → `432`. **Six pages print no number at
all — PDF pp. 433, 434, 435, 436, 437 and 438, the six middle index pages — and they are cited by
PDF index only below; no folio has been predicted for any of them.** **PDF p. 442, the ISBN page,
also prints no number and is cited by PDF index only.** The offset therefore did not break at the
end of the book; it simply stopped being printed for six pages in the middle of the index and never
resumed after p. 441.

**Page-number placement changes inside this chunk and should not be mistaken for a break.** On
pp. 427–429 and 439–441 the number sits in the running head, verso-left and recto-right as usual.
**On p. 430 and p. 432 it sits at the FOOT of the page** (`.42r`[text-layer?] below the `Kiifa`
glossary entry; `423` below the `Bengal` index entry), because those two pages carry a display
heading — `GLOSSARY OF UNCOMMON WORDS FOUND IN THE POEMS` and `INDEX` — instead of a running head.
Both are odd/recto pages (421, 423).

**A navigation note the consolidator will need, flagged as derived and not as a finding.** Every page
reference printed inside the index and the glossary is one of the book's own printed page numbers,
i.e. a folio, not a PDF index. On the −9 offset verified in chunks 001–018 (PDF pp. 1–291) and again
here (PDF pp. 427–441), **printed page N is PDF page N + 9**. **I have not verified the offset for
PDF pp. 292–426, which fall in chunks 019–023 and which I have not read**, so any index folio between
283 and 417 should be converted only after those chunks confirm the offset held there too. **No
conversion has been performed anywhere below: every index and glossary page figure is quoted as the
book prints it.**

**Running heads: `BRIT` for `BHIT` occurs once in this chunk, at p. 429 (folio 420)** —

**What this chunk is worth to the archive, in one line: the index is the single most productive page
range in the whole book for finding shrine material, because it proves — with printed page numbers —
that Sorley discusses Muslim saints, the veneration of holy men, waqf, pirs, murshids and murids,
fakirs, qalandars, dervishes, religious mendicants, inam and jagir tenure, Muslim and Hindu feasts,
and the Sufism of the "common man", and it names twenty-one surs of the Risalo with their pages.**
