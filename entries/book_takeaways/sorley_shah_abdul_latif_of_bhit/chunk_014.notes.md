## Arguments and interpretations

- **The chapter's thesis: genius is inexplicable, and therefore the Risalo cannot be accounted for by
  its background.** "THE emergence of genius is an event which in the present state of human
  knowledge approaches the 'inexplicable. Heredity and environment are certainly an inadequate
  explanation" (p. 212, folio 203). "We are thus reduced to the simple fact ·that genius knows no
  limiting bounds" (p. 213, folio 204). **Sorley builds the entire chapter as a negative argument:
  Sind's education was poor, its literature poorer, and the Risalo is therefore a prodigy.** "These'
  facts about edricatioq[text-layer?] and learning iri[text-layer?] Sind make . still more remarkable
  the creation ju[text-layer?] the eighteenth century of the great Sindhi classic of Shah Abdul
  Latif" (p. 226, folio 217).
- **The counter-argument he raises against himself.** "If he lives amongst a simple, rustic and
  largely unlettered people, · he obtains · easily a reputation for wonderful achievement which the
  critical examination of a later age may find to be undeserved" (p. 212, folio 203). **He states
  the deflationary case explicitly and then does not apply it to Shah Abdul Latif; a reviewer should
