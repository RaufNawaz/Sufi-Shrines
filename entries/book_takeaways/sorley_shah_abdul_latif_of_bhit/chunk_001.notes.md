So this chunk is **apparatus for slightly more than half its length and substance for the rest**.
The apparatus is worth recording because it fixes the edition, the author's purpose, his method and
his sources. The body pages are not about shrines at all: they are Moghul decline, the Kalhora
succession, and Sind's geography and isolation. **Sorley states his own subject as the poet and the
poet's world, and in these twenty-three pages he does not describe the shrine at Bhit, or any shrine,
tomb, ʿurs, khanqah, sajjada nashin or endowment in Sind.** He does, however, give a general
statement about mosques and tombs as the only substantial old buildings in Sind (p. 20), and about
the Sayids as a priestly class holding virtual power (p. 23). See Doubts before using anything here
as a fact about a site.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Mosques and tombs are the only substantial old buildings in Sind — the chunk's single most
  relevant statement for shrine architecture.** "Even the buildings of burnt brick (and these are
  nearly all mosques and tombs) have suffered from the assault of the river, the salinity of the
  soil and the rigour of a climate which for six months in the year is as hot as anything to be
  found in India and is cursed with dust storms" (p. 20, folio 11).
- **And they are not old.** "Few buildings exist in sufficient preservation to display the·
  civilization- of any century previous to the fifteenth and, with rare exceptions, the buildings
  that are now accounted old are generally not earlier than the seventeenth century and most are
  even late� [text-layer?] in date." (p. 20, folio 11). The ordinary fabric is "mud, sticks or
  wattles, just as they are outside all the large towns today" (p. 20, folio 11).
- **No archives, no libraries, no learned institutions survive.** "There are no relics of old seats
  of learning, no old libraries and no collections of ancient2 documents. The men of learning who in
  earlier centuries lived in Sind have disappeared , leaving behind them only trifling remains for
  the use of modem [text-layer?] scholarship." (p. 20, folio 11). Old inhabited sites "have gradually
  risen on mounds of debris devoid of permanent reFcs [text-layer?], except broken pottery of no
  archaeological value" (p. 20, folio 11).
- **A theocratic state with a priestly class holding real power.** "since the time of the Siimra
  [text-layer?] in the thirteenth century and onwards, the land has been predominantly Muslim in
  population and the government had a strong theocratic basis with much virtual power in the hands
  of the priestly class of Sayids." (p. 23, folio 14). Sind's history, Sorley says, shows "to a late
  date the theo cratic foundation of Islamic society in a very marked way" (p. 21, folio 12), and the
  Kalhora–Talpur state added to Moghul administration "a peculiar brand of Islamic
  quasi-theocracy" (p. 19, folio 10), its administration "based partly on the theory of the Koran and
  partly on the system which the Moghuls had perfected" (p. 23, folio 14).

## Doubts

- **This chunk supports no factual statement about any shrine, including Bhit.** The shrine at Bhit,
  its buildings, its ʿurs, its custodians, its endowments and the poet's death and burial are all
  **(not stated)** in pp. 1–23. What the chunk gives about `bhit-bhit-shah` is bibliographic and
  literary: the book's title, Sorley's purpose, the text he translates, and the poems' dating to the
  first half of the eighteenth century. "of Bhit" appears only as part of the poet's name.
- **Archive locations named with no shrine content — do not map any of these.** The chunk names
  **Sehwan** (only "the Imperial army and fort at Sehwan", p. 22 — nothing of `lal-shahbaz-qalandar`),
  **Umarkot** (only as Akbar's birthplace, p. 22 — nothing of `umarkot-amarkot-shiv-mandir`),
  **Alor** (only as a "dead site", p. 20 fn. 1 — nothing of
  `kalka-cave-temple-asthan-of-kalka-devi`, whose archive location is Aror), **Shikarpur, Sukkur,
  Rohri, Hyderabad, Karachi** (only as towns and their dates of growth, p. 20 — nothing of the
  Shikarpur, Sukkur, Hyderabad or Karachi rows), **Multan** (only as Aurangzeb's governorship,
  p. 22), and **Tatta** and **Bakhar** (not archive rows). Each is a toponym coincidence, not a
  mapping.
- **"Alor" versus the archive's "Aror" is a spelling question I am not resolving.** The book prints
  `Alor` (p. 20, footnote 1). Whether this is the same place as the archive's Aror, Sukkur District,
  is a judgement a human should make; the chunk gives no coordinates, no description and no
  temple.
- **The front matter is out of sequence in this scan and its roman folios do not fit the offset.**
  The acknowledgements page carries printed **ix** at PDF p. 5 (p. 5), while the Preface carries
  printed **vii** at PDF p. 7 and **viii** at PDF p. 8. So ix precedes vii in the PDF. Either the
