# sorley_shah_abdul_latif_of_bhit — chunk 022 — pp. 373–399

**This chunk is neither prose nor bibliography, contents, index or appendix. It is twenty-seven
unbroken pages of translated verse** — an English rendering of the Sasui and Punhun poems, set out
in numbered sections and carrying speaker rubrics `Sasui.` and `Sayid.`. There is **not one
sentence of Sorley's own prose in the whole chunk**: no chapter heading, no section heading, no
footnote, no citation, no bracketed editorial note, no source reference. The `## Bibliography and
apparatus` heading is therefore not used; what apparatus there is (the roman-numeral section
numbers, the speaker rubrics and two printer's signature figures) is inventoried under
`## Arguments and interpretations` and under Doubts.

**The word "sur" does not occur anywhere in this chunk**, in either of its two senses. **No sur
title is printed** — the poems run under roman numerals alone, with no heading naming which sur
they belong to — so **the identification of this run as a particular sur cannot be made from the
chunk** and is **(not stated)**. Likewise **no translator is named**, consistent with the standing
finding for this book: the verse arrives with no attribution of any kind.

**Section numbering runs `IV` (p. 373) to `XLVI` (p. 399), and the chunk breaks off mid-section
XLVI at p. 399.** The chunk opens mid-poem: the first twenty-one lines of p. 373 are the tail of a
section whose number falls before the chunk boundary. **Two sections' numerals are damaged in the
text layer — `XX III` for XXIII (p. 385, folio 376) and `{(XXII` for XXXII (p. 389, folio 380) —
and one is spaced, `XL I` for XLI (p. 396, folio 387).** Seven numerals print in lower case —

(p. 391) — which is most likely small capitals flattened by the text layer rather than a printing
difference; it is recorded, not repaired.

**Two printer's signature figures appear at the page foot and are not folios: `24` at p. 378
(folio 369) and `25` at p. 394 (folio 385).** They are the only bare numerals in the chunk that are
not folios or section numbers, and they must not be read as page references.

**The two things in this chunk that matter most to the archive are (1) the material and economic
content inside the poems — spinning for a piece-rate fee, Persian wheels, caravans of workmen and
weavers, cowries, camel-purchase of a person, a washerman, jars of water, henna — and (2) two verse
passages that duplicate, word for word in places, verse already quoted in Sorley's prose hundreds
of pages earlier.** See the first two bullets below, which are the chunk's highest-value findings.
