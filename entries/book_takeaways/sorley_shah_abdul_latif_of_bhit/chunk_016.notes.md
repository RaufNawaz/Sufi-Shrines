# sorley_shah_abdul_latif_of_bhit — chunk 016 — pp. 243–258

**This chunk is continuous prose, not bibliography, contents, index or appendix**, so no
`## Bibliography and apparatus` heading is used; the authorities the chapter cites are recorded
under `## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)` and `## Doubts`, as chunk 012 did for
its running citations.

**This is the book's literary-sources chapter, and it is the first chunk to name the surs in
quantity.** It opens in the closing pages of a chapter on the poems themselves (pp. 243–244), which
end with Sorley's summation "This is the poet's quest" (p. 244, folio 235); `CHAPTER III` /
`A WEB OF MANY STRANDS` then begins at p. 245 and runs to the end of the chunk. The new chapter is
divided by lettered sections: `(a) A mixture of influences` (p. 245), `(b) A rabian[text-layer?]
influences` (p. 247, folio 238), `( c) Per.sian[text-layer?] influences` (p. 251, folio 242),
`(d} Baluchi[text-layer?] influences` (p. 255, folio 246) and `(e) Urdu and Hindu influences`
(p. 258, folio 249), where the chunk breaks off mid-section.

**Wanted material that is here in quantity: seven surs named, the Sindhi and Baluchi verse-form
vocabulary with definitions, the professional musician castes who carried it, and — the single most
important line in the chunk for the archive — the statement that the best Sindhi music and singing
is performed at the tombs and shrines of Muslim saints (p. 249, folio 240).** Two archive shrines
appear: `bhit-bhit-shah` throughout, and `lal-shahbaz-qalandar` at p. 257 (folio 248), which is the
**first time in the transcribed book that the word "Sehwan" is printed at all** and the first time
an annual cross-community gathering is attached to that shrine.
