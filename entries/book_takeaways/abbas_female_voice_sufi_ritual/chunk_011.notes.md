**This is BACK MATTER and it is the best finding aid this book has.** The chunk is the whole of the
works-cited list from its A-entries onward (pp. 219–235, folios not stated) and the whole of the

is `Wahabi,` (p. 240, folio not stated) and the last page carries `sheet 239 of 239`.

**Opening seam.** The chunk begins **mid-list and mid-author**: the first entry on p. 219 (folio not
stated) is a repeat-author entry, `$. ‘‘Primary Education in South Asia: Language Planning,
Curriculum Design and Educational Management.’’`, whose author's name stands on the previous page
and therefore in chunk 010. The heading of the works-cited list — whether it reads "Works Cited" or

may have dropped to a blank line in chunk 010 as well. The alphabetical run resumes at `Abe, Isamu`

**No closing seam.** The book ends inside this chunk (p. 240, folio not stated).

## The dominant defect, measured on this chunk

**Not one Arabic digit survives anywhere in the book's own type across all 22 pages.** Every
publication year, every volume and issue number, every page range, every edition ordinal, every
website access date and every Arabic index locator extracts as nothing at all. The only Arabic
digits present anywhere in the chunk belong to the two marks that must never be cited: the
printing-sheet index `6657 Abbas / THE FEMALE VOICE IN SUFI RITUAL / sheet N of 239` (on all 22
pages; `sheet = p − 1` holds throughout, p. 219 → sheet 218, p. 240 → sheet 239) and the typesetting
timestamp `Tseng 2002.9.25 07:23` (on all 22 pages). This is a clean confirmation of the run's
measured defect: digits survive only where the printer set them in a different face.

**Two consequences that matter for everything below.**

1. **Roman-numeral locators SURVIVE, Arabic ones do not.** The index's front-matter locators come
   through intact as letters — `xix`, `xx`, `xxi`, `xxii`, `xxiii`, `xxiv`, `xxv`, `xxvi–xxvii`,
   `xiii`, `xv`, `xvii`, `xviii`, `x`, `ix`, `xii`, `xxx`. Every locator pointing into the body of
   the book is gone. **These roman locators are the book's own printed page numbers. They are
   recorded below as printed locators and are NEVER written as `(p. N)`.**
2. **Words survive.** `Written in the Thirteenth Century by Shaikh Shahabuddin Umar bin Muhammad
   Surhawardi` (p. 221, folio not stated) keeps its century because it is spelled out. So does
   `Vols.` and `no.` — the labels survive, the figures do not.

**Nothing below supplies a missing number.** No publication year, no page locator, no death year has
been filled in from anywhere. Every gap is marked `[numeral dropped, text-layer]`.

## Practices, institutions, economy

**All locators in this section are the book's own printed index locators, not PDF pages.** Roman
locators are printed as they survive; Arabic ones are `[dropped, text-layer]` without exception.

- **ʿUrs.** The index headword `rituals` carries `urs, xxii, [numeral dropped]` (p. 238, folio not
  stated). Two works-cited titles attach an ʿurs to a named context: `Proceedings of Shah Abdul Latif
  Conference on His [numeral dropped] Urs Celebrations` (p. 219, folio not stated) — **the dropped
  numeral here is almost certainly the ordinal of the ʿurs, and it must not be guessed** — and
  `Lahori, Tahir. ‘‘Aulīyā ke Urs.’’ In Sohna Shehr Lahore` (p. 227, folio not stated).

  the body.
- **Mannat (vow/offering).** `mannat, xx, [numeral dropped]` under `rituals` (p. 238, folio not
  stated).
- **Dhammāl.** Indexed three times: under `rituals` (p. 238), under `Sidi / rituals` (p. 238) and
  under `Qalandar, Hazrat Lal Shahbaz` (p. 239) — all folios not stated, all Arabic locators

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The index's taxonomy is itself the book's argument, and it is recoverable even with every Arabic
  locator destroyed.** The index sorts sainthood into three mutually exclusive headwords — `Suﬁ
  Saints belonging to Pakistan-Indian silsilās`, `Suﬁs of the Middle East` and `Suﬁ musicians, poets
  and scholars` (p. 239, folio not stated) — and sorts persons by gender into `female musicians`
  (p. 236), `male musicians` (p. 237), `female mystics` (p. 236) and `female myths` (p. 236), all
  folios not stated. **Sultan Bahu, Khwaja Ghulam Farid, Shah Hussain, Sachal Sarmast, Shah Abdul
  Latif, Bulle Shah and Waris Shah are filed as poets and musicians, not as saints; Bibi Pak Daman,
  Data Ganj Bakhsh, Mian Mir, Lal Shahbaz Qalandar, Bahauddin Zakariya, Shah Rukunuddin Alam and Baba
  Farid Ganj-e Shakar are filed as saints.** That division — shrine-saint on one side, poet on the
  other — is a substantive editorial claim of this book and can be cited from the index alone.
- **Guru Nanak is filed under `Bhakti`, with Kabir, Surdas and Tulsidas** (p. 236, folio not stated),
  not under any Sufi or shrine headword. The book's frame for him is devotional poetry.
- **`Bibi Pak Daman` is the only archive figure the index files under `female mystics`** (p. 236,
  folio not stated), alongside `Al-Adawiyya, Rabia` — a two-name list. The book's female sainthood is
  Rabia plus the Bibis, and nothing else.
- **The bibliography's shape shows the method.** The list is dominated not by Islamic studies but by
  the ethnography of speaking and conversation analysis — Hymes (six entries), Gumperz (six), Sherzer
  (seven), Bauman (seven), Bauman and Briggs, Bauman and Sherzer, Jakobson (six), Goﬀman (six),
  Labov, Sacks/Schegloﬀ/Jeﬀerson, Atkinson and Heritage, Garﬁnkel, Ochs, Tannen, Schenkein, Kendon
  (pp. 219–235, folios not stated) — and by ethnomusicology — Feld (five), Qureshi (six), Nettl,
  Lomax, Slawek, Wade, Neuman, List, Rouget, Frisbie, Meyer, Richman (pp. 219–235, folios not
  stated). The index confirms it: the single longest index entry in the book is `sociolinguistics`,
  running two full columns with sub-entries from `codes` and `conversation analysis` to `turn-taking`
  and `word-play` (pp. 238–239, folios not stated). **This is a sociolinguistic ethnography of
  performance that takes Sufi ritual as its field, not a study of shrines.**
- **The third strand is feminist and postcolonial theory**, cited directly: hooks (three entries),
  Anzaldúa, Minh-ha (two), Guha and Spivak, Said, Fanon (two), Memmi, Mernissi, Sabbah, Butler,
