## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Fakir service duty at the shrine is rostered and draws fakirs from outside Sindh.** A "visiting
  fakir from Lahore who in the autumn of 2011 was in Sehwan to perform his duty at the shrine of Lal";
  he keeps "his fakir retreat by the banks of the river" (p. 195, folio 178, ch. 5 n. 9). **A dated,
  located observation.**
- **Riverine offering, present tense.** "Offerings to the Indus River can still be documented in the
  wider region of Sindh" (p. 195, folio 178, ch. 5 n. 11).
- **Succession and the lodge, defined together.** `sajjadah-­n ashin` = "Successor to a spiritual
  position or shrine, mostly a male biological descendant; head of a kafi in Sehwan" (p. 200, folio

  and defines `kafi` as "A fakir lodge in Sehwan; khanaqah or zawiyah in other Sufi contexts" (p. 199,

- **Shrine employment.** `khadim` = "Shrine employee, the one who performs service" (p. 199, folio 182).
- **Endowment vocabulary.** `auqaf` = "Religious or charitable endowments; sing. waqf" (p. 198, folio
  not stated). The corpus lead for it is Malik 1990 on waqf in Pakistan (p. 211, folio 194).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Saintly authority is territorial and derives from intimacy.** "the authority of a wali, which stems
  from the saint's intimacy with Allah, is made manifest in his ability and authority to protect a
  region or wilayat" (pp. 195–196, folios 178–179, ch. 5 n. 18) — **the book's own compressed statement
  of its title concept, "public intimacy", applied to jurisdiction.**
- **Antinomian dwelling read as spatial inversion.** Either tradition about where the saint first
  settled "confirms a spatial inversion that is characteristic of antinomian piety" (p. 195, folio 178,
  ch. 5 n. 16).
- **What makes a qalandar distinct.** "Qalandars are distinct in that such figures, marked by
  renunciation, celibacy, antinomianism, and itinerancy, turn the idea of such spatiality into a
  defining trait (see Karamustafa 2006; Watenpaugh 2005)" (p. 196, folio 179, ch. 5 n. 21).
- **Taming the wild as a saintly signature.** "It pertains to a widely held notion that taming the wild
  is a key characteristic of Sufis and Islamic saints as friends of Allah" (p. 196, folio 179, ch. 5
  n. 22).
- **The author's self-criticism, printed in a note.** "Its symbolic power aside, privileging a spatial
  performance of social death risks underestimating fakirs' active interest in self-­empowerment. For
  more, see Kasmani (2017b)" (p. 196, folio 179, ch. 5 n. 23).
- **A disclaimer about the book's central term.** "Unstraight in this work does not characterize fakirs
  or saints as queer in identitarian terms" (p. 196, folio 179, Coda n. 2); and "This does not entail
  one category taking over the other" (Coda n. 1).
- **Religion and queerness as mutual correctives**, summarising Nikki Young (2017): "While religion, she
  argues, tempers queer's rush toward individuality, queer studies can help interrogate the narratives
  and meaning making of humanity that religion so readily performs" (p. 196, folio 179, Coda n. 5).
- **Kugle's body-part corpus, and Kasmani's gloss on its loss.** "These include the bones, belly, eyes,
  lips, and heart of both male and female spiritual figures between North Africa and South Asia (see
  also Kugle 2003)" (p. 197, folio 180, Coda n. 9); "For Kugle, this loss has to do with Islamic
  fundamentalism and the rise of the Wahhabi movement in contemporary Muslim societies" (p. 197, folio
  180, Coda n. 10). **This is the archive's own Kugle title being summarised, and it is a summary a
  human should check against that book.**
- **Method names.** Heather White's move is "'religion-­ing' rather than 'queering'" (p. 197, folio 180,
  Coda n. 12); Schippert's implication is that queer theory "can finally enable religious studies to
  become (more) undisciplined" (p. 197, folio 180, Coda n. 13).
- **Position on the piety turn.** "On ethics and the politics of Islamic piety in Cairo, see Mahmood
  (2005). For critique on the piety turn in the anthropology of Islam, see Mittermaier (2012),
  Schielke (2010), Soares and Osella (2009)" (p. 197, folio 180, Coda n. 14) — **the author aligns
  himself with the critics, by placement.**
