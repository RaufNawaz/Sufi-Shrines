# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 006 — pp. 64–76

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-sentence with Dara Shikoh at Lahore: he gathers support, writes secretly to the nobles and to Shah Shuja, and loses adherents, among them Raja Rajrup[OCR?] and "گورو هر راے سجاده نشین بابا نانک" (p. 76). Then comes "در بیان ماجراے[OCR?] سلیمان شکوه خلف بزرگ دارا شکوه" (pp. 73–76): Sulaiman's army breaks up, and he wanders between Allahabad, the Doab and pargana Nadina[OCR?] before taking refuge in the hills of "سری نگر". Next are "در بیان جلوس حضرت خدیو جهان بر اورنگ خلافت و جهانبانی" (pp. 71–73), the first accession at the garden of Aazzabad[OCR?] on Friday "غره ذی قعد سال هزار و شصت و هفت[OCR?] هجری", and "در بیان نهضت موکب مقدس بدفع دارا شکوه بجانب ولایت پنجاب" (pp. 69–70), the march in the rains and the crossing of the Sutlej. "در بیان فرار دارا شکوه از لاهور بجانب ملتان بعجلت تمام" (pp. 67–68) has Dara empty Lahore's treasuries and leave for Multan. "در بیان نهضت موکب مقدس بجانب ملتان بتعاقب دارا شکوه" (pp. 65–67) covers the pursuit: Haibatpur Patti[OCR?], Dara's flight from Multan toward Bhakkar[OCR?] with Multan's treasury, the camp near Multan, the submission of Multan's governor, and the emperor's visit to the tomb of Bahauddin Zakariya (p. 65). The chunk closes with "در بیان معاودت موکب مقدس از ملتان بجانب پایتخت[OCR?] جهانبانی[OCR?] بدفع شورش محمّد شجاع" (pp. 64–65): the return by Ichhra[OCR?] and Lahore (the fort, the Wazir Khan mosque, the Shalamar), new governors for Lahore and Thatta, and the march to the capital by Sirhind and Thanesar. It ends mid-procession (p. 64).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A ruler at a shrine.** The emperor made a ziyarat to Bahauddin Zakariya's tomb. He sought "استمداد همت و استفاضه انوار" there and apparently gave to the poor coming and going (p. 65).
- **"سجاده نشین" for the Sikh Guru.** The book calls Guru Har Rai "سجاده نشین بابا نانک", and he came to Dara with an armed following (p. 76).
- **Treasuries.**
  - Lahore's stores and treasuries came to more than one crore rupees in coin and uncoined gold and silver (p. 68).
  - Multan's treasury: "بست[OCR?] و دو لک روپیه" (p. 66).
  - The treasure was moved by river boats (pp. 66, 68).
- **Zamindars.**
  - Haji Khan Baluch, "از عمده زمینداران ملتان" (pp. 65–66).
  - The "زمینداران کوهستان", whom Rajrup claimed he would win over (p. 76).
  - Zamindars guided Bahadur Khan to a river crossing (p. 69).
- **Jagir and pargana officials.** Pargana Nadina[OCR?] "باقطاع بیگم صاحب تعلق داشت". Sulaiman imprisoned its karori and took more than "دولک[OCR?] روپیه" from the inhabitants. He later had the karori, "سید بیگناه", put to death (pp. 73–74).
- **Grants and pay.**
  - "یک کرور[OCR?] دام انعام" to Khalilullah Khan (p. 64), and "کرودوام[OCR?] انعام" to Jaswant Singh (p. 69).
  - "بست هزار اشرفی بجهت تنخواه سپاه" (p. 66).
  - Ibrahim Khan, son of Ali Mardan Khan, was retired on "شصت هزار روپیه سالیانه" (p. 71).
- **Accession protocol.**
  - The emperor took the throne after "پانزده[OCR?] گهری و بست[OCR?] و دو پل" (p. 72).
  - "سکه و خطبه و تعیین لقب" were held over to the "جلوس ثانی" (p. 71).
  - The amirs put off their peshkash and offered a customary nazr (p. 71).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Kingship.** The accession section opens with the author's own theory (p. 73). Whenever the age falls into disorder, divine wisdom places "افسر خلافت" on a fortunate head. The ruler then "احکام شریعت بکرسی نشاند" and clothes "برهنگان[OCR?] وادی احتیاج".
- **On Dara.** Outwardly he was preparing for battle, but "در باطن مغلوب رعب و هراس گشته". People knew he would flee, so they began to leave him (p. 76). His "افسون" worked on Shuja and on Khanjar Khan (p. 76).
- **On the march in the rains.** The author sets out the whole case against marching, then has the emperor set aside "رائے ظاهر بینان و کنکاش[OCR?] عاقبت گزینان" (p. 70).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 76 down to p. 64.
  - It opens mid-sentence at "برفاقت[OCR?] او در لاهور رسیده" (p. 76), continuing from the previous chunk.
  - It ends with the procession into the capital, "نقاره ها اے نشاط و شادی بنوازش[OCR?] درامد" (p. 64), which presumably continues in the next chunk.
  - All page joins read through.
- A library stamp at the foot of p. 69 ("Sri Satguru Jagjit Singh Ji eLibrary") is not text.
- **The ruler is never named in the chunk.** He is "حضرت خدیو جهان", "خدیو گیهان" or "شهنشاه". Calling him Aurangzeb rests on the brief's map of the book, not on this chunk.

**Dates**
- **Contradictory printed years for the same stay near Multan.** The camp is "هفتم محرم سنه[OCR?] ۱۰۶۹[OCR?]" (p. 66), but the departure is "دوازدهم محرم سنه[OCR?] ۱۰۶۸[OCR?]" (p. 65).
  - Both numerals and both "سنه" are flagged, and the book says the stay lasted "پنج روز" (p. 65).
  - This is not resolved. As a result, the year of the Bahauddin visit (p. 65) is not stated.
- Other flagged dates:
  - The accession: "هزار و شصت و هفت[OCR?] هجری" and "یازدهم[OCR?] امرداد" (p. 72).
  - The start of the march: "هفتم[OCR?] ذی قعد مطابق هفدهم[OCR?] امرداد" (p. 70).
  - The camps: "دهم[OCR?]" Dhu al-Hijja (p. 67) and "هفدهم[OCR?]" and "بست[OCR?] و پنجم" Dhu al-Qa'da (p. 69).
- None of these dates is converted.

**The Bahauddin visit**
- The sentence does not name the visitor. He is read as the emperor from the honorific plural and the run of the narrative (p. 65).
- "فیض امین[OCR?]" and "برآوردند[OCR?]" are flagged. "از روشنائی" is unflagged, but its sense in "از روشنائی جیب مراد مساکین" is unclear, and the gloss "gave to the poor" is tentative.

**Could not place: Shaikh Musa Gilani**
- "شیخ موسے گیلانی[OCR?]" is named only as the man "که از طرف دارا شکوه حکومت ملتان داشت" (p. 66). He is NOT mapped person-level.
- The finished chunk 042 notes record "شیخ موسی گیلانی" in this book's list of saints buried in Multan (p. 528). There it was mapped by bare name to `shrine-of-syed-musa-pak`.
- The book does not say the governor and the saint are one man, and the wave-2 rule says not to guess for governors. The governor fact therefore enters only the Multan place-level line, which already carries that row.
- The consolidator should weigh whether the two are the same person. "گیلانی" is flagged here.

**Person-level and place-level attachments (weigh accordingly)**
- **Guru Nanak's 18 rows.** This is person-level, on a bare name: Nanak appears only inside Guru Har Rai's title. No site is named, and Har Rai has no row. "کشیر[OCR?]" is read "کثیر" (large) tentatively.
- **Lahore (35) and Multan (8).** These attachments are place-level. They are campaign material, but the book says things about each place itself, which wave-2 point 3 counts:
  - Lahore: its treasuries, the city "از حاکم خالی", the fort, the halt.
  - Multan: its treasury, its governor, its chief zamindar, the camp at the river junction.
- Held-identity rows on these lines are attached only place-level, and nothing in the chunk raises their questions. On the Lahore line these are shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh and shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri; on the Multan line, the "Shams" row shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari.
- **Ichhra.**
  - **shah-jamal** and **shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal** are attached place-level by toponym_map's "ichhra", from a passing camp mention. The index places shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal only "near Ichhra".
  - "اچهره" is flagged at every occurrence.
  - The book puts it "ان طرف لاهور" (p. 67) and "نزدیکی لاهور" (p. 65).
- **Khushab.** **amb-temples-amb-sharif** is attached place-level by toponym_map's "khushab". The book's "خوشاب" is a faujdari named jointly with Bhera and not further located. The row is a temple complex, of which nothing is said.
- **Lahore fort and Shalamar.** The Lahore fort material ("دروازه هیتبه[OCR?] پول[OCR?] قلعه مبارک") stays in the Lahore line, which already carries both "lahore fort" rows. "باغ فیض بخش المشهور بشالامار" goes under Other.
  - This follows the brief: a named garden that is not itself a row is not city material. It also matches chunk 042.
  - madho-lal-hussain is NOT attached through "shalimar gardens", because the book says nothing of Shah Husain.
- **The other "قلعه مبارک".** The "دولتخانه قلعه مبارک" entered on 4 Rabi al-Awwal later on p. 64 is at the capital, not Lahore.

**Province bullets (province-level, weigh accordingly)**
- Each province bullet is a Mughal suba, not the modern province's boundaries. The book's own units are kept: "صوبه پنجاب", "صوبه داری لاهور", "صوبه داری ملتان", "صوبه داری تهتهه".
- "کوهستان پنجاب" is not located by the book, so its relation to the modern province is unknown.
- The crossings and camps on the march (Rupar, Goindwal, Sultanpur, Talwan, Haibatpur Patti, Mominpur) have no rows. They are not assigned to the modern province.
- Bhakkar ("بهکهر[OCR?]") has no row in toponym_map and is not taken to sindh.
- "بلوچ" in Haji Khan Baluch's name is not treated as province balochistan.

**Traps avoided**
- "صوبه داری گجرات" (p. 65), held by Murad Bakhsh's men, is suba Gujarat, not Punjab Gujrat. It takes no row.
- "سری نگر" is the hill country reached from the Ganga, with its own raja ("مرزبان سری نگر"). The book does not equate it with Kashmir, and Sharada is not named.
- These are titles or names, not saints or sites:
  - "قطب ربانی", an epithet of Bahauddin;
  - "شیخ میر" and "شیخ عبدالکریم", nobles and officials;
  - "سید عزت خان", "سید قاسم بارهه" and "سادات بارهه", Barha nobles;
  - "گنج علیخان[OCR?]", a title given to Abdullah Beg;
  - "شاه نواز خان";
  - the garden name "فیض بخش".
- "راجه تودر مل[OCR?]" at Sirhind (p. 64) is not identified further by the book.
