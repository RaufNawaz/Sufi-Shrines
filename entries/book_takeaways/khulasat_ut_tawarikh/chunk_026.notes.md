# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 026 — pp. 323–334

This chunk is the political history of Delhi across five reigns. The scan runs back to front, so it reads from p. 334 down to p. 323. It opens at the heading "سلطان ناصر الدین محمود شاه بن سلطان محمد شاه" (p. 334), the late-Tughluq years:
- the rival sultan Nusrat Shah at Firuzabad, and Mallu Iqbal Khan's rise;
- Mirza Pir Muhammad's invasion, then Timur's, both by way of Uch and Multan;
- the sack of Delhi, and the massacres at Hardwar and in Jammu;
- Timur's grant of Lahore, Multan and Dipalpur to Khizr Khan, and the division of Hindustan among the amirs;
- Iqbal Khan's death fighting Khizr Khan, and the closing count of "the Turks'" line (pp. 329–334).

The next heading, "رایات عالی حضرت خضر خان بن ملک سلیمان پسر خوانده ملک مروان از امرائے کبار فیروزشاهی" (pp. 327–328), covers how Khizr Khan took Delhi, his coin and khutba in Shah Rukh's name, and his character. "سلطان مبارک شاه بن رایات اعلی خضر خان" (pp. 325–327) is mostly a war on two fronts around Lahore, Dipalpur, Multan and Jalandhar: against Shaikha and Jasrath Khokhar, and against Shaikh Ali of Kabul. It ends with the Sultan's murder at the Mubarakabad mosque. "سلطان محمد شاه بن سلطان مبارک شاه بن رایات اعلی خضر خان" (pp. 324–325) has the Sultan's visit to the tombs of the shaikhs at Multan and Bahlul Lodi's rise. The chunk ends inside "سلطان علاء الدین بن سلطان محمد شاه بن سلطان مبارک شاه بن رایات اعلی خضر خان" (pp. 323–324), mid-sentence at Hamid Khan's flight from Badaun (p. 323). No archive saint is named. The shrine material is the unnamed "مزارات مشایخ" at Multan (p. 324) and an oath sworn at the tomb of Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki in Delhi (p. 334).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **prahladpuri-temple**, **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya**, **shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani**, **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-ali-akbar-shah-ali-akbar-shamsi**, **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**, **shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari**, **shrine-of-shah-yusaf-gardez**, **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak** (the book's "ملتان"):
  - **The tombs of the shaikhs.** "در سنه ۸۳۵[OCR?] سلطان بملتان رسیده مزارات مشایخ را طواف نموده فوجی بر سر جسرته[OCR?] کهوکهر تعین کرده بدهلی رجعت کرد". Sultan Muhammad Shah reached Multan and circumambulated the tombs of the shaikhs. He then sent a force against Jasrath Khokhar and went back to Delhi. The book names no shaikh and no tomb (p. 324).
  - **The Langah rising.** "درین ایام جماعة لنگاه در ملتان بغی[OCR?] ورزیده لوائے سروری بر افراشت": in these days the Langah group in Multan rebelled and raised the standard of sovereignty (p. 324).
  - **Mirza Pir Muhammad.** Timur's grandson ("نبیره") came from Khurasan, "از اب سند گذشت و حصار اوچ و ملتان بتصرف در اورده چند روز در ملتان توقف ورزید" (p. 333).
  - **Timur at Multan.** Timur came from Kabul, "در سنه ۸۰۱[OCR?] [illegible] را تاخته بملتان نزول اجلال فرمودند", and put to death "کسانی را که بدست میرزا پیر محمد اسیر شده بودند". He then left Multan "براه ریگستان" for Bhatnir (p. 333).
  - **Khizr Khan's government (Timur's grant).** Timur granted Khizr Khan "ایالت لاهور و ملتان و دیپالپور" (p. 331). After Timur left, Khizr Khan held "لاهور و ملتان و دیپالپور تا نواحی سند" (p. 330). Iqbal Khan then marched against him "بجانب دیپالپور و ملتان" (p. 330).
  - **Khizr Khan's government (his base).** "خضر خان از ملتان با لشکر گران" reached Samana and Sirhind. He "سه مرتبه از ملتان لشکر فراهم آورده بر سر دهلی آمده" (p. 329). His base is given as "فتح پور تابع ملتان که دارالایالت او بود" (p. 328).
  - **Shaikh Ali of Kabul.** After laying waste up to the Jhelum, Shaikh Ali "متوجه ملتان گردید", and "در نواحی ملتان محاربه رو داد"; he was beaten there (p. 326). He came back and raided "حوالی لاهور و ملتان". Sultan Mubarak Shah then set out "بطرف لاهور و ملتان" (p. 326).

  - **Shaikha Khokhar ejected.** In the year of Nasir al-Din Mahmud Shah's accession ("همدرین سال"), an army was sent against "شیخا کهوکهر که لاهور را متصرف شده بود". "شیخا دوازده کروهے لاهور محاربه عظیم نموده شکست خورده در کوه جمهون[OCR?] رفت و از نواحی لاهور رفع فتنه[OCR?] گردید" (p. 334).
  - **Khizr Khan's government.** Lahore heads Timur's grant to Khizr Khan, "ایالت لاهور و ملتان و دیپالپور" (p. 331), and the list of what he held afterwards (p. 330).
  - **Mubarak Shah's restoration of the city and fort.** After driving Shaikha Khokhar beyond the Chenab, Mubarak Shah "در لاهور نزول اجلال فرموده دران مصر جامع طرح اقامت افگند". He resettled, "بدلاسا و استمالت", those whom Shaikha's oppression had ruined. "در آبادی ان مصر توجه بر گماشته حکم ترمیم و تعمیر قلعه فرموده": he ordered the fort repaired and rebuilt (p. 327).
  - **Shaikha's second siege.** Shaikha "بار دیگر در لاهور آمده محاصره نمود چون کار پیش نرفت", and turned to Kalanaur instead. He fought "راجه بهیم جمووال که برائے کمک حاکم لاهور بر آمده بود" and killed him. Afterwards he "بلاهور و دیالپور[OCR?] تاخت و ان ولایت را در تصرف خود اورده". He died later, and his son Malik Jasrath took his place (p. 327).
  - **Shaikh Ali's raid and Jasrath's siege.** Shaikh Ali plundered round Jalandhar and took many captives, "در لاهور اورد" (p. 326). Jasrath "از جالندهر بلاهور آمده محاصره نمود". When the wazir Malik Sarwar reached Samana, Jasrath "محاصره لاهور گذاشته بمسکن خویش شتافت" (p. 326).
  - **Shaikh Ali takes the fort.** Shaikh Ali "بلاهور رسیده قلعه را متصرف گشت و دوازده هزار سوار انتخابی برائے محافظت گذاشته" before taking Dipalpur. The Sultan then, "از هم[OCR?] شور و لاهور و آن نواحی خاطر جمع نموده", went back to Delhi (p. 325).
  - **Bahlul Lodi's grant.** Sultan Muhammad Shah gave Malik Bahlul the title "خان خانانی" and "ولایت لاهور و دیپالپور" (p. 324).
  - **Bahlul's independence.** In Ala al-Din's reign, "در لاهور و دیپالپور و سهرند تا پانی پت ملک بهلول دم استقلال می زد" (p. 323).

- **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari**, **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht**, **tomb-of-baha-al-halim-uch-sharif**, **tomb-of-javindi-bibi**, **tomb-of-ustad-nuriya** (the book's "اوچ"):
  - Mirza Pir Muhammad crossed the Sind and "حصار اوچ و ملتان بتصرف در اورده": he took the fortress of Uch along with Multan's. Nothing else is said of Uch (p. 333).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Saints, tombs and holy places outside the archive**
- **Shaikh Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki (Delhi).** Iqbal Khan professed devotion to Nusrat Shah. "بر مزار شیخ قطب الدین بختیار کاکی قدس الله سره کلام مجید در میان آورده از طرفین عقد موافقت بستند": both parties swore their pact on the Qur'an at the Shaikh's tomb. On the third day Iqbal Khan "از روے مکر و غدر" tried to seize Nusrat Shah (p. 334).
- **"هردوار گنگ".** The book describes it as the place where the Ganges comes out of the Siwalik hills. Hindus gather there "در اوان معهود" to bathe; shaving the head and beard there is "از آئین دین", and casting the bones of the dead into the Ganges is held to be "رستگاری فرورفتگان". When Timur came there was a great crowd, and he ordered a general massacre; the survivors fled into the hills (p. 331).
- **"گذر خواجه خضر".** A ford near Delhi, named only as a limit of Bahadur Khan Lodi's holding from Sambhal (p. 323).
- **"مسجد جامع مبارک آباد".** Malik Sarwar's party killed Mubarak Shah there while he was at prayer (p. 325).

**The Sayyid house and the rulers**
- **Khizr Khan.**
  - His heading reads "خضر خان بن ملک سلیمان پسر خوانده ملک مروان از امرائے کبار فیروزشاهی" (p. 328).
  - Timur imprisoned all the Indian amirs who came to him "غیر از خضرخان که نیک مرد و سید زاده بود" (p. 331).
  - He took Delhi from Daulat Khan after a four-month siege. He imprisoned Daulat Khan, who died at "حصار فیروزه" (p. 328).
  - He put coin and khutba in the name of "شاهرخ میرزا خلف حضرت صاحبقران", and later in his own name (p. 328).
  - The book describes him as "سیدزاده جوان صادق القول پسندیده اطوار صاحب دانش و پاکیزه طینت" (p. 328).
  - "اسم سلطانی بر خود اطلاق نکرد و برایات اعلی خود را مخاطب کرده". He reigned "هفت سال و سه ماه" (p. 327).
- **Nasir al-Din Mahmud Shah.**
  - He was the younger brother of "سلطان علاء الدین سکندر شاه". His coin and khutba are dated "در سنه ۹۶[OCR?]" (p. 334).
  - He was a figurehead under Iqbal Khan, "محض نمونه[OCR?]" (pp. 332–333). He went to Gujarat during Timur's invasion and came back "در سنه[OCR?]" (no year printed) (p. 330).
  - He escaped to Sultan Ibrahim, then took Qanauj (p. 330). He was re-enthroned "دهم[OCR?] ماه جمادی الاول و [illegible] شنبه" (p. 329).
  - He died after a hunt; his reign was "که غیر از نام نبود بست سال و دو ماه" (p. 329).
- **Nusrat Shah and Iqbal Khan.**
  - Nusrat Shah was "نصرت شاه بن فیروز خان بن سلطان فیروز شاه", enthroned at Firuzabad (p. 334).
  - Iqbal Khan was "فضل الله بلخی[OCR?] عرف ملو خان". He killed his rival Muqarrab Khan (pp. 333–334) and defeated Tatar Khan at Panipat (p. 333).
  - He was routed by Timur and fled to "قصبه برن" (p. 332). He was later captured and killed fighting Khizr Khan near Dipalpur (pp. 329–330).
- **Holders of Hindustan after Timur.** The book lists them as follows (p. 330):
  - Gujarat: "اعظم همایون ظفرخان و تاتارخان پسرش".
  - Mahoba and Kalpi: Mahmud Khan.
  - Qanauj to Jaunpur: "سلطان الشرق عرف خواجه جهان".
  - Malwa: Dilawar Khan.
  - Samana: "عالم خان[OCR?]" (editor's footnote variant: "غالب خان").
  - Bayana: "شمس الدین اوحدی".
  - Khwaja Jahan's earlier jagir and his own coin are on p. 334.
- **Later Sayyid rulers.**
  - Mubarak Shah acceded "در سنه ۸۲۴[OCR?]" (p. 327).
  - Muhammad Shah, "خلف[OCR?] شاهزاده فرید بن رایات اعلی خضر خان", was raised by Mubarak Shah as his son. He acceded "در سنه ۸۳۷[OCR?]" at Mubarakabad (p. 325).
  - Ala al-Din acceded "در سنه ۸۴۹[OCR?]" (p. 324). He withdrew to Badaun "در سنه ۸۵۳[OCR?]" (p. 323).
- **Court figures.**
  - The wazir Malik Sarwar held the title "خانجهانی" (pp. 324–326). Malik Kamal al-Din, "کمال الملک", was his rival (p. 325).
  - Bahlul Lodi was styled "فرزند" and "خان خانان" (p. 324).
  - "حسام خان عرف حاجی شرقی[OCR?]" and the wazir Hamid Khan appear in Ala al-Din's reign (p. 323).

**The Khokhars and Shaikh Ali of Kabul**
- **Shaikha Khokhar, his raids.**
  - He seized "سلطان شاه علی[OCR?] مرزبان کشمیر", who had conquered "تتهه[OCR?]" (p. 327).
  - He crossed the Sutlej and plundered "مشته[OCR?] تلوندی رائے کمال الدین مبین[OCR?]", and took the country "از لودیانه تا روپر" (p. 327).
  - He fought the governor of Sirhind, "سلطان[OCR?] منبه[OCR?] [margin] [illegible] لودی" (editor's footnote variant: "اسلام خان لودهی ــ فرشته جلد اول صفحه ۱۶۴[OCR?]") (p. 327).
- **Shaikha Khokhar, his defeat and death.**
  - He fought the Sultan for forty days at Ludhiana and fled to the Chenab (p. 327).
  - "راجه بهیم زمیندار جمبون" joined the Sultan, and "بهکهر مسکن شیخا" was laid waste (p. 327).
  - He died after taking Lahore's territory, and his son Jasrath succeeded (p. 327).
- **Jasrath Khokhar.**
  - He took Kalanaur and Jalandhar besides his father's lands. He was beaten near Jalandhar and went home to "بهکهر[OCR?]" (p. 326).
  - He later captured Malik Iskandar and raided "بجواره[OCR?] کوتهی[OCR?]" (p. 326).
  - He made peace with Bahlul and gave him "نوید سلطنت دهلی" (p. 324).
- **Shaikh Ali, governor of Kabul for Shah Rukh.**
  - He raided Hindustan because Mubarak Shah had turned away "از اطاعت شاهرخ میرزا", unlike his father (p. 326).
  - "در سنه ۸۴۳[OCR?]", at the call of the rebel Malik Fulad, he came to "بهنده[OCR?]", then Jalandhar, Lahore, "تلواره[OCR?]" and "خطپور[OCR?]". He crossed the Ravi and laid waste as far as the Jhelum (p. 326).
  - "ملک شه لودی عم سلطان بهلول لودی که حاکم دیپالپور بود با شیخ علی جنگ کرده شهادت یافت" (p. 326).
  - He was beaten near Multan and again at "شور" (p. 326). He took Lahore's fort and Dipalpur (p. 325).
  - "قلعه شور", held by his nephew, was besieged and settled by a marriage (p. 325).

**Timur outside the archive's places**

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Royal visit to shrines.** Sultan Muhammad Shah's tawaf of "مزارات مشایخ" at Multan, between campaigns (p. 324).
- **Oath at a saint's tomb.** A pact was sworn on the Qur'an at Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki's tomb, and broken on the third day (p. 334).
- **Sikka and khutba as the sign of sovereignty.**
  - Nasir al-Din's (p. 334) and Khwaja Jahan's own (p. 334).
  - Timur's at Delhi, with coin "از سکه اتیموری[OCR?]" (p. 331).
  - Khizr Khan's, first in Shah Rukh's name and then his own (p. 328).
  - Mubarak Shah's (p. 327) and Muhammad Shah's (p. 325).
- **Jagir, iqta and pay.**
  - "مواجب و جاگیر ... بدستور سابق بحال داشت" under Nasir al-Din (p. 334) and Mubarak Shah (p. 327).
  - Khwaja Jahan's Jaunpur jagir was extended "از قنوج تا بهار" (p. 334).
  - "راپری و چندوار اقطاع قطب الدین لودی" (p. 323).
  - Bahlul seized parganas "سوائے جائگیر خود" (p. 324).
- **Revenue collapse.** Under Muhammad Shah, "تحصیل خراج خلل افتاد" (p. 324). Under Ala al-Din the rebels "از ادائے مال واجب سر باز داشتند" (p. 323).
- **Lahore restored by the state.** Mubarak Shah resettled Lahore and ordered "ترمیم و تعمیر قلعه" (p. 327). Shaikh Ali garrisoned Lahore fort with "دوازده هزار سوار انتخابی" (p. 325).
- **Timur's levy and army.**
  - Officers collected "مال امانی" from Delhi, and resistance to them led to the order for general killing and captivity (p. 332).
  - "هر لشکرے را حصه پنجاه کس" of captives (p. 332). The plunder lasted "تا پانزده[OCR?] و روز" (p. 332).
  - The army review at Samana: the "یسال" was six farsangs long. "هر فرسنگ دوازده هزار سوار احاطه میکند پس سوای نوکر هفتاد و دو هزار سوار بوده باشد" (p. 333).
- **Resettlement after Timur.** Those made homeless by Timur's coming "در ایام حکومت او آمده آباد گشتند" under Khizr Khan (p. 328). Delhi lay "خراب مطلق" for two months after Timur left (p. 331).
- **Hindu pilgrimage practice at Hardwar.** Bathing at fixed seasons, ritual shaving, and casting the bones of the dead into the Ganges (p. 331).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- None concern an archive row.
- **Khizr Khan and Timur.** The book gives Khizr Khan's rise a providential frame. Timur gave him "بشارت سلطنت", and Khizr Khan credited his success to "برکات توجهات حضرت صاحب قران" (p. 328). Timur's grant is put in a prophetic register: "بر زبان وحی ترجمان گذشت که من سلطنت هندوستان بخضرخان بخشیدیم" (p. 331). A verse on his death reads "ذات پاکش که بود خضر شعار / بکرامات رفت بر سر کار" (p. 327). He is a ruler, not a saint.
- **Hardwar.** The belief that casting bones into the Ganges brings "رستگاری فرورفتگان" is attributed to "اهل هند" (p. 331).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Moral judgements, with verses.**
  - Iqbal Khan's end is "نتیجه حرام نمکی و عهد شکنی" (p. 329).
  - The peace with Mahmud of Malwa was "باعث زبونی و بد نقشی سلطان" (p. 324).
  - On two kings at once: "پریشان شود کار ملک از دو شاه" (p. 334).
  - Ala al-Din was weaker even than his father ("از پدر هم[OCR?] … سست تر است") (pp. 323–324).
  - Hisam Khan's counsel against leaving Delhi is endorsed in a verse on rash resolve (p. 323).
- **Dynastic count, Tughluqs.** "از ابتدائے سلطان غیاث الدین تغلق شاه عرف غازی الملک تا سلطان ناصر الدین محمود شاه هشت تن مدت نود و شش سال و دو ماه و یازده روز" (p. 329).
- **Dynastic count, the Turks.** "سلسله سلطنت نسل[OCR?] ترکان که فرزندان و خویشان و غلامان سلطان شهاب الدین غوری بودند بست و چهار تن دوصد و سی و سه سال" ended here (p. 329).
- **Predestination.** "سلطنت نصیب خضر خان از ازل مقرر بود" (p. 328).
- **The editor's apparatus.** The 1918 editor's footnotes cite "فرشته جلد اول" at pages "۱۶۲[OCR?]", "۱۶۴[OCR?]", "۱۶۵[OCR?]" and "۱۶۷[OCR?]" (pp. 325–327). He was collating names against Firishta.

## Doubts

**Reading order and chunk edges**
- **Reading order.** The chunk reads from p. 334 down to p. 323, and each page join was checked. It opens at the reign heading of Nasir al-Din Mahmud Shah (p. 334). It ends mid-sentence in Ala al-Din's reign, at "او از بداؤن فرار نموده در دهلی آمده بالاتفاق" (p. 323).
- **Zero site-specific mappings.** No archive saint, shrine or principal figure is named in this chunk. All 48 ids are place-level.

**Place-level attachments (ruling a). Weigh accordingly.**
- **Multan (8).**
  - The "مزارات مشایخ" visited in 835[OCR?] are unnamed. The book does not say which tombs, so no single row (e.g. Bahauddin Zakariya) is chosen. The attachment is place-level only.
  - The clause "بملتان رسیده مزارات مشایخ را طواف نموده" places the tombs at Multan only by sequence.
  - The Multan line includes the held "Shams" row and prahladpuri-temple. No name in the chunk raises either question.
  - "ملتان" is sometimes the city ("در ملتان توقف ورزید", "حصار ... ملتان", p. 333) and sometimes a governorship ("فتح پور تابع ملتان", "ایالت ... ملتان", pp. 328, 331).
- **Fatehpur.** "از فتح پور تابع ملتان که دارالایالت او بود" (p. 328) is ambiguous: the "دارالایالت" may be Fatehpur or Multan. It is not resolved.
- **Lahore (35).**
  - This is siege, fort, garrison, resettlement and governorship material, so it meets the addendum's test for toponym material.
  - Within the line, the held-identity rows (shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri) are attached place-level only. No name raises their question.
  - "قلعه" at Lahore (pp. 325, 327) is not otherwise identified. The two "lahore fort" rows are already on the line.
- **Uch (5).** The book says only that Pir Muhammad took its "حصار" (p. 333). The held Uch Jalaluddin / Jahaniyan question is not raised, because neither man is named.
- **Governorships named after cities.** "ولایت لاهور و دیپالپور" (p. 324), "ایالت لاهور و ملتان و دیپالپور" (p. 331) and "ان ولایت" (p. 327) are governorships named after cities. The book gives no extent, there is no suba in this period, and "پنجاب" does not occur in the chunk. They are kept under the city toponyms, and no PROVINCE punjab bullet is made.
- **"تا نواحی سند" (p. 330).** This is not made a PROVINCE sindh bullet. The sentence says only that Khizr Khan's holding reached the environs of "سند", not that he held Sind. The same chunk uses "اب سند" for the river (p. 333), so "سند" here may be the river. A consolidator may weigh it.

**Traps avoided (not mapped)**
- **Names that look like archive saints.**
  - "ملک سرور" (the wazir, pp. 324–326), "لوائے سروری" (p. 324) and "علم سروری" (p. 330) are not **sakhi-sarwar**.
  - "شاهزاده فرید" (p. 325) is Khizr Khan's son, not Baba Farid.
  - "شمس الدین اوحدی" of Bayana (p. 330) is not the Multan "Shams".
  - "ملک سلیمان" (p. 328) matches no row.
  - "خواجه خضر" occurs only in a ford's name near Delhi (p. 323).
  - Khizr Khan "سیدزاده" has no row.
- **Places that look like archive toponyms.**
  - "آب جهلم" (p. 326) is the river, so the toponym "jhelum" (gurdwara-choa-sahib, tilla-jogian) is not attached.
  - "قلعه شور" / "شور" (pp. 325–326) is not equated by the book with "شورکوت", so **garh-maharaja-shorkot** is not attached. A consolidator may weigh this.
  - Dipalpur has no row. The chunk never mentions Ajodhan or Patan, so nothing goes to shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar.
  - "دیالپور[OCR?]" (p. 327), paired with Lahore, may be Dipalpur. It has no row either way.
  - "بهکهر" / "بهکهر[OCR?]", the Khokhars' seat (pp. 326–327), is not "بهکر". Neither has a row.
  - "تتهه[OCR?]", conquered by the Kashmir ruler (p. 327), cannot be confirmed as Thatta (تهته). There is no row either way, and it is not province material.
- **"تلوندی".** "تلوندی رائے کمال الدین مبین[OCR?]" (p. 327) and "تلوندی[OCR?] تهاره[OCR?]" (p. 330) are not taken to the 7 Nankana Sahib rows.
  - The book ties both to a Rai Kamal al-Din and "زمینداران تهاره", in campaign itineraries (across the Sutlej toward Ludhiana; on Iqbal Khan's road to Dipalpur).
  - It nowhere connects them with Nanak or with the "تلونڈی رائے بهوئه" of p. 520 (chunk 042).
  - The brief's equation Talwandi = Nankana Sahib is read as applying to Nanak's Talwandi. This is flagged for the consolidator rather than applied.
- **Other non-mappings.** Hardwar names no deity, so no many-row person ids apply. "مرزبان کشمیر" is the Kashmir valley ruler, and **sharada-peeth** is not named. "گجرات" (pp. 330, 332, 333) is suba Gujarat (Zafar Khan "حاکم گجرات"), not Punjab Gujrat.

**Internal points not resolved**
- **Muhammad Shah's dates.** His visit to Multan is dated "۸۳۵[OCR?]" (p. 324), but his accession is "۸۳۷[OCR?]" (p. 325). Both are flagged, and the contradiction is kept.
- **Muhammad Shah's parentage.** Ala al-Din's heading calls Muhammad Shah "بن سلطان مبارک شاه" (p. 324). But p. 325 makes him the son of Prince Farid, raised by Mubarak Shah as his son. "سلطان انتقام خون پدر گرفت" (p. 324) refers to Mubarak Shah.
- **Khizr Khan's genealogy.** In "خضر خان بن ملک سلیمان پسر خوانده ملک مروان" (p. 328), it is unclear whether Sulaiman or Khizr Khan is the adopted son. It is quoted, not resolved.
- **Garbled or ambiguous clauses, quoted as printed.**
  - "بر سر بهرام خان که بعد بهرام خان و در سامانه دم استقلال میزد" (p. 329).
  - "چون بر سلطان ابراهیم نتوانست تسلط کرد و جونپور را فتح نموده" (p. 330). It is not clear whether Jaunpur was taken.
  - "ادنی کسے را ببست[OCR?] تقریر ده[OCR?] ببدست[OCR?] آمده بود" (p. 332).
- **Editor's footnotes without a visible anchor.** Several have no visible anchor in the transcription: "(۱) میرضیا" (p. 329); "(۱) رفته[OCR?] فرشته جلد اول صفحه ۱۶۲[OCR?]" (p. 327); "(۱) سیوتر[OCR?] فرشته جلد اول صفحه ۱۶۷[OCR?]" (p. 326). Their target words are not identified. Footnotes on pp. 323, 325 and 333 are wholly or partly [illegible].
