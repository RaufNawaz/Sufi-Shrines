# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 013 — pp. 156–169

The book reads from the highest page down. The chunk opens mid-sentence on p. 169 with the end of the hill-zamindar section that closes chunk 014 (Raja Basu pardoned, "در کوهستان امنیت پدید آمد"). It continues with "در بیان جشن والا" (pp. 168–169: a Nowruz festival, cash given away from a tank, and a fire in the great tent), then "در بیان رحلت میان[OCR?] تان سین و مولاناے عرفی شیرازی و شیخ ابوالفیض فیضی ملک الشعرا" (pp. 166–168) and "در بیان رسیدن برهان الملک بدرگاه والا و مرحمت شدن کومک باو بر مهم اطراف دکن" (pp. 164–166: Burhan al-Mulk, Chand Bibi, Prince Murad's death in the Deccan, Abul Fazl sent). The one section that touches the archive is "نهضت موکب مقدس از لاهور بجانب ولایت دکن" (pp. 163–164). The emperor leaves Lahore, deals with a fight between Muslim faqirs and Sannyasis at Achal near Batala, crosses the Beas, visits Guru Arjan, "سجاده نشین بابا نانک", hears Nanak's verses and lowers the revenue demand at his request. He then punishes an oppressor at Thanesar and reaches Burhanpur. After "در بیان تسخیر قلعه اسیر و ولایت احمدنگر" (pp. 161–162) comes a digression on the Deccan dynasties (Bahmanis, "عادل خانیه", "قطب الملک حاکم ولایت گولکنده", "نظام الملک حاکم ولایت احمدنگر"; pp. 157–161), which names Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya. The chunk ends with "در بیان کشته شدن شیخ ابوالفضل و ازینمعنی آشوب خاطر مقدس" (pp. 156–157), which breaks off mid-sentence.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Saints, faqirs and a temple**
- **Achal near Batala (outside Pakistan per the brief, no row).** "در حوالی قصبه بتاله بعرض والا رسید که در مه[OCR?] مکان اچل[OCR?] فقیران مسلمین و طایفه سناسیان[OCR?] با یکدیگر جنگ کرده" (p. 164).
  - Near Batala the emperor was told that Muslim faqirs and the Sannyasis[OCR?] had fought at "مه[OCR?] مکان اچل[OCR?]". The Muslim faqirs won and "بتخانه انجا[OCR?] را از روۓ تعدی بر انداخته": they wrongfully pulled down the idol-temple there.
  - Acting "بمقتضاۓ عدالت گستری" (as justice required), the emperor sent most of that party to prison and ordered "بتخانه را که منهدم[OCR?] شده است بتجدید تعمیر کنند": the destroyed temple was to be rebuilt (p. 164).
- **Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya (no row).** "سلطان محمد علاء الدین[OCR?] عرف حسن گانگو که از معتقدان قدوة الاصفیا شیخ نظام الدین اولیا بود و بموجب دعاۓ شیخ در سنه ۷۴۸[OCR?] دکن را متصرف شده سکه و خطبه بنام خود کرد" (p. 161). Hasan Gangu, the founder of the Bahmani line, was a devotee of the Shaikh. By the Shaikh's prayer he took the Deccan in "۷۴۸[OCR?]" and struck coin and read the khutba in his own name.
- **Faqirs as recipients.** The cash from the "انوپ[OCR?] تالاب" went to amirs, "بفقراے[OCR?] و مساکین و سایر الناس" (p. 168).

- **Bangash ("بنگش[OCR?]").** Burhan al-Mulk, after his mansab of a thousand, "تعینات بنگش[OCR?] گردید" (was posted to Bangash) for some years (p. 166).

"گوالیار[OCR?]", which Raja Basu had seized, was handed to "راین داس[OCR?] گوالیاری[OCR?]". At the eldest prince's request Basu's offences were pardoned (p. 169).

- **Thanesar ("تهانیسر[OCR?]", outside Pakistan).** The peasants complained of "ظلم تعدی[OCR?] سلطان[OCR?] کردے[OCR?]". The charge was proved, and "او را بخلق[OCR?] کشیدند" (p. 164).

**The Deccan (outside Pakistan, briefly)**
- **Burhan al-Mulk.**
  - He was angry with his uncle "اسمعیل[OCR?] بیگ[OCR?] نظام الملک" and came to court through "قطب الدین خان غزنوی". He was given a mansab of 600, then 1000 (p. 166).
  - The amirs of "صوبه مالوه" and Raja Ali Khan of Khandesh were sent to help him. He won Ahmadnagar, then grew arrogant and rebelled. Faizi was sent to bring him back to obedience, without success (pp. 165–166).
  - He died after "شخصے[OCR?] او را سیماب کشته که بحد کمال نرسیده بود بخوردن داد": someone gave him imperfectly prepared mercury to eat (p. 165).
- **Prince Murad and Abul Fazl.**
  - Chand Bibi raised Burhan's young son Ibrahim. Prince Sultan Murad crossed the Narbada and took Berar. Raja Ali Khan was killed fighting on his side (p. 165).
  - The prince was "دایم الخمر" (always drunk). Abul Fazl was sent, and the prince died "از افراط[OCR?] بیمارے" just as he arrived. Abul Fazl restored order (p. 165).
- **The emperor in the Deccan.**
  - Prince Daniyal was sent ahead and the emperor followed (p. 164).
  - At Burhanpur "دار السرور" a Nowruz festival was held, and Abul Fazl came from Ahmadnagar (p. 163). Burhanpur's revenue was assigned "در انعام امرا" according to mansab, and Abul Fazl was given mansab 4000 (p. 162).
  - Asir fort, held by "بهادر نبیره راجے علی خان", was taken when Abul Fazl climbed in by a rope. He was given standard, drum and a robe, then mansab 5000, and sent against Nasik (p. 162).
  - Ahmadnagar and "تمام ولایت نظام الملک" fell "بشمشیر همت شیخ ابوالفضل", and Telangana fell to his son Shaikh Abdur Rahman. "بهادر نظام الملک نبیره برهان نظام الملک" was captured (p. 162).
  - "خاندیس را داندیس نام نهاده بشاهزاده مرحمت فرمودند": Khandesh was renamed Dandes and given to Daniyal. The court returned to Akbarabad (pp. 161–162).
- **The Deccan digression.** It is introduced with "از بعضے تواریخ چنان بمطالعه[OCR?] درآمده" (p. 161).
  - All the Deccan was once under Delhi. Sultan Muhammad Shah "فخرالدین جونا" renamed Deogir Daulatabad and made it his capital. The "امیر صدها" rebelled, and Hasan Gangu, a soldier of Malik Lachin, took power at Daulatabad. He was called "بهمنه" from descent from "بهمن بن اسفندیار بن گشتاسپ" (p. 161).
  - The Bahmani reigns are listed (pp. 159–161), totalling "هفده نفر یکصد و هشتاد و هفت سال". Barid ruled in the Bahmanis' name for "چهل و هشت سال". In "سنه نهصد و سی و پنج هجری" the amirs divided the Deccan, and "تا حال اولاد انها دران ممالک حکومت دارد" (p. 159).
  - The Adil Khans of Bijapur (pp. 158–159). Yusuf Adil Khan is described as "غلام چرکس". The line runs "لغایت سال هزار و دو هجری", and "تا حال ... اولادش دران ولایت حکومت دارد" (p. 158).
  - The Qutb al-Mulks of Golconda (p. 158). Muhammad Quli "شهر بهاگ نگر[OCR?] بنام ان لولی بنا کرد" (founded the city after the lūlī "بهاگی[OCR?]"). His reign runs "لغایت سنه هزار و ده هجری".
  - The Nizam al-Mulks of Ahmadnagar (pp. 157–158). Ahmad Bahri founded Ahmadnagar. The line held the country "از ابتداۓ سنه ۹۳۵ لغایت سنه هزار و دو هجری شصت و هفت سال" (p. 157).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Revenue relief at Guru Arjan's petition.** The jama of the parganas had been raised during dear grain. On his petition the diwans were to reduce it "بحساب ده دوازده[OCR?] انده[OCR?]", and the amils were told "زیاده طلبی ننمایند" (p. 164).
- **The state rebuilds a temple.** At Achal the destroyed "بتخانه" was ordered "بتجدید تعمیر", and most of the Muslim faqirs who had destroyed it were imprisoned (p. 164).
- **Peshkash.** Guru Arjan[OCR?] presented a "پیشکش لایق" (p. 164). Tansen was himself sent as peshkash by a zamindar (p. 167).
- **Punishing oppressors.** The oppressor at Thanesar[OCR?] was executed on the peasants' complaint (p. 164).
- **Charity from a tank of coin.** The "تالابے موسوم بانوپ[OCR?] تالاب" measured "بست در بست[OCR?] درعه[OCR?]" and "دو قامت آدم عمیق". It was filled with "تنگ[OCR?] نقد", which was given to amirs by rank, "بفقراے[OCR?] و مساکین و سایر الناس" (p. 168).
- **The court's tent.** The "بارگاه جهان آرا" was said to hold "پانزده هزار[OCR?] کس". It burned, was rebuilt within days, and the "بزم شرف" was held (p. 168).
- **Assignments and ranks.**
  - Burhanpur's revenue went "در انعام امرا" according to "تفاوت مناصب" while the court stayed (p. 162).
  - Abul Fazl received mansab 4000 and then 5000, with "علم و نقاره و اسپ و خلعت خاصه" (p. 162).
  - Burhan al-Mulk received mansab 600 and then 1000 (p. 166).
- **Measured road.** From Akbarabad to Burhanpur was "دو صد و بست و هفت کروه[OCR?]", measured "بجریب" (p. 163).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The prayer of Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya.** Hasan Gangu took the Deccan "بموجب دعاۓ شیخ", the prayer of Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya (p. 161).
- **The gold chain at the Narbada.** When the royal elephant crossed the Narbada, its iron chain came out "تمام از طلا" (wholly of gold). The emperor said "همانا درین دریا سنگی خواهد بود که بزبان هندی انرا پارس گویند", a stone whose touch turns iron and other metals to gold. Divers searched with other chained elephants, "لیکن بهم نرسید و زنجیر دیگر طلا نگشت" (p. 163).
- **"از غرایب".** On the same day as the court fire, fire broke out in Prince Sultan Murad's palace in the Deccan (p. 168).
- **Tansen's music.** It is said to have drawn "وحشیان در صحرا و طایران در هوا" into the snare of love for him (p. 168).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On Burhan al-Mulk.** The author's moral is "کافران نعمت زود بپاداش کردار میرسند": the ungrateful are soon repaid. He reads Burhan's death as "مکافات اعمال" (p. 165).
- **On Abul Fazl.**
  - "اگر درین وقت شیخ نرسیدے آن ولایت که بتلاش بدست آمده بود از دست میرفت" (p. 165).
  - Ahmadnagar was won "بشمشیر همت علامی شیخ ابوالفضل" (pp. 157, 162).
  - The murder section praises his "جوانمردی و شجاعت ذاتی" (p. 156).
- **On the emperor's justice.** The Achal punishment was "بمقتضاۓ عدالت گستری". Verses on care of subjects follow the Arjan petition and the Thanesar execution (p. 164).
- **On the poets and musicians.**
  - Tansen: "بے شایبه اغراق در فن موسیقی و نغمه هندی از آغاز آفرینش تا حال مثل او کم کسے بود" (pp. 167–168).
  - Urfi: matchless in verse "اما خیلے متکبر و خودستا بود", given to gambling and wine (p. 167).
  - Faizi: "در شعر متین و سخن رنگین ... نظیر خود نداشت" (p. 167).
- **Sources.**
  - The Deccan history is taken "از بعضے تواریخ" (p. 161), and the author points to Abul Fazl's elegy for Faizi "در اکبر نامه" (p. 166).
  - The 1918 editor's footnotes give variant reign lengths from "تاریخ فرشته" and "طبقات اکبری" (pp. 157–161).
- **The author's present.** "تا حال که این نسخه تحریر[OCR?] می درآید" (p. 158) and "تا حال" (pp. 158, 159) say that the Bijapur, Golconda and other Deccan houses still rule.
