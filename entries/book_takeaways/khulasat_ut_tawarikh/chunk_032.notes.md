# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 032 — pp. 397–411

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-sentence, at the end of Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni's reign. It covers his boat campaign "بجانب ملتان وسنده", his character and his patronage of poets (Firdawsi and the Shahnama), the story of Malik Ayaz, a digression on hair-dye in old age, and Mahmud's death (pp. 406–411). Short run-in paragraphs follow on Amir Mas'ud, Amir Abu Sa'id, Bahram Shah, Khusraw Shah and Khusraw Malik, the last two ruling "ولایت پنجاب" from Lahore (pp. 405–406). The section "سلطان شهاب الدین عرف معزالدین محمد" (pp. 401–405) numbers his campaigns: Multan and Uch, Gujarat, three on Lahore with the rebuilding of Sialkot fort, Dewal/Thatta, the two battles at "نراین", Kanauj and the Khokhars. It ends with his martyrdom and with Qutb al-Din Aibak's rise, which has no heading of its own. Aibak's reign at Lahore runs from p. 401 to p. 400. It is followed by "سلطان آرام شاه پسرخوانده سلطان قطب الدین" (pp. 399–400), "سلطان شمش الدین عرف ملک التمش" (pp. 398–399) and "سلطان رکن الدین فیروزشاه بن سلطان شمس الدین التمش" (pp. 397–398). The chunk breaks off inside "سلطان بی بی رضیه بنت سلطان شمس الدین التمش[OCR?]" at "سلطان رضیه در اندک" (p. 397). No saint, dervish or shrine is named anywhere in the chunk.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak** (the book's "ملک قطب الدین ایبک", "سلطان قطب الدین ایبک", "سلطان قطب الدین"):
  - After the victory at "نراین", Sultan Shihab al-Din left "ملک قطب الدین ایبک را که غلام برگزیده او بود درقصبه کهرام هفتاد کروهے دهلی نایب سلطنت". That is, his chosen slave was made viceroy at the qasba of Kuhram, seventy kos from Delhi (p. 402).
  - As deputy ("نیابتاً"), after the Sultan went back to Ghazni: "دراسرع اوقات قلعه دهلی ومیرته تسخیر نموده بهرجا حکام نصب گردانید". In the second year he took "قلعه کول", and then the forts of Gwalior, Budaun "ودیگر قلاع" (pp. 401–402).
  - He marched on Gujarat. There he took revenge on "راۓ بهیم دیو گجراتی" for the Sultan's earlier defeat ("انتقام شکست سلطان که سابقاً بتحریر درامده") and plundered the country. He returned to Delhi and "خطبه وسکه بنام سلطان رواج داد" (p. 401).
  - "ازان تاریخ شهر دهلی دارالخلافت سلاطین مسلمین مقرر گردیده": from then on Delhi was the capital of the Muslim sultans (p. 401).
  - In the Sultan's tenth campaign, against the Khokhars near Lahore, "ملک قطب الدین نیز از دهلی بخدمت سلطان رسیده باتفاق یکدیگر گوشمال انجماعت ... کردند" (p. 401).
  - Status: "سلطان قطب الدین ایبک غلام زرخرید سلطان شهاب الدین نیابتاً حکومت هندوستان داشت". He was the Sultan's purchased slave and governed Hindustan as his deputy (p. 401).
  - Sovereignty at Lahore: "بعد ازانکه سلطان درجه شهادت یافت از دهلی برامده درلاهور رسید اصالتاً سریراراۓ خلافت گشت ویازدهم ربیع الاول سنه ۶۰۳[OCR?] سکه وخطبه بنام خود کرد" (p. 401).
  - The name: "چون انگشت خنصر او شکسته بود ازینجهت اورا ایبک گفتندے", that is, because his little finger was broken (p. 401).
  - "سلطان غیاث الدین محمود[OCR?] برادرزاده سلطان شهاب الدین از فیروزه کوه" sent him "چتر وتجملات بادشاهی" and addressed him with the title of Sultan ("بخطاب سلطانی مخاطب ساخت") (pp. 400–401).
  - Generosity. A long passage praises his giving: "دست جود وسخایش گرد افلاس ازچهره مفلسان برافشاندی". It ends: "چون لکها باهل احتیاج انعام دادی ازینجهت درهند اورا لکهه بخش گفتندی", that is, he gave lakhs to the needy and so in Hind was called Lakh-bakhsh. A couplet follows: "باهمت او حوصله دریا تنگ   بارفعت او مرتبه گردون پست" (p. 400).
  - Taj al-Din Yildiz. "تاج الدین یلدوز", one of Shihab al-Din's "بندهاۓ خاص", had been given the name of sultan in Ghazni after Shihab al-Din's death. A dispute arose between him and Aibak "برسر لاهور", and they fought a pitched battle. "بالآخر تاج الدین تاب مقاومت نیاورده منهزم گشته درکرمان اقامت ورزید وسلطان مظفر ومنصور شده باستقلال کمال بسلطنت پرداخت" (p. 400).
  - Death: "درسنه [illegible] درچوگان بازی از اسپ افتاده گوۓ زندگی بجولانگاه آخرت رسانید". He fell from his horse at chaugan and died. The year is illegible, and the book names no place of death and no burial (p. 400).
  - Length of rule: "مدت بست سال حکومت هندوستان نمود ازانجمله سلطنت غزنین چهار سال" (p. 400). See Doubts.
  - Heir. "سلطان مرحوم پسر صلبی که سزاوار جهانبانی تواند بود نداشت". The nobles therefore seated "آرام بخش پسرخوانده سلطان", who was the only heir, "درسنه ۶۰۷[OCR?] درخطه دل کشاۓ لاهور" as Sultan Aram Shah (p. 400). Later: "از سلطان آرام شاه امور خلافت تمشیت نیافت" (p. 399).
  - Iltutmish. "اورا سلطان قطب الدین ایبک به[OCR?] لک جیتل خریده بفرزندی گرفته دختر خود را درمناکحت او درآورده بود": Aibak bought him, adopted him and married him to his daughter. "بعد فتح گوالیار امارت انجا باو ارزانی داشت". Aibak then gave him "برن[OCR?]" and its region and, step by step, Budaun. He rose to amir al-umara and "خط آزادی از سلطان یافت", that is, received his manumission from the Sultan (p. 399).

- **darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz** (the book's "ملک ایاز", "ایاز"):
  - "وسلطان اگرچه چندین هزار غلام داشت اماسرامد ایشان ملک ایاز بود که سلطان درکمند عشق اوگرفتاری داشت". That is, of Sultan Mahmud's many thousand slaves, Malik Ayaz was the chief, and the Sultan was caught in the noose of love for him (p. 410).
  - Origin, introduced with "گویند": "این ایاز خلف والی کشمیر بود". As a small child he went with his father to a hunting ground. "جمعی ازعیاران آدم دزد" seized him, took him out of that country and sold him in Badakhshan to a merchant "بقیمت خاطرخواه". "ازتقدیرات قادر مطلق شاهزاده بغلامی منسوب گشت" (p. 410).
  - The merchant carried rich goods and slaves. "نظر برحسن وجمال ایاز" he valued Ayaz above all of them and saw to his upbringing. He came "بقصد تجارت ازبدخشان درغزنین" (p. 410).
  - In Ghazni, word spread of a slave too beautiful to look at. Great and small came to see him, saying he could fairly be likened to the moon, and "درحسن وخوبی به یوسف کنعان" (pp. 409–410).
  - The Sultan heard of him and summoned the merchant with him. He bought Ayaz "بقیمت گران انچه سوداگر میخواست بلکه زیاده بران" and made him "انیس انجمن خاص وجلیس مجلس اختصاص" (p. 409).
  - A digression on love follows: "بدرگاهش شاه ودرویش برابر ودرحضرتش خواجه وغلام همسر" (p. 409).
  - "اورا برتمامی خادم وحشم برگزیده": the Sultan preferred him above all his servants and retinue (p. 409).
  - Verses on this love are in "محمود نامه ودیگر رساله". As the sentence runs, the Sultan himself composed them, "سوز درونی وراز نهانی خویش بعرصه ظهور آورده" (p. 409).
  - "قصه عشق ونیاز سلطان وحسن وناز ایاز مشهور هست[OCR?] ودراکثر کتب مسطور علی الخصوص مولانا زلالی" (p. 409). For Zulali's dream version, see Legends (pp. 408–409).
  - Introduced with "بعضی شعرا نوشته اند": "ایاز اگرچه حسن صوری وجمال ظاهری چندان نداشت لیکن دراخلاق پسندیده طاق ... بود". The Sultan's devotion was to his good character ("سیرت نیک خصایل"), not to his looks (p. 408).
  - This chunk says nothing of Ayaz in Lahore, of any office he held, or of his death or tomb.

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE punjab (book's word: "ولایت پنجاب"; refers to: the rule of Khusraw Shah and his son Khusraw Malik from Lahore, as the book places it. Khusraw Shah died "درسنه ۵۵۵", and Khusraw Malik went to Ghazni "درسنه ۵۸۲ هجری"):
  - Khusraw Shah, having taken Lahore, "تاآخر عمر بحکومت ولایت پنجاب قیام ورزیده" (p. 405).
  - "سلطان خسرو ملک بن خسروشاه بعد رحلت پدر برمسند حکومت ولایت پنجاب متمکن گشت" (p. 405).
- PROVINCE sindh. Book's words: "سنده" / "سند"; also "دریاۓ سنده", which is the river. Refers to: three periods, as the book places them. All three items are campaign or flight mentions.
  - Mahmud's reign: "لشکر عظیم بجانب ملتان وسنده کشیده تا چهار هزار کشتی ساختند". Each boat had three iron spikes, one at the prow and one on each side. The people opposing them sent their families to the islands and fought, and most were killed or drowned (p. 411).
  - Shihab al-Din's fourth campaign, "درسنه ۵[illegible]": "برسر دیول که بتهته مشهور است رفته تمام ولایت درکنار دریاۓ سنده بضبط درآورد ونفایس ونوادر ان دیار بامال بسیار گرفته برگشت" (p. 404).
  - Iltutmish's reign: Jalal al-Din fled "بطرف سند وسیوستان" (p. 399).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Ghazni rulers and their court (pp. 405–411)**
- **Sultan Mahmud.**
  - Character: just, generous and pious. "بقصد حصول ثواب دوازده نوبت بهندوستان یورش نموده ... ازینجهت اهل اسلام اورا غازی گفتندے" (p. 411).
  - Letters: he wrote verse and honoured "فضلا وشعرا" (p. 411).
  - Old age and death. He took to dyeing his beard on his doctors' advice (p. 408). He died "درسنه ۲۱[OCR?] بعلت دق وزحمت ضیق النفس" (p. 407).
  - Deathbed ("گویند"). He had all his treasures laid before him, "دانگی بکسی نداد", and died grieving at parting from them (pp. 406–407).
  - "مدت سلطنت سی وپنجسال" (p. 406).
- **Firdawsi.** "مولاناۓ حسن ... درشعر خود فردوسی تخلص مینمود". The name came from a garden at Tus that its governor named "فردوس", where the poet's father was gardener (p. 411). At Mahmud's order he wrote the Shahnama, "بشصت هزار بیت درمدت سی سال" (p. 410).
- **Mahmud's successors.**
  - "امیر مسعود بن سلطان محمود" could make no campaign on Hindustan (p. 406).
  - "امیر ابوسعید بن امیر مسعود ازشش برادر کلان تر": two campaigns on Hindustan, returning "بعد انهدام بتخانه وبعضی امکنه" (p. 406).
  - "بهرام شاه": in his time "مخزن اسرار" of "مولاناۓ نظام الدین گنجوی" and "کلیله ودمنه" of "علامه نصرالله مستوفی" were completed. He died "درسنه ۵۴۷" (p. 406).
  - Khusraw Shah: see the Lahore bullet. His reign is given as "پنجاه وهشت سال" (p. 405).
- **Summary of the line:** "ازابتداۓ سلطان ناصرالدین سبکتگین تاخسرو ملک هفت تن مدت دوصد وهفتده سال سلطنت غزنین وبلاد هندوستان کردند" (p. 405).

**Sultan Shihab al-Din alias Mu'izz al-Din Muhammad (pp. 401–405)**
- **Background.** His elder brother "سلطان شمس الدین مشهور بغیاث الدین ولد سلطان علاۂ الدین حسین" held Ghur. Shihab al-Din took Ghazni "درسنه ۵۶۹" and held it as his brother's deputy (p. 405).
- **Campaigns as numbered by the book:**
  - 1: Multan and Uch.
  - 2, "۵۷۴[OCR?]": Gujarat, defeated by "راۓ بهیم دیو".
  - 3, "۵۷۵[OCR?]": Lahore.
  - 4, "۵[illegible]": Dewal/Thatta.
  - 5, "۵۸۰[OCR?]": Lahore and Sialkot.
  - 6, "۵۸۲[OCR?]": Lahore.
  - 7, "۵۸۷[OCR?]": Sirhind, then "موضع نراین که الحال ان را تلاوری گویند". Defeat by "راۓ پتهورا". "کهاندی راۓ" wounded the Sultan's arm with a lance, and "غلام خلج بچه" carried him from the field. Rai Pithora then took Sirhind "بعد محاصره یکسال ویکماه".
  - 8, "۵۸۸[OCR?]": "نراین[OCR?]". Victory: Rai Pithora captured and killed, Khande Rai fled. "قلعه سرسنی[OCR?] که تلاوری بوده باشد", Hansi and Ajmer were taken.
  - 9, "۵۹۶[OCR?]": Kanauj, with "سیصد زنجیر فیل".
  - 10: the Khokhars near Lahore.
  - Pages: pp. 401–405.
- **Sirhind.** The editor's footnote to "قلعه سهرند" (seventh campaign) reads "قلعه بهتنده فرشته جلد اول صفحه ۵۷[OCR?]" (p. 404).
- **Ghiyath al-Din's death.** The Sultan divided Ghur, "ترکستان وخارزم" among the heirs and kept only Ghazni (p. 401).

  - Piety and the Friday attack. He went to the Jami mosque every Friday. "ملحدان دهلی" attacked him there; he escaped, and the townspeople killed them from the roofs and gates (pp. 398–399).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Signs of sovereignty: coin and khutba.**
  - Aibak struck in his own name at Lahore, "یازدهم ربیع الاول سنه ۶۰۳[OCR?]" (p. 401). Earlier he had struck in Shihab al-Din's name (p. 401).
  - Raziya "سکه وخطبه بنام خود رواج داد" (p. 397). The nobles had struck in her name on deposing Rukn al-Din (p. 398).
- **Regalia and investiture.**
  - "چتر وتجملات بادشاهی" and the title Sultan were sent to Aibak from Firuzkuh (p. 400).
  - "جامه خلافت" was brought to Iltutmish by an "ایلچی عرب" in "۶۲۶" (p. 399).
- **Slavery and manumission.** Aibak was a "غلام زرخرید" (p. 401). Iltutmish was bought for "به[OCR?] لک جیتل" and later received "خط آزادی" (p. 399). Ayaz was bought in Ghazni "بقیمت گران" (p. 409).
- **Tribute.** Khusraw Malik sent his son and one elephant as peshkash, and the Sultan fixed "پیشکش هرساله" (p. 404).
- **Deputies (نایب)** were left at Multan and Uch (p. 405), at Sialkot (p. 404) and at Lahore (p. 404). Aibak was viceroy at Kuhram (p. 402). Twelve hundred horse were left at Sirhind (p. 404).
- **Forts.**
  - Sialkot fort was rebuilt (p. 404).
  - "قلعه لاهور" served as Khusraw Malik's refuge (p. 404).
  - Aibak "بهرجا حکام نصب گردانید" in the forts he took (p. 401).
- **Temples.** Mahakal was destroyed and its images were buried at the mosque door "تا لگدکوب خلایق گردد" (p. 399). Abu Sa'id destroyed a "بتخانه" (p. 406).
- **Royal giving.** Aibak's "لکهه بخش" (p. 400). Mahmud's honours to poets (p. 411).
- **Mahmud's boat fleet:** "چهار هزار کشتی", each with three iron spikes, manned with archers and "بندوق وقاروره" (p. 411).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Malik Ayaz: the dream.** The book attributes this to "مولانا زلالی". "سلطان ان ماه دل فریب را درخواب دیده وبودن آن لعل کان دلبری را دربدخشان درهمان خواب معلوم کرده". Like Zulaikha, he fell in love, went to Badakhshan "بلباس فقیری", recognised Ayaz from the form he had seen "درعالم مثال", bought him at a high price and brought him to Ghazni (pp. 408–409).
- **Malik Ayaz: origin.** The book introduces it with "گویند": a prince of Kashmir, stolen by kidnappers (p. 410).
- **No saint's karamat** appears in this chunk.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Providence at "نراین".** The author explains the victory by divine will. "چون مشیت قادر علی الاطلاق بران شد که سلسله جهانبانی هندوستان از فرقه هنود منقطع گردد واین مملکت درظل سلطنت ... فرمانروایان مسلمین درآید" (p. 402).
- **Earlier defeats.** "قانونی است قدیم": God puts his chosen one through hardship before opening the gates of success (p. 403).
- **Hair-dye.** The author first praises dye as restoring youth's lustre. He then sets against it the "قانون گویان اقلیم وجود": "عود جوانی بیرون از حیز امکانی است", and dyeing in old age is "محض ابلهی". He closes with "پیری است نه کافری نهان نتوان کرد" (pp. 407–408).
- **Mahmud's death as a moral.** The treasure scene is followed by verses on giving while one can (p. 406).
- **Ayaz.** The author, following "بعضی شعرا", holds that the Sultan loved Ayaz for his character, not his face (p. 408).
- **Judgements on women rulers.**
  - On Shah Turkan: "بمقتضاۓ کوته اندیشی که در سرشت زنان مخمر است" (p. 398).
  - On Raziya: "در شجاعت وعدالت از مردان بهره وافی داشت" (p. 397).
- **Sources and hedges.**
  - "گویند" is used for Ayaz's origin (p. 410), Mahmud's deathbed (p. 407), Shihab al-Din's treasure (p. 401) and Iltutmish's name (p. 399).
  - "بعضی شعرا نوشته اند" (p. 408); "مولانا زلالی" and "محمود نامه" (p. 409).
  - Cross-references to earlier pages of the book: "که سابقاً بتحریر درامده" and "چنانچه تفصیل آن بقلم درآمده" (p. 401); "که شمه از احوال او بتحریر درآمده" (p. 404).

## Doubts

**Reading order and chunk edges**
- **Reading order.** The chunk runs from p. 411 down to p. 397. Every page join was checked, high to low, and each continues the sentence.
- **Opening.** The chunk opens mid-sentence: "لشکر عظیم بجانب ملتان وسنده کشیده" (p. 411). The people Mahmud's boats were sent against are named on the previous page, not here. The words "رد باستیصال معابد اوردوان مردم خبردار شده" are printed unflagged and are quoted, not read.
- **Close.** The chunk ends mid-sentence: "سلطان رضیه در اندک" (p. 397).
- **No saints.** No saint, dervish, tomb or shrine is named in the chunk, so the addendum's item 4 yields nothing here.

**tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak**
- **Mapped by the person rule.** The book gives no place of death, no burial and no tomb. Lahore is named for his accession (p. 401) and his quarrel with Yildiz (p. 400), not for his death.
- **His death year is "[illegible]"** (p. 400). It is not filled.
- **The accession date "۶۰۳[OCR?]" is flagged** (p. 401).
- **"ازانجمله سلطنت غزنین چهار سال" (p. 400) is not explained in this chunk.** The same page says Yildiz held the name of sultan in Ghazni. It is quoted, not interpreted.
- **Not Aibak:** "قطب الدین پسر سلطان", killed by Shah Turkan (p. 398). He is a son of Sultan Shams al-Din, and is not mapped.
- **Iltutmish's price "به[OCR?] لک جیتل" and the town "برن[OCR?]"** are flagged (p. 399).

**darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz**
- **The basis for mapping** is the name "ملک ایاز" plus the book's "سرامد" of Sultan Mahmud's slaves. The row's principal figure is described as Mahmud's slave.
- **What the chunk does not give:** no Lahore, no governorship, no minister's office, no death and no tomb.
- **The row has no province in the index.**
- **"ملک اعزالدین[OCR?] ایاز حاکم ملتان" (p. 398) is NOT mapped to this row.** The book places him in Rukn al-Din Firuz Shah's reign as governor of Multan, a different man by the book's own chronology. Part of his name is flagged. He appears only in the Multan place-level bullet.
- **"Kashmir"** appears only as "والی کشمیر", Ayaz's father's realm. **sharada-peeth** is not attached; the book names no site in Kashmir.

**Place-level attachments (ruling a). Weigh accordingly.**
- **Groups attached:** Lahore (35), Sialkot (5), Multan (8) and Uch Sharif (5).
- **Siwistan → Sehwan Sharif (2)** follows the brief's equation of سیوستان with Sehwan. toponym_map has no "siwistan" key, and Lal Shahbaz is not named.
- **Passing mentions (addendum item 3):**
  - Multan: p. 411 and the second campaign on p. 405.
  - Uch: the second campaign on p. 405.
  - Siwistan: p. 399.
  - The substantive place material is:
    - Lahore: governors, sieges, its fort, enthronements and its environs plundered (pp. 399–405).
    - Sialkot: the ancient fort rebuilt and besieged (p. 404).
    - Multan: conquest from the Qaramita (p. 405) and its governor (p. 398).
    - Uch: conquest from "هتاهته[OCR?]" (p. 405).
- **Held-identity rows** on the Lahore and Uch lines are attached only place-level. That covers shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh and shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri, and the Uch Jalaluddin/Jahaniyan pair. No name in the chunk raises their questions.
- **"ولایت لاهور" (p. 404) and "ولایت ملتان" (p. 405)** are attached to the city lines (ruling a), not made province bullets. In both passages the material is a siege or conquest of the town.
- **"از لاهور" at p. 399.** In "وسلطان بالشکر بسیار از لاهور رسیده مقابله نمود" the printed "از لاهور" (from Lahore) is quoted as printed. Whether Iltutmish came from Lahore or to it is not settled here.
- **The p. 400/399 join.** It is read as "از استماع آمدن / ملک از لاهور درحوالی دهلی ...": Aram Shah, hearing of the Malik's coming, moved from Lahore toward Delhi.

**Province bullets: province-level, weigh accordingly**
- **punjab.** "ولایت پنجاب" is the book's unit under Khusraw Shah and Khusraw Malik, not the modern province's boundaries. No extent is given or supplied.
- **sindh.**
  - All three items are campaign or flight mentions.
  - "تمام ولایت درکنار دریاۓ سنده" (p. 404) names the river, not a province, and is attached province-level by judgement.
  - The periods differ: Mahmud, Shihab al-Din and Iltutmish.
  - Thatta/Dewal has no row in toponym_map.

**Traps avoided**
- **"گجرات" on pp. 401, 404 and 405 is Rai Bhim Dev's Gujarat**, reached "براه ریگستان". It is not Punjab Gujrat, and shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai is not attached.
- **"بتخانه مهاکال" in Malwa (p. 399)** is a non-archive site, and the book names no deity there. The Shiva rows are not attached (addendum item 1).
- **Hansi (p. 402)** is named as Rai Pithora's fort; no saint is connected with it here.
- **"شمس الدین"** here is Iltutmish and **"جلال الدین"** is the prince fleeing Chingiz Khan. Neither is a saint, and neither is mapped.

**Internal points not resolved**
- **Campaign count.** The book numbers Shihab al-Din's campaigns only up to the tenth, but totals them as "پانزده نوبت" (p. 401).
- **Talawari.** On p. 404 Talawari is "موضع نراین که الحال ان را تلاوری گویند". On p. 402 it is "قلعه سرسنی[OCR?] که تلاوری بوده باشد". These are two identifications, both quoted.
- **Rukn al-Din's accession date.** It is printed "۶۲۶[OCR?]" (p. 398). That is the same figure as Iltutmish's caliphal robe "۶۲۶" and lower than his Malwa campaign "۶۳۱" (p. 399). The contradiction is left standing, and no interval is computed.
- **Name forms.** "سلطان ملک خسرو" (p. 404) and "خسرو ملک" (pp. 404–405) are two forms of one name. So are "شمش الدین" (p. 399, heading) and "شمس الدین" (pp. 397–398).
- **Two descriptions of the same commander.** "امیر علی اسمٰعیل حاکم دهلی" (p. 400) and "امیر اسمٰعیل سپه سالار" (p. 399).
- **Khusraw Shah's reign** is printed "پنجاه وهشت سال" (p. 405). The summary "هفت تن ... دوصد وهفتده سال" (p. 405) is quoted, not checked.
- **Raziya's place of imprisonment** is printed as Sirhind (p. 397). The editor's Bathinda footnote belongs to the seventh campaign on p. 404, not to p. 397.
