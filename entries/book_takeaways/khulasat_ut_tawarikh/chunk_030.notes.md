# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 030 — pp. 372–382

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-sentence in the reign of "سلطان معز الدین کیقباد" (named at p. 375), with long set-pieces on his pleasures: music ("سرود"), chess, ganjifa (p. 382), nard and chaupar (p. 381), and a praise of wine (p. 380). It goes on to his handing affairs to "امیر الامرا فخر الدین", the killing of the man summoned "از ملتان", a Mongol raid "در نواحی لاهور" and its defeat, and the punishment of the captives (pp. 379–380). Next come the killing of the Mongol amirs, the letters between him and his father "ناصر الدین بغرا خان" at "لکهنوتی", and the two rulers' meeting on the "دریائے سرجو" (pp. 376–378), his relapse, illness, and death at Kilokhri ("کیلوکهری") (pp. 375–376). Then comes the section "سلطان جلال الدین فیروز خلجی از نسل خالج خان داماد چنگیز خان" (pp. 372–375): his accession in "سنه ۶۸۹[OCR?]", his entry into Delhi and the festivities, Malik Chhajju's[OCR?] revolt and the sultan's pardon, and his refusal to shed blood (thieves set free, the siege of "رنتهنبور[OCR?]" abandoned). No saint at a Pakistani site is named in this chunk. The only archive attachments are two place-level toponyms, Multan and Lahore.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**A literary figure, outside Pakistan**
- "حضرت امیر خسرو دهلوی", called "مجموعه کمالات صوری ومعنوی", served Jalal al-Din Firuz "بخدمت مصحف داری". Every day he brought "غزلی تازه" and received a reward for it (p. 373). No row has him as principal figure.
- The book cites his "کتاب قران السعدین" on the meeting of Kaiqubad and Bughra Khan. According to it, the father had marched from "لکهنوتی عرف بنگاله" to take Delhi, and the son set out from Delhi to meet him. After meeting they made peace and went home (p. 376). The author sets this against his own account of a meeting of affection (see Arguments).

**Kaiqubad's reign (pp. 375–382)**
- Pastimes. Music: "سرود است که …" is repeated as a refrain (p. 382). Chess "مرغوب طبع بادشاهان" and ganjifa (pp. 381–382). Nard, and "چوپر از عجایب بازیهائے هندوستان", whose founder's name "از صحف متقدمین و کتب متاخرین یافته نمیشود[OCR?]". Through this game "کوران[OCR?]" overcame "پاندوان" (p. 381). He was "دایم الخمر" (p. 381), and wine is praised in prose and verse (p. 380).
- The treasury was spent on "لولی و مسخره و مطرب و مطربه", and affairs were left to "امیر الامرا فخر الدین" (p. 380).
- The Mongol captives are described at length as hideous (p. 379). On the vizirs' advice they were punished: trampled by elephants, put to the sword, half-buried, "چار میخ", and paraded with shaven heads and beards (p. 379).
- Fakhr al-Din warned that many amirs "از قوم مغل" might plot (pp. 378–379). The Mongol amirs were killed in one day, and some of Balban's maliks imprisoned in forts. "خواجه خضر خان", one of the vizirs, was paraded on an ass (p. 378).
- Bughra Khan's letter from "لکهنوتی" reached the sultan "در کیلوکهری", and his reply followed (p. 378). The armies camped on both banks of the "دریائے سرجو" (pp. 377–378). After "سه روزه" of talks it was agreed that the son sit on the throne and the father pay homage. Instead the son came down from the throne and seated his father beside him (p. 377).
- The father's parting advice on the treasury, the subjects and the soldiers, with a quiet word on removing Fakhr al-Din (p. 376). The sultan returned to the capital and abstained for a few days, then relapsed with "لولی بچه" (p. 376).
- Decline (p. 375). He poisoned Fakhr al-Din ("امیر الامرا فخر الدین را بزهر کشت") and called "ملک جلال الدین فیروز که عارض ممالک بود از سامانه". He was struck by "مرض لقوه و فالج". The amirs raised his young son "کیومرث" as "سلطان شمس الدین". Jalal al-Din later imprisoned the child. A man whose father the sultan had had killed was sent to Kilokhri, where he kicked the dying sultan and threw him "در دریائے جمنا".
- Totals given: "مدت سلطنت سه سال و سه ماه". From "سلطان شهاب الدین غوری" to Kaiqubad, "یازده تن از نسل سلطان غوری مدت یکصد و یک سال و یازده ماه و هفت روز سلطنت کردند" (p. 375).

**Jalal al-Din Firuz Khalji (pp. 372–375)**
- He had been "عارض ممالک … که بزبان عرف بخشی گویند" with the title "شایسته خانی". He became ruler "در سنه ۶۸۹[OCR?]" (p. 375).
- The city's notables were not for him, so he stayed at "کیلوکهری" and began a new city and a stone fort there. Later the townsmen came to swear allegiance and asked him into Delhi (p. 375).
- His decorated entry "از کیلوکهرے[OCR?] روانه بشهر دهلی" (p. 374). He prayed two rak'as and sat on the throne, saying "سالها پیش این تخت سجده کرده ایم" (p. 374). Lamps were lit on a thousand boats "در آب جمنا", with fireworks (p. 373).
- Malik Chhajju[OCR?], "برادرزاده سلطان غیاث الدین بلبن", held "ولایت کوره". In "سال دویم" he struck coin and had the khutba read in his own name and marched on Delhi. He was defeated by "خانخانان پسر خورد" and pardoned, as were Balban's amirs (p. 373); for Multan, see above.
- The sultan said: "هفتاد سال گذشته خون مسلمانان نریخته" (p. 373). He refused to kill Balban's men, having served Balban (p. 372). He let "هزار نفر دزد وقطاع الطریق" go on oath (p. 372). He abandoned the siege of "قلعه رنتهنبور[OCR?]", saying it was not worth one man's death (p. 372).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Offices.** "عارض ممالک" is glossed "بزبان عرف بخشی" (p. 375), and "امیر الامرا" (pp. 375–380). Under Jalal al-Din every amir got "مواجب وجاگیر", and once a jagir was fixed it was never changed, even for a man at fault (p. 373).
- **The treasury as the basis of rule.** "خزانه که اساس سلطنت و قوت بازوے خلافت است" (p. 380). Bughra Khan's advice was that a king without enough treasure to help his army or his subjects in famine "او را بادشاه نتوان گفت" (p. 376).
- **Accession largesse.** Gold and silver were scattered along the route into Delhi (p. 374).
- **Punishment and parading** ("تشهیر") (pp. 378–379).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author on Kaiqubad's decline.** He gives a maxim "فرموده بزرگان": when a man's luck turns, "نخستین عقل او تیره گردد" (p. 375).
- **The author on punishment.** Wise rulers count sending "شرارت انگیزان مردم آزار" to death as "صلاح ملکی" (p. 379).
- **Sources set against each other.** The author tells the meeting of father and son as one of love. He then notes that Amir Khusrau's "قران السعدین" shows the father came to conquer Delhi, and the son set out to repel him (p. 376).
- **Jalal al-Din's clemency** is praised throughout, with verse (pp. 372–373).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 382 down to p. 372.
  - It opens mid-sentence at "شیرین ادا از نغمه خوشش" (p. 382), continuing from p. 383.
  - It ends at p. 372 with a verse on clemency, presumably continued on p. 371.
- The joins 382→381, 380→379, 379→378, 378→377, 377→376, 376→375, 375→374, 374→373 and 373→372 read through.
- The join 381→380 crosses "[illegible] می" (p. 381) into "و وسمه ابروے شاهد بیغمی" (p. 380), which is plausible as part of the praise of wine but not certain.
- The sultan of p. 372 is not named on that page. He is read as Jalal al-Din Firuz from the run of the section.

**Multan: who was summoned and killed (p. 380)**
- "باغوائے کیخسرو عم خود را از ملتان طلبداشت" is ambiguous as printed. It can be read as "at [someone's] instigation he summoned Kaikhusrau, his kinsman ('عم' as printed), from Multan". The instigator is not named in the clause; Fakhr al-Din is named just before.
- The victim is then only "آن بے گناه". This is not resolved, and no identification is added.

**Place-level attachments (weigh accordingly)**

- **Lahore (35 rows).** This is place-level, from "نواحی لاهور" (the environs), not the city. The book describes the devastation of the region, so it is more than a bare "marched via".
