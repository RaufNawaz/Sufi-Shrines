# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 045 — pp. 553–565

This chunk takes the book's reading order from the end of its general account of Hindustan into the first suba of its geography. In this back-to-front scan that order runs from p. 565 down to p. 553. Pp. 565–562 finish the account of the people of Hindustan. They cover a sect whose ascetics ("سرپوره") the Brahmins shun, the four ashrams and four varnas, the honesty of the sarrafs with their "هنڈی" (hundi) and "بیما" (bima), the soldiers, jauhar and sati, and the beauty of the land's people. The account closes with totals for all Hindustan in subas, sarkars, mahals and dams (pp. 561–562). On p. 561 the heading "صوبه دار الخلافت شاهجهان آباد" opens suba Delhi. It covers the succession of capitals from Hastinapur and Indrapat to Shahjahanabad (pp. 560–561), and the new city's fort, canal, wall, residents, bazaars, Jama Masjid and hammam (pp. 557–560). Next come the tombs around the city: Humayun, Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki and Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya, with a passage on Abdul Qadir Gilani (pp. 555–556). After them come saints' tombs in the suba's towns (Panipat, Sirhind, Sadhaura, Sunam, Hansi) and then, by the book's own announced turn, Hindu sites: Bhaya Devi / Panjur[OCR?], Thanesar–Kurukshetra, Sambhal, Nanak Mata and Kumaon (pp. 553–555). The chunk breaks off on p. 553 in the first of the suba's two rivers, the Yamuna. The heading "درویشان هند" does not appear in this chunk. Archive material is person-level only (Farid Ganjshakar, Bahauddin Zakariya, Qutbuddin Aibak, Baba Nanak), plus one bare mention of Multan. There is nothing on suba Lahore, Multan or Thatta, and no province-level material.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** (the book's "شیخ فرید الدین گنجشکر"):
  - In the notice of Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya: "در بست سالگی بقصبه اجودهن رسیده از شیخ فرید الدین گنجشکر ارادت گرفت". At twenty Nizamuddin reached the qasba of Ajodhan and became Farid's disciple. Having obtained "کلید گنجینه معنوی" (the key of the spiritual treasure), he was given leave ("رخصت") for Delhi to guide people (p. 556).
  - Among the saints' tombs of suba Delhi: "در هانسی که معموره پاستانی است مزار شیخ جمال الدین خلیفه شیخ فرید الدین گنجشکر". At Hansi, an ancient town, is the tomb of Shaikh Jamal al-Din, Farid's khalifa (p. 554).
  - The chunk says nothing of Farid's own tomb, dates or descendants, and does not name Pakpattan.

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (the book's "شیخ بهار الدین ذکریا", printed so):
  - In the notice of Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki: after Baghdad, "بملتان وارد شده شیخ بهار الدین ذکریا را دریافت". He came to Multan and met Shaikh Baha[r] al-Din Zakariya. He then came to Delhi in the reign of Sultan Shams al-Din Iltutmish (p. 556). Nothing is said of Zakariya's shrine.

- **tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak** (the book's "سلطان قطب الدین ایبک"):
  - In the succession of Delhi's forts and cities: "سلطان قطب الدین ایبک و سلطان شمس الدین التمش در قلعه رائے پتهورا می بودند". He and Iltutmish lived in Rai Pithora's fort (p. 561). Nothing is said of his tomb.

- **gurdwara-babay-de-ber**, **gurdwara-balila-sahib-bal-lila-sahib**, **gurdwara-chakki-sahib**, **gurdwara-choa-sahib**, **gurdwara-darbar-sahib-kartarpur**, **gurdwara-khoohi-bhai-lalo-bhai-lalo-di-khooi**, **gurdwara-malji-sahib**, **gurdwara-panja-sahib**, **gurdwara-patti-sahib**, **gurdwara-pehli-patshahi-jind-pir-sukkur**, **gurdwara-rori-sahib**, **gurdwara-sach-khand-sahib**, **gurdwara-sacha-sauda**, **gurdwara-sahib-saidpur-guru-nanak-dev-ji**, **gurdwara-sri-kiara-sahib**, **gurdwara-sri-tilganji-sahib**, **gurdwara-tambo-sahib**, **gurudwara-janam-asthan-nankana-sahib** (the book's "بابا نانک"):
  - Forty kos north of the capital is Sambhal, an ancient town with "هرمندر", an old temple. "و پیوست ان نانک متا جاے است که مریدان و معتقدان بابا نانک هجوم نموده نیایش بجا آورند": adjoining it is Nanak Mata, a place where Baba Nanak's disciples and devotees throng and worship (p. 553). The chunk has nothing else on him.

- **prahladpuri-temple**, **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya**, **shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani**, **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-ali-akbar-shah-ali-akbar-shamsi**, **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**, **shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari**, **shrine-of-shah-yusaf-gardez**, **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak** (the book's "ملتان"):
  - Named once, as the place where Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki, coming from Baghdad, met Shaikh Baha[r] al-Din Zakariya (p. 556). Nothing else is said of the city.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Delhi's successive capitals** (pp. 560–561):
  - Hastinapur on the Ganges, then the Pandavas' move to Indrapat on the Jamna.
  - Raja Anangpal Tonwar founds Delhi "درسنه چهار صد و چهل بکرماجیت". Rai Pithora's fort and city follow, "درسنه یکهزار و دو صد و کسرے بکرماجیت".
  - Balban's Marzghan "۶۶۶". Kaiqubad's Kilokhri "۶۸۶", praised by Amir Khusrau in "قران السعدین".
  - Kushk Lal (Jalal al-Din Khalji) and Kushk Siri (Ala al-Din).
  - Tughlaqabad "۷۲۵", and the thousand-pillared hall of "سلطان محمد فخر الدین جونا".
  - Firuzabad "۷۵۵[OCR?]", with the "لاٹهه فیروزشاه". Mubarakabad.
  - Humayun rebuilds Indrapat as Din-panah "۹۳۹" (editor's footnote variant: "سنه ۹۴۰[OCR?] اکبرنامه جلد اول صفحه ۱۲۴").
  - Sher Shah's new city, and Salim Shah's Salimgarh "۹۵۳" in the Jamna.
  - Shahjahanabad, "درسنه ۱۰۴۸[OCR?] هجری مطابق سنه دوازدهم جلوس" of Shah Jahan.
- **Shahjahanabad** (pp. 557–560):
  - A red-stone fort with a moat, and a canal cut from the Sirmur hills (p. 560).
  - A wall of stone and lime, and residents from many lands, from Rum to Kashmir (p. 559).
  - Bazaars (pp. 558–559).
  - The Jama Masjid, "درسنه هزار و شصت هجری مطابق سال بست و چهارم شاهجهانی", of red stone (p. 558). The royal hammam (pp. 557–558).
  - The author compares the city favourably with Istanbul, Qazvin and Isfahan (p. 557).
- **Humayun's tomb**, at "کیلوکهری کیقباد" on the Jamna. The tombs of amirs and scholars form "شهریست جداگانه" (p. 556).
- **Khwaja Qutbuddin Muhammad Bakhtiyar Kaki** ("بن خواجه کمال الدین احمد موسی"):
  - His resting place is two or three kos from the city (editor's footnote variant "خانقاه[OCR?]" for "خوابگاه") (p. 556).
  - He was a native of Farghana. He received khilafat from Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti in a dream at eighteen, then went to Baghdad and Multan (above) (p. 556).
  - He reached Delhi under Iltutmish, where Muinuddin came from Ajmer to meet him. He died "چهاردهم ربیع الاول سنه ۶۳۳ هجری" (p. 556).
- **Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya** ("عرف محمد پور احمد دانیال"):
  - His tomb is nearby. He was born "در خطه غزنین سنه ۶۳۳ هجری", studied at Badaun and was called "نظام محفل شکن" (p. 556).
  - His khalifas, by place (pp. 555–556):
    - Delhi: Nasiruddin Mahmud Chiragh Dehlavi and Amir Khusrau.
    - Bengal: Ala al-Haq Akhi Siraj.
    - Chanderi: Wajihuddin Yusuf.
    - Malwa: Shaikh Yaqub and Shaikh Kamal.
    - Dhar: Maulana Ghiyasuddin.
    - Ujjain: Maulana Mughis.
    - "در گجرات": Shaikh Husamuddin.
    - Deccan: Shaikh Burhanuddin Gharib and Khwaja Hasan.
  - He died "چاشتگاه چهارشنبه هزدهم ربیع الثانی سنه ۷۲۵[OCR?] هجری" (p. 555).
- **Abdul Qadir Gilani** ("پیر پیران حضرت میران محی الدین عبد القادر گیلانی") (p. 555):
  - The book says "سلسله علیه این بار یافتگان" reaches him.
  - He was a Husaini sayyid, four links from Shaikh Shibli, from the village of Jil near Baghdad.
  - He was born "چهار صد و هفتاد و یک هجری" and took the khirqa from Shaikh Abu Sa'id Mubarak.
  - He died "درسنه پانصد و شصت و یک هجری در نود سالگی".
- **Shaikh Sharaf Abu Ali Qalandar**, at Panipat, 30 kos from Shahjahanabad (p. 555):
  - He came to Delhi at forty, "بزیارت خواجه قطب الدین محمد بختیار", and studied for twenty years.
  - He threw his books into the Jamna and went to Rum, where he met "شمس الدین تبریزی و مولانائے رومی که مثنوی معنوی از وست".
  - He returned and died at Panipat. His tomb is a place of ziyarat.
- **Sirhind** (pp. 554–555):
  - It was one of the dependencies of Samana. Firuz Shah made it a separate pargana "سنه هفتصد و شصت هجری" (p. 555).
  - Shaikh Farid al-Din Thani ("شیخ فرید الدین ثانی") and Shaikh Muhammad Ma'sum Kabuli, who guided people in Shah Jahan's time, are buried there (p. 554).
- **Other saints' tombs in the suba** (p. 554):
  - Shah Qamis at Sadhaura, and Shaikh Nabawi at Sunam.
  - Shaikh Jamal al-Din at Hansi (see Farid above).
  - "درین صوبه مزارات اولیائے کرام انقدر است که بشمار درنیاید".
- **Hindu sites** (pp. 553–554):
  - Bhaya Devi ("بهیا دیوی") near "گهات بهوانه", 20 kos from Sirhind, renamed Panjur[OCR?] (p. 554).
  - Thanesar, 30 kos south of Sirhind, with the Kurukshetra tank. Its holy places along the "سرستی" are reckoned at 48 large kos, and the Pandavas and Kauravas fought there (pp. 553–554).
  - Sambhal's "هرمندر" and the Kumaon hills (p. 553).
- **The Yamuna** (p. 553): it rises beyond Tibet. The raja of Sirmur sends snow by boat as tribute and is called "برفی راجه". Shah Jahan's palace at "مخلص پور" stands on the river, and the "شاه نهر" was brought from there to the capital.
- **The general account of Hindustan** (pp. 562–565):
  - A sect whose ascetics ("سرپوره") pluck out their hair and do not bathe; the Brahmins reject them (p. 565).
  - The four ashrams and four varnas (p. 565).
  - The soldiers (pp. 563–564), jauhar among the refractory hill people, sati, and widows' abstinence and refusal to remarry (p. 563).
  - Foreigners settle and "همه هندوستانی میشوند" (p. 562).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Custodianship at Sirhind:** "اکنون از اولاد ایشان سجاده نشین هستند". The descendants of Farid al-Din Thani and Muhammad Ma'sum Kabuli are now sajjada nashins (p. 554).
- **Pilgrimage:**
  - Shaikh Nabawi's tomb at Sunam is "زیارت گاه خلایق" (p. 554). Abu Ali Qalandar's tomb is "زیارت گاه اهل روزگار" (p. 555).
  - At Nanak Mata, Baba Nanak's devotees "هجوم نموده نیایش بجا آورند" (p. 553).
  - At Kurukshetra, crowds come on days of solar and lunar eclipse and give alms openly and in secret. Even the miserly give beyond their means (p. 554).
- **State intervention at a Hindu sacred site** (p. 554):
  - In "سنه چهارم عالم گیری", Fidai Khan Koka took Bhaya Devi as his home and named it "پنجور[OCR?]". By royal order he expelled its hereditary raja ("اباً عن جد ریاست داشت").
  - He laid out a five-terraced garden with a canal and fountains.
  - The author saw "چهل من گل گلاب بوزن عالم گیری" brought to the rose-water house in a single spring day.
- **Building dates in Delhi:** Jama Masjid 1060 AH = 24th Shahjahani year (p. 558). Shahjahanabad "۱۰۴۸[OCR?]" (p. 560). The other dates are in the "Delhi's successive capitals" bullet above (pp. 560–561).
- **Banking:**
  - Sarrafs return deposits without witnesses.
  - They issue a paper "بخط هندی" payable by their agents up to two hundred farsangs away, called "هنڈی". It can be sold on.
  - For a fee they carry merchants' goods safely, which is called "بیما" (p. 564).
- **Totals of the empire:**
  - "تمام هندوستان معه صوبه بنگاله و دکهن و قندهار که تفصیل ان درین نسخه اندراج نیافته مشتمل بر بست صوبه و یکصد و نود و دو سرکار و چهار هزار و یکصد و پنجاه و دو محال" (p. 562).
  - Revenue: "هشت" (end of p. 562) + "ارب و شصت و هشت کرور و بست و شش لک و هشتاد هزار و پانصد و هفتاد(۱) و سه دام" (p. 561). Editor's footnote variant: "هفتاد دام".
- **Kumaon:**
  - Mines of gold, silver, copper, lead, iron, arsenic and verdigris.
  - Musk deer, the "گاو قطاس", silkworms, falcons, "اسپ گوت" and white honey.
  - Its zamindars are refractory (p. 553).
- **Delhi's bazaar:** it can furnish the royal workshops in one day and equip "صد هزار سپاه" in an hour (p. 559).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Khizr passed by the young Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki. At eighteen he received khilafat "در عالم خواب" from Muinuddin Chishti, who later came from Ajmer to Delhi "بالهام ربانی" to meet him (p. 556).
- Abu Ali Qalandar, when "جذبه الهی" came, threw all his books into the Jamna. "بسا خارق عادات ایشان یادگار" (p. 555).
- Abdul Qadir Gilani's "خوارق عادات و بوارق کرامات" filled the world (p. 555).
- "گویند": the last manifestation of divine light will appear from Sambhal's Har Mandir (p. 553).
- Kurukshetra is called "ناف زمین" in Hindu books, the place where creation began (p. 554).
- "گویند": stones in Tibet with the properties of "پارس" turn the iron shoes of grazing animals to gold, and its ruler's vessels and drums are all of gold (p. 553).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's own observations:**
  - He states he visited the Panjur[OCR?] garden himself in spring (p. 554).
  - He dates his work from Abdul Qadir Gilani's death: "از رحلت ایشان لغایت این نسخه پانصد و پنجاه سال منقضی میشود" (p. 555). Not computed.
  - He announces his order: having finished "امکنه شریفه اولیائے مسلمین", he turns to "اماکن هنود" (p. 554).
  - He twice says the tombs of saints in the suba and around Delhi are past counting (pp. 554, 556).
- **His claims about Delhi and Hindustan:**
  - Abroad, "دهلی" was the known name of the capital. With Shahjahanabad, all the older sultans' cities took its name, as rivers entering the Ganges take the name "گنگ" (p. 560).
  - On Delhi's residents: "اردو گفتار که اصل زبان هندوستان از همین جاست" (p. 559).
  - No city on earth matches Shahjahanabad, and Istanbul is not a tenth of a tenth of it (p. 557).
- **The Brahmins' view of the "سرپوره":** the book attributes to the Brahmins the saying that one should walk towards a rampaging elephant rather than towards a "سرپوره" (p. 565).

## Doubts

- **Zakariya's name is printed "بهار الدین".** "شیخ بهار الدین ذکریا" is printed with "بهار" and carries no transcriber flag. I read it as Baha al-Din. The mapping rests on "ذکریا" together with "ملتان" in the same clause (p. 556). It is person-level only; nothing is said of the shrine.
- **Farid's mapping is person-level, not via Ajodhan.** The book places Farid at "قصبه اجودهن" as a living shaikh (p. 556). It does not equate Ajodhan with Pakpattan in this chunk and does not name his tomb, so Ajodhan is not treated as a Pakpattan toponym; the row is taken by the person rule. The Hansi tomb (p. 554) is his khalifa's, not his, and Hansi has no row in `toponym_map.txt`.
- **"شیخ فرید الدین ثانی" at Sirhind (p. 554) is not mapped.** The book names a different, later saint, active "در زمان حضرت شاهجهان".
- **Guru Nanak's 18 rows are a person-level attachment.** The many-row rule is written for passages that name no site. Here the book names a site, Nanak Mata near Sambhal, that is none of the 18 rows; the book places it 40 kos north of Delhi. I mapped all 18 because of the person rule and the coordinator's instruction for Delhi-context mentions, and because the fact (devotees' worship) concerns his following, not a Pakistani site. The consolidator may prefer to treat it as an "Other sites" item. Recorded here rather than resolved.
- **The Multan bullet is place-level.** It rests on one bare toponym inside Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki's itinerary (p. 556). Weigh accordingly.
- **tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak is person-level only.** Aibak is a bare name in the Delhi fort sequence (p. 561). Nothing concerns the Lahore tomb.
- **"شمس الدین تبریزی" (p. 555) is not mapped.** The book places him in Rum with Maulana Rumi, not at Multan, so he is not mapped to shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari. This touches the held "Shams" (Multan) identity question and is left for the consolidator. "سلطان شمس الدین التمش" (pp. 556, 561) is the sultan and is not mapped.
- **"در گجرات" (p. 556), Shaikh Husamuddin's seat, is read as suba Gujarat (India).** It stands in a list with Bengal, Chanderi, Malwa, Dhar, Ujjain and the Deccan, so it is not mapped to Gujrat / Shah Daula.
- **Kashmir and Kabul are not mapped.** They appear only in the list of lands Delhi's residents come from (p. 559). Sharada is not named.
- **The referent of "سلسله علیه این بار یافتگان" (p. 555) is not explicit.** The sentence follows directly on Nizamuddin's death and says that silsila reaches Abdul Qadir Gilani. Recorded as printed, not interpreted.
- **The same year, "۶۳۳", is printed twice on p. 556.** It is given both for Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki's death and for Nizamuddin's birth "در خطه غزنین". Neither is flagged. Carried as printed; check against the lithograph.
- **The scope of "تفصیل ان درین نسخه اندراج نیافته" (p. 562) is unclear.** It is not clear whether it covers Bengal, the Deccan and Qandahar or only Qandahar. Recorded as printed.
- **The heading "درویشان هند" is not in this chunk.** P. 565 opens mid-passage on a sect rejected by the Brahmins; its start is on a higher page, outside this chunk. The suba Delhi section continues below p. 553 (mid-sentence: "براورده بدار الخلافه"), so its sarkars and revenue, if given, are outside this chunk.

  - `[illegible]` at the end of p. 554 and in a footnote on p. 563.
