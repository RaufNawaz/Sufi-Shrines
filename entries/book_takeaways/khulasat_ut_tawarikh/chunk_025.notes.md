# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 025 — pp. 311–322

The chunk reads from p. 322 down to p. 311. It opens mid-story at the end of the Sayyid sultans. Husam Khan takes the city and shames the sultan's household, Hamid Khan summons Malik Bahlul, and Sultan Ala al-Din withdraws to Badaun and dies there. The page closes with a reign total for the four rulers from Khizr Khan (p. 322). The section "سلطان بهلول لودی افغان المخاطب بخان خانان" (pp. 316–322) covers Bahlul's whole career:
- his descent from Malik Bahram, who came to Multan under Firuz Shah;
- his birth after his father's death and his upbringing by his uncle Islam Khan at Sirhind;
- the dervish of Samana who "sold" him the kingship of Delhi;
- his rise, his taking of Delhi from Hamid Khan and his wars with the sultans of Jaunpur;
- his death.

"سلطان سکندر معروف بشاهزاده نظام خان بن سلطان بهلول" (pp. 313–316) covers Sikandar's reign:
- the disputed succession;
- the conquest of Jaunpur, with a digression on the Sharqi sultans;
- his religious policy toward Hindus (temples, Mathura, Thanesar, a cloth badge, jizya, a brahman put to death);
- his government and his death.

"سلطان ابراهیم بن سلطان سکندر بن سلطان بهلول" (pp. 311–313) covers Ibrahim's reign:
- the jewelled throne;
- Prince Jalal Khan's revolt and death;
- the killing of Mian Bhuwa, with the story of the "Moth" mosque at Delhi;
- the revolts of Islam Khan, Bahar Khan[OCR?] and Daulat Khan Lodi.

The chunk breaks off mid-sentence as Daulat Khan turns "از لاهور پیش حضرت ظهیر الدین" (p. 311).

In the reign of Sultan Firuz Shah, after a quarrel with his brother, he "در ملتان آمده نوکر ملک مروان حاکم آنجا که امرائے فیروزشاهی بود گردید". That is, he came to Multan and entered the service of its governor, Malik Marwan (as printed "ملک مروان"), one of Firuz Shah's amirs (p. 322).
  - **His five sons in Multan.** He had five sons: "ملک شه[OCR?] و ملک کالا و ملک فیروز و ملک محمد و ملک خواجه". "این هر پنج برادر بعد رحلت پدر در ملتان سکونت داشتند": all five lived in Multan after their father's death (p. 322). Their later careers are under Other.
  - **Bahlul sets out for Multan.** When Sultan Mahmud of Jaunpur besieged Delhi, "سلطان بجانب ملتان راهی شده بود". On hearing the news he turned back "از دیپالپور" toward the capital (p. 318). This is a bare "set out toward"; see Doubts.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**The dervish of Samana (a saint inside the history)**
- **The dervish and his name.** Malik Bahlul came with two companions ("دو رفیق[OCR?]") to "سامانه". There lived "سید[OCR?] ابن[OCR?] نام درویشے صاحب حال و قال از نزدیکان درگاه ایزد متعال" (p. 321). The editor's footnote gives the variant "بندرابن[OCR?]" for the name (p. 321).
- **How the book describes him.** It praises him at length. The lights of divine knowledge showed on his brow, and his white beard bore witness to his purity. Of his ash-smeared body it says "از تن خاکستر الودش[OCR?] نور معنی بسان شمع از پرده فانوس جلوه گر گردیده". A short poem follows on his illumined heart and his inward watching ("مراقب") (p. 321).
- **The sale of the kingship.** The prophecy and the "sale" are given under Legends (pp. 320–321).
- **Bahlul's belief.** Bahlul kept the dervish's "اشارت" in mind "از صغر سن" (p. 320). Later, having taken the title of sultan at Sirhind, "از بشارت درویش میدانست" that the kingship would come to him (p. 320). The verse that follows calls the dervish "مخدوم عالم پناه" and "آن شه": "بشارات مخدوم عالم پناه   مرا منتظر دارد این جایگاه[OCR?] / مرا داد آن شه بشاهی نوید" (p. 320).

**The Lodi family before Bahlul's rise (continuing the Multan material)**
- **Malik Shah[OCR?] (Islam Khan).** He was the eldest of Malik Bahram's sons. He became the servant of Khizr Khan, and in the battle against Iqbal Khan "تردد نمایاں کرده اقبال خان را کشت". He received "خطاب اسلام خانی" and in the end the governorship of "سهرند[OCR?]" (pp. 321–322).
- **Malik Kala, Bahlul's father.** He governed "دوراله[OCR?] تابع سهرند" for his brother Islam Khan. He was killed fighting "افغانان نیازی" while Bahlul was still in the womb (p. 321).
- **Bahlul's upbringing.** At one month old he was brought to Islam Khan at Sirhind. Islam Khan gave him to a nurse and named him Bahlul. "افغانان از روے حقارت او را بابو[OCR?] گفتندے". Islam Khan later adopted him and married him to his own daughter (p. 321).
- **Bahlul succeeds his uncle.** In Sultan Mubarak Shah's reign "اسلام خان عرف ملک شه[OCR?] در جنگ شیخ علی کابلی[OCR?] درجه شهادت یافت", and Malik Bahlul took his uncle's place. He rose to "امیر الامرا" (p. 320).
- **Bahlul under Muhammad Shah.** Sultan Muhammad Shah gave him the title "خانخانان" and called him his son. Bahlul later fell out with him (p. 320). The chunk's heading for him reads "المخاطب بخان خانان" (p. 322).

**The end of the Sayyid sultans and Bahlul's rise (Delhi, Badaun; no rows)**
- **Husam Khan.** He took the city, put the sultan's women out "سر برهنه" and seized the treasures. The sultan "بهانه برسات نموده از انتقام اغماض کرد" (p. 322).
- **Bahlul summoned to Delhi.** Hamid Khan summoned Malik Bahlul to be sultan, in name, and Bahlul came "از دیپالپور" (p. 322). Ala al-Din wrote that Bahlul's father had called him son, and that he was content with "بیک پرگنه بداؤن" (p. 322). (On pp. 318–319 the same story is told differently; see Doubts.)
- **Ala al-Din's death and the reign totals.** Ala al-Din died at Badaun "باجل طبیعی". His reign, "که محض برائے اسم بود", lasted "هشت سال و سه ماه", and his rule of Badaun "بست و هشت سال". From "حضرخان" to Ala al-Din, "چهار تن مدت سی و نه سال و هفت ماه و شانزده روز" ruled (p. 322).
- **Battle at Garha[OCR?].** Qutb Khan, son of Islam Khan and Bahlul's cousin, joined Husam Khan against Bahlul. They fought "در موضع گده[OCR?] توابع[OCR?] بوریه[OCR?] یسا[OCR?] و مهوره[OCR?]", and Bahlul won (p. 320).
- **Bahlul's terms to Muhammad Shah.** Bahlul demanded that Husam Khan be killed and Hamid Khan made vizier. The sultan removed Husam Khan and made Hamid Khan vizier (p. 320).
- **Bahlul declares himself sultan.** He besieged Delhi and failed, went back to Sirhind and "خود را سلطان بهلول خطاب نموده" (p. 320).
- **Muhammad Shah's death and Ala al-Din's retreat.** Muhammad Shah died and Ala al-Din took the throne. Under Ala al-Din "طوایف الملوک" arose, and he settled at Badaun (p. 319).
- **Hamid Khan in Delhi (p. 319 version).** Hamid Khan escaped from Badaun, shamed the sultan's women, and put them out "سر برهنه از حصار دهلی". He then summoned Sultan Bahlul "از سهرند" and handed him the keys of the fort (p. 319).
- **The Afghans' antics at Hamid Khan's gatherings** (pp. 318–319):
  - They tied their shoes to their waists, set shoes on the niche above Hamid Khan's head "ملاحظه دزد[OCR?]", asked for a carpet to make caps for their children, licked the lime and ate the flowers and pan.
  - Bahlul explained them as "مردم روستائی و کوه نشین و بیقید".
  - Next day the Afghans forced their way in, and "قطب خان بنی[OCR?] عم سلطان" laid a chain before Hamid Khan. Hamid Khan was imprisoned.
  - Bahlul took Delhi "با خزاین و دفاین و کارخانجات", and "در سنه ۸۵۵[OCR?] سکه و خطبه بنام خود کرده" (p. 318).
- **Sultan Mahmud of Jaunpur.**
  - Some nobles invited him. He besieged Delhi and was beaten at "نزیله[OCR?]" (p. 318).
  - The second time a peace divided the lands: what Sultan Mubarak Shah had held went to Bahlul, and what Sultan Ibrahim of Jaunpur had held went to Mahmud (p. 318).
- **Sultan Husain of Jaunpur.**
  - Urged on by his wife "ملکه جهان بنت سلطان علاء الدین", he marched on Delhi with "یک لک سوار و چهل هزار پیاده و چهار صد فیل" (p. 317).
  - Bahlul won with "پانزده هزار سوار" and sent the captured queen back with honour (p. 317).
  - Seven more wars followed. Husain fled toward "قنوج و پتنه", and Bahlul took Jaunpur and gave it to "مبارک خان لوحانی" (p. 317).
- **Badaun.**
  - Ala al-Din died at Badaun "از تاریخ بودن انجا بعد بست سال" (p. 317). Compare p. 322; see Doubts.
  - Bahlul went from Etawa to Badaun to condole, then took Badaun "از پسران سلطان مرحوم" (pp. 316–317).

**Bahlul's death and Sikandar's accession**
- **Bahlul's death.** He died "در موضع ملاوقی[OCR?] از اعمال سکیت[OCR?] بمرگ طبعی". The chronogram verse reads "بهشتصد و نود و هشت[OCR?] رفت از عالم   خدیو ملکستان و جهان کشا بهلول". His reign: "سی و هشت سال و هشت ماه و هشت روز" (p. 316).
- **The disputed succession.** Some amirs wanted "شاهزاده باربک", and some "اعظم همایون نبیره سلطان". Sikandar's mother was "زرگر زاده".
- **The quarrel.** "عیسے خان بودے[OCR?]" ("بودے" printed unflagged) insulted her. "خانخانان فرملی[OCR?]" answered him, then led the amirs in enthroning Nizam Khan as Sultan Sikandar "در سنه ۸۹۴[OCR?] بقصبه جلالی". The editor's footnote reads "سنه ۸۹۴[OCR?] ھ ۔ فرشتہ جلد اول صفحہ ۱۸۲[OCR?]" (p. 316).

**Sikandar and Jaunpur; the Sharqi digression**
- Sikandar defeated his elder brother Barbak Shah at Jaunpur and left him in place there. He then fought Sultan Husain of Jaunpur repeatedly (p. 315).
- **The Sharqi digression, part 1.** Sultan Muhammad Shah bin Firuz Shah gave "ملک سرور خواجه سرا را که خطاب خواجه جهانی داشت" the title "سلطان الشرق" and Jaunpur in jagir (p. 315).
- **Part 2.** The Sultan al-Sharq took "کول و اتاوه و کنسپله[OCR?] و بهرایچ و رایپری[OCR?] و بهار و ترهت". "در سنه ۷۹۶[OCR?]" he struck coin and khutba in his own name. He ruled "شانزده سال" (p. 315).
- **Part 3.** The book then lists his successors with their reigns: Mubarak Shah, Ibrahim Sharqi, Mahmud, Muhammad Shah and Husain (p. 315).
- **Annexation.** Sikandar took the country from Husain. The lands "که از تصرف[OCR?] سلاطین دهلی بدر رفته بود بعد یکصد و دو سال ضمیمه ولایت دهلی گردید" (p. 315).

**Ibrahim's reign**
- **The throne and the accession.** The jewelled throne is described (p. 313). The marginal date reads "۹۲۵[OCR?]", and the text "در [illegible] سلطان بر آن تخت جلوس نموده" (p. 313).
- **Jalal Khan's revolt.**
  - Some amirs set up "شاهزاده جلال خان". It was first agreed that he would rule at Jaunpur (pp. 312–313).
  - He refused to return when recalled and struck coin as "سلطان جلال الدین". He allied with "اعظم همایون شروانی[OCR?]", who then deserted him (p. 312).
- **Jalal Khan's flight and death.**
  - He fled to "راجه بکرماجیت ولد راجه مان حاکم گوالیار", then to Malwa and "ولایت گوندوانه" (p. 312).
  - He was handed over and sent toward "قلعه هانسی", and was killed on the way "از روۓ بیرحمی" (p. 312).
- **Mian Bhuwa ("میان بهویه").** The book calls him "از سادات[OCR?] عظام و وزراۓ کرام". He was imprisoned without fault and killed "ناحق". The editor's footnote: Firishta writes "بہورا[OCR?]" ("[illegible] بموافقہ[OCR?] تاریخ فرشتہ جلد اول صفحہ ۱۹۱[OCR?]") (p. 311).
- **His mosque at Delhi.** The anecdote is under Legends. Its end: "آن مسجد باسم موتهه[OCR?] موسوم گشت و تا حال در دهلی قائم است و بهمان نام شهرت دارد" (p. 311).
- **Azam Humayun and Islam Khan.**
  - "اعظم همایون شروانی[OCR?]" was recalled from the siege of Gwalior to Agra and imprisoned, and he died in prison (p. 311).
  - His son "اسلام خان ... حاکم مانکپور" rose with "چهل هزار سوار و پانصد زنجیر فیل" and was killed in battle (p. 311).
- **Bahar Khan[OCR?] in Bihar.**
  - "بهار خان[OCR?] پسر دریا خان" rebelled in Bihar with nearly one lakh horse, as far as "ولایت سنبهل". He took the style "سلطان محمد" (p. 311).
  - Editor's footnote variant for his name: "بہادر خان یا شہباز خان" (p. 311).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Coin and khutba as the mark of sovereignty.** They recur throughout:
  - the Sultan al-Sharq, "سنه ۷۹۶[OCR?]" (p. 315);
  - Bahlul at Delhi, "سنه ۸۵۵[OCR?]" (p. 318), and at Jaunpur (p. 317);
  - Sikandar at Jalali (p. 316) and at Jaunpur (p. 315);
  - Ibrahim (p. 313);
  - Jalal Khan (p. 312) and Bahar Khan[OCR?] (p. 311).
- **Grants.**
  - Jaunpur and its region given in jagir to Malik Sarwar (p. 315).
  - Jaunpur given to Mubarak Khan Lohani (p. 317).
  - Ala al-Din kept only "بیک پرگنه بداؤن" (p. 322).
- **Sikandar's religious policy: temples and bathing.**
  - "از اکثر اماکن بت خانها منهدم ساخته مساجد و مدارس تعمیر نمود" (p. 315).
  - At Mathura and elsewhere he forbade Hindus to bathe, and allowed no one "برسم کفر سر و ریش تواند تراشید" (p. 315).
  - He wished to demolish the temples of Thanesar. The ulama ruled "انداختن بتخانهاۓ قدیم جایز نیست", but that the bathing and the crowds ("ازدحام[OCR?]") could be stopped. He accused them of taking the infidels' side (pp. 314–315).
- **Sikandar's religious policy: badge, books and jizya.**
  - Hindus had to sew a piece of blue cloth ("پارچه نیله") near the shoulder of their garment. Hindu books found anywhere were burned or thrown into water (p. 314).
  - Jizya "بقرار اعلیٰ" was taken from any Hindu who wore a turban. Many therefore stopped wearing turbans and tied blue cloth on their heads (p. 314).
- **The brahman put to death.** A "زنار دار" was charged with having professed Islam. The sultan gathered "تمامی علما و فضلاۓ ممالک محروسه", the charge was proved, and when he refused Islam he was killed (p. 314).
- **Sikandar's endowments and charity.**
  - He gave much in alms "بمستحقان در روز عاشوره و غیره[OCR?]" (p. 314).
  - He appointed "امام و موذن و خطیب و مدرس" in mosques and madrasas, with "وظائف ... از سرکار" (p. 314).
- **Sikandar's government.**
  - Grain stayed cheap ("نرخ غلات ارزان") (p. 315).
  - He went about at night in disguise to learn how his amirs and people lived (p. 314).
  - He sent two farmans a day to commanders in the field. He kept a post ("داک[OCR?] چوکی") (p. 314).
- **Receiving a farman.** Frontier amirs went "دو سه کروه" out to meet a farman. They stood in line, took it with both hands and set it on their heads, and read it there or "در مسجد جامع بر سر منبر" (pp. 313–314).
- **Daily reports.** "واقعات ولایات و پرگنات" reached the sultan every day (p. 313).
- **Bahlul's piety.** "بیشتر اوقات بمصاحبت علما و مجالست فقرا گذرانیدے و تفقد احوال فقیران و مسکینان کردے" (p. 316).
- **Mosque building from a grain.** Mian Bhuwa built a mosque at Delhi from the proceeds of the moth grain, and it still stood in the author's day (p. 311). See Legends.
- **Army figures, as printed** (no computation):
  - Sultan Husain: "یک لک سوار و چهل هزار پیاده و چهار صد فیل" (p. 317). Bahlul: "پانزده هزار سوار" (p. 317).
  - Azam Humayun against Gwalior: "سی هزار سوار و سیصد زنجیر فیل" (p. 312).
  - Islam Khan of Manikpur: "چهل هزار سوار و پانصد زنجیر فیل" (p. 311).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The dervish of Samana sells the kingship of Delhi** (pp. 320–321).
  - The dervish looked kindly on Malik Bahlul and said "از شما کسی هست که بادشاهی دهلی بدو هزار تنکه بخرد" (p. 321).
  - Bahlul at once laid "یکهزار و سیصد تنکه نقد[OCR?]" before him. The dervish accepted it and said "سلطنت هندوستان مبارک باشد". Bahlul's companions mocked (p. 321).
  - Bahlul replied: "اگر این قضیه بوقوع خواهد یافت سودائے مفت است والا خدمت درویش خدا اندیش کردم" (p. 320).
  - The book then tells his rise as the working of the dervish's "اشارت" and "بشارت" (p. 320).
- **Bahlul's birth.** "بارادت الهی", near the time of birth the roof fell and his mother died. Since the pregnancy was past eight months, they cut her open, and Bahlul, "که رمقے از حیات داشت زنده بر آمد". The book frames this as destiny: "ازانجا که سلطنت هندوستان نصیب آن طفل بود" (p. 321).
- **Sikandar's secret knowledge.** "مردم ظن مے بردند که سلطان بجن آشناست". Others said "چراغے از طلسمات" had come into his hands: lit, it brought the world of the jinn into view, showed the state of the earth and of other kings, and fetched treasures on demand (p. 314). These are attributed to "مردم" and "بعضے".
- **Mian Bhuwa's grain** (attributed "گویند").
  - In the Jami mosque Sultan Sikandar picked up a grain of "موتہ[OCR?]" and gave it to Mian Bhuwa. Mian Bhuwa planted it in his garden and resowed it for several years (p. 311).
  - From the proceeds he built a mosque at Delhi. Sikandar praised him and raised his pay (p. 311).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On kings' fickleness.** "از تلون طبیعت بادشاهان برحذر باید بود". Kings are angered by a greeting and give robes for an insult; they may send the innocent "بشهرستان عدم" or cast down "مخلصان راسخ الاعتقاد" (p. 311). The author draws this from Ibrahim's alienation of his amirs.
- **On Ibrahim.** He was young, acted without his ministers, and jailed nobles for slight faults or none (p. 312). He killed Mian Bhuwa "ناحق", "باغواۓ بداندیشان" (p. 311). He killed his brother "از روۓ بیرحمی", and the book adds a verse against shedding a brother's blood for a kingdom (p. 312).
- **On Sikandar.** "سلطان خیلے متعصب بود" (p. 314), and he was "اول کسے[OCR?] که از سلاطین هندوستان هندوان را خوار و نزار داشته و بدعوی اسلام برهمن را کشته" (p. 314). He was also "خیلے هوشیار و صاحب دانش" (p. 314). His reign was just, with cheap grain (p. 315).
- **On Bahlul.**
  - His taking of Badaun from Ala al-Din's sons was "بیوفائے و بیمروتی", with a verse that "مروت" and "وفا" are now as rare as the anqa (p. 316).
  - Otherwise he is praised for following the sharia, for justice and for his company of ulama and fuqara (p. 316).
  - His victory with few men is put down to his being "قوی اقبال و بیدار بخت" (p. 317).
- **On Ala al-Din.** "از بے همتی انتقام نگرفت" (p. 319); "غیرت بخاطر نیاورده" (p. 322).
- **The editor's footnotes are not the author's.** On pp. 311, 313 and 316 they compare the text with "تاریخ فرشتہ جلد اول" (pp. "۱۸۲[OCR?]", "۱۸۸[OCR?]", "۱۹۱[OCR?]").

## Citable passages

- "در ملتان آمده نوکر ملک مروان حاکم آنجا که امرائے فیروزشاهی بود گردید" — "He came to Multan and became the servant of Malik Marwan, its governor, one of Firuz Shah's amirs." (p. 322)

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 322 down to p. 311, and all page joins read through.
- It opens mid-story at "حسام خان شهر را متصرف گشت" (p. 322), continuing from the previous chunk.
- It ends mid-sentence at "دولت خان لودی تخالف[OCR?] ورزیده از لاهور پیش حضرت ظهیر الدین" (p. 311), which continues in the next chunk.
- A library stamp at the foot of p. 319 ("Sri Satguru Jagjit Singh Ji eLibrary") is not text.
- Calling "حضرت ظهیر الدین" Babur rests on the brief's map of the book (Babur ~310), not on this chunk.

**Internal contradictions (recorded, not resolved)**
- **Who seized Delhi.** p. 322 has "حسام خان" take the city and put the sultan's women out bareheaded. p. 319 has "حمید خان" do this, and adds "چنانچه سابقا نوشته شد".
- **Where Bahlul came from.** p. 322 says he came to Delhi "از دیپالپور"; p. 319 says Hamid Khan summoned him "از سهرند".
- **Ala al-Din's years at Badaun.** p. 322 gives "حکومت بداوں بست و هشت سال"; p. 317 says he died there "بعد بست سال".
- **Bahlul's death and Sikandar's accession.** The chronogram verse puts Bahlul's death in "بهشتصد و نود و هشت[OCR?]" (p. 316), but Sikandar is enthroned "در سنه ۸۹۴[OCR?]" on the same page. "هشت" is flagged. No interval is computed.
- **Sikandar's reign.** The text gives "بست و شش[OCR?] سال و پنج ماه" (p. 313); the editor's footnote gives "بست و هشت[OCR?] سال و پنج ماه ۔ فرشتہ جلد اول صفحہ ۱۸۸[OCR?]".
- **Ibrahim's accession.** The margin has "۹۲۵[OCR?]"; the editor's footnote has "مربع[OCR?] ده[OCR?] ۱[OCR?] ھ ۹۲۳[OCR?]" (p. 313). The year in the text is "[illegible]".

**Traps avoided**
- "ملک سرور خواجه سرا", the Sultan al-Sharq (p. 315), is a eunuch noble of Jaunpur, not Sakhi Sarwar. "سروری" (pp. 321, 322) means "lordship". Neither is mapped to sakhi-sarwar.
- "خطاب خواجه جهانی" (p. 315) and "ملکه جهان" (p. 317) are titles. They are not Makhdoom Jahaniyan of Uch.
- "شاهزاده جلال خان" / "سلطان جلال الدین" (pp. 312–313) and "قصبه جلالی" (p. 316) have nothing to do with Jalaluddin Bukhari of Uch. No Uch row is attached.
- "ملک فیروز" and "سلطان فیروز شاه" are not Farid. "لعل بدخشان" (p. 313) is a ruby. "عنایت" (pp. 311, 314) is vocabulary.
- There are two different "اسلام خان": Malik Shah, Bahlul's uncle (pp. 320–322), and the son of Azam Humayun, governor of Manikpur (p. 311).
- "شیخ علی کابلی[OCR?]" (p. 320) is named only as the enemy in whose battle Islam Khan fell. The book gives him no saintly role.
- "میان بهویه" is called "از سادات[OCR?] عظام", but he is a vizier, not a saint, and has no row.
- No ruler in the chunk is a row's principal figure (wave-1 rule 2). This covers Bahlul, Sikandar, Ibrahim, the Sayyid and Sharqi sultans, Firuz Shah and Khizr Khan.
