# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 018 — pp. 224–235

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-section with the conquest of Rani Durgavati's country by Asaf Khan and the treasure of "قلعہ چوراگڈہ[OCR?]" (p. 235). Next comes "در بیان تعمیر قلعہ اکبر آباد" (pp. 234–235), the building of the Agra fort, begun "در سال دہم جلوس والا[OCR?] مطابق سنہ ۹۷۳[OCR?]". Then "در بیان قتل علی قلی خان و بہادر خان" (pp. 232–234): the brothers' rise, the Shaham Beg affair, their revolt, and their deaths. The longest section, "در بیان شورش میرزایان و تادیب و تخریب انہا و تسخیر ولایت گجرات" (pp. 224–231), covers the Mirzas' risings, the conquest of suba Gujarat (Ahmadabad, Cambay, Sarnal, Surat), the emperor's verdict on Ahmadabad's climate, the flight of two Mirzas into Punjab and their defeat near Multan, the nine-day forced ride to Ahmadabad, and the later wars against Sultan Muzaffar to his suicide. The chunk ends with a retrospective on the Gujarat sultans' origin under Firuz Shah and Timur's invasion, breaking off mid-sentence (p. 224). Almost all of the chunk is suba Gujarat and northern India. The only archive mapping is one place-level Multan bullet; no saint who is a row's principal figure is named.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **prahladpuri-temple**, **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya**, **shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani**, **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-ali-akbar-shah-ali-akbar-shamsi**, **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**, **shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari**, **shrine-of-shah-yusaf-gardez**, **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak** (the book's "ملتان"):
  - Khan Jahan, "حاکم پنجاب", went after Ibrahim Husain Mirza and Masud Husain Mirza and "در حوالی تهتهه[OCR?] تابع ملتان رسیده محاربه نمود". That is, he gave battle near a place printed "تهتهه[OCR?]", which the book calls subordinate to Multan (p. 228).
  - After a short fight Masud Husain Mirza was taken. Ibrahim Husain Mirza "گریخته انطرف ملتان در خانه بلوچی متواریے گشت": he fled to the far side of Multan and hid in a Baluch's house (p. 228).
  - "بلوچیان او را بدست آورده بسعید خان حاکم ملتان سپردند": the Baluchis seized him and handed him to Said Khan, governor of Multan. He died of the wound he had received "در جنگ تهتهه[OCR?]" (p. 228).
  - Nothing is said of any shrine or saint of Multan.

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE punjab.
  - Book's words: "پنجاب" and "خان جهان حاکم پنجاب" (p. 228); "بسمت پنجاب" and "از پنجاب" (p. 231).
  - Refers to: the emperor's reign as the book dates it in this chunk, between "سال سیوم[OCR?] جلوس والا" and "سال بست سویم" (pp. 226, 232). The book calls the unit "پنجاب" here, with a "حاکم", not "صوبه".
  - When the imperial standards moved toward Punjab ("چون رایات عالیات بسمت پنجاب نہضت فرمود"), the Mirzas came out of Sambhal, plundered, and besieged Delhi fort. On hearing this the emperor went "از پنجاب بسمت دہلی" (p. 231).
  - Ibrahim Husain Mirza and Masud Husain Mirza left Sambhal and "براه دیپالپور[OCR?] رو به پنجاب آوردند" (p. 228).
  - The governor of Punjab, Khan Jahan, "که مهم نگر کوت داشت و کار به نزدیک رسانیده بود", made peace with the raja there and marched against the Mirzas (p. 228). For the battle near Multan, see the Multan bullet.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Suba Gujarat (India, no rows)**
- The Mirzas take "قلعہ چانپانیر و سورت" without a fight, then Broach (p. 231). The emperor resolves on "تسخیر ولایت گجرات" (pp. 230–231).
- Sultan Muzaffar "عرف نتهو[OCR?]", a child "از اولاد[OCR?] سلطان بهادر", is taken and spared, then escapes. Itimad Khan the eunuch and the other amirs submit, and the country is won "بے جنگ" (p. 230).
- Ahmadabad "که جامع مصر است". Mirza Aziz Kokaltash is given the title Khan-i Azam and "صوبه داری گجرات". The emperor goes to the port of "کنبهایت[OCR?]", thirty kos from Ahmadabad, and views the sea (p. 230).
- The battle at "قصبه سرنال[OCR?]", the march to Surat, and the fall of Surat fort (pp. 229–230).
- **The emperor on Ahmadabad.** He found its air and water unpleasant and wondered why its founder built a city on such ground. Its wells are mostly brackish and bitter, and the notables store rainwater in cisterns under their houses (p. 229). Yet its people are handsome and prosperous, and its "درویشان ریاضت کیش" walk "بر جاده خدا جوسے و حق پرستی" (the word before them is printed "مبسان[OCR?]"). It is "گویا که قطع دوزخ است" ("as if a piece of hell"), peopled by the houris of paradise (pp. 228–229). No dervish is named.
- The nine-day forced ride by camel "براه فتحپور" to relieve Ahmadabad, besieged by Muhammad Husain Mirza and Ikhtiyar al-Mulk. Muhammad Husain Mirza is taken and Ikhtiyar al-Mulk is killed (pp. 226–228). The campaign took "در عرض چهل روز" (p. 226).
- Gulrukh Begum, daughter of Kamran Mirza, and her son Muzaffar Husain Mirza. Raja Todar Mal, there "برائے تشخیص جمع صوبه گجرات", defeats them (p. 226).
- Later wars with Sultan Muzaffar: Mirza Khan, son of Bairam Khan, wins at Ahmadabad and Cambay and receives the title Khankhanan (p. 225). Khan-i Azam then defeats Muzaffar, the Jam, and Daulat Khan, zamindar of Sorath, and takes "قلعه جوناگڑھ و سومنات و دوارکا" (p. 225). Muzaffar is found in Kachh and cuts his own throat with a razor "به بهانه وضو" (p. 225).
- The Gujarat sultans' origin: Firuz Shah, lost while hunting, is sheltered by a headman in a village near "تهانیسر". He later makes the man a Muslim with the title "وجهه الملک[OCR?]", and his son Zafar Khan is made governor of Gujarat (p. 224). Timur reaches the neighbourhood of Delhi and Sultan Nasir al-Din Mahmud flees to Gujarat (p. 224).

**Elsewhere in India (no rows), briefly**
- Rani Durgavati fights from an elephant, then kills herself with a dagger: "مردن به نیکنامی به از زیستن بعار است" (p. 235). Her country is conquered by Asaf Khan and Wazir Khan. They are "از اولاد شیخ زین الدین خوافی که حضرت امیر تیمور باو اعتقاد تمام داشتند"; this shaikh is named only as an ancestor (p. 235).
- "قلعہ چوراگڈہ[OCR?]": a hoard of 101 chests of gold ashrafis, "اصنام طلا", and a thousand elephants. Asaf Khan sent none of it to court (p. 235).
- Agra fort and Akbarabad (pp. 234–235): see Practices.
- Ali Quli Khan (Khan Zaman) and Bahadur Khan: sons of Haidar Sultan, who was sent by Shah Tahmasp with the emperor's father on his return from Iraq. Sarkar Sambhal was Khan Zaman's jagir and he held "از سنبہل تا اودہ" (p. 234). After the Shaham Beg affair at pargana "ترہم پور[OCR?]" he revolted (p. 233). Bahadur Khan was taken and beheaded by Shahbaz Khan[OCR?] near pargana "بہگرقند[OCR?]", and Khan Zaman was killed by an elephant (p. 232).
- The Mirzas in Sambhal (pargana Azampur), Malwa and Khandesh (p. 231); Sambhal and Delhi (p. 228); Nagarkot (p. 228); Fatehpur (pp. 226, 228).

**In Pakistan's area but no row**
- "دیپالپور[OCR?]", on the Mirzas' road into Punjab (p. 228).
- "تهتهه[OCR?] تابع ملتان", the battle site (p. 228). See Doubts.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Building of Agra fort.** It was founded "در سال دہم جلوس والا[OCR?] مطابق سنہ ۹۷۳[OCR?]", with "چہار ہزار اوستاد کار" a day (p. 235). It reached "ششصت[OCR?] درعہ[OCR?]" in height to the battlements, of dressed stone, and was finished "در عرض ہشت سال" and named Akbarabad (p. 234).
- **Revenue.** Todar Mal was in Gujarat "برائے تشخیص جمع صوبه گجرات" (p. 226).
- **Bounties for heads.** After the battle: "ہر کس کہ سر مغل از حرام نمکان بیارد یک اشرفی و ہر کہ ہندوستانی بیارد یک دیک[OCR?] روپیہ انعام یابد" (p. 232).
- **Rajput valour.** At Surat the court talked of Rajputs charging onto a two-headed spear. The emperor set his own sword against his belly, and Raja Man Singh struck it away, wounding his hand (pp. 229–230).
- **Ahmadabad's water.** The notables build cisterns ("بر که") under their houses, lined with lime, to collect rainwater for the whole year (p. 229).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On loyalty.** The verses at p. 232 condemn betraying "صاحب نمک"/"ولی نعمت". The author blames Khan Zaman's revolt on "صحبت بد ذاتان" (p. 234).
- **On Sultan Muzaffar.** "از روسے بد قولی و بد طینتی" he killed Qutb al-Din Muhammad Khan after giving his word (p. 225).
- **The emperor's two-sided verdict on Ahmadabad**, hell in climate and paradise in its people (p. 229).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 235 down to p. 224, and all page joins read through.
- It opens mid-section at "چون صفت آن ولایت بعرض مقدس رسید" (p. 235), so the name of Durgavati's country is not in this chunk (not stated).
- It ends mid-sentence at "و از ظفر خان سلوک" (p. 224).
- A library stamp at the foot of p. 231 is not text.
- pp. 231–235 print "ہ" and "ئے" where pp. 224–230 print "ه" and "سے". Quotations follow each page.
- The emperor is never named. He is "آنحضرت", "خاقان زمان" or "حضرت بادشاه". His father is "بادشاہ غفران پناہ" / "بادشاہ جنت آرامگاہ".

**Multan (place-level, weigh accordingly)**
- The 8 Multan ids are attached place-level, by ruling (a). What the book says about Multan itself is limited to three things:
  - its governor, Said Khan;
  - a battle site "تابع ملتان";
  - Baluchis "انطرف ملتان" who gave up the fugitive.
- This is campaign material. It is kept because the book states Multan's governor and dependency, which matches the calibration's treatment of governor facts. A consolidator may judge it too thin.
- The "Shams" row (shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari) is a held identity. It is on the line only place-level, and nothing here raises the question.
- **"تهتهه[OCR?] تابع ملتان".** It is flagged, and the book makes it a dependency of Multan. It is therefore NOT read as suba Thatta, and no sindh province bullet is made. No toponym row exists for it, and I do not identify the place further.
- "بلوچ"/"بلوچیان" is not taken as province balochistan.

**Province (province-level, weigh accordingly)**
- "پنجاب" (pp. 228, 231) is the book's own unit under a "حاکم" in this reign. It is not the modern province's boundaries; the book gives no extent.
- Nagarkot, where the governor of Punjab was campaigning, is not located by the book. It is NOT counted as Pakistani territory.

**Traps avoided**
- **"گجرات".** All the many occurrences (pp. 224–231) are suba Gujarat. The context is Ahmadabad, Cambay, Surat, Broach, Champaner, Sorath, Junagarh, Somnath, Dwarka, Kachh and the Gujarat sultans. None is Punjab Gujrat, so shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai is not attached. "اعتماد خان گجراتی" and "اختیار الملک گجراتی" are Gujarat nobles.
- "سومنات" (p. 225) is a fort or place taken in the Gujarat war. No row.
- "شیخ زین الدین خوافی" (p. 235) is named only as Asaf Khan's ancestor and is not in the index. "شهباز خان[OCR?]" (p. 232) is the officer who beheaded Bahadur Khan, not a saint. "شیر شاہ" (p. 233) is a title the Afghans gave to Sultan Mubariz Khan's son.
- "تهانیسر" (p. 224) is outside Pakistan.

**Dates (as printed, not converted)**
- "سال دہم جلوس والا[OCR?] مطابق سنہ ۹۷۳[OCR?]" (p. 235). The regnal spans are "از ابتدائے سال سیوم[OCR?] جلوس والا بغایت سال یازدہم" with "سال دوازدہم" preceded by "[illegible]" (p. 232), and "از ابتداسے سال یازدهم جلوس والا لغایت سال بست سویم" (p. 226).
