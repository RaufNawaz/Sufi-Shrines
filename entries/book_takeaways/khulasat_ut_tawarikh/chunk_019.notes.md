# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 019 — pp. 236–248

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order, the chunk opens in the early years of the reign: Pir Muhammad Khan (Maulana Nasir al-Mulk) puts Hemu's father to death in Mewat (p. 248). Next comes "در بیان فتح قلعه مانکوت و اخراج سلطان سکندر …" (pp. 247–248): the march from Delhi into Punjab, a divination from Hafiz's diwan, the siege of Mankot, and Sikandar's submission and withdrawal. "در بیان بے اعتدالیهائے خانخانان بیرام خان و اخراج او از ممالک محروسه" (pp. 244–247) follows: Bairam Khan's excesses, his fall, his revolt in Punjab and defeat, his pardon and leave for the Hajj, his murder at Patan in Gujarat, and his burial "در حوالی مقبره شیخ حسام الدین خلیفه شیخ نظام الدین اولیا" (p. 244). It closes with a notice of his son Mirza Abd al-Rahim Khan-i Khanan. "در بیان تسخیر ولایت مالوه" (pp. 239–244) tells the story of Baz Bahadur and Rup Mati, gives a list of the rulers of Malwa, and ends with Adham Khan's revolt and death. "در بیان تسخیر ولایت گکھران" (pp. 238–239) covers the Gakhar country between the Bahat and the Sindh, Rohtas fort, Sultan Sarang, Sultan Adam and Kamal Khan. Then come "در بیان رسیدن زخم بحضرت بادشاہ" (p. 237), where the emperor is wounded by an arrow on his way back from the tomb of Shaikh Nizam al-Din Auliya, and "در بیان کشتہ شدن شاہ ابوالمعانی" (pp. 236–237). The chunk ends with the opening of "در بیان ولایت گڈھہ کہ انرا گوندوالہ گویند" (p. 236), on Rani Durgavati.

  - In the Gakhar section, Sher Shah and Islam Shah "مدتی بتسخیر آن ولایت ترددات[OCR?] نمودند و قلعہ روہتاس را بر سر حد احداث کردند تا جماعت گکھران عاجز شدہ مطیع شوند" (p. 239). That is, they built Rohtas fort on the border to force the Gakhars into submission.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Saints and tombs named in the history**
- **Tomb of Shaikh Nizam al-Din Auliya.** "روزے انحضرت بزیارت روضہ منورہ شیخ نظام الدین اولیا قدس اللہ سرہ تشریف بردند" (p. 237).
  - On the way back, at the "چہار سوے[OCR?]" of the city, a man lying in ambush shot an arrow. It lodged in the emperor's right shoulder, nearly a span deep.
  - The assailant was killed at once on the emperor's order, without an inquiry, so that people would not be harmed unjustly. The emperor rode back to the palace and the wound healed in seven days.
  - The assailant turned out to be "قتلق[OCR?] نام غلام شرف الدین حسین میرزا", sent by the Mirza (p. 237). The city is not named in the passage.
- **Tomb of Shaikh Husam al-Din and Bairam Khan's burial.** After Bairam Khan was killed, "جمعی از فقرا قالب خونیں[OCR?] او را که درجه شهادت یافته بود بر داشته در حوالی مقبره شیخ حسام الدین خلیفه شیخ نظام الدین اولیا بخاک سپردند بعد ازان استخوانش بمشهد مقدس رسانیده" (p. 244).
  - That is, a group of faqirs buried him near the tomb of Shaikh Husam al-Din, khalifa of Shaikh Nizam al-Din Auliya, and his bones were later taken to Mashhad.
  - The book does not name the town of the tomb. The murder happened at "شهر پتن از مضافات احمد آباد گجرات" (p. 245), so this is outside Pakistan.
  - The chronogram is "گفتا که شهید شد محمد بیرام" (p. 244).
- **Shaikh Umar the dervish.** The wounded Rup Mati was lodged "در خانه شیخ عمر درویش که بخدا پرستی و تقوے و ریاضت در آن دیار مشهور بود", and treated there among the dervish's women and household (p. 242). This was in Malwa, outside Pakistan.
- **Makhdum al-Mulk.** "مولانا [illegible] عبدالله[OCR?] سلطان پوری المشهور بمخدوم الملک" was sent first to fetch Bairam Khan, then Munim Khan (p. 245). He is an official, not a shrine.

**Shah Abu al-Maali (pp. 236–237)**
- Under "بادشاہ جنت آرامگاہ" he was a favourite at court "بوسیلہ حسن صوری". The author calls him "بدخو بد افعال" (p. 237).
- At Kalanaur he excused himself from the assembly. He was arrested, sent to Lahore and escaped (p. 237).
- He was later held in the fort of Bayana and freed by Bairam Khan, then went to Mecca. Back in India he stirred up trouble (p. 236).
- In Kabul he married Mirza Muhammad Hakim's sister and had Chuchak[OCR?] Begum killed. He was defeated on the Ghorband by Mirza Sulaiman of Badakhshan and captured at "موضع چاریکاران".
- Mirza Hakim cut his throat "القصاص[OCR?] خون والدہ" (p. 236). The book calls him "سیدزادہ".

**Gakhar country (pp. 238–239)**
- "این ولایت مابین دریائے بہت و سندہ واقع است". Its ruler never obeyed the rulers of Delhi.
- "در بعضے تواریخ" it was anciently part of Kashmir. Sultan Mahmud Ghaznavi took it and gave it to one of his amirs, "از کیان زادگان[OCR?]", named Gakhar, whose line has held it since (p. 239).
- Islam Shah imprisoned Sultan Sarang and his son Kamal Khan in Gwalior and had the prison blown up with gunpowder. Kamal Khan survived "بقدرت الہی" (p. 238).
- Kamal Khan served the new reign in the battle with Hemu. A farman told Sultan Adam, Sarang's brother, who had seized Kamran Mirza for "حضرت بادشاہ جنت آرام گاہ", to hand half the country to Kamal Khan (p. 238).
- Adam refused and came as far as "قصبہ ہشتگان[OCR?] این رو سے آب بہت[OCR?]", where he was defeated. The whole country was given to Kamal Khan (p. 238). Editor's footnote variant: "صلان[OCR?] یا نیلان[OCR?]". The footnote marker's place in the text is not shown.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Ziyarat by the ruler.** The emperor visited the tomb of Nizam al-Din Auliya (p. 237).
- **Faqirs bury a "شهید".** Faqirs buried Bairam Khan near a saint's maqbara, and his bones were later taken to Mashhad (p. 244).
- **Divination from Hafiz.** Before Mankot "حضرت از دیوان لسان الغیب تفال جستند". The verse "سکندر را نمی بخشند آبے[OCR?] …" was taken as good news of victory (p. 248).
- **Forced conversion refused.** Nasir al-Mulk called on Hemu's eighty-year-old father to accept Islam. He answered that after eighty years of worshipping the Creator "بائین خویش" he would not change his religion now, and was put to the sword (p. 248).
- **Jauhar.** The custom is described "بر رسم هندوستان", with women put to the sword or burned in such disasters (p. 242).
- **Insignia and grants.** Bairam's "علم و نقاره و تمن[OCR?] و طورغ[OCR?] و منصب" were given to Atka Khan (p. 246). Abd al-Rahim got "منصب پنجهزاری", the highest of that time (p. 244).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Against music.** In the author's own voice, "سرود و نغمه مردود اهل دلان پاک نهاد و مقبول ارباب فسق[OCR?]". He calls it oil on the fire of lust, which raises "علم بت[OCR?] پرستی" in the hearts of the irreligious. This is aimed at Baz Bahadur (p. 243).
- **On Bairam Khan.** He took the emperor for "خورد سال" and credited the victories to his own effort. Among his worst acts the author lists the killing of Tardi Beg Khan without orders (p. 247).
- **On Abu al-Maali.** The author says he was handsome but ill-natured, and adds the verse "حیف باشد کہ نکو روی نکو خوے نبود" (p. 237).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 248 down to p. 236. The pages join in that order, for example "دست گرفته بالا" (p. 248) → "در حضور اقدس حاضر گردانند" (p. 247).
- It opens mid-sentence at p. 248 ("تردوات[OCR?] نمایان …") and ends at p. 236 inside the Durgavati section.
- The ruler is never named. He is "حضرت بادشاہ", "خاقان زمان" or "آنحضرت".
- The source stamp on p. 242 is not text.

**Mapping**
- **gurdwara-choa-sahib** is attached place-level through toponym_map's "rohtas fort". The book places this Rohtas on the Gakhar border, between the Bahat and the Sindh. It says nothing of the gurdwara.
- **Multan (8)** is attached place-level. It rests on a single retrospective clause about a fief grant (p. 241). Weigh accordingly.
- **Lahore (35)** is attached place-level from two passing mentions: Abu al-Maali's imprisonment and escape (p. 237) and a stay of a few days (p. 245). This is campaign or "marched via" material in the sense of the brief's point 3. A consolidator may drop it.
- **Shah Abu al-Maali is NOT mapped to darbar-abul-muali-qadri.** The book presents a "سیدزادہ" courtier, killed near Kabul by Mirza Hakim (pp. 236–237). It does not connect him to any saint or tomb in Lahore. The similar name alone is no ground.
- **Province bullets** are province-level, so weigh accordingly. "ولایت پنجاب" and "تهتهه[OCR?]" are the book's own units, not the modern provinces. Mankot, Nurpur, Nagarkot, Talwara, Machhiwara and Sirhind are not taken as Pakistan. "تهتهه" is flagged.
- **Traps avoided**
  - Every "گجرات" in the chunk is suba Gujarat: Bahadur Shah "والی گجرات" (p. 240), Zafar Khan (p. 241), Abd al-Rahim's conquest (p. 244) and "پتن از مضافات احمد آباد گجرات" (p. 245). None takes a row.
  - "ہشتگان[OCR?]" is not the "hashtnagri" of Peshawar.
  - The river "بہت" is not the town of Jhelum.
  - "سلطان محمود غزنوی" is not a row's principal figure.

**Dates and figures**
- "از ابتدائے سنہ ۹۹۶[OCR?] ہجری" for the start of Dilawar Khan's rule (p. 241) is printed and flagged as shown. It is not corrected.
- "۶۹۶[OCR?] … ۹۶۶[OCR?] … یکصد و ہفتاد و یک سال" (p. 240) is quoted as printed. No interval has been computed.
- The editor's footnotes give variant regnal lengths from Firishta (p. 241), "شجاع خان" from the Tabaqat-i Akbari (p. 240), and "قتلق فولاد" from Firishta and the Akbarnama (p. 237). None touches a mapped fact.
