# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 007 — pp. 77–88

The book reads from the highest page down. This chunk covers the war of succession, not a settled reign: Shah Jahan ("حضرت اعلی") is alive, and the narrative follows the prince the book calls "خدیو گیهان" / "خدیو آفاق". It opens mid-sentence (p. 88) with Dara Shikoh startled by the imperial army's crossing of the Chambal. He draws up his army near Dholpur and refuses the peace Shah Jahan urges. The camp moves to "موضع راجپور[OCR?]", ten kos from Akbarabad, on 6 Ramadan, and the battle is fought on "هفتم[OCR?] رمضان المبارک مطابق بیستم[OCR?] خورداد ماه الهی" (pp. 85–88). Dara flees to Akbarabad and then toward Shahjahanabad; the victor halts at "عمارات سموگر[OCR?]" and the garden "نور منزل[OCR?] که بباغ دهره[OCR?] مشهور", and Prince Muhammad Sultan takes charge of the Agra fort (pp. 81–84). He marches on 22 Ramadan, keeps Id al-Fitr at the Salimpur[OCR?] garden (p. 80), and then comes "در بیان قید گردیدن پادشاهزاده محمد مرادبخش" (pp. 78–79): Murad Bakhsh is seized at Mathura on 4 Shawwal. The last section, "در بیان بقیه ماجراے احوال دارا شکوه" (pp. 77–78), has Dara at Shahjahanabad and then at Lahore, which he reaches on 17 Shawwal. It breaks off with Raja Rajrup of Nurpur joining him (p. 77). The section continues in chunk 006 at p. 76. **No saint, tomb or shrine is named anywhere in the chunk.** The only mappings are place-level (Lahore, Multan, Peshawar).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Kingship cannot be shared** (p. 79). Kings are "ظلال حضرت آفریدگار", and sovereignty, like divinity, admits no partner. The author gives a verse: "شریک ملک چون شریک حق محال است". Murad's claim is his example of this.
- **Shah Jahan's partiality.** "حضرت اعلی را کمال توجه بحال خسران مال دارا شکوه بود", so the planned audience was given up (p. 81).
- **On Dara.**
  - "چون بهره از شجاعت و مردانگی نداشت" (p. 84).
  - He had no knowledge of "قواعد پیکار" (p. 86).
  - He wore out his own army in the heat (p. 88).
  - His defeat is put in astral terms: "کوکب بخت سوز[OCR?] دارا شکوهی" (p. 88).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 88 down to p. 77. All page joins read through.
- It opens mid-sentence at "موکب والا با این چستی و چالاکی" (p. 88), continuing from the next-higher chunk.
- It ends at p. 77 with Rajrup joining "در میان دهلی و سهرند", which runs on into chunk 006's p. 76 ("برفاقت[OCR?] او در لاهور رسیده").
- The coordinator described the chunk as "Shah Jahan's reign". In fact it is the succession war of 1068–69: Shah Jahan appears only as the living "حضرت اعلی". The victor is never named outright. He is "خدیو گیهان", and the book links him to the name "عالمگیر" only through the sword and "خورشید جهان افروز عالمگیری" (pp. 82, 88). No Hijri year is printed in this chunk.
- A library stamp at the foot of p. 79 is not text.

**Place-level attachments (weigh accordingly)**
- **Lahore (35).** The material is about the place itself: its treasury and the royal fort entered on 17 Shawwal. Held-identity rows on this line (shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri) are attached only place-level, and nothing here raises their questions.
- **Multan (8).** The only fact is that Multan was in Dara's iqta, named jointly with Bhakkar and Thatta. The held "Shams" row is attached only place-level.
- **Peshawar (10).** The only fact is the line naming Peshawar's (and Kabul's) armies under Mahabat Khan's governorship. This is a thin, administrative attachment.
- **"تهته[OCR?]".** It is bare, not "صوبه تهته", and is grouped with faujdars, so it is NOT taken to PROVINCE sindh. Like Bhakkar, it is left under Other.
- **Province punjab.** It rests on "ان صوبه" referring back to Lahore. Province-level; a Mughal suba, not the modern province's boundaries — the book's own unit.
