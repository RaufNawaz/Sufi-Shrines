**The edition's manuscripts, as the editor states**
- The editor states he first learned of the book from its references in Sir Sayyid Ahmad Khan's *Asar-us-sanadid* (p. 9).
- He states that W. M. Hailey, Chief Commissioner of Delhi, wrote to the Jaipur Darbar on his behalf, and that he did not use the Jaipur MSS. He also states that Sir John Marshall examined his MSS (p. 9).
- Five MSS were collated. Variant readings are in the footnotes, and the text is "marked with the letter "ب[OCR?]"" (p. 10).
- MS I: Shikasta, partly worm-eaten, "complete and comparatively old and correct". It has 273[OCR?] leaves, 35[OCR?]–38[OCR?] lines a page, and the first ten leaves were recopied. Its colophon is dated in the first regnal year of "Muiz-ud-din Alamgir II", "one thousand one hundred and sixty eight Hijra". It is the editor's own copy, bought at Delhi. He calls it the "most reliable" because it lacks the added death notice of Aurangzeb (pp. 10–11).
- MS II: Nastaliq, 505[OCR?] leaves, "Total 63[OCR?] juze … and one leaf". Its endorsement names "Sujan Rai Bhandari, the resident of Batala" and dates the copy "circa one thousand two hundred Hijra" under "Abkar, son of … Shah Alam" and Jagat Singh of "Sawai Jaipur". A last line reads "Sa[illegible] one thousand eight[OCR?] hundred and sixty four". The editor states the copyist "seems to have been a Hindu". Bought at Lucknow (pp. 11–12).
- MS III: Nastaliq, 495[OCR?] leaves, transcription date obliterated. Procured at Moradabad through Maulvi Abrar Hasan (p. 12).
- MS IV: Nastaliq, 282[OCR?] leaves. Its endorsement calls the author "accomplished in Hindi, Persian and Sanskrit". Lent by Sayyid Ahsan Shah, Rais of Sardhana (p. 12).
- MS V: Shikasta, incomplete, ending at Shah Jahan's deposition ("see page 490[OCR?]"). It has 308[OCR?] leaves and belongs to a college whose name is covered by the stamp ([illegible]). It was lent through "Maulvi Abdul Rahman, the Arabic professor" (pp. 12–13).
- The editor states that three of his MSS carry the added notice of Aurangzeb's death. He translates it from MS II: "Friday, the 28th[OCR?] Zulqada of the year 1118[OCR?] A. H.", "at the age of 91[OCR?] years 17[OCR?] days and 2 hours", at Ahmadnagar (p. 10).
- Helpers named: Pir Wilayet Shah; Maulvi Ashfaq Ali; the editor's brothers Hafiz Muhammad Hasan, Maqbul Hasan, Fazal Hasan and Bashir Ahmad Hashmi, who helped with the index; his father Maulvi Shariat Ullah; and Maulvi Abrar Hasan. ## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

These are the editor's judgements (1918), not the author's.
- **Authorship.** The editor states that the text never names its author. Only the endorsements of MSS II, III and IV give "Sujan Rai Bhandari of Batala". He notes that Dowson read the name as "Subhan Rai" and Batala as "Patyala". From the use of Hindu eras he presumes the author was a Hindu, and he takes the concealed name as a sign of an unbiased writer (p. 13).
- **Date and scope.** The editor states the book was written "in the 40th[OCR?] year of the reign of Aurangzeb, corresponding to 1107[OCR?] A. H. (1695-6[OCR?] A. D.)" over some two years. He calls it "chiefly a history of Delhi" from Judhishtar onward (p. 13).
- **Three parts.** The editor gives them as: I, the geography of India under Aurangzeb; II, the rajas from Judhishtar to "Rai Pithura"; III, the Muhammadan emperors from Subuktagin to Aurangzeb (p. 14).
- **Value of Part I.** The editor judges Part I "the most valuable portion". It covers "the 18 Subas", their Sarkars, Muhals and revenue, "supplementing the Ain-i-Akbari". He adds that "The account of the Panjab, particularly that of Lahore and the Sarkar of Batala … deserves especial mention, containing very minute detail based on his personal knowledge" (p. 14).
- **Value of Part III.** The editor states it is largely borrowed, except for the contest between Aurangzeb and his brothers, written "on the personal information of the author". He argues it was "not composed under the court influence" (p. 14).

## Citable passages
