# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 035 — pp. 441–452

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order, this chunk is part of the account of the Hindu rajas and has no section heading of its own. It opens mid-story at p. 452 with Raja Bhartrihari ("راجه بهرتهری"). He tests his queen's devotion and she dies. He renounces the kingdom and becomes an ascetic, and the demon "ببیر[OCR?] بتال" then falls upon the city of Ujjain (p. 452). Pages 451–448 tell how Ujjain is ravaged, how the townspeople agree to give the demon one "one-day king" each day, and how Bikramajit ("بکرماجیت"), Bhartrihari's exiled brother, arrives with a Banjara grain caravan from Gujarat. He hears a jackal's prophecy, takes a potter's son's turn, wrestles the demon to a pact, is recognised and is enthroned at Holi. Pages 447–444 cover Bikramajit's reign: his justice, his learning and his conquests. They also give the origin of the *Singhasan Battisi* under Raja Bhoj, the date of his era, and the conflicting accounts of his death (Salbahan in the Akbarnama; Samandarpal the jogi in the *Rajavali* and *Rajtarangini*). Pages 443–442 give the story of Samandarpal the jogi taking over the raja's body, which the author disputes, and a long denunciation of the jogi's hypocrisy. The chunk closes on p. 441 with Samandarpal's reign total and the start of the list of his "پال" successors, down to Raja Harpal.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Bhartrihari and the demon of Ujjain (pp. 452–451)**
- Raja Bhartrihari puts out a false report that a demon has killed him on the hunt and sends a blood-stained robe as proof. His queen dies at once on hearing it ("قالب تهی نموده") (p. 452).
- A variant from "بعضے نسخه" says he had two wives. "انگ سیتا[OCR?]" was the faithless one, who died after falling from a roof over her love for the "میراخور". "پنگلا", the faithful one, died at the false news (p. 452).
- Bhartrihari then left the kingdom for "بادیه تجرید", took up austerities and became one of the "واصلان درگاه احدیت". By the fruit mentioned earlier, or by his austerities, he gained eternal life: "بقول اهل هند تا حال در خلعت هستی بوده بطریق اخفا در عالم سفلی سیار است" (p. 452).
- With the land left without a ruler, demons spread. At Ujjain, "ببیر[OCR?] بتال نام دیوی که سرحلقه دیوان مردم آزار" began to devour the people, and many fled (p. 452). Ujjain, which had once rivalled "شهر اندر", was laid waste (p. 451).
- The notables agreed with the demon on a daily turn. Each day one townsman sat on the throne as king for a day, with full royal state, and was the demon's food at night (p. 451).

**Bikramajit at Ujjain (pp. 451–448)**
- "جماعة نقله غله که به زبان عرف بنجاره گویند" came "از جانب ولایت گجرات" and camped by a river near Ujjain. With them was "بکرماجیت برادر راجه بهرتهری که بموجب اغوائے رانی اخراج یافته بود", who had become "نوکر بنجاره" (pp. 451–450).
- A jackal said that a corpse would come down the river with "چهار لعل بے بها" at its waist and a turquoise ring, and that whoever fed the corpse to it would win "سلطنت روئے زمین". Bikramajit knew the tongues of beasts. He found the corpse and the stones, and left the body for the jackal (p. 450).
- Bikramajit took the turn of a potter's ("کلال") son. He went to the fort in royal dress on an elephant, sat on the throne and had sweets and drinks set at the gate by which the demon came (pp. 450–449).
- He fought the demon by night. The demon offered peace, gave up the city to him, and promised to come whenever he was called. In the morning the people found him alive and guessed he was "از نژاد ملائک" or "بکرماجیت برادر راجه بهرتهری" (p. 449).
- He was recognised and enthroned amid celebrations "چون ایام هولے بود که آن جشنی هندی است مشهور" (pp. 448–447).

**Bikramajit's reign (pp. 447–444)**
- In his reign the rains came on time, there was no famine or plague, no one was left poor, and theft and highway robbery ended (p. 447).
- He knew all the sciences and arts, many languages and "زبان وحش و طیر", and could tell the past, present and future (p. 447).
- Conquests: "تمامے ممالک وسیع دکن و اودیسه و بنگ و بهار و گجرات و سومنات". Last of all "ولایت اندرپت که اکنون به دهلی شهرت" [continues on p. 446] "دارد". There he killed "راجه سکونت" in battle and made his coin current "تا کابل و آن ممالک" (pp. 447–446).
- The *Singhasan Battisi* ("سنگهاسن بتیسی"). Long after his death, "درسنه ۵۴۲[OCR?]", Raja Bhoj ruled Malwa, with his vizier "برج پنڈت" (pp. 446–445). While hunting, Bhoj saw a child king judging from a mound. He had the mound dug up and found a jewelled throne bearing "سی و دو صورت". The figures told him thirty-two tales of Bikramajit, and "برج[OCR?] پنڈت" wrote them down in Sanskrit as the *Singhasan Battisi* (p. 445).
- His era: "تاریخ جلوس او برا اورنگ فرمان روائے مالوه بقولے روز تسخیر ولایت دهلی و کشتن راجه سکونت بسنه سه هزار و چهل و چهار راجه جدهشتر در دفاتر اهل هند ... ثبت گردید". To the time of writing "یکهزار و هفتصد و پنجاه و سه از سنه او منقضی میگردد" (p. 444). No figure is converted.

**Bikramajit's death: three versions (pp. 444–442)**
- Akbarnama ("در اکبرنامه چنین مے نویسند"): he was taken by "سالباهن مرزبان" of the Deccan. He asked that his era be kept, and Salbahan killed him but kept it. Salbahan's own era also comes from this (p. 444).
- "نسخه راجاولی و راج ترنگنی": he died at the hands of "سمندرپال جوگی", after a long description of his old age (pp. 444–443).
- The jogi, a master of sorcery and of "علم خلع بدن", won over the raja and his court. He taught the aged raja to move into a fresh young corpse, then entered the raja's own body and killed the youth (p. 443).
- On p. 442: "مدت عمر راجه یکهزار و یکصد سال ازانجمله جهانبانی دهلی نود و[OCR?] سال". The number after "نود و" is missing or flagged; it is not filled.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Banjaras.** "جماعة نقله غله که به زبان عرف بنجاره گویند", grain carriers travelling from Gujarat (p. 451).
- **Holi.** "هولے ... جشنی هندی است مشهور", kept with "گلال افشانی", saffron, pan, oil, attar and incense (pp. 448–447).
- **Jogi practices**, as the author describes them hostilely in Samandarpal's case: ash-smearing, nakedness, silence, a raised arm, hanging upside down, selling medicine and ash for gold, and claims of alchemy (p. 442).
- **Eras.** The Bikramajit era is dated from his conquest of Delhi in "سه هزار و چهل و چهار" of Yudhishthira's era (p. 444). The Salbahan era is explained by Salbahan's capture of Bikramajit (p. 444). Raja Bhoj's date is "۵۴۲[OCR?]" with no era named (p. 446).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Bhartrihari gained eternal life and, "بقول اهل هند", still wanders hidden in the world (p. 452).
- A jackal's prophecy of kingship, understood by Bikramajit, who knew the tongues of animals (p. 450).
- Bikramajit overcame the man-eating demon, who became his ally on call (p. 449).
- The throne with thirty-two figures, which speak to Raja Bhoj (p. 445).
- Samandarpal transfers his soul into the raja's body (p. 443); the author rejects this (see below).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author disputes the body-transfer story** (p. 443). "عقلای صائب رای آن را قبول نمی دارند". He gives two reasons:
  - The soul does not age; youth and age belong to bodies. An old, failing body would not become young because the jogi entered it.
  - Had the jogi entered the raja's body, people would have gone on calling him Raja Bikramajit, not Samandarpal.
  - His conclusion: "حکایت خلع بدن جوگی فروغی[OCR?] از صدق ندارد محض اراجیف است". Either the raja died a natural death or Salbahan killed him, and the jogi took the throne with the nobles' agreement, or else the jogi did away with him (p. 443).
- **The author sets his sources against each other.** He weighs the Akbarnama against the *Rajavali* and *Rajtarangini* on Bikramajit's death (p. 444), and notes "چنانچه در ولادت بکرماجیت اختلاف بسیار است هم چنان در مردن او نیز اختلاف دارد" (p. 442).
- **Why an era is remembered.** Only a ruler of great deeds and justice has his accession date kept, and of earlier kings only Yudhishthira's era survived before Bikramajit's (p. 445).
- **Moral judgement on Samandarpal.** He traded "درویشے را ببادشاهی" and "خداطلبی را بدنیاطلبی" (p. 441).

## Citable passages

- "بقول اهل هند تا حال در خلعت هستی بوده بطریق اخفا در عالم سفلی سیار است" — "According to the people of Hind he is still alive, wandering hidden in the lower world" (of Bhartrihari) (p. 452).
- "همانا که حکایت خلع بدن جوگی فروغی[OCR?] از صدق ندارد محض اراجیف است" — "Indeed the story of the jogi's leaving his body has no light of truth; it is mere rumour" (p. 443).
- "جماعة نقله غله که به زبان عرف بنجاره گویند از جانب ولایت گجرات" — "a company of grain carriers, whom common speech calls Banjara, from the province of Gujarat" (p. 451).
- "یعنی درویشے را ببادشاهی بدل کرد و خداطلبی را بدنیاطلبی معاوضه نمود" — "that is, he exchanged dervishhood for kingship, and seeking God for seeking the world" (p. 441).
