# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 039 — pp. 487–497

There is no section heading anywhere in the chunk: it is one continuous stretch of the book's Mahabharata narrative, inside the Hindu-rajas part of the book. In reading order it opens mid-verse (p. 497), in praise of Bhim Sen's strength, then describes the gifts of Arjun, Nakul and Sahdev and the brothers' devotion to Yudhishthira (pp. 496–497). **The narrative, briefly**
- Bhim Sen's strength; Arjun's archery and magic arrows, explained as "علم خاصه اهل ملکوت", and "ارجن ملک نژاد و قدسی نهاد بود" (p. 497).
- Duryodhan's envy, poisonings and attempts to drown Bhim Sen (p. 496). Dhritarashtra splits the kingdom. The lac house is burned by the Pandavas themselves, who escape by a tunnel; "بهیل زنی با پنج پسر خود" is burned in it and taken for them (p. 496). They then wander "بلباس زاهدان در معابد و اماکن متبرکه" (p. 496).
- The svayamvar: Arjun, disguised as a beggar, strings the bow and shoots the golden fish into the pot of oil. By their mother's order the five share Draupadi, "هر یک هفتاد روز نوبت" (p. 495).
- Dhritarashtra recalls them, halves the kingdom again, and sends them to Indrapat. Yudhishthira conquers widely and completes "جگ راجسو" (p. 495).
- The dice game: Yudhishthira loses wealth, kingdom, his four brothers, himself and Draupadi. Dushasan tries to strip her and cloth keeps appearing "بقدرت الهی" (pp. 493–494). Then the second game and its terms: twelve years in the wilderness, the thirteenth hidden, and twelve more if discovered (p. 493). Karan and Dushasan mock Draupadi (p. 493).
- The exile: Arjun goes "دساندلوک[OCR?]" and spends "پنجسال در اندرلوک" learning archery from the angels, while Yudhishthira makes a circuit of the "تیرتهها" (p. 492). Duryodhan refuses even five villages and both sides call in allies (p. 492).
- The war: seven divisions on the Pandava side, led off by Bhim Sen (p. 490); Duryodhan's ranks of elephants, each with fifty horse and seven foot behind (p. 490); Bhishma's ten days of fighting (p. 489); eighteen days of battle, and "روز هژدهم درجودهن از ضرب گرز کوه شکن بهیم سین" died (p. 488).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Rajasuya, as the book defines it.** "جگ راجسو باصطلاح هند عبارت از عبادت عظمی است": food for many thousand brahmins, gifts of gold and silver vessels, offerings burned with spells, and the kings of the earth doing every service in person, "حتی که آب کشی و طعام پزی و[OCR?] ظروف[OCR?] شوی". Only one whose "حکمش بر هفت کشور جاری باشد" can perform it (p. 495).
- **Svayamvar.** "آن را در زبان هندی سوینبر[OCR?] گویند": kings were summoned "بائین نیاگان خویش" so that the daughter could choose a husband (p. 495).
- **Widows burning themselves.** After the war "بیشتر زندگانی وبال دانسته خودها در آتش انداخته خاکستر کردند" (p. 487). The book does not use a term for the practice.
- **Army sizes, as printed (not computed).** One "کهیونی باصطلاح اهل هند" is "بست و یک هزار و سه صد و هفتاد فیل سوار و بقدر همین عرابه سوار [illegible]یک لک و نود و شش هزار و سه صد و سی اسپ و سه لک و هشت هزار و پنجاه پیاده", the total of one "کهوهن" being "[illegible]نج[OCR?] لک و چهل و هفت هزار و یک صد و بست نفر" (p. 487). Both armies together: "نود و هشت [illegible]لک و چهل و هشت هزار و یک صد و شصت نفر آدم سوای فیل و اسپ و شتر" (p. 487). "هفت کهیونی از جانب پاندوان و یازده کهیونی از طرف کوروان که همگی هژده کهیونی" (p. 488).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Kurukshetra, by the Hindus' account** ("بقول دانشمندان هند"): Brahma came into being there "از گل نیلوفر" and created the world from that place. "اعتقاد ان جماعت" is that any holy person who dies there is freed from rebirth ("تناسخ") and gains paradise. This is why the site was chosen for the battle (p. 491).
- **Draupadi's cloth.** She prayed to "ایزد تعالی که ستار العیوب است", and cloth kept appearing on her as Dushasan pulled it away (p. 493).
- **Omens at the exile** ("گویند"): an earthquake, lightning without cloud, a total eclipse on a day not due, stars falling by day, jackals in the bazaar, vultures on the gate, lotuses on desert trees, the river running backward, fruit out of season and animals bearing young of another kind. The diviners read them as ruin for Dhritarashtra's sons (pp. 492–493).
- **Headless bodies.** "بقول فضلاے هند": where ten thousand are killed, one lifeless body dances. In this war most of the dead danced, and headless trunks moved about like fighters (p. 488).
- **Arjun's arrows** split into thousands, made fire, wind, dust and water, and he changed size and vanished in battle (p. 497).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's own reckoning.** "لغایت تسوید این نسخه چهار هزار و هشت صد و سی و پنج سال منقضی می شود": up to the writing of this copy, four thousand eight hundred and thirty-five years have passed (since the war, by context). He adds that the war is still a byword for great battles, and that "آثار استخوان و امثال آن دران مرز معاینه [illegible]گردد" (bones and the like can be seen in that land) (p. 487). Not converted.
- **On the outcome.** The victory was decreed "در دیوان کده ازل" in Yudhishthira's name, and Duryodhan got what his deeds toward his cousins deserved (p. 488). Yudhishthira's losses at dice are put down to fate ("تقدیر قادر مطلق") despite "آن قدر عقل و دانش" (p. 494).
- **On Arjun's arts.** Such feats are beyond human power and belong to the angels, so they are not remarkable in Arjun, who was "ملک نژاد" (p. 497).
