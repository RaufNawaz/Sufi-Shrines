# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 037 — pp. 465–475

This chunk sits in the book's account of the Hindu rajas. Read from the highest page down, it opens mid-sentence in the story of Raja Parikshit ("راجه پریچهت[OCR?]"). Because of the curse of a dervish's son, he expects to die after seven days. He builds a refuge on a great wooden pillar set in "دریای گنگ", but on the seventh day the serpent "بهجنگ[OCR?]" reaches him and bites him (pp. 473–475). On the way the serpent meets and bribes the physician "کنت[OCR?] دهنتر[OCR?]", who had come to cure the raja (pp. 474–475). The Parikshit section closes with "مدت سلطنت راجه پریچهت[OCR?] شصت سال" (p. 473). Then comes the section headed "راجه جنمجه[OCR?] بن راجه پریچهت[OCR?]" (pp. 465–473). It covers his accession, his campaign in the north and the abid "اونگ[OCR?]" urging him to avenge his father (p. 472), then the snake sacrifice and the intercession of the dervish "استک[OCR?]" (p. 471). After the feast for the Brahmans (p. 470), Byas Dev ("بیاس دیو") foretells a great sin. That sin is the episode of the horse, the beautiful woman in the wilderness and the slaughter of the Brahmans (pp. 466–470). The section ends with his penance, his hearing of the Mahabharata, and his reign of 84 years (p. 465). The chunk closes with a bare king-list from "راجه اسمند[OCR?]" to "راجه اوگرسین[OCR?]", which continues in the next chunk (p. 465).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **krishna-mandir-kabari-bazar**, **krishna-mandir-ravi-road** (the book's "سری کرشن"):
  - In his refuge Parikshit listened to "کتاب بهاگوت". The book describes it as "مشتمل بر جلایل علوم حقیقت و طریقت متضمن احوال سری کرشن که نزد ارباب هند معتبر و بزرگ تراست", that is, containing the account of Sri Krishna, held weighty and great by the people of Hind (p. 473).
  - The book says nothing more of Krishna, and it names no site for him (p. 473).

No other archive row is named in this chunk. Hastinapur, the Ganga, Sesh Nag, Byas Dev and the Pandavas have no rows (see Other and Doubts).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Parikshit (pp. 473–475; reading order 475 → 473)**
- He had been told that "بموجب دعائے درویش زاده مستجاب الدعوات بعد هفت روز جهان گذران را پدرود باید نمود" (p. 475). Before that, the page opens mid-sentence with "گستاخی شده".
- His refuge: "ستونی از چوب کلان درمیان دریای گنگ ایستاده کرده" with a building on it. He surrounded it with "افسون خوانان و مارگیران", kept proven antidotes by him, ate nothing, and thought of nothing but God. Six days passed this way (p. 475).
- On the seventh day "بهجنگ[OCR?] مار" took human form and set out (p. 475).
- **The physician "کنت[OCR?] دهنتر[OCR?]"**, said to revive the snake-bitten. He met the serpent on the road. To test him, the serpent burned a great tree to ash with its poison, and the physician restored it, with the men and birds on it (pp. 474–475). The serpent then paid him in jewels to go home, and he did so, "مغلوب طمع و حرص" (p. 474). The name is printed "کنت[OCR?] وهنتر[OCR?]" twice on p. 474.
- At "هستناپور" the serpent's sons, disguised as Brahmans, brought fruit to the raja. The serpent hid inside one fruit "بصورت کرمک خورد". The raja set the worm on his neck in mockery, and it grew and bit him (pp. 473–474).
- The building burned with the raja, and the pillar fell with a noise like a thunderbolt. That night the people of Hastinapur did not sleep. Next day "جسد(۱) سوخته راجه را ... در آب گنگا انداختند" (editor's footnote variant: "استخوان") (p. 473).
- During his days in the refuge he heard "بیدانت شاستر" and the Bhagavata "از زبان ... سوامی سکهدیو[OCR?] بن بیاس دیو" (p. 473).
- "مدت سلطنت راجه پریچهت[OCR?] شصت سال" (p. 473).

**Janamejaya (pp. 465–473; reading order 473 → 465)**
- As "خلف بزرگ" he was placed on the throne by the amirs and wazirs "با وجود خوردسالی". He led an army "در ولایت شمال" and returned to "دار السلطنت هستناپور" (p. 472).
- "اونگ[OCR?] نامی عابد" rebuked him for warring on rajas who had done him no harm. The abid told him that "بهجنگ[OCR?] مار" had killed his father and urged revenge (p. 472).
- **The snake sacrifice.** It was conducted with "برهمنان بید خوان" and a fire enclosure (pp. 471–472). The snakes came in waves: "اول بیست هزار مار ... بعد ازان یک لک پس ازان یازده لک و سپس آن کرور ... ده کرور و بعد آن بیشمار مار" (p. 471).
- "سیس(۱)[OCR?] ناگ" was shaken, "که بقول هند تخته زمین بر یک سر از هزار سر او قائم است" (editor's footnote variant: "سیکه[OCR?] ناگ") (p. 471).
- "استک[OCR?] نامی درویش" interceded. The rest of the snakes were spared, "و گویند که بهجنگ[OCR?] مار نیز ... بشفاعت عابد نجات یافت" (p. 471).
- **The feast and the dating remark.** Brahmans and visiting rulers were given gifts. "این جگ راجه جنمجه[OCR?] تا حال که چهار هزار و هفتصد سال و کسری منقضی میشود مشهور و معروف است" (p. 470). The figure is quoted as printed and not converted.
- **Byas Dev's prophecy.** Byas Dev answered the raja's question about why the Pandavas fought despite their foreknowledge. He then foretold a horse, a woman in the wilderness, and a great sin if the raja obeyed her (pp. 469–470).
- The horse came. The raja mounted it and was carried to a wilderness, where he married "نازنین چهارده ساله" "بآئین و دین[OCR?] هنود[OCR?]". He made her chief rani and was ruled by her (pp. 467–469).
- At a feast she came before the Brahmans, who were struck senseless by her beauty. The raja in anger killed them all: "تمامی برهمنان را در طرفة العین بر خاک هلاک انداخت" (pp. 466–467).
- **Penance.** On Byas Dev's counsel he gave away his treasuries. He heard "کتاب مهابهارت" from "سناتن[OCR?] که شاگرد رشید بیاس دیو بود", "و ازان زمان کتاب مهابهارت در عالمیان مشهور و شائع گردید". "مدت سلطنت هشتاد و چهار سال" (p. 465).

**King-list (p. 465)**
- The author says that from Raja Asmand on he had not read the Pandava line in detail. He did not admit what he had heard orally, and gives only names with reign lengths "که از بعضے نسخه بنظر در آمده" (p. 465). The reigns as printed:
  - "راجه اسمند[OCR?] بن راجه خنبجه[OCR?]": "هشتاد و دو سال و دو ماه"
  - "راجه ادهن[OCR?]": "هشتاد و هشت سال و دو ماه"
  - "راجه مهاجسی[OCR?]": "هشتاد و یکسال و یازده ماه"
  - "راجه جسرتهه[OCR?]": "هفتاد و پنج سال و دو ماه"
  - "راجه وشنت وان[OCR?]": "هفتاد و شش سال و سه ماه"
  - "راجه اوگرسین[OCR?]": "هفتاد و هشت سال و هشت(۱) ماه" (editor's footnote variant: "ششماه[OCR?]")
- All of these are on p. 465.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- The physician restores a tree burned to ash by "فسونی که اعجاز مسیحائی" (p. 475).
- The serpent takes human form, and then the form of a tiny worm hidden in a fruit (pp. 473–475).
- The snakes are drawn into the sacrificial fire by spells. Some had horses' faces, elephants' trunks, or two to four heads, and some were one to three kos long. Their fat ran in streams (p. 471).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's source caution.** From Raja Asmand on he says the detailed history was not available to him. He rejected oral report as "شایان اعتبار ندانسته" and gives only names and reign lengths from "بعضے نسخه" (p. 465).
- **Fate.** The author repeatedly frames events as decreed. In Byas Dev's words: "از آفریدها کیست که تقدیر آفریدگار را تواند رفع کرد" (p. 470). He also writes that the arrow of fate "از هفت سپر تدبیر انسان درگذرد" (p. 473).

## Citable passages

- "این جگ راجه جنمجه[OCR?] تا حال که چهار هزار و هفتصد سال و کسری منقضی میشود مشهور و معروف است" — "This sacrifice of Raja Janamejaya is famous to this day, when four thousand seven hundred years and more have passed." (p. 470)

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk opens mid-sentence at "گستاخی شده" (p. 475), continuing from the previous chunk.
- It ends with Ugrasen's reign (p. 465). The king-list presumably continues on p. 464.
- All page joins read through high → low.

**Mapping**
- **Krishna (2 rows) is attached person-level, on a bare name.** "سری کرشن" is named only as the subject of the Bhagavata, with no site (p. 473). By the many-row person rule this takes both Krishna rows. Brief point 1 does not bar it, because no particular site is named. Weigh accordingly; the consolidator may judge this too thin.
- **Not mapped: "پاندوان" (the Pandavas; pp. 465, 470).** `panj-tirath`'s principal figure is "King Pandu (Mahabharata)", but the book names the Pandava line, not Pandu himself, and names no Peshawar site.
- **Not mapped: no rows.** None of the following has an archive row:
  - Byas Dev, Sukhdev, Sanatan[OCR?], the abid Aung[OCR?] and the dervish Astak[OCR?];
  - Sesh Nag and the serpent Bhujang[OCR?];
  - Hastinapur and the Ganga.
- **Taxila / Takshaka.** The serpent is called "بهجنگ[OCR?]" throughout. "تکشک" and any form of Taxila do not appear, so there is no toponym question.
- "ولایت شمال" (p. 472) is not located by the book. It is not taken as a province.
- Rulers take no ids (brief point 2).
- The chunk has no Punjab or Sindh place and no province-level material.

**Names and figures**
- Janamejaya is spelled three ways: "جنمجه[OCR?]" (pp. 470, 472, 473), "جنبجه[OCR?]" and "خنبجه[OCR?]" (both p. 465).
- The physician is "کنت[OCR?] دهنتر[OCR?]" (pp. 474, 475), but "کنت[OCR?] وهنتر[OCR?]" twice on p. 474.
- The 84-year reign (p. 465) is not named to a raja in its sentence. By the run of the narrative it closes the Janamejaya section.
- The "چهار هزار و هفتصد سال و کسری" (p. 470) is the author's own reckoning to his time. It is not converted.
