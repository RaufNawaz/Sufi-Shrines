# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 036 — pp. 453–464

The book reads from the highest page down, so this chunk runs from p. 464 to p. 453. It is entirely the king-lists of the Hindu rajas of Indarpat/Delhi, with no section heading of its own until the Bikramajit entry. It opens with the end of the Pandava line. Each entry is a run of "راجه X بن راجه Y" plus a reign length, from "راجه سوسین[OCR?] بن راجه اوگرسین[OCR?]" down to "راجه کهیمن[OCR?]" (pp. 464–463). Khemin is killed by his vizier Bisrwa[OCR?], and "نسل پاندوان" ends (p. 463). Three more dynasties follow, each founded by a vizier who killed his king: Bisrwa[OCR?]'s line ends with the bhang-addicted Budhmal[OCR?]/Budhal[OCR?] (pp. 463–461); Birmah[OCR?]'s line ends with Adhast[OCR?] (pp. 461–460); and Dandhar[OCR?]'s line ends with Raj Pal (pp. 460–459). Raj Pal is overthrown by "سکونت", a tributary who held land "دامنه کوه کمایون". Sakwant, an opium addict, is then defeated and killed by Bikramajit of Ujjain (pp. 459–457). The entry "راجه بکرماجیت بن گندهرپ سین در بیان ولادت راجه بکرماجیت و شرح احوال او" (p. 457) tells his birth legend after a translation of the "سنگهاسن بتیسی": Gandharb Sen, son of Indra, the donkey-husband at Dharanagri, and the iron fort (pp. 457–454). It then covers the rule of Malwa from Ujjain by the brothers Bharthari and Bikramajit, and Bharthari's queen. The chunk ends in the fruit-of-immortality story and a variant sati story, breaking off mid-sentence at "تعریف همت" (p. 453).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's sources and method.** He weighs the "اکبرنامه و دیگر تواریخ" against the "سنگهاسن بتیسی" translation and follows the latter for Bikramajit (p. 457). For the line of Bisrwa he gives names and reigns only, because details are "مفصل ظاهر نیست" (p. 463).
- **Kingship and negligence.** In a recurring moral, neglect of rule loses the throne to a vizier: Khemin (p. 463), Adhast[OCR?] (p. 460), and Budhmal[OCR?] with bhang (pp. 462–461). Pride brings a fall: Raj Pal (p. 459). Opium ruins a king: Sakwant (p. 458).
- **Essays.** The author writes on bhang. He claims to have seen bhang-eaters fall "از صاحب دولتے بمفلسی", and that the habit passes to their descendants (pp. 462–461). He also writes on opium, "کوکنار مذموم ترین کیفها است" (p. 458), and on women, in verse and prose, around the Bharthari story (p. 453).
