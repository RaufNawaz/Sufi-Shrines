# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 043 — pp. 531–541

This chunk covers the geography of seven Indian subas. It reads from p. 541 down to p. 531, because the scan runs back to front, and every page join checks. It opens mid-sentence inside a suba whose heading falls before p. 541. Its sarkars include "تانڈه", "جنت اباد", "سلهت" and "چاتگانو", and its western border is "صوبه بهار". That suba's part covers Lakhnauti, Sylhet, Ghoraghat, Kamrup, Assam, Mahachin and Chin, and closes with its size and revenue (pp. 540–541). The suba sections that follow are:
- "صوبه عشرت سرشت اودیسه" (pp. 539–540): the Jagannath temple at Parsotampur.
- "صوبه خجسته بنیاد اورنگ آباد" (pp. 538–539).
- "صوبه برار" (pp. 537–538).
- "صوبه خاندیس" (pp. 536–537).
- "صوبه مالوه" (pp. 535–536): Ujjain, Chanderi and Mandu.
- "صوبه دار الخیر اجمیر" (pp. 534–535): the tomb of Muin al-Din Chishti, Chitor and Sambhar.

The chunk ends inside "صوبه دار البرکات گجرات" (pp. 531–534): Ahmadabad, the tombs of Shah Alam Bukhari and Qutb Alam, Somnath, Dwarka and Kutch. It breaks off at the foot of p. 531, partway through Gujarat's boundaries. **There is no suba Thatta, Multan or Lahore material in this chunk. Hinglaj, Sehwan and the Manchhar lake do not appear.** Pakistan-side material comes down to two lines: a bare "سند" west of Kutch (p. 531), and "دیپالپور تابع ملتان" as suba Ajmer's western limit (p. 534). Every archive mapping below is person-level only (Krishna, Mahadev, Jagannath), and every site involved is in India.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **krishna-mandir-kabari-bazar**, **krishna-mandir-ravi-road** (the book's "سری کشن"; person-level, with both passages set in suba Gujarat):
  - The book describes Dwarka, "که ان را جگت نیز گویند", as "معبدیست مشهور". It is counted a great place of worship because "سری کشن از متهرا برامده دران مکان رفته توطن گزیده بود": Sri Krishn came out of Mathura, went there and settled (p. 531).
  - "سبهالکا", half a kos from Somnath, is "مکانی است بزرگ". There "سری کشن را از دست صیادی تیر بر پا رسیده و بر کناره دریائے سرستی زیر درخت پیپل انجهانی شدند": a hunter's arrow struck his foot, and he passed away under a pipal tree on the bank of the Sarasvati. The place is also called "پیپل" and is held to be a "بزرگ معبد" (p. 532).

- **amb-temples-amb-sharif**, **bhagnari-mandir**, **chandragup-baba-chandragup**, **katas-raj-temples**, **shahwala-teja-singh-mandir**, **shiv-mandir-chiti-ghati**, **shree-ratneshwar-mahadev-temple-karachi**, **umarkot-amarkot-shiv-mandir** (the book's "مهادیو"; person-level, with the passages set in suba Gujarat and suba Berar):
  - "در قصبه مول معبدیست منسوب بمهادیو": in qasba Mul there is a temple ascribed to Mahadev. Each year before the rains, on a fixed day, a creature "که بزبان هند سنگهه(۱) گویند" appears (the editor's footnote on the name reads [illegible]).
    - It is a little smaller than a pigeon, foul-smelling, and white and black. It settles on top of the temple, rolls about and dies.
    - That day the townspeople gather and burn perfumes. They read the coming rains from its colours: "از سیاهی باریدگی و از سفیدی خشکی تفول کنند", i.e. black means rain and white means drought (pp. 531–532).
  - Among the rivers of suba Berar: "گنگ هندوستان را بمهادیو نسبت میدهند". The Godavari ("گنگ گوتمی که ان را گوداوری گویند") is instead ascribed to Gautam, "که عابد مشهور بود", of whom wondrous tales are told (pp. 537–538).

- **jagannath-temple-sialkot** (the book's "جگناتهه"; person-level, with the temple named being in suba Orissa):
  - "بر سمت جنوب و ساحل دریائے شور در شهر پرسوتم پور تبخانه جگناتهه است که راجه اندرمن احداث نموده". The book adds: "تاریخ بنائے ان زیاده از چهار هزار سال ظاهر می شود" (p. 539).
  - A temple ascribed to the sun ("منسوب بافتاب") adjoins it (p. 539). That temple's cost and dimensions are given under "Other saints, sites and events".

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE punjab (book's words: "دیپالپور تابع ملتان"; refers to the author's time, c. 1107 AH, a Mughal unit. The book does not say whether "ملتان" means the suba, the sarkar or the city):

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Shah Alam Bukhari (شاه عالم بخاری), Ahmadabad:** "در پوره رسول اباد مزار شاه عالم بخاریست که بولایت شهرت داشتند و طوایف انام از مریدان و معتقدان انجناب است" (p. 533).
- **Qutb Alam (قطب عالم), "پدر شاه عالم بخاری", at Batwa:** "بتوه" (editor's footnote variant "بتوده") is a qasba three kos from Ahmadabad. It is "خوابگاه اکثر اولیائے", above all his tomb. A piece the size of a hand ("پارچه باندازه دست") has fallen on the tomb. Some take it for wood, some for stone and some for iron, and "شگرف داستانی برگذارند" (p. 533).
- **Khwaja Muin al-Din Chishti (خواجه معین الدین چشتی), Ajmer:**
  - Tomb: "درون شهر بر دامن کوه بر ساحل کولاب جهاره[OCR?]".
  - Family: "پور غیاث الدین حسن از زبده سادات حسینی".
  - Birth: "درسال پانصد و سی و هفت هجری در قصبه سنجر از دار سجستان". His father died when he was fifteen.
  - Teachers: the gaze of "ابراهیم قندوری[OCR?]" fell on him. He kept company with "خواجه عثمان چشتی" at "هرون تابع نیشاپور". At twenty he received grace from "شیخ عبد القادر گیلانی یعنی حضرت میران محی الدین".
  - Arrival: "درسنه پانصد و هشتاد و هشت هجری", when "سلطان شهاب الدین غوری" conquered Hindustan, he came to Delhi and reached Ajmer "بقصد عزلت".
  - Death: "روز شنبه ششم ماه رجب سنه ششصد و سی هجری".
  - The book closes: "مزار مقدس زیارت گاه خلایق است" (p. 535).
- **Gujarat (suba, not the Punjab town):**
  - Ahmadabad was founded by "سلطان احمد بن سلطان محمد بن سلطان مظفرشاه". He came to the throne "درسنه هشتصد و دوازده هجری", built the city on the Sabarmati and made it "دار السلطنت". He ruled "سی و دو سال". The city has 360 "پوره", and the book mentions "هزار مسجد و خانقاه و مینار با کتابهائے شگرف". Earlier capitals were Patan and Champaner (p. 533).
  - Other places (pp. 531–533):
    - Patan, with two forts.
    - Champaner, a fort that was once the capital.
    - Surat, a port where "قومی زردشتی کیش از فارس آمده" keep their fire-worship "بائین خویش".
    - Baglana, with seven forts.
    - Broach.
    - Sarkar Sorath, which "خانخانان اکبرشاهی" brought under control.
    - Junagadh, taken by "سلطان محمود والی گجرات".
    - Girnar, with a hill fort and "بزرگ معبد".
    - Somnath: "پرستشگاه قدیم", three kos from the sea and counted "از بزرگ ترین اماکن".
    - Mul, Dwarka (also "جگت") and Kathi (the Ahirs).
    - Kutch, famous for its Arab-bred horses.
- **Ajmer (suba):**
  - Ajmer city: Anasagar lake with "عمارات بادشاهی", and the fort "بیٹلی[OCR?]" of "راجه بیٹل[OCR?]" (p. 535).
  - A lake "بهکر[OCR?]" lies three kos from Ajmer. "بموجب کتب هندوان", no pilgrim's bathing at the other holy places bears merit until he bathes there (p. 535).
  - Chitor, with copper and other mines (p. 535). It was held by the Rana until Akbar took it (pp. 534–535).
  - The Rana: of "قوم گهلوت از نژاد نوشیروان عادل", called "سیسودها" (editor's variant "سیسودیا"). At accession "قشقه از خون ادم" is given him (p. 534).
  - The Sambhar salt lake (p. 534).
- **Malwa (suba)** (p. 536):
  - Ujjain, capital of "راجه بکرماجیت که تا حال در هندوستان سنه او مینویسند". Its river "شیپره[OCR?]" sometimes surges with milk.
  - Chanderi, with "دوازده هزار مسجد".
  - A temple at "تومن" where a drum beaten inside cannot be heard outside.
  - Mandu, with "مزارات سلاطین خلج". The dome over the tomb of "سلطان محمود پور سلطان هوشنگ" oozes water in summer, and "مردم بدو گروند".
  - Dhar, once the capital of "راجه بهوج".

- **Khandesh (suba)** (p. 537):
  - Burhanpur.
  - "چانگ(۱) دیو" (editor's variant "چاپک[OCR?] دیو"), at the Tapti–Purna confluence, called "چکر تیرتهه".
  - The fort of Asir, taken through "شیخ ابوالفضل علامی" under Akbar.
  - The country was given to "شاهزاده دانیال" and renamed "داندیس" (printed "بداندیس"). It had earlier been named after "غریب خان".
- **Berar (suba)** (p. 538): the Hiragarh diamond mine, a well and a spring that turn things to stone, and the "کرک نات" fowl.
- **Aurangabad (suba)** (p. 539):
  - The city was "دهارانگری", then "دیوگیر". "سلطان محمد فخر الدین جونا والی دهلی" renamed it Daulatabad.
  - It was regained "بعد سه صد سال" under Shah Jahan.
  - Aurangzeb laid out Aurangabad as prince at "قصبه کهرکی" (editor's variant "کرکی").
- **Orissa (suba)** (p. 539):

  - "تریا راج", a neighbouring country run by women.

  - Sylhet, Ghoraghat, and the tides at Bagla.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Krishna dies from a hunter's arrow under the pipal on the Sarasvati, half a kos from Somnath (p. 532). The book states this without "گویند".
- "مشهور است که", some five thousand years ago ("قریب پنجهزار سال"), "پنجاه و شش کرور ادم از قوم جادوان" sank between the Sarasvati and the Hiran (p. 532).
- At Mahadev's temple at Mul, a creature dies each year and foretells the rains (pp. 531–532).
- An object fallen on Qutb Alam's tomb at Batwa is variously taken for wood, stone or iron, "شگرف داستانی برگذارند" (p. 533).
- The Shipra at Ujjain surges with milk, "بقدرت الهی بارها چنین بظهور امده" (p. 536).
- The dome of Sultan Mahmud son of Hoshang at Mandu oozes water in summer (p. 536).
- Kutch's Arab horses descend from a wrecked merchant's hundred horses, "گویند" (p. 531).
- Alexander's ("سلطان سکندر رومی") talismanic hand on the sea, and the sorcery of Kamrup, "شگرف داستانها برگذارند" (pp. 540–541).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author names his sources for Gujarat: "از تواریخ ولایت گجرات خصوص از تاریخ سلطان بهادر شاه والی انولایت" (p. 534). For Aurangabad he cites "از بعضی تواریخ" (p. 539), and for the Ajmer lake "بموجب کتب هندوان" (p. 535).
- On Ujjain's former size he reports rather than asserts: "بسیار از بسیار نوشته اند" (p. 536).
- He explains political unrest by terrain: the Rajputs' rebelliousness comes "از ریگستان" (p. 534), and Sorath's people "از افزونی درخت زار و درهم پیچیدگی کوهستان طریقه گردن کشی دارند" (p. 532).

## Doubts

- **The expected content is not here.** The chunk holds no suba Thatta material: no Hinglaj, Sehwan, Manchhar, Thatta, Bhakkar or Sukkur. Reading high to low, the chunk runs from the end of the suba before Orissa (p. 541) down to suba Gujarat (p. 531). Gujarat breaks off at the foot of p. 531 ("... جنوبی کوه بهر جا پیوستگی دارد"). By the book's back-to-front order, Thatta and the other western subas would lie at p. 530 and below, which this chunk does not reach.
- **Page joins.** Every join checks high to low: 541→540 ("جمیع اموال / باو مدفون کنند"), 540→539 ("کیوزه / صحرا صحرا"), 539→538, 538→537, 537→536 (the Khandesh revenue figure), 536→535, 535→534 (Akbar and Chitor), 534→533 ("بمطالعه / درامده"), 533→532 ("سورت از نامور / بنادر"), and 532→531 (the Mul creature). The opening words on p. 541 ("ازین دیار براید و سه گونه می باشد") continue from p. 542. Their subject, from context ("خصیتین", "خواجه سرایان"), is eunuchs.
- **All 11 mappings are person-level.** They apply the protocol's person rule and the many-row person rule (Krishna 2, Shiva 8, Jagannath 1).
  - Every site the book names in these passages is in India: Dwarka, "سبهالکا" near Somnath, qasba Mul, the Godavari in Berar, and Parsotampur in Orissa. None is an archive site.
  - The coordinator asked for the Indian subas to stay under "Other". I mapped anyway because the person rule is ruled and is not to be re-litigated. Weigh or drop these at consolidation.
  - The Mahadev evidence is a temple dedication and a river attribution, not biography.
- **The sun temple's figures are ambiguous** (p. 539). The "خراج دوازده ساله" and the wall dimensions follow "پیوست ان تبخانه ایست منسوب بافتاب" grammatically, so they are recorded for the sun temple, not for the Jagannath temple.
- **Province bullets:**
  - Both are province-level. Each is a Mughal unit in the author's time, not the modern province's boundaries; it is the book's own unit, so weigh accordingly.
  - "سند" (p. 531) is a bare word. The book does not say whether it means the country or the river, and it does not use "صوبه تهته".
  - "دیپالپور تابع ملتان" (p. 534) is about suba Ajmer's western limit and about Dipalpur's attachment, not about Multan city. It is therefore not attached to the 8 Multan city rows under ruling (a). The book does not say whether "ملتان" here is the suba, the sarkar or the city.
- **Gujarat vs Gujrat:** every "گجرات" in this chunk is suba Gujarat. These are "صوبه دار البرکات گجرات" (p. 534), Ajmer's southern border "گجرات احمد آباد" (p. 534), Malwa's western border (p. 535) and "سلطان محمود والی گجرات" (p. 532). None is the Punjab town, so `shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai` is not mapped.
- **Name traps not mapped:**
  - "شاه عالم بخاری" and "قطب عالم" (p. 533) are placed by the book at Ahmadabad and Batwa. The book does not connect them to Uch, so neither Uch Bukhari row is mapped.
  - "حضرت جلال الدین محمد اکبر بادشاه" (pp. 535, 537) is Akbar, not Jalaluddin Bukhari.
  - "Shah Alam" here is not Lahore's Shah Alam Market (darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz).
  - "سلطان محمود والی گجرات" (p. 532) and "سلطان محمود پور سلطان هوشنگ" (p. 536) are not Mahmud of Ghazni.
  - "بهکر[OCR?]" (p. 535) is a lake three kos from Ajmer, not Bhakkar on the Indus. It is flagged, and there is no row either way. I offer no reading.
  - "سنده" (p. 536) is listed among the rivers of suba Malwa and is not taken as Sind.
- **The suba of pp. 540–541 is not named in this chunk.** It is identified here only by its sarkars ("تانڈه", "جنت اباد", "سلهت", "چاتگانو" …) and its western border "صوبه بهار". Its heading falls before p. 541.
