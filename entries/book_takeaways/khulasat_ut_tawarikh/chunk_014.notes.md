# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 014 — pp. 170–182

The book reads from the highest page down. The chunk falls in the reign that the brief's map gives to Akbar, though the chunk itself never names the emperor. It opens mid-sentence with the author's description of the Kashmiris at the end of a Kashmir visit. The return follows: through Pakhli[OCR?] and Damtur[OCR?] to Hasan Abdal, on to Kabul, and back by Dakka[OCR?] and Rohtas, where an elephant threw the emperor (pp. 181–182). Next come "در بیان رحلت راجہ تودرمل دیوان اعلی" (pp. 179–181: Todar Mal's death, life and revenue reforms), "نہضت موکب مقدس مرتبہ دویم بسیر کشمیر بے نظیر" (pp. 177–179: the Kashmir visit of regnal year 37 and Yadgar's revolt), "نہضت موکب والا بسر کشمیر جنت تاثیر مرتبہ سوم" (p. 177: the visit of year 42) and "در بیان مقرر گردیدن پرگنہ گجرات و امن آباد" (pp. 176–177: the founding of the parganas of Gujrat, "Herat" at Kunjah and Aminabad). After "در بیان تسخیر ولایت اودیسہ[OCR?]" (p. 176) and "در بیان تسخیر ولایت قندہار" (pp. 175–176) comes the Sindh material. "در بیان تسخیر ممالک تهته و آمدن جانی بیگ" (pp. 174–175) covers the taking of Sehwan fort, the siege of Jani Beg at Nasirpur[OCR?], the plague and a dervishes' dream, and Jani Beg's submission. A digression drawn from "تاریخ سلطان بهادر شاه[OCR?]" follows on the rulers of "ولایت سنده": the Soomras, the Samma Jams, the Arghuns and the Tarkhans (pp. 171–174). Then "در بیان تسخیر ولایت بهکر" (p. 171) gives the famine siege of Bhakkar. The chunk ends with "در بیان تادیب زمینداران کوهستان پنجاب" (p. 170), which includes a martyr's tomb near Kalanaur that still receives vows, and breaks off mid-sentence.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **lal-shahbaz-qalandar**, **shrine-of-lakhi-shah-saddar** (the book's "سهوان", in "قلعه سهوان" and "ولایت سهوان"):
  - **First capture, under the Sammas.** Under "جام نظام الدین که بجام نندا مشهور است", "میرزا شاه بیگ حاکم قندهار" sent Mirza Isa Tarkhan against the Jam and then came himself. He took Bhakkar fort, "و بعد ازان قلعه سهوان را نیز گرفته بقندهار معاودت کرد": after that he also took the fort of Sehwan and went back to Qandahar (p. 172).
  - **The Khankhanan's Thatta campaign.** "راول[OCR?] بهیم مرزبان ولایت جیسلمیر" and "دلیپ[OCR?] پسر رائے راے سنگه بیکانیر" joined him. "اولا قلعه سهوان را مفتوح نموده روانه پیشتر شدند": they took the fort of Sehwan first, then went on against Jani Beg (p. 175).
  - **The peace.** Mirza Jani Beg could not hold out, and "صلح در میان آورده ولایت سهوان داخل ممالک محروسه نموده": he made peace and brought the territory of Sehwan into the empire. In the same settlement he gave his daughter to Mirza Iraj, the Khankhanan's son (p. 174).
  - The chunk names no saint, tomb or shrine at Sehwan.

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE sindh.
  - Book's words: "ولایت سنده" (pp. 171, 172, 173), "سلاطین ولایت سنده" (p. 173), "ممالک تهته" (heading, p. 175), "ولایت تهتهه" (p. 175), and "درین دیار" / "مردم این ملک" (p. 174).
  - Refers to: as the book states, from rule by the descendants of "بنی تمیم[OCR?] انصاری" and the Soomras, through the Sammas from "سنه ۷۳۵[OCR?]" and the Arghuns and Tarkhans, to the annexation in "سال سی و هشتم مطابق سنه هزار و یک[OCR?] هجری". The book does not call it a suba in this chunk.
  - **Source.** "پوشیده نماند که در تاریخ سلطان بهادر شاه[OCR?] احوال سلاطین ولایت سنده چنان بقلم آورده اند" (pp. 173–174).
  - **Earliest rulers.** The descendants of "بنی تمیم[OCR?] انصاری" ruled "یکصد و سی و نه سال". Then the "سومرگان" (Soomras) took over [illegible]: among the zamindars of that region they stood out for strength and numbers of followers. "پانصد سال در خانواده سومرگان حکومت ماند". Rule then passed to the "سمه گان[OCR?]" (Sammas) (p. 173).
  - **The title "جام".** The first Samma ruler was "جام انزار". The word "جام", which they apply to their "مقدم و کلانتر" (headman and chief), is traced "از [illegible] و جمشید" (p. 173).
  - **The Delhi sultan.** In the time of "جام بانی تهتهه[OCR?] بن جام انزار", "سلطان فیروز شاه فرمان روائے دهلی" marched against the Jam three times. He finally took "ولایت سنده" and brought the Jam to Delhi. Pleased with his service, he restored the country to him (p. 173).
  - **The Samma Jams and their reigns, as printed** (pp. 172–173):
    - Jam Anzar: from "سنه ۷۳۵[OCR?]", "[illegible] سال و شش ماه".
    - Jam Junan[OCR?], his brother: 14 years.
    - Jam Bani Thatta[OCR?], son of Anzar: 15 years.
    - Jam Tamachi[OCR?], his brother: 13 years.
    - Jam Salahuddin: 11 years and some months.
    - Jam Nizamuddin, son of Salahuddin: 2 years and some months.
    - Jam Ali Sher: 6 years and some months.
    - Jam Karan[OCR?], son of Tamachi: "یک و نیم روز".
    - Jam Fath Khan, son of Sikandar: 15 years. The notables raised him "چون آن ولایت از حاکم خالی گردید".
    - Jam Tughluq, son of Sikandar: "هیزده[OCR?]" years.

    - Jam Sikandar, son of Fath Khan: 1 year 6 months.
    - Jam Sanjar: 8 years and some months.
    - Jam Nizamuddin, "که بجام نندا مشهور است": 62 years.

  - **The end of the Sammas.**
    - Jam Salahuddin, a kinsman of Jam Firuz whose sister was married to Sultan Muzaffar of Gujarat, twice raised armies from Gujarat and won.
    - Jam Firuz called in Mirza Shah Beg Arghun of Qandahar. Salahuddin was killed, and "حکومت ولایت سنده بلاشرکت بجام فیروز تعلق گرفت".
    - "در سنه ۹۲۷[OCR?] میرزا شاه بیگ ارغون از قندهار لشکر کشیده تهته را متصرف شد". Jam Firuz went to Gujarat, served Sultan Bahadur Shah, gave him his daughter in marriage and died there. "از آنجا حکومت سلسله سمه گان منقطع گردید" (p. 172).
  - **Arghuns and Tarkhans** (p. 171):
    - Mirza Shah Beg "بقوت و شجاعت ولایت سنده متصرف گشت" and died "سنه ۹۳۰[OCR?]". His rule is given as "سه سال".
    - Mirza Shah Husain refortified Bhakkar and took Multan. He died "سنه ۹۶۲[OCR?]", after 32 years.
    - "میرزا عیسی ترخان از امراے میرزا شاه بیگ در تهته و سلطان محمود در بهکر دم استقلال میزدند": both claimed independence, sometimes at peace and sometimes at war. Isa Tarkhan died "سنه ۹۶۵[OCR?]", after 13 years.
    - Mirza Muhammad Baqi overcame his brother Khan Baba, kept up his father's dealings with Sultan Mahmud of Bhakkar, and ruled "هیزده[OCR?] سال".
    - Mirza Jani Beg, "ابن پاینده محمد بن محمد باقی", came to court with the Khankhanan "در سنه هزار و یک[OCR?] هجری", and "آن ولایت داخل ممالک محروسه گردید". He had ruled eight years.
  - **The conquest.**
    - The order was "نخستین انتزاع ولایت تهتهه پیش نهاد همت ساز و بعد ازان بتسخیر قندهار پرداز" (p. 175).
    - "در آن نواحی وبائے عظیم روے داد" (a great plague broke out in that region). Dervishes dreamt that the plague came from "سرتابی مردم این ملک" (the disobedience of the people of this land), and everyone made vows. See Legends and Practices (p. 174).
    - Jani Beg was given "منصب سه هزاری و جاگیر تهتهه". "لاهری بندر که از طرف تهتهه واقع است بخالصه شریفه مقرر گشت" (p. 174).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**A martyr's tomb near Kalanaur (outside Pakistan per the brief, so no row)**
- Raja Basu raided khalisa villages near "پتهان[OCR?]". Taj Khan "بن هندال میرزا" was sent against him, and after him Chin Qilich Khan.
- As the troops were pitching camp, Basu appeared, and "جمیل بیگ پسر تاج خان" fought "کار رستمانه" and "بدرجه شهادت رسیده" (p. 170).
- "نعش آن مغفور ... در حوالی خطه دل کشاے کلانور بخاک سپردند و بر مزارش عمارت عالی ساختند که تا حال قایم است و طوایف انام نذورات می آرند و بحکمت ایزدی مرادات مردم که در آن مکان نذر قبول میکنند بحصول می انجامد" (p. 170). That is: his body was buried near Kalanaur, and a lofty building was raised over his grave that still stands. All kinds of people bring offerings, and by God's wisdom the wishes of those who make vows there are fulfilled.

**Sindh places with no row**
- **Bhakkar ("بهکر"), the fort.**
  - Under Jam Nanda, Shah Beg besieged the fort, "که در آن ایام بآئین استحکام نبود" (not then properly fortified), and took it quickly (p. 172).
  - Shah Husain "قلعه بهکر را بتجدید استحکام داد" (refortified it) (p. 171). Sultan Mahmud held Bhakkar independently in the Tarkhan period (p. 171).
- **Bhakkar: the conquest, "در بیان تسخیر ولایت بهکر"** (p. 171).
  - "پیش از آنکه تهته مفتوح شود در سال نوزدهم جلوس والا", Muhibb Ali Khan and Mujahid Khan were sent to take Bhakkar and besieged it.
  - The siege dragged on. "قحط عظیم و وبائے بسیار در قلعه روے داد" (a great famine and much plague in the fort), and many of the garrison died. "هر که پوست درخت سرس جوشانیده میخورد ازو باسلامت می ماند": whoever boiled and ate the bark of the "سرس" tree survived.
  - Sultan Mahmud offered to give up the fort "بپیشکش شاهزاده والا گهر" if Muhibb Ali Khan withdrew. He died a natural death before an answer came, and the two khans brought Bhakkar into the empire "در سنه ۹۸۹[OCR?]".
- **Thatta ("تهته" / "تهتهه"), the town and the campaign.**
  - Shah Beg took Thatta in "۹۲۷[OCR?]" (p. 172).
  - Jani Beg, "والی تهتهه", entrenched at "نصیرپور[OCR?] که یک جانب دریاے سنده و جانب دیگر رود خانها داشت". There he built a "قلعه گلین[OCR?]" (mud fort) and dug a ditch. The Khankhanan's long siege ran short of grain (p. 175).
  - Grain boats and reinforcements came. The Khankhanan moved "بر سر تهتهه", sent forces in several directions and camped "در قصبه جام", with fighting every day (p. 174).
  - "دهارو[OCR?] پسر راجه تودرمل" was killed by a spear wound in the forehead (p. 174).
  - Jani Beg was beaten and fled, and the Khankhanan "قلعه اساس نهاده میرزا را منهدم گردانید" (pulled down the fort the Mirza had built) (p. 174).
  - Jani Beg agreed to come to court after the rains, and the Khankhanan brought him "در اواسط سال سی و هشتم مطابق سنه هزار و یک[OCR?] هجری" (p. 174).
- **Lahari Bandar ("لاهری بندر"),** "که از طرف تهتهه واقع است": made khalisa (p. 174).
- **The Indus ("دریاے سنده")** (p. 175).

**Punjab and route places with no row**
- **Kunjah and pargana "Herat".** "در [illegible] کہ کنجاہ مشہور است پرگنہ ہرات مقرر کردند". The book says ("گویند") that the emperor hunted a large deer near Kunjah and remarked that as Herat in Iran breeds matchless horses, this land breeds matchless deer, and so the pargana came to be called Herat (p. 176).
- **Rohtas ("رہتاس").** On the way back from Kabul, "بعد رسیدن در رہتاس", the emperor mounted an elephant, "پیل نول[OCR?] نگار[OCR?] [illegible] کہ در جوش مستی بود" (in musth). It ran at a female elephant and threw him; he lay unconscious a long time, a vein of his right arm was opened, and he recovered (pp. 181–182). Not mapped: see Doubts.
- **The road from Kashmir.** "براه پکھلی[OCR?] و دمتور[OCR?]", a road described at length as hard going (p. 182). Also the manzil "دکہ[OCR?]", where the emperor fell from his horse and spent six days in bed (p. 182).
- **Rivers.** The Chenab, crossed on the Kashmir journeys (pp. 176–178), and the Ravi (p. 179).
- **Hill places on p. 170.** "پتهان[OCR?]", and the hill rajas of Nagarkot, "جمهون" (Jammu), "مو[OCR?]", "گوالیار[OCR?]", Lakhanpur, "جبروته[OCR?]" and "مانکوت[OCR?]". Also Rudra Singh[OCR?] of Kumaon, presented through "مهترا داس[OCR?] عامل بریلی" (p. 170). Raja Man of Jammu was defeated and hid "در شعاب جبال" (p. 170).

**Kashmir (the valley, no row) and Kabul (outside Pakistan)**
- **End of the first visit.**
  - The author describes the Kashmiris' woollen "پتو", worn unwashed until it tears, and their "ناپاکی" (see Arguments) (p. 182).
  - Id al-Fitr was kept in Kashmir. At Mirza Yusuf Khan's intercession, "یعقوب پسر یوسف خان حاکم آنجا" was pardoned and given the emperor's shoes, which he bound on his head (p. 182).
- **Second visit (year 37).**
  - Qazi Nurullah had been sent to assess "جمیع محصول محال کشمیر". The Kashmiris, fearing higher demands, rebelled and won over Yadgar, whom Mirza Yusuf Khan had left as his deputy. Yadgar struck "سکه و خطبه بنام خود" (pp. 178–179).
  - Yadgar fought at "کوه کپرتل[OCR?]" and fled to "بیرپور[OCR?]". Yusuf Khan's men killed him there, and his head reached the emperor "در منزل بہنبر[OCR?]" (p. 178).
  - Then came the saffron fields and the lamp festival. Kashmir was left to Mirza Yusuf Khan at the eldest prince's request (pp. 177–178).
- **Third visit (year 42).** The pretender (see the Aminabad bullet) was dealt with. There was a lamp festival with "دو ہزار کشتی" on "آب دل" (p. 177).
- **Kabul.**
  - "قاسم خان صوبہ دار آنجا" was ordered to build a garden and buildings "در گذر گاه متصل شہر کہ حضرت ظہیر الدین محمد بابر پادشاه و ہندال میرزا و محمد حکیم میرزا آسوده اند": at the place by the city where Babur, Hindal Mirza and Muhammad Hakim Mirza lie buried (p. 182).
  - "خان دوران عرف شاه بیگ خان", governor of "صوبه کابل", was given Qandahar (p. 175).

**Outside Pakistan, briefly**
- **Orissa** (p. 176). Isa Khan succeeded Qutlu[OCR?] Afghan, and Raja Man Singh conquered the country. "جگناتہ[OCR?]" entered the empire and 150 elephants were sent to court. "در سال سی و ہفتم جلوس والا مطابق سنہ ہزار[OCR?] ہجری" the whole of Orissa "کہ بر ساحل دریاے شور است" was annexed.
- **Qandahar** (pp. 175–176). Muzaffar Husain Mirza and Rustam Mirza quarrelled with Iran. Rustam, beaten by his brother, came to court with four sons. Abu Said Mirza, Bahram Mirza and Muzaffar Husain Mirza followed, and "در آن تاریخ قندهار داخل ممالک محروسه شد".
- **Other places.** Jaisalmer and Bikaner (p. 175). The Gujarat sultans Muzaffar and Bahadur Shah, and Delhi under Firuz Shah (pp. 172–173). Gujarat and Bengal, where Todar Mal fought (p. 181). Kumaon and Bareilly (p. 170).

**People**
- **Raja Todar Mal, "دیوان اعلی"** (pp. 179–181).
  - His father died while he was a child, and his widowed mother raised him "در کمال افلاس و تنگدستی". He entered service among the royal scribes and rose to be "صاحب کوس و علم".
  - He fought in Gujarat and Bengal and rose to "وزارت کل". "در سال بیست و پنجم جلوس والا وکیل مطلق و وزیر اعظم گردید".
  - He died at Lahore (see the Lahore bullet), and the news reached the emperor on the way back from Kabul. After his death "میرزا خانخانان" received "منصب وکالت" (p. 179). For his reforms see Practices.
- **The Khankhanan** ("میرزا جان[OCR?] خانخانان", p. 176). Conqueror of Thatta; the author praises him (p. 174).
- **Others.** Abul Fazl, and "مقرب خان عرف شیخ بہینا[OCR?] جراح", who treated the emperor's thigh wound from a deer's horn; he healed "بعد یک ماه و ہفت روز" (p. 181). Mir Fathullah Shirazi and Hakim Abul Fath Gilani, buried at Hasan Abdal (p. 182). Zain Khan Koka, Raja Basu, Husain Beg Shaikh Umari and Jamil Beg (p. 170). Muhibb Ali Khan and Mujahid Khan (p. 171).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Vows at a martyr's tomb.** At Jamil Beg's tomb near Kalanaur, "طوایف انام نذورات می آرند" and vows made there are fulfilled. The author says the building "تا حال قایم است" (p. 170). This is outside Pakistan, with no row.
- **Collective vows in the Thatta country.** When the dervishes' dream spread, "همه کس باندازه حال نذری بر بست": everyone, each according to his means, vowed that victory would go to the imperial army and that coin and khutba would be in the emperor's name (p. 174).
- **Famine food at Bhakkar.** Boiled bark of the "سرس" tree (p. 171).
- **Todar Mal's revenue and record system** (pp. 179–180). The author says it is still followed.
  - Before him, records were kept by "ہندوان ہندوی نویس" "بخط ہندوی". He introduced a "دفتر فارسی بقوانین اہل ایران".
  - All pargana lands were brought "بقید ضبط", and the area of each village of each pargana was entered in the imperial register.
  - The rope jarib, "پنجاه و پنج ذرعی", which varied between wet and dry, was replaced by a "جریب شصت ذرعی" of "سنے[OCR?]" joined with iron links.
  - "پرگنات مخلوطہ را سرکار و چند سرکار را صوبہ مقرر ساخت": parganas were grouped into sarkars, and sarkars into subas.
  - "نقدی روپیہ چہل دام": the rupee was set at forty dams.
  - "بر کرور دام عامل[OCR?] نصب ساخت کہ بزبان عرف[OCR?] آن را کرورے گویند": an amil was placed over each crore of dams, called in common speech a karori.
  - "داغ اسپ" (branding of horses) was enforced. The author notes that Alauddin Khalji and Sher Shah had devised it "اما رواج نیافتہ بود".
  - There were seven daily chaukis, seven chauki-navis and seven waqia-navis (pp. 179–180).
- **Court institutions** (p. 179).
  - Four thousand horsemen at court "کہ آن فریق را بزبان وقت احدی گویند", with their own daroga.
  - Thousands of slaves, bought or taken "دارالحرب", were freed and given the title "چیلی", "کہ بندہاے خدا را بنده و غلام گفتن سزاوار نیست".
- **Karoris and new parganas.**
  - The karori Muhammad Beg was punished for oppressing a tappa under Sialkot (p. 177).
  - The karori[OCR?] Muhammad Amin founded the qasba and pargana of Aminabad (p. 176).
  - The parganas of Gujrat (with a "قلعچہ و قصبہ") and Herat at Kunjah were carved out of Sialkot land (p. 176).
- **Jagirs, mansabs and khalisa.**
  - Jani Beg: "منصب سه هزاری و جاگیر تهتهه". Lahari Bandar was made khalisa (p. 174).
  - Rustam Mirza: mansab five thousand, with the jagir of Multan and Baluchistan, "زیاده از محصول قندهار" (p. 175).
  - The Khankhanan's jagir lay "در میان ملتان و بهکر" (p. 175).
  - "دیهات خالصه شریفه" near Pathan[OCR?] were raided by Raja Basu (p. 170).
- **Revenue.**
  - Kashmir: "جمع تمام صوبہ کشمیر سی و یک لک خروار" (p. 177).
  - Kabul: "تا ہشت سال ہشتم حصہ از خراج مقرری" was remitted to the cultivators (p. 182).
  - After the elephant accident, "رعیت از مال گذاری دست باز کشیدند" until the court reached Lahore (p. 181).
- **Building.**
  - A garden laid out at Hasan Abdal (p. 182).
  - A garden and buildings at the Kabul burial place of Babur, Hindal and Muhammad Hakim (p. 182).
  - "عمارت عالی" over Jamil Beg's grave (p. 170).
- **Grain by river.** Grain boats went from Lahore and Multan to the army in Sindh (p. 174).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Jamil Beg's tomb near Kalanaur.** "بحکمت ایزدی مرادات مردم که در آن مکان نذر قبول میکنند بحصول می انجامد" (p. 170).
- **The dervishes' dream in the Thatta country.** "بعضے درویشان صاحب حال" saw in dreams that "سکه و خطبه بنام نامی حضرت خدیو جهان درین دیار می شود", and that the plague was due to "سرتابی مردم این ملک" (p. 174).
- **The verse at the Ravi.** Crossing the Ravi below Lahore fort, the emperor asked about the verse "کلاه خسروی و تاج شاہی / بہر گل کی رسد". "قضا را در ہمان روز" (as fate had it, that same day) Yadgar revolted in Kashmir, though the court knew nothing of it (p. 179).
- **The prediction of Yadgar's death.** The emperor foretold that "این لولی بچہ بمجرد[OCR?] بر آمدن سہیل کشتہ خواہد شد". He was killed "بمجرد[OCR?] بر آمدن ستاره یمانی", as "بر زبان الہام ترجمان رفتہ بود" (p. 178).
- **"از غرایب".** When Yadgar struck coin in his own name he fell into a shaking fever, and a splinter of steel flew into the eye of his seal-engraver (p. 178).
- **Abul Fazl's omen.** He took an omen "از دیوان لسان الغیب" and drew "آن خوش خبر کجاست کزین فتح مژده داد" (p. 178).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On Todar Mal** (pp. 179–181). The author's long praise includes "در دقایق سیاق و حقایق حساب بے نظیر". His rules "از او یادگار است در ہندوستان کہ تا حال بر ہمان قواعد و ضوابط در ممالک محروسہ بعمل می در آید". Later wazirs tried, "و تا حال میکوشند", to replace them, and have failed (p. 179).
- **On the Kashmiris** (p. 182). The author's own hostile judgement: their "ناپاکی", a proverb about a Kashmiri who does not wash, and "بد طینتی و نفاق سرشتی انہا". He adds a verse comparing them to the scorpion and the snake.
- **On the Khankhanan** (p. 174). He bore hardships, showed courage, wisdom and "فنون تدابیر صایبه", and won praise from the emperor and his contemporaries.
- **Sources.**
  - The Sind digression is attributed to "تاریخ سلطان بهادر شاه[OCR?]" (pp. 173–174).
  - The 1918 editor's footnotes check reign lengths against "آئین اکبری", "تاریخ فرشته" and "طبقات اکبری" (pp. 172–173). They also cite "اکبر نامہ" (p. 177) and "اقبال نامہ[OCR?]" (p. 182).
- **The author's present.** Jamil Beg's tomb "تا حال قایم است" (p. 170). Aminabad was founded "درین ولا[OCR?]" (p. 176). Todar Mal's rules are in force "تا حال" (pp. 179–180).

## Citable passages

- "بر مزارش عمارت عالی ساختند که تا حال قایم است و طوایف انام نذورات می آرند" — "Over his grave they raised a lofty building that stands to this day, and all kinds of people bring offerings." (p. 170)
- "بعضے درویشان صاحب حال در خواب دیدند که سکه و خطبه بنام نامی حضرت خدیو جهان درین دیار می شود" — "Certain dervishes of spiritual state saw in dreams that coin and khutba would be struck in the emperor's name in this land." (p. 174)

- "هر که پوست درخت سرس جوشانیده میخورد ازو باسلامت می ماند" — "Whoever boiled and ate the bark of the sirs tree survived [the siege of Bhakkar]." (p. 171)

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 182 down to p. 170.
  - It opens mid-sentence at "کرتہ پشمین است کہ عبارت از پتو باشد" (p. 182), continuing the previous chunk's Kashmir description.
  - It ends mid-sentence at "و راجه باسو" (p. 170).
- Two page joins are rough:
  - pp. 176→175 runs through an [illegible] ("و حکم شد کہ [illegible]" / "گردد اگر کلان تران بلوچستان").
  - pp. 175→174 ("در تنگی" / "یافت") reads as if something is missing.
  - Neither is repaired.
- A library stamp at the foot of p. 176 is not text.
- **The arrangement is topical, not chronological.** In reading order: the end of the first Kashmir visit and the return via Kabul (p. 182); Todar Mal; the visits of year 37 (p. 179) and year 42 (p. 177); Orissa (year 37); Qandahar; Thatta (year 38); the Sind history going back to "۷۳۵[OCR?]"; Bhakkar (year 19); then the hill zamindars. Nothing has been reordered or dated beyond what is printed.
- **The transcription convention changes at p. 176.** pp. 170–175 use Persian ه / ک ("لاهور", "تهته"). pp. 176–182 use Urdu-style ہ, ھ, ٹ, ڑ ("لاہور", "پکھلی", "کروڑے"). Quotations are kept as printed.
- **The emperor is never named.** He is "خاقان زمان", "حضرت خدیو جهان", "حضرت خدیو گیهان" or "آن حضرت"; calling him Akbar rests only on the brief's map.
  - On p. 176, "درین ولا[OCR?] در عہد خلافت حضرت خدیو گیہان" appears to mean the author's own time. The chunk 006 notes use the same epithet for the emperor of that (Aurangzeb) section.
  - On p. 175, "حضرت خدیو گیهان در لاهور" is the emperor of the narrative.
  - Not resolved.
