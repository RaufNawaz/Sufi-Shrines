# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 023 — pp. 286–297

The book reads from the highest page down, and in that order the chunk opens mid-sentence inside the book's account of the rulers of Multan, whose opening and heading lie on an earlier page outside this chunk (p. 297). It gives Multan left without a ruler under yearly Mughul raids from Kabul, the raising of Shaikh Yusuf Qureshi, a descendant of Bahauddin Zakariya, and his overthrow by the Langah chief Rai Sanhra[OCR?]. Then come the reigns headed "سلطان قطب الدین عرف رائے سنسهره[OCR?] لنگاه", "سلطان حسین بن سلطان قطب الدین", "سلطان محمود بن فیروز شاه بن سلطان حسین", "سلطان حسین بن سلطان محمود" and "میرزا شاه حسین ارغون", ending with Kamran Mirza's taking of Multan (pp. 294–297). With no new heading the narrative returns to Babur: Humayun's illness at Agra, Babur's offering of himself for his son, and his death and burial at Kabul (pp. 293–294). Under "حضرت نصیر الدین محمد همایون بادشاه بن ظهیر الدین محمد بابر بادشاه" (p. 293) follow Mir Khalifa's plan, Humayun's accession, Kalinjar and Jaunpur, the Gujarat war with Sultan Bahadur, Sher Khan's rise in the east and Bengal, the rout on the Ganges and the water-carrier Nizam, Qannauj, the brothers' council at Lahore, Kamran's departure for Kabul and Mirza Haidar's conquest of Kashmir (pp. 286–293). The chunk ends mid-sentence at "و معاند غالب است" (p. 286).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (the book's "مخدوم العالم شیخ بهاؤالدین ذکریا قدس الله سره"):
  - He is named only as the ancestor of Shaikh Yusuf Qureshi, whom the people of Multan raised to rule. The text reads: "شیخ یوسف قریشی را که از اولاد شناور دریاۓ حقیقت عارف معارف طریقت سر حلقه اولیاۓ مخدوم العالم شیخ بهاؤالدین ذکریا قدس الله سره بود". The epithets mean "swimmer of the ocean of reality, knower of the gnosis of the path, head of the circle of saints" (p. 297).
  - The book's verdict on the descendant's rule: "درویشی را بجهانبانی همرنگ و ولایت را بسلطنت هم سنگ گردانید و قلوب رعایا و برایا بخود رام ساخت". He made dervishhood one colour with world-rule and *wilayat* equal in weight with kingship, and won the subjects' hearts (p. 297).
  - The family's fortunes in this chunk:
    - Shaikh Yusuf struck coin and read the khutba in his own name "در ۸۵۵ [OCR?]" (p. 297).
    - He reigned "دو سال" before Rai Sanhra[OCR?] seized him (p. 296).
    - He escaped to Sultan Bahlul Lodi at Delhi, who married his own daughter to "شیخ عبد الله خلف شیخ مذکور", the Shaikh's son (p. 296).
    - Bahlul's army was then sent against Multan "بتحریک شیخ یوسف" (p. 296).

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE punjab. Book's words: "ولایت ملتان" (pp. 296–297), "آن ولایت" (p. 294), "پنجاب" (pp. 295–296), "ولایت لاهور" (p. 286). Refers to the Langah period, from Shaikh Yusuf's "۸۵۵ [OCR?]" to Kamran Mirza's possession "لغایت سنه ۹۳۶[OCR?]", and to Humayun's war with Sher Khan (946[OCR?]), as the book dates them. The unit is the book's own "ولایت", not a Mughal suba.
  - **Langah expansion.** Sultan Husain took "قلعه شور را از تصرف غازیخان" and Chiniot (see langer-makhdoom), and "در سرعت[OCR?] اوقات[OCR?] تا کروکوت[OCR?] و دهنکوت متصرف شد" (p. 296).
  - **Punjab's governor.** "تاتار خان حاکم پنجاب" was sent by Sultan Bahlul Lodi, with Bahlul's son, against Sultan Husain (p. 296).
  - **Baluch settlement.**
    - "ملک سهراب دودائی[OCR?] پدر اسمعیل خان و فتح خان" came "با قوم و قبیله خود از نواحی کچ و مکران" to Sultan Husain. The Sultan gave "ملک[OCR?] سهراب" in jagir "از قلعه کوت کردز[OCR?] تا دهنکوت[OCR?]" (p. 295).
    - "الموع[OCR?] بسیار" of Baluch followed. "بقیه ولایت کنار دریای سند به بلوچان مقرر گردید رفته رفته از سیت پور[OCR?] تا دهنکوت[OCR?] به بلوچان قرار یافت چنانچه ازان ایام آن ولایت در تصرف اولاد ملک سهراب است" (p. 295).
  - **Border treaty with Delhi.** After the "قوت" (as printed) of Sultan Bahlul, "سلطان سکندر والی دهلی" made peace with Sultan Husain: "طرفین بر ولایت خود قانع بوده بر حد[OCR?] یکدیگر یورش نکنند" (p. 295).
  - **Babur's grant.** Babur, "بعد رسیدن در پنجاب", granted Multan to Arghun with the charge "در آبادانی[OCR?] ملک و رفاهیت[OCR?] رعایا کوشد" (p. 295).
  - **Eighty years outside Delhi.** "در آن ولایت چهار سال حکومت میرزا شاه حسین ارغون [illegible] و از ابتدا[OCR?] گشته [illegible] لغایت سنه ۹۳۶[OCR?] مدت هشتاد سال [illegible] آن ولایت از تصرف فرمان دهان دهلی بسیردن[OCR?] بود درین ولا داخل کشور دهلی گردید و شاهزاده کامران میرزا بر آن قابض گشت" (p. 294).

  - **The Jams.** "جام بایزید و جام ابراهیم از جام[OCR?] نند[OCR?] حاکم ولایت تهته رنجیده بخدمت سلطان حسین پیوستند". Offended with Jam Nand[OCR?], ruler of the country of Thatta, the two joined Sultan Husain, who received them with favour and assigned them "جاگیر مسر[OCR?]" (p. 295).
  - Jam Bayazid later helped Sultan Husain kill the vizier Imad al-Mulk (p. 295).
  - **Arghun.** Babur's farman went "بمیرزا شاه حسین ارغون حاکم تهته", and "میرزا شاه حسین ارغون از تهته آمده" to fight for Multan (p. 295).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Multan and the Langah**
- **Shaikh Yusuf Qureshi.** A descendant of Bahauddin Zakariya (see that row).
  - He was raised to rule by Multan's notables and townspeople, and struck coin "در ۸۵۵ [OCR?]" (p. 297).
  - He was "ساده لوح" and was deposed after "دو سال" (pp. 296–297).
  - "بقابو[OCR?] که یافت از قید فرار نموده" to Sultan Bahlul Lodi at Delhi. Bahlul held his coming a godsend, "کمال احترام بجا آورده", and married his daughter to the Shaikh's son Shaikh Abd Allah (p. 296).
  - He moved Bahlul to send Barbak Shah[OCR?] and Tatar Khan against Sultan Husain (p. 296).
- **Rai Sanhra[OCR?], later Sultan Qutb al-Din Langah.** He was "سردار جماعت لنگاهان" and held "قصبه سیوی" (p. 297). For the coup and his reign see the Multan bullet (pp. 296–297).
- **Sultan Husain b. Qutb al-Din.**
  - His full brother rebelled as "سلطان شهاب الدین", and Husain captured him (p. 296).
  - In old age he gave his son the title "سلطان فیروز شاه" and had the khutba read in his name. The son was "خلوق[OCR?] آنرا[OCR?] و ستمگار", and the vizier "عماد الملک" poisoned him (p. 295).
  - Husain took the khutba back, made "سلطان محمود پسر سلطان فیروز شاه" heir, and killed Imad al-Mulk "باتفاق جام بایزید" to avenge his son. He died "باجل طبعی" (p. 295).
  - His good name drew the Jams and the Baluch (see Province-level material) (p. 295).
- **Sultan Mahmud b. Firuz Shah.** He was "خوردسال"; "اوباش و اجلاف" gathered round him, and the nobles kept away (p. 295).
- **Others named:**
  - Sultan Ala al-Din of Delhi, overcome by Sultan Bahlul Lodi (p. 297).
  - "باربک شاه[OCR?]"; "غازیخان"; "ملک مانجهی[OCR?] کهوکهر[OCR?] گماشته سید خان" (p. 296).
  - Malik Sohrab, Ismail Khan and Fath Khan (p. 295).
  - Qawam Khan; Lashkar Khan; the slave Shams al-Din (p. 294).
- **Places with no row:** "قصبه سیوی" (p. 297); "قلعه شور", "کروکوت[OCR?]" and "دهنکوت" (p. 296); "قلعه کوت کردز[OCR?]", "سیت پور[OCR?]", "کچ و مکران" and Thatta (p. 295).

**Babur and Humayun (outside the archive's places unless noted; brief)**
- **Babur's end (pp. 293–294).**
  - Humayun had been sent to "دیار سنبل". He fell gravely ill and was brought by river to Agra, where the physicians failed (pp. 293–294).
  - The diamond "که والده سلطان ابراهیم گذرانیده بود" was proposed as alms. Babur said "من خود را صدقه آن نور حدقه می نمایم" and walked round the prince's bed (p. 293).
  - He died "در عمر چهل و نه سال و بقولی پنجاه سالگی". His body was taken to Kabul and buried "در گذرگاه بر لب آبجوئی[OCR?]" (p. 293).
  - "مدت سلطنت سی و هشت سال ازانجمله در هندوستان پنج سال و پنج روز" (p. 293).
- **Humayun's accession and early campaigns.**
  - Mir Khalifa planned to raise Babur's son-in-law Khwaja Mahdi (p. 293). The amirs instead enthroned Humayun "در سنه ۹۳۷[OCR?] ... در بست و چهار سالگی" (p. 292).
  - The raja of Kalinjar paid "دوازده من طلا پیشکش". Sultan Mahmud b. Sultan Sikandar withdrew to Patna and Bengal. Muhammad Zaman Mirza was held in Bayana fort, escaped and went to Sultan Bahadur of Gujarat (p. 292).
- **The Gujarat war (pp. 290–291).** Tatar Khan was killed in battle (p. 291). The campaign passed through Mandsaur, Cambay and Champanir, which Humayun took by escalade. Gujarat was given in jagir to Askari, who lost it (p. 291). Sultan Bahadur drowned after fleeing to the "فرنگیان" (p. 290).
- **Sher Khan in the east.** He took "ولایت جونپور و بهار و قلعه روهتاس و چناوه[OCR?]" and Bengal. "نصیب شاه[OCR?] والی بنگاله" came wounded to Humayun's court. Sher Khan's son Jalal Khan withdrew to join his father at "جهارکهند[OCR?]" (p. 290).
- **Hindal's revolt and the Ganges rout.**
  - Hindal rebelled at Agra (p. 289).
  - The rout came at "بهوچ پور[OCR?] تهته[OCR?] کنار دریای گنگ" (p. 289). The book dates it "در سنه ۹۴۶[OCR?] بر کنار دریای گنگ در بهوجپور" (p. 288).
  - Haji Begum fell captive and was returned with honour by Sher Khan (p. 288).
- **Qannauj (pp. 286–287).** The second battle came "در سنه ۹۴۶[OCR?]" (p. 287). Humayun crossed the Ganges on an elephant and was helped up the bank by "میر شمس الدین محمد غزنوی", who for this was made "اتکگی[OCR?]" of Prince Akbar (p. 286).
- **Kabul and Kashmir (p. 286).** Kamran took "غزنین[OCR?] و قندهار و بدخشان". Mirza Haidar Kashghari conquered Kashmir. He kept the coin and khutba of "نازک شاه والی آنجا" at first and later Humayun's.
- **The water-carrier Nizam.**
  - Humayun answered his name with "نظام اولیاست[OCR?]". The book does not explain the remark (p. 288).
  - He was seated on the throne "نیمروز" and called "سلطان نیمروز" (p. 288). Kamran objected to "اجلاس سقا بر تخت سلطنت" (p. 287).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Coin and khutba as the act of sovereignty.** Shaikh Yusuf (p. 297); Qutb al-Din Langah (p. 296); Firuz Shah's khutba and its withdrawal (p. 295); Hindal at Agra (p. 289); Kamran at Kabul and Mirza Haidar in Kashmir (p. 286). The water-carrier's coins are under Legends (p. 287).
- **Rule by the city's choice.** Multan's "اعیان و ارکان" and "اهالی و موالی شهر" together chose their ruler (p. 297).
- **Jagir and grants.**
  - The Baluch along the Sind river and the Jams' "جاگیر مسر[OCR?]" (p. 295).
  - Multan to Arghun (p. 295).
  - Lahore and Multan to Kamran, and "جاگیر دیگر" to Lashkar Khan (p. 294).
  - Gujarat to Askari (p. 291).
- **Marriage alliances.** Rai Sanhra[OCR?]'s daughter to Shaikh Yusuf, with gifts and visits (p. 297); Bahlul's daughter to Shaikh Abd Allah (p. 296).
- **Multan's fort and city.** The fort had "هر چهار دروازه". Fort and city are distinct: "از بیرون شهر درون قلعه", "از قلعه بشهر" (p. 296).
- **Administration.** Lashkar Khan's "کار مالی و ملکی"; the "محال ملتان" (p. 294).
- **The sack's captives.** Townspeople "از هفت ساله تا هفتاد ساله" were taken captive (p. 294).
- **Alms for healing.** The court proposed giving away the best treasure as "تصدق" for the prince's recovery. The king offered himself instead (p. 293).
- **Peshkash.** Kalinjar paid twelve maunds of gold (p. 292).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On the Multan coup.** The author calls Shaikh Yusuf "ساده لوح از مکر و خداع غافل" and Rai Sanhra[OCR?] "آن مکار" (p. 297).
- **On the Langah sultans.** Sultan Mahmud kept a court of "اوباش و اجلاف" (p. 295). The child Husain's reign was "محض برای اسم" (p. 294).
- **On Humayun.**
  - "اینهمه شکست از غفلت و بی پروای آنحضرت بوقوع آمد", followed by a maxim on rulers who lack sound counsel (p. 288). There is also a reflection on how ill fortune darkens the mind (p. 289).
  - "سلطنت نه امریست که بسعی و تلاش شخصی و یا با مداد و اعانت کسی میسر تواند شد" (p. 293).
  - Kamran's "حسد فطری و عداوت جبلی" (p. 287).
- **Variants and hearsay.** "بقولی پنجاه سالگی" (p. 293); "گویند" for the leather coins (p. 287).
- **Editor's footnote variant (p. 297).** "فذالک [OCR?] سنه ۸۵۰ [OCR?] است [illegible] تاریخ فرشته جلد دوم صفحہ [illegible] و [illegible] اکبری صفحہ [illegible] [illegible]". This sets a year 850 against the text's "۸۵۵ [OCR?]" for Shaikh Yusuf, citing "تاریخ فرشته جلد دوم" and an "…اکبری".

## Citable passages

- "شیخ یوسف قریشی را که از اولاد ... مخدوم العالم شیخ بهاؤالدین ذکریا قدس الله سره بود ... بسلطنت ولایت ملتان برداشتند [OCR?]" — "They raised Shaikh Yusuf Qureshi, of the descendants of Makhdum al-Alam Shaikh Baha al-Din Zakariya, to the rule of Multan." (p. 297)
- "درویشی را بجهانبانی همرنگ و ولایت را بسلطنت هم سنگ گردانید" — "He made dervishhood one colour with world-rule, and sainthood equal in weight with kingship." (p. 297)
- "شهریان از هفت ساله تا هفتاد ساله در بند افتادند" — "The townspeople, from seven years old to seventy, fell into captivity." (Arghun's sack of Multan) (p. 294)

## Doubts

**Reading order and boundaries**
- The chunk runs from p. 297 down to p. 286. The Multan account opens before p. 297, so its heading and its first ruler are not in this chunk. p. 297 begins mid-sentence ("تر گردید ...").
- The Multan account sits inside Babur's reign. It closes with "درین ایام بعرض والا رسید که شاهزاده محمد همایون میرزا ..." (p. 294), and Babur's death follows.
- The chunk ends mid-sentence at "و معاند غالب است" (p. 286).

**Mappings**
- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya: person-level, weigh accordingly.** The saint is named only as Shaikh Yusuf's ancestor (p. 297). Everything else in his bullet concerns the descendant.
- **"شیخ یوسف قریشی" is not mapped to `shrine-of-shah-yusaf-gardez` or `shah-yousuf`.** The book calls him "قریشی" and a descendant of Bahauddin, and gives him no tomb.
- **Multan, 8 rows: place-level, weigh accordingly.** The coup "درون قلعه" (p. 296) is fort material. toponym_map lists "fort of multan" for **prahladpuri-temple**, which is already on the Multan line. The book says nothing of a temple.
- **langer-makhdoom: place-level only, weigh accordingly.** It is mapped through the toponym "chiniot", whose single row it is. The row's location reads "Langer Makhdoom, Chiniot", and the book's "قصبه چنیوت" is a town. Held identity question: no saint is named here, so the held identity of this row is not touched.
- **Lahore, 35 rows: place-level, weigh accordingly.** Most of these are passing mentions of Kamran's and Humayun's movements (pp. 286–287, 290, 294). Only the brothers' council at Lahore (p. 286) and Kamran's arrival there as jagirdar (p. 294) say anything of the city as a seat.
- **Every province bullet is province-level. Weigh accordingly.**
  - **punjab:** the book's units are "ولایت ملتان", "پنجاب" and "ولایت لاهور" in the Langah and early Mughal periods. They are not Mughal subas and not the modern province's boundaries. No extent is supplied.
  - "کنار دریای سند" (p. 295) names the river, not the province sindh. The Baluch jagir is kept under punjab because the book gives it as Sultan Husain's grant within his realm. The book does not say where Kot Kardaz[OCR?], Sitpur[OCR?] or Dhankot lie.
  - **sindh:** "حاکم ولایت تهته" is the country of Thatta. "حاکم تهته" and "از تهته" may mean the city; the book does not say, and Thatta has no row.
  - "سیوی" and "کچ و مکران" are not taken as province balochistan: the book does not use that unit.

**Traps avoided**
- **"سلطان قطب الدین" (Rai Sanhra[OCR?] Langah)** is not Qutb al-Din Aibak (`tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak`).
- **Two "شمس الدین" in the chunk:** Arghun's slave "شمس الدین" (p. 294) and "میر شمس الدین محمد غزنوی" (p. 286). Neither is the Multan "Shams" (`shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari`).
- **"جلال خان", son of Sher Khan (p. 290),** is not the Uch Jalal.
- **"غازیخان" (p. 296)** is a person, not Dera Ghazi Khan (`sakhi-sarwar`). The "سخی[OCR?]" on p. 293 is an adjective for Khwaja Mahdi.
- **"تهته[OCR?]" on p. 289** stands in "بهوچ پور[OCR?] تهته[OCR?] کنار دریای گنگ", on the Ganges. It is not Thatta and is kept out of the sindh bullet.
- **"قلعه روهتاس" (p. 290)** is listed with Jaunpur, Bihar and Chunar among Sher Khan's conquests, after which Humayun turned to "ولایت شرقیه". It is not mapped to `gurdwara-choa-sahib` (toponym "rohtas fort").
- **"قلعه شور" (p. 296)** is not mapped to `garh-maharaja-shorkot`. The book writes "شور", not "شورکوت", and does not equate them. It could not be placed.
- **"گجرات"** throughout (Sultan Bahadur, Champanir, Cambay) is suba Gujarat, not Punjab Gujrat. "پتنه" (p. 292) is not Pakpattan.
- **"کشمیر" (p. 286)** is Mirza Haidar's conquest. `sharada-peeth` is not named.
- **"نظام اولیاست[OCR?]" (p. 288)** is left unidentified.

**Internal points not resolved**
- **The printed years are out of narrative order.** Husain b. Mahmud is enthroned "در سنه ۹۴۵[OCR?]" (p. 294), yet the Arghun conquest that ends his seven-year reign is dated "۹۳۲[OCR?]". Delhi's loss of Multan ends "لغایت سنه ۹۳۶[OCR?]". Not resolved; no interval is computed.
- **Shaikh Yusuf's year.** The text has "۸۵۵ [OCR?]"; the editor's footnote has "۸۵۰ [OCR?]" (p. 297).
- **Name forms:**
  - "راۓ سنهره [OCR?]" (p. 297) against the heading "رائے سنسهره[OCR?]" (p. 296).
  - "باربک شاه[OCR?]" (p. 296) against "بابا شاه[OCR?]" (p. 295). They are taken as one army from context; the book does not say so.
  - "لشکر خان نگاه[OCR?]" against "لشکر خان نگاهدار" (p. 294).
  - "کروکوت[OCR?]" (p. 296) against "قلعه کوت کردز[OCR?]" (p. 295); the book does not equate them.
- **A parallel account outside this chunk.** The geography section (p. 525, chunk 042) gives the Langah grant to Malik Sohrab as "از کرورکوت تا مانکوت". This chunk has "از قلعه کوت کردز[OCR?] تا دهنکوت[OCR?]". For the consolidator; not resolved.
- **"Tatar Khan".** "تاتار خان حاکم پنجاب" under Bahlul (p. 296) and the Tatar Khan killed under Humayun (pp. 291–292) are not equated by the book. On p. 292 the latter is "تاتار خان پسرش", after "سلطان [illegible] دل سلطان بهلول لودی".
- **The Chiniot sentence (p. 295).** The subject of "گماشته سلطان حسین را که در چنیوت بود بعد جنگ بدست آورده بعدم خانه فرستاد" is, as printed, the fleeing army. The reading is left as printed.
- **Unflagged words quoted as printed:** "قوت" for Bahlul's death (p. 295) and "مسلطان[OCR?]" (p. 296).
- **Phrases left unread:** "منته [OCR?] در سفهستان [OCR?] شیخ" and "سال [OCR?] واجب" (p. 297) are quoted and not read.
