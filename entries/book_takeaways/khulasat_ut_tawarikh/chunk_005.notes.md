# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 005 — pp. 52–63

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-procession, with the emperor entering the capital through the bazaar, praying `asr` at the Jami mosque and entering the fort (p. 63). This continues from the end of chunk 006 (p. 64). Next comes "در بیان جشن وزن حضرت خدیوِ گیهان خداوند جهان" (pp. 62–63): the lunar weighing on "هفتم ربیع الاول مطابق دوازدهم اذر ماه الهی سنه[OCR?] ۱۰۶۸[OCR?]", promotions, three days of fireworks and lamps on the Jamna, and then the emperor's ziyarats to the tomb of Humayun, to Shaikh Nizam al-Din Auliya and to Shaikh Qutb al-Din Kaki near Shahjahanabad (p. 62). The section "در بیان ماجراے[OCR?] احوال با شاهزاده محمّد شجاع و توجه موکب والا بدفع او" (pp. 59–61) covers Shuja's grant of Bihar and Monghyr, his advance on Allahabad while the court was in Punjab, and the emperor's march out by Soron. On the way the emperor visits the tomb of "سید بدیع الدین … بشاه مدار و شاه باز مشهور" at "کمن پور[OCR?]" (p. 60). "در بیان شرح شوخی مهاراجه جسونت سنگه در جنگ حضرت خدیوِ گیهان با[OCR?] محمّد شجاع و رایت فتح برافراختن و فرار محمّد شجاع از معرکه کارزار" (pp. 53–59) gives the order of battle on "نوزدهم شهر ربیع الثانی … سنه[OCR?] ۱۰۶۸[OCR?]", Jaswant Singh's night desertion and plunder of the camp, the two days' fighting and Shuja's flight. The chunk closes (p. 52) with the pursuit of Shuja, the submission of Sayyid Qasim at Allahabad, news of Dara moving "از ملتان[OCR?] بسمت گجرات", and a force sent against Jaswant Singh. It ends mid-sentence.

**No saint or site in the archive is named in this chunk.** The three saints' tombs the emperor visits are all outside Pakistan. The only archive attachment is one place-level mention of Multan.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Saints' tombs visited by the emperor (all outside Pakistan, no rows)**
- **Sayyid Badi al-Din, "Shah Madar".** On "چهار دهم ربیع الثانی" the emperor camped at "کمن پور[OCR?]" and "بروضه قدسیه حضرت سید بدیع الدین قدس سره که بزبان عوام بشاه مدار و شاه باز مشهور هستند تشریف برده مراسم زیارت بجا اوردند". That is, he went to the holy tomb of Sayyid Badi al-Din, known in common speech as Shah Madar and Shah Baz, and performed the rites of ziyarat (p. 60). This was on the march against Shuja, three days before the camp at "کوره[OCR?]" (p. 59).
- **Shaikh Nizam al-Din Auliya.** On the second day after the visit to the Sahibabad garden on the 12th (of Rabi al-Awwal), the emperor went by boat to Humayun's tomb, then "بطواف[OCR?] مزار فایض الانوار حضرت شیخ نظام الدین اولیا تبرک جسته هزار[OCR?] روپیه بمستحقان و مجاوران عنایت فرمودند". He sought blessing at the grave and gave a thousand[OCR?] rupees to the deserving and to the mujawirs (p. 62).
- **Shaikh Qutb al-Din Kaki.** From there he went "بروضه قدسیه شیخ قطب الدین کاکی قدس الله سره که هفت کروهی شاهجهان اباد واقع است". The tomb lies seven kos from Shahjahanabad. He "رسم زیارت بجا اورده و استمداد همت نموده و هزار[OCR?] روپیه نذر گذرانیده", performing the ziyarat, seeking the saint's spiritual help and offering a thousand[OCR?] rupees as nazr. Then he returned to the palace (p. 62).
- **Humayun's tomb** (a ruler, not a saint): "بزیارت روضه منوره حضرت جنت آشیانی همایون بادشاه کشتی سوار تشریف برده فاتحه خوانده پنجهزار[OCR?] روپیه بمجاوران مرحمت کرده" — he read the fatiha and gave five thousand[OCR?] rupees to the mujawirs (p. 62).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A ruler at saints' tombs.**
  - Gifts: to the mujawirs at Humayun's tomb, "پنجهزار[OCR?] روپیه"; to "مستحقان و مجاوران" at Nizam al-Din's tomb, "هزار[OCR?] روپیه"; at Qutb al-Din Kaki's tomb, "هزار[OCR?] روپیه نذر" (p. 62).
  - "استمداد همت" was sought at Qutb al-Din Kaki's tomb (p. 62). Chunk 006 records the same phrase at Bahauddin's tomb.
  - The rites were performed at Shah Madar's tomb (p. 60).
- **The weighing ("جشن وزن").** It marked the end of the emperor's fortieth year and the start of the forty-first "بحساب شمسی". He was weighed "بزر و سیم و سایر اشیا اے معهود" in the "غسل خانه", and robes, swords, elephants, drums, standards and titles were given out (p. 63).
- **Grants and treasure.**
  - Muazzam Khan received "انعام ده لک روپیه نقد" and was raised to seven thousand (p. 53); the household servants received "سی هزار روپیه" (p. 53).
  - Zulfiqar Khan was told to take "یک[OCR?] کرور[OCR?] روپیه" from the treasury to Allahabad (p. 62).
  - Raja Sujan[OCR?] Singh presented "پانصد مهر[OCR?] و یک هزار روپیه نذر" (p. 53).
  - Shuja's men plundered the treasury, karkhanas and beasts in the night panic (p. 57). Shuja's 114[OCR?] guns were captured (p. 53).
- **Iqta.** Monghyr with suba Bihar was added to Bengal "بااقطاع[OCR?] او", that is, as Shuja's assignment (p. 61).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On Shuja.** "چون عقل معامله دان نداشت و سود از زیان نمیدانست": he had no practical sense and could not tell gain from loss. He was tempted by the empty capital and by flatterers (p. 61).
- **On Jaswant Singh.** Outwardly he was loyal, but "در باطن از خبث سریرت خویش" he fled the field by night (p. 57).
- **The victory as divine help.** It came "محض [illegible] تایید ربانی". The author's phrase is "بے منت سپاه و لشکر" (p. 53).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 63 down to p. 52, and all page joins read through.
  - It opens mid-procession at "و غریو[OCR?] طبل و کوس" (p. 63), continuing from chunk 006's last page (p. 64).
  - It ends mid-sentence at "که اراده پیوستن بدار اشکوه" (p. 52), which continues in the next chunk.
- The stamp at the foot of p. 59 is not text.
- The ruler is never named. He is "خدیو گیهان", "خدیو افاق" and so on.

**Mapping**
- **Multan (8 rows): place-level, on a bare departure point.** "ملتان[OCR?]" is flagged, and the book says nothing about the place. Under brief point 3 this is one short bullet at most, so the consolidator may drop it.
- **"گجرات" (p. 52) is not placed.** Dara heads there from Multan, but the chunk gives nothing to choose between suba Gujarat and Gujrat in Punjab. No row is attached; Gujrat's row is shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai, which is not mapped.
- **The Lahore governor (p. 62).** "صوبه دار لاهور" begins the page. The name before it falls across the page break after "خلیل الله خان [illegible]" (p. 63). Chunk 006 records Khalilullah Khan's appointment to Lahore (p. 64), but this chunk's text does not itself join the name to the title.
- Shah Madar, Nizam al-Din Auliya, Qutb al-Din Kaki and Humayun are all named at sites the book places at or near Shahjahanabad, or at "کمن پور[OCR?]". None is a row's principal figure, and none of these sites is in Pakistan.
- "سید عالم بارهه", "سید قاسم", "سید قلی اوزبک[OCR?]", "شیخ ولی فرملی[OCR?]" and "شیخ ظریت[OCR?]" are soldiers and nobles, not saints.
