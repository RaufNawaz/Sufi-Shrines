# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 033 — pp. 412–425

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-sentence, at the end of the reign that precedes Mahmud. That reign covers the Sultan's victory over Raja Jaipal, the broken treaty, the battle near "لمغان", the Sultan's death and his reign of "بست سال" (p. 425). The section "سلطان محمود بن سلطان ناصر الدین سبکتگین" (pp. 424–412) follows: his struggle with his elder brother Amir Isma'il, his accession at Ghazni, and the caliph's robe and titles (p. 424). The book then numbers his Indian campaigns one by one, framed by long seasonal set-pieces (spring and Eid, autumn, winter, summer). The campaigns are: (1) Peshawar and Bhatinda, (2) by way of Multan against "بجے راے[OCR?]" and Daud b. Nasir, (3) Anandpal and the fort of Bhimnagar with its lamp-and-fireworks festival, (4) Multan and the Qaramita, (5) Thanesar, (6) Nandana and Kashmir, (7) Kanauj, Baran, Mahaban and Mathura, (8) Raja Nanda, (9) Kashmir and "لاهور وبانگر", (10) Gwalior and Kalinjar, (11) Somnath, with the return through the desert toward Multan (pp. 424–412). The chunk breaks off at the start of the twelfth campaign, "دراواخر سنه ۴۱۷[OCR?]", against a group on the bank of "دریاۓ سنده[OCR?]" (p. 412); the sentence continues on p. 411 in chunk 032. No saint, dervish, tomb or shrine is named anywhere in the chunk, so every attachment below is place-level.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**The reign before Mahmud's (p. 425)**
- The Sultan, unnamed on this page, prevailed "بدین تدبیر صایب". The stratagem itself is described on the preceding page, outside this chunk. Raja Jaipal agreed to send "پنجاه زنجیر فیل با نقد فراوان" as peshkash and left "چندین از معتبران خود را برسم یرغمال". At home he broke the agreement and held the Sultan's agents in exchange for his own men (p. 425).
- To punish the breach, the Sultan marched on Hindustan. Jaipal came "با یک لک سوار و پیاده بیشمار و فیلان بسیار", and the armies met "در نواحی لمغان". The Sultan won, Jaipal fled to Hindustan, and "تالمغانات بتصرف سلطان در امده سکه و خطبه او رواج یافت" (p. 425).
- The Sultan died; no year or place is given. "مدت سلطنت او بست سال بود" (p. 425).

**Sultan Mahmud: accession and titles (p. 424)**
- After "سلطان ناصر الدین سبکتگین" died, his elder son "امیر اسمعیل" took the throne and denied Mahmud his inheritance. Mahmud overcame him and "در سنه ۳۸۸ [OCR?] (۱) در خطه عشرت آگین غزنین زیب افروز اورنگ خلافت گشت" (p. 424).
- Conquests named: "بلخ و بخارا و اورگنج (۲) و خوارزم و ترکستان و عراق و خراسان و سایر بلاد ایران و توران" (p. 424).
- "خلیفه بغداد" sent a robe "که پیش ازین احدی از خلفا هیچ یکے از سلاطین نفرستاده بود" and gave him the titles "یمین الدوله و امین الملت" (p. 424).

**Mahmud's campaigns, as the book numbers them (pp. 424–412)**
- **1, "۳۹۱".** Peshawar (see the Peshawar bullet).
  - Introduced with "گویند": the raja wore a necklace, "که بزبان هند مالا نامند", which the appraisers valued at "یک لک و هشتاد هزار دینار". The necklaces of his sons and brothers also went to the Sultan's treasury (p. 423).
  - He went on into Hindustan and took "بهتنده [OCR?] که دار الحکومت راجه جیپال بود". There he "در اکثر اماکن تعمیر مسجد نموده" and stayed for a time (p. 423).
  - After Eid, which fell in spring, he "از قلعه بهننده[OCR?] برامده بغزنین مراجعت نمود" (p. 422).
- **2, no year printed.** "بجے راے[OCR?]" was at the neighbourhood of "تهنه[OCR?]", with a strong fort. He fled toward Sind, was caught, and "سرداری" killed him. His head was brought to the Sultan, his followers were put to the sword, and the booty included "دویست وهشتاد زنجیر فیل" (pp. 421–420). The campaign went on to Multan (see the Multan bullet).
- **3, "۳۹۹".** The Sultan defeated "راجه انندپال" and took thirty elephants. He then took "قلعه بهیم نگر", and had its gold and silver thrones, treasure and diamonds laid out in a wide field for soldiers and subjects to see (p. 418). A festival with lamps and fireworks followed (see Practices) (pp. 418–417).
- **4.** Multan (see the Multan bullet) (p. 417).
- **5, "۳[OCR?]۰[OCR?]۲[OCR?]".** Thanesar.
  - The book calls it "شهریست قدیم". Near it is "کو نا [OCR?] تبیست [OCR?] [کولابیست?] بس عظیم", a great pool. "باعتقاد اهل هند" creation began there. Bathing in it is one of the "مشوبات [OCR?] عظمی", and to die there brings salvation. There is a great temple with an idol "چکرسوم [OCR?]" (p. 417).
  - "راجه نرو جیپال (۱) حاکم انجا" offered fifty elephants if the Sultan turned back. The Sultan refused, and the raja fled without a fight (pp. 417–416).
  - The city was plundered and "بت چکرسوم را بغزنین برده ... بردرگاه نهادند تا پے سپر خلایق گردد" (p. 416).
- **6, no year printed.** "قلعه نندنه که برکوه بالناته است". Narojaipal left a garrison and withdrew "درکوهسار دشوار گذار کشمیر". The fort was besieged "درنقب وساباط وسرکوب" and surrendered its keys. The Sultan followed the raja toward "دره کشمیر" but could not reach him. He took booty, converted many, and spread "احکام شرعیه دران دیار" (p. 416).
- **7, "۴۰۹".** Kanauj.
  - The Sultan crossed "هفت دریاۓ هولناک". "کوره" of Kanauj submitted (p. 416).
  - At "برن", "هردت" handed his fort to his people and hid. The fort paid "ده هزار بار هزار دام که دولک وپنجاه هزار روپیه بوده باشد" with elephants and got "[illegible]ان" (p. 416).
  - At "قلعه مهابن" on the Jumna, "کلچند" was caught trying to cross on an elephant and killed himself with a dagger (p. 416).
  - Mathura is described as "شهریست بزرگ مشتمل برچندین بتخانهاۓ عظیم ومولد کرشن بن بسدیو که باعتقاد اهل هند محل حلول ایزد تعالی است". Its temples were burned, a gold idol of "نود وهشت هزار وسیصد مثقال پخته" was broken, and a "یاقوت کحلی" of "چهار صد وپنجاه مثقال" was found (p. 416).
  - "گویند": the governor "چندراۓ" had a huge elephant that the Sultan could not buy. It ran one night to the Sultan's tent, and he named it "خداداد" (pp. 416–415).
  - Kanauj booty: "پنج لک وبست هزار درم وپنجاه وسه هزار برده وسیصد وپنجاه فیل" (p. 415).
- **8, "۴۱۰[OCR?]".** Raja Nanda.
  - "راجه نندا حاکم کالنجر" had killed Kora for submitting to the Sultan (p. 415).
  - At the Jumna, "راجه نروجپال", backed by Nanda, barred the way. Twenty slaves swam across and routed him (p. 415).
  - Introduced with "گویند", Nanda's force was "سی وشش هزار سوار وصد وچهل وپنجاه هزار پیاده وسی صد وچهل فیل" (as printed) (p. 415).
  - The Sultan saw its size, regretted coming, and prayed for victory. That night Nanda fled in fear, and "پانصد وهشتاد زنجیر فیل" were found in the forest (pp. 415–414).
- **9, "۴۱۲[OCR?]".** "قصد کشمیر". The Sultan could not take "قلعه لوه کوت" because of its height and strength, and turned toward Lahore and "بانگر" (p. 414).
- **10, no year printed.**
  - "قلعه گوالیار" is described as "کارنامه ایست ازفرمان دهان پیشین". Its ruler gave "سی وپنج زنجیر فیل" (p. 414).
  - "قلعه کالنجر مسکن راجه نندا" was besieged. Nanda gave "سیصد زنجیر فیل" and released them without mahouts, and the Sultan's Turks rode and tamed them (pp. 414–413).
  - Nanda sent a "شعر هندی" praising the Sultan, and "زبان دانان هند" explained it. The Sultan granted him "منشور حکومت پانزده قلعه ضمیمه کالنجر" (p. 413).
- **11, no year printed.** Somnath.
  - The book calls it "شهریست بزرگ برساحل دریاۓ محیط وبتخانه مشهور معبد براهمه"; its greatest idol is called Somnath (p. 413).
  - The army passed "شهر نهراله", which had been abandoned, and took "راه سومنات یعنی پاتن" (p. 412).
  - The fort was taken and the temples razed. "سنگ سومنات را پارچه پارچه کرده پاره ان را بغزنین برده بردرمسجد گذاشته که پے سپر خلایق گردد" (p. 412).
  - On the way back, "بهیرم[OCR?] دیو راجه عظیم الشان" blocked the road and plundered the baggage (p. 412).
- **12, "دراواخر سنه ۴۱۷[OCR?]".** This begins on p. 412 (see the sindh bullet) and continues in chunk 032.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Tribute and peshkash.**
  - Multan: "هشت[OCR?] هزار دام هرسال" (p. 420).
  - Jaipal's treaty of fifty elephants and cash, with hostages "برسم یرغمال" (p. 425). Narojaipal's offer of fifty elephants (p. 417). Kora's peshkash (p. 416).
  - "برن": "ده هزار بار هزار دام که دولک وپنجاه هزار روپیه بوده باشد", the author's own gloss of the figure (p. 416).
  - Gwalior: thirty-five elephants. Kalinjar: three hundred (p. 414).
- **Coin and khutba.** "سکه و خطبه او رواج یافت" up to Lamghanat (p. 425).
- **Imposing Islam.**
  - Multan: "احکام شرعیه را رواج داد" (p. 420).
  - Around Nandana and Kashmir: "رواج احکام شرعیه دران دیار" (p. 416).
  - Around Bhatinda, mosques were built "در اکثر اماکن" (p. 423).
- **Temples and idols.**
  - The Thanesar idol was taken to Ghazni and laid "بردرگاه ... تا پے سپر خلایق گردد" (p. 416).
  - A piece of the Somnath stone was laid "بردرمسجد" in Ghazni for the same purpose (p. 412).
  - The temples of Mathura were burned (p. 416). At an unnamed town by the Jumna, the slaves "بتخانها را مسمار نمودند" (p. 415).
- **Pilgrimage beliefs at Thanesar.** Bathing in the pool, and dying there, are believed to bring reward and salvation (p. 417). This is a non-archive site.
- **Investiture.** The caliph's robe and the titles "یمین الدوله و امین الملت" (p. 424).
- **Court festival at "قلعه بهیم نگر" (pp. 418–417).**
  - Lamps were set along the garden walls, coloured lanterns hung on the trees, and thousands of candles lit along the channels. Gold basins and paper plants stood beside them.
  - Fireworks are named: "ماهتابی", "شب افروز", "گل ریز", "چرخی", "ستاره", "هوائے", "پاکر [OCR?] یعنی تونکه [OCR?]" and "هوشک [OCR?] یعنی چکچو [OCR?]".
- **Army and siege.**
  - Army reviews ("عرض لشکر") were held by "یساولان" and "قورچیان" (p. 413) and by "بخشیان" (p. 412).
  - Siege works "نقب وساباط وسرکوب" (p. 416).
- **Booty.**
  - Elephants: "دویست وهشتاد" (p. 420), thirty (p. 418) and "پانصد وهشتاد" (p. 414).
  - Kanauj: see above (p. 415).
  - Jaipal's necklace, "یک لک و هشتاد هزار دینار" (p. 423).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Somnath idol ("گویند").** "درزمان پیغمبر ان بت را ازخانه کعبه براورده درانجا گذاشته اند" (p. 413).

- **Nanda's flight.** The Sultan's prayer was answered: "بمشیت ایزدی همان شب خوفے عظیم بخاطر نندا راه یافت" (p. 414).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Motive, as the author states it.**
  - "بمقتضائے مراسم دین داری بقصد جهاد" (p. 424). At Multan, "حمیت دین" (p. 420). Thanesar was attacked "بقصد جهاد" (p. 417).
  - "ازانجا که سلطان قوے اقبال بود بهر طرف که روے عزیمت می کرد فتح ونصرت استقبال می نمود" (p. 420).
- **Punishment.** After the Qaramita punishments the author quotes: "اگر سلطان نفرماید سیاست   زند هر کس بخود لاف ریاست" (p. 417).
- **Judgement on Bije Rai:** "از روے بے همتی و بے تدبیری" (p. 421).
- **Verses.** The author mocks the idols (p. 416). He sets Kalchand's suicide against "زیستن چون بکام خصم بود" (p. 416). He praises humble prayer: "کین تضرع را برحق قدرها ست" (p. 415).

- **Sources and hedges.**
  - "بکتب سلف" for Peshawar's names (p. 424). "درکتب سلف اهل هند مرقوم است" (p. 413). "باعتقاد اهل هند" (pp. 417, 416).

## Doubts

**Reading order and chunk edges**
- **Reading order.** The chunk runs from p. 425 down to p. 412. Every page join was checked, high to low, and each continues the sentence or the verse. The joins at 425→424 and 412→411 are the chunk's edges.
- **Opening.** The chunk opens mid-sentence, "رفتار بماند", inside a cold-weather passage (p. 425). The "تدبیر صایب" by which the Sultan won is described on p. 426, outside this chunk.
- **The Sultan of p. 425 is not named on that page.** He is placed as Sabuktigin only by the next heading and its first words: "بعد ازانکه سلطان ناصر الدین سبکتگین ودیعت حیات سپرد" (p. 424).
- **Close.** The chunk ends mid-sentence: "وآزار واضرار رسانیده" (p. 412). The group to be punished is printed only as "جمعی", and no people's name appears on p. 412.
- **No saints.** No saint, dervish, tomb or shrine is named, so the addendum's item 4 yields nothing. No row is attached person-level or site-specifically; all three bold lines are place-level (ruling a).

**Multan (8 rows): place-level, weigh accordingly**
- **Substantive material:**
  - Daud b. Nasir's separate rule (p. 420).
  - The submission, the yearly "هشت[OCR?] هزار دام", and the shari'a (p. 420).
  - Multan's hot air (p. 419).
  - The Qaramita, and Daud's imprisonment and death (p. 417).
- **Passing mentions (addendum item 3):** "از راه ملتان" (p. 421) and "براه ریگستان متوجه ملتان" (p. 412).
- **"ولایت" (p. 417) and "ان ملک" (p. 420)** are attached to the city line, not made a province bullet. This follows chunk 032's handling of "ولایت ملتان": the passages are a conquest of the town and its ruler, not a description of a Mughal suba.
- **The tribute figure "هشت[OCR?]" is flagged.**
- **Daud's two campaigns.** Daud b. Nasir is made "ایل ومنقاد" in the second campaign (p. 420) and imprisoned in the fourth (p. 417). The book presents these in sequence, and both are recorded.
- **"غورک[OCR?]"** is not located by the book.
- **Held identity.** shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari, the held "Shams" (Multan), is on the line only place-level. No "Shams" is named.
- **Cross-chunk.** Chunk 032 (p. 405) has Shihab al-Din taking Multan "ازتصرف قرامطه" as well. Here the Qaramita are seized under Mahmud (p. 417). Both are left for the consolidator.

**Peshawar (10 rows): place-level, weigh accordingly**
- **What attaches here:** the old names "فرشاور", "پرشاور" and "بگرام", and the battle of "۳۹۱" (p. 424).
- **The editor's footnote line on p. 424 is "[illegible]".** It sits under the eLibrary stamp. Its only visible fragment, "(۱) ... شاور [OCR?]", is printed against marker (۱), although the note on Peshawar is marker (۴). Which note the fragment belongs to is not settled.
- **Jaipal's fate.** "راجه جیپال با پانزده نفر از پسر و برادر و خویش اسیر گشته بقتل رسید" (p. 424) is quoted as printed. The sentence breaks off at "جائے که شهباز همت". The chunk afterwards names Bhatinda "که دار الحکومت راجه جیپال بود" (p. 423) and "راجه انندپال بن راجه جیپال" (p. 420), but it does not settle whether Jaipal himself or only his companions were killed.
