# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 044 — pp. 542–552

This chunk reads from p. 552 down to p. 542, because the scan runs back to front, and every page join checks. It opens inside the river section of suba Shahjahanabad (Delhi), whose heading is not in this chunk; the book names that suba "صوبه شاهجهان آباد" at p. 548. That section covers the Jamna from the hills past Shahjahanabad, Mathura and Agra to Allahabad, and the Ganga from its source by Kailas, Badrinath and Hardwar down to Dhaka and its mouths (pp. 550–552). It then gives that suba's boundaries and revenue (p. 550). Five suba sections follow:
- "صوبه مستقر الخلافه اکبر آباد" (pp. 548–550): Agra, Bayana, Fathpur Sikri, Gwalior, Kalpi, Mathura, Kannauj, and Shah Madar's tomb at Makanpur.
- "صوبه وسیعه فسیحه اله اباد" (pp. 546–548): Prag/Tirbeni, Banaras, Chunar, Kalinjar and Jaunpur.
- "صوبه اوده" (pp. 545–546): Ajodhya, Kabir's tomb, Salar Masud at Bahraich, Nimkhar, Lucknow and Bilgram.
- "صوبه بهار عرف پتنه" (pp. 542–544): Patna, Baijnath, Tirhut, Rohtas and the rivers.
- "صوبه عمده بنگالا" (p. 542), which opens at the foot of the chunk and continues on p. 541 (chunk 043).

**Everything described lies in India. This is a zero-mapping chunk: no archive id is assigned.** The only Pakistan-side material is one line, where suba Lahore is named as suba Delhi's western neighbour (p. 550).

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE punjab (book's words: "صوبه لاهور"; refers to the author's time, c. 1107 AH, a Mughal suba; the book gives no other period):
  - Suba Shahjahanabad (Delhi) is bounded "جانب مشرق صوبه اکبر آباد و مغرب صوبه لاهور و جنوب اجمیر و شمال کوهستان کمایون". Suba Lahore lies on its west (p. 550).
  - The book says nothing else about suba Lahore here and does not draw the line between the two subas. For suba Delhi's length it gives "از پلول جانب اکبر آباد تا لودیانه ساحل دریائے ستلج … صد و شصت کروه". It also lists "سهرند" among suba Delhi's eight sarkars (p. 550). The book itself places both of these inside suba Delhi, so they take no province (see Doubts).

The sentence after the annual gathering, "در زمان سلطان ابراهیم شرقی درسنه ۸۴۵[OCR?] ازانجا مطلبی دراصل[OCR?] کم شده", is unclear (p. 548; see Doubts).

  - **Shaikh Mina, Lucknow:** "شیخ مینا که مردم را گمان ولایت بدوست درانجا اسوده اند" (p. 545).
  - **Shaikh Muhammad Ghaus, Gwalior:** "مزار شیخ محمد غوث است که در زمان خود در رتبه ولایت داشت" (p. 549).
  - **Shaikh Salim, Sikri:** Akbar built there "باشاره زبده اولیائے کرام شیخ سلیم": a stone fort, buildings, "مساجد و مدارس و خوانق". He named it Fathpur and made it "دار الخلافه" (p. 549).
  - **Agra and Kalpi:** At Agra "اکثر مزارات اولیائے عظام و فضلائے کرام در دست", and the tombs of Akbar and Shah Jahan stand near the city. Kalpi is "خوابگاه بسیار اولیا" (p. 549).

  - **Baijnath:** "در سرکار مونگیر … در دامن کوه بیج ناتهه مکانی است منسوب بمهادیو" (p. 544). Its pipal and its cave appear under Legends.
  - **Nimkhar:** a fort on the "گودی", with a pool "منسوب به برهما" (p. 545).
  - **A temple 30 kos south of Patna (unnamed):** people give alms there for their ancestors' souls (p. 544).
  - **Prag (Allahabad):** in common speech "ترمینی[OCR?]", for the meeting of three rivers. The ancient tree "اکهی برگو" stands in the fort. Hindus hold the place to be "بادشاه معبد" (p. 548).

    - Badrinath and Kailas (p. 552).
    - "هماچل(۱)", where "پاندوان … ابدان خود را بدانجا گداخته اند" (p. 552).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Gatherings and offerings at Muslim tombs:**
  - Bahraich (Salar Masud): "طوایف انام از دور دستها با علم زرین بزیارت می آیند و انجمنها می آرایند و فراوان نذور می گذرانند" (p. 546).
  - Makanpur (Shah Madar): "در سالی یکمرتبه طوایف انام از ممالک دور دست با علمهائے زرین آمده ازدحام می کنند و نذرات می گذرانند". There are several days of crowds and "تماشائے غریب" (p. 548).
  - Ajodhya: the tombs of Seth and Job are a "زیارت گاه اهل اسلام" (p. 546).
- **The state and religious sites:**
  - **Prag:** pilgrims come at "مکر", when the sun enters Capricorn. They stay a month, bathe daily and give alms, "و نیز مبلغی سراسامی بسرکار بادشاهی ادامی سازند": they also pay a per-head sum to the imperial treasury (pp. 547–548).
  - **Prag:** some once sought salvation by "خویشتن را در ارّه میدرا ودند[OCR?]". The book says "در عهد سلطنت حضرت شاهجهان بادشاه این عمل منع کردند" (p. 547).
  - **Prag:** "بفرموده حضرت نور الدین محمد جهانگیر بادشاه" the ancient tree was cut and an iron plate fixed over it (p. 548).
  - **Mathura:** "معبد کیشورائے … بحکم عالمگیر بادشاه انداخته مسجد مقرر کرده اند". The faujdar "عبد النبی خان" built a stairway on the Jamna, called "بسرانت", and a mosque in the middle of the city (p. 549).
  - **Sikri:** Akbar's buildings at Shaikh Salim's indication (p. 549).
  - **Jaunpur:** "فوجدار انجا را دایماً بگردن کشان آماده محاربه باید بود" (p. 547).
  - **Prisons:** Bayana's fort formerly held "اسیران عصیان هند". Gwalior holds "زندانیان مستوجب سیاست" (p. 549).
- **Hindu pilgrimage (brief):**
  - **Hardwar:** the main gathering is at "بیساکهی", and the largest at "کنبهه", which comes "بعد دوازده سال". Pilgrims bathe, give alms and shave their heads and beards, and cast the bones of the dead into the Ganga. Its water is carried far off (p. 551).
  - **The Ganga's water:** "سلاطین هندوستان و امرائے والاشان بهر جا که باشند اب گنگ میخورند" (p. 551).
  - **Banaras:** the old and the sick come there to die (p. 547).
  - **Ancestor rites south of Patna:** held in "چله دی" (p. 544).
  - **Badrinath:** pilgrims cross rope bridges called "چهینکه" in fear (p. 552).
- **Learning:**
  - Tirhut is "از دیرباز دار العلم و جامع هندی فضیلت" (p. 544).
  - Banaras is "معدن فضیلت و جامع فضلائے مدرسه طلبه علم هند" (p. 547).
- **Revenue and trade:**
  - **Bengal's revenue custom:** "رسم ضبط و غله بخشی نیست ادائے مالواجب بر نسق است رعایا در سال هشت ماه محصول را پایه پایه ادا سازند" (p. 542).
  - **The Bahraich market:** goods from the northern hills come in on men's backs and on hill ponies ("اسپ کونت"):
    - metals: gold, copper and lead;
    - musk, "قطاس", honey and "چوک ترش";
    - hawks: "باز و جره و شاهین و باشه";
    - and more.
    
    The market is a gathering of several months (pp. 545–546).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Baijnath's pipal (p. 544):**
  - A temple servant in need fasts beneath it and prays to Mahadev. After two or three days a leaf falls, "مخطوط بخط هندی رقم زده کلک غیب". It is a draft for a sum on some person "هر چند که مسکن ان پانصد کروه باشد", naming him, his family and his home.
  - The temple writes it out as a "هندوی بیجناتهه", and the person named pays "بلا عذر و حیله".
- **Baijnath's cave (p. 544):** once a year, on "شیوراتت یعنی شیوبرت", the chief servant brings earth out of the cave. "بقدر نصیب هر کدام ازان خاک طلا می شود".
- **Tirhut (p. 544):** a milk-seller who waters his milk suffers "از غیب گزندی".
- **Nimkhar (p. 545):**
  - Brahma's pool boils so that no one can sink in it.
  - Hindu books lost to time were brought to light again on its bank.
  - At a nearby spring, while Brahmins chant, "از ریگ پیکر مهادیو پدید آید" and soon vanishes.
  - At "چرمتی", fire kindles itself in the heat.
- **Bilgram (p. 545):** whoever drinks from a certain well for forty days gains understanding and comeliness.
- **Prag (p. 548):** the tree Jahangir had cut grew up through the iron plate, "بحکمت ایزدی".
- **Banaras (p. 547):** when Jupiter enters Leo, a small hill rises from the Ganga for a month. It is "نموداریست از بدایع قدرت ایزدی".
- **Kalinjar (p. 547):** "معبدگاه بهیرون که شگرف داستانها ازان برگذارند".
- **Ajodhya (p. 546):** Rama's bridge, his armies of monkeys and bears, and Ravan.
- **The Ganga's source (p. 552):** by Hindu belief it descends "از بهشت", by Kailas and through China. Firdausi's Shahnameh is cited for Siyavush's stone buildings on the Ganga. The Pandavs dissolved their bodies at "هماچل(۱)".
- **The Ganga's water (p. 551):**
  - It never spoils, even if kept for years.
  - It heals the sick and strengthens the healthy.
- **The Ganga's banks (p. 550):** the people at the river's head and mouth are robbers and rebels. Travellers explain this by sins washed off in the river being reborn there "بطریق تناسخ".
- **Other rivers (p. 543):**
  - The Gandak's water swells the throat ("جاغور").
  - Saligram stones are found within forty kos of the Gandak.
  - The Karamnasa is shunned, lest a drop touch the body.
  - "گویند که هفتاد و دو دریائے کشتی رو" join the Ganga by Patna. This is repeated on p. 551.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's own testimony (p. 544).** He reports a Baijnath hundi paid by himself: "برنگارنده این صحیفه زنار داری هندوی بیجناتهه آورده بود سعادت خود دانسته ادائے زر کرده". He presents the whole as "امری عجیب … که باعث حیرت ظاهر بینان میتواند بود".
- **He checks popular explanation against the Hindu books (p. 548).** After the common account of Tirbeni, he notes "اما در کتب هند برامدن سرستی ازانجا مندرج نیست".
- **He marks belief and hearsay as such:**
  - On the Ganga's source: "هیچکس واقف نیست اما اعتقاد اهل هند انکه از بهشت نازل میشود" (p. 552).
  - On the people of the Ganga's banks: "از سیاحان ممالک چنان استماع یافته" (p. 550).
- **He grades sainthood:**
  - Shaikh Mina is one "که مردم را گمان ولایت بدوست" (p. 545). This is reported, not asserted.
  - Shaikh Muhammad Ghaus "در زمان خود در رتبه ولایت داشت" (p. 549), and Shah Madar "از اولیائے بزرگ هند بود" (p. 548). Both are asserted.

## Doubts

- **Zero mappings.** The chunk covers suba Delhi's rivers and the subas of Agra, Allahabad, Awadh, Bihar and the opening of Bengal. All lie in India, and the brief gives them no rows. No Pakistani city, saint or site is named. The only Pakistan-side word is "صوبه لاهور" (p. 550).
- **Person-level candidates deliberately not mapped**, under brief §9.316 rule 1 (a deity named at a particular non-archive site):
  - **"سری کشن" at Mathura (p. 549).** This is the brief's own example, so the two Krishna rows are not mapped.
  - **"مهادیو" at Baijnath (p. 544), Nimkhar (p. 545) and Banaras (p. 547).** Shiva's 8 rows are not mapped.
  - **"راجه رام چندر" at Ajodhya (p. 546).** Rama is the principal figure of a single row, `ram-mandir-saidpur-ram-kund-mandir`. The §9.316 rule speaks of "many-row person ids", but its examples include "a Guru at an Indian town". I applied it by analogy, as a deity at a particular non-archive site. If the consolidator holds that the single-row person rule (18 Sept) prevails, the Ajodhya bullet is the material.
  - **"پاندوان" (p. 552).** The Pandavs; `panj-tirath`'s principal figure is "King Pandu (Mahabharata)", a different person, so not mapped. "پاندوگڈه" (p. 546) is a place, Allahabad's southern limit.
- **The province bullet** is province-level; a Mughal suba, not the modern province's boundaries; the book's own unit, so weigh accordingly.
  - It is only an adjacency statement inside suba Delhi's description. It says nothing about suba Lahore's own content.
  - "لودیانه ساحل دریائے ستلج" and the sarkar "سهرند" are placed by the book inside suba Delhi, not suba Lahore. Ludhiana is not in `toponym_map.txt`, and Sirhind is on the brief's outside-Pakistan list. Neither takes a province or a row.
- **Name traps, none mapped:**
  - "حضرت جلال الدین محمد اکبر بادشاه" (pp. 548–550) is Akbar, not Jalaluddin Bukhari.
  - "حضرت شهاب الدین محمد شاهجهان بادشاه" (p. 549) is Shah Jahan.
  - "شاه مدار دشهباز" (p. 549) is at Makanpur in sarkar Kannauj, not Lal Shahbaz Qalandar. "دشهباز" is printed as one word without a flag, and I offer no reading.
  - "بارئ" (p. 548) is a sarkar of suba Agra, not Bari Imam.
  - "گورکهپور" (p. 545) is a sarkar, not Guru Gorakhnath.
  - "سری نگر" (p. 552) lies on the Ganga between Badrinath and "رکهی کیش", "بمسکن مرزبان ان ولایت". It is not the Kashmir valley, and there is no row either way.
  - "سلطان محمود غزنوی" (p. 546) is named only as Salar Masud's kinsman. He is not a principal figure in `shrine_index.tsv`; the Malik Ayaz row's figure is Malik Ayaz.
- **The Shah Madar sentence (p. 548)** reads "در زمان سلطان ابراهیم شرقی درسنه ۸۴۵[OCR?] ازانجا مطلبی دراصل[OCR?] کم شده".
  - Both the year and the key word are flagged.
  - The words may be a note that text is lost from the source, "مطلبی در اصل گم شده". The next sentence turns abruptly to the suba's rivers. This reading is tentative.
  - The year should not be attached to Shah Madar or to any event. It is carried as printed and not converted.
- **"اله آباش" (p. 548)** is printed so, without a flag, where the book names Allahabad. It is not repaired.
- **Editor's footnotes:**
