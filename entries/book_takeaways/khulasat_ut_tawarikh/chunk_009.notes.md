# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 009 — pp. 102–115

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens at p. 115, continuing from the previous chunk. Shah Jahan, still a prince, besieges Thatta, which is held for Shahzada Shahryar. He gives the siege up and goes back to the Deccan, to Junnar[OCR?]. Next is "بیان رسیدن مهابت خان در حضور اشرف اقدس و مصدر گستاخی گردیدن و آصف خان را قید کردن" (pp. 109–115). Mahabat Khan is disgraced, then seizes the emperor on the bank of the "دریای بهت". Nur Jahan fails to fight her way across the river, and Asaf Khan is taken at the fort of "اتک بنارس". The court goes to Kabul and comes back, and Mahabat is sent off toward Thatta; he then flees to the Rana's hills and finally joins Shah Jahan. "در بیان رحلت نمودن حضرت خاقان زمان بممالک جاودان" (pp. 106–109) covers the emperor's last journey from Kashmir: a hunting accident at "بهیرم کله[OCR?]", his death on "بست و هشتم شهر صفر سنه ۱۰۳۷[OCR?]", the succession struggle at Lahore (Dawar Bakhsh enthroned, Shahryar blinded, the princes put to death "در لاهور"), and his burial by the Ravi at Shahdara. The emperor is named as Jahangir in the chronogram and the following heading. The last section is headed "ابو المظفر شهاب الدین محمد شاه جهان بادشاه غازی صاحب قران ثانی خلف سویم نورالدین محمد جهانگیر بادشاه" (pp. 102–106). It covers coin and khutba in Shah Jahan's name at Lahore, the runner Banarasi bringing the news, the march by Gujarat and Ajmer, the accession at Akbarabad with its chronograms and a moonlight feast, and a short summary of the reign that points readers to Muhammad Waris's "شاهجهان نامه". It ends with the realm divided among the four sons and the illness of "هفتم[OCR?] ذی الحجه سنه ۱۰۶۶[OCR?]", where the chunk breaks off (p. 102).

  - **The fight.** Asaf Khan had set Dawar Bakhsh on the throne with "سکه و خطبه" in his name. When Dawar Bakhsh and the amirs reached "نواحی لاهور", Shahryar drew up his ranks against them. At the first shock he saw his lines break and fled, and "بقلعه درآمده بپائے خود در دام افتاد" (he went into the fort and walked into the trap himself). Most of his men took guarantees ("قول گرفته") and went over to Asaf Khan (p. 107).
  - **The fort.** "روز دیگر داور بخش در قلعه ارک رسیده بر تخت خلافت نشست". Shahryar, "که نتوانست قلعه هم مضبوط نگاه داشت", was brought out of the harem with both hands tied with his own waistband and set before Dawar Bakhsh's throne. He made his kornish and was imprisoned at once. Two days later he was blinded ("میل در چشمش کشیده") (p. 107).
  - **Shahryar's verse.** He spoke a quatrain extempore, ending "بگو کور شد دیده آفتاب". Danyal's sons Tahmuras and Hoshang were taken and confined a few days later (p. 107).
  - **The killings.** Shah Jahan sent orders from the Deccan to Asaf Khan, who held "عنان اختیار سلطنت". By those orders, "داور بخش و گشتاسپ برادرش و شهریار و طهمورث و هوشنگ را در لاهور مسافر صحرائے عدم نموده": they were sent "to the desert of non-existence" in Lahore (p. 107).
  - **The body.** The dead emperor's body ("نقش[OCR?]" [نعش?]) was sent "از چکر[OCR?] هستی[OCR?] مصحوب[OCR?] مقصود خان بلاهور", from Chakar[OCR?] Hasti[OCR?] to Lahore in Maqsud Khan's charge. It was buried by the Ravi at Shahdara (see Other) (pp. 106–107).
  - **Shah Jahan's coin and khutba.** "روز یکشنبه بست و دویم[OCR?] جمادی الاول سنه ۱۰۳۷[OCR?] هجری در ایوان خاص و عام دولتخانه والا واقعه دارالسلطنت لاهور". In the hall of public and private audience of the palace at Lahore, the pillars of state fixed "سکه و خطبه" in Shah Jahan's name. His age is given as "سی و هفت سال" lunar and "سی و شش سال" solar (p. 106).
  - **Passing mentions.** Mahabat Khan told Afzal Khan "هرگاه از لاهور بگذرم آصف خان را خلاص کرده روانه حضور پر نور مینمایم" (p. 110). After the accession, Asaf Khan "از لاهور در اکبرآباد رسیده" and was made "وکیل السلطنت و مدار الملک" (p. 103).

**Thatta (no row)**
- "در تهتهه شریف الملک که نوکر[OCR?] شاهزاده شهریار" fortified the fort with "توپ و تفنگ". Shah Jahan camped round it and fought for some days, and some of his good men fell. He judged that "تسخیر تهتهه وقت ضایع کردن از صلاح دور است" (p. 115).
- He left before Mahabat Khan, sent against him "بجانب تهتهه", could arrive, and went "از راه ولایت بهار و گجرات" to the Deccan, to "ناسک تربنک از مضافات احمدنگر" and Junnar[OCR?] (p. 115).

**The Bihat river, Attock and Kabul (Mahabat Khan's coup)**
- Mahabat came with "پنجهزار سوار راجپوت" to "لب دریای بهت", where the camp was on its way "سیر و شکار کابل" (p. 114).
- Asaf Khan crossed by the bridge with his household, and most amirs and the karkhanas followed him. That left the emperor "این روی آب" with few men. Mahabat set two thousand horse to fire the bridge. He entered the palace with two hundred Rajputs, broke the door of the ghusl-khana and took the emperor to his own tent on an elephant (pp. 113–114).
- Nur Jahan tried to cross back, with Asaf Khan and Khwaja Abul Hasan. On her elephant, she drew an arrow from an attendant's arm "بدست خود". Her elephant was wounded and swam back across (p. 112).
- Asaf Khan fled to "قلعه اتک بنارس که جاگیر او بود" and shut himself in (p. 112). On the way to Kabul, Mahabat took him there, with Abu Talib and others (p. 111).
- At Kabul, a clash between Rajputs and ahadis left "قریب هشتصد راجپوت" dead. Khwaja Qasim and Badi al-Zaman were paraded bareheaded "در بازار کابل" (p. 111).
- Mahabat released Asaf Khan on the Bihat's bank as he set out for Thatta. The author: "از غرایب آنکه مشهورش[OCR?] مهابت خان بر ساحل دریای بهت واقع شده بود و خلاصی آصف خان … نیز بر ساحل همین دریا اتفاق افتاد" (p. 110).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Coin and khutba mark sovereignty.** They were struck and read first for Dawar Bakhsh (p. 107), then for Shah Jahan at Lahore (p. 106).
- **Jagirs.**
  - Attock was Asaf Khan's jagir (p. 112).
  - Mahabat Khan received the subadari of Ajmer and parganas round it in jagir (pp. 104–105). Later his jagir mahals and the Ajmer subadari were given to the Khankhanan (p. 109).
  - Rana Karan Singh[OCR?]'s jagir was confirmed (p. 104).
- **Recovering money.**
  - Mahabat Khan was charged with "زر کلیه مطالبه بسرکار معلی", with sums he had taken from amirs' jagir mahals, and with the Bengal elephants (pp. 114–115).
  - About "یک لک روپیه" in cash and goods was taken from Barkhurdar (p. 114).
  - "محمد محسن کروری پرگنه بتاله[OCR?]" was jailed and money was got from him "بضرب و شلاق[OCR?]" (p. 114).
- **Mansabs and subadaris at Ahmadabad.** Sher Khan Barha[OCR?] received 5000 zat / 5000 sawar and Gujarat. Mirza Isa Tarkhan received 4000 / 2000 and Thatta (p. 105).
- **Gifts.** Rana Karan Singh received a robe, "دکهکمی[OCR?] لعل قطبی قیمت بسی هزار روپیه", a jewelled sword, a dagger, an elephant and a horse with a gold saddle (p. 104).
- **Orders to provincial officials.** Farmans to subadars, faujdars and amils enjoined "معموری ملک و آبادی رعیت", justice and "امنیت طرق و مسالک" (p. 102).
- **Court ceremonies.** The accession durbar at Akbarabad (p. 104). A "جشن ماهتابے" (moonlight feast) at which walls, courts, trees and furnishings were all dressed in white (p. 103).
- **The royal hunt at Bahiram Kala[OCR?].** Zamindars drove deer up to the crest, and the emperor shot from a stand at the mountain's foot (p. 109).
- **A tomb building.** A building was put up over Jahangir's grave at Shahdara (p. 106; see Other).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Leaving the reign to another book.** The author declines to narrate Shah Jahan's reign in detail. He refers readers to Muhammad Waris's "شاهجهان نامه" and says a full account would need "کتابے علیحده" (p. 102).
- **On Mahabat Khan's disgrace.**
  - He blames divine will, "چشم زخمی", for "امری که از صلاح دور و بفساد نزدیک". He says Mahabat was rebuked "بیموجب", at the request of Nur Jahan and Asaf Khan (p. 115).
  - He faults Asaf Khan's "نهایت غفلت و عدم احتیاط" (p. 114) and calls him the man "که این همه شورش ازو بود" (p. 112).
- **On the emperor.** He was "مست باده عشق و اسیر دام محبت نورجهان بیگم" (p. 113), and he twice put his hand to his sword before being dissuaded (p. 113).
- **On Dawar Bakhsh.** The amirs saw him as "گوسپند قربانی", a sacrificial sheep (p. 107).
- **Coincidence and omen.** The author notes the coincidence at the Bihat (p. 110) and reads the footman's fall as the angel of death appearing (p. 108).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 115 down to p. 102.
  - It opens at p. 115 with "چون در تهتهه شریف الملک…", inside the account of Shah Jahan's movements begun in the previous chunk. P. 110 refers back to it ("چنانچه سابقاً تحریر یافته").
  - It ends mid-sentence at p. 102, "…و تشریف لمین[OCR?] نمی آوردند", which continues in the next chunk.
- All page joins read through.
  - The 113→112 join runs through "[illegible]" into a verse.
  - At the 111→110 join the words before "حکم صادر فرمود" are illegible, so the order's opening is lost.
- A library stamp on p. 110 ("Sri Satguru Jagjit Singh Ji eLibrary") is not text.
- **Scope.** Despite the task label, the chunk is mostly Jahangir's last years and Shah Jahan's accession. Shah Jahan's later reign takes up only the summary on p. 102.
- **The emperor in pp. 107–115** is "حضرت خاقان زمان" / "آنحضرت". He is named only through the chronogram "جهانگیر از جهان رفت" and the heading on p. 106.
- **Narrative order.** P. 115 reports news of Parviz's death in the Deccan. The new section on the same page then has Mahabat Khan with Parviz and taking leave of him (pp. 114–115). The book steps back in time with the new heading. This is left as printed.
