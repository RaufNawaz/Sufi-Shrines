# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 012 — pp. 143–155

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-narrative at the end of the account of Shaikh Abu'l-Fazl's death (pp. 155–153): the ruler's grief, the force sent against "راجه بیر سنگه دیو بدال[OCR?]", the lineage of Abu'l-Fazl, his father Shaikh Mubarak's trials and stipend, Faizi's and Abu'l-Fazl's arrival at court, Abu'l-Fazl's fall from favour, his posting to the Deccan, and the author's praise of him. Next, "در بیان بغی[OCR?] شاهزاده سلیم خلف بزرگ" (pp. 153–151) covers Prince Salim at Ajmer and then Allahabad ("اله باس[OCR?]"), his taking of the Bihar treasury, the reconciliation through Salima Sultan Begam and Maryam Makani, and Maryam Makani's death. Then come "در بیان رحلت شاهزاده سلطان دانیال" (pp. 151–150), Daniyal's death from drink in the Deccan, and "در بیان رحلت حضرت خاقان زمان" (pp. 150–148), the old emperor's illness, death at Agra, burial in the Sikandra garden, chronogram and length of reign. The new reign is headed "ابو المظفر نور الدین محمد جهانگیر بادشاه عرف شاهزاده سلطان سلیم بن جلال الدین محمد اکبر بادشاه" (pp. 148–147), covering the accession at Agra and the first titles, including Shaikh Farid Bukhari as mir bakhshi. "در بیان بغی شاهزاده سلطان خسرو خلف بزرگ و دستگیر شدن او" (pp. 147–144) follows Khusrau's flight to Lahore, his failure to take Lahore fort, the battle with Shaikh Farid Bukhari near Goindwal[OCR?], his capture near Gujrat, the punishments at Lahore and his later death. The chunk ends with "توجه موکب والا بلاهور[OCR?] کابل و بعضی سوانح بدائع در منزل علی مسجد وغیره" (pp. 144–143): the trip from Lahore to Kabul, the Jahanara garden, and the inquiry into the incorrupt body at "خواجه تابوت" near Bamiyan (p. 143).

Khusrau was led on an elephant between the stakes, then "در قلعه آورده در زندان تادیب محبوس نمودند" (p. 145).
  - "در آغاز سال دویم از لاهور متوجه سیر و شکار کابل شدند": at the start of the second regnal year the emperor left Lahore for Kabul (p. 144).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Khwaja Tabut, near Bamiyan (outside Pakistan)**
- "درمیان ضحاک[OCR?] و بامیان که جانب بلخ سرحد کابل واقع شده کوهی است که درآن مدفن خواجه تابوت میگویند": the book places "a burial called Khwaja Tabut" in a mountain between Zahak[OCR?] and Bamiyan, on the Balkh side of the Kabul frontier (p. 143).
- What was reported at Kabul: four hundred years are given since his death, and his limbs have not decayed. "مردم رفته زیارت میکنند": people go there on ziyarat. On his neck there is a wound that bleeds when the cotton is lifted from it and does not stop until the cotton is put back (p. 143).
- The inquiry: "معتمد خان عرف محمد شریف مؤلف اقبال نامه جهانگیری" was sent with a surgeon to see the wound with his own eyes. Led by local people, he went under a hill by Bamiyan [illegible] and found a door two and a half "درعه[OCR?]" above the ground. Inside were an ivan and a square chamber "چهار درعه[OCR?] در چهار درعه[OCR?]" holding a coffin (p. 143).
- The body lay "بآئین اسلام رو بقبله", the left hand over the private parts, with half a درعه[OCR?] of karpas over them. Parts touching the ground had decayed. The rest was sound: eyes closed, a tooth showing above and one below, nails of hands and feet intact. "لیکن زخم معلوم نگشت": but no wound was found (p. 143).
- The elders of the region said that "در جنگ چنگیز خان و سلطان جلال الدین منکبرنی[OCR?] در [illegible] مسلمان مرد شهید شده" and had lain there ever since (p. 143).
- On the report the emperor said "از عجایب قدرتهای ایزدی و بدایع حکمتهای سرمدی وقوع این غرایب چه عجب" (p. 143).

**Holy men and scholars**
- "چند روپ[OCR?] سنیاسی[OCR?]": some years after Khusrau's imprisonment the emperor met him and "از صحبت آن درویش خدا اندیش بسیار محظوظ شدند". "آن صاحب حال و قال" interceded "بدلایل معقول و منقول" for Khusrau's release. The sentence breaks at "[illegible] یافت" (p. 145), and the next page opens "اما خلاصی او نگردید": he was not released (p. 144).
- Shaikh Mubarak, father of Abu'l-Fazl and son of "شیخ خضر", was "افضل الفضلا و عالم العلما" and taught "در شهر آگره". Because he was "درویش نهاد و فقیر وضع" and "بطریقه صلح کل زیست می کرد", some mullas accused him of leaving Islam, drew up a mahzar and had muftis write a fatwa for his death. He fled with his sons and hid. Through amirs who had been his pupils the truth reached the emperor, and his accusers were shamed (p. 155). He then resumed teaching "در امان", and "وظیفه از سرکار معلی مقرر گشت" (p. 154). He wrote a tafsir of the Quran without putting the emperor's name in it (p. 154).
- Shaikh Abu'l-Faiz "که در اشعار فیضی تخلص داشت", Mubarak's eldest son, came to court "در سال دوازدهم[OCR?] جلوس مقدس" (p. 154).
- Shaikh Abu'l-Fazl (pp. 152–155):
  - In "سال نوزدهم" he presented a tafsir of Ayat al-Kursi in the emperor's name (p. 154).
  - He rose above the amirs and was envied by courtiers and princes. After Mubarak's death he had the father's tafsir copied without the emperor's name and sent it to "ایران و توران و روم[OCR?] و شام". The emperor was angered, and Prince Salim and the amirs used the moment; he was barred from the kornish, then forgiven (p. 154).
  - He was sent to the Deccan so that he might value the emperor's favour (pp. 154–153). "بالاخر ممبطلی[OCR?] که نوشته شد درجه شهادت یافت" (p. 153).
  - The author's praise: he finished all the current sciences at fifteen[OCR?], read the scriptures of all religions "توریت و انجیل و صحف هنود", took the Deccan by the sword and rose to "پنجهزاری[OCR?]" and the rank of sipahsalar (p. 153).
  - The emperor's grief: two days and nights without food or sleep. "بر شاهزاده بسیار اعتراضات گشت": many reproaches fell on the prince (p. 155).
  - "راۓ رایان پتهرداس[OCR?]" (a three-thousandi and faujdar of those parts) and "شیخ عبدالرحمن ولد شیخ ابوالفضل" were sent against "راجه بیر سنگه دیو بدال[OCR?]" with orders not to stop until they brought his head (p. 155).
- Shaikh Farid Bukhari (not Baba Farid; see Doubts):
  - "از سادات عظام موسوی و تربیت یافته حضور حضرت بادشاه جنت آرامگاه". He had held the junior bakhshi's office, and was raised to "پنجهزاری[OCR?] ذات" and mir bakhshi at the accession (p. 147).
  - As "بخشی الممالک" he was sent ahead the same night Khusrau fled (p. 147). He came "با لشکر گران در نواحی سلطان پور" and fought Khusrau near "گوندوال[OCR?]". The emperor embraced him and spent the night in his tent (p. 146).
  - He received the title Murtaza Khan. At his request "در پرگنه نهروال[OCR?] بمکانی که شیخ رایت فتح بر افراشته بود شهر آباد و سرای وسیع احداث نموده بفتح آباد[OCR?] موسوم گردانید", and the pargana was given him in jagir (p. 144).
  - At the old emperor's death, Hakim Ali "بجانب شیخ فرید بخاری گریخته رفت" (p. 149).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Shrines visited for a ruler's health.** During the old emperor's last illness "خیر خواهان برای صحت و شفا بمزارات شریفه و اماکن متبرکه [illegible]": well-wishers [went] to the noble tombs and blessed places for his health and recovery. The sentence breaks at the gap, and no shrine is named (p. 150).
- **Ziyarat at Khwaja Tabut.** "مردم رفته زیارت میکنند" (p. 143). It is the subject of a state inquiry sent with a surgeon (p. 143).
- **A victory town.** A town and a large sarai named Fathabad[OCR?] were founded at the battle site in pargana Nahrwal[OCR?], and the pargana was given in jagir to Shaikh Farid (p. 144).
- **Treasury and revenue.**
  - Salim took "سی لک[OCR?] روپیه خزانه صوبه بهار و آن حدود" collected by the diwan "کهنسور داس[OCR?]" (editor's footnote variant: "کنسور داس[OCR?]"). He also took suba Allahabad, "که بجاگیر آصف خان جعفر تعلق داشت", into his own sarkar (p. 152).
  - Nazr and peshkash at the reconciliation: "یک هزار[OCR?] مهر طلا بجیغه نذر و نهصد[OCR?] و هفتاد و هفت[OCR?] مثقال زنجیر فیل پیشکش" (p. 151).
- **State stipend to a scholar-dervish.** "وظیفه از سرکار معلی مقرر گشت" for Shaikh Mubarak (p. 154).
- **Mahzar and fatwa** against Mubarak, sealed by notables (p. 155).
- **Zamindars and local officials.** Raja Basu of the Punjab hills (p. 146) and the "چودهری[OCR?]" of Sodhra[OCR?], who controlled the boatmen at the ford (p. 146).
- **Mourning custom.**
  - At Maryam Makani's death the emperor "بآئین نیاکان خویش سر و ریش مبارک ستریده[OCR?] لباس ماتم پوشیدند", shouldered the bier for a few steps, and sent it to Delhi (p. 151).
  - At the old emperor's death the harem women tore their hair and scratched their faces (p. 149).
- **Titles and mansabs at the accession** (p. 147), and the chronogram by Asaf Khan Jafar (p. 148).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Khwaja Tabut: reported at Kabul, his body uncorrupted after four hundred years, and a wound on the neck that bleeds whenever the cotton is lifted and stops only when it is put back. The book attributes this to report ("میگویند", "بعرض رسید"). Mu'tamad Khan's inquiry found the body partly intact, but "زخم معلوم نگشت" (p. 143).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On Khusrau.** The author blames "صحبت خوشامد گویان کور باطن" for his ambition. He reports that the late emperor ("حضرت بادشاه غفران پناه") had said at his death that Salim "عیش دوست است قابلیت سلطنت ندارد" and Khusrau was "قابل خلافت", which lodged as "ماخولیا[OCR?]" in Khusrau's head (p. 147). He calls him "شاهزاده نادان" (p. 146). Shahjahan's part in his death is given only as rumour, "بر زبانها افتاد" (p. 144).
- **On Salim at Allahabad.** His actions without orders showed "آثار بغی و گردن تابی", yet the author says "ارباب غرض سخنان دور از کار از جانب شاهزاده بعرض ... رسانیدند" (p. 152).
- **On Shaikh Mubarak's accusers.** They acted "از روۓ غایت عداوت و عناد" (p. 155).
- **On Abu'l-Fazl.** There is a long eulogy, and the author wishes he had lived his natural span or given his life in some notable service (p. 153).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 155 down to p. 143.
  - It opens mid-narrative at "همانجا ماند" (p. 155), inside the account of Abu'l-Fazl's death begun in the previous chunk.
  - It ends with the return "بهندوستان فیض نشان" and a "؎" (p. 143). The Kabul section presumably continues on p. 142.
  - Every page join reads through, though the joins at pp. 147→146 and 145→144 cross an `[illegible]`.
- Library stamps on pp. 143 and 154 are not text. The margin note on p. 144 ("[illegible] شاه لالا[OCR?]") is unplaced.
- **The rulers.** The dying emperor "حضرت خاقان زمان" is named by the chunk itself as Akbar ("فوت اکبر شه", p. 148; "جلال الدین محمد اکبر بادشاه", p. 148), and the new ruler as Jahangir (p. 148). "حضرت بادشاه جنت آرامگاه" (p. 147), under whom Farid Bukhari was raised, is not identified by the chunk.

**"فرید" is not Baba Farid**
- Every "فرید" in the chunk is "شیخ فرید بخاری" (pp. 144, 146, 147, 149), "از سادات عظام موسوی" (p. 147), titled Murtaza Khan (p. 144). **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar is NOT mapped.**
- "بزم فریدونی" (p. 148) is Faridun.
- "بخاری" in his name is not taken to any Bukhari row (Mauj Darya, Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh, Shah Jamal).
