# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 027 — pp. 335–347

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-couplet ("چو دخلت نیست خرج آهسته تر کن", p. 347) in the reign of the sultan whom the book later calls "سلطان محمدشاه" (p. 344) and "سلطان محمد شاه الف خان[OCR?] مرحوم" (p. 339): his failed campaign into "کوه همانچل", the revolt of Malik Bahram at Multan and the sparing of Multan at the intercession of "شیخ الاسلام شیخ رکن الدین", the ruin of the Doab, the famine at Delhi, his recognition by the Abbasid caliph in Egypt, and his death four kos from Thatta after 26 years (pp. 345–347). Then comes "سلطان فیروزشاه باربک[OCR?]" (pp. 338–344): his enthronement on the bank of the Indus, the submission of Ahmad Ayaz, the grant of "شیخ الاسلامی" to Shaikh Sadr al-Din of Bahauddin Zakariya's line, his towns and canals, the Nagarkot/Kangra campaign with the shrines of Bhawan and Jwalamukhi, the Thatta campaign against the Jam, his fiscal remissions, his buildings, the ban on women visiting tombs, and his old age and joint rule with his son. The short reigns follow: "سلطان تغلق شاه بن شاهزاده فتح خان بن سلطان فیروز شاه" (p. 337), "سلطان ابوبکر بادشاه بن ظفر خان…" (pp. 336–337), "سلطان محمد شاه بن فیروز شاه" (pp. 335–336) and "سلطان علاء الدین سکندر شاه عرف همایون شاه بن سلطان محمد شاه" (p. 335), where the chunk ends. This is the Tughluq line, not the Lodi/Sayyid period the coordinator expected (see Doubts).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Ziyarat restricted.** Firuz Shah "رفتن عورات مسلمه و هنود[OCR?] بر مزارات و بتخانه منع کرد": he forbade Muslim and Hindu women from going to tombs and temples, alongside banning wine, bhang and other forbidden things (p. 339).
- **A royal Shaikh al-Islam.** The title "شیخ الاسلامی" was given to Shaikh Sadr al-Din of Bahauddin Zakariya's line (p. 344); Shaikh Rukn al-Din is styled "شیخ الاسلام" at Multan (p. 347).
- **Sufi intercession with the state.** The sparing of Multan "به شفاعت" of Shaikh Rukn al-Din (p. 347).
- **Building programme.** Firuz Shah built "مساجد و مقابر و مدارس و خوانق و دارالشفا و رباط…". "در بعضے تواریخ" the count is "سی شهر و چهل جامع مسجد پخته[OCR?] و سی[OCR?] مدرسه و بست[OCR?] خانقاه و دویست[OCR?] رباط و صد نهر و صد کوشک و صد و پنجاه چاه و ده حمام و پنج دارالشفا و صد مقبره و ده مینار کلان", most still standing "تا حال که زیاده از سیصد سال منقضی میشود" (p. 339).
- **Pilgrimage economy at Bhawan.** Vows and offerings in cash and kind "بحدی انبارها و تودها میگردد که حساب دانان … نمیتوانند اورد" (p. 342).
- **Taxes remitted** by Firuz Shah: a long list of cesses (flower-selling, indigo, fish, cotton-carding, rope, roasted grain, shops, gambling houses, kotwali, butchery, marriage, birth of a son, sugarcane, oil-pressers, bricks, milk, weighing, fines, musicians at weddings, sale of land, earthenware, and "کوله استخوان هندوان که بگنگ می برند", the Hindus' bones carried to the Ganges), many terms flagged (p. 340). Revenue was taken "موافق حاصلات و بقدر قوت رعایا" (p. 340).
- **Famine and coercion.** Advances from the treasury for cultivation; those who took money and did not sow were killed; "گندم قمیت[OCR?] ادم[OCR?] پیدا کرد برنج بهمسنگ[OCR?] طلا گردید"; the city gates were shut and many died; people fled to Bengal and "اندیا[OCR?]" (p. 346).
- **Caliphal patent.** The sultan put the caliph's name on coins, received the "منشور" in "سنه ۷۴۴[OCR?]", and issued every order in the caliph's name (pp. 345–346); Firuz Shah also received a patent from "ابوالفتح خلیفه مصر" (p. 344).
- **Grants.** Jagirs to amirs and to Ahmad Ayaz (p. 344); "نقود از طلا و نقره و جامه و جاگیر" to the translator (p. 341).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- At the Bhawan temple, as the book reports: "بعضے گنگ مادرزا[OCR?] نوک زبان خود بریده پایان دیوار معبد شبانگاه بر زمین میخوابند و بقدرت الهی روز دیگر زبان انها درست می شود" — some born mute cut off the tip of their tongue, sleep at the foot of the temple wall, and next day can speak; some cut off their own heads "و باز درست می گردد"; "مرادات مردم بحصول می انجامد" (p. 342).
- The eternal flame of Jwalamukhi, which water would not put out, judged by the sultan a sign of divine power (p. 341).
- Elephant and human bones dug up on the canal "سلیمه[OCR?]" were held to be from "جنگ پاندوان و کوروان" (p. 343).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On Muhammad Shah.** The author treats his rule as oppression: "خلایق از ظلم او نجات یافت" at his death (p. 345); his plan to take the hills "تا دیار چین" was "اراده فاسد" (p. 347); he is "سلطان بے رحم و آهن دل" (p. 346).
- **On Firuz Shah.** Praised for justice and building: "سیاست را مطلق ترک داد هیچ مسلمانی را آزار نداد" (p. 339); "الناس علی دین ملوکهم" — his governors followed his justice (p. 340).
- **Anti-sulphur reasoning at Jwalamukhi.** The sultan's test is set against "بعضے اهل تعصب" who said it was a sulphur mine (p. 341).

## Doubts

**Period and reading order**
- The coordinator described this range as Lodi/Sayyid. The chunk itself is the Tughluq line, from Muhammad Shah's last years to Sikandar Shah / Humayun Shah (pp. 335–347). Nothing is added from outside; ruler names are given as printed.
- The chunk runs from p. 347 down to p. 335. It opens mid-couplet at "چو دخلت نیست…" (p. 347), continuing from the previous chunk, and ends with "مدت سلطنت او یک ماه و شانزده روز" (p. 335), closing Sikandar Shah's reign.
- Joins read through except two rough ones: p. 344 ends "باعث افتخار دانسته" (on the caliph's patent) and p. 343 opens "بامور جهانبانی قیام ورزید" — the sense continues loosely; and p. 336 ends "…نزول اجلال نموده بیعت" while p. 335 opens "بفرخی و سعادت در ان دیار آمد". Possibly text lost at the page breaks; not filled.
- A library stamp at the foot of p. 341 is not text.

**Mapping**
- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** rests on the name "شیخ الاسلام شیخ رکن الدین" at Multan. The book does not use "رکن عالم" and gives no tomb. It is NOT taken as Bahauddin Zakariya despite the brief's note that "شیخ الاسلام" at Multan can mean him, because the book names Rukn al-Din explicitly. Consolidator should weigh.
- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya**: a bare ancestor mention (person rule, 18 Sep). Sadr al-Din himself has no row.
- **Multan (8) and Lahore (35)** are place-level attachments. Multan: a revolt, a threatened massacre, letters ordering killings. Lahore is thin: seized by Shaikha Khokhar, an abandoned expedition, the letters (pp. 335–336). Held-identity rows on these lines (shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri; the "Shams" row shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari) are attached only place-level; nothing here raises their questions.
- **Ahmad Ayaz is NOT mapped to darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz.** The book's "احمد ایاز عرف خواجه جهان" is "از مقربان سلطان محمدشاه" (p. 344); the row's figure is Malik Ayaz of Mahmud Ghaznavi per the index. Name trap only.
- **Rajab Salar** ("رتبه ولایت داشت", p. 344) has no row; no place or tomb is given.
- **"مخدوم زاده بغداد"** (p. 345): not identified by the book; no row. "مخدوم" is not taken as any Makhdum row.
- **Thatta** ("تهته[OCR?]", "تهتهه", pp. 340, 345) has no row in toponym_map. It is not taken as PROVINCE sindh: the book names a town, its Jam and "ان دیار", not a suba, and protocol (c)(4) treats a named town as toponym. "دریاے سند" (pp. 340, 344) is likewise not province material. No province bullet is written.
- **"گجرات"** throughout (pp. 338, 340, 345) is the western Gujarat (with Devgir, Bharuch), not Punjab Gujrat; no row.
- Not names: "سرور" in verses (pp. 342, 343), "جهانیان" (pp. 340, 344), "شیخا کهوکهر" (a chief), "شیخ الاسلامی" as a title.
