# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 029 — pp. 360–371

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-verse (p. 371) near the end of the reign of the sultan who is named in the next heading as "سلطان جلال الدین خلجی". It covers the dervish "سیدی موله[OCR?]" at Delhi and his execution, then the drought and famine at Delhi (pp. 370–371). Next come the Mongol move on Punjab dated "سنه ۶۹۳[OCR?]", the Mongols settled at Ghiyathpur ("مغل پوره"), and Malik Ala al-Din's rebellion from Kara and his murder of the sultan between Kara and Manikpur (pp. 368–370). The reign heading "سلطان علار الدین برادرزاده و داماد سلطان جلال الدین خلجی" (p. 368) begins the next section. It covers the accession "در سنه ۶۹۶[OCR?]"; the siege of Multan, where Jalal al-Din's sons submit through Shaikh Rukn al-Din (pp. 367–368); Gujarat and Somnat; the Mongol siege of Delhi; and the sultan's plan for a new religion, with Malik Ala al-Mulk's counsel (pp. 366–367). After that come Ranthambhor, and a long romance of "پدماوت[OCR?]" and Rai Ratan Sen of Chitor (pp. 362–366). Then the sultan's reforms, the saints and poets of his reign, and his death (pp. 361–362). The chunk ends with the heading "سلطان شهاب الدین بن سلطان علاء الدین", followed by Malik Naib's regency and a moralising digression on wine, gambling and chess (pp. 360–361). It ends mid-sentence (p. 360).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** (the book's "شیخ رکن الدین" (pp. 367–368) and "شیخ رکن الدین ملتانی" (p. 361)):
  - When Alf Khan and Zafar Khan besieged Multan, Jalal al-Din's sons could not hold out. They came and met the besiegers "بوسیله دانائی اسرار کونی و آگهی جویائے رموز سفیدی و سیاهی زبدة العارفین قدوة الواصلین شیخ رکن الدین قدس سره". That is, they came through the mediation of Shaikh Rukn al-Din, "may his secret be sanctified" (the sentence runs across pp. 368–367).
  - Alf Khan "بمقتضائے اهلیت و مردمی شرایط تعظیم بجا آورده" and brought them with him to Delhi. There the sultan "حق نشناختند[OCR?]" and blinded both "مرزبی زاده[OCR?]" and their companions and imprisoned them (p. 367). The book does not say whether the saint's mediation carried any promise.
  - Among the holy men of Ala al-Din's reign, the book lists: "سلطان العارفین شیخ قطب الدین و قدوة الاصفیا شیخ نظام الدین اولیا و زبدة الواصلین شیخ صدر الدین عارف و شیخ رکن الدین ملتانی در زمان او بودند" (p. 361).
  - The book gives no tomb, date or further detail (not stated).

- **prahladpuri-temple**, **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya**, **shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani**, **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-ali-akbar-shah-ali-akbar-shamsi**, **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**, **shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari**, **shrine-of-shah-yusaf-gardez**, **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak** (the book's "ملتان"):
  - After their father's killing, Jalal al-Din's sons "رکن الدین و ابراهیم" "از دهلی گریخته بملتان رفته بودند". The sultan sent "چهل هزار سوار بسرداری الف خان و ظفر خان" against them (p. 368).
  - "امرائے مذکور رفته ملتان را محاصره کردند". The besieged princes "تاب نیاورده" and submitted through Shaikh Rukn al-Din (see above) (pp. 368–367).
  - Malik Ala al-Mulk's counsel: "سامانه و دیپالپور و ملتان که دراد[OCR?] مغل است مستحکم باید نمود". That is, Samana, Dipalpur and Multan, which lie in the Mongols' path (reading tentative, see Doubts), must be fortified (p. 366).

  - Book's word: "پنجاب" (p. 370).
  - Refers to: the reign of Jalal al-Din Khalji, the year printed "سنه ۶۹۳[OCR?]". The editor's footnote gives the variant "سنه ۶۹۱[OCR?] فرشته جلد اول صفحه ۹۴[OCR?]".
  - "مغول چنگیزی بالشکر گران عازم پنجاب گشت". The sultan marched against them. The Mongols made peace, and their leader, "که قرابت هلاکو[OCR?] خان بود", came in and became a Muslim with some other amirs. The sultan called him son, gave him a daughter in marriage, and settled them at "غیاث پور", which came to be called "مغل پوره" (p. 370).
  - The book does not say where in Punjab the Mongols arrived or met the sultan.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Saints and dervishes (Delhi, no rows)**
- **"سیدی موله[OCR?]"**, a dervish. The passage opens garbled: "شخصے را که سلطان کشنده[OCR?] آن بود که سیدی موله[OCR?] نام درویش در دهلی آمده اقامت ورزید" (p. 371).
  - He built "خانقاه عظیم" at great cost and kept open table. The daily figures are "هزار من میده پانصد من مسلمخ[OCR?] وسیصد من شکر ودو صد من روغن زرد", with two sittings a day for "خاص وعام". He himself ate only dry bread and took nothing from anyone. People ascribed his spending to "کیمیا وسیمیا" (p. 371).
  - Many amirs and maliks became his murids, and so did "خانخانان پسر بزرگ سلطان" (p. 371).
  - He was accused of "خیال سلطنت". The sultan scattered his followers on pretexts, and had the dervish "در پاے فیل مست بعقوبت تمام بکشت". For what followed, see Legends (p. 371).
- **"شیخ نظام الدین اولیا"**. People credited his blessings with the Mongols' failure to harm Hindustan. The epithets are "پیشوائے ارباب طریقت مقتدائے اصحاب حقیقت سلطان العارفین برهان المحققین … قدوه بار یافتگان درگاه کبریا" (p. 361).
  - Ala al-Din "اگر چه بظاهر ملاقات شیخ نمیکرد اما استمداد از باطن قدسی مواطن نموده". He sent messengers, letters and gifts as marks of devotion (p. 361).
  - He is named again in the list of the reign's saints (p. 361).
- "سلطان العارفین شیخ قطب الدین" and "زبدة الواصلین شیخ صدر الدین عارف" are named in the same list, with no place given (p. 361).
- "امیر خسرو دهلوی", the chief of the poets, drew "ده هزار[OCR?] تنکه زر مواجب" and wrote a "خمسه" in the sultan's name (p. 361).

**Places with no row**
- Delhi: the famine and the Mongol siege (pp. 367, 370). Kara ("کوره", with the editor's variant "کره"), Manikpur, Devgadh ("دیوگده"), Ranthambhor, Chitor, Chanderi, Malwa, Badaun, Gujarat and the idol of Somnat, "سنگلدیب[OCR?]", and "کوشک لعل" as the seat of rule (pp. 362–370).
- Samana and Dipalpur, named with Multan as places to fortify (p. 366). Samana is also where Mahmud Salim, one of Jalal al-Din's killers, came from: "از اجلاف سامانه" (p. 369).

**Rulers and the Padmavat romance (briefly)**
- Jalal al-Din Khalji was killed at the riverbank at Ala al-Din's signal. Mahmud Salim struck him and Ikhtiyar al-Din beheaded him. The book records each killer's punishment, Mahmud by leprosy and Ikhtiyar al-Din by madness (pp. 368–369). His reign is given as "هفت سال و یک ماه و بست روز" (p. 368).
- Ala al-Din's other campaigns: the revolt of Akat Khan ("اکتخان[OCR?]") on the road to Ranthambhor; the blinding of two nephews at Badaun; the killing of "حاجی مولا" at Delhi; and the taking of Ranthambhor and killing of "همیر دیو از نسل رائے پتهورا" (p. 366).
- The Padmavat romance. Ratan Sen married her after seeking her in "سنگلدیب[OCR?]". Ala al-Din besieged Chitor, made peace, and seized Ratan Sen by treachery. Padmavat freed him with a ruse of "یکهزار و سیصد دوله" full of soldiers (pp. 362–366). The book names "کتابی مسمی به پدماوت[OCR?] … در زبان فرس و هند" (p. 362).
- Ala al-Din's death: "ملک نایب … سلطان را مسموم گردانید و بعضے میگویند[OCR?] که بزحمت استسقا برحمت حق پیوست". His reign is given as "بست[OCR?] سال و سه ماه" (p. 361).
- Shihab al-Din was enthroned by Malik Naib "در سنه ۷۱۶[OCR?]" (p. 361). Malik Naib blinded Khizr Khan and Shadi Khan and kept Mubarak Khan in prison (p. 360).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A dervish's langar at Delhi.** Sidi Maula kept a khanqah with a public table, and the book gives daily quantities (see Other) (p. 371). Its scale and his following among the amirs were the grounds for suspicion against him (p. 371).
- **A ruler's devotion to a saint at a distance.** Ala al-Din sent messengers, letters and gifts to Nizam al-Din Auliya without meeting him (p. 361).
- **Ala al-Din's measures** (p. 362):
  - Wine was banned throughout the realm, and those who drank anyway were killed.
  - The property of "مردم اراذل" was taken into the khalisa.
  - The amirs were barred from mixing with and entertaining one another.
  - Revenue was assessed "بحکم مساحت".
  - The chaudhris and muqaddams who took the peasants' small dues were curbed.
  - Grain prices were fixed in his presence and stayed the same through the reign.
  - He introduced "داغ اسپ و داچوکی[OCR?] و واقعه نگاری".
- **Famine at Delhi** (p. 370). The book describes dearth, hoarders ("ارباب احتکار"), and people drowning themselves in the Jamna. It says people treated dogs, cats and even human flesh as lawful.
- **Pay.** Amir Khusrau received "ده هزار[OCR?] تنکه" (p. 361).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- On the day Sidi Maula was killed, "بحکمت ایزدی دران روز ابر سیاه وباد تند برخاست وطوفان گرد وخاک برامد وعالم تاریک گردید". "ودران سال باران نگردید", and the drought and famine followed (pp. 371–370).
- The Mongols could not trouble Hindustan in Ala al-Din's time, and "مردم این معنی را از برکات توجهات … شیخ نظام الدین اولیا سے[OCR?] تصور میکردند" (p. 361).
- Jalal al-Din's killers met divine retribution, Mahmud Salim by leprosy and Ikhtiyar al-Din by madness, and Ala al-Din's line died out (p. 368).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Kingship as gardening.** The author justifies dispersing a crowd that gathers without need, "هرچند ناملایم از انها بظهور نرسد". He compares the king to a gardener who thins the growth (p. 371).
- **Ala al-Din's plan for a new religion.** The book calls it "خیالات فاسده". It has Ala al-Mulk argue that it would bring "خرابی ملک و سلطنت" (p. 367).
- **Moralising digressions** against wine, gambling, chess and ganjifa (p. 360).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 371 down to p. 360, and all page joins read through.
  - It opens mid-verse at "غلط گفتم اے یار فرخنده خوئے" (p. 371).
  - It ends mid-sentence at "و در ایناے[OCR?]" (p. 360).
- The Jalal al-Din section's heading falls in the previous chunk. The sultan of pp. 369–371 is identified by the heading on p. 368, "سلطان جلال الدین خلجی".

**Mapping Shaikh Rukn al-Din (person rule)**
- The book writes "شیخ رکن الدین" and "شیخ رکن الدین ملتانی". It never writes "رکن عالم".
- The mapping to shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam rests on the name and the Multan setting, since the row's principal figure is "Rukn-ud-Din Abul Fath". The chunk itself makes no further identification and names no tomb. The consolidator should weigh this.
- Do not confuse him with Jalal al-Din's son "رکن الدین" (p. 368), a prince, who takes no id.

**Multan (place-level, weigh accordingly)**
- The Multan line is place-level campaign material: the princes' flight, the siege, and the counsel to fortify.
- The shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari row on this line is attached only place-level. Nothing here raises its held-identity question.
- "دراد[OCR?]" in "که دراد[OCR?] مغل است" is flagged, and its sense ("in the path of"?) is not settled.

**Other doubts**
- The province bullet uses the bare word "پنجاب" from the Mongol campaign of "سنه ۶۹۳[OCR?]" (editor's variant ۶۹۱[OCR?]). This is province-level, so weigh accordingly. It is not a Mughal suba here, and the book gives no extent.
- Saints with no rows: Nizam al-Din Auliya, Shaikh Qutb al-Din and Sidi Maula are at Delhi or are not located. Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif is not placed by the book. None takes an id.
- Sidi Maula's name, "سیدی موله[OCR?]", is flagged, and the clause introducing him ("سلطان کشنده[OCR?]") is garbled.
- Flagged dates: the accession year "۶۹۶[OCR?]" (p. 368), Shihab al-Din's year "۷۱۶[OCR?]" (p. 361), and the reign length "بست[OCR?] سال" (p. 361). None is converted.
- Who is the "دایم الخمر" ruler of the wine digression (p. 360)? The sentence reads as Malik Naib's, but it is not certain. The relationships "برادران غیر مادری سلطان" and "مبارک خان برادر حقیقی سلطان" are quoted as printed.
