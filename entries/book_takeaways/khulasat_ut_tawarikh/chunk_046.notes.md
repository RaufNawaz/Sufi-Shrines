# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 046 — pp. 566–577

In reading order (p. 577 down to p. 566), the chunk opens mid-sentence in the book's account of what Hindustan produces. It covers the fruits, led by the mango ("انبه"), then sugarcane and the betel leaf ("برگ تنبول") (pp. 577–576), and then the flowers (pp. 576–575). Next come the animals: horses, the elephant, the rhinoceros ("کرگدن"), wild and town buffalo, the Gujarati ox, and the carts (pp. 575–572). After them the water-clock ("گهڑیال") and the sand-glass (pp. 572–571). The Indian sciences begin with "از علوم هندی چه نگارد": the Veda, the Upanishads, the six shastras, the dharm shastra, karm bhag, the eighteen Puranas and a long list of *bidyas* (pp. 571–568). On p. 567 comes the heading "درویشان هند" (the dervishes of Hind). It sets out five classes ("صنف"): Sannyasis, Jogis, Bairagis, the Udasi "از معتقدان بابا نانک", and the Jati and Sarpura. The last class breaks off mid-sentence at the foot of p. 566. In this chunk's part of the section, no Muslim dervish, Sufi order, tomb or place in Pakistan is named. The only archive mapping is Baba Nanak, at person level. The rains do not appear in this chunk.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Practices of the classes of "درویشان هند":**
  - **Sannyasis** (p. 567):
    - Matted hair ("ژولیده مویان"), wholly naked ("سراپا برهنه"), bodies smeared with ash ("خاکستر آلود").
    - Severe austerities: "ریاضات شاقه و مجاهدات سخت".
    - Some keep silence ("مهر خموشی برلب نهاده"). Some keep both hands raised toward the sky. Some hang upside down from a tree ("خود را معکوس بدرخت اویخته"). Some gaze at the sun. Some stand day and night "بسان کوه ثابت قدم".
    - The class has many ways and named groups ("انواع طریقه انیقه دارند و اقسام نام اقوام هستند"). The author leaves them out: "اگر تفصیل طاعات و شرح اقوام برنگارد داستان دراز میشود".
  - **Jogis** (p. 567):
    - They are occupied "بیاد رب العباد".
    - Their named techniques are breath retention ("حبس نفس", which makes them long-lived), "علم خلع بدن", "سیمیا" and "کیمیا".
    - They heal the sick and practise "سحر سازی و جادو پردازی". Their reported powers are under Legends.
  - **Bairagis** (pp. 567–566):
    - They spread "سجاده عبادت". They are divided into groups, each named after its leader: "بنام هر مقتدی قومی علیحده است".
    - Most count song and melody "بزبان هند", in praise of the Creator, among the greatest acts of worship.
    - Some dance and whirl ("رقص و چرخ") in ecstasy. Some practise "تسبیح و تهلیل" and dhikr. Some sit in "مراقبه" fixed on a particular form ("مصور خاص").
    - Some study the books of ultimate truth, "که عبارت از بید انت شاستر و غیر ذلک".
  - **Udasi:** worship by singing their murshid's verses. See the Baba Nanak bullet above (p. 566).
  - **Jati and Sarpura** (p. 566):
    - Fasts of up to forty days ("تا چهل روز").
    - They stay in one place for the "چهار ماه برسات" so as not to harm insects. "خلاصه عبادات انها نگاهبانی جانداران است".
    - They go barefoot. They hold digging wells and laying out gardens to be blameworthy.
    - They light no lamp or fire at night, cook nothing for themselves and draw no water from wells. They get water and food by begging "از خانه مریدان", and eat nothing at night.
    - They avoid fruit and vegetables "مثل بادانجان و توری و چنار و سبزیها", and keep nothing but the cloth they need.
- **Varna and ashrama in the dharm shastra:**
  - Four "برن": "برهمن و چهتری و بیس و سودر" (p. 570).
  - Four "آشرم": "برهمچرج و گرهست و وان برست و سنیاس" (p. 570).
  - The dharm shastra also covers fasts, charity, expiation of sins, lawsuits and justice (p. 570).
- **Animals and transport:**
  - Horses: in some parts of the country they are "بهتر از اسپان عراقی و عربی" (p. 575).
  - The elephant's size:
    - Trunk "سه چهار درعه" long and tusk "یک درعه" long; ivory is called "عاج" (p. 575).
    - Up to "هشت درعه" broad and "پنج درعه" high (p. 574).
  - The war elephant: experienced men reckon one war elephant "برابر هزار سوار جرار" (p. 574). Elephant fights are run by mahouts and the goad ("کجک") (p. 573).
  - Its life: it becomes young after "شصت سال" and has a natural span of "یکصد و بست سال". The female carries for "هژده ماه". It breeds only in the wild (p. 573).
  - The rhinoceros comes "خال خال" into the hunts of sultans and khans (p. 573).
  - Sultans set wild buffalo against lions, "این شکار خاصه هندوستان است". Town buffalo also fight, and the she-buffalo is called "گاؤمیش" (pp. 573–572).
  - The "گاؤ گجراتی" (Gujarati ox) and the carts: "بهل", which seats four, and "رتهه", fit for kings (p. 572).
- **Timekeeping:**
  - The "گهڑیال" is a copper bowl "دوازده انگشت" high and wide with a small hole, set in a basin of water; it fills in one "گهڑی". A cast brass plate is struck to sound the hour (p. 572).
  - Day and night are each divided into four "پهر". A پهر is not more than "نه گهڑی" and not less than "شش گهڑی" (pp. 572–571).
  - "سیصد و شصت دم" make one گهڑی, and "بست و یک هزار و ششصد دم" a day and night (p. 571).
  - On journeys a sand-glass of two bottles is used (p. 571).
- **Flowers and fruits:**
  - Indian flowers: a long list of names is given (p. 576). "کیوڑه و کیتکی" are singled out (pp. 576–575). "رائے بیل و چنبیلی" are used at weddings and births (p. 575).
  - The mango "انبه": its blossom is called "مولا" and its fruit "انبا" (p. 577).
  - Sugarcane, whose product is sent "به ولایت دور دست بطریق تحفه" (p. 577).
  - Greens are called "ساگ", and the betel leaf "برگ تنبول" is praised (pp. 577–576).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Jogis** (p. 567). The book says that most of them, "بقوت ریاضت":
  - "بهوا می پرند و بر سطح دریا می خرامند";
  - put their soul into another body;
  - change their form, and turn ash into gold;
  - command "عالم اجنه";
  - know the secrets of hearts.
- Some Sannyasis can do the same (p. 567).
- The master of "پاتنجل شاستر", through control of breath, flies on the air, walks on the river, knows men's thoughts, and tells the past, present and future (p. 570).
- The Veda came from Brahma, who appeared out of a lotus on the primal water (p. 571).
- "سیس ناگ" carries the earth, "بقول اهل هند" (p. 569).
- Masters of archery draw many arrows from one "بقوت علم" (p. 568). Nat performers cut a man limb from limb and restore him "بهمان زمان" (p. 568).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's vocabulary for the dervishes.** He describes the non-Muslim ascetics in the vocabulary of Sufi devotion (pp. 567–566):
  - Sannyasis: "صاحبان معارف و حقایق".
  - Jogis: "صاحب حال و قال".
  - Bairagis: "سالکان مسالک حقیقت و رهروان راه طریقت", with "سجاده", "تسبیح و تهلیل", "ذکر حضرت جل شانه" and "مراقبه".
  - He ranks the Udasi, Baba Nanak's believers, as one of the "درویشان هند".
- **His judgement on the Jati and Sarpura** (p. 566):
  - He calls them "این بدکیش".
  - They do not accept a Creator ("آفریننده مخلوقات را قبول نکنند"). They hold that men and animals reproduce of themselves from eternity, "چنانچه گیاه خود بخود می روید". They take no account of punishment in the hereafter.
  - Their account of man as "مجموعه چهار عنصر" breaks off at the chunk's edge.
- **His equations and comparisons:**
  - Dharm shastra "را در فارسی فقه گویند" (p. 570).
  - Vedanta's knowers are "صاحب توحید": "هرچند عالم از وست اما همه اوست" (p. 570).
  - The Upanishad concerns "توحید واحد مطلق" (p. 571).
  - The Arabs fixed these rules well, but Hindi scholars have worked hard on them ("چهند") (p. 569).
  - "ارباب فرس" trace astrology to the Torah and Gospel, while the people of Hind trace it to the sun or the Veda (p. 569).
  - Some Indian flowers "در ایران و توران و اکناف ان پیدا نیابد" (p. 575).
- **The inexpressibility motif** recurs: of the sciences, "تفصیل ان بتحریر درنیاید" (p. 571), and of the Sannyasis, "داستان دراز میشود" (p. 567).

## Doubts

- **The Guru Nanak mapping is person-level.** "بابا نانک" is a bare name: he is the object of the Udasi's belief. The passage does not itself say that "مقتدای خویش" and "مرشد خویش" are Baba Nanak, though that is the natural reading. No site is named, so all 18 rows are attached under the many-row person rule. Weigh it accordingly.
- **Udasi rows are not mapped by category.** The archive has a "Nanakpanthi / Udasi Darbar" category, mostly in Sindh. The chunk names no Udasi darbar, sant or place, and the protocol has no category-level mapping, so none is attached beyond the Guru Nanak rows. The consolidator may wish to note the general Udasi passage (p. 566) for those rows.
- **Jogis are not mapped to Gorakhnath's rows.** The chunk describes "جوگیان" in general (p. 567). It never names Gorakhnath, Nath, Tilla or any place, so **tilla-jogian** and **gorakhnath-goraknath-temple** are not mapped.
- **The Jati and Sarpura are not mapped.** No teacher, temple or place is named for them, and the chunk does not connect them to any archive category. No other person in the chunk (Brahma, the six swamis, Byas Dev, Sesh Nag) is a principal figure of any row.
- **No Muslim dervish in this chunk.** Baba Farid, Sakhi Sarwar and every other Muslim saint are absent from pp. 566–577. Any Muslim dervishes in the section would be on lower pages (p. 565 and below).
- **"گجرات" here is suba Gujarat.** The book itself pairs it with Ahmadabad ("ولایت گجرات احمدآباد", p. 572; also "گاؤ گجراتی"). It is not Gujrat in Punjab, and takes no row.
- **No toponym or province material.** "این ملک", "این دیار" and "هندوستان" refer to Hindustan as a whole, and no suba is named.
- **Chunk edges and an internal break:**
  - The chunk opens mid-sentence at p. 577 ("و دیگر گوناگون میوه هاست"); the text before it is on p. 578.
