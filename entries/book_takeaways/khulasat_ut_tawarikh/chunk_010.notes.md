# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 010 — pp. 116–128

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens at p. 128, partway through an account of jugglers ("بازی گران", "سحرکاران سامری کردار") performing before the emperor and Prince Sultan Khurram. That account began in the pages above p. 128 and ends at p. 125 with their reward, the name "سیمیا" for their art, and a pointer to the emperor's own "جهانگیرنامه". Next is "در بیان تسخیر قلعه کانگره که مقدمه مستنخ[OCR?] کوهستان پنجاب است" (pp. 122–125). It covers the Kangra campaign from regnal year "سیزدهم[OCR?]", Raja Suraj Mal's revolt, the founding of Nurpur at Dhamheri, a description of the fort, and its surrender by Raja Tilokchand to Raja Bikramajit. The section "نهضت موکب والا بعد سیر کانگره بعرصه دلکشای کشمیر" (pp. 120–122) follows the emperor from Akbarabad: Itimad al-Daula dies and is buried by the Beas, the emperor visits Kangra fort and orders a mosque, he sees the fire at Jwalamukhi, and he goes on to Kashmir, where Sultan Shuja falls from a window unhurt; from regnal year sixteen the emperor goes to Kashmir every year. The last section, "در بیان بغی[OCR?] بادشاهزاده شاهجهان" (pp. 116–120), covers Shah Jahan's rise in rank and the Dholpur jagir quarrel. Then come Mahabat Khan's recall from Kabul, the emperor's march from Lahore, Raja Bikramajit's death, and Shah Jahan's flight by the Deccan, Golconda, Orissa and Bengal. After his defeat near Patna he goes back, and the section ends with his move from Ajmer toward Thatta. This runs straight on into p. 115 (chunk 009).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Kangra fort (the Punjab hills; outside Pakistan, no row)**
- **The first attempt.** At the start of "سال سیزدهم[OCR?] جلوس والا", "الشیخ فرید مرتضی خان میر بخشی" was sent with a large army to take the fort. With him went Raja Suraj Mal, son of Raja Basu, given rank after his father's death ("دو هزاری[OCR?] سه هزار[OCR?] سوار[OCR?]"). Suraj Mal quarrelled with the Shaikh's men, and the Shaikh reported his "اراده بغی[OCR?]" (p. 125). Suraj Mal turned to Sultan Khurram for protection. Murtaza Khan died, Suraj Mal was taken with the prince to the Deccan, and the Kangra campaign was halted (p. 124).
- **Suraj Mal's revolt.** After the Deccan campaign, Suraj Mal undertook to take Kangra. He went with the prince's bakhshi, Muhammad Taqi, but the two did not get on. Taqi was recalled and "راجه بکرماجیت برهمن" was sent in his place. Suraj Mal then rebelled openly and killed "سید صفی بارمه[OCR?]" with some of his brothers (p. 124).
  - He also plundered "پرگنات دامنه کوه و محال خالصه شریفه تا پرگنه بنتاله[OCR?] و کلانور" (p. 124).
  - When Bikramajit came, Suraj Mal shut himself up. The forts "مو[OCR?] و دمهری" fell after "اندک زد و خورد", and he fled into the mountain ravines (p. 124).
- **Jagat Singh and Nurpur.** Suraj Mal's younger brother Jagat Singh held a rank of 400 and was posted to Bengal. He was recalled and given "هزاری ذات و پانصد سوار و خطاب راجگی", then sent back to his hereditary land. "از دمهری که مسکن راجه مذکور است شهرے موسوم به نور پور بنام نور جهان بیگم آباد گشت". He was then assigned to the Kangra siege with Bikramajit (p. 124).
- **The fort described.** It is "کانگره قلعه ایست قدیم بر سمت شمالی لاهور در میان کوهستان", famous for its strength. It has "بست[OCR?] و سه برج و هفت دروازه" (p. 124).
  - Inside, it measures "یک کروه و یانزده[OCR?] طناب". Its length is "یک[OCR?] کروه و دو طناب", its breadth "بست[OCR?] و دو طناب" and its height "یکصد و چهار درعه[OCR?]". There are two large tanks inside (pp. 123–124).
  - No one but God knows when it was founded: "در هیچ نسخه اندراج نیافته" (p. 123).
- **Earlier sieges.** Sultan Firuz Shah besieged it in person, made peace and turned back, content that the Raja came to him. Other rulers' armies failed, and so did those sent under "حضرت بادشاه عفران[OCR?] پناه" (p. 123).
- **The fall.** Bikramajit set up the batteries ("مورچلها") and cut off supplies. The grain ran out, and what was left "کرم افتاد". The garrison lived for four months on "علفهای خشک بے نمک جوشانیده". "راجه تلوکچند" asked for quarter, sent the keys ("مکالید[OCR?]") to Bikramajit, and came in through Raja Jagat Singh (p. 123).
  - The fort fell on "غره محرم سنه [illegible] جلوس والا مطابق سنه [illegible] روز سه شنبه" (pp. 122–123), and Bikramajit was rewarded (p. 122).
- **The emperor's visit.** From "سیبه[OCR?]" the camp went four stages to "دریای بان گنگا". The Raja of Chamba sent his brother with tribute. Chamba is "بنسبت[OCR?] بیست[OCR?] و پنج کروهی کانگره", and its Raja, the leading zamindar of the hills, had never before submitted to Delhi (p. 122).
  - The emperor went up to the fort and had "بانگ نماز و شرایط اسلام" performed there. He ordered "مسجدی عالی" to be built (p. 122).
  - He then came down to "بهون … که پایان قلعه واقعست". Beneath a large umbrella said to have stood over an idol ("بت[OCR?] بندی[OCR?]") "از زمان پاندوان", a small umbrella of gold was set up. He spent some days there hunting (p. 122).

**Jwalamukhi (outside Pakistan, no row)**
- "جوالامکھی" lies "دوازده کروهے کانگره" under a great mountain. There "شبانه روز از زمین و دیوارها آتش زبانه میزند" (p. 122).
- "بعضے مردم اهل تعصب" said the flames came from a sulphur mine. On the emperor's order the ground was dug and watered. No smell of sulphur and no trace of a mine was found, and the fire did not go out (p. 122).
- The chamber was then rebuilt "بعمارات متین", and buildings were put up round it by order (pp. 121–122).
- Firuz Shah had made the same test on his way to Kangra and found "کان گوگرد نیست" (p. 121).

**Itimad al-Daula's death and tomb (a named tomb, outside Pakistan)**
- The emperor set out from "دارالخلافه اکبرآباد". Near "دسوهه[OCR?]", Itimad al-Daula died. He was buried "متصل هتواره[OCR?] از اعمال دسوهه[OCR?] بر لب دریای بیاه", and a lofty building was founded over his grave (p. 122).
- His jagir mahals, his insignia of rank and all his cash and goods were given to Nur Jahan Begum (p. 122).

**Kashmir (the valley; Sharada not named)**
- The road is very hard, but on arrival the province makes up for it. The streets and bazaar are busy. "مردمش بدمعاش و بدلباس و بدطینت و بدخو هستند", but the plains and hills are "نمونه بهشت برین", with flowers, fruit, streams and waterfalls. A verse follows (p. 121).
- **Shuja's fall.** "سلطان محمد شجاع خلف بادشاهزاده[OCR?] محمد خرم" fell from a window on the river side, "از هفت درع[OCR?] ارتفاع". He landed on a folded cloth and a farrash's back and was unhurt (p. 121).
  - "جوتک[OCR?] رای منجم" had foretold this four months before. Alms were given and the astrologer was rewarded (p. 121).
- **The yearly move.** Because of "دم گرفتگی و کوتاهی نفس", it was fixed "از سنه شانزدهم جلوس والا" that the emperor would go to Kashmir every spring and return to Hindustan in winter. A small building was put up at each stage (p. 120).

**Shah Jahan's rebellion (pp. 116–120), briefly**
- **His ranks.** He had 8,000/4,000 in regnal year two (p. 120). In year eight Asaf Khan's daughter married him and was titled Mumtaz Mahal, and he had 10,000/"شش هزار سوار سه اسپه[OCR?]". He rose next to 15,000/8,000. After the Rana campaign he had 20,000/10,000 and the title "شاه خورم". For the Deccan campaign he had 30,000/20,000 and the title "شاهجهان" (p. 120).
- **The quarrel.** Nur Jahan's daughter "از صلب علی قلی شیر افگن" was married to Shahryar, so Nur Jahan favoured him (p. 120).
  - "پرگنه دهول پور" was given to both princes. Their agents fought, and Shahryar's man "شریعت الملک" was killed (p. 120).
- **Mahabat Khan's recall.** Asaf Khan was accused of siding with Shah Jahan. Mahabat Khan was called from Kabul and refused to come while Asaf Khan was at court, so Asaf Khan was sent to "اکبرآباد[OCR?]". Mahabat's son Aman Allah was left as his deputy "در کابل" (p. 119).
  - Shah Jahan's jagir mahals "از میان دوآب و غیره" were transferred to Shahryar (p. 119).
- **The fighting.** Many amirs were punished for writing to Shah Jahan (p. 119). In the battle after Shah Jahan reached "کهسرآباد[OCR?]", near "کوتله[OCR?] میوات[OCR?]", his side was winning. Then a stray shot from a dying musketeer killed Raja Bikramajit, and the army broke (p. 118).
  - Parviz, Mahabat and about forty thousand horse were sent after him (p. 118). Mandu, Asir and Burhanpur follow (p. 117).
- **Golconda, Orissa and Bengal.** He went "براه گولکنده و بندر مچهلی پتن" to Orissa and Bengal, and Qutb al-Mulk sent provisions (p. 117).
  - He took the forts of Bardwan and Akbarnagar, and the subadar Ibrahim Khan was killed. He went on to Dhaka and Patna, and sent Abdullah Khan to Allahabad and Darya Khan Afghan to "اوده[OCR?]" (p. 117).
- **Defeat and return.** Raja Bhim, son of Rana Karan, was killed, and Shah Jahan's army was routed. Abdullah Khan put him on his own horse (p. 116).
  - Murad Bakhsh was born in "سال نوزدهم[OCR?] جلوس والا" and was left with his mother "در قلعه رهتاس". Darab Khan's wife was also sent there as a hostage. Abdullah Khan killed Darab's son, and Darab was later put to death by imperial order (p. 116).
  - Shah Jahan went back to the Deccan and then to "دارالخیر اجمیر" (p. 116).
- **Toward Thatta (no row).** From Ajmer Shah Jahan went "از راه[OCR?] جیسلمیر[OCR?] بصوب تهتهه آمده قصد ایران نمود", and sent Dara Shikoh, Shuja and Aurangzeb to court (p. 116). The siege of Thatta follows at p. 115 (chunk 009).
- **Kabul (out of scope).** It appears only as Mahabat Khan's post (p. 119).

**The jugglers (pp. 125–128), briefly**
- **Fighting animals.** Cocks fought with fire flying from their wings. They turned into partridges, which sang "بهمان قهقهه که در آن کوه میزنند", and then into two black snakes (p. 128).
  - From two tents set up facing each other, any beast named was brought out to fight: ostriches, and, at "شاهزاده سلطان خورم"'s request, nilgai[OCR?] (p. 128).
- **Water and colour.** A pool was frozen so hard that elephants crossed it (p. 128). A rose and a skein of thread changed colour in water (pp. 127–128).
- **Changing objects.** A four-sided cage showed different birds on each side, and a carpet changed its pattern each time it was turned (p. 127). A ewer poured water and then fire (p. 127). Fruit went into one end of a sack and other fruit came out of the other (p. 127). Twenty snakes were drawn from a man's mouth (p. 127).
- **Food and books.** Empty jars filled with honey and sugar (pp. 126–127). A covered platter changed dishes (p. 126). A basin produced fish, ducks and snakes (p. 126). Sa'di's Kulliyat put into a bag came out as the diwans of "خواجه حافظ", "سلیمان[OCR?]" and Anwari (p. 126).
- **The chain and the swords.** A fifty-cubit chain thrown into the air stood upright, and a dog, a leopard and a lion climbed it and vanished (p. 126). A ring changed its stone (p. 126). A man was rolled over upturned swords unhurt (pp. 125–126). A blank album showed new paintings on each leaf (p. 125).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Islam marked at a conquered fort.** At Kangra, "بانگ نماز و شرایط اسلام بتقدیم رسانیدند" and a lofty mosque was ordered (p. 122). The author frames the conquest as the banner of Islam raised "بر آن قلعه چه بلکه تمامی آن دیار کهسار" (p. 123).
- **Buildings.**
  - A lofty building went up over Itimad al-Daula's grave by the Beas (p. 122).
  - Solid buildings were put up at and round the Jwalamukhi fire chamber by order (pp. 121–122).
  - A small building was raised at each stage of the Kashmir route (p. 120).
  - A gold umbrella was set up at "بهون" below Kangra (p. 122).
- **A town named for the queen.** Nurpur was founded at Dhamheri "بنام نور جهان بیگم" (p. 124).
- **Jagirs and property.**
  - The Dholpur pargana was given to both princes, which led to bloodshed (p. 120).
  - Shah Jahan's Doab mahals were moved to Shahryar (p. 119).
  - Itimad al-Daula's jagir and estate went to Nur Jahan (p. 122).
  - Shah Jahan confiscated "چهل لک روپیه نقد" besides goods and elephants from Ibrahim Khan's property (p. 117).
- **Hostages.** Darab Khan's wife and son were held "بطریق یرغمال" (p. 116).
- **Tribute.** It came from the Raja of Chamba (p. 122) and, on the march, from Qutb al-Mulk of Golconda in cash, goods, grain and fruit (p. 117).
- **Rewards.**
  - The jugglers got "پنجاه هزار روپیه نقد و خلایع[OCR?]" from the emperor. The princes and khans gave them about "دو لک روپیه" more (p. 125).
  - Alms were given for Shuja's escape, and the astrologer's pay was raised (p. 121).
- **Siege practice.** At Kangra: batteries, a blockade of supplies, and a garrison starved into terms (p. 123).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The fire at Jwalamukhi.** In the book's words, "از عجایب قدرت قادر حقیقی است که از آغاز آفرینش خود بخود آتش شعله افروز میگردد" (p. 121). Digging and water put out nothing (p. 122).
- **The umbrella over the idol.** Local people point it out as standing there "از زمان پاندوان" ("نشان میدهند") (p. 122).
- **Kangra never taken.** "اعتقاد مردم آن دیار آنست": the fort never passed from one people to another and no outsider ever held it (p. 123).
- **The astrologer's prediction.** Jotik[OCR?] Rai foretold Shuja's fall and that it would do him no harm (p. 121).
- **The jugglers' wonders.** The author calls them "سحر سازی", not miracles, and names the art "سیمیا" (p. 125).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On Shah Jahan's revolt.** The heading calls it "بغی[OCR?]", but the text blames others.
  - The fault lies with "بد اندیشان" and "فتنه سازی کوته اندیشان و اغوای نورجهان بیگم". They drove into conflict "بیموجب" a son "که غیر از اطاعت و فرمان پذیری امری دیگری نداشت" (p. 119).
  - A masnavi on the guile of women follows (p. 119).
- **A law of fortune.** It is "قانونی است مستمر" that one near great fortune must first suffer hardship. That is why Shah Jahan's winning battle turned to defeat (p. 118).
- **Against the sulphur explanation.** The author calls those who proposed it "اهل تعصب" and reports that both Jahangir's test and Firuz Shah's showed no mine (pp. 121–122).
- **Sources and silences.**
  - Kangra's founding date is "در هیچ نسخه اندراج نیافته" (p. 123).
  - For the jugglers he refers readers to the emperor's own "جهانگیرنامه", which records them "مفصل و مشروح" (p. 125).
  - He names the art "سیمیا" and says it is common "در میان فرنگ" (p. 125).
- **On Kashmir.** The land is a paradise, but its people are judged harshly (p. 121).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 128 down to p. 116.
  - It opens partway through the jugglers' account ("این خروس ها بال بهم میزدند…"), which continues from the pages above p. 128.
  - It ends at p. 116 with "…پسران خود را بدرگاه معلی فرستاد". That runs straight into p. 115 (chunk 009, "چون در تهتهه شریف الملک…").
- Every page join reads through, including 123→122 across the two illegible years and 121→120, where "القصه" resumes the Kashmir stay.
- The emperor is "حضرت خاقان زمان" / "آن حضرت". He is named only through "نسخه جهانگیرنامه که آن حضرت … بتصنیف نموده اند" (p. 125).
- A library stamp on p. 121 ("Sri Satguru Jagjit Singh Ji eLibrary") is not text.
- There are no editor's footnotes (`حاشیہ:`) in this chunk.

**Dates (as printed; none converted or filled)**
- Kangra's fall is "غره محرم سنه [illegible] جلوس والا مطابق سنه [illegible] روز سه شنبه" (pp. 122–123). Both the regnal and the Hijri year are lost.
- Other regnal years: "سیزدهم[OCR?]" for the Kangra campaign (p. 125), "شانزدهم" for the yearly Kashmir move (p. 120, unflagged), "نوزدهم[OCR?]" for Murad Bakhsh's birth (p. 116), and years "دویم" and "هشتم" for Shah Jahan's ranks (p. 120).

**Lahore (place-level, bare; weigh accordingly)**
- The 35 Lahore ids are attached only for three bare mentions: the court's two departures from Lahore (pp. 118–119) and Kangra placed "بر سمت شمالی لاهور" (p. 124).
- Under wave-1 point 3 this is one short bullet at most. The consolidator may drop it.
- Held-identity rows on that line (shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri) are attached place-level only. Nothing in the chunk raises their questions.

**Traps — not mapped**
- **"الشیخ فرید مرتضی خان میر بخشی" (p. 125)** is a Mughal noble. He is not Baba Farid, and shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar is not attached (brief: "Farid is also a common name").
- **"قلعه رهتاس" (p. 116)** is where Murad Bakhsh, his mother and Darab Khan's wife were left during Shah Jahan's Bengal and Patna campaign. By the book's own sequence it is not the Rohtas of the Kabul road (chunk 009), so gurdwara-choa-sahib is not attached. The book does not locate it further.
- **"کوهستان پنجاب" (heading, p. 125)** refers to the Kangra hills. By the book's own account Kangra lies "بر سمت شمالی لاهور در میان کوهستان", and it is outside Pakistan, so under ruling (c)(3) there is no PROVINCE punjab bullet. Nothing in the chunk is a statement about suba Lahore, Multan or Thatta, so there is no Province-level section.
- **"بصوب تهتهه" (p. 116)** means "toward Thatta" (the town), not "suba". Thatta has no row, so it goes under Other.
- **"زمان پاندوان" (p. 122)** dates an umbrella over an idol at "بهون" below Kangra. That is a non-archive site, and the book names the Pandavas, not Pandu. Under wave-1 point 1, panj-tirath (principal figure: King Pandu) is not attached.
- **"دیوان خواجه حافظ" (p. 126)** is a book produced in a juggler's trick. It has nothing to do with Hafiz Muhammad Jamal Multani, and no id is attached.
