# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 038 — pp. 476–486

This chunk is part of the book's account of the Hindu rajas, and it reads from p. 486 down to p. 476. It opens mid-sentence in the mourning after the great war (p. 486). Bhishma ("بهیکهم پتامه"), dying on the field, persuades Yudhishthira ("راجه جدهشتر") not to renounce the world. Yudhishthira then rules from Hastinapur, and on Vyasa's command he performs the horse sacrifice ("جگ اسومید", pp. 485–486). The book praises his reign and dates it from the present (p. 484). Dhritarashtra withdraws and dies (p. 483). The book then turns to Vyasa ("بیاس دیو"): the Mahabharata's size and name, Vyasa's miraculous birth, his division of the Veda and the books ascribed to him (pp. 481–483). Next come the four yugas (p. 480) and the parable of the buried treasure at Hastinapur that marks the arrival of Kaliyuga (pp. 479–480). After that the Pandavas abdicate and make their last journey (p. 479), the book gives its reign totals, and Parikshit ("پریچهت[OCR?]") is born and reigns (p. 478). The chunk ends with Parikshit's hunt and his insult to a hermit, which brings the hermit's son's curse (pp. 476–477). It breaks off mid-sentence at "مصدر[OCR?]" (p. 476). The chunk has no section headings of its own. Apart from province-level material, only three ids are mapped, all person-level on bare names.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **krishna-mandir-kabari-bazar**, **krishna-mandir-ravi-road** (the book's "سری کشن"):
  - News reached Yudhishthira of "هلاکت تمامی جادوان و رفتن سری کشن و بلبهدر[OCR?] ازین جهان فانی", that is, the destruction of all the Yadavas and the passing of Sri Kishan and Balbhadar from this world. The book refers the reader to the Mahabharata for the account. The news was one reason Yudhishthira decided to give up his rule (p. 479).
  - The book names "سری کشن و بهیم و ارجن و کرن و درجودهن و دیگر بهادران فیل افگن" as men credited with "کارهای بزرگ و امور سترگ". People of the present age, it says, hear of these deeds in wonder (p. 481).
  - No site is named for Krishna in either passage. The Pandavas weeping for him at Dwarka is under "Other" (p. 479). See Doubts.

- **panj-tirath** (the book's "راجه پاندو"):
  - Named only in a simile about Parikshit: "بسان راجه پاندو نیاگ خود شکار دوست بود", meaning that like his ancestor Raja Pandu he loved hunting (p. 477).
  - Nothing else is said of Pandu, and no site is named. See Doubts.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The Ashvamedha ("جگ اسومید").**
  - In "اصطلاح هند" it is a rite in which a horse with set qualities is let loose "بدون بند و ریسمان", and troops follow it. Rulers who welcome it bring peshkash, and anyone who binds it is punished. Only a king whose "حکم او بر تمام عالم جاری باشد" can perform it (pp. 485–486).
  - The horse must be "نقره خنگ" with "هر دو گوش آن سیاه" (p. 485).
  - Vyasa said the rite would relieve Yudhishthira's grief over his kinsmen and be "کفارت جرایم متخففه[OCR?] و موهومه" (p. 486).
  - Arjun followed the horse "بعد از یکسال" and came back with peshkash. Yudhishthira then gave so much in alms that "برهمنان اطراف ممالک و فقرای و مساکین" no longer needed anything (p. 485).
- **Feeding brahmans.** "هر روز هشتاد هزار برهمن از مایده افضال او" were fed (p. 484).
- **Living observance.** "تا این زمان فرقه هنود بر دین و آئین او عمل می کنند": to this day the Hindus follow his religion and way, and they count recounting his wonders as worship (p. 484). His accession is recorded "در جراید روزگار و تقاویم اهل هند" (p. 484).
- **Prosperity of the reign.** Rain fell on time, famine and calamity did not strike, and cultivated land and fields gave "ربع بیشتر". Theft and highway robbery ceased (pp. 484–485).
- **Dhritarashtra's control of the treasury.** Yudhishthira put "خزاین و کارخانجات در حکم او" (p. 483).
- **The hunt.** The book gives a long set-piece of hunting animals and birds: leopard, caracal, dog, hawk, falcon, "چرغ", "بحری", "شکره", "باشه" (p. 477).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Vyasa's mother was born of a fish. A raja's seed was sent home on a leaf by a falcon, spilled in a fight over the Jamuna, and swallowed by a fish, which bore twins (pp. 482–483).
- Parashar raised "ابر سیاه" by "افسونی" so that no one could see. The girl conceived and bore Vyasa at once, and the boy became like a fourteen-year-old. Her maidenhood stayed intact, and her fish smell turned to a sweet scent. All this took no more than "یکپاس[OCR?]" (p. 482).
- The book reports a belief among "آن جماعت" that Vyasa "خلعت هستی جاوید بدوش داشته تا حال در عالم علوی و سفلی سیار است", that is, he still wanders the upper and lower worlds (p. 481). It says he knew past, future and present (p. 481).
- Yudhishthira did not melt in the snow and went to paradise in his body (pp. 478–479).
- The hermit's son "سرنگی[OCR?]" was born "از تخم این زاهد و شکم آهو ماده", with a horn on his head, "که این قصه مشهور است". He prayed that whoever had hung the snake on his father would be bitten "بعد هفت روز" by the "بهجنگ(۲)[OCR?] مار" [editor's footnote variant: "تچهک[OCR?]"], and the prayer "مستجاب گشت" (p. 476).
- **The treasure parable** (pp. 479–480):
  - Before Kaliyuga, the buyer and the seller of a house each refused treasure found under it.
  - After Kaliyuga came, each claimed it, and Yudhishthira took this as the sign of the age.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The four yugas, "بموجب کتب معتبره و صحف قدیم" of Indian scholars** (p. 480). The figures are quoted as printed, not computed:
  - "ست جگ هفده لک و بست و هشت هزار سال", with a natural lifespan of "یک لک سال".
  - "تریتا جگ دوازده لک و نود و شش هزار سال", in which "دهم حصه" remains, with a lifespan of "ده هزار سال".
  - "دواپر هشت لک و شصت و چهار هزار سال", in which "نه حصه زایل می گردد", with a lifespan of "هزار سال".
  - "کلجگ چهار لک و سی و دو هزار سال", in which "دهم حصه" remains, with a lifespan of "صد سال". It is the worst age.
- **The author's explanation.** People of earlier ages had greater power. Their deeds seem beyond human strength only to the people of this age (pp. 480–481). The Pandavas lived "در اواخر دور دواپر" (p. 480).
- **The Mahabharata's size and contents.**
  - It has "ده باب" and "یک لک اشلوک".
  - "هشتاد و شش هزار اشلوک" deal with religion, ethics and stories, and "بست و چهار هزار اشلوک" with battles (p. 483).
- **Two explanations of the Mahabharata's name.** "مها بزرگ و معنی بهارت جنگ است", or descent from Raja Bharat (p. 483).
- **The scholars' canon.** The Indian learned hold that what Vyasa put into circulation is authoritative and brings reward in the hereafter. Their list is "سمرت", "پوران", "گیان", and works of wisdom and astronomy. Anything others "از خود اختراع کرده اند" is "شایان اعتبار ... نیست" (p. 481).
- **On Vedanta.** The author calls it unequalled in "علم الهی و مناظره". He glosses its teaching as the unity of the Real behind many veils and supports this with a Persian verse: "بحر یکی موج هزاران هزار" (p. 481).
- **Reign totals.** "ایام سلطنت پاندوان و کوروان مدت یک صد و بست و پنج سال". Of these, the two sides ruled together "هفتاد و شش سال", "درجودهن[OCR?] بعد اخراج پاندوان سیزده سال", and "راجه جدهشتر[OCR?] بعد جنگ مهابهارت سی و شش سال" (p. 478).
- **Present-day dating.** "تا حال چهار هزار و هشت صد و سی و شش سال می گذرد" since Yudhishthira's accession (p. 484). The era is unstated, and the figure is quoted only, not converted.

**Basis for the person-level mappings (brief point 1, protocol many-row rule)**
- **Krishna.** Krishna is principal figure of exactly 2 rows. The two passages mapped (pp. 479, 481) name no particular site for him, so under the protocol's 22 September ruling they take both rows.
  - The one passage that places him, the Pandavas weeping for him at Dwarka (p. 479), names a non-archive site. Under brief point 1 it takes no ids and is under Other.
  - The mapping is person-level on a bare name. Neither temple, Lahore nor Rawalpindi is mentioned, so weigh it accordingly.
- **Pandu.** "راجه پاندو" (p. 477) is mapped to **panj-tirath**, whose principal figure is "King Pandu (Mahabharata)", under the 18 September person rule (a bare reference still counts).
  - The book's Pandu is Parikshit's ancestor, so he is the Mahabharata figure. He appears only in a simile, and the row's site (Peshawar) is not named.
  - The many "پاندوان" (Pandavas) are his sons, not Pandu himself, and are not mapped.
  - This is a bare-name, person-level mapping; weigh accordingly.
- **Rulers.** No other raja in the chunk is a row's principal figure (brief point 2). Brahma, Vyasa, Bhishma and the rest have no rows.

**Multan not mapped place-level, and the province bullets**

  - Under brief point 3 (a bare passing journey) it is placed under PROVINCE punjab, not on the 8 Multan city rows.
  - A consolidator who reads "ملتان" as the city could attach those 8 rows place-level.

  - The period is mythic: the Pandavas at the start of Kaliyuga, as the book places it.
