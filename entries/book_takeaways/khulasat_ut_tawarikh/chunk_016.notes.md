# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 016 — pp. 196–209

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-sentence (p. 209) at the end of a fort's fall: the defenders drown or leap from the walls, the emperor (never named; "آنحضرت", "خاقان زمان") rides thirty kos in pursuit, Munim Khan Khan-i Khanan is sent to take Bengal from Daud, and the emperor returns to Fatehpur "از راه اجمیر بعد زیارت روضه رضویه[OCR?]", ordering a well and a minaret at every kos from Ajmer to Fatehpur. Daud submits, revolts again after Munim Khan's death and is killed by Khan Jahan and Raja Todar Mal (pp. 208–209). Then comes a king-list of the sultans of Bengal, from Malik Muhammad Bakhtiyar, "از امراے بزرگ سلطان قطب الدین ایبک", through the Ilyas Shahis, Raja Kans and the Habshi and Husain Shahi rulers to Sulaiman Karrani and Daud, with heavy editor's footnotes from Firishta and the Tabaqat-i Akbari (pp. 206–208). The narrative resumes with Muzaffar Khan's governorship of Bengal, the revolt of Masum Khan Kabuli, the Qaqshals and Mirza Sharafuddin Husain, the fall of Tanda and Muzaffar Khan's death, and the attempt to read the khutba for Muhammad Hakim Mirza (pp. 204–205); the founding of the fort and city of "الله باس" at the Ganga–Jamna confluence (p. 203); and "دربیان بغی محمد حکیم میرزا برادر حضرت بادشاه خاقان زمان" (pp. 199–203): the Mirza's siege of the Lahore fort, the execution of Khwaja Shah Mansur, the building of "اتک بنارس" on the Indus, the march to Peshawar and Kabul, the Mirza's death, and the camp at Rawalpindi. "دربیان کشته شدن راجه دانش[OCR?] وبیربل" (pp. 197–199) narrates the Yusufzai campaign in Bajaur and Swat and Birbal's death; the chunk closes with "در بیان رسیدن میرزا سلیمان والی بدخشان در حضور پر نور و تفرقه در بدخشان" (pp. 196–197), breaking off mid-sentence at "لشکر" (p. 196).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A ruler's tomb visit and pilgrim road works**: ziyarat at Ajmer, and a well and minaret at every kos to Fatehpur (p. 209).
- **Fort and city building**: Attock, regnal year 26, two years, under Khwaja Shamsuddin Khwafi (p. 201); Allahabas, completed regnal year 28, with a dam against the Ganga's flood (p. 203).
- **Offices**: "صوبه دار لاهور" (Raja Bhagwant Das) and "فوجدار سیالکوت" (Kunwar Man Singh) (p. 203); "کرورے پرگنه" (p. 205); "دیوان بیوتات", "دیوان اعلی" (p. 205).
- **Horse-branding and jagirs**: Masum Khan Kabuli's revolt began over "مقدمه داغ اسپ"; the Qaqshals revolted "بسبب بازیافت جاگیر" (p. 205).
- **Sums**: "پنجاه هزار روپیه نقد" to Mirza Sulaiman (p. 196); "بست هزار اشرفی" sent secretly by Muzaffar Khan (p. 205); "بست لک روپیه" in a tank (p. 205).
- **Sovereignty markers**: rebels claiming "سکه وخطبه" (pp. 202, 204, 207).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author denies the rumour that Todar Mal was behind Shah Mansur's death: "اگرچه برزبانها افتاده بود که راجه تودرمل بانی این منصوبه شده اما خلاف است شاید مردم دیگر اختراع کرده باشند" (p. 201).
- He moralises on Hakim Mirza's death from drink (verse "مبادا خردمند غرق شراب") (p. 200) and on harsh officials (verse "نباشے[OCR?] بکار جهان سخت گیر") (p. 201).
- He reckons Bengal outside Delhi's control "از ابتداے ۷۴۶[OCR?] لغایت سنه مذکور [۹۸۳[OCR?]] که دوصد وسی وهفت سال بود باشد" (p. 206).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 209 down to p. 196. It opens mid-sentence at "کشتی غرق گشتند" (p. 209), continuing from p. 210, and ends mid-sentence at "عبدالله خان از نفاق ایشان واقف گشته لشکر" (p. 196), continuing on p. 195. All page joins read through.
- Library stamps at the foot of pp. 198 and 209 are not text.
- The emperor is never named ("آنحضرت", "خاقان زمان", "حضرت بادشاه"); he is the brother of Muhammad Hakim Mirza (p. 203). No identification is added here.

**Mappings (weigh accordingly)**
- **Lahore (35 rows)**: place-level. The siege of the Lahore fort and its subadar are statements about the place itself. Held-identity rows on the line (shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri) are attached only place-level; nothing in the chunk raises their questions.
- **tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak**: person-level on a bare name; Aibak is named only as Bakhtiyar's master. He also appears in the Lahore line by toponym; the separate bullet carries the person mention.
- **Sialkot (5 rows)**: place-level on a bare office title ("فوجدار سیالکوت"); nothing else about the place. Weigh lightly. imam-ali-ul-haq is not named.
- **Rawalpindi (6 rows)** and **Peshawar (10 rows)**: bare camp / march mentions (brief point 3). Each is one short bullet; the book says nothing about either place itself.

**Not mapped**
