# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 028 — pp. 348–359

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-sentence (p. 359) at the end of the reign of Sultan Shihab al-Din: his gambling, chaupar on the roof of the Hazar Sutun, the killing of Malik Naib and his own imprisonment in Gwalior, a reign "سه ماه و چند روزه" (p. 359). Then comes "سلطان قطب الدین مبارکشاه عرف مبارک خان برادر حقیقی سلطان شهاب الدین بن سلطان علاء الدین" (pp. 355–359): the release of all prisoners, the rise of the favourite Hasan as Khusrau Khan, the blinding and killing of Khizr Khan and Shadi Khan, the sultan's hostility to Shaikh Nizam al-Din Auliya, his summoning of "شیخ رکن الدین ملتانی", and his murder in the Hazar Sutun. Next are "سلطان ناصرالدین عرف خسرو خان" (pp. 354–355), whose reign ends when Ghazi al-Malik, governor of Dipalpur, marches on Delhi with the governor of Multan, and "سلطان غیاث الدین تغلق شاه عرف غازی الملک" (pp. 351–354): accession, Bengal, Sonargaon and Tirhut campaigns, and his death in the collapsing pavilion near Tughluqabad, with several versions, one of which places Shaikh Rukn al-Din Multani in the pavilion (p. 351). The chunk closes with "سلطان محمد شاه الغ خان عرف فخرالدین بن جونا سلطان بن سلطان غیاث الدین تغلق شاه" (pp. 348–351): his character, generosity, tyranny, the move of the capital to Daulatabad and the copper coinage. It ends at a "بیت" heading (p. 348).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** (the book's "شیخ رکن الدین ملتانی", named twice; no tomb or site given):
  - **Under Qutb al-Din Mubarak Shah.** The passage on the sultan's ill-will toward the shaikhs: because Khizr Khan was a disciple of Shaikh Nizam al-Din Auliya, the sultan turned against the Shaikh, kept people from visiting him, and favoured "شیخزاده جام را که از مخالفان مشایخ[OCR?] بود". In the same run: "و بار یافته درگاه سبحانی شیخ رکن الدین ملتانی را [illegible] نزد خود طلبیده نشسته". That is, he summoned to himself Shaikh Rukn al-Din Multani, "one admitted to the court of the Glorious [God]". A word is illegible right before "نزد خود طلبیده", and the sense of "نشسته" here is not settled (p. 357).
  - **At the death of Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq.** This is one of several versions of how the new pavilion fell: "شیخ رکن الدین ملتانی براے ملاقات سلطان در ان قصر رفته بود برمز و ایما[OCR?] بجهت زود[OCR?] بر خاستن میگفت اما سلطان تفهم[OCR?] نکرد چون شیخ برخاست قصر بر نشست". The Shaikh had gone into the pavilion to meet the sultan. By hint and sign he urged him to get up quickly, but the sultan did not understand, and when the Shaikh rose the pavilion collapsed (p. 351).
  - The book reports this as one version among others ("بعضے مینویسند…"), and goes on to call a different account "انچه تحقیق پیوسته", the verified one (the Nizam al-Din story; see Other) (p. 351).

- **prahladpuri-temple**, **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya**, **shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani**, **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-ali-akbar-shah-ali-akbar-shamsi**, **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**, **shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari**, **shrine-of-shah-yusaf-gardez**, **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak** (the book's "ملتان"):
  - Its governor only: Ghazi al-Malik marched on Delhi "به اتفاق بهرام حاکم ملتان و او نیز[OCR?] کمر انتقام بسته", together with Bahram, governor of Multan, who had also girded himself for revenge (p. 354). Nothing else is said of the place. See Doubts.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Saints and religious figures (all at non-archive sites or no site)**
- **Shaikh Nizam al-Din Auliya** ("پیشوائے اهل الله شیخ نظام الدین اولیا", "قدوة الاصفیا"):
  - Khizr Khan was his disciple ("مرید"). Qutb al-Din took the Shaikh for Khizr Khan's ally, spoke against him, envied him, kept people from his dwelling ("منزل") and behaved disrespectfully (p. 357).
  - The book's verified account of Ghiyath al-Din's end: the sultan, offended with the Shaikh, sent word from the halt that when he entered Delhi the Shaikh should leave the city. The Shaikh replied "هنوز دهلی دور است", a saying "در میان اهل هند مشهور" (p. 351).
  - "در همین سال شیخ نظام الدین اولیا و امیر خسرو دهلوی از تنگناے جسمانی بعالم جاودانی شتافتند": in the same year Nizam al-Din and Amir Khusrau died (p. 351). The book puts no number on that year here.
- **Amir Khusrau** ("امیر خسرو", "امیر خسرو دهلوی"): he told the story of Ghazi al-Malik Tughluq Shah's war with Nasir al-Din Khusrau Khan "بزبان پنجاب", which they call "هند وار[OCR?]" (p. 354). His death is above (p. 351).
- **"شیخزاده جام"**:
  - Under Qutb al-Din he was an opponent of the shaikhs and a favourite (p. 357).
  - Muhammad Shah killed him unjustly "که کلمه از ظلم او بر زبان آورده بود" (p. 349).
- **"صوفی و قلندر"** are listed first among the classes Muhammad Shah punished severely for small faults (p. 349).
- **An unnamed "مخدومان[OCR?]"**: at Qutb al-Din's murder, "یکے از مخدومان[OCR?]" remarked that Ala al-Din had overthrown the house of his uncle and benefactor Jalal al-Din, so it was no wonder his own house was falling (p. 355).
- Maulana Jalal al-Din Hisam[OCR?] brought Muhammad Shah a qasida and was rewarded after its opening verse (p. 350).

**Places in Pakistan with no row**
- **Dipalpur** ("دیپالپور"): Ghazi al-Malik "حکومت دیپالپور داشت" (p. 354).
- **"پنجاب"**: Tughluq's mother was "از قوم جت پنجاب" (p. 354), and Amir Khusrau's work was "بزبان پنجاب" (p. 354). Both are about a people and a language, not the land. No province bullet (see Doubts).

**Outside Pakistan, briefly**
- Delhi: the "کوشک هزار ستون" (scene of Qutb al-Din's murder, the gambling and the mourning; pp. 354–356, 359) and the "کوشک لعل" (p. 353). Tughluqabad fort, built by Ghiyath al-Din (p. 352), and the pavilion three kos from it (p. 352).
- Gwalior fort, where Shihab al-Din, Khizr Khan and Shadi Khan were imprisoned and killed (pp. 357, 359).
- Suba Gujarat (Zafar Khan's governorship, given to Khusrau Khan, "وطن قدیم او"; p. 357). This is sultanate Gujarat, not Punjab Gujrat.
- Lakhnauti alias Bengal, Sonargaon (Bahadur Shah), Tirhut (p. 352). Ujjain, Deogir renamed Daulatabad, Malwa, "کنبله[OCR?]", "دهور سمندر" (pp. 348, 350).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Releasing prisoners as thanksgiving.** On his accession Qutb al-Din, who had himself been imprisoned, ordered all prisoners in Delhi and in forts and towns, near and far, to be freed, and sent farmans to the governors (p. 359).
- **Grants and stipends.** Tughluq gave "وظایف نان[OCR?] و ادرارات" to the surviving household of Ala al-Din and Qutb al-Din. He recovered the gold Khusrau Khan had scattered, and set the soldiers' gifts against their pay (p. 353).
- **Building.** Tughluq built Tughluqabad. Ulugh Khan built a pavilion for the sultan's feast three kos from it (p. 352).
- **Muhammad Shah's measures** (pp. 348–350):
  - The Doab kharaj was raised "بده یکے[OCR?] بده قست[OCR?]" and agriculture failed (p. 349).
  - The capital moved to Daulatabad, with sarais and rest-houses on the road. Raw grain was given for Hindu travellers and cooked food for Muslims, and Delhi's people were moved (p. 348).
  - Copper coins were struck to pass as gold and silver tanka (p. 348).
  - He enrolled "سه لک[OCR?] و هفتاد[OCR?] هزار سوار" (p. 348).
- **Under Khusrau Khan**, "بت پرستی و تخریب مساجد شایع گشت": idol-worship and the wrecking of mosques spread (p. 355).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- The pavilion fell as soon as Shaikh Rukn al-Din Multani stood up, after the sultan missed his warning. The book gives this as one version (p. 351).
- Nizam al-Din's "هنوز دهلی دور است", given in the book's "verified" account of the same death (p. 351).
- Other versions of the collapse: lightning (on the authority of "حاجی محمد قندهاری"), the shock of elephants brought from Bengal, an earthquake, or Ulugh Khan's deliberate building. "صدرجهان گجراتی" is cited for the last (pp. 351–352).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author judges Muhammad Shah "از عجایب مخلوقات و جامع اضداد": unmatched in generosity and unmatched in tyranny (pp. 350–351).
- He moralises on Qutb al-Din's raising of a low-born favourite to the wazirate (p. 358). On gambling, he cites "راجه نل و پاندوان" as examples (p. 359).
- He weighs the versions of Tughluq's death and calls the Nizam al-Din account "انچه تحقیق پیوسته" (p. 351).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 359 down to p. 348. It opens mid-sentence at "عیشش[OCR?] سرسبز[OCR?]…" (p. 359), continuing from the previous chunk, and ends at a "بیت" heading (p. 348). All page joins read through.
- The library stamp at the foot of p. 352 is not text.

**Rukn al-Din mapping (person rule, on a name)**
- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** is attached because the book's "شیخ رکن الدین ملتانی" matches the row's principal figure "Rukn-ud-Din". The book does not use "رکن عالم", names no tomb, and gives no date or lineage. The consolidator should weigh the identification.
- In the p. 357 sentence, the word before "نزد خود طلبیده" is `[illegible]` and is not filled. Whether the summons was hostile or honorific is not clear from the text.
- At p. 351, "ایما[OCR?]", "زود[OCR?]" and "تفهم[OCR?]" are flagged inside the quoted version.

**Multan (place-level, weigh accordingly)**
- The 8 Multan ids rest on one campaign mention: the governor "بهرام حاکم ملتان" joining Ghazi al-Malik (p. 354). This is at most a bare mention, and only the governorship is said of the place. "نیز[OCR?]" is flagged.
- The held "Shams" row shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari is attached only place-level. Nothing raises its question.

**Not mapped**
- Dipalpur has no row in toponym_map.
- "قوم جت پنجاب" and "بزبان پنجاب" (p. 354) are about a people and a language, so they are not treated as province punjab. No province bullet is written.
- Nizam al-Din Auliya, Amir Khusrau and Shaikhzada Jam are at Delhi or have no site, so they take no ids.
- Gujarat (p. 357) is the sultanate province, so it takes no row.
- Rulers (Tughluq, Muhammad Shah, Qutb al-Din, Khusrau Khan) are not principal figures of any row.

**Dates**
- These dates are not converted:
  - Tughluq's accession: "در سنه ۷۲۰[OCR?]" (p. 353). The editor's footnote gives the variants "سنه ۷۲۱[OCR?] تاریخ فرشته جلد اول صفحه ۱۲۹[OCR?]" and "سنه ۷۲۰[OCR?] تاریخ فیروزشاهی ضیاءالدین برنی صفحه ۴۲۵[OCR?]".
  - Muhammad Shah's accession: "در سنه ۷۲۵[OCR?]" (p. 351).
- Nizam al-Din's death is dated only "در همین سال" (p. 351), and no year is supplied.
- The reign lengths are as printed: Shihab al-Din "سه ماه و چند روزه" (p. 359); Qutb al-Din "چهار سال و چهار ماه"; the four Khaljis "سی و چهار سال و یازده ماه" (p. 355); Khusrau Khan "چهار ماه و چند روزه" (p. 354); Tughluq "چهار سال و دو ماه" (p. 351).
