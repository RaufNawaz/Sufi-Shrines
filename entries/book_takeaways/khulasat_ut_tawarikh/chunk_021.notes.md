# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 021 — pp. 262–273

The book reads from the highest page down. This chunk covers the Sur rulers, not Akbar: Akbar does not appear, and Humayun's return is one clause (p. 267). In that order it opens mid-sentence inside the life of Sher Shah, whom the book calls "فرید خان" (Farid Khan, son of Hasan Khan) until the title "شیر خان" is given (pp. 272–273). That part covers his mother's dream and a dervish's prophecy, his jagir of Sahsaram, and his service under Bahar Khan "سلطان محمد" of Patna and Sultan Junaid Barlas. It goes on through his rise in Bihar and Bengal, Chunar, and the taking of the Bihar fort of Rohtas by the doli trick (pp. 270–273). Then come the emperor's defeats at "بهوج پور تهتیبه[OCR?]" and Kanauj and his flight by Lahore "بسمت ملتان و تهته". Sher Khan pursues to Lahore and founds a fort "برهتاس موسوم" near the Gakhar country. He takes the title Sher Shah "در سنه ۹۴۷[OCR?]" and fights Raisen and Maldeo, and Haji Begum is restored to Humayun at Kabul. The book then gives his justice and administration, including the sarais "از بنگاله تا رهتاس پنجاب", and his death at Kalinjar (pp. 265–269). The book's heading "اسلام شاه المشهور بسلیم شاه عرف شاهزاده جلال خان بن شیر شاه" (p. 265) opens Islam Shah's reign (pp. 262–265). It covers his accession "در سنه ۹۵۲[OCR?]", the break with prince Adil Khan and Khawas Khan, and the Niazi revolt of Azam Humayun (Haibat Khan) "در لاہور". The revolt runs through the fight near Ambala and the plundering of Punjab. The chunk ends with Azam Humayun fleeing into "کوہستان گکھران" (p. 262).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The road and post (Sher Shah).** These are the sarais and post horses "از بنگاله تا رهتاس پنجاب" (see gurdwara-choa-sahib) (p. 266).
  - At every sarai, from the royal establishment, "بمسافرین مسلمین طعام و هندوان آرد و روغن[OCR?]" (p. 266).
  - Fruit trees were planted on both sides of the road, free to all travellers (p. 266).
  - Afghan villages were settled on both sides "از نیلاب تا دهلی" as a barrier against the Mughals (p. 266).
- **Law and the army.** Sher Shah based his rule on "قوانین سلطان علاء الدین خلجی که در تاریخ فیروز شاهی مندرج است" and brought back Alauddin's "داغ اسپ", the horse-branding (p. 266).
- **Islam Shah and his soldiers.** A soldier he faulted had his jagir changed but was kept on in service with his men. Slackness brought reproof and even punishment of sons (p. 263).
- **Jagir and pargana.** Hasan Khan held "پرگنه سهسرام و تانده" with "پانصد سوار" (p. 273).
  - As "داروغه جاگیر", Farid Khan increased "زراعت", "حاصلات" and "آبادی دیهات". The unruly submitted "تن بمال گذاری و رعیت گری دادند" (p. 273).
- **A fatwa.** "علماے دین فتوی دادند" that Puran Mal, who kept Muslim women in his house, must be killed "مطابق امر شرع[OCR?]" despite the promise of safety (p. 268).
- **Memory in song.** Khawas Khan's deeds "تا حال در هندوستان ... در سرود و نغمه می سرایند" (p. 269). Puran Mal's courage "تا حال در اشعار هندیه باکناف[OCR?] گیتی مشهور است" (p. 268).
- **Education.** Farid Khan read Arabic "تا کافیه" and, in Persian, "گلستان و بوستان و سکندر نامه" (p. 273).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- His mother told Hasan Khan a good dream, and he gave her "تازیانه چند". His reason, as the book reports it: "بزرگان آزموده کار فرموده اند" that after a good dream one must not sleep again that night, or its fruit is lost (p. 273).
- The unnamed dervish's prophecy over the four-year-old Farid Khan: "سبحان الله بادشاه هندوستان بطلب درمی گریه می نماید". Hasan Khan took it as confirming the dream (p. 273).
- An astrologer chose an auspicious hour, and in it Sher Khan conquered Bengal "بتائیدات الهی" (p. 271).
- The book's account of the emperor and Sher Khan:
  - Kings' judgement is "پرتویست از شعشه[OCR?] انوار ایزدی" (p. 272).
  - The emperor saw that "چشمان این افغان شوخ مینماید" and ordered his arrest.
  - Sher Khan sensed it "به تفرس" and escaped (p. 272).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **On Sher Shah.** The author calls him "بغایت نیک ذات و فرخنده صفات" and "بے نظیر" in statecraft. In justice and generosity he "گوے سبقت از نوشیروان عادل برده", treating kin and stranger alike (p. 267).
  - The anecdote of the bania's wife and prince Adil Khan illustrates this: "نزدیک من در عدالت فرزند و رعیت برابر است" (pp. 266–267).
- **On Islam Shah.** The author calls him "در رعیت پروری و عدالت گستری ... بے نظیر". But he kept the soldiery "بغایت تنگ و ازرده", and a verse argues that this made amirs defect (p. 263).
- **The duty of kings.** Crushing rebels "بر ذمت ہمت سلاطین والا اقتدار لازم است" (p. 262).
- **Sher Khan's view of the Mughals and the Afghans.** He judged the Mughals easy to drive out because the emperor left affairs to wazirs and was given to the hunt and pleasure. The Afghans' fault, he said, is that "با خود اتفاق[OCR?] ندارند" (p. 272).

## Doubts

**Scope**
- The brief describes this stretch as "Humayun's return and Akbar's early reign". The text shows the Sur rulers instead: Sher Shah, then Islam Shah.
  - Akbar is not named.
  - Humayun's return is the single clause "از عراق و خراسان معاودت نموده در کابل رسیده اند" (p. 267).
- **"فرید" is Sher Shah, not Baba Farid.** The book says so itself: "او فرید خان را شیر خان خطاب داده" (p. 272). Farid Khan is Hasan Khan's son throughout p. 273. Nothing is mapped to shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar.
- There is no named saint anywhere in the chunk.

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 273 down to p. 262.
  - It opens mid-sentence: "همان وقت بیدار گشته این خواب ... بشوهر خویش گذارش نمود" (p. 273). The dream itself is in the previous chunk.
  - It ends mid-sentence: "اعظم ہمایون گریختہ در کوہستان گکھران رفتہ" (p. 262).
  - All page joins read through.
