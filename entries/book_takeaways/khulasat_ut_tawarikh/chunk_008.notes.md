# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 008 — pp. 89–101

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-narrative with Shah Jahan ("حضرت اعلی") ill and cut off from his subjects. Dara Shikoh takes control and closes the roads to news, and disorder spreads across the subas. Murad Bakhsh crowns himself in Gujarat, and Shah Shuja marches from Bengal to Benares. Shah Jahan moves from Shahjahanabad to Akbarabad on "بستم[OCR?] محرم سنه ۱۰۶۸[OCR?]" (p. 101). Sulaiman Shikoh and Raja Jai Singh defeat Shuja near Benares. Jaswant Singh and Qasim Khan are sent to Malwa (pp. 99–100). Dara recalls the Deccan troops, which forces Aurangzeb to make peace with Bijapur, and the author summarises how the war ends and when Shah Jahan dies (p. 99). The reign closes with its length (p. 98). The new reign opens under the heading "ابوالمظفر محی الدین اورنگ زیب عالمگیر بادشاه … در سنه ۱۰۶۹[OCR?] سریر آرائے خلافت گشت" (p. 98), beginning with the charge that Dara had inclined to the Hindu religion (p. 98). Then come Aurangzeb's march from Aurangabad and Burhanpur across the Narmada (pp. 95–97), "در بیان جنگ خدیو آفاق با راجه جسونت سنگه و فتح و نصرت یافتن" near Ujjain (pp. 91–95), "در بیان ماجرای[OCR?] احوال داراشکوه" (pp. 89–91), and "در بیان محاربه خدیو گیهان با داراشکوه و لوای[OCR?] فتح برافراختن" (p. 89), which ends mid-sentence after the crossing of the Chambal.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Holy men and religion: the charge against Dara Shikoh (p. 98)**
- In the Deccan, Aurangzeb was hearing "که داراشکوه بدین و آئین هندو مایل شد", that Dara had inclined to the Hindu religion and its customs (p. 98).
- Dara admitted to his company "برهمنان که علمای مذهب هندواند و جوگیان و سنایسان[OCR?] که درویشان دین انها هستند", that is, Brahmins, "who are the learned of the Hindu religion", and jogis and sannyasis[OCR?], "who are the dervishes of their religion". He held "آن مردم را مرشد کامل و هادی مطلق[OCR?]", perfect spiritual guides and absolute[OCR?] leaders (p. 98).
- He called their book, "که به بید مشهور است" (known as the Bed [Veda]), "کتاب آسمانی و خطاب ربانی", a heavenly book and divine address, and was setting out to translate it ("درصدد[OCR?] ترجمه آن شده[OCR?]") (p. 98).
- He composed Hindi verses himself ("اشمار[OCR?] هندی", presumably "اشعار") and called them "تصوف محض", pure Sufism (p. 98).
- In place of God's name he used a Hindi name "که اهل هند آن را پر بتو[OCR?] الهی می نامند و اسم اعظم میداند". He had it engraved in Hindi script on diamond, ruby, emerald and other stones, wore it in his clothing, and treated it as a blessing ("تبرک و تقصور[OCR?]") (p. 98).
- He turned away "از نماز و از روزه و آئین مسلمانی", from prayer, the fast and Muslim custom (p. 98).
- None of this is tied to any site.

**Envoy**
- "کب رائے[OCR?] نامی برهمن که فهمیده و دانائے وقت بود": a Brahmin named Kab Rai[OCR?], "understanding and wise of the age", was sent to Jaswant Singh to say "ما را جنگ عزم نیست". Jaswant refused (pp. 94–95).

**The succession war, briefly (all sites in India)**
- Shah Jahan's illness and the disorder: he was hidden even from the amirs. Dara blocked the news and seized letters, and no one believed the emperor was alive. Ryots stopped paying revenue, officials sat idle, and merchants stopped travelling (p. 101).
- Murad Bakhsh "در گجرات رایت بغی بر افراشته", sat on the throne, struck coin and had the khutba read in his own name as "مروج الدین محمد مرادبخش بادشاه". He sent a force to "سورت بندر". He imprisoned "محمد شریف پسر اسلام خان", the Begum Sahib's agent there, and seized imperial and Begum Sahib property. He killed with his own hand "میر علی تقی دیوان سرکار خود" (p. 101).
- Shah Shuja moved from Bengal against Patna and reached Benares (p. 101). Sulaiman Shikoh, with "راجه جے سنگهـ کچهواهه", was sent on "چهارم[OCR?] ربیع الاول سنه الیه[OCR?]". He camped at "بهادرپر[OCR?]" on the Ganga by Benares and surprised Shuja on "بست[OCR?] و یکم[OCR?] جمادی الاول". Shuja fled by boat to Monger and then to Bengal, and Patna and Monger passed to Sulaiman's men. Captured servants of Shuja were mutilated at Akbarabad, and some died (p. 100).
- Jaswant Singh was sent to Malwa on "بست[OCR?] و دویم[OCR?] ربیع الاول سنه الیه[OCR?]" to hold the Narmada crossings. Qasim Khan, "داروغه توپ خانه", was sent against Murad, and they reached "اوجین که دارالایالت صوبه مالوه است" (pp. 99–100).
- Bijapur: Aurangzeb, besieging it, made peace "با عادل خان والی[OCR?] انجا" for "ویک[OCR?] کرور روپیه پیشکش" and left Muazzam[OCR?] Khan to collect it, because Dara had recalled the Deccan troops (p. 99).
- From Aurangabad, Aurangzeb set out on Friday "دوازدهم جمادی الاول". He made Muhammad Muazzam subadar of the Deccan, left the newborn Muhammad Akbar at Daulatabad fort, imprisoned Muazzam Khan there, and wrote to Murad "که از گجرات متوجه والا شود" (p. 97). From Burhanpur he left on Friday "بست و پنجم[OCR?] جمادی الثانی مطابق دویم[OCR?] فروردین". Shah Nawaz Khan was seized and held in Burhanpur fort, and titles were given at the Narmada (p. 96).
- Murad joined from Gujarat near "موضع دیپالپور" on "بست[OCR?] و یکم[OCR?]" Rajab. The army camped near "دهرات[OCR?] پور" (also "دهرامات[OCR?] پور"), "هفت[OCR?] کروسے از اوجین". Other places named: "کاچروده[OCR?]" and "قلعه دهار[OCR?]" (p. 95). See Doubts on Dipalpur.
- The battle with Jaswant Singh took place on Friday "بست و دویم رجب مطابق هفتم اردی بهشت" (p. 94). Murshid Quli Khan, "دیوان سرکار معلی", was killed, and Zulfiqar Khan was wounded (p. 94). Several Rajput chiefs fell, Jaswant fled toward "جودهپور مسکن خود", and "قریب شش هزار کس" of the slain were counted (p. 92). Rewards followed (p. 91). The camp moved by Ujjain and marched for Akbarabad on "بست و هفتم رجب سه شنبه[OCR?]" (p. 91).
- Shah Jahan had set out for Shahjahanabad on "هیژدهم[OCR?] رجب" because of the heat. At "بلوچ پور[OCR?]" on 2 Shaban he heard of the defeat, and he returned to Akbarabad on 9 Shaban (p. 90). Dara gathered "قریب شصت هزار سوار" and briefly imprisoned Muhammad Amin Khan (p. 90). Shah Jahan urged him not to fight, and Dara would not listen (p. 90).
- Dara left Akbarabad on "بست پنجم شعبان[OCR?]" to hold the Chambal fords at Dholpur (p. 89). Aurangzeb reached the Gwalior area on "ببست و هشتم شعبان". A zamindar showed him an unguarded ford ("گذر مهد وریبه[OCR?]", twenty kos from Gwalior), and he crossed on "غره رمضان المبارک" (p. 89).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's case for Aurangzeb's march** (p. 97). He was moved by "حمیت دین مسلمانی و غیرت سلطنت و جهانبانی و رشک برادری و جوشش نفسانی[OCR?]". He meant to free Shah Jahan from Dara's control, because otherwise Dara would become an unrestrained ruler and "دین مهنو[OCR?] در ابواج[OCR?] میدهد" (sense unclear; flagged).
- **Dara's religion** is set out as the first cause of the new reign (p. 98; see Other).
- **Providence:** the throne was kept "بامانت" since eternity for Aurangzeb, and "تفاول نام نامی" (his name) is a proof (p. 89). In "تقدیر دگر باشد و تدبیر دگر", Dara's plans are undone (p. 99).

## Doubts

**Zero mapping: the checks made**
- "موضع دیپالپور" / "دیپال پور" (p. 95) lies on the march between the Narmada and Ujjain, seven kos from which the army camped. It is a Malwa village in context, not the Punjab Dipalpur, and no row or toponym exists for either.
- "گجرات" (pp. 95, 97, 99, 101) is suba **Gujarat**, Murad Bakhsh's seat, with Surat. It is not Punjab Gujrat, so it takes no row (no Shah Daula).
- "بلوچ پور[OCR?]" (p. 90) is a camp on the road from Akbarabad to Shahjahanabad. It is not Balochistan.
- "درویشان" (p. 98) refers to Hindu jogis and sannyasis in general, at no site. Neither Gorakhnath nor any deity is named, so the many-row person rule is not triggered.
- "شیخ میر", "شاه نواز خان", "سید شاه محمد" (made Murtaza Khan) and "میر ضیاءالدین" are nobles, not saints. "بادشاه عالمگیر مهر" (p. 94) and "شاه عالمگیر بهار" (p. 96) are metaphors (the sun, spring). Rulers are not shrines.
