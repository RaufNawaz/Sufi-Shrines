# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 022 — pp. 274–285

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk is Humayun's flight and exile, his return and his wars with his brothers, and then the start of the Sur section. The narrative has no section headings until p. 274. It opens with the emperor deciding against a halt at Lahore and going by the Chenab and "براه ملتان" to Bhakkar, with Khawas Khan pursuing "تا ملتان و اوچ". Then come the fighting near Thatta with Shah Husain Mirza Arghun, Yadgar Nasir Mirza's desertion, the turn toward Maldeo's country by way of Uch, Bikaner and Jodhpur, and the waterless desert road (p. 285). At "حصار امرکوت" Akbar is born on "پنجم رجب سنه ۹۴۹[OCR?]" (p. 284). Next comes the exile in Iran (pp. 281–283): Herat on "غره[OCR?] ذی قعده سنه ۹۵۰[OCR?]", the tombs of Khwaja Abdullah Ansari, Ahmad Jam ("زنده فیل") and Imam Riza, Damghan, and the meeting and feasts with Shah Tahmasp. Then the return (pp. 274–281): the siege and taking of Qandahar, the long struggle with Kamran Mirza for Kabul and Badakhshan, and Balkh in "سنه ۹۵۶[OCR?]". Hindal Mirza is killed in a night attack and buried near his father's tomb at Kabul. Kamran flees to Salim Khan Sur in Punjab and then to Sultan Adam Gakhar, who hands him over. He is blinded, sent to Mecca, and dies there in "سنه ۹۶۴[OCR?]" (pp. 274–275). The chunk ends with the heading "شیر شاه[OCR?] عرف فرید خان افغان سور" and the opening of Sher Shah's ancestry (p. 274).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Royal ziyarat.** In exile the emperor visited the tombs of saints at Herat, Jam, Mashhad, Ardabil and Tabriz (pp. 281, 283). All of these are outside the archive.
- **The blinding.** Babur had willed that the brothers not seek each other's lives. So, "بصلاح دولت خواهان", Kamran was blinded rather than killed and given leave for Mecca (p. 274).
- **Gifts at the Persian court.** There is a long inventory: Iraqi horses, mules, camels, jewelled swords, purses of coin, Frankish, Yazdi and Kashi stuffs, and gold and silver vessels. In return the emperor gave a great diamond and "دویست و پنجاه لعل بدخشانی" (p. 282).
- **Persian military aid.** Shah Tahmasp gave his son Sultan Murad with "دوازده ہزار سوار". Qandahar, once taken, was to be handed to the Shah's men (pp. 280–281).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Sher Shah's mother, while carrying him, dreamt that the shining moon came down from the sky into her lap. The author makes this the sign that heralds a fortunate king (p. 274).
- At four, the infant Akbar picked out his mother "بے تامل" from among many veiled women. The court took it as a sign of divine favour (p. 280).
- The author presents Akbar's survival on Kabul's battlements as "حفظ و حمایت ایزدی": Kamran had hung him there facing the imperial guns (p. 279).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Divine choice of rulers.** Whoever has sovereignty "از کار فرمایان قضا و قدر در ازل مقرر کرده باشند" cannot be harmed by enemies (p. 279). The same idea is applied to Sher Shah's rise (p. 274).
- **Causes of the loss of Hindustan.** In the emperor's own words to the Shah: "از بیوفائی ہمراہان و بے التفاتی برادران سلطنت ہندوستان از دست رفت" (p. 281).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- The chunk runs from p. 285 down to p. 274.
  - It opens mid-narrative at "بنا چار توقف در لاہور" (p. 285).
  - It ends mid-sentence at "ناگزیر یا[OCR?]" (p. 274), just before the Sher Shah heading's text continues.
  - All page joins read through, including the verse heading "قطعه" at the foot of p. 285, whose verses open p. 284.
- The order of events across the joins does not match the chronology, and the chunk is not in time order: Balkh "۹۵۶" (p. 278) comes after Akbar's birth "۹۴۹" (p. 284) in reading order, which is correct. This is not corrected or smoothed.
- The emperor is never named. He is "آنحضرت" or "حضرت بادشاه". Reading him as Humayun rests on the brief's map and the run of the narrative.
- The brief places the Langah sultans "~297". No Langah material falls in this chunk.

**Mapping**
- **Amarkot.** "امرکوت" is attached by the index's own row name "Umarkot (Amarkot) Shiv Mandir" and toponym_map's "umarkot" / "umerkot". This is place-level.
  - shrine-of-makhdoom-abdul-rahim-girhori lies only "near Pithoro, Umarkot", so its attachment is weaker still.
  - The book says nothing of either the temple or the saint. Weigh accordingly.
- **Lahore, Multan, Uch.** All three are place-level and are passing campaign mentions, flagged per brief point 3.
  - The Multan and Uch lines carry the book's one statement about the places themselves: "اوچ تابع ملتان".
  - "و را[OCR?] و اوچ" is garbled. The reading "by way of Uch" is tentative.
  - The Uch line carries both held-identity rows (Jalaluddin / Jahaniyan). The book names no saint, so this is place-level only; held identity question not raised.
  - The Lahore line's held rows (Shah Chiragh, Shah Inayat) and the Multan "Shams" row are likewise place-level only.
- **Province punjab** is province-level, weigh accordingly. It is Salim Khan's sphere under the Sur, not a Mughal suba, and the book gives no boundary. "قصبه بنر[OCR?]" is not located and is not taken to any row.
- **Thatta, Bhakkar and the Indus are not mapped.** Neither Thatta nor Bhakkar has a row in toponym_map. "تہتہ" is Shah Husain Arghun's realm here, not called a suba, so it is not taken to province sindh.
- **"ہر زبان[OCR?]"** after "سلطان محمود" at Bhakkar is probably "مرزبان" (ruler). It is not repaired in quotation.
- **Not mapped:**
  - "زنده فیل احمد جام", Abdullah Ansari and Imam Riza are saints at non-Pakistan sites, so they take no ids.
  - Babur's tomb is a ruler's tomb outside Pakistan.
  - "قلندرانه" (p. 275, of Kamran's disguise) is vocabulary, not a figure.
