# khulasat_ut_tawarikh — chunk 031 — pp. 383–396

The book reads from the highest page down. In that order the chunk opens mid-sentence at the end of Sultan Raziya's reign: her march on Delhi, her defeat near "کیتهل[OCR?]", her capture and death (p. 396). It then gives the whole of "سلطان معزالدین بهرام شاه بن سلطان شمس الدین التمش" (pp. 395–396). That section covers the murder of Malik Ikhtiyar al-Din, the conspiracy of the qazis, the Chingizi Mongols' siege and ruin of Lahore, the revolt of the wazir Nizam al-Mulk on the Beas, the mission of "شیخ الاسلام قطب الدین بختیار اوشی", and the fall of Delhi. Next come the whole of "سلطان علاءالدین مسعود شاه" (pp. 394–395) and of "سلطان ناصرالدین ولد سلطان شمس الدین" (pp. 392–394), with Balban as wazir and Nasir al-Din's piety: Qurans copied for his living, revenue spent on dervishes and khanqahs. The longest section is "سلطان غیاث الدین بلبن عرف الغخان بنده وداماد سلطان شمس الدین" (pp. 384–392). It covers his justice and piety, his birds and hunting, the Mewati and Katehriya campaigns, and Prince Muhammad Sultan's jagir of "مملکت سنده" and governorship of Multan. In Multan the prince keeps Amir Khusrau and Amir Hasan and twice invites Sa'di. The section also has the halala marriage of a princess to "شیخ صدرالدین", son of "شیخ بهاءالدین ذکریا", the prince's death fighting the Mongols, Kaikhusrau's posting to Multan and Balban's death. The chunk ends in the opening of "سلطان معزالدین کیقباد بن ناصرالدین بغراخان ابن سلطان غیاث الدین بلبن" (pp. 383–384), breaking off mid-sentence (p. 383).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

**Saints and men of letters**
- **Shaikh Sadr al-Din, son of Bahauddin Zakariya** ("شیخ صدرالدین"). The book gives him his own epithets: "مرکز دایره صدق و یقین محیط نقطه توکل و تمکین زبده خداشناسان حق بین اسوه یزدان پرستان اهل دین" (p. 386).
  - "گویند": a daughter of the late Sultan Shams al-Din was married to Prince Muhammad Sultan, and "در حالت مستی طلاق بر زبان شاهزاده رفت". Since "غیر از حلاله علاجے نبود", "بنابر انقیاد امر شریعت غرا" she was married to Shaikh Sadr al-Din. The Shaikh took her home and the marriage was consummated (the book says so in figurative verse and prose) (p. 386).
  - "موجب قرار داد" they asked the Shaikh to divorce her. She said: "من از خانه آن فاسق برامده بجناب افادت پناه رو آورده ام خدا را روا مدار که باز بدست او گرفتار شوم" (p. 385).
  - The Shaikh answered: "در همت و وفا از عورت کم نتوان بود". Despite the prince's insistence he did not divorce her and kept her (p. 385).
  - The prince "ازیمعنی برآشفت و در مقام انتقام گردید". He was killed soon after by the Mongols, which the book ties to his enmity with the Shaikh (see Legends) (p. 385).
  - The chunk gives no tomb, place or date for Sadr al-Din.
- **Shaikh Sa'di** ("شیخ مصلح الدین محمد سعدی شیرازی رحمة الله علیه"; also "عمده مشایخ کبار زبده عارفان نامدار ... خورشید اوج ولایت").
  - Invited twice from Multan to Shiraz (see the Multan bullet). Both times he sent instead a "سفینه" of verse, "نسخه گلستان و بوستان بخط خویش", with his apology and "سفارش اکمل الشعرا امیر خسرو" (p. 386).
- **Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar** ("شیخ الاسلام قطب الدین بختیار اوشی"). Bahram Shah sent him "برائے تسلی امرا" when they rebelled on the Beas. "امرا بهیچ وجه مطمئن خاطر نشدند وشیخ برگشته بدهلی آمد" (p. 395).
- **Amir Khusrau Dihlavi and Amir Hasan.**
  - Both were in the prince's service at Multan. Khusrau gets a long string of epithets, "ابلغ البلغا ... جامع الکمالات صوری و معنوی" (p. 386).
  - Khusrau was with the prince at his death. He was captured by the Mongols, went to "بلخ[OCR?]", escaped and came back to Hindustan (p. 385).
- **Unnamed "درویشان خدا آگاه"**: they received Sultan Nasir al-Din's "نذر[OCR?]" (p. 393). Balban sat "در صحبت اهل وعظ" (p. 391).

**Rulers, governors and officials (no rows)**
- **Sultan Raziya.** "ملک اختیارالدین" was taken with her by "زمینداران آن ضلع" after the defeat near "کیتهل[OCR?]". She died on "بست وپنجم ماه ربیع الاول سنه ۶۳۷[OCR?]". Reign: "سه سال وشش ماه وشش روز" (p. 396).
- **Mu'izz al-Din Bahram Shah.**
  - Accession: "روز دوشنبه بست وهشتم ماه رمضان سنه ۶۳۷[OCR?]" (p. 396).
  - Malik Ikhtiyar al-Din, with the wazir "نظام الملک مهذب الدین", took over affairs. He married the Sultan's sister ("سابق منکوحه قاضی اختیارالدین") and kept an elephant at his door. The Sultan's "فدائیان" killed him, and Muhadhdhab al-Din escaped with two wounds (p. 396).
  - Badr al-Din Sunqur Rumi became "امیر حاجب". Conspirators met "روز دوشنبه هفدهم ماه صفر" in the house of "صدرالملک تاج الدین که مشرف ممالک بود". Those named are "قاضی جلال الدین کاشانی وقاضی کبیرالدین وشیخ محمد شامی" (p. 396).
  - Taj al-Din warned the Sultan, who dispersed them. Badr al-Din was sent to Badaun, and Qazi Jalal al-Din was dismissed (pp. 395–396).
  - Badr al-Din and "ملک تاج الدین موسٰی" were later killed. "قاضی شمس الدین که قاضی مهرپوره[OCR?] بود" was thrown under an elephant (p. 395).
  - After the Lahore episode the amirs besieged Delhi. On "روز دوشنبه هشتم ماه ذیقعده سال مذکور" they took it, held Bahram Shah some days and killed him. Reign: "دو سال ویک ماه وپانزده روز" (p. 395).
- **Ala al-Din Mas'ud Shah.**
  - After "نظام الملک کورنمک" killed Bahram, "ملک معزالدین[OCR?] [margin] [illegible] بلبن امیرالامرا" seized the throne, but the nobles would not accept him (pp. 394–395).
  - Ala al-Din was brought from prison and enthroned "در سنه ۶۳۹". Nasir al-Din and Jalal al-Din, sons of Shams al-Din, were freed and given "ولایت بهرایچ" and "ولایت قنوج". Nizam al-Mulk was killed (p. 394).
  - Ala al-Din later turned to "اخذ وقتل". The amirs brought Nasir al-Din from Bahraich, and Ala al-Din died in prison. Reign: "چهار سال ویک ماه ویک روز" (p. 394).
- **Nasir al-Din.**
  - He came from Bahraich "در سنه ۶۴۴[OCR?]". He made Balban wazir with "خطاب الغ[OCR?] خانی وعطائے چتر ودورباش[OCR?]" (p. 394).
  - He copied two Qurans a year "بخط خود" and lived on their price. When a servant bought one at an inflated price to please him, he ordered that they be sold only secretly (p. 393).
  - He kept no slave-girl. His wife cooked, and he refused to buy her a servant: "بیت المال حق سپاه ومساکین ومستحقین است" (p. 393).
  - He died a natural death. Reign: "نوزده سال وسه ماه وهفت روز" (pp. 392–393).
- **Ghiyas al-Din Balban** ("الغ خان"). Raised by the amirs "در سنه ۶۶۶[OCR?] هجری" (p. 392).
  - "گویند": "فخرا نامی رئیس بازار" offered great gifts if the Sultan would speak with him once. The Sultan refused (pp. 391–392).
  - The amir al-umara Fakhr al-Din refused a gift ("اگر رشوت بگیرم ...") and won back the jagirs of old soldiers (p. 387).
  - Hulaku Khan, "والی بغداد", praised Balban's daily hunting as a cover for drill (p. 388).
  - Balban suppressed the "میواتیان" near Delhi and the "کاتهیر[OCR?]" toward Amroha and Badaun (pp. 387–388).
  - Balban's age at his son's death: "از هشتاد گذشته". He summoned Nasir al-Din Bughra Khan from "لکهنوتی عرف بنگاله", but Bughra Khan left without leave. Balban's reign: "بیست سال و سه ماه" (p. 384).
- **Kaiqubad**, son of "ناصرالدین[OCR?] ابغراخان[OCR?]". He was enthroned at "هزده[OCR?] ساله" "در سنه ۶۸۶[OCR?]", with Fakhr al-Din in control. He built a palace-city at "کیلوکهری بر کنار دریائے جمنا" (p. 383).

Also Kaithal[OCR?] (p. 396), Badaun (pp. 395, 387), Bahraich and Qannauj (p. 394), Amroha (p. 387), "قصبه ریواری[OCR?]" (p. 388), Kilokhri (p. 383), Lakhnauti alias Bengal (p. 384), "قصبه سلطان پور" on the Beas (p. 395), Balkh[OCR?] (p. 385), Shiraz (p. 386), Baghdad (p. 388), and "ولایت گجرات ومالوه" (p. 388).

## Practices, institutions, economy

**Religious patronage**
- **A planned khanqah and waqf at Multan for Sa'di.** The prince wished to build it and endow villages; the book records only the wish (p. 386).
- **Stipends for poets at Multan.** Amir Khusrau and Amir Hasan "در سلک ندما مواجب و انعام می یافتند" (p. 386).
- **Nasir al-Din's spending.** He spent "خراج وباج ممالک" on soldiers' pay and "نذر[OCR?] درویشان خدا آگاه". Also on "وظایف وادرار فضلا وعلما", and on "عمارات مساجد وخانقاه وقنطره ومنازل وسرائے برائے مسافران", gardens and canals. He took nothing for himself (p. 393).
- **Balban's devotions.** After his accession he repented of "تمام ملاهی". He kept the fard prayers, "تهجد واشراق وچاشت", and Friday congregation, and was never without wudu (p. 391).
  - He visited the houses of "بزرگان", and "بعد از نماز جمعه زیارت مقابر کردی" (p. 391).
  - He attended the funerals of notables, paid condolence calls, gave gifts to the heirs and kept their "وظایف بحال[OCR?]" (p. 391).
- **Halala.** The book narrates it as the sharia remedy after the prince's drunken divorce (p. 386).

**State and revenue**
- **Jagirs.**
  - "مملکت سنده" was given in jagir to the heir (p. 387).
  - Balban ordered the jagirs of old, unfit soldiers resumed and replaced with "مدد معاش". He restored them at Fakhr al-Din's plea (p. 387).
  - Balban posted thanas around Delhi and divided land among the thanadars (p. 387).
- **Royal insignia.**
  - "چتر و دورباش" were granted to Kaikhusrau at Multan (p. 384) and to Balban as wazir (p. 394).
  - Elephants were a sultan's prerogative: "دران زمان غیر از سلاطین دیگرے فیل نمیداشت" (p. 396).
- **Balban's hunting establishment.** Game was preserved "تا هشت کروهے حوالی دهلی". "هزار سوار بایک هزار تیر انداز" rode with him and ate from his table (p. 388).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The prince's death, as the author explains it.** "مقرر است که هر کس که با درویشان خدا شناس در افتد زود که بنیاد زندگی و دولت او بر افتد". Because Sadr al-Din was "از باریافتگان درگاه ایزدی", "شاهزاده را عداوت شیخ باعث انقطاع رشته بقا گردید". The prince fell soon after in "اندک جنگ" with the Mongols (p. 385).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Saint over prince.** The author attributes Prince Muhammad Sultan's death to his enmity with Shaikh Sadr al-Din, son of Bahauddin Zakariya (p. 385).
- **Hearsay.** The halala story is introduced with "گویند" (p. 386), and so is the Fakhra anecdote (p. 392).
- **Sa'di's books.** The author claims the Gulistan and Bustan spread in India through the copies Sa'di sent to Multan: "ازان وقت هر دو کتاب متبرکه[OCR?] در هندوستان رواج یافت" (p. 386).
- **Judgements on rulers.**
  - On Bughra Khan: "در اصل ناصرالدین از سلطنت بے نصیب بود و اثار ناقابلیت ازو هویدا" (p. 384).
  - On Balban's severity: "در باب بغی واهل طغیان خدا پرستی را بگوشه گذاشته کمال جباری وقهاری کردی" (p. 390).
  - On the Katehriya massacre, the author adds a verse of disapproval: "گنه بود مردم ستمکاره را / چه تاوان زن وطفل بیچاره را" (p. 387).
  - On the disorder of Iltutmish's sons: "سابقا از غفلت وبے پروائی پسران سلطان شمس الدین در جمیع امور جهانبانے اختلال رو داد" (p. 388).
- **Balban's frontier reasoning.** As reported, he would not campaign far from Delhi because the Mongols raided Punjab (p. 388).

## Doubts

**Reading order and edges**
- **Reading order.** The chunk runs from p. 396 down to p. 383. It opens mid-sentence in Raziya's reign ("مدت باتفاق امرا بترتیب عساکر پرداخته", p. 396). It ends mid-sentence in Kaiqubad's reign ("سرایندگان قمری نوا و ترانه سنجان", p. 383).
- **Page joins.** All page joins read continuously. The Sind jagir and the Multan posting form one sentence across pp. 387–386.

**Mapping judgements**
- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya: person-level, weigh accordingly.** He is named only as Sadr al-Din's father, with epithets and "قدس الله سره". The chunk does not place him at Multan or mention his tomb. The Multan setting comes only from the prince's posting there, and the book does not say where Sadr al-Din lived. One word in his epithets is "[illegible]" ("اولیاے [illegible] اصفیاے").
- **Multan line: place-level, weigh accordingly.** It is attached under addendum point 3 because the book speaks of Multan's governors, its court and a planned building. None of the eight rows' saints or sites is named, apart from Bahauddin as a father.
- **Lahore line: place-level, weigh accordingly.** The material is a siege, the governor's flight and the city's ruin, so it is about the place itself. The held-identity rows (shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri) are attached only place-level; no name in the chunk raises their question.
- **No rows for these.** Shaikh Sadr al-Din, Sa'di, Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar, Amir Khusrau and Amir Hasan are not principal figures of any row.
- **"قصبه سلطان پور" on the Beas** has no row in toponym_map and is not treated as Lahore.

**Traps avoided**
- **"شیخ الاسلام" (p. 395)** here is Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar at Delhi. It is not Bahauddin, whatever the brief's Multan default says.
- **"قطب الدین بختیار اوشی"** is not **tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak**, whose principal figure is Sultan Qutb ud-Din Aibak. Aibak is not named in this chunk.
- **"شمس الدین"** in this chunk is always Sultan Shams al-Din ("التمش", p. 396) or "قاضی شمس الدین" of Mihrpura[OCR?] (p. 395). Neither is the Multan "Shams".
- **"جلال الدین"** is a son of Shams al-Din given Qannauj (p. 394), or "قاضی جلال الدین کاشانی" (pp. 395–396). Neither is the Uch Jalal.
- **"سرور" (p. 390, "چهره شاهد سرور")** means "joy", not Sakhi Sarwar. "لعل" and "جمال" occur only as ordinary vocabulary.
- **"ولایت گجرات ومالوه" (p. 388)** is Gujarat with Malwa, not Punjab Gujrat. It is not attached to shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai.

**Province bullets**
- **Both province bullets are province-level. Weigh accordingly.** "ولایت پنجاب" and "مملکت سنده" are the book's own units for Balban's time, not the modern provinces' boundaries. No extent is supplied from outside the text.
- **What "مملکت سنده" covers is not stated.** The book does not say whether it includes Multan. It only joins the Sind jagir and the Multan posting in one sentence (pp. 386–387).
- **"ولایت پنجاب"** occurs in Balban's reported speech about Mongol raids (p. 388).

**Unresolved internal points**
- **The p. 395 usurper.** The name of the man who seized the throne after Bahram Shah is damaged: "ملک معزالدین[OCR?] [margin] [illegible] بلبن امیرالامرا". It is not resolved who is meant, or how this "بلبن" relates to the later Sultan Balban. The gap is not filled.
- **Two "ملک اختیارالدین" on p. 396.** One is taken with Raziya; the other takes over affairs under Bahram Shah and is killed. The book does not say whether they are one man. A third man, "قاضی اختیارالدین", is the sister's former husband.
- **Bahram Shah's accession date.** On p. 396 he is already on the throne when Raziya marches. Yet his accession is printed "بست وهشتم ماه رمضان سنه ۶۳۷[OCR?]", which comes after her death on "بست وپنجم ماه ربیع الاول سنه ۶۳۷[OCR?]". Both years are flagged. This is recorded, not resolved.
- **Bughra Khan's name.** It is printed "ناصرالدین[OCR?] ابغراخان[OCR?]" on p. 383 but "ناصرالدین بغراخان" on p. 384.
