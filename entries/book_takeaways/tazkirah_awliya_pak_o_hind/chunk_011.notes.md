# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 011 — pp. 211–230

**The tail of an unnamed entry plus chapters 41 to 45 of the compendium.** The chunk runs: the closing
pages of an entry whose subject is never named on them, a Qadiri shaykh whose wilayat the book places at
**کتھیل[OCR?]** (Kaithal [OCR?]) (pp. 211–213, folios 207–209); باب 41 حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین (pp. 214–216,
folios 210–212); باب 42 حضرت شیخ احمد محمد[OCR?] شیبانی (pp. 217–219, folios 213–215); باب 43 حضرت خواجہ
خانو[OCR?] (p. 220, folio 216); باب 44 حضرت مولانا شیخ جمالی (pp. 221–226, folios 217–222); and باب 45
حضرت شیخ عبدالقدوس (pp. 227–230, folios 223–226), whose text is complete but whose footnote block is cut
off at the chunk's end.

**Only one archive row is engaged, and it is engaged incidentally** — Bahauddin Zakariya of Multan, whose
mazar Shaikh Jamali visits on his travels (p. 222, folio 218). Every other figure in the chunk is
stationed by the book at **کتھیل[OCR?]** (Kaithal [OCR?]), **قصبہ جنید[OCR?]**, **نار نول** (Narnaul),
**اجمیر** (Ajmer), **ناگور** (Nagaur), **منڈو** (Mandu), **گوالیار** (Gwalior), **مہرولی** (Mehrauli),
**ردولی** (Rudauli), **شاہ آباد** (Shahabad) or **گنگوہ** (Gangoh), none of which the chunk connects to
any archive location. Three name-collisions with archive rows occur and none of them is a match: see
Doubts.

**The chunk's institutional yield**: an exclusive territorial wilayat policed against other shaykhs, a
seventy-year residence at a shrine darbar, a philosopher's stone offered to a starving khanqah household
and disposed of in a river, a khirqa conferred at the Kaaba with unrestricted power to initiate, two
court-patronised migrations of a khanqah, a grand mausoleum at Mehrauli, and a saint who farmed.

**Folio relation**: every page from p. 211 to p. 230 prints both a `[p. N]` and a `[folio N]` marker, and
the printed pairs run p. 211 → folio 207 through p. 230 → folio 226. No page in this chunk is marked
`[blank page]`.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی):
  - **He appears only as the occupant of a mazar visited by a travelling shaykh.** In the account of Shaikh
    Jamali's journeys: "ملتان میں آپ حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے مزار پر انوار پر حاضر
    ہوئے اور شیخ صدر الدین سے ملاقات کی" — in **ملتان** (Multan) he presented himself at the luminous mazar
    of Hazrat Baha al-Din Zakariya Multani and met **شیخ صدر الدین** (Shaikh Sadr al-Din) (p. 222,
    folio 218). The nisba as printed is "ملتانی", Multani; the town as printed is "ملتان", Multan.
  - **The mazar is described only by the formula "مزار پر انوار"** (luminous mazar) (p. 222, folio 218).
    Nothing on its buildings, its urs, its custodianship or its endowment.
  - **Multan stands in a list of stops on one itinerary**, between the Haramain and مصر (Egypt), بغداد
    (Baghdad), بیت المقدس, روم, شام, عراق, عرب و عجم, آزربائجان, گیلان, مازندران and خراسان (p. 222,
    folio 218, حاشیہ 3 = المشاہیر صفحہ 209).
  - **Death year: (not stated). Silsila: (not stated).** The chunk attaches no order-name and no date to
    him; the archive row's "1267" and "Suhrawardi" are neither confirmed nor contradicted here.
  - **A second, distinct "بہاء الدین" occupies باب 41 of this same chunk** and must not be confused with
    him — see Doubts.

  water and victuals, "عشق و محبت کی آگ", whose food is "خون جگر اور خاشاک", and "محبوب و معشوق کی آگ",
  whose food is "حسن و جمال اور اوصاف کمال" (p. 230, folio 226). Under "فرض": "خانہ بشریت سے نکل کر شہر
  احدیث[OCR?] کی طرف ہجرت کرنا چاہئے اور حضرت صمدیت کا مشتاق ہونا چاہیے" (p. 230, folio 226, حاشیہ 13).
- **مولانا چندن** (p. 230, folio 226): teacher of Abdul Quddus's son Shaikh Rukn al-Din and himself
  bai'at to Abdul Quddus; the subject of the tank karamat.
- **Places named**: **کتھیل[OCR?]** (pp. 211, 212; folios 207, 208), **قصبہ جنید[OCR?]** and **حرم شریف /
  کعبہ** (p. 214, folio 210), **نار نول** (pp. 217, 218; folios 213, 214), **اجمیر** (pp. 217, 218, 219;
  folios 213, 214, 215), **ناگور** (p. 218, folio 214), **منڈو** (pp. 218, 219; folios 214, 215),
  **گوالیار** (p. 220, folio 216), **ملتان، مصر، بغداد، بیت المقدس، روم، شام، عراق، عرب و عجم،
  آزربائجان، گیلان، مازندران، خراسان، مدینہ منورہ، حرمین شریف** (p. 222, folio 218), **ہرات** (pp. 222,
  223, 225; folios 218, 219, 221), **دہلی** (pp. 223, 228, 229; folios 219, 224, 225), **سنبھل** (p. 223,
  folio 219), **مہرولی** and **پانی پت** (pp. 224, 225; folios 220, 221), **ردولی** (pp. 227, 228;
  folios 223, 224), **شاہ آباد** and **گنگوہ** (p. 228, folio 224), **موضع چھاجپور[OCR?]** (p. 230,
  folio 226).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **An exclusive territorial wilayat, policed by permission and enforced by stripping other shaykhs.**
  "آپ کتھیل[OCR?] کے صاحب ولایت تھے" — he was the holder of the wilayat of **کتھیل[OCR?]** (p. 211,
  folio 207) — and "کوئی صاحب ولایت کتھیل[OCR?] کے قریب بغیر آپ کی اجازت کے نہیں آ سکتا تھا، اگر کوئی ہمت
  کرتا تو اس کی ساری صلاحیتیں سلب کر لیتے تھے" — **no holder of wilayat could come near Kaithal[OCR?]
  without his permission**, and if anyone ventured it he stripped away all that man's capacities (p. 211,
  folio 207). **The sharpest statement of a saint's jurisdiction over a territory anywhere in the chunk**,
  and the closest this book comes to describing a spiritual monopoly as a defended franchise.
- **The same power used inside the family, against the display of karamat.** "آپ نے اپنے بڑے صاحب زادے شاہ
  عمادالدین کی صلاحیتیں ان سے کرامت سرزد ہونے پر سلب کر لی" — he stripped his elder son **شاہ عمادالدین**
  of his capacities when a karamat issued from him; and when a karamat issued from the younger son
  **نورالدین**, "آپ نے ان کے سینے پر اپنا ہاتھ پھیرا، ہاتھ پھیرنا تھا کہ ان کا انتقال ہو گیا", he passed
  his hand over his chest and at the passing of the hand the son died (p. 211, folio 207). Succession in
  this household is thus described as broken by the father himself; **the chunk names no sajjada nashin
  for him.**
- **The tank as the public space of the shrine town.** A Hindu faqir "اپنی آنتوں کو نکال کر کتھیل[OCR?] کے
  تالاب کے کنارے صاف کرایا کرتا تھا" — used to take out his intestines and have them cleaned on the bank
  of the **تالاب** (tank) of Kaithal[OCR?] (p. 212, folio 208). A second tank, unlocated, is the scene of
  the Maulana Chandan episode (p. 230, folio 226), and a third is where that maulana "کپڑے دھونے تالاب پر
  گئے", went to wash clothes.
- **A philosopher's stone brought to relieve a starving khanqah household, and thrown into the river.**
  Bawa Smel[OCR?] Puri found the shaykh's younger son "پژمردہ، ناتواں اور کمزور" — withered, feeble and
  weak — and the boy, too young to conceal it, "صاف صاف بتا دیا کہ کئی دن کھانا کھائے ہو گئے ہیں", said
  plainly that several days had passed since they ate; he went back at once and returned with "ایک پارس
  پتھر", presenting it with the submission "کہ اس سے اگر لوہے کو مس کیا جائے لوہا سونا بن جاتا ہے", that
  iron touched with it becomes gold (p. 212, folio 208). Returning later he was astonished that "سنگ پارس
  کے ہوتے ہوئے یہ افلاس یہ غربت اور یہ ناداری" — with the paras stone in the house, such destitution,
  poverty and want (p. 213, folio 209). The shaykh took him outside, threw a clod after istinja, the
  ground became gold, and he said "جتنا چاہو بلا تکلف اٹھالو", take as much as you wish without ceremony;
  then he gave the reason for the fasting: "فاقہ کشی کی اصل وجہ یہ ہے کہ سنت رسول ﷺ ادا کر رہا ہوں", the
  real reason for the starving is that I am performing the sunna of the Prophet (p. 213, folio 209).
  **Afterwards** "بعد ازاں باوا سمیل[OCR?] پوری کا پیش کردہ سنگ پارس دریا میں ڈالوا دیا" — he had the
  paras stone presented by Bawa Smel[OCR?] Puri **thrown into the river** (p. 213, folio 209). **The
  chunk's central economic statement: destitution at a khanqah is a chosen practice, and an instrument of
  unlimited wealth is deliberately destroyed.**
- **Initiation by کلاہ.** After the tawajjuh, "وہ آپ کے حلقہ ارادت میں داخل ہوئے، آپ نے ان کو کلاہ دے کر
  سرفراز فرمایا" — he entered the circle of discipleship and the shaykh honoured him by giving him the
  **کلاہ** (cap) (p. 212, folio 208).
- **A khirqa conferred at the Kaaba, with unrestricted power to initiate and invest.** Shaikh Baha al-Din
  states his own nisbat: the talqin descended from "شیخ السماوات والارض، شیخ محی الدین عبدالقادر الجیلی"
  to his son "شیخ عبدالرزاق", and through "شیوخا[OCR?]" to "میرے شیخ و مرشد سید احمد الجیلی القادری
  الشافعی"; "اور میرے شیخ نے تلقین کئے مجھ کو تمام اذکار، اور پہنایا مجھ کو خرقۂ قادریہ حرم شریف میں کعبہ
  کے پاس اور اجازت دی مجھ کو مطلقہ کہ اجازت دوں، میں اس کو جو مجھے سے اجازت مانگے اور تلقین کروں اور
  پہناؤں خرقہ اس کو جھے سے تلقین چاہیے" — my shaykh instructed me in all the azkar and **clothed me in the
  khirqa-e Qadiriya in the Haram Sharif beside the Kaaba**, and gave me **unrestricted ijazat** to
  authorise whoever asks authorisation of me, and to instruct and to invest with the khirqa whoever wants
  instruction from me (p. 214, folio 210). **The most explicit grant of transmission rights in the chunk,
  and the only one located at a named sanctuary rather than a khanqah.**
- **A seventy-year residence at a shrine darbar.** "اٹھارہ سال کی عمر میں آپ اپنے وطن نار نول کو چھوڑ کر
  اجمیر آئے اور دربار خواجہ غریب نواز میں رہنے لگے۔ اجمیر میں (70) ستر سال رہے" — at eighteen he left his
  home town **نار نول** for **اجمیر** and took up residence **in the darbar of Khwaja Gharib Nawaz**; he
  remained **seventy years** in Ajmer (p. 217, folio 213, حاشیہ 1 = اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) صفحہ 387,
  followed by "[illegible]" — the transcriber records "a short superscript mark follows the footnote
  number"). **The longest residence figure in the chunk and the only instance of a saint living at another
  saint's shrine.**
- **An evacuation ordered from the shrine, and the shrine's own occupant leaving.** "خواجہ غریب نواز کا
  روحانی اشارہ پا کر آپ نے اجمیر کے باشندوں کو اجمیر پر آنے والی مصیبت سے آگاہ کر دیا تھا، اور خود بھی
  اجمیر سے چلے گئے" — having received a spiritual indication from Khwaja Gharib Nawaz he warned the
  inhabitants of Ajmer of a calamity coming upon Ajmer, and himself left Ajmer (pp. 217–218,
  folios 213–214); printed again under "کرامت" as "آپ بفرمان خواجہ غریب نواز اجمیر سے باہر چلے گئے"
  (p. 219, folio 215). The calamity is never named.
- **Dress as an occasional and social marker.** "آپ معمولی قسم کے کپڑے زیب تن فرماتے تھے۔ آپ کے پاس ایک
  خاص پوشاک اور ایک دستار رہتی تھی، یہ خاص کپڑے عید اور جمعہ کی نماز کے موقع پر پہنتے تھے یا اگر کوئی
  دنیا دار آتا تھا، جب پہنتے تھے" — he wore ordinary clothes but kept **one special garment and one
  dastar**, worn on the occasion of the **Eid and Friday prayers**, or when a worldly person came (p. 218,
  folio 214). Cf. the red and military-cut dress of the unnamed shaykh (p. 212, folio 208).
- **سماع as an avowed taste, twice.** "سماع کا شوق تھا" of Shaikh Ahmad Shaibani (p. 218, folio 214); and
  of Abdul Quddus, "سماع کا شوق تھا"; in sama' "اکثر آپ پر کیفیت طاری ہوتی اور آپ رقص کرنے لگتے اور
  والہانہ انداز میں کچھ فرما دیتے" — a state would often come over him and he would begin to **dance**
  and utter things rapturously (p. 229, folio 225).
- **A mahfil-e sama' at Delhi with ulama in attendance, and a dispute inside it.** "ایک مرتبہ دہلی میں
  محفل سماع میں آپ شریک تھے" — once he was present at a **محفل سماع** at **دہلی**; in wajd he stood and
  repeated "منصور کو نادانوں نے قتل کیا", Mansur was killed by the ignorant; "علماء بھی محفل میں شریک
  تھے", ulama too were in the assembly; one of them named a great alim of Mansur's time and asked "کیا وہ
  نادان تھا"; he answered in the same state "اسی کو کہتا ہوں", him I mean, "وہ سب خاموش ہو گئے", and they
  all fell silent (p. 229, folio 225). **The chunk's one recorded confrontation between a shrine's
  devotional practice and the ulama present at it.**
- **Court etiquette refused as bid'at.** At **منڈو** (Mandu), "منڈو کے بادشاہوں کے سامنے جب لوگ جاتے تھے
  تو جھک کر اور انگشت شہادت کو زمین پر رکھ کر سلام کرتے تھے" — people going before the kings of Mandu
  saluted by bowing and placing the index finger on the ground; he declared "اس طرح سلام کرنا اسلامی
  تعلیم کے خلاف ہے اور یہ طریقہ بدعت ہے", that such salutation is contrary to Islamic teaching and the
  practice is **بدعت**; "آپ جب بادشاہ کے سامنے گئے تو سلام علیکم کہا اور اس کے برابر بیٹھ گئے", when he
  went before the king he said salam alaikum and sat down level with him (pp. 218–219, folios 214–215;
  the passage contradicts itself — see Doubts). His stated principle, "اہل دین کو اہل دنیا کے سامنے ذلیل
  نہ ہونا چاہیے، کیوں کہ وہ لوگ بظاہر بین[OCR?] ہیں" (p. 219, folio 215, حاشیہ 4).
- **Refusal of the courtesy of rising, justified as the state of faqirs.** Khwaja Khanu[OCR?] "کسی کی
  تعظیم کے لئے کھڑے نہیں ہوتے تھے"; asked why, he said "میں بوڑھا اور ضعیف ہو گیا ہوں، ہر آنے جانے والے
  کی تعظیم کے لئے قیام نہیں کر سکتا، بعض کے لئے خصوصیت سے قیام کرنا حال فقراء کے لائق نہیں، مجھ کو معذور
  رکھیں" — I have grown old and weak and cannot rise for every comer, and to rise specially for some is
  not fitting to the state of faqirs; hold me excused (p. 220, folio 216, حاشیہ 2).
- **Personal service of the pir, itemised, as the route to succession.** Of Jamali: "پیر و مرشد کو وضو
  کرانے کی خدمت آپ کے سپرد تھیں رومال، طشت اور لوٹا، آپ اپنے پاس رکھتے تھے، آپ شہر سے باہر جا کر پیر و
  مرشد کے واسطے استنجے کے ڈیلے ٹوکری میں بھر کر اور ٹوکری سر پر رکھ لیتے تھے" — the service of assisting
  his pir's ablutions was assigned to him; he kept the **رومال، طشت اور لوٹا** (cloth, basin and ewer) by
  him, and he would go outside the city, fill a **ٹوکری** (basket) with istinja clods for his pir and
  carry the basket on his head (p. 221, folio 217). **The same office — "پیر و مرشد کو وضو کرانے کی خدمت
  آپ کے سپرد" — is worded identically in this book for another saint; it reads as a formula of the genre.**
- **A shaykh's mazar visited as a station of a long journey, and a living successor met at the same
  stop.** In Multan, the mazar of Baha al-Din Zakariya Multani and a meeting with Shaikh Sadr al-Din; in
  Baghdad, "غوث الاعظم پیران پیر اور حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین سہروردی کے مزارات پر حاضر ہو کر دولت سرمدی حاصل
  کی", at the mazars of Ghaus al-Azam Piran Pir and Shaikh Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi he gained everlasting
  wealth; in Madina he benefited from "فیضان بنوی" (p. 222, folio 218, حاشیہ 3 = المشاہیر صفحہ 209).
  **A tazkirah-level record of shrine tourism as a stage in a shaykh's formation.**
- **Royal patronage as poetry correction and repeated visits.** Sikandar Lodhi "آپ سے اصلاح لیا کرتا تھا",
  used to have his verse corrected by him, and sent a verse letter on learning of his return to Delhi
  (p. 223, folio 219); "بابر اور ہمایوں کو بھی آپ سے بے حد محبت تھی، وہ دونوں بادشاہ بھی آپ کا بڑا اعزاز
  و اکرام کرتے تھے اور کئی بار گلہائے عقیدت پیش کرنے آپ کے در دولت پر حاضر ہوئے" — **Babur and Humayun**
  both held him in great affection and honour and came **several times** to his "در دولت" to present
  flowers of devotion (p. 224, folio 220, حاشیہ 5 = المشاہیر صفحہ 215). **The chunk's only record of
  reigning sovereigns visiting a living shaykh's residence.** No grant, stipend or land is named in
  connection with any of them.
- **A grand mausoleum, and three chronograms of the death date.** "آپ کا مزار مبارک مہرولی میں واقع ہے۔
  آپ کا مقبرہ عالی شان ہے" — the mazar is at **مہرولی** and "آپ کا مقبرہ عالی شان ہے", his **maqbara is
  of grand construction**; "آپ کی تاریخ وفات 'خسرو ہند بودہ' ہے", the chronogram of his death is "خسرو
  ہند بودہ", and "طالب اہل جمال معرفت" and "ماہ خلد بریں" are also chronograms of his death (p. 224,
  folio 220, حاشیہ 6 = خیزینۃ الاصفیا[OCR?]). **The only building description in the chunk**, and the only
  place the book records a تاریخ وفات at all. No urs, no custodian and no endowment for this tomb.
- **Sweeping a mazar as a novice's service.** Of Abdul Quddus: "آپ جب ذرا بڑے ہوئے تو حضرت شیخ احمد
  عبدالخالق[OCR?] رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے مزار پر جھاڑو دینا شروع کیا" — when he grew a little he began
  **sweeping the mazar** of Shaikh Ahmad Abd al-Khaliq[OCR?] (p. 227, folio 223). His whole vocation is
  dated from a sound heard "مزار کے اندر", inside the mazar (p. 227, folio 223).
- **A khanqah moved at the request of a court amir, and held thirty years.** "عمر خان کاشی جو سلطان سکندر
  لودھی کے امراء میں تھا' آپ کا معتقد و منقاد تھا' اس کی درخواست پر آپ مع اہل و عیال ردولی سے سکونت ترک
  کر کے شاہ آباد ' جو دہلی کے قرب و جوار میں واقع ہے۔ تشریف لے گئے۔ اور وہاں (30) تیس[OCR?] سال سے زیادہ
  قیام فرمایا" — **عمر خان کاشی**, one of Sultan Sikandar Lodhi's amirs, was his devotee and follower, and
  **at his request** he gave up residence at **ردولی** and went with his household to **شاہ آباد**, which
  lies in the neighbourhood of Delhi, and stayed there **more than thirty[OCR?] years** (p. 228,
  folio 224). **A named nobleman relocating a shaykh's establishment — the nearest thing in this chunk to
  a patronage transaction, and no grant or stipend is attached to it.**
- **The same establishment moved again by a military destruction of the town.** "شاہ آباد پٹھانوں کا مرکز
  سمجھا جاتا تھا۔ وہاں افغان کافی تعداد میں تھے۔ جب بابر نے ہندوستان کو فتح کیا تو افغان کو منتشر کرنے کی
  غرض سے شاہ آباد کو برباد کیا۔ آپ سے شاہ آباد کی بربادی نہ دیکھی گئی۔ آپ نے وہاں سے سکونت ترک کرنے کی
  ٹھان لی' چنانچہ آپ مع متعلقین گنگوہ تشریف لے گئے۔ اور وہیں سکونت پذیر ہوئے" — **شاہ آباد** was reckoned
  a centre of the Pathans and Afghans were there in considerable numbers; when **بابر** conquered
  Hindustan he **laid Shahabad waste** in order to scatter the Afghans; he could not bear to watch
  Shahabad's ruin, resolved to leave, and went with his dependants to **گنگوہ**, settling there (p. 228,
  folio 224, حاشیہ 7). **The chunk's one account of state violence displacing a khanqah, and the origin it
  gives for the Gangoh seat.**
- **A saint who farmed.** "آپ کاشت کرتے تھے" — he practised **cultivation** (p. 229, folio 225,
  حاشیہ 11). A bare four words, and the only statement of a productive livelihood for any figure in this
  chunk.
- **A village warned to evacuate with its property.** At "موضع چھاجپور[OCR?]", in a state of absorption,
  he said aloud "کہ گاؤں کے لوگوں کو چاہیے کہ اپنا مال و اسباب لے کر گھروں سے باہر نکل جائیں' گاؤں کو آگ
  لگنے والی ہے" — the village people should take **their goods and belongings** and go out of their
  houses, the village is about to catch fire; shortly the village caught fire and "جن لوگوں نے آپ کی
  ہدایت کے موافق عمل نہیں کیا' وہ پشیمان ہوئے اور ان کو نقصان برداشت کرنا پڑا", those who did not act on
  his instruction regretted it and had to bear the loss (p. 230, folio 226, حاشیہ 14).
- **The pir as a standing guardian of his murid's conduct.** After the tank episode the shaykh smiled and
  told Maulana Chandan "کچھ دہشت کی بات نہیں ہے' پیر محافظ وقت ہوتے ہیں" — there is nothing to be alarmed
  at; the pir is the guardian of the moment (p. 230, folio 226). **A statement of what the pir–murid bond
  is understood to deliver, in the book's own words.**

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Uwaisi transmission from a long-dead saint's spirit, twice.** The author states of the unnamed shaykh
  that he had fayz "حضرت غوث اعظم میران محی الدین سید عبدالقادر جیلانی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کی روح پر فتوح سے
  براہ راست اویسی طریقے سے" — directly from the spirit of Ghaus-e Azam, by the Uwaisi method (p. 211,

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Every chapter is footnoted to a named earlier work, and the canon widens here beyond the Persian
  tazkirah staples.** The closing entry cites "گلزار الخوارق" (twice), "جواہر محمدیہ" (twice) and "مبدأ و
  معاد (اردو ترجمہ) ص 3,2" (p. 213, folio 209); باب 41–43 cite only "اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ)"
  (pp. 216, 219, 220; folios 212, 215, 216); باب 44 cites "المشاہیر" three times, "روضۃ الاقطاب", "دربار
  اکبری", "خیزینۃ الاصفیا[OCR?]", "خزانہ عامری", "درباری اکبری" and "اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ)" (p. 226,
  folio 222); باب 45's surviving footnotes cite "سفینۃ الاولیاء (فارسی) صفحہ 101" and "سیر الاقطاب
  (فارسی) صفحہ 215" (p. 230, folio 226). **Nothing in the chunk is presented as the compiler's own
  inquiry, and the one evaluative voice in it is quoted (Mujaddid Alf-e Sani, p. 211, folio 207; Abd
  al-Haq Muhaddis Dehlavi, p. 225, folio 221).**
- **He prints variants side by side four times rather than choosing.** Jamali's original name and takhallus
  have two versions, the second introduced "یہ بھی کہا جاتا ہے" and separately footnoted (p. 221,
  folio 217, حواشی 1–2); Abdul Quddus's father has two names, "بعض کے نزدیک" (p. 227, folio 223,
  حواشی 2–3); his death has two years, "بعض نے آپ کی وفات 945ھ میں ہونا لکھا ہے" (p. 228, folio 224,
  حواشی 9–10); and his bai'at has two pirs plus a third, "اصل میں", real source (pp. 227–228,
  folios 223–224). **This is the same editorial habit as the book's handling of variant karamat: two
  readings printed, neither preferred.**
- **A conflicting affiliation stated flatly and left unexplained.** Baha al-Din is described in one
  sentence as "قادری سلسلہ کے ممتاز بزرگ" who "شطاری مشرب کی پیروی کرتے تھے", a prominent elder of the
  Qadiri silsila who followed the Shattari mashrab, and his own nisbat-statement is purely Qadiri (p. 214,
  folio 210) while his entire teaching section expounds "طریق شطاریہ" as the nearest road to God, better
  than the two others (pp. 215–216, folios 211–212). **The book neither notices the tension nor resolves
  it.**
- **The entries run on the book's fixed grid of Urdu sub-headings, applied very unevenly.** باب 44 carries
  some fifteen headings over six pages (pp. 221–226, folios 217–222) — نام اور تخلص, تعلیم و تربیت,
  بیعت و خلافت, پیر و مرشد کی آپ سے محبت, سیر و سیاحت, ہرات کا ایک واقعہ, بادشاہوں سے تعلقات, شادی اور
  اولاد, وفات شریف, سیرت پاک, علمی ذوق, شعر و سخن, کرامات — while باب 43 carries four over one page
  (p. 220, folio 216): بیعت و خلافت, خواجہ بزرگ سے استفادہ, وفات, خلفاء, سیرت. **Absence of a heading is
  the book's silence, not a gap in the transcription**; باب 43 accordingly gives no birth, no parentage
  and no karamat at all.
- **Two headings label a conversion narrative with the same phrase.** "کایا پلٹ" (transformation) heads the
  abandonment of disputation in باب 42 (p. 217, folio 213) and the abandonment of writing in باب 45
  (p. 227, folio 223) — in both cases the turn is away from exoteric learning, and in both it follows
  contact with a pir or a mazar.

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and its subject is never named.** Pages 211–213 (folios 207–209) carry the
  tail of a name list, then "سیرت پاک", "ارشادات", "کشف و کرامات" and a five-item حواشی block, with **no
  chapter heading and no name for the subject**; the next heading, "باب 41", appears only on p. 214
  (folio 210). Inside the quoted words of Mujaddid Alf-e Sani he is called "شاہ صاحب" (p. 211, folio 207),
  and the same quotation names "حضرت شاہ کمال قادری قدس سرہ" in a chain of transmission — but the chunk
  never states that the two are the same person, and no name is printed as the entry's own. **Do not
  attribute anything on pp. 211–213 to a named saint until the previous chunk is read.**
- **This unnamed Kaithal[OCR?] figure must not be mapped to `shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal`.** That row's
  location is "Muslim Town / Samanabad (near Ichhra), Lahore" and its figure "Hazrat Baba Shah Kamal",
  Qadiri; the chunk's only locational statement about its subject is "کتھیل[OCR?]" (pp. 211, 212;
  folios 207, 208), which is not the row's location, and the chunk gives him no death year and no mazar at
  all. The silsila agreement (Qadiri) is not enough. **Left unmapped, deliberately.**
- **حضرت شیخ طاہر بندگی resembles `tahir-bandagi-qadri` and is left unmapped.** That row is "Darbar
  Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri", Miani Sahib Graveyard, Lahore; figure "Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri"; died
  "8 Muharram 1040 AH / 8 August 1630 CE"; Qadri. The book's "حضرت شیخ طاہر بندگی" appears **once**, as
  one of four who received "جلاؤ بقاء[OCR?]" from the unnamed Qadiri shaykh (p. 211, folio 207), with
  **no town, no district, no pir named as his, no death date and no silsila of his own**. Under the mapping
  rule a bare name inside a list is not a match. A human should settle whether the previous chunk's pages
  give this figure a location.
- **"شاہ یوسف غوث بھکری" in the p. 211 name list is not `shah-yousuf`.** That row is "Shah Yousuf,
  Shahpur, Punjab"; the book's nisba as printed is "بھکری", not Shahpur, and the name stands in an
  undifferentiated list with no dates, no shrine and no silsila (p. 211, folio 207). Not mapped.
- **"دربار خواجہ غریب نواز" at اجمیر is not `khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz`.** That row is "Darbar
  Hazrat Khawaja Feroz-ud-Din Gharib Nawaz Chishti Nizami", Miani Sahib Graveyard, Lahore, died
  "November 27, 1981 (Friday)". The book's "خواجہ غریب نواز" is a darbar at **اجمیر** in which a saint who
  died in "927ھ" resided for seventy years (pp. 217, 219; folios 213, 215) — a different person and a
  different place carrying the same title. **Do not map.**
- **باب 41's "حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین" is not the mapped Bahauddin Zakariya.** The book gives باب 41's subject
  a Qadiri silsila and Shattari mashrab, a father "ابراہیم" son of "عطاءاللہ انصاری", a home town "قصبہ
  جنید[OCR?]" and a death in "961ھ" (p. 214, folio 210), while "حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا ملتانی" appears
  separately at p. 222 (folio 218) as the occupant of a mazar at Multan. **Two distinct figures sharing a
  given name inside one chunk** — a human checking the mapping should not merge them.
- **Silence, not conflict, on the one row that is mapped.** For Bahauddin Zakariya the chunk gives **no
  death year and no silsila** (p. 222, folio 218); the archive row's "1267" and "Suhrawardi" are simply
  not addressed. Do not read the chunk as corroborating either. It also gives the shrine **no urs, no
  custodian, no endowment and no building date.**
- **"شیخ صدر الدین", met at Multan, has no archive row** (p. 222, folio 218). The chunk gives him only the
  name and the meeting — no mazar, no date, no silsila — so he cannot be proposed as a row from these
  pages alone.
- **باب 42's descent statement is circular.** The chapter opens "حضرت شیخ محمد[OCR?] شیبانی کی اولاد سے
  ہیں" and then, under "خاندانی حالات", "آپ حضرت شیخ احمد محمد[OCR?] شیبانی کی اولاد سے ہیں" — making the
  subject, whose own name the heading gives as "حضرت شیخ احمد محمد[OCR?] شیبانی", a descendant of himself
  (p. 217, folio 213). Either the ancestor's name or the subject's is misprinted; the flagged word is
  "محمد[OCR?]" in both. Do not build a lineage on this page.
- **Two spellings of one pir's name across two adjacent chapters.** باب 42's pir is "حضرت خواجہ
  حسن[OCR?] ناگوری" (p. 217, folio 213) and باب 43's is "حضرت خواجہ حسین ناگوری" (p. 220, folio 216). The
  first is OCR-flagged, the second is not. They may be one figure; the chunk does not say so.
- **Two spellings of one name inside four lines.** "شیخ محمد دہلوی نے جو شیخ الاسلام تھے" and then "آپ نے
  شیخ محمود[OCR?] دہلوی سے کہا" of the same man in the same anecdote (p. 218, folio 214); the second is
  flagged, the first is not.
- **The Mandu salutation passage contradicts itself.** "آپ نے بادشاہ کو اس طرح سلام کیا۔ اور فرمایا کہ اس
  طرح سلام کرنا اسلامی تعلیم کے خلاف ہے اور یہ طریقہ بدعت ہے" — he saluted the king in that manner and
  said that saluting in that manner is contrary to Islamic teaching and is bid'at — followed immediately
  by "آپ جب بادشاہ کے سامنے گئے تو سلام علیکم کہا اور اس کے برابر بیٹھ گئے" (p. 219, folio 215). A
  negative has probably been dropped; **do not state that he did or did not perform the prostration
  without re-reading this page from the PDF.**
- **Two spellings of one name inside one page.** "باوا شیل[OCR?] پوری" in the name list and "باوا
  سمیل[OCR?] پوری" five lines later (p. 211, folio 207), both flagged; and "حضرت شاہ ہاشم بنوتی[OCR?]"
  against "ہاشم نجوتوی[OCR?]" in the same list (p. 211, folio 207), both flagged. Probably one person in
  each pair; the chunk does not say so.
- **The Hindu faqir and Bawa Smel[OCR?] Puri are identified only by juxtaposition.** The episode opens
  "ایک ہندو فقیر" (a Hindu faqir) and, without a transition, calls the same man "باوا سمیل[OCR?] پوری"
  three lines later (p. 212, folio 208). The identification is the book's, made by placement rather than
  by statement, and his conversion to the shaykh's circle is what the passage records.
- **Three printed Urdu glosses do not carry what their originals print.**
