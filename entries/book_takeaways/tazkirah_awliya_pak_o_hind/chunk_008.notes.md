# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 008 — pp. 148–165

**The close of an unnamed entry, plus chapters 24 and 25.** The chunk runs: the tail of an entry whose
subject is **never named on these pages** (pp. 148–150, folios 144–146), ending in its حواشی block;
باب 24 حضرت شیخ فتح اللہ لواودھی (pp. 151–152, folios 147–148), a short complete entry with its own
حواشی; and باب 25 حضرت سید محمد گیسو دراز (pp. 153–165, folios 149–161), which fills thirteen of the
chunk's eighteen pages and **breaks off mid-prescription** at the end.

**No archive row is engaged by this chunk, and none should be.** Every figure here is stationed by the
book at **محمد پور, ظفر آباد, دہلی, دولت آباد, خلد آباد, گوالیار, چندیری, بڑودہ, کھمبائت** or
**گلبرگہ** — all in India — and the one shrine the chunk locates, Gesu Daraz's, it puts at
**گلبرگہ** (p. 156, folio 152). The archive's 169 rows carry nothing at Gulbarga or at any of these
places. The unnamed saint's mazar is described at length but **its town is never stated** (p. 150,
folio 146), so it cannot be placed either. See Doubts for the two names a mapper might be tempted by.

**The chunk's value for this project is institutional and liturgical, not locational**: a royal
dome built over a mazar by a named sultan, a mazar advertised as a cure for آسیب, a forced capital
migration with exact dates, a daily table of forty to fifty men, a teaching post at Gulbarga, a
sajjada nashini passing to a younger son, a library given away on a pir's order, rules on when a
darwesh may take a loan, and some twenty pages of prescribed اوراد, اذکار and سماع doctrine.

**Folio relation**: every page in the chunk prints a marker and the relation observed is folio =
PDF index − 4 throughout (p. 148 → folio 144 … p. 165 → folio 161). No page in this chunk is marked
`[blank page]`.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A royal dome built over a mazar, named as proof of a sultan's devotion.** Of Sultan Ahmad, younger
  brother of Sultan Firoz Bahmani: "اس کی عقیدت و محبت کا ثبوت آپ کے مزار مبارک پر عالی شان گنبد ہے،
  جو اس نے آپ کی وفات کے بعد بنوایا" — the proof of his devotion and love is the **عالی شان گنبد**
  (magnificent dome) over the mazar, **which he had built after the saint's death** (p. 155,
  folio 151). **The chunk's single building fact**: a named patron, a named structure, and a relative
  date (after 16 Zi-Qa'da 825 AH, p. 156, folio 152). **No cost, no endowment and no builder are
  named**, and the book gives the dome no dedication date of its own.
- **A sultan's invitation, a state reception, and a permanent relocation.** "سلطان فیروز شاہ بہمنی کو
  جب آپ کی دکن میں آمد کی اطلاع ہوئی تو وہ آپ سے گلبرگہ تشریف لانے کا خواستگار ہوا، آپ نے درخواست
  منظور فرمائی" — on learning of his arrival in the Deccan, Sultan Firoz Shah Bahmani **requested** him
  to come to Gulbarga and he accepted; "سلطان نے آپ کا شان دار استقبال کیا اور نہایت تعظیم و تکریم سے
  پیش آیا", the sultan gave him a splendid reception and treated him with the utmost honour; "پھر
  گلبرگہ میں مستقل سکونت اختیار فرما کر" — he then took **permanent residence at گلبرگہ** and began
  guiding the people (p. 155, folio 151). **The clearest statement of state patronage in the chunk**;
  no grant, stipend, village or land is mentioned with it.
- **A mazar in present use as a cure for possession, with a named alternative rite.** "آج بھی آپ کا
  مزار آسیب کو دور کرنے کے لئے مشہور ہے' جن لوگوں پر آسیب کا اثر ہوتا ہے' وہ آپ کے مزار مبارک کی برکت
  سے اچھے ہو جاتے ہیں" — **to this day** his mazar is famous for driving off **آسیب** (possession /
  affliction by spirits), and those affected are made well by the barakat of the mazar; "یہ بھی کہا
  جاتا [as printed] کہ آپ کا نام نامی [OCR?] اسم گرامی پڑھ کر دم کرنے سے آسیب زدہ کا اثر جاتا رہتا
  ہے" — it is also said that reciting his noble name and **blowing (دم)** removes the effect from the
  afflicted (p. 150, folio 146). **The chunk's only living-practice statement about any shrine, and
  the only place it uses the present tense of a mazar** — but see Doubts: the saint and the town are
  both unnamed on this page, so the practice cannot yet be attached to a site.
- **A mazar described as the qibla of the people's needs.** "مزار فیض آثار گلبرگہ میں قبلہ حاجات خلائق
  ہے" — the grace-bearing mazar at **گلبرگہ** is the **قبلہ حاجات خلائق**, the qibla of the creatures'
  needs (p. 156, folio 152). This is the chunk's only phrase about a shrine's standing as a place of
  petition. **No urs date, no offerings, no custodial body, no endowment and no visitor figure are
  given for it.**
- **Sajjada nashini passing over a dead elder son to the younger.** "آپ کی وفات کے بعد آپ کے چھوٹے
  صاحب زادے حضرت محمد یوسف عرف سید محمد اصغر حسینی آپ کے سجادہ نشین ہوئے" — after his death his
  **younger son** Muhammad Yusuf, alias Sayyid Muhammad Asghar Husaini, became his **سجادہ نشین**
  (p. 156, folio 152), the elder son having predeceased him (p. 156, folio 152). Ten "ممتاز خلفاء" are
  then listed with the sajjada nashin at their head (p. 156, folio 152). **Filial succession, stated in
  the book's own term, with no mention of a document, a seal or an investiture.**
- **A daily table of forty to fifty men in the Deccan.** "جب آپ دکن تشریف لے گئے، وہاں عشاء کی نماز کے
  بعد آپ کے ساتھ چالیس پچاس آدمی روزانہ کھانا کھاتے تھے" — after he went to the **دکن**, **forty or
  fifty men ate with him daily after the isha prayer** (p. 158, folio 154). **The chunk's one
  quantified commensality figure**; the book does not call it a langar, does not say who provisioned
  it, and does not say at which of his residences it was kept.
- **A teaching post at the shrine town.** "گلبرگہ میں آپ تفسیر و حدیث کا درس دیا کرتے تھے" — he used to
  **teach tafsir and hadith at گلبرگہ** (p. 158, folio 154). No madrasa is named, no students counted
  and no stipend mentioned.
- **A library given away on a pir's order, in two stages, as the condition of attainment.** Shaikh
  Fath Allah Lawaudhi complained to his pir that many مجاہدے had brought him nothing; the pir told him
  "شغل درس و تدریس چھوڑ دو اور ساری کتابیں طلباء میں تقسیم کردو" — **give up the occupation of
  teaching and distribute all your books among the students**; "آپ نے چند خاص کتابیں رکھ لیں اور باقی
  کتابیں تقسیم کر دیں", he kept a few special books and distributed the rest, "لیکن پھر بھی کوئی فائدہ
  نہ ہوا، جب ان کتابوں کو بھی علیحدہ کر دیا تو آپ درجہ کمال کو پہنچے" — and still gained nothing until
  he parted with those too, whereupon he reached the degree of perfection (p. 151, folio 147). **A
  teaching livelihood and a book collection renounced as an institutional act**, and the only
  property-renunciation in the chunk.
- **A forced capital migration, dated to the day, carrying a shrine family with it.** "سلطان بن محمد
  تغلق[OCR?] نے بجائے دہلی کے دولت آباد کو دارالخلافت قرار دے کر دہلی کے باشندوں کو حکم دیا کہ وہ دولت
  آباد جا کر آباد ہوں" — the sultan made **دولت آباد** the dar al-khilafat in place of **دہلی** and
  ordered Delhi's inhabitants to go and settle there; "آپ کے والد ماجد 2 رمضان 728ھ کو دہلی سے سکونت
  ترک کرکے مع اہل و عیال دولت آباد روانہ ہوئے اور 17 محرم 729ھ کو دولت آباد پہنچ کر وہاں سکونت پذیر
  ہوئے" — his father left Delhi with his household on **2 Ramzan 728 AH** and reached Daulatabad on
  **17 Muharram 729 AH**, settling there (p. 154, folio 150). **A state order, a four-and-a-half-month
  journey, and the only instance in the chunk of state policy moving a saintly household.**
- **A family quarrel with a provincial governor, and a return migration.** After the father's death
  ("5، شوال 731ھ", p. 154, folio 150) the mother remained some years at **خلد آباد**, then "اپنے بھائی
  ملک الامراء سید ابراہیم مستوفی سے جو دولت آباد کے گورنر ہو گئے تھے، کسی اختلاف کے باعث خلد آباد سے
  سکونت ترک کرکے… 4، رجب 736ھ کو دہلی آئیں اور وہیں رہنے لگیں" — **because of some disagreement** with
  her brother, who had become **governor of دولت آباد**, she left Khuldabad with her two sons and came
  to **دہلی** on **4 Rajab 736 AH** (p. 154, folio 150). **A saintly family's movement driven by a
  dispute with the provincial administration** — the nearest thing to a shrine-state conflict in the
  chunk.
- **A pir's prayer that kings would carry his palanquin.** "نصیر الدین چراغ دہلوی نے آپ کے حق میں دعا
  فرمائی کہ بادشاہ ان کی پالکی اٹھائیں" — Nasir al-Din Chiragh Dehlvi prayed that **kings should
  carry his پالکی** (p. 155, folio 151, حاشیہ 3). The book records the prayer and does not say it was
  fulfilled. It pairs with the palanquin of the وجہ تسمیہ (p. 154, folio 150), where the future saint
  is himself the bearer.
- **Personal service to the pir as an assigned duty.** "اپنے پیرو مرشد کو آپ ہی وضو کراتے تھے" — it was
  he who assisted his pir's **وضو** (p. 158, folio 154). Compare the graduated devotional load the pir
  imposed: "انہوں نے مجاہدات و ریاضات کا بوجھ آپ پر ایک دم نہیں ڈالا، بلکہ بتدریج اضافہ فرماتے رہے" —
  he did not lay the burden of mujahadat on him at once but increased it **by degrees** (p. 157,
  folio 153).
- **The graduated devotional regime itself, quoted as a dialogue and countable.** Over several
  exchanges the pir adds: two rakats of **اشراق** on the dawn wudu (p. 157, folio 153); two rakats
  "شکر النہا[OCR?]" and **استخارہ** (p. 157, folio 153); four rakats of **چاشت** to be joined to the
  ishraq rather than prayed later (p. 158, folio 154); **twenty-one more days of fasting** added to
  Rajab and Shaban so that "پورے تین مہینے کے روزے ہو جایا کریں گے", the full three months' fasts are
  made up (p. 158, folio 154); and, in place of **صوم داؤدی**, **صوم دوام** — "ہمارے خواجگان صوم
  داؤدی نہیں رکھا کرتے تھے، بلکہ صوم دوام رکھتے تھے، اس کے بعد تم بھی صوم دوام رکھا کرو", **our
  khwajagan did not keep the Davidic fast but the perpetual fast; you keep the perpetual fast too**
  (p. 158, folio 154). His own practice: the five prayers in congregation, regular fasts (p. 156,
  folio 152), waking at midnight for **تہجد** and then اذکار و اشغال (p. 158, folio 154), and the
  "شش عید" fasts after Ramzan (p. 158, folio 154).
- **سماع as an institution and a graded practice.** Fath Allah Lawaudhi "کو سماع کا بہت شوق تھا"
  (p. 151, folio 147); Gesu Daraz likewise "آپ کو سماع کا بہت شوق تھا" (p. 158, folio 154) and
  divides **سماع** into three kinds: ecstasy on merely hearing the qawwal's verse without attending
  to its sense or tune; the same after listening and reflecting; and hearing it "یاروں کی موافقت کے
  سبب", for fellowship's sake — in which case "اس کو بھی کچھ نہ کچھ حصہ ملے گا", he too gets some
  portion, "جیسے شراب خانے میں کوئی شخص جائے تو اگرچہ وہ شراب نہ پئے، مگر بو ہی سونگھے گا" — as one
  who enters the wine-house will at least smell it (p. 163, folio 159, حاشیہ 11). **A named
  ritual role (قوال) and an explicit doctrine of partial benefit for the bystander — directly
  citable on sama at shrines.**
- **Prescribed اوراد و وظائف, with counts.** From the unnamed saint: "حاجت پوری ہونے کے واسطے: سورہ
  اخلاص ہزار بار یا سو مرتبہ پڑھیں" — for a need to be fulfilled, recite **Surah Ikhlas a thousand
  times or a hundred**; and "ہر مقصد کے پورا ہونے کے واسطے: سورہ یٰسین جس کام کے لئے پڑھے' وہ کام پورا
  ہو" — Surah Yasin for any purpose (p. 149, folio 145, حاشیہ 8). From Gesu Daraz: a four-rakat prayer
  on **Friday night**, with "ایک سو ایک بار" (101 times) after the Fatiha in the first, third and
  fourth rakats and "ایک سو بار" (100) in the second, each with its own named verse, and "رب انی مغلوب
  فانتصر" a hundred times after the salam (p. 164, folio 160). **Countable devotional quotas of the
  kind shrine practice runs on.**
- **Four named اذکار with a prescribed posture and stroke.** "ذکر فنا و بقا": the **ضرب** is struck
  first on the "دہن قلب" and then with the head bowed toward the qibla, or to the right, or to the
  left (pp. 164–165, folios 160–161, حاشیہ 12); "تمام اذکار کی بیٹھک یہ ہے کہ دونوں گھٹنے زمین پر
  رکھے ہوں اور دونوں ہاتھوں سے گھٹنوں کو پکڑے رہے" — the seat for **all** azkar is on both knees with
  both hands gripping them, contemplating one of "لا معبود الا اللّٰہ یا لا موجود الا اللّٰہ یا لا
  مطلوب الا اللّٰہ یا لا مشہود الا اللّٰہ", and "ان میں سے جس کا تصور کرے گا، اسی کے موافق اس پر کشف
  پر [as printed] گا", the kashf will come to him according to whichever he contemplates (p. 165,
  folio 161). **"ذکر کشف قرآن"** requires **four copies of the Quran** placed in front, to the right,
  to the left and in the lap, with the ضرب struck on them in a set order, "اس ذکر کی تاثیر سے کماحقہ،
  تجلی قرآن اس پر ہو گی" (p. 165, folio 161, حاشیہ 13). **"ذکر کشف ارواح"**: twenty-one "یا رب", then
  facing the sky with "یا روح" and "یا روح الروح" and a stroke on the heart, by which "ہر ایک روح کا
  حال منکشف ہو جاتا ہے" (p. 165, folio 161, حاشیہ 14). **"ذکر اجابت دعوت"**: "یا قوی" to the right,
  "یا رقیب" to the left, "یا محیط" to the heart and "یا مجیب" upward, then rising on the knees and
  spreading the hands to the sky (p. 165, folio 161, حاشیہ 15). **The densest run of prescribed
  technique in the chunk, and it is where the chunk breaks off.**
- **اعتکاف in three forms, one of them internal.** "اعتکاف کی تین قسمیں ہیں، ایک اعتکاف معین، دوسرا
  دوام، تیسرا اعتکاف دل۔ یعنی اہل دل اپنے خانہ دل کے اندر اعتکاف کرتے ہیں" — **اعتکاف معین**, **دوام**
  and **اعتکاف دل**, the people of the heart making retreat inside the house of their own heart
  (p. 160, folio 156). A retreat doctrine that requires no building.
- **Rules on when a darwesh may take a loan.** "قرض ہرگز نہ لے، مگر جب بہت ہی ضرورت ہو، جس کی تین
  قسمیں ہیں۔ ایک تو اپنی ذاتی ضرورت جس سے مجبور ہو جائے، دوسری ضرورت مہمان کی خاطر، تیسری صلہ رحم کی
  ضرورت۔ اپنی بھوک پیاس رفع کرنے کے واسطے بھی قرض لے سکتا ہے" — **never take a loan (قرض)** except in
  great need, of which there are three kinds: one's own compelling personal need, need **for the sake
  of a guest**, and the need of **صلہ رحم** (kindness to kin); he may also borrow to relieve his own
  hunger and thirst (p. 163, folio 159). **The chunk's one rule touching the economy of hospitality —
  a khanqah's guest-keeping expressly made a legitimate ground for debt.**
- **Sixteen conditions of ارادت, several of them material.** Among them: the second, that the seeker
  "اپنے دل سے گھر بار اور مال و اسباب اور جو رو بچوں کا تعلق منقطع کر لے" — **sever from his heart the
  tie to home, property and goods, and to wife and children** (p. 160, folio 156); the fifth, to adopt
  **خلوت** and solitude (p. 161, folio 157); the sixth, to keep apart from women and, if married, not
  to go to his wife except in dire need (p. 161, folio 157); the seventh, "اکل حلال کا انتظام کرے" —
  to **arrange lawful food** — eating only enough to keep the body's business going, with "طے[OCR?]
  کا روزہ بہت بہتر ہے" and little water (p. 162, folio 158); the eighth, prompt obedience to the pir
  (p. 162, folio 158); the twelfth, not to boast of "آباؤ اجداد اور علم و عقل", ancestry, learning and
  intellect (p. 162, folio 158); the fifteenth, "اپنے واسطے کوئی خاص لباس و ہیئت اختیار نہ کرے" —
  **adopt no special dress or appearance** (p. 162, folio 158). **The clothing and ancestry clauses cut
  directly against hereditary shrine display, and are worth reading beside the sajjada nashini
  above.**
- **The hours at which prayer is accepted, catalogued as a dispute.** Under "وقت مقبول" the book lists
  at least nine positions on when دعا is granted — dawn, between the sunna and fard of fajr, after the
  fard until sunrise, چاشت, زوال, after asr until sunset, from maghrib to isha, midnight, and the last
  part of the night — and adds, in brackets, "(مرید صادق اور طالب راسخ ان تمام اوقات کو ذکر، شغل،
  مراقبہ، تلاوت یا نوافل میں صرف کرے)", that the sincere murid should spend all of them so; and "شب قدر
  کی تلاش میں جو لوگ سرگردان رہتے ہیں، یہ نہیں جانتے کہ ہر شبانہ روز میں وہ وقت موجود ہے" — those who
  wander in search of **شب قدر** do not know that the hour is present in every day and night
  (pp. 159–160, folios 155–156, حاشیہ 7). **A named devotional calendar collapsed into the daily
  round.**
- **خدمت خلق ranked above supererogatory prayer.** "بعض لوگوں کا یہ خیال ہے کہ نوافل پڑھنا خدمت خلق سے
  بہتر ہے ان کا یہ خیال غلط ہے… خدمت خلق نوافل پڑھنے سے بہتر ہے" — some think praying nawafil better
  than **service to God's creatures**; their view is wrong, and service to creatures is better than
  nawafil (p. 149, folio 145, حاشیہ 7). **The chunk's one explicit ranking of social service over
  private devotion — the most citable line in it for the welfare side of this project.**

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Four sons foretold at محمد پور.** Under the heading "کشف و کرامات" the author reports that on
  reaching محمد پور he gave مولوی سید خاں "یہ مژدہ… کہ ان کے چار لڑکے ہوں گے' جو علوم فضل و کمال سے
  آراستہ ہوں گے' ایسا ہی ہوا" — the good news that he would have four sons adorned with learning and
  excellence, **and so it happened** (pp. 149–150, folios 145–146).
- **A mock funeral that became a real one.** The author reports, on the authority of معارج الولایت,
  that at ظفر آباد some people, to mock him, shrouded **a living man**, laid him on a bed and asked him
  to lead the "نمازہ [as printed] جنازہ"; he refused at first, then stood; "تکبیر پر جیسا کہ طے ہوا تھا
  کہ وہ شخص اٹھ بیٹھے گا' وہ نہیں اٹھا' تو لوگوں نے پاس جا کر دیکھا' اس شخص کو مردہ پایا" — at the
  takbir the man did not sit up as arranged, and on going close the people **found him dead** (p. 150,
  folio 146, حاشیہ 9).
- **A qalandar struck dead at a challenge.** The author reports that a qalandar named علی, with many
  qalandars in company, asked "جہانگیری کہاں سے پائی؟"; told it came from his pir, he demanded proof;
  "یہ سن کر آپ کو غصہ آیا' اور وہ قلندر جس کا نام علی تھا' زمین پر گرا اور مر گیا" — at this the saint
  grew angry and the qalandar fell to the ground and died (p. 150, folio 146).
- **A mazar that drives off possession, with an alternative said of it.** The author states that the
  mazar is famous "آج بھی", to this day, for removing **آسیب**, and adds "یہ بھی کہا جاتا [as
  printed]" — it is also said — that reciting the saint's name and blowing does the same (p. 150,
  folio 146). Recorded here as the book presents it: the first clause asserted, the second reported.
- **Locks caught in a palanquin, and a couplet in reward.** The author relates, on the authority of
  حاشیہ 2, that his hair caught in the palanquin's leg and he neither freed it nor halted the
  palanquin, and that Chiragh Dehlvi on hearing of it was much pleased and "اسی وقت یہ شعر پڑھا",
  recited the couplet on the spot (p. 154, folio 150). **The book presents this under "وجہ تسمیہ", as
  the origin of a name, not under کرامات.**
- **A prayer for royal service.** The book states without comment that the pir "آپ کے حق میں دعا
  فرمائی کہ بادشاہ ان کی پالکی اٹھائیں" — prayed that kings would carry his palanquin (p. 155,
  folio 151, حاشیہ 3). No fulfilment is claimed.
- **A pir's attention granted at fifteen.** He himself relates: "ایک دفعہ میں شیخ اسلام شیخ نصیر الدین
  رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کی خدمت میں حاضر ہوا، انہوں نے فرمایا۔ تم جب آتے ہو، شام کے وقت آتے ہو، اور میں اس
  وقت ملول ہوتا ہوں، البتہ میرا جی چاہتا ہے کہ تم سے کوئی بات کروں"; "اس وقت میری عمر صرف پندرہ سال
  تھی", he was then only **fifteen**, and answered "سبحان اللّٰہ!… زہے دولت!" (p. 157, folio 153). **The
  one place where the book lets the subject narrate in the first person**, and it is a report of favour,
  not of a miracle.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The compiler works from a named short canon and prints his sources page by page.** The حواشی block
  for the unnamed entry lists nine items: "لطائف اشرفی فی بیان طوائف صوفی (فارسی) ص 990", "اخبار
  الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 446", "تذکرۃ العابدین ص 262", "لطائف اشرفی' حصہ اول' اردو ترجمہ' ص 27", the
  same at "ص 17" and "ص 21", "لطائف اشرفی ص 84", "لطائف اشرفی بیان طوائف صوفی" (**without a page**),
  and "معارج الولایت" (**without a page**) — seven of the nine from one work in three different forms
  of its title (p. 150, folio 146). باب 24 rests on "سال السا کلین[OCR?] (جلد دوم) ص 224" and then
  "ایضاً”" (p. 152, folio 148). **For باب 25 the chunk carries fifteen footnote markers and no حواشی
  block at all** — see Doubts.
- **Titles are explained by narrative, not asserted.** "گیسو دراز" is given a "وجہ تسمیہ" heading and
  derived from the palanquin episode (p. 154, folio 150), exactly as the compiler treats other laqabs;
  where a title has no story — "صدر الدین", "ولی الاکبر", "الصادق" — it is simply listed under
  "القاب" (p. 153, folio 149).
- **The entries run on a fixed grid of Urdu sub-headings, applied very unevenly.** باب 25 uses some
  twenty-five headings (خاندانی حالات، والد ماجد، والدہ ماجدہ، بھائی، ولادت مبارک، نام نامی، کنیت،
  القاب، وجہ تسمیہ، دولت آباد میں آمد، بچپن کا صدمہ، واپسی، تعلیم و تربیت، بیعت و خلافت، پیرو مرشد کی
  دعا، ترک سکونت، سلاطین سے تعلقات، شادی اور اولاد، وفات شریف، خلفاء، سیرت پاک، علمی ذوق، شعر و شاعری،
  تعلیمات، اقوال، اوراد و وظائف) across thirteen pages, while باب 24 uses six across one (p. 151,
  folio 147). **The absence of a مزار or urs heading in باب 24 is the book's silence, not a gap in the
  transcription.**
- **The compiler prints a doctrinal self-contradiction without resolving it.** Under "حبس دم" the
  teaching is that beyond the listed precautions the salik should "جوگیوں کی ہر ایک بات سے پرہیز رکھے"
  — **keep away from every single practice of the jogis** — and the very next sentence names the three
  things as "حبس دم نشست مخصوص اور ظفر تکیہ[OCR?]" and says "یہ تینوں چیزیں سالک کے واسطے بہت ضروی [as
  printed] ہیں", **these three things are very necessary for the salik** (pp. 162–163, folios
  158–159). See Doubts: as transcribed the passage both forbids and prescribes the same three items.
- **Shariat, tariqat and haqiqat are argued as one substance, by simile.** "یہ عقیدہ نہ رکھو کہ شریعت،
  طریقت اور حقیقت ایک دوسرے سے جدا ہیں۔ دیکھو بادام کے اندر تین چیزیں ہیں۔ پوست، مغز اور روغن… پوست کا
  خلاصہ مغز ہے اور مغز کا خلاصہ روغن، اسی طرح شریعت کا خلاصہ طریقت اور طریقت کا خلاصہ حقیقت ہے" — do not
  hold that they are separate: as the **almond** has husk, kernel and oil, each the essence of the
  last, so tariqat is the essence of shariat and haqiqat of tariqat (p. 162, folio 158, حاشیہ 9).
- **Two kinds of murshid, and the disciple's risk stated plainly.** "مرشد دو قسم کے ہوتے ہیں، ایک ہادی
  اور ایک منذر[OCR?]۔ ان دونوں میں کوئی تمیز بہت مشکل ہے" — a **ہادی** (guide) and a **منذر [OCR?]**
  (warner), and telling them apart is very difficult, since both admonish; and of the poor man who
  cannot tell them apart, "اگر مرشد ہادی ہوئے، قمود المرا[OCR?] اور اگر منذر ہوئے تب ان کو اس جہاں کی
  خبر نہ ہوگی" (p. 160, folio 156 — the consequence clause is OCR-flagged and cannot be glossed; see
  Doubts). **A rare admission in a hagiography that a seeker may attach himself to the wrong pir.**
- **Two kinds of seeker, with the rational one ranked lower.** "ایک طالب وہ شخص جو اپنی عقل و فہم سے
  خدا کی طلب اختیار کرے… یہ شخص حکمت کی راہ سے طالب ہوا ہے، عاشق نہیں ہے۔ عاشق کے اندر جو طلب ہوتی ہے،
  وہ خدا کی طرف سے اس میں ڈالی جاتی ہے" — the man who seeks God by his own reason is a seeker by the
  road of wisdom, **not a lover**; the lover's seeking is cast into him from God's side (p. 162,
  folio 158, حاشیہ 8).

## Citable passages

- "آج بھی آپ کا مزار آسیب کو دور کرنے کے لئے مشہور ہے۔" — "To this day his mazar is famous for driving
  off possession." (p. 150, folio 146)
- "مزار فیض آثار گلبرگہ میں قبلہ حاجات خلائق ہے۔" — "The grace-bearing mazar at Gulbarga is the qibla
  of the creatures' needs." (p. 156, folio 152)
- "اس کی عقیدت و محبت کا ثبوت آپ کے مزار مبارک پر عالی شان گنبد ہے۔" — "The proof of his devotion is
  the magnificent dome over the mazar." (p. 155, folio 151)
- "خدمت خلق نوافل پڑھنے سے بہتر ہے۔" — "Service to God's creatures is better than praying nawafil."
  (p. 149, folio 145)
- "شغل درس و تدریس چھوڑ دو اور ساری کتابیں طلباء میں تقسیم کردو۔" — a pir's order: "Give up teaching
  and distribute all your books among the students." (p. 151, folio 147)

## Doubts

- **No archive row is engaged by this chunk, and nothing here should be mapped.** The three subjects are
  placed at محمد پور / ظفر آباد (town of the mazar **not stated**), at دہلی, and at
  دہلی → دولت آباد → خلد آباد → گوالیار → چندیری → بڑودہ → کھمبائت → خلد آباد → **گلبرگہ**. **The
  archive has no row at Gulbarga or anywhere in the Deccan**, and under the mapping rule a saint the
  book places in India must not be attached to a same-named Pakistani row. Two names in the chunk could
  tempt a mapper and must not be followed: **"مولانا شرف الدین کیتھلی[OCR?]"**, a teacher of Gesu Daraz
  at Delhi (p. 155, folio 151), shares the کیتھلی nisba with the known trap شاہ کمال کیتھلی and has
  **nothing** to do with `shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal`; and **"حضرت شیخ فتح اللہ لواودھی"** (p. 151,
  folio 147) shares only the element "فتح" with `wadpagga-sharif`'s "Ghazi Syed Shah Fateh Muhammad
  Bukhari" of Peshawar — a different name, a different place, and not a match.
- **The chunk opens mid-entry and its subject is never named.** Pages 148–150 (folios 144–146) carry
  a سیرت, a ghazal, four teaching sub-headings, four اقوال, two اوراد and four karamat, then the
  حواشی block, **with no name, no nisba and no chapter heading**; باب 24's heading appears only on
  p. 151 (folio 147). The chunk's internal evidence about him is: the takhallus **"اشرف"** (p. 148,
  folio 144); a named work **"بشارت المریدین"** (p. 148, folio 144); seven of nine footnotes to
  **لطائف اشرفی** in its several title-forms (p. 150, folio 146); and the qalandar's question about his
  **"جہانگیری"** (p. 150, folio 146). **Do not name him from this, and do not attribute anything on
  pp. 148–150 to any saint until the previous chunk is read.** In particular the آسیب-cure practice and
  the four karamat are at present unattached to any person or place.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** "ذکر اجابت دعوت" breaks off at "دائیں اور بائیں طرف اور دل پر یا رب
  کہے اور آسمان کی طرف مونہہ کرکے کہے۔ یا ربی" (p. 165, folio 161), with no closing quotation mark and
  no further text. The rest of باب 25 falls outside this chunk.
- **باب 25 has fifteen footnote markers and no حواشی block in this chunk.** Markers 1 (birth, p. 153,
  folio 149), 2 (the couplet, p. 154, folio 150), 3 (the pir's prayer, p. 155, folio 151), 4 (death,
  p. 156, folio 152), 5 (سیرت, p. 156, folio 152), 6 ("الاسما", p. 158, folio 154), 7 (وقت مقبول,
  p. 159, folio 155), 8 (طالبوں کی قسمیں, p. 162, folio 158), 9 (شریعت/طریقت/حقیقت, p. 162, folio 158),
  10 (حبس دم, p. 162, folio 158), 11 (سماع, p. 163, folio 159), 12 (ذکر فنا و بقا, p. 164, folio 160),
  13, 14 and 15 (the three azkar, p. 165, folio 161) all appear in the text, **but the footnote block
  they point to does not.** **No statement in باب 25 can be attributed to a named earlier work from
  this chunk** — including the death date, the Gulbarga mazar and the dome.
- **Markers 1–3 of the unnamed entry's حواشی fall before this chunk.** The block lists nine items
  (p. 150, folio 146) but only markers 4–9 appear in the transcribed text, so "لطائف اشرفی فی بیان
  طوائف صوفی (فارسی) ص 990", "اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 446" and "تذکرۃ العابدین ص 262" cannot be
  attached to any statement here.
- **A doctrinal passage that both forbids and prescribes the same three practices.** Under حبس دم:
  "جوگیوں کی ہر ایک بات سے پرہیز رکھے اور وہ تیوں چیزیں یہ ہیں۔ حبس دم نشست مخصوص اور ظفر تکیہ[OCR?]۔
  یہ تینوں چیزیں سالک کے واسطے بہت ضروی [as printed] ہیں" (pp. 162–163, folios 158–159). As transcribed
  the sentence tells the salik to avoid every jogi practice, names the three, and then calls them very
  necessary. "ظفر تکیہ" is OCR-flagged and "ضروی" is printed for ضروری without a flag. **Do not quote
  this passage as doctrine without re-reading the page from the PDF**; the negation may have been lost
  in print or in transcription.
- **A son's nisba printed two ways on one page.** The second son is "حضرت محمد یوسف عرف سید محمد اصغر
  حسنی[OCR?]" under شادی اور اولاد and in the khalifa list, but "حضرت محمد یوسف عرف سید محمد اصغر
  حسینی" in the sajjada-nashini sentence, **all on p. 156 (folio 152)**. The flagged form is the one
  that appears twice. Record both; prefer neither.
- **The khalifa list's last names run together.** "…شیخ صدر الدین، خونذیر[OCR?] (ایرچہ[OCR?]) سید ابو
  المعالی بن سید احمد سید جمال الدین شیخ ابو الفتح بن مولانا علاؤ الدین گوالیاری" (p. 156, folio 152)
  carries no separators after "شیخ صدر الدین"; whether "سید جمال الدین" is a separate khalifa or part
  of the preceding patronymic **cannot be settled from the transcription**, so the list's length is
  uncertain. "خونذیر[OCR?] (ایرچہ[OCR?])" is doubly flagged and may be a name, a place or both.
- **Khuldabad and Daulatabad are used without explanation.** The father moves the household to
  **دولت آباد** (p. 154, folio 150) but the mother is then said to have stayed after his death in
  **خلد آباد**, and the education section also places the boy's first exoteric studies at **خلد آباد**
  (pp. 154–155, folios 150–151). The book never says the two names refer to one place or to two. Record
  each as printed.
- **Two Rajab 736 AH dates twelve days apart.** The family "4، رجب 736ھ کو دہلی آئیں" (p. 154,
  folio 150) and the bai'at is "16، رجب 736ھ" (p. 155, folio 151). Both are printed and neither is
  flagged; they are consistent but tight, and the second is the only date the book gives for the
  bai'at.
- **The printed age at death agrees with the printed years, and is the book's own figure.** Birth
  "4 رجب 720ھ" (p. 153, folio 149), death "16 ذیقعدہ 825ھ" and "بوقت وفات آپ کی عمر ایک سو پانچ سال
  کی تھی" (p. 156, folio 152). **The book states the age; it has not been computed here, and no CE
  equivalent is given by the book for any of its dates.** Note also that the same number, one hundred
  and five, is given for the count of his books (p. 158, folio 154) — a coincidence in the print, not
  an inference.
- **"سلطان بن محمد تغلق[OCR?]" is OCR-flagged** (p. 154, folio 150) and is the only form in which the
  ruler who moved the capital is named. Do not normalise it.
- **باب 24's only source is a garbled title.** "سال السا کلین[OCR?] (جلد دوم) ص 224", followed by
  "ایضاً”" with a stray closing quotation mark (p. 152, folio 148). **Both the death year 821ھ and the
  pir's name rest on this one flagged citation.** Do not cite the source title from this chunk as it
  stands.
- **The pir's name in باب 24 is flagged at its distinguishing element**: "حضرت شیخ صدر الدین طبیب
  دلہا[OCR?]" (p. 151, folio 147). The nisba or epithet after "طبیب" is unreliable, and it is the only
  thing distinguishing this شیخ صدر الدین from the several others the book names (Gesu Daraz's own laqab
  "صدر الدین", p. 153, folio 149; a khalifa "شیخ صدر الدین", p. 156, folio 152).
- **An OCR-flagged consequence clause inside a doctrinal statement.** "اگر مرشد ہادی ہوئے، قمود
  المرا[OCR?] اور اگر منذر ہوئے تب ان کو اس جہاں کی خبر نہ ہوگی" (p. 160, folio 156): what happens if
  the murshid turns out to be a ہادی is lost in the flagged phrase, and that is the point of the
  sentence. **Do not gloss the two-murshid teaching as a whole.**
- **A Quranic quotation in the Friday wazifa is flagged mid-verse**: "فی سبحینا[OCR?] و نجیناہ من الغم
  و کذالک منتجی المومنین" (p. 164, folio 160). The verse text as printed should not be reproduced in an
  entry; quote the rakat-counts instead.
- **The two ghazals and the verse specimens should not be quoted as texts.** The unnamed saint's ghazal

  [OCR?] روئے تو"، "گوبٹو [OCR?]", "آتش عشق از بسوخت خرمن ماگو بسوز [OCR?]" — and its refrain is
  printed variously as "گومباش" throughout. Gesu Daraz's specimen verses (p. 159, folio 155) carry
  "بنود[OCR?]", "غیورم[OCR?]", "ٹھہر[OCR?]", "ندارو[OCR?]".
- **Unflagged printing oddities, recorded as printed.** "خلفائے راشیدین" for راشدین (p. 148,
  folio 144); "نمازہ جنازہ" (p. 150, folio 146); "یہ بھی کہا جاتا" without ہے (p. 150, folio 146);
