# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 019 — pp. 360–379

**The tail of an unnamed entry, then chapters 67 and 68 complete, the divider "حصہ نہم" (Part Nine),
and the opening of chapter 69.** The chunk runs: the closing lists of اشغال and the کرامات of a saint
whose heading falls outside the chunk (pp. 360–361, folios 356–357) with its twelve-item حواشی block
(p. 362, folio 358); باب 67 حضرت خواجہ محمد سلیمان (pp. 363–368, folios 359–364); باب 68 حضرت سید غوث
علی شاہ (pp. 369–375, folios 365–371); the divider "حصہ نہم" with a Persian couplet (p. 377,
folio 373); and باب 69 حضرت مولانا شاہ فضل رحمٰن, breaking off after the "وصیت" heading (pp. 378–379,
folios 374–375).

**One archive row is engaged, and it is engaged squarely** — Khwaja Muhammad Sulaiman of Taunsa, for
whom the book states both a mazar town and a death date. Everything else is stationed by the book at
**بریلی (Bareilly), دہلی (Delhi), پانی پت (Panipat), گنج مراد آباد (Ganj Murad Abad), قصبہ ملانواں
(Malanwan) or لکھنؤ (Lucknow)** — Indian, and not to be mapped. Three Pakistani place-names appear but
carry no shrine statement: **کوٹ مٹھن**, **اچ** and **مہار** (see Doubts).

**The chunk's institutional yield is unusually good.** It contains the book's most explicit passage on
**shrine finance** — a merchant's doubt that langar rests on "دنیا داروں کے روپے", answered by a
precedent about Nizam al-Din Auliya and Sultan Ghiyath al-Din and by the hujra niche as a treasury
(pp. 366–367, folios 362–363) — a **دارالعلوم** founded at Taunsa with a stated purpose and
curriculum, a grandson's sajjada nashini, a faqir's flat prohibition on soliciting, a colonial-office
patronage story with a Hindu sarishtadar as devotee, and three dated وظائف with recitation counts.

**Folio relation**: folio = PDF index − 4 on every page that prints a marker (p. 360 → folio 356
through p. 379 → folio 375). Page 376 is marked `[blank page]` and prints no marker, so **folio 372
never appears**; the offset of 4 is unbroken across it (p. 377 → folio 373).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a** (حضرت خواجہ محمد سلیمان):
  - **The book's name for him carries no nisba and no "شاہ"**: the chapter heading reads "باب 67 /
    حضرت خواجہ محمد سلیمان رحمۃ اللہ علیہ", and under "نام" simply "آپ کا نام محمد سلیمان ہے" (p. 363,
    folio 359). The honorific line is "حضرت خواجہ محمد سلیمان مقتدائے ارباب یقین ہیں" — the exemplar
    of the people of certainty (p. 363, folio 359).
  - **Mazar at Taunsa, stated in the book's standard formula.** "آپ کا مزار پر انوار تونسہ میں فیوض و
    برکات کا سرچشمہ ہے" — his luminous mazar at **تونسہ** is a fountainhead of effusions and blessings
    (p. 365, folio 361). **This is the statement the mapping rests on**, together with the residence
    narrative below; it agrees with the row's location "Taunsa, Taunsa Sharif, Punjab".
  - **Death date as printed: "آپ 7 صفر 1267ھ کو جوار رحمت میں داخل ہوئے"** — he entered the vicinity of
    mercy on 7 Safar 1267 AH (p. 365, folio 361, حاشیہ 4 = انوار العارفین (فارسی) ص 457). The archive
    row's `figure_died` is "1850"; the two values are recorded side by side under Doubts and **no
    conversion is made here**.
  - **Lineage: Afghan, Ja'fariya tribe.** "آپ کے پردادا عمر خاں اور آپ کے دادا عبدالوہاب افغانی جعفریہ
    قبیلہ سے تھے" — his great-grandfather was **عمر خاں** and his grandfather **عبدالوہاب افغانی**, of
    the **جعفریہ قبیلہ** (p. 363, folio 359). Father: "آپ کے والد ماجد کا نام زکریا خان ہے" — **زکریا
    خان** (p. 363, folio 359, حاشیہ 1 = انوار العارفین (فارسی) ص 456).
  - **Birth: "آپ نے گڑ گوجی[OCR?] میں 1184ھ میں اس عالم کو زینت بخشی"** — he adorned this world in
    **گڑ گوجی [OCR?]** in **1184ھ** (p. 363, folio 359). **The birthplace is OCR-flagged**; see Doubts.
  - **Orphaned at four**: "چار سال کی عمر میں آپ کے والد کا سایہ آپ کے سر سے اٹھ گیا" — at the age of
    four his father's shade was lifted from his head (p. 363, folio 359).
  - **Schooling, named teacher by teacher.** Raised and taught under his mother's supervision; fifteen
    paras of the Quran from **ملا یوسف جعفر** and the remaining fifteen from **میاں حسن علی**,
    completing the Quran; then Attar's "پند نامہ" and Sa'di's "گلستان" and "بوستان" and other books
    from the same (p. 363, folio 359). Then to **لانگھ [OCR?]**, where he read further Persian
    literature with **مولوی ولی محمد**; from Langh to **کوٹ مٹھن**, entering the **مدرسہ** of **قاضی
    محمد عاقل**, where in a short time he finished the Arabic textbooks and completed "تحصیل علوم
    ظاہری" (p. 363, folio 359).
  - **Bai'at at Uch, aged sixteen.** "آپ کا کوٹ مٹھن میں قیام تھا تو حضرت خواجہ نور محمد مہاروی رحمۃ
    اللہ علیہ اچ تشریف لائے… آپ اچ جا کر ان کی خدمت میں حاضر ہوئے" — while he was resident at Kot
    Mithan, **خواجہ نور محمد مہاروی** came to **اچ**; he went to Uch and presented himself, and was
    honoured with bai'at, "اس وقت آپ کی عمر سولہ سال کی تھی" — he was then sixteen (p. 364, folio 360,
    حاشیہ 2 = انوار العارفین (فارسی) ص 456).
  - **He commuted to مہار, sometimes on foot**: "آپ اپنے پیرو مرشد کے پاس مہار آتے جاتے رہے' کبھی کبھی
    ایسا بھی ہوا کہ آپ پیدل ہی مہار روانہ ہو گئے" (p. 364, folio 360).
  - **What his pir taught him, named**: "ریاضت و مجاہدات ذکر جہر اور پاس انفاس کی تعلیم فرمائی" —
    riyazat and mujahidat, **ذکر جہر** (vocal zikr) and **پاس انفاس** (watch over the breaths); and
    books of tasawwuf, of which "آداب الطالبین' عشرہ کاملہ" and "فصوص الحکم" are named (p. 364,
    folio 360, حاشیہ 3 = انوار العارفین (فارسی) ص 456).
  - **Khilafat at twenty-two, by the Prophet's permission.** "آپ کے پیرو مرشد نے سرور عالم صلی اللہ علیہ
    وسلم کی اجازت سے آپ کو خرقہ خلافت عطا فرمایا' اس وقت آپ کی عمر بائیس سال کی تھی" (p. 364,
    folio 360).
  - **Forty days of i'tikaf at a Delhi mazar.** After the passing of **حضرت مولانا فخر الدین فخر جہاں**
    he reached **دہلی** and "چالیس روز تک ان کے مزار مبارک پر معتکف رہے' چہلم کی فاتحہ کے بعد مہار
    واپس آئے" — remained forty days in i'tikaf at his blessed mazar and returned to Mahar after the
    **چہلم کی فاتحہ** (p. 364, folio 360).
  - **Taunsa was his pir's assignment, not his choice.** "آپ کے پیر روشن ضمیر نے آپ کو تونسہ میں رہنے
    کا حکم دیا" — his pir ordered him to live at Taunsa (p. 364, folio 360); "اپنے پیرو مرشد کے مطابق
    آپ نے تونسہ میں سکونت اختیار کی۔ وہاں ایک جھونپڑی ڈال کر رہنے لگے" — he took up residence there
    **in a جھونپڑی (hut)** (p. 364, folio 360). See Practices for the town's growth and the دارالعلوم.
  - **Both sons predeceased him**: "آپ کے دونوں صاحب زادے' خواجہ گل محمد اور خواجہ درویش محمد آپ کی
    حیات میں وفات پا گئے" — **خواجہ گل محمد** and **خواجہ درویش محمد** died in his lifetime (p. 365,
    folio 361). **No wife is named** — see Doubts on the heading.
  - **Succession passed to a grandson.** "آپ کی وفات کے بعد آپ کے پوتے حضرت اللہ بخش آپ کے سجادہ نشین
    ہوئے" — after his death his grandson **حضرت اللہ بخش** became his **سجادہ نشین**; "آپ کے خلفاء بہت
    ہیں", his khalifas are many, of whom the famous are "شیخ محمد یار' حافظ محمد علی خبر آیادی[OCR?]'
    مولوی محمد علی' میاں عبداللہ شاہ" (p. 365, folio 361).
  - **Character and possessions**: passionate love of his pir, obedience and service reckoned a source
    of felicity; unequalled in "ترک و تجرید"; "سوائے ایک لنگی کے آپ کے پاس اور کچھ نہ تھا" — he had
    nothing but a **لنگی**, and a **چٹائی** was spread in the hujra on which he sat to worship (p. 365,
    folio 361).
  - **A sama' practitioner.** "آپ اہل سماع و صاحب شوق و ذوق تھے" (p. 365, folio 361, حاشیہ 5 = انوار
    العارفین (فارسی) ص 457); "بعض اوقات حالت رقص و وجد میں اشک بار ہوتے تھے" — at times he shed tears
    in a state of **رقص و وجد** (p. 365, folio 361, حاشیہ 6 = مناقب محجوبین[OCR?]).
  - **A scholar as well as a saint**: "قرآن' حدیث اور فقہ میں دست گاہ حاصل تھی" — command of Quran,
    hadith and fiqh (p. 365, folio 361).
  - **Teachings and aqwal.** His life passed in worship, riyazat, guidance and instruction; he insisted
    that the purpose of human life is the worship of God and often recited "وما خلقت الجن والانس الا
    لیعبدون" (pp. 365–366, folios 361–362). He held following the Prophet indispensable for both
    worldly and religious advancement; held a good life impossible without acquiring good qualities;
    enjoined avoidance of "جھوٹ' غیبت' بے ایمانی' لالچ' حسد' غرور اور تکبر"; condemned love of the
    world and the company of "دنیا داروں", "جہاں تک ممکن ہو سکے' دنیا داروں کی صحبت سے بچنا چاہیے"; and
    commended humility, "اپنے کو ادنٰی اور سب سے بدتر سمجھنا چاہیے" (p. 366, folio 362). Aqwal: none can
    be accepted at God's court without following the shariat outwardly and inwardly; Muslims should keep
    away from "غیر مشروع امور"; "صفائی قلب اور روحانی ترقی کے لئے اتباع شریعت از بس ضروری ہے" (p. 366,
    folio 362).
  - **Silsila: (not stated).** The chunk attaches **no order-name** to him; it names only his pir
    (خواجہ نور محمد مہاروی) and the techniques he was taught (p. 364, folio 360). The row's
    "Chishti-Nizami" is neither confirmed nor contradicted. The "اشغال چشتیہ" list on p. 360
    (folio 356) belongs to the **preceding, unnamed entry** and must not be read onto him.
  - **Nothing on his urs, offerings, endowment, land or buildings other than the دارالعلوم** (p. 364,
    folio 360) — no urs date, no visitor figure, no Auqaf or custodianship arrangement beyond the
    grandson's sajjada nashini.

  during the Bareilly fire, and his سائس (grooms) (pp. 360–361, folios 356–357).
- **عطا محمد**, a wealthy but childless devotee of Ghaus Ali Shah (p. 374, folio 370); an unnamed **نائب
  تحصیل دار** of Panipat (p. 374, folio 370); **پنڈت شو مدت[OCR?]**, سرشتہ دار to the Commissioner at
  Delhi (pp. 374–375, folios 370–371).
- **Places named**: **بریلی** and **محلہ قطب** (p. 360, folio 356), **گڑ گوجی[OCR?]** and **لانگھ[OCR?]**
  and **کوٹ مٹھن** (p. 363, folio 359), **اچ**, **مہار**, **دہلی** and **تونسہ** (pp. 364–365, folios
  360–361), **موضع استہاون[OCR?]** (p. 369, folio 365), **مکہ معظمہ' مدینہ منورہ' بغداد' نجف اشرف' بیت
  المقدس' مصر' روم و شام** and **پانی پت** (p. 370, folio 366), **لکھنؤ**, **قصبہ ملانواں** and **گنج
  مراد آباد** (pp. 378–379, folios 374–375).
- **حصہ نہم (Part Nine) opens with a Persian couplet and nothing else** (p. 377, folio 373): "دست مرد
  گیر تا مردے شوی / جز بمرداں نیست راہے رہبر".

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A shrine's income doubted, and the doubt answered with a precedent rather than an argument — the
  chunk's most important passage for this project.** A **سوداگر** (merchant) was a devotee of Khwaja
  Muhammad Sulaiman: "وہ ہندوستان آتا' مال فروخت کرتا اور پھر واپس چلا جاتا۔ جب واپس ہوتا تو آپ کی خدمت
  با برکت میں حاضر ہوتا' نذرانہ پیش کرتا' دعا کا طالب ہوتا اور چلا جاتا" — he came to Hindustan, sold
  his goods and went back, and on returning presented himself, **offered a نذرانہ**, sought a prayer and
  left (p. 366, folio 362). Once, seeing much "فتوحات" come in before him, "اس کو یہ دیکھ کر خیال آیا کہ
  یہ سب لنگر اور داد و دہش دنیا داروں کے روپے سے ہے' اگر سامنے نذرانے نہیں آئیں گے تو یہ سب کام ختم ہو
  جائے گا" — he thought that all this **langar and داد و دہش (giving and largesse) runs on worldly
  people's rupees, and that if the offerings stopped coming the whole operation would end** (pp. 366–367,
  folios 362–363). The reply is a narrated precedent: when **سلطان غیاث الدین** had the same suspicion
  about Nizam al-Din Auliya's langar and largesse, "اس نے ممانعت کر دی تھی کہ اس کے دربار کا کوئی آدمی
  حضرت نظام الدین اولیاء رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کی خدمت میں نہ جائے اور نہ کوئی نذرانہ وغیرہ پیش کرے" — he
  **forbade anyone of his court to attend on Nizam al-Din or present any offering**; learning of it,
  Nizam al-Din "دونا خرچ کرنے کی تاکید فرمائی' خادم کو ہدایت فرمائی کہ حجرہ کے طاق میں سے ضرورت کے
  موافق لے لیا کرے" — **ordered spending to be doubled** and instructed his servant **to take from the
  niche (طاق) of the hujra as need required** (p. 367, folio 363). **A state boycott of shrine offerings,
  a deliberate counter-response of doubled expenditure, and a hujra niche functioning as the treasury,
  all in five lines.**
- **A دارالعلوم founded at Taunsa, with its purpose and its teacher stated.** "آپ نے تونسہ میں ایک
  دارالعلوم قائم کیا۔ علوم شریعت سے بہت لوگ ناواقف تھے۔ اس دارالعلوم کا مقصد علوم شریعت کی تعلیم تھا۔
  آپ بھی اس دارالعلوم میں خاص خاص طلباء کو سلوک و احسان کی کتب کا درس دیتے تھے' رفتہ رفتہ یہ دارالعلوم
  بڑھتا گیا اور ایک اچھا خاصا اسلامی علوم کا مرکز بن گیا" — he **founded a darul uloom at Taunsa**
  because many people were ignorant of the shariat sciences; its purpose was the teaching of those
  sciences; **he himself taught books of سلوک و احسان to select students**; and it grew by degrees into
  a considerable centre of Islamic learning (p. 364, folio 360). **No endowment, no funding source, no
  student numbers and no building date are given** — (not stated).
- **A shrine town's growth attributed to the saint's settlement there.** "وہاں ایک جھونپڑی ڈال کر رہنے
  لگے۔ عبادت' ریاضت اور مجاہدہ میں مشغول رہتے تھے۔ رفتہ رفتہ تونسہ کو ترقی ملی" — he put up a **hut**
  and lived there, occupied in worship and austerity, and **"gradually Taunsa came to prosper"**
  (pp. 364–365, folios 360–361). The chunk's only statement connecting a settlement's development to a
  shrine.
- **Sajjada nashini at Taunsa is filial but skips a generation.** Both sons died in his lifetime
  (p. 365, folio 361), and "آپ کی وفات کے بعد آپ کے پوتے حضرت اللہ بخش آپ کے سجادہ نشین ہوئے" — his
  **grandson اللہ بخش** became sajjada nashin, alongside "بہت" khalifas of whom four are named (p. 365,
  folio 361). The term "سجادہ نشین" is the book's own.
- **Sajjada nashini at Panipat passes to a khalifa, not a son.** "آپ کے وصال کے بعد آپ کے خلیفہ محمد
  یوسف آپ کے سجادہ نشین ہوئے" — after Ghaus Ali Shah's passing his khalifa **محمد یوسف** became sajjada
  nashin; the prominent khalifas are "محمد یوسف' مولوی گل حسن" (p. 370, folio 366). **The two
  successions in this chunk are of opposite types and stand a few pages apart.**
- **A saint living in a cell of another saint's shrine.** "آپ نے زندگی کے آخری ایام پانی پت میں گذرائے۔
  قلندر صاحب کے مزار کے ایک حجرے میں رہتے تھے" — Ghaus Ali Shah spent his last days at Panipat and
  **lived in one of the hujras of the mazar of قلندر صاحب** (p. 370, folio 366). A shrine precinct
  housing a living shaykh, whose own mazar is then also at Panipat (p. 370, folio 366).
- **Multiple simultaneous initiations, one per silsila, each with its own pir.** "آپ کئی سلسلوں میں
  بیعت ہیں۔ آپ کئی سلسلے کے بزرگوں سے خرقہ خلافت پا کر سرفراز ہوئے" — he holds bai'at in several
  silsilas and received the **خرقہ خلافت** from the elders of several (p. 369, folio 365). First from his
  own father; benefit from "حضرت لعل شاہ"'s spiritual effusions; "خاندان سہروردیہ میں آپ مرید و خلیفہ
  حضرت سید فدا حسین رسول شاہی کے ہیں" (حاشیہ 3); "خاندان قادریہ میں… حضرت سید اعظم علی شاہ" (حاشیہ 4);
  "نقش بندی سلسلہ میں… حضرت حبیب اللہ شاہ" (حاشیہ 5); "خاندان چشتیہ میں… حضرت امیر الدین" (حاشیہ 6)
  (pp. 369–370, folios 365–366). **Four orders, four named pirs, four separate footnotes.**
- **A flat prohibition on soliciting, argued in terms of the dignity of faqr.** Under "غیر اللہ سے بے
  پروائی" Ghaus Ali Shah instructs the seeker: "غیر اللہ سے کبھی ملتجی نہ ہونا۔ کسی حاجت کے واسطے سوال
  نہ کرنا۔ کیونکہ سوال کرنا اصول طریقت کے بالکل خلاف ہے… سوال کرنا فقر کی شان عظمت کے منافی ہے' اور سبب
  تذلیل ہے۔ یہ رکیک فعل اعزاز فقراء کے پاک و شفاف دامن پر بد نما دھبہ لگاتا ہے" — never petition any but
  God, ask for nothing for any need, because asking is **wholly against the principles of tariqat**,
  contrary to the majesty of faqr and a cause of humiliation, and this base act leaves an ugly stain on
  the pure and spotless skirt of the honour of the fuqara (p. 371, folio 367, حاشیہ 9 = سیرت غوثیہ
  ص 27،28،31). **The clearest normative statement in the chunk about what a faqir may and may not take.**
- **Immediate redistribution as a stated practice.** "آپ کی سخاوت کا یہ حال تھا کہ جو کچھ آتا' اسی وقت
  محتاجوں' بیواؤں اور مسکینوں کو تقسیم کر دیتے تھے۔ سائل آپ کے پاس سے کبھی خالی ہاتھ نہیں گیا" —
  whatever came in was distributed **that very moment** to the needy, **widows** and the poor, and no
  petitioner ever left him empty-handed (p. 370, folio 366). Named beneficiary categories, no sums.
- **A named, timed daily office at Taunsa.** "آپ اپنے معمولات کے سختی سے پابند تھے۔ کسی حالت میں بھی
  اپنے معمولات میں فرق نہیں آنے دیتے تھے" — he was strict in his **معمولات** and never let them vary:
  after tahajjud, **ذکر جہر**; after fajr, a short rest in the hujra; then out to "رشد و ہدایت"
  (guidance); **قیلولہ** after the midday meal, always; after zuhr, recitation of the Quran; then asr;
  after maghrib, "ذکر جہر میں ایک پہر گذارتے" — **one پہر spent in vocal zikr**; the night meal, isha
  with the congregation, then rest in the hujra (p. 365, folio 361).
- **Three وظائف with exact counts, prescribed by Ghaus Ali Shah — a priced-free but strictly
  quantified ritual economy** (p. 373, folio 369, حواشی 12–14 = سیرت غوثیہ ص 64،65 / ص 66،27[OCR?] /
  ص 66): **for childlessness**, after fajr 21 durood, then "ایک سو اکیس (121) بار رب لا تذر نی فردا و
  انت خیر الوارثین", then 21 durood, breathe on oneself, breathe on water, drink it and give it to the
  wife; **for fever**, after fajr 7 durood, then 151 ("ایک سو اکیاون") times "قلنا یا نار کونی بردا و
  سلاما علٰی ابراہیم", breathe on oneself and on water or medicine and drink; **for deliverance from
  calamity**, after isha 11 durood, then 51 ("اکاون") times "لا الہ الا انت سبحانک انی کنت من الظلمین",
  then 11 durood and a prayer.
- **A conditional benefaction with a secrecy clause and a stated daily rate.** A man in "عسرت اور تنگی
  معاش" was given "ایک چٹکی خاص[OCR?]" to keep at his pillow with 21 daily recitations of a Persian
  couplet after fajr; "اس کو پانچ روپے روزانہ سرہانے سے ملنے لگے" — **five rupees a day** began to come
  from under his pillow; "کچھ دنوں کے بعد اس شخص نے آپ کی ہدایت کے خلاف اس بات کا ذکر لوگوں سے کیا' اسی
  روز سے روپے ملنا بند ہو گئے" — he mentioned it to people against instruction and **from that day the
  rupees stopped** (pp. 373–374, folios 369–370, حاشیہ 15 = سیرت غوثیہ ص 52،53). The only cash rate
  named in the chunk.
- **Colonial office as the medium of shrine patronage.** **پنڈت شو مدت[OCR?]**, "دہلی میں کمشنر کے
  سرشتہ دار", fell out with the Commissioner, who "ان کو برخاست کرنے کی دھمکی دی" — threatened
  dismissal; "پنڈت جی کو آپ سے بہت عقیدت تھی' وہ دہلی سے پانی پت آئے" — being much devoted to the
  saint he came from Delhi to Panipat and **presented a مرتبان (jar) filled with مربہ** (p. 374,
  folio 370). The outcome is given in the language of the service: the Commissioner had made "پر زور
  سفارش" for his appointment as **ایکٹرا اسٹنٹ کمشنر**, and "کچھ ہی دنوں کے بعد پنڈت جی ایکٹرا اسٹنٹ
  کمشنر ہو گئے" (p. 375, folio 371, حاشیہ 18). A second official, "ایک شخص جو پانی پت میں نائب تحصیل
  دار تھا", came about an examination and hoped "اگر مستقل ہو جائے" — that his post be made permanent
  (p. 374, folio 370, حاشیہ 17). **A Hindu government clerk and a Muslim revenue officer as devotees,
  with offerings and career outcomes named: directly usable for the economy of nineteenth-century
  shrine patronage.**
- **A third-day fatiha at a khanqah, and a bai'at dated by it.** A stranger sent ahead to the khanqah
  "جب خانقاہ میں آیا تو دیکھا کہ آپ کی… سوم کی فاتحہ ہو رہی ہے" — found **the سوم کی فاتحہ (third-day
  fatiha) in progress** (p. 361, folio 357, حاشیہ 12 = کرامات نظامیہ ص 61،62). The one funerary rite
  named for this saint; his urs is (not stated).
- **A ولایتی with his own khanqah.** In the illness-transfer story, "جب ولایتی اپنی خانقاہ میں آیا تو وہ
  بھی دو ایک روز میں اچھا ہو گیا" — when the ولایتی came **to his own khanqah** he too recovered in a
  day or two (p. 360, folio 356, حاشیہ 10 = کرامات نظامیہ ص 37،38).
- **Service assigned as the form of attachment to a shrine household.** The revived boy "آپ کی خدمت میں
  رہنے لگا' آپ نے پانی بھرنے کی خدمت اس کے سپرد فرمائی" — stayed in Khwaja Muhammad Sulaiman's service
  and was **entrusted with the service of drawing water** (p. 367, folio 363). A named menial office
  inside the establishment, as with the langar office in earlier chunks.
- **Urban fabric in a fire.** At Bareilly "بہت سے مکان جل کر خاک ہو گئے" — many houses burnt to ashes;
  horses were "چھپر میں بندھے ہوئے" — tied in a **thatched shed**, and a رسالدار's grooms were trying
  to get them out (pp. 360–361, folios 356–357).
- **Forty-day i'tikaf at a mazar and a چہلم فاتحہ as a completed devotional programme** (p. 364,
  folio 360) — see the Sulaiman bullet above. The book treats a mazar as a place of residence for forty
  days, then a return home.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **An illness transferred to a substitute body, then worn off in a khanqah.** Under the heading
  "کرامات" the author relates that at چودھری بسنت رائے's house, seeing a relative sick with "استقاء"
  (dropsy), the saint ordered a **ولایتی** in the man's company to lie on the ground beside the
  patient's bed; "اس ولایتی کا پیٹ اور جسم پھولنے لگا او[as printed] اس مریض کو افاقہ ہونا شروع ہوا" —
  the ولایتی's belly and body began to swell and the patient began to mend; the patient was healed and
  "اس نے پیٹ بھر کے کھانا کھایا", ate his fill, while the ولایتی's disease grew worse, and he in turn
  recovered on reaching his own khanqah (p. 360, folio 356, حاشیہ 10 = کرامات نظامیہ ص 37،38).
- **A fire at Bareilly turned by a glance, and the saint's own explanation of it.** The author reports
  that, roused at midday and coming out "بغیر کرتہ پہنے" (without putting on a shirt), "آپ نے نظر اٹھائی'
  آگ کا رخ بدل گیا۔ چھپر کا ایک کونہ جلنے پایا تھا کہ وہ بھی خود بخود بجھ گیا" — he raised his eyes, the
  fire changed direction, and the one corner of the shed that had caught went out of itself; asked next
  day by his son what he had recited and blown, he answered "پڑھنا کیا تھا' سمندر بن کر ایک چھینٹا مارا'
  فوراً بجھ گئی" (pp. 360–361, folios 356–357, حاشیہ 11 = کرامات نظامیہ ص 61،62). The رسالدار is quoted
  telling his grooms to stop trying: "اگر بچانا ہو گا تو حضرت خود بچا لیں گے' ورنہ حضرت کے سامنے ان کو
  جلنے دو" (p. 361, folio 357).
- **A bai'at conferred after the pir's death.** The author relates that a traveller met a man who, on
  hearing his errand, said "جس شخص کے پاس تم جاتے ہو' وہ نیاز احمد میں ہی ہوں"; the traveller took
  bai'at on the spot, was told to go on to the khanqah, and there found the third-day fatiha in
  progress: "اب اس شخص کو معلوم ہوا کہ آپ نے اس کو وصال کے بعد بیعت کیا" — he then learned that the
  saint had initiated him **after his own passing** (p. 361, folio 357, حاشیہ 12 = کرامات نظامیہ
  ص 61،62).
- **A doubt about shrine finances read by kashf, and a river of money under the prayer-mat.** Under
  "کشف و کرامات" the author relates that Khwaja Muhammad Sulaiman "اس سوداگر کے خطرے سے بذریعہ کشف آگاہ
  ہوئے" — became aware of the merchant's misgiving by kashf (p. 367, folio 363); sent to fetch the
  **مصلا** from the hujra, the merchant lifted it and "اس کو یہ دیکھ کر حیرت ہوئی کہ اس کے نیچے ایک
  دریا ہے اور اس کی تین دھاریں ہیں' ایک دھار اشرفی کی' دوسری دھار روپیوں کی اور تیسری دھار جواہرات کی" —
  found beneath it a river with **three currents, one of ashrafis, one of rupees, one of jewels**; the
  musalla fell from his hand and he came out and begged pardon (p. 367, folio 363).
- **A dead boy's pulse restored.** The author reports that an old woman came "چیختی چلاتی اور روتی
  ہوئی" saying her only son was dead; a servant urged the saint to go, suggesting "ممکن ہے کہ اس کو
  سکتہ ہو گیا ہو" (it may be a catalepsy); the saint found the boy dead and no pulse anywhere, and on
  the servant's request looked closely: "آپ کی توجہ قلبی سے اس کی نبض میں حرکت ہوئی' دوبارہ دیکھنے پر
  نبض اچھی طرح چلنے لگی' وہ لڑکا اٹھ کر بیٹھ گیا" — by his **توجہ قلبی** the pulse moved, on a second
  look ran properly, and the boy sat up; "اس واقعہ کے بہت دنوں بعد تک وہ لڑکا بقید حیات رہا"
  (pp. 367–368, folios 363–364).
- **Five rupees a day from under a pillow, ending when disclosed.** The author reports the pinch of
  "خاص[OCR?]", the prescribed couplet "اے کریمے کہ از خزانہ غیب گبر و ترسا وظیفہ خور داری / دوستاں را
  کجا کنی محروم تو کہ با دشمناں نظر داری", the daily five rupees, and their cessation on the day the man
  spoke of it (pp. 373–374, folios 369–370, حاشیہ 15 = سیرت غوثیہ ص 52،53).
- **A son granted to عطا محمد in old age.** The author reports that Ghaus Ali Shah said "خدا نے چاہا تو
  بیٹا پیدا ہو گا" and "آپ کی دعا سے عطا محمد کا گھر روشن ہوا"; the child was brought, the saint laid
  one hand on the boy's head and the other on the father's and recited "پیرے کہ دم زعشق زند بس غنیمت
  است / از شاخ کہنہ میوہ نورس غنیمت است" (p. 374, folio 370, حاشیہ 16 = سیرت غوثیہ ص 52،53).
- **An examination result foretold in a proverb.** The author reports that to the naib tehsildar the
  saint smiled and said "ایسی خوش خبری اول باپ دیا کرتا ہے" — such good news is first given by a father
  — and the man on reaching home found his father's letter conveying exactly that news (p. 374,
  folio 370, حاشیہ 17 = سیرت غوثیہ ص 53،55،56).
- **A promotion announced before it was known, with a pun on the gift.** The author reports that on
  being handed the jar of مربہ the saint said "بہت خوب' 'مربی بیارو مربہ بخور'", then "کھو صاحب[OCR?]'
  اب تو کمشنر صاحب تم پر بہت مہربان ہیں" and "میاں! تم کو تو اس نے ڈپٹی کر دیا ہے۔ تم پریشان نہ ہو۔ خدا
  فضل کرے گا' وہ بھی مہربان ہو گا"; the Pandit afterwards learned of the Commissioner's strong
  recommendation (pp. 374–375, folios 370–371, حاشیہ 18 = سیرت غوثیہ ص 53،55،56).
- **A birth and a destiny foretold.** The author reports that Shah Abd al-Rahman Lakhnawi gave Shah
  Fazl Rahman's father the news of the birth before it occurred, and at the boy's sixth year said on
  first sight of him "یہ لڑکا ہندوستان کا قطب ہو گا" — this boy will be the **qutb of Hindustan** — and
  set his own lip to the child's lip (p. 378, folio 374).
- **A child's scruple over an ear of grain, printed under "آثار بزرگی".** The author relates that at
  eight, when his father plucked a **خوشہ** from a field for the caged bird, he stopped where he was and
  said "جب تک کھیت کا مالک نہ آئے گا اور معاف نہ کراؤں گا نہ آؤں گا" — I will not come until the
  field's owner comes and I obtain his pardon — whereupon his father threw the ear away (p. 379,
  folio 375). Reported as a **sign of sanctity**, not as a miracle.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Every karamat in this chunk is footnoted to a named earlier work, and the works change with the
  chapter.** The unnamed Bareilly saint's karamat rest entirely on **"کرامات نظامیہ"**, twelve citations
  in one block (p. 362, folio 358); باب 67 rests on **"انوار العارفین (فارسی)"** for lineage, bai'at,
  books, death and sama', with one item from **"مناقب محجوبین[OCR?]"** (p. 368, folio 364); باب 68 rests
  entirely on **"سیرت غوثیہ"**, eighteen citations (p. 375, folio 371). **The compiler is working
  one-source-per-entry and says so**; nothing is presented as his own inquiry. Notably, **the karamat of
  باب 67 (the merchant, the revived boy) carry no footnote markers at all** — see Doubts.
- **A doubt about shrine finance is answered by precedent, not by principle.** Asked implicitly whether
  langar funded by "دنیا داروں کے روپے" is legitimate, the book has the saint recite the Ghiyath
  al-Din/Nizam al-Din episode and then stage the miracle of the river under the musalla (pp. 366–367,
  folios 362–363). **The argument is that the money does not come from where the donor thinks it does**
  — the narrative form carries the claim, and the book offers no discussion of it.
- **The entries run on the book's fixed grid of Urdu sub-headings**, applied unevenly: باب 67 uses
  خاندانی حالات, والد ماجد, ولادت مبارک, نام, بچپن کا صدمہ, تعلیم و تربیت, بیعت و خلافت, دہلی میں آمد,
  پیرو مرشد کی ہدایت, تونسہ میں سکونت, شادی اور اولاد, وفات, خلفاء, سیرت, معمولات, تعلیمات, اقوال, کشف و
  کرامات (pp. 363–368, folios 359–364); باب 68 adds doctrinal headings the rest of the book does not use
  — **اسلام کی زندگی, غیر اللہ سے بے پروائی, توحید, سلوک, اورادو وظائف** (pp. 371–373, folios 367–369);
  باب 69 adds narrative ones — **آپ کی ولادت کی خبر, پیشین گوئی, بچپن کا ایک واقعہ, آثار بزرگی, وصیت**
  (pp. 378–379, folios 374–375). **Absence of a heading is the book's silence, not a transcription gap.**
- **باب 68 is the one entry in the chunk the compiler recommends to a general readership.** "آپ کی
  تعلیمات اہل ذوق و شوق اہل تصوف اور اہل عرفان ہی کے لئے مفید نہیں ہیں' بلکہ ہر شخص آپ کی تعلیمات سے
  خاطر خواہ فائدہ اٹھا سکتا ہے" — his teachings are useful not only to the people of taste, tasawwuf and
  gnosis, but anyone can profit from them (p. 371, folio 367). One of the compiler's few evaluative
  statements.
- **A three-tier scheme of توحید is attributed to Ghaus Ali Shah in his own voice** (pp. 371–372, folios
  367–368, حاشیہ 10 = سیرت غوثیہ ص 37): "توحید عامہ' جس کو توحید شریعت کہتے ہیں"; "توحید خاص و توحید
  طریقت"; "خاص الخاص توحید معرفت و حقیقت۔ توحید عالی بھی اس کا نام ہے", with the first shared by "جمہور
  خلائق" and resting on obedience to the shariat and the other two obtained "از روئے فیوضات باطنی" and
  reserved to prophets and awliya. **The book presents this as quotation, marked with quotation marks,
  not as its own doctrine.**
- **Interfaith contact is recorded without comment.** Ghaus Ali Shah's meetings with "جوگیوں'
  سناسیوں اور دیگر مذاہب کی بزرگ ہستیوں" are listed among his spiritual benefits — "ان سے استفادہ کیا",
  he drew benefit from them — with no defence and no qualification (p. 370, folio 366); the Hindu
  devotees چودھری بسنت رائے (p. 360, folio 356) and پنڈت شو مدت[OCR?] (pp. 374–375, folios 370–371) are
  likewise narrated without remark.

## Citable passages

- "آپ کا مزار پر انوار تونسہ میں فیوض و برکات کا سرچشمہ ہے۔" — "His luminous mazar at Taunsa is a
  fountainhead of effusions and blessings." (p. 365, folio 361)
- "یہ سب لنگر اور داد و دہش دنیا داروں کے روپے سے ہے۔" — the merchant's misgiving: "All this langar and
  largesse is from worldly people's rupees." (p. 366, folio 362)
- "غیر اللہ سے کبھی ملتجی نہ ہونا۔ کسی حاجت کے واسطے سوال نہ کرنا۔" — "Never petition any but God. Ask
  for nothing for any need." (p. 371, folio 367)
- "جس شخص کے پاس تم جاتے ہو' وہ نیاز احمد میں ہی ہوں۔" — "The person you are going to — I am that Niaz
  Ahmad." (p. 361, folio 357)
- "پڑھنا کیا تھا' سمندر بن کر ایک چھینٹا مارا' فوراً بجھ گئی۔" — of the fire: "What was there to recite?
  I became an ocean and threw one splash; it went out at once." (p. 361, folio 357)

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry, and the saint is named only inside his own karamat.** Pages 360–361
  (folios 356–357) carry three lists of اشغال and three miracle stories with **no chapter heading and no
  name block**; the name reaches the reader only through a quoted sentence, "وہ نیاز احمد میں ہی ہوں",
  and a parenthetical gloss, "(حضرت شاہ نیاز رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کی)" (p. 361, folio 357). His **chapter
  number, his lineage, his death date, his silsila and his mazar all fall outside this chunk**; the book
  states only that the fire and the khanqah are at **بریلی** and **محلہ قطب** (p. 360, folio 356). Do
  not attribute anything on p. 360 to a named saint until the previous chunk is read.
- **The name "شاہ نیاز / نیاز احمد" resembles a name inside an archive row's silsila field, and this is
  a resemblance only.** The `wasif-ali-wasif` row records bai'at to "Hazrat Shah Niyaz Ahmad Chisti
  Qadri". **That row is a Lahore shrine of a different figure** (Wasif Ali Wasif, Miani Sahib graveyard,
  d. 18 January 1993), the chunk says nothing whatever about Wasif Ali Wasif, and it places this saint's
  khanqah at **بریلی** in India (p. 360, folio 356). **No mapping is made**, and none should be made from
  the name; a human may wish to note the cross-reference for the row's silsila field only.
- **The Taunsa map: one conflict of stated values, recorded on both sides.** The book gives
  "7 صفر 1267ھ" (p. 365, folio 361); the row `darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a`
  gives `figure_died` "1850". **No conversion is performed here.** The book's name for him is "خواجہ
  محمد سلیمان" with **no "شاہ" and no "تونسوی"**, both of which the row's title carries (p. 363,
  folio 359). **The book states no silsila for him**, so the row's "Chishti-Nizami" is neither confirmed
  nor contradicted; do not read the "اشغال چشتیہ" list of p. 360 (folio 356) onto him, as it belongs to
  the preceding, unnamed entry. The mapping itself rests on the mazar statement "مزار… تونسہ میں"
  (p. 365, folio 361) plus the residence narrative and the دارالعلوم (p. 364, folio 360), which together
  satisfy the location test.
- **His birthplace, the one datum that would corroborate the row independently, is OCR-flagged.**
  "گڑ گوجی[OCR?]" (p. 363, folio 359) cannot be used for placement; the Taunsa identification therefore
  rests on the mazar and residence statements alone.
- **کوٹ مٹھن is named but not mapped.** The book places قاضی محمد عاقل's **مدرسہ** at **کوٹ مٹھن** and
  Khwaja Muhammad Sulaiman's studies there (p. 363, folio 359). This matches the location of
  **`mithankot-kot-mithan`** ("Mithankot (Kot Mithan), Rajanpur, Punjab"), **but the chunk says nothing
  about that shrine, nothing about Khwaja Ghulam Farid, and nothing about a mazar, urs or custodian at
  Kot Mithan** — only a town and a madrasa. Under the mapping rule this is not a match.
- **اچ is named but not mapped, and could not be resolved to one row in any case.** The bai'at takes
  place at **اچ** because Khwaja Nur Muhammad Maharvi came there (p. 364, folio 360). The archive has
  five Uch rows (`shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari`,
  `shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht`, `tomb-of-baha-al-halim-uch-sharif`, `tomb-of-javindi-bibi`,
  `tomb-of-ustad-nuriya`); the chunk mentions **none** of their figures, shrines or dates. Leave unmapped;
  never pick.
- **مہار has no archive row and the chunk gives it no shrine.** It is named only as the seat of the pir
  خواجہ نور محمد مہاروی, to and from which the disciple travelled (p. 364, folio 360). خواجہ نور محمد
  مہاروی himself is given **no mazar, no death year and no silsila** in this chunk — a candidate figure
  for the archive, not a match to it.
- **A heading whose content does not match it.** Under "شادی اور اولاد" (marriage and children) the text
  says only "آپ کے دونوں صاحب زادے' خواجہ گل محمد اور خواجہ درویش محمد آپ کی حیات میں وفات پا گئے"
  (p. 365, folio 361) — **no marriage and no wife is mentioned at all**, and the two sons appear only as
  predeceased. The same pattern as chunk 004's "والدہ کا وصال". Do not infer a marriage or a wife's name
  from this heading.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** باب 69 breaks off after the "وصیت" paragraph (p. 379, folio 375). Its
  **حواشی block is outside the chunk**, although footnote markers **1** (after the hadith sanad) and **2**
  (after the second marriage) appear in the text; the rest of Shah Fazl Rahman's entry — death, mazar,
  khalifas, karamat — is not here. **Nothing in this chunk is the end of his entry**, and no death year
  or mazar should be attributed to him from it.
- **A حواشی block nine items longer than its anchors.** The block on p. 362 (folio 358) lists **twelve**
  items, all "کرامات نظامیہ", but only markers **10, 11 and 12** appear in the transcribed text
  (pp. 360–361, folios 356–357); markers 1–9 anchor to pages before this chunk. Do not attach حواشی 1–9
  to anything recorded here.
- **Two karamat with no footnote anchors at all.** باب 67's merchant story and revived-boy story
  (pp. 366–368, folios 362–364) carry **no markers**, while its six حواشی are exhausted by the lineage,
  bai'at, books, death date and sama' statements. Attribute both stories to the book itself, not to
  انوار العارفین, until the pages are re-read.
- **One حاشیہ without a page number, and one with a suspect page pair.** حاشیہ 6 of باب 67 reads
  "مناقب محجوبین[OCR?]" with **no page reference**, unlike the other five in its block (p. 368,
  folio 364). حاشیہ 13 of باب 68 reads "سیرت غوثیہ ص 66،27[OCR?]" — the second figure is OCR-flagged and
  runs backwards against حاشیہ 12 (ص 64،65) and حاشیہ 14 (ص 66) (p. 375, folio 371).
- **Chapter numbering is unbroken here** — باب 67, باب 68, باب 69 in sequence (pp. 363, 369, 378;
  folios 359, 365, 374) — but **the part divider falls between two chapters, not at a chapter
  boundary of its own**: "حصہ نہم" appears on p. 377 (folio 373) between باب 68 and باب 69, with only a
  Persian couplet on the page.
- **Internal arithmetic on Ghaus Ali Shah's dates, for the record.** Birth "1219ھ" (p. 369, folio 365)
  and death "26 ربیع الاول 1297ھ" (p. 370, folio 366) are seventy-eight lunar years apart, and the
  printed age at death reads "اٹھتہر[OCR?] سال" (p. 370, folio 366) — but **the age word is
  OCR-flagged**, so the agreement is between a flagged word and a computation, not between two printed
  figures. For باب 67: birth "1184ھ" (p. 363, folio 359) and death "7 صفر 1267ھ" (p. 365, folio 361) are
  eighty-three lunar years apart; **the book states no age**. The internal ages it does state — father's
  death at four, bai'at at sixteen, khilafat at twenty-two (pp. 363–364, folios 359–360) — are relative
  and cannot be checked against the years.
- **"آپ نواسی[OCR?] بزرگوں سے ملے"** (p. 370, folio 366): the number of elders met is a flagged word.
  Do not give a figure.
- **The "دیگر اشغال" list is the densest OCR risk in the chunk and should not be quoted as a list**
  (p. 360, folio 356): fourteen of its eighteen items are flagged — "انہد[OCR?]", "شغل ہنسہ[OCR?]",
  "الک وند[OCR?]", "شغل انگلا پگلا[OCR?]", "شغل سکھمنا[OCR?]", "چورانی آسن[OCR?]", "شغل ادہی ہی[OCR?]",
  "اوام پہوا[OCR?]", "ذکر کاوتری[OCR?]", "ذکر کاوتی[OCR?]", "ذکر بشمب[OCR?]", "شغل ترکٹی[OCR?]" plus
  "شغل نصیرا[OCR?]" and "شغل وید[OCR?]" in the preceding numbered series, and "ذکر کاوتری" / "ذکر کاوتی"
  and "ذکر چہارم برم" / "ذکر برم" stand as near-duplicate pairs in the same list.
- **All other `[OCR?]` flags carried over, by page.** p. 363 (folio 359): birthplace "گڑ گوجی[OCR?]",
  place "لانگھ[OCR?]" — note the same place is printed **unflagged** as "لانگھ" two lines later in the
  same paragraph. p. 365 (folio 361): the khalifa "حافظ محمد علی خبر آیادی[OCR?]", whose nisba is lost.
  p. 368 (folio 364): "مناقب محجوبین[OCR?]". p. 369 (folio 365): birthplace "موضع استہاون[OCR?]".
  p. 370 (folio 366): "نواسی[OCR?]", "اٹھتہر[OCR?]". p. 373 (folio 369): "ایک چٹکی خاص[OCR?]" — **what
  was given is lost**, and that is the object at the centre of the story. p. 374 (folio 370): "پنڈت شو
  مدت[OCR?]" (the Pandit's name), "کھو صاحب[OCR?]" (a form of address inside a quotation). p. 375
  (folio 371): "ص 66،27[OCR?]". p. 378 (folio 374): "حضرت شیخ محمد المعروف بہ صباح العاشقین [OCR?]
  چشتی", **the ancestor's own by-name**, on which the Malanwan mazar statement hangs.
- **One transcriber-level oddity that is not flagged**: "اس ولایتی کا پیٹ اور جسم پھولنے لگا او اس مریض
  کو افاقہ ہونا شروع ہوا" (p. 360, folio 356) prints "او" where "اور" is expected; recorded as printed.
- **No Urdu gloss conflicts to report.** The Persian couplets (pp. 373–374, 377; folios 369–370, 373)
  and the Arabic phrases in the وظائف (p. 373, folio 369) are printed **without Urdu glosses**, so the
  chunk offers no gloss-versus-original disagreement of the kind earlier chunks found. The Arabic is
  recorded exactly as printed, including "انی کنت من الظلمین" (p. 373, folio 369) and "وما خلقت الجن
  والانس الا لیعبدون" (p. 366, folio 362); neither is OCR-flagged.
