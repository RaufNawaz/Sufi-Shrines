# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 020 — pp. 380–392

**The end of the book.** The chunk runs: the tail of an **unnamed** entry, opening at its "وفات" heading
(pp. 380–381, folios 376–377); **باب 70 حضرت حاجی وارث علی شاہ**, complete from its opening line to its
closing حواشی (pp. 382–388, folios 378–384); a blank page (p. 389); and **four pages of back matter**
(pp. 390–392) — a tiled publisher's endpaper carrying an imprint line and a telephone number, the same
endpaper without the imprint, and a back-cover **publisher's sales catalogue** of eighteen titles.
**باب 70 is the last chapter: the compendium runs to seventy باب and stops.**

**Almost nothing here is in the archive.** Both saints are placed by the book in **India**: the unnamed
saint's mazar at **گنج مراد آباد (یو پی)** (p. 380, folio 376), Haji Waris Ali Shah's at **دیوا**, district
**بارہ بنکی (یو پی)** (pp. 382, 383; folios 378, 379). **One archive id is engaged, and only through an
advertisement line** — see the single bullet below and the Doubts note attached to it. Two of the traps
already recorded for this book are **positively confirmed** here, with the book's own locations and dates
against them: حاجی وارث علی شاہ is not `mausoleum-of-waris-shah`, and خواجہ غریب نواز at اجمیر is not
`khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz`.

**What the chunk is actually worth is provenance and apparatus.** It settles the shape of the book's
source apparatus (per-chapter حواشی only — **no کتابیات, no مآخذ, no index, no errata anywhere in the
back matter**), it names the publisher and two non-agreeing addresses, and — importantly — it shows that
**there is no colophon at the back of this book at all**: no press, no year, no edition statement, no
print run. See Doubts on what that does and does not do to the 1965/1999 conflict.

**Folio relation**: folio = PDF index − 4 on every page here that prints a marker (p. 380 → folio 376
through p. 388 → folio 384). **Folio 384 (p. 388) is the last printed folio in the book.** p. 389 is
marked `[blank page]` and prints no marker, so folio 385 never appears; the back matter (pp. 390–392)
prints no folio at all.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The book's imprint, and the only provenance statement in the back matter.** The publisher's endpaper
  carries a device repeated across the page, its wording transcribed once: the motto "تفقہ فی الدین" and
  its Urdu gloss "دین میں خوب سمجھ بوجھ پیدا کرو" — acquire a thorough understanding in religion — then
  the imprint "**پروگریسو بکس / بہاری [OCR?] اردو بازار لاہور / 7352795**" — **Progressive Books,
  Bihari[OCR?] Urdu Bazar, Lahore, telephone 7352795** (p. 390). The back cover repeats the house name
  in a different address form: "**پروگریسو بکس ○ 40 [OCR?] بی اردو بازار ○ لاہور**" — Progressive Books,
  40[OCR?]-B Urdu Bazar, Lahore (p. 392). **No press, no year, no edition, no print run and no patron
  appears on any of these pages** — see Doubts.
- **The publisher's sales catalogue, drawn as a three-dimensional box on the back cover** (p. 392):
  "ہمارے ہاں مذہبی دینی اور عارفانہ کلام کی خوبصورت ترین کتابیں دستیاب ہیں' آج ہی آئیں اور اپنی مطلوبہ
  کتب خرید فرمائیں" — the most beautiful books of religious and mystical کلام are available with us;
  come today and buy the books you want. Eighteen titles in two columns, **title | author as printed**:
  "رسول اکرم صلی اللہ علیہ وسلم کی وصیتیں | مولانا محمد صدیق ہزاروی"; "بہجۃ الاسرار | امام ابوالحسن
  الشطنوفی [OCR?] الشافعی"; "فتوح الغیب | حضرت سید عبدالقادر جیلانی"; "ہشت بہشت | مجموعہ خواجگان چشت
  اہل بہشت [OCR?]"; "کلام یا ہو | حضرت سلطان باہو رحمۃ اللہ علیہ"; "کلام بلھے شاہ | حضرت بلھے شاہ رحمۃ
  اللہ علیہ"; "کلام خواجہ غلام فرید | خواجہ غلام فرید کوٹ مٹھن شریف"; "کلام شاہ حسین رحمۃ اللہ علیہ |
  خواجہ شاہ حسین"; "کلام بابا فرید | حضرت بابا فرید الدین مسعود گنج شکر رحمۃ اللہ علیہ"; "تجلیات
  اعتکاف | مولانا محمد صدیق ہزاروی"; "قرآنی تعلیم (جلد اول ودوم) | مولانا عبدالواجد قادری"; "قرآنی
  علوم | مولانا عبدالواجد قادری"; "بزم اولیا | مولانا عبدالقادری مصباحی [OCR?]"; "زلف و زنجیر | علامہ
  ارشد القادری"; "زیر و زبر | علامہ ارشد القادری"; "تبلیغی جماعت | علامہ ارشد القادری"; "زلزلہ | علامہ
  ارشد القادری"; "نقش کربلا | علامہ ارشد القادری". **This is the publisher's stock list, not the book's
  sources** — see Doubts. **No prices are printed.**
- **Wage labour as a saint's livelihood, with the only money figure in the chunk.** Of the unnamed saint:
  "آپ نے نوکری نہیں کی۔ دہلی میں کتاب کی تصحیح کا کام کرتے تھے۔ دو ڈھائی روپے اجرت کے مل جاتے تھے" — he
  took no employment; at **دہلی** he did the work of **correcting books** and would receive **two to two
  and a half rupees** in wages; "جو کچھ آتا تھا' شام تک خرچ کر دیتے تھے" — whatever came in he spent by
  evening (p. 380, folio 376). A named trade, a named city, a named daily rate, and no accumulation.
- **His household economy, itemised**: bread of **باجرہ** (millet); food eaten from **مٹی کے برتن**
  (earthen vessels); meat given up; no bed at all, only a **چھوٹی سی چوکی** with a **چٹائی** spread on
  it, on which he also prayed; "بہت سخی تھے۔ تحفے جو آتے تھے' تقسیم کر دیتے تھے" — very generous, and
  the gifts that came in he distributed (p. 380, folio 376).
- **Sajjada nashini at Ganj Muradabad, filial.** "آپ کے صاحب زادے حضرت احمد میاں اور مولا سید محمد علی
  کانپوری آپ کے خلیفہ ہیں' حضرت احمد میاں آپ کے سجادہ نشین ہوئے" — his son Ahmad Mian and Maula Sayyid
  Muhammad Ali Kanpuri are his khalifas; **Ahmad Mian became his sajjada nashin** (p. 380, folio 376).
  Two khalifas, one seat, and the seat goes to the son — the term "سجادہ نشین" is the book's own.
- **Shrine etiquette enforced at the gate of a darbar, and a lifelong vow made of it.** At **اجمیر
  شریف** Waris Ali Shah "آستانہ میں داخل ہونا چاہتے تھے کہ دروازہ پر جو شخص بیٹھا ہوا تھا' اس نے آپ سے
  کہا کہ جوتا پہن کر اندر جانا ادب کے خلاف ہے" — he wished to enter the **آستانہ** when **the man
  sitting at the door** told him that going in wearing shoes is against **ادب**; "آپ نے وہیں جوتا اتارا
  اور پھر ساری عمر جوتا نہیں پہنا" — he took his shoes off there and never wore shoes again in his life
  (p. 383, folio 379). **The clearest shrine-practice datum in the chunk: a doorkeeper, a rule of
  access, and compliance turned into a permanent discipline.**
- **A saint's estate seized by kin, and litigation threatened.** Returning home from his travels,
  "آپ نے دیکھا کہ مکان شکستہ ہو چکا ہے اور آپ کے ساز و سامان پر آپ کے رشتہ دار قابض ہیں" — he found his
  **house ruined** and his **relatives in possession** of his goods; "ان کو یہ فکر ہوئی شاید آپ جائداد
  وغیرہ واپس لیں گے اور ممکن ہے عدالتی کارروائی کریں" — they feared he would take back the **جائداد**
  (estate) and might begin **عدالتی کارروائی** (court proceedings); their coldness pained him and
  "آپ نے وطن میں زیادہ قیام کرنا مناسب نہیں سمجھا" (p. 383, folio 379). **The only property dispute in
  the chunk, and it is settled by the saint's departure, not by a court.**
- **Total refusal of cash, and reciprocal giving above value.** "آپ جائداد' مکان' سازو سامان وغیرہ سے
  بے نیاز تھے۔ فقر میں بادشاہی کرتے تھے… روپے پیسے کو ہاتھ نہ لگاتے تھے" — indifferent to estate, house
  and goods; he exercised kingship in faqr; **he would not touch rupees and paisa**; "اگر کوئی شخص آپ کو
  کوئی تحفہ پیش کرتا تو آپ اس سے بہتر چیز اس کو عطا فرماتے تھے" — if anyone presented him a gift he gave
  that person something **better** in return (p. 384, folio 380). A gift economy running at a deliberate
  loss to the shrine.
- **Conveyance refused by category.** "آپ کسی قسم کی سواری پسند نہیں کرتے تھے۔ تانگے' بگھی' یکے میں
  نہیں بیٹھتے تھے ریل اور جہاز میں نہیں بیٹھتے تھے' کمزوری کے باعث پالکی میں بادل ناخواستہ بیٹھتے تھے" —
  he liked no kind of conveyance and would not sit in a **تانگہ, بگھی or یکہ**, nor in the **ریل**
  (railway) or a **جہاز** (ship); through weakness he sat unwillingly in a **پالکی** (palanquin)
  (p. 384, folio 380). **The book elsewhere has him crossing to Jeddah and to Africa by ship (p. 383,
  folio 379); it does not reconcile the two.**
- **A regime of eating, dress and sleep, with its turning points located.** He travelled much and
  "خوارک بہت کم تھی"; for long periods he ate **once a week**, then began eating **on the third day**;
  through weakness he later ate a little "روز یا دوسرے دن [OCR?]"; he ate **ثرید [OCR?]** with relish;
  after eating he did **خلال** and then washed his hands; "آپ نے جب سے احرام باندھنا شروع کیا' پھر اتارا
  نہیں" — from the time he first put on **احرام** he never took it off; and "کربلا پہنچ کر آپ نے یہ طے
  کیا کہ تخت یا پلنگ پر نہ سوئیں گے' تمام عمر اس پر کاربند رہے" — **on reaching کربلا** he resolved
  never to sleep on a **تخت or پلنگ**, and held to it all his life (p. 384, folio 380).
- **A prescribed wazifa against poverty.** "جو تصدیق کے ساتھ 'یا باسط' پڑھتا ہے' وہ تنگ دست نہیں رہتا" —
  whoever recites **"یا باسط"** with conviction does not remain in **تنگ دستی** (straitened means)
  (p. 385, folio 381, حاشیہ 21 = حیات وارث ص 226). The chunk's one economic remedy offered as devotion.
- **Two prescribed forms of درود, one with a counted quota.** He enjoined reciting **درود شریف** in the
  form "الھم صلی اللہ علی محمد وعلی ال محمد وبارک وسلم" [as printed] (p. 385, folio 381, حاشیہ 22 =
  حیات وارث ص 272). To a needy man he prescribed: "شب کو دو رکعت نماز نفل کے بعد درود تاج پڑھ کر سویا

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A wazifa given in a dream and re-given awake.** The author relates, under "کرامات", that the unnamed
  saint taught a devotee a **وظیفہ** in a dream; long after, the man came asking for one, and he replied
  "وظیفہ تو بہت پہلے بتا دیا تھا" — I told you the wazifa long ago — then instructed him in the same one
  (p. 381, folio 377, حاشیہ 4 = مسالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 191،193).
- **The horse drawn out of the marsh, and the hoofmarks on the saint's back.** The author relates that a
  man travelling to him had his horse stuck in a **دلدل** at a **ندی**; as he began to drown he
  remembered the saint and sought his aid, and "گھوڑا فوراً دلدل سے نکل گیا"; reaching the saint he
  found him sitting in his **حجرہ** wrapped in a sheet, who said "لوگ مجھے تکلیف دیتے ہیں" — people
  give me trouble — and showed his back, on which "گھوڑوں کے چاروں سم کے نشان معہ کیچڑ کے موجود تھے",
  the marks of all four hooves were present, with the mud (p. 381, folio 377, حاشیہ 5 = مسالک السالکین
  جلد دوم ص 191،193).
- **The ship halted mid-ocean and the captain's thrice-repeated dream.** The author relates that on his
  first hajj Waris Ali Shah travelled with no **زاد راہ** and went hungry for days; the ship stopped in
  mid-ocean; its Muslim captain saw the Prophet in a dream, who said "لوگ بھوکے ہیں اور تم سیر ہو کر
  کھاتے ہو' یہ اس کا وبال ہے" — people are hungry and you eat your fill; this is the consequence of it.
  The captain feasted all the passengers twice and the saint attended neither; at the third
  "بشارت ہوئی [OCR?]" he called the roll, found the one absent passenger, brought him food, "آپ نے
  کھانا کھایا' جہاز روانہ ہوا" — he ate, and the ship set off (p. 386, folio 382).
- **A soldier promised presence in water, fire and at a thousand kos.** The author relates that the saint
  told the Risaldar "علی محمد! اگر تم پانی میں ہو گے تو ہم تمہارے ساتھ ہیں' آگ میں ہو گے تو ہمراہ ہیں
  اور ہزار کوس پر ہو گے تو ہم تمہارے پاس ہیں" (p. 386, folio 382).
- **A campaign and a decoration foretold in riddling speech.** The author relates that before the
  Risaldar's orders were mentioned the saint said "علی محمد مصر کے چاقو اچھے ہوتے ہیں" — the knives of
  Egypt are good — and then "اگر کوئی ہندوستانی افسر کہیں کار نمایاں سر انجام دے تو ملکہ اس کی بڑی
  خاطر کرتی ہوں گی' ولایت اچھا شہر ہے' اب تم جاؤ" (pp. 386–387, folios 382–383). The sequel — the Indian
  troops' valour, the Egyptian defeat, the journey to England, Queen Victoria's honour and the knives
  brought back — is narrated as the fulfilment (p. 387, folio 383).
- **His own death announced the day before.** "وفات سے ایک دن قبل آپ نے اپنے ایک مرید سے فرمایا:
  'ہم کل صبح چار بجے چلیں گے'" — a day before his death he told a murid, we shall depart tomorrow at
  four in the morning (p. 383, folio 379). The book places the death itself seven days later in its own
  Hijri reckoning — see Doubts.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Each of the two entries rests on a single named earlier work, and the book says so item by item.**
  The unnamed entry's five حواشی are all **"مسالک السالکین جلد دوم"** (ص 180،181 twice; ص 195; ص 191،193
  twice) (p. 381, folio 377). **All twenty-four of باب 70's حواشی are "حیات وارث"**, running from ص 224
  to ص 360 (pp. 387–388, folios 383–384). **باب 70 is a single-source chapter**, and *Hayat-e Waris* is
  the whole of its authority for anything footnoted.
- **But the footnote markers cover only part of the chapter.** In باب 70 markers 1–20 sit on the اقوال
  and 21–24 on the اوراد و وظائف; the biographical headings, the تعلیمات and **the whole of the
  کرامات** carry **no marker at all** (pp. 382–387, folios 378–383). Everything narrative in the chapter
  therefore stands on the compiler's own authority as far as this chunk shows — see Doubts.
- **The entry grid is wider here than earlier in the book.** باب 70 runs: خاندانی حالات, والد, ولادت,
  **بچپن کا صدمہ**, تعلیم و تربیت, بچپن, بیعت و خلافت, **بشارت**, **سیر و سیاحت**, **واپسی**, **سفر**,
  وفات, **حلقہ ارادت**, سیرت, تعلیمات, اقوال, **اوراد و وظائف**, کرامات (pp. 382–386, folios 378–382).
  The seven headings in bold do not appear in the grid recorded for chunks earlier in the book;
  **اوراد و وظائف in particular turns the entry into a manual of practice.** As before, a missing
  heading is the book's silence, not a gap in transcription.
- **The book supplies its own CE equivalents here, and nowhere else in this chunk.** Waris Ali Shah's
  two dates are printed as Hijri "مطابق" CE (p. 383, folio 379); the unnamed saint's death is Hijri only
  ("22 ربیع الاول 1313ھ", p. 380, folio 376), as are the birth ("1232ھ") and the arrival at Mecca
  ("29 شعبان 1253ھ") (pp. 382, 383; folios 378, 379). **The compiler converts selectively**, which is
  itself a fact about how this book's dates were produced.
- **A teaching argued by numerology rather than text.** On the hadith of seventy-three sects — "تہتر
  فرقوں میں سے بہتر ناری ہیں اور ایک ناجی" — he asked "حسد کے کتنے عدد ہیں", how many are the numbers
  of **حسد** (envy); those present answered "حسد کے عدد بہتر ہیں", seventy-two; and he concluded
  "بس جو فرقہ حسد سے باہر وہ ناجی ہے" — the sect that is outside envy is the saved one (p. 384,
  folio 380). The identification of the saved sect is made to turn on a letter-count, and the book
  reports the count from the audience, not from itself.
- **Wilayat used in a wholly different sense from earlier in the book.** Here "ولایت اچھا شہر ہے"
  (p. 387, folio 383) plainly means **England** — the Risaldar is duly sent to "انگلستان" (p. 387,
  folio 383) — whereas earlier chapters use "ولایت" for a saint's assigned territorial jurisdiction.
  **The same word, two senses, in one compendium**: do not read the two usages together.
- **A reading programme prescribed with a statistic attached.** "مثنوی مولانا روم بہت پڑھا کرو' تین سو
  آدمی قطب و ابدال ہو گئے ہیں" — read the Masnavi of Maulana Rum often; **three hundred men** have
  become **قطب and ابدال** (p. 380, folio 376); and asked whether to read it with the meaning or with
  the bare words, the answer as printed is "فقط لفظ کے پڑھنے والے" (p. 380, folio 376). See Doubts: the
  question carries an `[OCR?]` flag and the answer is a fragment.
- **Sainthood redefined as two dispositions.** "اشتیاق لقائے الٰہی یہی ولایت ہے' اتباع سنت یہی غوثیت
  اور قطبیت ہے" — longing for the meeting with God is itself **ولایت**; following the sunna is itself
  **غوثیت and قطبیت** (p. 380, folio 376). The offices are made attributes rather than ranks.
- **نسبت ranked above حال and مقام, with a transferable benefit.** "صاحب حال اور صاحب مقام ہونا آسان
  ہے' بانسبت [as printed] ہونا مشکل ہے"; and "صاحب نسبت سے بیعت کرنا باعث نجات ہے۔ قیامت کے دن جب اس کے
  حال پر عنایت ہو گی تو اس کا پر تو اس کے مریدوں کو پہنچے گا اور سب اس کے ہمراہ جنت کو جائیں گے" —
  bai'at with a possessor of **نسبت** is a cause of salvation; on the Day of Judgement, when favour
  falls on his state, its **reflection** reaches his murids and all go with him to paradise (p. 380,
  folio 376). **The doctrinal warrant for shrine allegiance as collective insurance** — the most
  citable idea in the chunk for this project.
- **The pir–murid bond stated as a two-sided obligation.** Among the اقوال: the murid who thinks the pir
  distant is defective **and so is the pir who stays distant from the murid** (no. 13); the murid is a
  patient and the pir a physician, and the patient who follows instructions heals faster (no. 16);
  "مرید کا مرکز تسلیم و محبت ہے" (no. 17); "فی الحقیقت مرید وہ ہے' جس کی مراد اس کا پیر ہو" (no. 18);
  and the drop that is called a river once it has joined it (no. 19) (p. 385, folio 381, حواشی 13, 16,
  17, 18, 19 = حیات وارث ص 338، 266، 272، 348، 349). **Unusually for this book, the reciprocal duty is
  laid on the pir as well.**

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the saint of pp. 380–381 is unnamed.** The text begins at the "وفات"
  heading; the chapter number, the name, the titles, the lineage, the pir and the silsila all fall in
  the previous chunk. **Do not attribute anything on pp. 380–381 (folios 376–377) to any saint** until
  the preceding chunk is read. The only identifying details this chunk gives him are the death
  "22 ربیع الاول 1313ھ", the mazar at **گنج مراد آباد (یو پی)**, the two khalifas and the sajjada nashin
  son "حضرت احمد میاں" (p. 380, folio 376); its chapter is presumably باب 69, since باب 70 begins on the
  next page (p. 382, folio 378), **but the number is not printed in this chunk.**
- **حاجی وارث علی شاہ is NOT `mausoleum-of-waris-shah`, and this chunk confirms it with positive
  evidence rather than mere caution.** The book gives his mazar at **دیوا**, district **بارہ بنکی
  (یو پی)** (pp. 382, 383; folios 378, 379), his death as "30 محرم الحرام 1323ھ مطابق 12 اپریل 1905ء"
  (p. 383, folio 379), and his silsila as **وارثی**, founded by him (p. 382, folio 378). The archive row
  is "Jandiala Sher Khan, Sheikhupura District, Punjab, Pakistan", figure "Waris Shah", died 1798,
  Chishti. **Shrine town, district, country, death year and silsila all differ.** Not a candidate.
- **"غریب نواز" at اجمیر شریف is NOT `khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz`.** The book places the darbar
  and آستانہ at **اجمیر شریف** (p. 383, folio 379); the row is "Miani Sahib Graveyard, Lahore",
  figure "Hazrat Khawaja Feroz-ud-Din Gharib Nawaz Chishti Nizami", died 1981. The chunk gives the
  Ajmer figure no personal name, no date and no silsila, so **nothing here is mappable to any row**.
- **The book's own two Hijri/CE pairings for Waris Ali Shah do not agree in interval.** "9 محرم الحرام
  1323ھ مطابق 26 مارچ 1905ء" and "30 محرم الحرام 1323ھ مطابق 12 اپریل 1905ء" (p. 383, folio 379) are
  **21 lunar days apart in the Hijri reckoning but 17 days apart in the CE**. Neither date is
  OCR-flagged. Both are recorded as printed above and **neither should be converted**; a human must
  decide which member of which pair is wrong before either enters an entry. Note also that the day of
  the illness (9 Muharram) is the eve of Ashura and the death day is given as **30 Muharram**.
- **There is no colophon at the back of this book, and that is the chunk's chief bearing on the
  1965/1999 problem.** Pages 390–392 carry a publisher's endpaper and a sales catalogue and **no press
  name, no place of printing, no year, no edition statement, no print run and no patron**. So this
  chunk **cannot** adjudicate between the preface dated "۴ اپریل ۱۹۶۵ء[OCR?]" and the colophon
  "بار اول اکتوبر 1999ء" reported from chunk 001 — **it establishes instead that the 1999 statement
  cannot be a back-of-book colophon and must sit in the front matter**, which is where a human should
  go to settle it. What this chunk adds to the dating file is only the publisher's identity
  (**پروگریسو بکس**, Progressive Books), two address forms and a telephone number (p. 390, p. 392).
- **Two non-agreeing publisher addresses inside this chunk alone.** p. 390: "پروگریسو بکس / بہاری
  [OCR?] اردو بازار لاہور / 7352795". p. 392: "پروگریسو بکس ○ 40 [OCR?] بی اردو بازار ○ لاہور". Both are
  اردو بازار, Lahore, but the street-or-house element differs and **both elements carry `[OCR?]`**. The
  telephone number appears only on p. 390 and is not flagged. **These must be collated with the two
  non-agreeing publisher addresses already reported from chunk 001** — a human should check whether this
  chunk's two forms are the same pair or a third and fourth.
- **The back-cover list is a sales catalogue, not the book's bibliography — do not read it as the
  compendium's sources** (p. 392). This chunk contains **no کتابیات, no مآخذ, no فہرست/index and no
  errata**, and since p. 388 (folio 384) is the last printed folio of the book, **the book has none at
  the back at all.** The compendium's chain of transmission is carried **only** in the per-chapter
  حواشی, and must be reconstructed chunk by chunk. In this chunk that chain is exactly two works:
  **"مسالک السالکین جلد دوم"** (ص 180–195) and **"حیات وارث"** (ص 224–360).

  all five حواشی); the notes for chunk 004 of the same book record the same work as **"سالک
  السالکین"**. Neither form is OCR-flagged in its chunk. A human should decide which is the print's
  form before either is cited; the two may be a genuine inconsistency in the book.
