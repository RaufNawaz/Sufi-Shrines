# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 002 — pp. 35–52

**Five and a half tazkirah entries.** The chunk opens inside an entry already in progress (pp. 35–39,
folios 31–35) whose subject is never named by a heading here; then باب 3 حضرت شیخ محمد ترک نارنولی
(pp. 41–42, folios 37–38), باب 4 حضرت خواجہ محمود موئینہ دوز (p. 43, folio 39), باب 5 حضرت خواجہ فخر
الدین ابو الخیر (pp. 44–45, folios 40–41), باب 6 حضرت شیخ احمد نہروانی (pp. 46–47, folios 42–43), and
باب 7 حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا (pp. 48–52, folios 44–48), which breaks off mid-entry at the chunk's
end. **One archive row is the subject of an entry** — Bahauddin Zakariya of Multan — and he is also the
second figure of the opening entry, so he carries material on two fronts. The chunk's richest
non-hagiographical material is a **tax-remission farman on a named mauza**, a **stone-and-sugar vow
practice at a mazar**, a **langar plus large-scale agriculture and trade**, and the **only sajjada-nashin
statement in the chunk**.

**Folio relation, verified at both ends**: folio = PDF index − 4 (p. 35 → folio 31, p. 52 → folio 48), and
every marker in between reads exactly that.

**Each chapter carries its own numbered footnote apparatus** (حواشی) printed at the chapter's end: nineteen
notes at p. 40 (folio 36), four at p. 45 (folio 41), two at p. 47 (folio 43). Sources named are فوائد
الفوائد (اردو ترجمہ), اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ), سالک السالکین[OCR?] (جلد دوم), سیر الاولیاء (فارسی),
خیر المجالس (اردو ترجمہ), جامع الکلام and "معین الہند" از ڈاکٹر ظہور الحسن شارب.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا, called in the text also "بہاء الدین زکریا
  ملتانی", subject of باب 7, pp. 48–52, folios 44–48; and the arbitrator of the Delhi trial in the opening
  entry, pp. 35–37, folios 31–33):
  - **Titles as printed**: "پیر لاثانی", peerless pir; "مقبول بارگاہ رحمانی"; "برہان ملت"; "گنج عزلت";
    "مخزن سخا", treasury of generosity; "معدن وفا"; "کان صفا"; "قطب الاولیاء"; "شیخ الاتقیاء"; "قدوۃ
    الاصفیاء"; and "اکابر اولیاء میں آپ کا شمار ہے", he is counted among the greatest of the auliya
    (p. 48, folio 44). In the khirqa dream the Prophet names him "شیخ بہاء الدین غوث العالم" (p. 51,
    folio 47).
  - **Silsila as the book gives it**: "آپ حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین عمر سہروردی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے مرید اور
    خلیفہ ہیں" — he is the murid and khalifa of Hazrat Shaikh Shihab al-Din Umar Suhrawardi (p. 48,
    folio 44); the bai'at was taken at Baghdad (p. 50, folio 46). The book nowhere uses the word
    "سہروردیہ" as an order name in this entry.
  - **Lineage, given as a set of variants rather than one line.** "آپ قریشی' اسدی' ہاشمی تھے" — he was
    Qurashi, Asadi, Hashimi (p. 48, folio 44, حاشیہ 1). "یہ بھی کہا جاتا ہے" that he was of the progeny of
    "مہیار[OCR?] بن اسود بن مطلب بن اسد بن عبد العزیٰ[OCR?] بن قصی" (حاشیہ 2); some accepted only "القرشی
    الاسدی" (حاشیہ 3); some wrote, in Persian, "سلسلہ او باسد قریش کہ جد مادری حضرت امیر المومنین علی کرم
    اللہ وجہہ منتہی می شود", his lineage ends at Asad of Quraish, the maternal grandfather of Ali — the
    book prints its own Urdu translation beneath it (حاشیہ 4). Then the one adjudication: "آپ ہاشمی ہیں اور
    آپ کے ہاشمی ہونے میں کوئی شک و شبہ نہیں" — he is Hashimi and in his being Hashimi there is no doubt
    whatever (p. 48, folio 44, حاشیہ 5).
  - **The family's route to Multan.** His "جد امجد" Maulana Shaikh Kamal al-Din Aliya Shah[OCR?], a
    dervish-natured elder and honoured member of the Quraish, "مکہ سے سکونت ترک کرکے خوارزم میں رہے' پھر
    خوارزم سے ملتان تشریف لائے اور وہاں مستقل سکونت اختیار کی" — left Mecca, lived in Khwarazm, then came
    from Khwarazm to Multan and settled there permanently (p. 48, folio 44, حاشیہ 6), where many became
    his devotees (p. 49, folio 45). The book's reason: "ملتان اس زمانہ میں اسلامی علوم و فنون کا مرکز تھا"
    — Multan in that age was a centre of Islamic sciences and arts (p. 48, folio 44).
  - **Father**: Shaikh Wajih al-Din (شیخ وجیہہ الدین) (p. 49, folio 45). **Mother**: "بی بی فاطمہ", Bibi
    Fatima, given first as the daughter of Maulana Husam al-Din Tirmizi, who came from his homeland Tirmiz
    to the fort of "قلعہ کوٹ کڑور[OCR?]" when the Tatar plundering began (حاشیہ 7); and then, "یہ بھی کہا
    جاتا ہے", as the daughter of Hazrat Isa, of the progeny of Ghaus al-Azam Shaikh Jilani, resident at
    "ہامہ[OCR?]", who married her to Shaikh Wajih al-Din when he visited there (p. 49, folio 45). Both are
    printed without preference; see Doubts.
  - **Birth as printed**: "آپ صبح کے وقت' جمعہ کے دن 27 رمضان 566ھ کو کوٹ کڑور میں پیدا ہوئے" — born in
    the morning, on Friday, 27 Ramazan 566 AH, at Kot Karor (p. 49, folio 45). Name: Bahauddin. Kunya:
    Abu Muhammad, and "بعض نے آپ کی کنیت ابو البرکات بھی لکھی ہے", some also wrote Abu al-Barakat (حاشیہ 8).
  - **Born a wali**: "آپ مادر زاد ولی تھے" (p. 49, folio 45) — as an infant he would stop suckling to
    listen when his father recited the Quran, and while still at maktab said "جس وقت خداوند تعالیٰ نے الست
    بربکم فرمایا تھا' اس وقت سے لے کر اب تک کے واقعات مجھے یاد ہیں", I remember what has happened from the
    moment God said *Am I not your Lord?* until now (حاشیہ 9). His father died when he was twelve.
  - **Training, quantified.** Memorised the Quran "سات سال کی عمر میں سات قرات کے ساتھ", at the age of
    seven with the seven readings; after his father's death went to **Khurasan** and spent seven years in
    the outward lessons (حاشیہ 10); then **Bukhara**, where he completed his learning and "وہاں آپ 'بہاء
    الدین فرشتہ' کے نام سے مشہور ہوئے", became famous there as *Bahauddin Farishta*; then **Medina**, where
    he took hadith from Shaikh Kamal al-Din Muhammad Yamani, of the great muhaddithin, and "اجازت نامہ بھی
    حاصل کیا", obtained an ijazat-nama as well, staying five years (p. 50, folio 46). He met dervishes in
    "خراسان' بخارا' مکہ' مدینہ اور بیت المقدس" (p. 50, folio 46).
  - **Seventy days that caused resentment.** Nizam al-Din Auliya is quoted that he stayed "ستر[OCR?] روز"
    in Shihab al-Din Umar Suhrawardi's service and received much bounty; the other murids resented it, and
    the shaykh answered them: "تم مثل لکڑی کے تر ہو اور زکریا مثل لکڑی خشک کے ہے' پس آگ خشک لکڑی کو جلد
    پکڑتی ہے" — you are like wet wood and Zakariya like dry; fire catches dry wood quickly (p. 50,
    folio 46, حاشیہ 11).
  - **The khirqa of khilafat, dreamt and then delivered** (pp. 50–51, folios 46–47): see Legends.
    After conferring it his pir "آپ کو ملتان جانے کی تاکید فرمائی", enjoined him to go to Multan (p. 51,
    folio 47) — the book's stated reason for his presence at Multan.
  - **On the road to Multan** he met a learned qalandar, Sayyid Abd al-Quddus of Mosul, who had donned the
    qalandars' dress at the grave of Sayyid Jamal al-Din Mujarrad; "آپ نے ان کا قلندرانہ جامہ اتروایا اور
    عالم جذب سے ان کو عالم سلوک کو پہنچایا" — he had the qalandari garment removed and brought him from
    the state of *jazb* to the state of *suluk* (p. 51, folio 47). Shaikh Jalal al-Din Tabrizi travelled
    with him as far as **Nishapur**, where they parted (p. 51, folio 47).
  - **Arrival at Multan and the dervishes' objection.** "آپ نے ملتان پہنچ کر مستقل سکونت اختیار فرمائی" —
    he took permanent residence at Multan; the dervishes of Multan were displeased and tried to have him
    leave, sending him a cup filled with milk to say "اس شہر میں دوسرے کی گنجائش نہیں ہے", there is no room
    for another in this city; he placed a flower on the cup and sent it back, meaning his place in the city
    would be "جیسے کہ پھول دودھ پر ہے", as a flower is on milk; they were astonished and became his
    devotees (p. 51, folio 47).
  - **Death as printed**: "آپ 7 صفر 666ھ کو بروز جمعرات جوار رحمت میں داخل ہوئے" — 7 Safar 666 AH, on a
    Thursday (p. 52, folio 48, حاشیہ 15), with "آپ کی عمر (100) سو سال کی تھی", his age one hundred years.
  - **Sons**: "آپ کے ساتھ [OCR?] لڑکے تھے", he had [OCR?] sons — the numeral is OCR-flagged; seven names
    follow: Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif, Shaikh Burhan al-Din, Shaikh Zia al-Din, "شیخ علاوٴ الدین [OCR?]",
    Shaikh Shihab al-Din, Shaikh Qudwat al-Din, Shaikh Shams al-Din (p. 52, folio 48).
  - **Succession and khalifas** — see Practices; the sajjada nashin is his son Sadr al-Din Arif (p. 52,
    folio 48).
  - **His household economy and habits** — see Practices for the langar, the agriculture and the trade
    (p. 52, folio 48). Also: he completed one Quran every night; "تحمل و بردباری' ذوق و شوق' بے ہوشی اور
    استغراق آپ کی نمایاں خصوصیات تھیں"; and "آپ کو سماع کا شوق تھا", he was fond of *sama* (p. 52,
    folio 48). He fasted much in early life but "آخری عمر میں روزانہ روزہ نہ رکھتے تھے", did not fast daily
    in his last years (حاشیہ 17).
  - **He was also a poet**: "آپ شاعر بھی تھے", and he has a famous *manqabat* in honour of Ghaus al-Azam
    Shaikh Abd al-Qadir Jilani, "جس کے چند اشعار ذیل میں پیش کئے" — a few verses of which are presented
    below (p. 52, folio 48). The verses fall outside this chunk.
  - **In the opening entry he acts twice**, both times as a figure of authority rather than as the subject:
    he rebukes the unnamed saint for not naming Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi before Farid al-Din Attar, is
    displeased and separates from him (p. 35, folio 31, حاشیہ 6); and at Delhi, chosen as arbitrator
    (ثالث), he lifts the accused saint's shoes in his own hands, declares "میرے اوپر واجب ہے کہ شیخ جلال
    کی خاک پا کو اپنی آنکھوں کا سرمہ بناؤں' کیوں کہ وہ سات برس تک سفر و خضر[OCR?] میں میرے پیر و مرشد کی
    خدمت میں مقیم رہے ہیں", it is obligatory on me to make the dust of Shaikh Jalal's feet the kohl of my
    eyes, because for seven years in travel and residence[OCR?] he stayed in the service of my pir and
    murshid — and then examines the woman, who confesses the whole plot (pp. 36–37, folios 32–33,
    حاشیہ 9). The estrangement of the first episode is what the accuser had counted on: "ان کو خیال تھا کہ
    حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کی آپ سے نیشاپور میں کشیدگی ہو گئی تھی" (p. 36, folio 32).
  - The chunk states no shrine, urs, endowment or custodianship for his own mazar beyond the sajjada-nashin
    line; the entry breaks off before any such section (see Doubts).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **The unnamed subject of the opening entry** (pp. 35–39, folios 31–35). He is named nowhere in the
  surviving text except inside Bahauddin Zakariya's quoted speech, as "شیخ جلال" (p. 36, folio 32); see
  Doubts. What the chunk says of him: he came from **Baghdad** and met **Farid al-Din Attar**, before whom
  "میں حضرت فرید الدین عطار سے اتنا متاثر ہوا اور ان کی عظمت اور بزرگی نے میرے دل و دماغ پر ایسا قبضہ کیا
  کہ ان کے سامنے مجھ کو کچھ یاد نہ رہا", he was so affected that nothing stayed in his mind (p. 35,
  folio 31); he travelled to **Multan** and thence to **کہتوال[OCR?]**, always seeking out the dervishes of
  a place (p. 35, folio 31); at **Delhi** he was received by "سلطان شمس الدین التمش[OCR?]" and then accused
  of zina in a plot by the Shaikh al-Islam (pp. 36–37, folios 32–33); he drew "فیوض و برکات" from Khwaja
  Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki at Delhi, and "یہ بھی کہا جاتا ہے کہ آپ نے حضرت خواجہ قطب الدین صاحب سے خرقہ
  خلافت حاصل کیا", it is also said he received the khirqa of khilafat from him (p. 37, folio 33); he left
  Delhi saying he had come as pure gold and left as silver; stayed at **بدایوں** (Badaun), where he
  converted a dacoit and named him **Ali**, clothed the child **Maulana Ala al-Din Usuli**, and humbled
  **Qazi Kamal al-Din Jafari** (pp. 37–38, folios 33–34); then "بدایوں سے ترک سکونت کرکے بنگال روانہ
  ہوئے", left Badaun for **Bengal**, where he settled permanently, taught to his last breath and "مسجد اور
  خانقاہ بنائی", built a mosque and a khanqah (pp. 38–39, folios 34–35). Death and shrine: "آپ 642ھ میں
  واصل حق ہوئے' مزار فیض آثار دیو محل بندر[OCR?] (سلہٹ) میں مرجع خاص و عام ہے" — he reached the Truth in
  642 AH; his mazar at Deo Mahal Bandar[OCR?] (Sylhet) is a resort of high and low (p. 39, folio 35). His
  distinguished murid is **Khwaja Ali** (p. 39, folio 35, حاشیہ 16). Habits: he prayed *ishraq* then slept,
  did *muraqaba* after Isha, stayed awake all night (حاشیہ 17), and "مرید بہت کم کرتے تھے", made very few
  murids (حاشیہ 18).
- **حضرت بابا فرید الدین مسعود گنج شکر** (p. 35–36, folios 31–32). At **کہتوال[OCR?]** the people called
  him "قاضی بچہ", Qazi Bacha; he "جامع مسجد میں رہتا ہے", lived in the Jami Masjid, and was "مرید حضرت
  قطب الدین بختیار[OCR?] کاکی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کا" (p. 35, folio 31). He was fasting when the pomegranate was
  eaten before him; he kept the fallen seed in his turban and broke his fast on it, "ان کی طبیعت میں
  انشراح اور دل میں روشنی پیدا ہوئی", producing expansion in his temperament and light in his heart (p. 35,
  folio 31, حاشیہ 7); at Delhi his pir told him "مسعود! جو انار کا دانہ تیری قسمت کا تھا' تجھ کو مل گیا'
  خاطر جمع رکھو", Masud, the pomegranate seed that was your portion you have received; be content (p. 36,
  folio 32, حاشیہ 8). **Deliberately left unmapped — see Doubts.**
- **حضرت شیخ محمد ترک نارنولی** (باب 3, pp. 41–42, folios 37–38): came from his homeland **Turkistan** to
  Hindustan and settled at **نارنول** (Narnaul). Titles: "پیر ترک" and "ترک سلطان". Bai'at and khilafat,
  both hedged: "یہ کہا جاتا ہے کہ آپ حضرت خواجہ عثمان ہارونی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے مرید ہیں اور حضرت خواجہ
  معین الدین حسن چشتی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ نے بھی آپ کو خرقہ خلافت سے سرفراز فرمایا تھا" — it is said he is a
  murid of Khwaja Usman Haruni and that Khwaja Muin al-Din Hasan Chishti also honoured him with the khirqa
  of khilafat. Death: "آپ نے 642ھ میں جام شہادت نوش فرمایا" — he drank the cup of martyrdom in 642 AH;
  "آپ کا مزار نارنول مرجع خاص و عام ہے", his mazar at Narnaul is a resort of high and low (p. 41,
  folio 37).
- **حضرت نصیر الدین محمود چراغ دہلوی** (pp. 41–42, folios 37–38): ordered by the king to go to **ٹھٹھ**
  (Thatta); he went by way of Narnaul, dismounted a *kos* short of the town and walked to the shrine; his
  pronouncements there are under Practices and Legends; after two or three stages news came of the king's
  death and "بجائے ٹھٹھ جانے کے وہ دہلی واپس تشریف لے آئے", instead of going to Thatta he returned to Delhi
  (p. 42, folio 38).
- **حضرت خواجہ محمود موئینہ دوز** (باب 4, p. 43, folio 39): "حضرت خواجہ قطب الدین بختیار کاکی رحمۃ اللہ
  علیہ سے بے حد عقیدت تھی", he had boundless devotion to Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki; he is a murid of
  Hazrat Qazi Hamid al-Din Nagauri. "آپ کی وفات 655ھ میں ہوئی' مزار دہلی میں واقع ہے" — he died in
  655 AH; his mazar is situated in **Delhi**.
- **حضرت خواجہ فخر الدین ابو الخیر** (باب 5, pp. 44–45, folios 40–41): "شاہ زمین و زمن"; on his father's
  side of the progeny of Imam Husain, "پس آپ حسینی ہیں"; "خواجہ خواجگان حضرت خواجہ معین الدین حسن چشتی
  رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے آپ بڑے صاحب زادے ہیں", the eldest son of Khwaja Muin al-Din Hasan Chishti; born of
  **Bibi Amat Allah** (حاشیہ 1, citing "معین الہند" از ڈاکٹر ظہور الحسن شارب ص 111، 191). Birth: "آپ کی
  ولادت با سعادت 591ھ میں ہوئی" (p. 44, folio 40). Brothers: the full brother **Khwaja Husam al-Din Abu
  Salih**, the half-brother **Khwaja Zia al-Din Abu Saeed**; full sister **Bibi Hafiza Jamal**. Livelihood
  and the farman are under Practices. Death: "آپ 5 شعبان 661ھ کو رحمت حق میں پیوست ہوئے' بوقت وفات آپ کی
  عمر (70) ستر سال کی تھی" — 5 Shaban 661 AH, aged seventy at death; and "یہ بھی کہا جاتا ہے کہ آپ کا
  وصال حضرت خواجہ غریب نواز کی وفات کے بیس سال بعد ہوا", it is also said his passing was twenty years after
  Khwaja Gharib Nawaz's death (p. 45, folio 41, حاشیہ 4). Shrine and urs under Practices.
- **حضرت شیخ احمد نہروانی** (باب 6, pp. 46–47, folios 42–43): he "اس مجلس سماع میں موجود تھے کہ جس میں
  قطب الاقطاب حضرت خواجہ قطب الدین بختیار کاکی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ نے وصال فرمایا", was present at the *majlis-e
  sama* in which Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki passed away; murid of Qazi Hamid al-Din Nagauri; "آپ بافندگی
  کا کام کرتے تھے", he worked as a weaver. His pir's question "احمد! یہ کام کب تک کرتے رہو گے", Ahmad, how
  long will you go on doing this work, was followed by his hand striking a nail and breaking; he smiled and
  said "اس بوڑھے (قاضی حمید الدین) نے میرا ہاتھ توڑ ڈالا", this old man has broken my hand, and from that
  day gave up weaving (p. 46, folio 42, حاشیہ 1). Death: "آپ نے 661ھ میں وفات پائی' مزار بدایوں میں ہے" —
  died 661 AH; mazar at **Badaun** (حاشیہ 2). Bahauddin Zakariya Multani is quoted on him: "اگر احمد کی
  مشغولی وزن کی جائے تو دس صوفیوں کی مشغولی کے برابر ہو گی", if Ahmad's absorption were weighed it would
  equal that of ten Sufis (p. 46, folio 42).
- **حضرت سید جلال الدین سرخ بخاری** and **حضرت سید عثمان معروف بہ لعل شہباز سندی[OCR?]** (p. 52, folio 48):
  both named in Bahauddin Zakariya's list of "مقتدر خلفاء", eminent khalifas, alongside **حضرت شیخ حسن
  افغان**, **حضرت شیخ فخر الدین ابراہیم عراقی** and **حضرت سید صدر الدین احمد معروف بہ سید حسین**
  (حاشیہ 16). The chunk gives none of them a place, a date or a shrine. **Deliberately left unmapped —

- **The Delhi actors of the plot** (pp. 36–37, folios 32–33): **نجم الدین مغربی**, "جو اس وقت شیخ اسلام
  تھے", then Shaikh al-Islam, who "بربنائے حسد" out of jealousy schemed to bring down the saint's standing;
  the courtesan **گوہر** (Gohar), also called "مطربہ"; and **احمد اشرف بقال**, the grocer with whom part of
  the bribe was deposited "بطور امانت", in trust. In باب 7 the same office-holder and the same accusation
  appear under the name **نجم الدین صغریٰ[OCR?]** (pp. 51–52, folios 47–48, حاشیہ 14); see Doubts.
- **Other figures named in passing**: **حضرت فرید الدین عطار** (p. 35, folio 31); **حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین

  (pp. 35–37, 43, 46; folios 31–33, 39, 42); **حضرت نظام الدین اولیاء**, quoted twice as a source of report
  (pp. 38, 50; folios 34, 46); **حضرت قاضی حمید الدین ناگوری**, pir of two of the chunk's subjects

  folio 37); **حضرت خواجہ معین الدین حسن چشتی / خواجہ غریب نواز** (pp. 41, 44–45; folios 37, 40–41);
  **بی بی امتہ اللہ** (p. 44, folio 40); **شیخ کمال الدین محمد یمنی** of Medina (p. 50, folio 46);
  **سید عبد القدوس** of Mosul and **سید جمال الدین مجرد** (p. 51, folio 47); **غوث الاعظم حضرت شیخ
  عبدالقادر جیلانی** (pp. 49, 52; folios 45, 48); **سلطان محمود غزنوی**, named as the conqueror of the

- **Places named**: بغداد, ملتان, کہتوال[OCR?], دہلی, بدایوں, بنگال, دیو محل بندر[OCR?] (سلہٹ), نیشاپور,
  ترکستان, نارنول, ٹھٹھ, خراسان, بخارا, مکہ, مدینہ, بیت المقدس, خوارزم, ترمذ, قلعہ کوٹ کڑور[OCR?],
  ہامہ[OCR?], موضع ماندل[OCR?] / ماندن[OCR?], سروار[OCR?], موصل, and the "مسجد منار" at Delhi
  (pp. 35–52, folios 31–48).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A langar open to all, on the back of large-scale agriculture and trade.** Of Bahauddin Zakariya: "آپ
  کے یہاں زراعت اور تجارت بڑے پیمانے پر ہوتی تھی' ہر حال میں خدا کا شکر ادا کرتے تھے' آپ کا لنگر عام تھا'
  آپ بہت مخیر تھے' دنیا سے بے پروا تھے' سادگی اور قناعت کے ساتھ زندگی گزارتے تھے' بہت کم کھاتے تھے' لیکن
  قوی غذا کھاتے تھے' علماء کرام مشائخ عظام اور مہمانوں کی بہت عزت اور خاطر کرتے تھے" — at his place
  **agriculture and trade were carried on on a large scale**; he gave thanks in every condition; **his
  langar was general** (open to all); he was very charitable, unconcerned with the world, lived with
  simplicity and contentment, ate very little but strong food, and greatly honoured the ulama, the great
  mashaikh and guests (p. 52, folio 48, حاشیہ 17). The clearest statement of a shrine household's
  productive base anywhere in this chunk: the langar is funded by farming and commerce, not by a grant.
- **The only custodianship statement in the chunk, and it is hereditary.** "آپ کے لڑکے حضرت شیخ صدر الدین
  عارف آپ کے خلیفہ اور سجادہ نشین تھے" — his son Hazrat Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif was his **khalifa and
  sajjada nashin** — set against a separate list of five "مقتدر خلفاء", eminent khalifas, who are not sons
  (p. 52, folio 48, حاشیہ 16). Succession to the seat and succession to the teaching are distinguished on
  one page.
- **A saint appointed to a state religious office, by an emperor, as the outcome of a slander case.**
  "سلطان شمس الدین التمش نے نجم الدین صغریٰ[OCR?] کو شیخ الاسلام کے عہدے سے علیحدہ کر دیا … اس جھوٹے
  بہتان کی سزا میں نجم الدین صغریٰ کو اپنے عہدے سے دست بردار ہونا پڑا۔ نجم الدین صغریٰ کے بجائے آپ شیخ
  الاسلام مقرر ہوئے" — Sultan Shams al-Din Altamsh removed Najm al-Din Sughra[OCR?] from the office of
  **Shaikh al-Islam**; as punishment for the false slander he had to relinquish his office, and in his
  place **Bahauddin Zakariya was appointed Shaikh al-Islam** (pp. 51–52, folios 47–48, حاشیہ 14). The
  chunk's most direct statement of state office held by a figure the archive carries.
- **A shariah tribunal convened at Delhi over a charge against a saint.** "جب سلطان التمش کے پاس شکایت
  پہنچی تو اس نے تمام مشاہیر اور مشائخ کو دہلی بلایا۔ دو سو سے زیادہ اولیائے کرام دہلی آئے۔ جمعہ کی نماز
  کے بعد سب مسجد منار میں جمع ہوئے" — when the complaint reached Sultan Altamsh he summoned all the
  notables and mashaikh to Delhi; **more than two hundred** auliya came; after the Friday prayer all
  assembled in the **Masjid Minar**. The Sultan let the accuser name an arbitrator (ثالث); he named
  Bahauddin Zakariya; when the accused entered "سب آپ کی تعظیم کے لئے کھڑے ہو گئے", all stood in respect,
  and the Sultan said "جب ثالث نے اتنی عزت کی تو پھر معاملہ طے ہو گیا", when the arbitrator has shown such
  honour the matter is decided (p. 36, folio 32, حاشیہ 9). A convened assembly, a named mosque, a named
  procedure, and a verdict reached by gesture before evidence.
- **The bribe as an accounted transaction.** "پانسو اشرفیوں کا لالچ دے کر ایک طوائف کو جس کا نام گوہر
  تھا' اس بات پر آمادہ کیا کہ آپ پر زنا کا الزام لگائے۔ ڈھائی سو اشرفیاں اس طوائف کو دیدی گئیں اور ڈھائی
  سو اشرفیاں یہ طے پایا کہ بعد میں دی جائیں گی۔ بقیہ ڈھائی سو اشرفیاں احمد اشرف بقال کے پاس بطور امانت
  رکھا دی گئیں" — a courtesan named Gohar was induced with the lure of **five hundred ashrafis**; two
  hundred and fifty were paid, two hundred and fifty were to follow, and the **remaining** two hundred and
  fifty were deposited in trust with Ahmad Ashraf the grocer (p. 36, folio 32). The arithmetic does not
  close; see Doubts. In the باب 7 retelling the figure is again "پانسو اشرفیاں" (p. 52, folio 48).
- **A revenue remission on a named village, obtained by a saint's intercession at the capital.** Of Khwaja
  Fakhr al-Din Abu al-Khair: "آپ موضع ماندل[OCR?] میں کاشت کرتے تھے' ایک مرتبہ حاکم وقت نے کچھ مزاحمت کرنا
  چاہی" — he cultivated in **mauza Mandal[OCR?]**, and once the ruler of the time sought to obstruct him
  (p. 44, folio 40, حاشیہ 2); he petitioned his father, and "حضرت خواجہ غریب نواز ایک کسان کی سفارش کے
  لئے دہلی تشریف لے جا رہے تھے۔ حضرت خواجہ غریب نواز کے دہلی پہنچنے پر موضوع ماندن[OCR?] (ماندل) کی معافی
  کا فرمان آپ (حضرت خواجہ فخر الدین) کے حق میں مل گیا" — Khwaja Gharib Nawaz was going to Delhi to
  intercede for a peasant, and on his reaching Delhi a **farman of remission (معافی) of mauza
  Mandan[OCR?] (Mandal)** was obtained in Fakhr al-Din's favour (pp. 44–45, folios 40–41, حاشیہ 3, citing
  سیر الاولیاء ص 53، جامع الکلام ص 207). A saint's family as cultivators, a revenue officer's
  interference, and a written exemption as the remedy.
- **An annual urs kept with pomp, at an OCR-flagged place.** "مزار پر انوار سروار[OCR?] میں فیوض و برکات
  کا سرچشمہ ہے' آپ کا عرس مبارک بڑے تزک و احتشام سے ہر سال ہوتا ہے" — his luminous mazar at Sarwar[OCR?]
  is a fountainhead of faiz and barakat, and **his blessed urs is held every year with great pomp and
  ceremony** (p. 45, folio 41). The only urs in the chunk.
- **A vow practice with a weighed offering, recorded as current usage at a mazar.** Of Khwaja Mahmud
  Muina-doz at Delhi: "لوگ حاجت بر آری کے واسطے آپ کے مزار سے ایک پتھر لے جا کر علیحدہ رکھ دیتے ہیں'
  مراد پوری ہونے پر اس پتھر کے برابر شکر تول کر تقسیم کر دیتے ہیں" — for the fulfilment of a need people
  **take a stone from his mazar and set it aside; when the wish is fulfilled they weigh out sugar equal to
  that stone and distribute it** (p. 43, folio 39). A quantified, self-assessing offering: the stone fixes
  the amount. The same page reports that petitioners came to him in life to have runaway slaves recovered
  by prayer, on condition of reporting the return.
- **A shrine declared, by a visiting saint, to be a place for hard difficulties.** At Narnaul, Nasir al-Din
  Mahmud Chiragh Dehlvi came out of *muraqaba* and said "جس کسی کو کوئی سخت مشکل درپیش ہو اور وہ اس روضہ
  کی طرف متوجہ ہو' امید ہے وہ مشکل آسان ہو جائے گی" — whoever is faced with a hard difficulty and turns
  toward **this rauza**, it is hoped his difficulty will be made easy; challenged that he himself was in
  trouble, being sent to Thatta against his will, he answered "اسی سبب سے میں کہتا ہوں کہ خدا تعالیٰ ان
  کی برکت سے میری مشکل آسان کر دے گا", for that very reason I say God will ease my difficulty through their
  barakat (p. 42, folio 38). The chunk also records the **etiquette of the visit**: he dismounted "جب نار
  نول ایک کوس رہ گیا", when one *kos* of Narnaul remained, and came to the mazar on foot; and **a stone set
  in front of the grave** — "آپ کی قبر کے سامنے ایک پتھر لگا ہوا تھا" — before which he stood a long while
  (p. 41, folio 37). Two of the chunk's three shrines with a practice attached have a **stone** at the
  centre of it.
- **A mosque and a khanqah built by the saint himself at the place of his final residence**: "مسجد اور
  خانقاہ بنائی" (p. 39, folio 35), the shrine then being "مرجع خاص و عام", a resort of high and low, at
  Deo Mahal Bandar[OCR?] (Sylhet) (p. 39, folio 35). "مرجع خاص و عام" is used again of Narnaul (p. 41,
  folio 37).
- **A cash offering accepted but left with the donor, then disbursed on instruction.** The new convert Ali
  "ایک لاکھ چیتل لا کر آپ کو نذر کئے" — brought **one lakh chital** and offered them as *nazr*; the saint
  accepted and directed "اسے اپنے پاس رکھو' جہاں میں کہوں گا' وہاں صرف کرنا", keep it with you and spend
  it where I tell you. The sum was then disbursed in grants of a hundred and of fifty chital until one
  chital remained, and that last one was given, on the saint's order, to a beggar who arrived at that
  moment (p. 37, folio 33, حاشیہ 11). Custody of the offering stays with the donor; the saint holds only
  the power of direction. The numerals are hard to construe as printed; see Doubts.
- **A murid's town assigned to him as a charge.** Refusing to let Ali follow him to Bengal, the saint
  ordered: "واپس جا' کیوں کہ یہ شہر تیری حمایت میں ہے" — go back, for **this city is in your protection**
  (p. 38, folio 34, حاشیہ 15). A territorial delegation stated in a single clause.
- **Certification and the transfer of authority as objects and documents.** An **ijazat-nama** in hadith
  taken at Medina (p. 50, folio 46); the **khirqa of khilafat** conferred by hand after being taken down
  from a rope of hanging khirqas, "طنطاب [OCR?] سے اتار کر", with the pir disclaiming any authority of his
  own: "یہ سب خرقے سرور عالم ﷺ کے ہیں' میں تو درمیان میں صرف ایک واسطہ ہوں' بے اجازت سرور عالم ﷺ کے کسی
  کو نہیں پہنا سکتا" — all these khirqas are the Prophet's; I am only an intermediary in between and can
  clothe no one without his permission (p. 51, folio 47, حاشیہ 13); and a khirqa reported, with "یہ بھی
  کہا جاتا ہے", from Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki (p. 37, folio 33).
- **Sama as an institution twice over**: Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki "نے وصال فرمایا" in a *majlis-e sama*
  (p. 46, folio 42), and Bahauddin Zakariya "کو سماع کا شوق تھا" (p. 52, folio 48).
- **Trades and their renunciation**: weaving given up after a broken hand (p. 46, folio 42); and, in the
  opening entry's sayings, "جس کسی نے صنعت میں دل لگایا' وہ دنیا کا بندہ ہو گیا", whoever set his heart on
  a craft became a slave of the world (p. 39, folio 35).
- **Multan's standing as a centre of learning is given as the reason a Quraishi family migrated there**
  (p. 48, folio 44), and **Tatar plundering is given as the reason another family moved from Tirmiz to the
  fort of Kot Karor[OCR?]** (p. 49, folio 45): two stated causes of settlement, one pull and one push.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The pomegranate seed**, reported by the author under the heading "انار کا دانہ" on the authority of
  اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 87: a single fallen seed, kept in a turban and taken as *iftar*, is said to
  have produced expansion of temperament and light in the heart, and Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki is then
  reported to have told Baba Farid that the seed was exactly his portion (pp. 35–36, folios 31–32,
  حواشی 7–8).
- **A dacoit converted at sight.** As the author tells it, a man of a band of dacoits from مواسی[OCR?],
  passing with a pot of buttermilk on his head, "جب اس نے آپ کو دیکھا دیکھتے ہی از خود رفتہ ہو گیا' اسی
  وقت مشرف بہ اسلام ہوا" — on seeing the saint he was beside himself and became Muslim on the spot, and was
  named Ali (p. 37, folio 33).
- **The blessing carried by a garment.** Nizam al-Din Auliya is quoted as saying of Maulana Ala al-Din
  that "جو اخلاق حمیدہ اور اوصاف ستودہ پائے جاتے ہیں' وہ سب اسی جامے کی برکت ہیں", all his praiseworthy
  morals are the blessing of that garment the saint took off and put on him as a child (p. 38, folio 34,
  حاشیہ 12).
- **The Qazi's dream of prayer on the Throne.** The author reports, on خیر المجالس (اردو ترجمہ) ص 23, that
  Qazi Kamal al-Din Jafari, unsatisfied by the saint's teaching on prayer, saw in a dream that the saint
  "عرش پر مصلا بچھائے ہوئے نماز ادا کر رہے ہیں", was praying with his prayer mat spread upon the Throne;
  the saint then told him this was only the **first** of the ranks of the *fuqara*, above the ulama's
  highest ambition of muftiship, teaching, a qaziship or "منصب صدر جہانی"; the Qazi begged pardon and had
  his son made a murid (p. 38, folio 34, حواشی 13–14).
- **A death announced from a river bank.** It is related that the saint performed his ablution and told the
  people that "دہلی کے شیخ الاسلام کا انتقام[OCR?] ہو گیا' نماز جنازہ پڑھیں", the Shaikh al-Islam of Delhi
  had died[OCR?] — pray the funeral prayer; and after the prayer said "شیخ الاسلام دہلی نے ہم کو شہر سے
  باہر کیا۔ ہمارے شیخ نے اس کو دنیا سے باہر کیا", the Shaikh al-Islam of Delhi put us out of the city; our
  shaikh has put him out of the world. "بعد کو معلوم ہوا" — it was learnt afterwards that he had died at
  that very hour (p. 39, folio 35).
- **The Prophet's spirituality seen in a stone.** Nasir al-Din Mahmud Chiragh Dehlvi is quoted as
  explaining his long standing before the stone at Narnaul: "میں نے جناب سید کائنات صلی اللہ علیہ وسلم کی
  روحانیت کو اس پتھر میں جلوہ افروز دیکھا", I saw the spirituality of the Prophet manifest in that stone,
  and turned to the shaikh's grave only when the meaning left his sight (p. 41, folio 37).
- **A journey cancelled by a death.** After his petition at the Narnaul rauza, the author reports, he had
  gone only two or three stages when "بادشاہ کے انتقال کی خبر ملی", news came of the king's death, and he
  returned to Delhi instead of going to Thatta (p. 42, folio 38) — the fulfilment the saint had predicted
  for himself.
- **Runaway slaves returned by prayer, and the condition attached.** The author reports of Khwaja Mahmud
  Muina-doz that whoever's slave absconded came to him and the slave returned; in one case the man failed
  to report the return, the slave fled again, and the saint said "چونکہ پہلے خبر نہ دی تھی' اب وہ نہ
  آئے گا", since you did not report it before, now he will not come (p. 43, folio 39).
- **The loom that worked by itself.** Of Shaikh Ahmad Nahrwani: "کبھی کبھی ایسا ہوتا تھا کہ آپ اپنا کام
  کرتے کرتے از خود رفتہ ہو جاتے تھے' آپ کام بند کر دیتے تھے' لیکن کپڑا بغیر آپ کی امداد کے بنتا جاتا تھا"
  — sometimes while working he became beside himself and stopped, but the cloth went on being woven without
  his help (p. 47, folio 43).
- **A hand broken by a pir's question.** Reported as his own remark, that the accidental blow on a nail
  which broke his hand just after his pir had asked how long he would go on weaving was the old man's doing
  (p. 46, folio 42, حاشیہ 1).
- **Memory of the primordial covenant**, claimed by Bahauddin Zakariya as a schoolboy: he remembered events
  from the moment God said "الست بربکم" (p. 49, folio 45, حاشیہ 9).
- **The khirqa dream and its waking confirmation.** The author reports that he saw in a dream a house of
  light with the Prophet present, his pir standing respectfully, and many khirqas hanging on a rope; he was
  summoned, his hand taken, and the Prophet said "اے عمر! اس خرقہ کو شیخ بہاء الدین غوث العالم کو پہنا دو",
  O Umar, clothe Shaikh Bahauddin Ghaus al-Alam in this khirqa; in the morning the pir called him, "آپ نے
  مکان اسی طرح کا پایا' جیسا کہ خواب میں دیکھا تھا", he found the house exactly as in the dream, and the
  same khirqa was taken down and put on him by hand (pp. 50–51, folios 46–47, حواشی 12–13).
- **The flower on the milk** (p. 51, folio 47) is told as a wordless exchange rather than a marvel, and
  ends with the dervishes of Multan becoming his devotees.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The compiler's method is compilation with a numbered apparatus, and he shows his hand.** Every chapter
  closes with its own حواشی; almost every substantive claim in the Bahauddin Zakariya entry carries a note.
  His stock of sources here is Urdu translations of Persian tazkiras — فوائد الفوائد (اردو ترجمہ), اخبار
  الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ), خیر المجالس (اردو ترجمہ) — with سیر الاولیاء cited in the Persian, سالک
  السالکین[OCR?] (جلد دوم), جامع الکلام, and one modern secondary work, "معین الہند" از ڈاکٹر ظہور الحسن
  شارب (pp. 40, 45, 47; folios 36, 41, 43).
- **He reports variants side by side and mostly declines to choose.** "یہ بھی کہا جاتا ہے" carries an
  alternative khirqa of khilafat (p. 37, folio 33), an alternative maternal grandfather and an alternative
  ancestral line (pp. 48–49, folios 44–45), an alternative kunya (p. 49, folio 45) and an alternative dating
  of a death (p. 45, folio 41); "بعض نے … لکھا ہے" introduces two further genealogies (p. 48, folio 44);
  "یہ کہا جاتا ہے" carries a whole bai'at and khilafat (p. 41, folio 37).
- **The one place he adjudicates is a claim of descent.** After printing four accounts of Bahauddin
  Zakariya's lineage he writes: "آپ ہاشمی ہیں اور آپ کے ہاشمی ہونے میں کوئی شک و شبہ نہیں" — he is Hashimi
  and in that there is no doubt whatever (p. 48, folio 44, حاشیہ 5). Descent is the one thing in this
  chunk he treats as settled.
- **He quotes Persian and translates it himself.** The genealogical sentence "سلسلہ او باسد قریش کہ جد
  مادری حضرت امیر المومنین علی کرم اللہ وجہہ منتہی می شود" is followed by "ترجمہ:" and a full Urdu rendering
  (p. 48, folio 44) — the only place in the chunk where the book shows a source's own words and its own
  reading of them together.
- **His interest in a saint's standing is often institutional rather than mystical.** The Bahauddin Zakariya
  entry devotes a whole headed section, "عہدہ", to the office of Shaikh al-Islam (pp. 51–52, folios 47–48),
  another, "خلفاء", to succession (p. 52, folio 48), and "ذریعہ معاش" in باب 5 to a livelihood and a revenue
  dispute (p. 44, folio 40).

- "آپ کے یہاں زراعت اور تجارت بڑے پیمانے پر ہوتی تھی … آپ کا لنگر عام تھا' آپ بہت مخیر تھے۔" — "At his
  place agriculture and trade were carried on on a large scale … his langar was open to all; he was very

- "لوگ حاجت بر آری کے واسطے آپ کے مزار سے ایک پتھر لے جا کر علیحدہ رکھ دیتے ہیں' مراد پوری ہونے پر اس
  پتھر کے برابر شکر تول کر تقسیم کر دیتے ہیں۔" — "To have a need fulfilled people take a stone from his

- "جس کسی کو کوئی سخت مشکل درپیش ہو اور وہ اس روضہ کی طرف متوجہ ہو' امید ہے وہ مشکل آسان ہو جائے گی۔" —

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens inside an entry whose subject is never named here.** Pages 35–39 (folios 31–35) carry
  the end of a chapter whose heading lies in the previous chunk. The only name the surviving text gives him
  is inside a quotation from Bahauddin Zakariya — "شیخ جلال" (p. 36, folio 32). The book's own later pages
  make the cross-reference: باب 7 says "نیشاپور تک حضرت شیخ جلال الدین تبریزی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ آپ کے ہمراہ
  آئے" (p. 51, folio 47), matching this entry's Nishapur estrangement, and then tells the same
  courtesan-Gohar accusation as having been laid against "حضرت جلال الدین تبریزی" (p. 52, folio 48).
  **A human should confirm the heading from chunk 001 or the PDF before the name is attached to any of
  this material**; I have not written it into the bullets above.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** Bahauddin Zakariya's chapter breaks off at p. 52 (folio 48) inside
  "شعر و شاعری", before the promised verses. **No statement about his own mazar, its site, its urs, its
  building or its endowment occurs in this chunk** — anything of that kind will be in chunk 003, and the
  archive must not take this chunk's silence as the book's silence.
- **The accuser has two names in one book.** In the opening entry the Shaikh al-Islam who plots is
  "نجم الدین مغربی" (p. 36, folio 32); in باب 7 the same office, the same courtesan Gohar, the same five
  hundred ashrafis and the same false charge belong to "نجم الدین صغریٰ[OCR?]" (pp. 51–52, folios 47–48,
  حاشیہ 14). The second name is OCR-flagged. The two accounts also end differently: in the first the
  courtesan confesses before Bahauddin Zakariya as arbitrator and nothing is said of the office; in the
  second she retracts, the accuser loses the office, and Bahauddin Zakariya is appointed in his place.
  **Neither passage mentions the other.** Record both names and both endings.
- **The bribe does not add up.** "پانسو اشرفیوں کا لالچ" — five hundred ashrafis promised, but the
  breakdown gives 250 paid, 250 "بعد میں", later, and a further "بقیہ ڈھائی سو", remaining two hundred and
  fifty, deposited with Ahmad Ashraf the grocer: 750 against a stated 500 (p. 36, folio 32). Either a
  numeral or the word "بقیہ" is corrupt; the page carries no OCR flag here, so this needs a human eye on
  the print.
- **The one-lakh-chital disbursement is unconstruable as printed.** "اس رقم میں سے کسی کو سو اور کسی کو
  پچاس چیتل بچا" — literally "of this sum, to some a hundred and to some fifty chital … was left"; the
  following sentences require that **one** chital remained and that the customary minimum grant was five
  (p. 37, folio 33). A word appears to be missing or misread; the sums of 100 and 50 and the final single
  chital should not be cited as exact until the page is checked.
- **حضرت بابا فرید الدین مسعود گنج شکر is deliberately left unmapped, and a human should decide.** The
  chunk names him with the archive's own full form of the name and gives his pir as Qutb al-Din
  Bakhtiyar[OCR?] Kaki (p. 35, folio 31) — but it places him at **کہتوال[OCR?]**, living in the Jami Masjid
  and known locally as "قاضی بچہ", and gives **no shrine, no town of burial, no death year and no silsila
  by name**. The archive row **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** is at **Pakpattan**, Chishti, died 1266.
  On the queue's rule — a differing or absent location means no mapping — this material sits under Other
  saints. If a human accepts the identification, the pomegranate episode and the "قاضی بچہ" nickname belong
  to that row.
- **حضرت سید جلال الدین سرخ بخاری is left unmapped.** He appears only as a name in a list of Bahauddin
  Zakariya's eminent khalifas (p. 52, folio 48, حاشیہ 16): **no place, no date, no shrine**. The archive row
  **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari** (Uch Sharif, d. 1291, Suhrawardi) is the obvious resemblance,
  and the Suhrawardi link through Bahauddin Zakariya is consistent with it, but a khalifa-list name is not
  a match. Leave unmapped until the book says more.
- **"حضرت سید عثمان معروف بہ لعل شہباز سندی [OCR?]" is left unmapped.** Same list, same page. The nisba as
  printed is **سندی[OCR?]**, OCR-flagged, and the chunk gives no place, no date and no shrine. The archive
  row **lal-shahbaz-qalandar** (Sehwan Sharif, d. 1274, Qalandari) is the resemblance; note that this book
  makes him a khalifa of a Suhrawardi shaykh, which is a statement about affiliation the archive row does
  not carry — worth recording once the identification is confirmed from a fuller passage, not from this
  list.
- **On shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya, one field to reconcile rather than a conflict.** The book gives
  "7 صفر 666ھ" with age one hundred and a birth of "27 رمضان 566ھ" (pp. 49, 52; folios 45, 48) — internally
  consistent — while the archive row's `figure_died` reads **1267**. The chunk states no Common-Era year, so
  a human should confirm the era conversion rather than let either field be rewritten. The row's silsila
  (Suhrawardi) agrees with the book's "مرید اور خلیفہ" of Shihab al-Din Umar Suhrawardi, and the row's
  location (Multan) agrees with the book's "ملتان پہنچ کر مستقل سکونت اختیار فرمائی". **No conflict is
  recorded here.**
- **Two maternal grandfathers for Bahauddin Zakariya, both printed.** Maulana Husam al-Din Tirmizi of Tirmiz
  (حاشیہ 7), and Hazrat Isa of the progeny of Shaikh Jilani, resident at "ہامہ[OCR?]" (p. 49, folio 45). The
  book does not choose, and both accounts end with the family at Kot Karor[OCR?]. Any entry using his
  descent must give both.
- **642ھ is printed as the death year of two different saints in this chunk** — the unnamed subject of the
  opening entry (p. 39, folio 35) and Shaikh Muhammad Turk Narnauli (p. 41, folio 37). Not a contradiction,
  but the coincidence invites transcription error and should be verified on both pages.
- **Fakhr al-Din Abu al-Khair's alternative dating cannot be resolved inside this chunk.** "آپ کا وصال حضرت
  خواجہ غریب نواز کی وفات کے بیس سال بعد ہوا" (p. 45, folio 41, حاشیہ 4) — twenty years after Khwaja Gharib
  Nawaz's death, but **the chunk nowhere gives a year for Gharib Nawaz's death**. Do not compute one. His
  stated dates, 591ھ and 5 شعبان 661ھ with age seventy, are internally consistent as printed.
- **His shrine's place name is OCR-flagged**: "مزار پر انوار سروار[OCR?] میں" (p. 45, folio 41). The annual
  urs recorded on the same line therefore has no securely readable location.
- **The number of Bahauddin Zakariya's sons is lost**: "آپ کے ساتھ [OCR?] لڑکے تھے" (p. 52, folio 48), with
