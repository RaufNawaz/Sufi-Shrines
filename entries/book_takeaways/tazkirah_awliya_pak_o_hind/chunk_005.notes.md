# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 005 — pp. 93–109

**Three tazkirah entries, all of saints the book places in India, and no archive row among them.** The
chunk opens mid-entry on an **unnamed** saint (pp. 93–96, folios 89–92) whose mazar it puts at **پانی پت**
(Panipat); then "باب 15 — حضرت شیخ شرف الدین احمد یحیٰی منیری", chapter 15, Shaikh Sharaf al-Din Ahmad
Yahya Muniri, whose seat and place of guidance is his home town **منیر** (Munir) (pp. 97–103, folios
93–99); then "باب 16 — حضرت شیخ شرف الدین بو علی قلندر", chapter 16, Shaikh Sharaf al-Din Bu Ali
Qalandar, born at **پانی پت** and with his mazar there (pp. 104–109, folios 100–105), the entry running
past the chunk's end. **Nothing here maps to an archive id**; several names collide with archive rows and
the collisions are set out under Doubts.

**The folio markers agree with the relation on every page**: folio = PDF index − 4, p. 93 → folio 89
through p. 109 → folio 105, each marker reading exactly that.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **The unnamed saint of the opening entry** (pp. 93–96, folios 89–92). His name is nowhere in the
  surviving text (see Doubts). What the chunk states about him:
  - He told his pir he had not yet the capacity to take up the "کار منصبی", the work of office, and asked
    leave to take employment for some days; "حضرت مخدوم نے درخواست منظور فرمائی", Hazrat Makhdum granted
    the request (p. 93, folio 89).
  - He asked his pir how he would get on with "پانی پت کے شاہ ولایت حضرت شیخ شرف الدین بو علی قلندر", the
    shah-e wilayat of Panipat, Bu Ali Qalandar; the Makhdum answered that there was nothing to worry about
    — "ان کے پانی پت پہنچنے پر وہ دوسری جگہ چلے جائیں گے", on his reaching Panipat the other would go
    elsewhere (p. 93, folio 89).
  - He took service "سلطان غیاث الدین بلبن کے لشکر میں", in the army of Sultan Ghiyath al-Din Balban
    (citing سیر الاقطاب (فارسی) ص 186), kept his riyazat and worship alongside his duties, concealed his
    state, and "باوجود امارت و اعزاز کے فقر و فاقہ میں گزارتے تھے", despite rank and honour lived in
    poverty and hunger (p. 93, folio 89).
  - When a karamat appeared and "سلطان بلبن اور لشکر والے آپ کے حال سے خبردار ہوئے", Balban and the army
    learned his state, he resigned and came to **کلیر** (Kalyar) (p. 93, folio 89).
  - His pir is named as **حضرت مخدوم صابر کلیری رحمۃ اللہ علیہ** (Makhdum Sabir Kaliari) (p. 94, folio 90).
    On the fourth day after completing his pir's "تجہیز و تکفین", funeral rites, he left for Panipat and on
    arrival "ایک دیوار کے سائے کے نیچے قیام فرمایا", lodged under the shade of a wall (p. 93, folio 89).
  - He married "سرکستان [OCR?] میں" and his son's name is given as "سید احمد حوار [OCR?]" (citing سیر
    الاقطاب ص 196); "آپ تن تنہا ہندوستان تشریف لائے تھے", he came to Hindustan entirely alone (p. 94,
    folio 90).
  - Death: "آپ نے 15 جمادی الثانی 716ھ کو اس دار فانی سے سفر دارلاخرت فرمایا" — **15 Jumada al-Thani
    716 AH** (p. 94, folio 90), with a footnote giving variants: "بعض نے آپ کی تاریخ وفات 10 شعبان اور بعض
    نے 19 شعبان لکھی ہے", some have written 10 Shaban and some 19 Shaban (p. 96, folio 92, حاشیہ 10).
  - Khalifa: "حضرت شیخ جلال الدین محمد کبیر الاولیاء آپ کے خلیفہ ہیں" (p. 94, folio 90). See Doubts.
  - Character: unmatched in outward and inward knowledge, famed for zuhd and taqwa, peerless in
    "ترک و تجرید' ریاضت' مجاہدہ اور عبادت"; "وضع قطع سے قلندر معلوم ہوتے تھے اور قلندروں کا سا لباس چڑی
    پہنتے تھے", in dress and bearing he appeared a Qalandar and wore Qalandar clothing "چڑی" [word as
    printed, unflagged — see Doubts]; and "جو کچھ زبان سے فرماتے تھے ویسا ہی ہو جاتا", whatever he said
    came to pass (p. 94, folio 90).
- **حضرت شیخ شرف الدین احمد یحیٰی منیری** (chapter 15, pp. 97–103, folios 93–99): counted "مشائخ کبار
  میں", among the great mashaikh (p. 97, folio 93). Son of **محمد یحیٰی منیری** (p. 97, folio 93). His name
  is "احمد یحیٰی منیری", on his own testimony in a letter opening "یار قدیم امام نظام الدین سلام و تحیت از
  فقیر حقیر احمد یحیٰی منیری الملقب بشرف مطالعہ کند" (citing سہ صدی مکتوبات (فارسی) ص 418'480) (p. 97,
  folio 93). Titles: laqab "شرف", also called **شرف الدین** and **شرف الاولیاء** (p. 97, folio 93). He came
  to **دہلی** (Delhi) hearing the fame of حضرت نظام الدین اولیاء, meaning to take bai'at, "لیکن آپ کے دہلی
  پہنچنے سے قبل حضرت نظام الدین اولیاء وصال فرما چکے تھے", but Nizam al-Din Auliya had died before he
  arrived (citing اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 257) (p. 97, folio 93). He then took bai'at from **حضرت
  شیخ نجیب الدین فردوسی**, murid and "خلیفہ اعظم" of **حضرت شیخ رکن الدین فردوسی** — his pir's greeting
  "شرفا! بیا … سالہائے سال مشتاق تو بودیم و انتظار تو می نمودیم", come, Sharaf, for years we have longed
  for you and awaited you, is quoted from کلمات الصادقین (p. 97, folio 93). After a long stay
  "آگرہ کے راستے میں ایک جنگل میں", in a jungle on the road to **آگرہ** (Agra), he reached "اپنے وطن منیر",
  his home town **منیر**, and guided there "تادم واپسیں", to his last breath (p. 99, folio 95). **Death
  date: (not stated); burial place: (not stated)** — the entry says only "آپ نے اس جہان فانی سے سفر دار
  الاخرت فرمایا" (p. 99, folio 95). Distinguished khalifas: **شیخ مظفر بلخی** and **شمس الدین** (p. 99,
  folio 95). He wrote many books and a شرح on **"آداب المریدین"**, and "آپ کے مکتوبات بہت مشہور ہیں اور آپ
  کی کتابوں کے مقابلے میں زیادہ مقبول ہیں", his letters are more popular than his books (p. 99, folio 95);
  the book quotes his teaching from **سہ صدی مکتوبات** throughout.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A saintly jurisdiction over a named town, assigned by a pir's farman and held by one man at a time.**
  This is the chunk's most institutionally exact passage. Asked what the milk-and-flower exchange meant,
  the unnamed saint said: **"ولایت پانی پت بفرمان میرے پیرو مرشد میرے سپرد ہو چکی ہے' دوسرے کی گنجائش
  نہیں"** — the wilayat of Panipat has been committed to me by my pir's command; there is no room for
  another. Bu Ali Qalandar's rose laid on the milk meant "کہ ولایت پانی پت سے ان کا کوئی تعلق نہ ہو گا' وہ
  پانی پت میں اس طرح رہیں گے' جیسے دودھ پر پھول", that he would have no connection with the wilayat of
  Panipat and would live in Panipat as a flower sits on milk; the people asked Bu Ali Qalandar too and
  "انہوں نے بھی وہی جواب دیا", he gave the same answer (citing سیر الاقطاب ص 189) (p. 94, folio 90).
  **And the jurisdiction is then physically vacated**: "کچھ ہی دنوں کے بعد حضرت بو علی قلندر رحمۃ اللہ
  علیہ پانی پت کی حدود سے باہر تشریف لے گئے", within a few days Bu Ali Qalandar went outside the bounds of
  Panipat (p. 94, folio 90) — matched from the other side in chapter 16: "حضرت شمس الدین ترک سے پانی پت
  تشریف لانے پر آپ پانی پت سے باگھوتی [OCR?] چلے گئے", on Shams al-Din Turk's coming he left Panipat for
  Baghuti[OCR?], stayed some years, then went to live at بڑھا کھیڑہ [OCR?] (pp. 105–106, folios 101–102).
  Territorial exclusivity, delegated authority, a boundary ("حدود"), and a resulting migration, all stated.
- **Both Panipat mazars described as functioning, in the present tense.** Of the unnamed saint:
  "مزار مبارک پانی پت میں حاجت روائے خلق ہے" — his blessed mazar in Panipat is the fulfiller of the
  people's needs (p. 94, folio 90). Of Bu Ali Qalandar: "مزار انوار پانی پت میں فیوض و برکات کا سرچشمہ
  ہے" — his luminous mazar in Panipat is a fountainhead of grace and blessings (p. 108, folio 104). The
  book gives **no urs date, no custodian, no sajjada nashin, no endowment and no building date for either**
  (not stated).
- **Death at one place, burial at another.** Bu Ali Qalandar "بڈھا کھیڑہ [OCR?] میں واصل بحق ہوئے", died
  at Budha Kherah[OCR?], while the mazar is at Panipat (p. 108, folio 104). The book does not explain the
  transfer of the body.
- **Succession by khirqa and by transfer of relics held in trust.** Najib al-Din Firdausi gave Sharaf
  al-Din Yahya bai'at, "خرقہ خلافت آپ کو عطا فرمایا اور تبرکات پیران عظام جو ان کے پاس بطور امانت آپ کے
  واسطے رکھے ہوئے تھے' آپ کے سپرد فرمائے" — bestowed the khirqa of khilafat and handed over the relics of
  the great pirs, which had been kept with him **in trust (امانت) for him** (p. 98, folio 94). Relics are
  treated as a deposit passed down a chain to a designated heir, and the heir is identified before he
  arrives.
- **Named successors recorded for each saint**, the archive's usual peg for a later custodial line:
  **حضرت شیخ جلال الدین محمد کبیر الاولیاء** as khalifa of the unnamed saint (p. 94, folio 90); **شیخ
  مظفر بلخی** and **شمس الدین** as Sharaf al-Din Yahya's "ممتاز خلفاء" (p. 99, folio 95); **مبارک خاں**,
  **اختیار الدین** and **شیخ احمد** as Bu Ali Qalandar's "ممتاز مرید" (p. 108, folio 104).
- **State gifts accepted only on a third party's account.** Ala al-Din Khalji, hearing of Bu Ali
  Qalandar's "روحانی تصرفات و کرامات", wished to present gifts, but "اس کے امراء آپ … کی خدمت میں جانے سے
  گھبراتے تھے۔ ان پر آپ کا ایسا رعب تھا کہ آپ کے پاس جانے کی ہمت نہ پڑتی تھی", his amirs were afraid to
  go, such was the awe he cast; so the Sultan took Nizam al-Din Auliya's permission and sent Amir Khusrau
  with the gifts (citing سیر الاقطاب ص 186[footnote 7]) (p. 106, folio 102). The gifts were then accepted
  **conditionally**: "سلطان علاؤ الدین خلجی کے بھیجے ہوئے تحائف یہ کہہ کر کہ اگر مولانا نظام الدین کا
  واسطہ درمیان میں نہ ہوتا تو قبول نہ کرتا' از راہ نوازش و کرم قبول فرمائے" — he accepted them as a favour,
  saying that had Maulana Nizam al-Din's mediation not stood between, he would not have accepted (p. 107–108,
  folios 103–104). A clean case of a shrine's offerings mediated through another shaikh's authority.
- **A khanqah lodging a royal envoy for three days**: "امیر خسرو کو تین روز خانقاہ میں مہمان رکھا"
  (p. 107, folio 103) — the only mention of the khanqah as a place of hospitality in this chunk.
- **A saint's written instruction to a sultan, and a dispute over the address.** He sent back two letters,
  one for Nizam al-Din Auliya and one for the Sultan; the Sultan's read "علاؤ الدین فوطہ دار دہلی مقرر
  داند کہ بابندگان خدائے تعالٰی نیکو کند", let Ala al-Din, **towel-keeper (فوطہ دار)** of Delhi, know that
  he should do good to the servants of God. "سلطان علاؤ الدین خلجی کے امراء نے چہ میگوئیاں کیں کہ بادشاہ
  کو فوطہ دار لکھنا ادب کے خلاف ہے", the amirs murmured that writing to the king as "towel-keeper" was
  against adab, and the Sultan replied "غنیمت ہے کہ انہوں نے اس نام سے یاد تو کیا' یہی بہت بڑی بات ہے",
  it is a gain that he remembered me by any name at all (p. 108, folio 104).
- **Paid state service as a stage in a saint's career, and resignation as its end.** The unnamed saint's
  entry gives the sequence its own headings — "ملازمت" (employment), then "استعفی" (resignation) — with
  the resignation caused precisely by the disclosure of his sanctity (p. 93, folio 89).
- **A twelve-year preaching tenure in a named mosque.** Bu Ali Qalandar "مسجد قوت الاسلام میں وعظ کہتے
  تھے اور یہ سلسلہ بارہ سال تک جاری رہا", preached in the Masjid Quwwat al-Islam, and this continued for
  twelve years (p. 104, folio 100). The mosque's town is not stated.
- **A prescribed wird-and-wazifa office, tariffed by purpose, including one against poverty.** From Sharaf
  al-Din Yahya (pp. 100–101, folios 96–97): for a need to be fulfilled, "صبح کو سنت و فرضوں کے درمیان
  اکتالیس (41) بار سورۃ فاتحہ", forty-one Fatihas in the morning between the sunnat and the farz; "برائے
  دفع شر: سورۃ تبت یدا [OCR?] ایک ہزار بار", a thousand recitations to repel evil; for a need,
  "سورۃ انعام اکتالیس (41) بار" and "سات ہزار مرتبہ سورۃ اخلاص"; for a difficult task, "عشاء کی نماز کے
  بعد سو بار یا فتاح [OCR?]", a hundred times after Isha; and **"برائے دفع تنگی معاش: ہر رات کو سورۃ جمعہ
  پڑھے"** — to drive off straitness of livelihood, recite Sura Jumu'a every night. A named remedy for
  economic distress, transmitted through a shaikh's letters and printed with folio citations to
  سہ صدی مکتوبات.
- **A long مناجات printed in full** (pp. 101–102, folios 97–98), a supplication in Persian and Arabic
  closing on "لا الہ الا اللہ لا معبود الا اللہ …" — liturgical material attached to the shaikh's name and
  reproduced as usable text, though badly damaged in transcription (see Doubts).
- **Teaching by correspondence as an institution**: "آپ نے اپنے بعض مریدوں کو مکتوبات کے ذریعے تعلیم
  فرمائی", he taught some of his murids by means of letters (p. 99, folio 95); the same for Bu Ali
  Qalandar's letters to Ikhtiyar al-Din (p. 109, folio 105).
- **Shariat enforced on a majzub by a scholar's own hand.** Bu Ali Qalandar's moustache grew long and
  nobody dared speak; "مولانا ضیاء الدین سنائی … اس کو خلاف شریعت سمجھ کر آپ کی داڑھی پکڑی اور قینچی ہاتھ
  میں لے کر آپ کی مونچھوں کے بال کاٹے", Maulana Ziya al-Din Sanai took him by the beard and cut the hairs
  with scissors, and "آپ نے کوئی اعتراض نہ کیا", he made no objection; thereafter he used to kiss his beard
  and say it had been seized in the way of the Muhammadan shariat (p. 108, folio 104). The book's standing
  claim that qalandari conduct and shariat are compatible, made through an incident rather than an argument.
- **The graded path stated as doctrine** (p. 99–100, folios 95–96): shariat is capital, then tariqat, then
  haqiqat, and "جو کوئی بے معروفت و بے شریعت اس راہ میں قدم رکھے' بیم ہلاکت کا ہو اور کسی مرتبے پر نہ
  پہنچے", whoever sets foot on this road without gnosis and without shariat is in fear of destruction and
  reaches no station (citing سہ صدی مکتوبات ص 99'100).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The milk and the rose.** The author reports, on سیر الاقطاب ص 189, that the saint sent a bowl filled
  with milk to Bu Ali Qalandar with his salam; the Qalandar smiled, laid a rose that was beside him on the
  milk and sent the bowl back with his own salam; on its return the sender also smiled, and those present
  "حاضرین حیران تھے' ان کی سمجھ میں یہ راز و نیاز اور رمزو کنایہ نہ آیا", were bewildered and could not
  grasp the hint (p. 93, folio 89; explained p. 94, folio 90).
- **Bu Ali Qalandar knows of the arrival "بنور باطن"**, by the light of the inward, before he is told
  (p. 93, folio 89).
- **The fire test of sayyid descent.** The author reports that in ترکستان (Turkistan), before he came to
  Hindustan, a man questioned his sayyid descent and was not satisfied; angry, he said "یہ کہا جاتا ہے کہ
  سید کا بال آگ میں نہیں جلتا", it is said a sayyid's hair does not burn in fire, and proposed they both
  enter it. He leapt in and "آپ کا لباس تک آگ سے محفوظ رہا", even his clothing was preserved; the
  challenger had only come near when a flame burned his clothes, and the saint came out and extinguished
  them, after which "حاضرین کو آپ کے سید ہونے کا پورا پورا یقین ہو گیا", those present were wholly
  convinced (pp. 94–95, folios 90–91).
- **The tent that stood through the storm.** During Balban's siege of a fort, a night storm brought down
  the army's tents and put out every fire; a **بہشتی** (water-carrier), out looking for fire to heat the
  Sultan's wudu water, found the saint's tent standing, a lamp burning and the saint reciting the Quran,
  and was told "اگر آگ چاہئے تو لے جاؤ", take fire if you need it (p. 95, folio 91).
- **Water made hot by wudu.** The water-carrier found, three mornings running and each time by hiding and
  watching, that the tank's water was cold before the saint performed his ablutions and hot after — "اب اس
  کو یقین ہوا کہ یہ بس آپ کی برکت کا نتیجہ ہے", he was now certain it was the result of the saint's baraka
  (p. 95, folio 91).
- **Balban tests the miracle himself and then asks for a prayer.** The Sultan hid at the tank with the
  water-carrier, found the water hot after the saint left, "اس کا کامل یقین ہو گیا کہ آپ کامل درویش ہیں",
  became wholly certain he was a perfect dervish, went to his tent, paid his respects and asked for a
  prayer — "آپ نے دعا کی اور قلعہ فتح ہوا", he prayed and the fort was taken (pp. 95–96, folios 91–92).
- **The elixir thrown into water.** The author reports (citing روضۃ اقطاب ص 55) that Sharaf al-Din Yahya
  presented **اکسیر** to his pir, who threw it into water "آپ کا امتحان لینے کی غرض سے", to test him; the
  disciple was glad, saying "اگر چہ ازیں خاک مس زر می شد اما گرانی بردل من می آورد", although this dust
  turned copper to gold it weighed on my heart (p. 98, folio 94).
- **The writing that disclosed the earth.** His pir wrote "چند حروف", a few characters, and gave them to
  him; "جب آپ نے اس عبارت پر نگاہ ڈالی تو ہر چیز زمین کی آپ پر مکشوف ظاہر ہوئی", when he looked at the
  text everything of the earth was disclosed to him — and he returned the paper, asking that it be given
  to someone who wanted it (p. 98, folio 94).
- **The pir's life said to hang on the disciple's devotion**: "بقائے من بر ارادت تو موقوف بود" (p. 98,
  folio 94), and the disciple, a little way down the road, hears the wailing from the house and returns as
  instructed (p. 99, folio 95).
- **Ali draws Bu Ali Qalandar from the river**, and from that hour he is "مست الست" (p. 104, folio 100).
- **Twelve years standing in the river.** He threw his books into the river and stood in it so long that
  "پنڈلیوں کا گوشت مچھلیاں کھا گئیں", fish ate the flesh of his calves; he met Khizr; after twelve years a
  voice from the unseen said "شرف الدین! تیری عبادت قبول کی' مانگ کیا مانگتا ہے", and then "اچھا پانی سے
  تو نکل", come out of the water — to which he answered "خود نہیں نکلوں گا تو اپنے ہاتھ سے نکال", I will
  not come out myself; take me out with your own hand (p. 105, folio 101).
- **The dervish's summons in the mosque.** While he preached, a dervish entered and called out
  "شرف الدین جس کام کے لئے پیدا ہوا ہے۔ اس کو بھول گیا' کب تک اس قیل و قال میں رہے گا", Sharaf al-Din has
  forgotten the work he was born for; how long will he stay in this wrangling — and left (p. 105,
  folio 101). The hinge of his career as the book tells it.
- **Amir Khusrau weeps at not understanding.** Asked why he wept at the Qalandar's ghazal, he said "اسی
  وجہ سے روتا ہوں کہ کچھ نہیں سمجھا", I weep because I understood nothing, at which the Qalandar was much
  pleased (pp. 107–108, folios 103–104).
- Of the unnamed saint's master's estimate, the author quotes Makhdum Sabir Kaliari (citing سیر الاقطاب
  ص 185): **"شمس مادر اولیاء چوں شمس است"**, with the book's own gloss "(ہمارا شمس اولیاء میں سورج کی طرح
  ہے)", our Shams is like the sun among the awliya (p. 94, folio 90).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The author's method is to print the variants and decide nothing.** Bu Ali Qalandar's bai'at is given
  **five ways on one page**, each with its own hedge: "بعض کا خیال ہے" that he was murid-khalifa of
  **حضرت خواجہ قطب الدین بختیار کاکی** and "تعلیم یافتہ حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین کے عاشق تھے" [text as printed];
  "یہ بھی کہا جاتا ہے" that he was murid and khalifa of Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar Kaki; "بعض کا خیال ہے" that he
  was of **حضرت نجم الدین قلندر**; "بعض کے نزدیک" that he was of **حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین عاشق خدا**, who was
  of **حضرت امام الدین ابدال**, who was of Qutb al-Din Bakhtiar Kaki; and "ایک روایت یہ بھی ہے" that he was
  murid and khalifa of **حضرت نظام الدین اولیا**, who "آپ کو عمر کے وقت جمنا کے کنارے بیعت سے مشرف فرمایا"
  [phrase as printed], gave him bai'at on the bank of the Jamna (p. 105, folio 101, footnotes 2–6). No
  silsila is named as such for him anywhere in the entry: the affiliation is left open.
- **The same neutrality on his mother's name** — بی بی فاطمہ, "بعض کا خیال ہے" بی بی حافظہ جمال (p. 104,
  folio 100) — **and on the unnamed saint's death date**, where the text's 15 Jumada al-Thani 716 AH stands
  in the body and the variants 10 Shaban and 19 Shaban are relegated to a footnote without comment (pp. 94,
  96; folios 90, 92). Where the sibling tazkirah's editor corrects the text, this book simply lists.
- **Descent from Abu Hanifa is stated with a source; the cousinhood with Jamal al-Din Hansvi only with
  "کہا جاتا ہے"** (p. 104, folio 100) — the author grades his own claims by the strength of the formula
  rather than by argument.
- **Every entry is built on Persian tazkirah authority, page by page.** The opening entry cites
  سیر الاقطاب at eight separate points (pp. 93–96, folios 89–92); the Maneri entry runs on سہ صدی مکتوبات
  with اخبار الاخیار, کلمات الصادقین and روضۃ اقطاب (pp. 97–103, folios 93–99). Where the book quotes
  Persian it supplies its own Urdu gloss in brackets, which is the author's translation and should be cited
  as such.
- **The book's own framing of its subjects' rank** is formulaic and worth noting as editorial voice rather
  than evidence: "مشائخ کبار میں ہوتا ہے" (p. 97, folio 93), "اولیائے نامدار میں سے ہیں" (p. 104,
  folio 100), "آپ صاحب عظمت و ولایت تھے" (p. 94, folio 90).

  Panipat has been committed to me by my pir's command; there is no room for another." (p. 94, folio 90)

  milk." (p. 94, folio 90)
- "مزار مبارک پانی پت میں حاجت روائے خلق ہے۔" — "His blessed mazar in Panipat is the fulfiller of the

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and its saint is never named.** The text begins "نہ رہیں۔" at p. 93
  (folio 89), already inside a section; the chapter heading and the name stood on the previous page, which
  is not in this chunk. What the surviving text gives is: pir **حضرت مخدوم صابر کلیری**; service under
  **سلطان غیاث الدین بلبن**; arrival at **پانی پت** and the wilayat dispute with **بو علی قلندر**; marriage
  in **سرکستان [OCR?]**; death **15 جمادی الثانی 716ھ**; mazar **پانی پت**; khalifa **شیخ جلال الدین محمد
  کبیر الاولیاء**. Two hints bear on the name and neither is a statement of it: his pir's saying quotes
  **"شمس"** ("شمس مادر اولیاء چوں شمس است", p. 94, folio 90), and chapter 16 says Bu Ali Qalandar left
  Panipat on the coming of **حضرت شمس الدین ترک** (p. 105, folio 101). **A human must read the chapter
  heading on the PDF page before p. 93 before any name is attached to this entry.** The chapter number is
  likewise (not stated) here: chapter 15 begins at p. 97 (folio 93), so this entry is presumably the
  preceding chapter, but the chunk does not print its number.
- **The chunk also closes mid-entry.** Bu Ali Qalandar's chapter breaks off at "دنیا کی آرائش : آپ فرماتے
  ہیں کہ : 15" (p. 109, folio 105); the quotation, the rest of the teachings and **the whole حواشی block
  for chapter 16** are in the next chunk. Footnote numbers 1–15 are cited in this entry with none of their
  sources printed here.
- **No archive row is the subject of any entry in this chunk, and the name collisions are dangerous.**
  Every shrine the chunk locates is in India — **Panipat** twice and **Munir** once. Specifically:
  - **The "شمس" of the opening entry must not be mapped to shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari.** That row
    is Shah Shams-ud-Din Sabzwari at **Multan**, d. 1276; this saint's mazar is at **Panipat** and he died
    **716 AH**. Nor to **shamsabad** (Shams Ali Qalandar, Okara, d. 1966). Name only, nothing else.
  - **حضرت شیخ شرف الدین بو علی قلندر must not be mapped to lal-shahbaz-qalandar** (Sehwan Sharif,
    d. 1274, Qalandari) on the strength of the shared title "قلندر", nor to shamsabad. The archive has no
    row at Panipat.
  - **حضرت خواجہ قطب الدین بختیار کاکی must not be mapped to tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak.** That row is Sultan
    Qutb ud-Din Aibak at Anarkali, **Lahore**, d. 1210, a ruler, not this shaikh; the shared first two
    elements of the name make this a likely future mis-match.
  - **حضرت شیخ جلال الدین محمد کبیر الاولیاء, the khalifa named at p. 94 (folio 90), could be read into
    either shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari (Uch Sharif, d. 1291) or
    shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht (Uch Sharif, Sayyid Jalaluddin, d. 1384).** The chunk gives him
    no location, no date, no silsila and no shrine — nothing but the name and the relationship. **Leave
    unmapped.**
  - **حضرت شیخ رکن الدین فردوسی (p. 97, folio 93) must not be mapped to shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**
    (Multan, d. 1335, Suhrawardi): the nisba here is **فردوسی** and the chunk gives no place or date.
  - **حضرت شیخ جمال الدین ہانسوی (p. 104, folio 100) must not be mapped to
    shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani** (Multan, d. 1811, Chishti).
- **Sharaf al-Din Ahmad Yahya Muniri's death date and burial place are both absent.** The entry ends only
  with "آپ نے اس جہان فانی سے سفر دار الاخرت فرمایا" (p. 99, folio 95) — **(not stated)** for the date,
  **(not stated)** for the mazar, though he is said to have guided at منیر to his last breath. Do not
  supply either.
- **Bu Ali Qalandar's dates and the Panipat sequence do not sit easily together, and the book does not
  reconcile them.** He is said to have left Panipat for good on Shams al-Din Turk's arrival and to have
  died at **بڈھا کھیڑہ [OCR?]** in **724ھ** (pp. 105–106, 108; folios 101–102, 104), yet his mazar is at
  **Panipat** (p. 108, folio 104); and the saint who displaced him from Panipat died in **716ھ** (p. 94,
  folio 90), eight years before him. Separately, one of the five bai'at reports has him initiated by
  **نظام الدین اولیاء**, who in the very next chapter has died before Sharaf al-Din Yahya could reach
  Delhi (p. 97, folio 93). Record the readings; do not choose.
- **The death place is spelled two ways, both flagged**: "بڑھا کھیڑہ [OCR?]" (p. 106, folio 102) and
  "بڈھا کھیڑہ [OCR?]" (p. 108, folio 104). The other place he moved to is "باگھوتی [OCR?]" (p. 106,
  folio 102).
- **The phrase "عمر کے وقت" in the Jamna bai'at report (p. 105, folio 101) is not flagged but does not
  read**; nor does **"بتور"** in the fire karamat, "آپ بتور سے جس میں آگ روشن تھی' نکل کر باہر تشریف
  لائے" (p. 95, folio 91), nor **"چڑی"** for Qalandar dress (p. 94, folio 90), nor **"ہیٹری [OCR?]"** in
  the Qalandar's question to Amir Khusrau (p. 106, folio 102). These four words carry facts (a bai'at
  occasion, the structure holding the fire, an item of dress, a form of address) and should be checked
  against the print.
- **A footnote is cited but not printed.** The مناجات carries marker 14 (p. 101, folio 97) and the chapter's
