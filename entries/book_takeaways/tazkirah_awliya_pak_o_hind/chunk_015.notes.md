# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 015 — pp. 286–303

**The tail of one entry and three chapters, in the book's Mughal-period stretch.** The chunk runs: the
second half of an unnumbered entry on an Agra shaykh the text calls **بابا ابو العلاء / امیر ابو العلی**
(pp. 286–291, folios 282–287), ending with a thirteen-item حواشی block; **باب 56 حضرت ملا شاہ**
(pp. 292–296, folios 288–292); **باب 57 حضرت سرمد شہید** (pp. 297–302, folios 293–298); and the opening
of **باب 58 حضرت میرسید محمد کالپوی**, breaking off mid-sentence (p. 303, folio 299).

**One archive row is engaged, and it is engaged as a pir, not as a shrine** — Mian Mir of Lahore, the
murshid of Mulla Shah. Everything else the chunk stations at **آگرہ / اکبر آباد (Agra), دہلی (Delhi),
ارکساء in روستاق بدخشاں, کاشان (Kashan), ٹھٹھہ (Thatta), سورت (Surat), عظیم آباد پٹنہ (Azimabad Patna)
or کشمیر (Kashmir)**. Two saints here have dated mazars — one at **لاہور**, one at **دہلی** — and neither
has an archive row; the Lahore one is the more important gap.

**The chunk's institutional payload is unusually good**: a sajjada nashini that passes over the elder son,
a ring and a khirqa as the regalia of bai'at, a pir's ban on living in a mosque and on eating bazaar
bread, a seasonal circuit between Kashmir and Lahore, two rival khanqahs competing for one arriving
disciple in the same city, a prince as a named khalifa, and a saint's shrine created by a state execution
after a qazi al-quzat and an imperial assembly of ulama.

**Folio relation**: folio = PDF index − 4 on every page of the chunk (p. 286 → folio 282 through p. 303 →
folio 299). Every page prints a marker; there are no `[blank page]` lines in this chunk.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (حضرت میاں میر):
  - **He appears only as a murshid, stationed at Lahore, across باب 56.** Mulla Shah, moving from Kashmir,
    "کشمیر سے لاہور تشریف لائے۔ لاہور میں اپنی جستجو میں کامیاب نہ ہو سکے" — came from Kashmir to
    **لاہور** and could not succeed in his search there (p. 292, folio 288); on the road to Agra he learned
    Mian Mir's name from a stranger and "آپ کو افسوس ہوا کہ لاہور میں حضرت میاں میر سے کیوں نہ ملے" —
    regretted not having met Hazrat Mian Mir **in Lahore** (p. 292, folio 288). He returned: "آگرہ سے
    لاہور تشریف لائے… لاہور پہنچ کر حضرت میاں میر کی خدمت بابرکت میں حاضر ہوئے" — came from Agra to Lahore
    and presented himself in Mian Mir's service (pp. 292–293, folios 288–289). **This is the chunk's
    locational basis for the mapping: Mian Mir is found, and only ever found, at Lahore.**
  - **He withheld attention for three years, then set two conditions of residence and diet.** "حضرت میاں
    میر نے تین سال تک آپ کی طرف کوئی التفات نہیں برتا" — for **three years** Mian Mir showed him no
    regard; then asked "کہاں رہتے ہو؟" and on the answer "مسجد میں رہتا ہوں" told him "مسجد میں رہنا
    مناسب نہیں"; then asked "کیا کھاتے ہو؟" and on "بازار کی روٹی کھاتا ہوں" told him "بازار کی روٹی نہ
    کھانا چاہیے" (p. 293, folio 289, حاشیہ 2 = سکینۃ الاولیاء اردو ترجمہ ص 125). Only after both
    renunciations: "آخر کار حضرت میاں میر نے آپ کو بیعت سے مشرف فرمایا اور خرقہ خلافت عطا کیا" — he
    honoured him with bai'at and bestowed the **خرقہ خلافت** (p. 293, folio 289).
  - **He named his disciple and predicted his own line through him.** "آپ کے پیرو مرشد حضرت میاں میر آپ کو
    'محمد شاہ' کے لقب سے پکارا کرتے تھے" — Mian Mir called him by the laqab "محمد شاہ" (p. 292, folio 288);
    asked for whom he had prayed, he answered "ملا شاہ کے بارے میں' جس سے میرا طریقہ روشن ہو گا" — about
    Mulla Shah, **through whom my tariqa will be illumined** (p. 293, folio 289).
  - **He held a seat that ran on a seasonal calendar.** With his permission Mulla Shah "گرمی کے موسم میں
    کشمیر چلے جاتے تھے اور جاڑے کے موسم میں لاہور آ کر اپنے پیرو مرشد کی خدمت میں رہتے" — went to
    **کشمیر** in the hot season and came to **لاہور** in the cold season to remain in his pir's service
    (p. 294, folio 290).
  - **His own judgement on his disciple's austerity, quoted**: "ملا شاہ! جو ریاضت کرنے کی ہے' وہ مشائخ
    سابق میں سے کسی نے بھی نہیں کی" — Mulla Shah, the riyazat you have performed none of the former
    mashaikh performed (p. 294, folio 290, حاشیہ 3 = سکینۃ الاولیاء اردو ترجمہ ص 119).
  - **Mazar: (not stated). Death year: (not stated). Silsila: not stated of him directly**; the chunk
    places his khalifa in the Qadiri line — "قادری سلسلے کے بزرگوں میں آپ کو نمایاں درجہ حاصل ہے"
    (p. 294, folio 290) — which is the only order-name attached to this lineage here. The archive row's
    "1635" and "Alam Ganj (Dharampura)" are neither confirmed nor contradicted; its "Qadiri" is
    corroborated only at one remove. See Doubts.
  - Nothing on his urs, custodianship, endowment or buildings.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **بابا ابو العلاء / امیر ابو العلی of آگرہ** (pp. 286–291, folios 282–287), subject of the entry the
  chunk opens inside; **not mapped, because the book puts his mazar at Agra**. Bai'at: "حسب فرمان خواجہ
  غریب نواز آپ حضرت امیر عبداللہ سے بیعت ہوئے" — by the command of Khwaja Gharib Nawaz he took bai'at
  from حضرت امیر عبداللہ, who "اپنے ہاتھ سے انگوٹھی اتار کر آپ کو پہنا دی" — drew a **ring** from his own
  hand and put it on him, then honoured him with the خرقہ خلافت (p. 286, folio 282). Marriage: also "خواجہ
  غریب نواز کے حکم کے مطابق" he married the daughter of "اپنے چچا اور پیرو مرشد حضرت امیر عبداللہ" — his
  **paternal uncle and pir** (p. 286, folio 282). Two sons, "حضرت امیر فیض اللہ اور حضرت امیر نور العلا
  زاہد", described as abstinent, pious and "صاحب مقامات" (p. 286, folio 282). Death: "آپ 9 صفر 1016ھ کو
  جوار رحمت میں داخل ہوئے" — **9 Safar 1016 AH**; "مزار فیض آثار آگرہ میں مرجع خاص و عام ہے" — his mazar
  at **آگرہ** is a resort of high and low; "بوقت وفات آپ کی عمر 71 سال کی تھی" — aged **71** at death
  (p. 286, folio 282, حاشیہ 8 = اسرار ابو العلی ص 87). Character: "صاحب نسبت اور صاحب کرامت"; renowned
  for "سخاوت' عطا و بخشش"; command of outward and inward knowledge; his scholarly memorial is
  **"رسالہ فنا بقاء"** (p. 286, folio 282). His تعلیمات are printed as a three-stage doctrine of
  annihilation — **فنائی الافعال** (pp. 286–287, folios 282–283), **فنائی الصفات** (p. 287, folio 283,
  حاشیہ 9), **فنائی الذات** (pp. 287–288, folios 283–284, حاشیہ 10) — with two hadith, "من عرف نفسہ فقد
  عرف ربہ" and "عرفت ربی بربی", each given an Urdu gloss (p. 288, folio 284), and a numbered ladder
  ("اول… بعدہ…") in which the salik first treats the whole world as a mirror and finally negates his own
  existence (p. 288, folio 284). His definition of a Sufi is printed twice: "دراصل صوفی ایسے شخص کو نہیں
  کہتے کہ وہ چلہ کشی کرے' خلوتوں میں ریاضتوں میں مشغول رہے' بلکہ صوفی وہ ہے کہ جو اپنے آپ کو فنا کر دے"
  (p. 288, folio 284) and again among the اقوال (p. 289, folio 285).
- **حضرت امیر عبداللہ** (p. 286, folio 282): paternal uncle, pir and father-in-law of the above; gives the
  ring and the khirqa. **No place, no date, no silsila stated.**
- **خواجہ غریب نواز** (p. 286, folio 282): named twice, as the authority whose "فرمان" and "حکم" direct
  both the bai'at and the marriage. **The chunk gives him no town, no shrine, no dates and no silsila** —
  see Doubts.
- **حضرت امیر نور العلا** and **حضرت امیر فیض اللہ** (p. 286, folio 282): the younger and elder sons; the
  younger becomes sajjada nashin (see Practices). Amir Nur al-Ala also appears as the addressee of the
  Shahjahan-court karamat (p. 289, folio 285).
- **The five further khalifas of Abu al-Ala** (p. 286, folio 282): "حضرت خواجہ محمد عرف خواجہ فولاد'
  حضرت ملا ولی محمد [OCR?]' حضرت لاڈ خاں' حضرت میر سید کالپوری' حضرت سید دوست محمد برہان پوری".
- **حضرت ملا عمر** (p. 289, folio 285): died in a state of سماع and was revived by a glance — see Legends.
- **نواب صداقت خاں** (p. 289, folio 285): his killing "شاہجہاں بادشاہ کے دربار میں" is the event perceived
  at a distance.
- **حضرت ملا شاہ** (باب 56, pp. 292–296, folios 288–292), **whose mazar the book places at لاہور and for
  whom the archive has no row**. Opening formula: "حضرت ملا شاہ عمدۃ الاسرار ہیں" (p. 292, folio 288).
  Ancestral home "موضع ارکساء [OCR?] (علاقہ روستاق بدخشاں)"; father "ملا عبدی", **qazi of that village**;
  born there; name "شاہ محمد"; laqab "محمد شاہ" from his pir, "اخوند" from his pir-brothers; early
  education at Arkasa (p. 292, folio 288, حاشیہ 1 = سکینۃ الاولیاء اردو ترجمہ ص 116). Migration: left
  Arkasa for **کشمیر** and stayed **three years** (p. 292, folio 288); then Lahore, Agra, Lahore. Service:
  "آپ اپنے پیرو مرشد کی خدمت میں تیس [OCR?] سال رہے" — **thirty [OCR?] years** in his pir's service
  (p. 293, folio 289). Death: "آپ نے 1069ھ میں وصال فرمایا۔ مزار پر انوار لاہور میں واقع ہے" — died
  **1069ھ**, luminous mazar **at لاہور** (p. 294, folio 290, حاشیہ 4 = سکینۃ الاولیاء اردو ترجمہ ص 120).
  Khalifa: "شہزادہ دارا شکوہ آپ کے ممتاز خلیفہ ہیں" — **Prince Dara Shikoh** is his eminent khalifa
  (p. 294, folio 290). Silsila: "قادری سلسلے کے بزرگوں میں آپ کو نمایاں درجہ حاصل ہے" (p. 294, folio 290).
  Austerity: for the first **seven years** ذکر خفی from after isha to morning with حبس نفس, "پوری رات دو
  سانس میں گذرا دیتے تھے"; "تیس سال تک آپ مطلق نہیں سوئے" — for thirty years he did not sleep at all
  (p. 294, folio 290). Poet with takhallus "شاہ" and "صاحب دیوان"; one Persian ghazal printed beginning
  "نہ چراغیست دریں خانہ ویرانہ ما" (pp. 294–295, folios 290–291, heavily OCR-flagged — see Doubts). His
  تعلیمات are drawn from letters: "مکتوب بنام شہزادہ دارا شکوہ" (p. 295, folio 291, حاشیہ 5) and a bare
  "مکتوب" (حاشیہ 6), the second containing "طلب کا انجام یا علت غائی سلوک ہے اور سلوک کی انتہا معرفت۔ لیکن
  معرفت کی کوئی انتہا نہیں" (p. 295, folio 291). اقوال: "ہر ایک فرد بشر میں عرفان کی استعداد ہے" and
  "وحدت کا کام دید ہے" (pp. 295–296, folios 291–292).
- **شہزادہ دارا شکوہ** (pp. 294, 295, 296, 298; folios 290, 291, 292, 294): Mulla Shah's eminent khalifa
  and the addressee of one of his letters; "صوفی منش اور فقیر دوست" and a devotee of Sarmad, whose
  intimacy he cultivated quickly on Sarmad's arrival in Delhi (p. 298, folio 294); defeated by Aurangzeb
  and left to wander "صحرا صحرا بستی بستی" (p. 298, folio 294).
- **حضرت سرمد شہید** (باب 57, pp. 297–302, folios 293–298), **whose mazar the book places at دہلی and for
  whom the archive has no row**. "فرید دہر" and "وحید عصر" (p. 297, folio 293). Origin: Shir Khan Lodhi's
  words "املش از فرنگستان دار منی بود" [OCR?] (حاشیہ 1 = مراۃ الخیال از شیر خاں لودھی); the book then says
  "وہ ایران کے کسی خاندان سے تھے' بعض کا خیال ہے کہ آپ عیسائی تھے اور بعض کہتے ہیں کہ آپ یہودی تھے" —
  some think him Christian, some Jewish (p. 297, folio 293). Homeland "کاشان" (حاشیہ 2 = ریاض الشعراء,
  manuscript in the library of the اشیاٹک [OCR?] Society of Bengal). He accepted Islam; known as سرمد,
  in some books "سعیدائے سرمد" [OCR?]; commanded Persian, knew Arabic; **his profession was trade**
  (p. 297, folio 293). He carried Iranian manufactures to Hindustan meaning to buy jewels and sell them in
  "ایزان [OCR?]", travelling the route by which "ایران سے سیاح سندھ ہو کر ہندوستان آتے تھے" — travellers
  came from Iran **through Sindh** (pp. 297–298, folios 293–294). "جب ٹھٹھہ پہنچے ایک ہندو لڑکے پر عاشق
  ہوئے" — at **ٹھٹھہ** he fell in love with a Hindu boy, lost all care for his goods, and eventually threw
  off his clothes and went naked (p. 298, folio 294). Death: "آپ کی شہادت 1070ھ میں ہوئی" —
  martyrdom in **1070ھ**; "آپ کا مزار دہلی جامع مسجد کے نیچے فیوض و برکات کا سرچشمہ ہے" — his mazar
  **below the Jami Masjid of Delhi** is a fountainhead of blessings (p. 300, folio 296). Character: "ایک
  کامل مجذوب"; his رقعات are famous as "رقعات سرمد" and his Persian quatrains as "رباعیات سرمد", "یہ
  رباعیات شائع ہو چکی ہیں" — these have been published (p. 301, folio 297).
- **اورنگ زیب** (pp. 298–301, folios 294–297): defeated Dara Shikoh and "شاہجہان کے بجائے خود عنان حکومت
  ہاتھ میں لی" (p. 298, folio 294); sent the qazi al-quzat to Sarmad and presided at the assembly; ruled

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A sajjada nashini that passes over the elder son, with both sons then listed as khalifas.** "آپ کی
  وفات کے بعد آپ کے چھوٹے صاحب زادے حضرت امیر نور العلا آپ کے سجادہ نشین ہوئے" — after his death his
  **younger** son Amir Nur al-Ala became his **سجادہ نشین**; the list of "مقتدر خلفاء" that follows opens
  with "آپ کے بڑے صاحب زادے حضرت امیر فیض اللہ اور آپ کے چھوٹے صاحب زادے حضرت امیر نور العلا", elder
  first, then five outsiders (p. 286, folio 282). **The book states the seniority inversion without
  remarking on it or giving a reason** — the most directly citable succession fact in the chunk.
- **A ring and a khirqa as the physical instruments of bai'at and khilafat.** The pir "اپنے ہاتھ سے انگوٹھی
  اتار کر آپ کو پہنا دی' بعد ازاں آپ کے پیرو مرشد نے آپ کو خرقہ خلافت سے سرفراز فرمایا" — drew the
  **انگوٹھی** (ring) from his own hand and put it on the disciple, and afterwards honoured him with the
  **خرقہ خلافت** (p. 286, folio 282). The ring is new in this book's regalia vocabulary; earlier chunks
  had the khirqa, musalla, asa, کلاہ and مقراض.
- **An endogamous marriage ordered from outside the household.** "خواجہ غریب نواز کے حکم کے مطابق آپ نے
  اپنے چچا اور پیرو مرشد حضرت امیر عبداللہ کی صاحب زادی سے شادی کی" — by Khwaja Gharib Nawaz's order he
  married his paternal uncle's and pir's daughter (p. 286, folio 282). **Pir, uncle and father-in-law are
  one man, and the match is directed by a third shaykh** — the chunk's clearest statement of how a seat
  was kept inside a family.
- **A mazar described in visitation terms.** "مزار فیض آثار آگرہ میں مرجع خاص و عام ہے" — his mazar at
  Agra is a **مرجع خاص و عام**, a resort of elite and commonalty alike (p. 286, folio 282). No urs date,
  no attendance figure, no custodian after Nur al-Ala.
- **A khanqah with a street and a route.** Abu al-Ala's **خانقاہ** is where he sits in state (p. 289,
  folio 285), the place he is walking to "جامع مسجد سے خانقاہ" when the elephant appears (p. 290,
  folio 286), and the place at whose **دروازہ** the elephant later stands (p. 290, folio 286). A
  jama masjid and a khanqah on one walkable circuit.
- **An animal conscripted into public river transport and renamed after the saint.** He told the elephant
  "مخلوق کو پریشان کرنا اچھا نہیں' بہتر یہ ہے کہ راج گھاٹ جا کر لوگوں کو دریا پار کراؤ" — go to **راج
  گھاٹ** and take people across the river; it went there and "لوگوں کو اپنی پیٹھ پر بٹھا کر دریا پار
  اتارنے لگا"; "کچھ ہی دنوں میں وہ ہاتھی میر صاحب کا ہاتھی کہلایا" — within days it was called **"میر
  صاحب کا ہاتھی"**, Mir Sahib's elephant (p. 290, folio 286). A named ferry crossing, a service performed
  for the public, and a saint's title attaching to the asset that performs it.
- **Prescribed awrad, plainly stated.** Under "اوراد و وظائف": for cleansing the heart the zikr
  "لا الہ الا اللہ" is beneficial; "آگاہی دوام بھی ضروری ہے" — continuous awareness is also necessary
  (حاشیہ 11 = رسالہ فنا و بقاء); and "مراقبے کی فوائد [OCR?] بہت ہیں" — the benefits of muraqaba are many
  (p. 289, folio 285).
- **A pir's ban on residing in a mosque and on eating market bread.** Mian Mir told Mulla Shah "مسجد میں
  رہنا مناسب نہیں" and "بازار کی روٹی نہ کھانا چاہیے"; on the second the disciple "بازار کی روٹی کھانا
  چھوڑ دی' فاقہ کرنا شروع کیا" — gave up bazaar bread and **began to starve** (p. 293, folio 289,
  حاشیہ 2). **Both prohibitions are about where a seeker may be housed and fed before he has a seat of his
  own** — the chunk's sharpest institutional detail, and the condition on which bai'at was finally granted.
- **A seasonal circuit between two provinces, run with the pir's licence.** "آپ اپنے پیرو مرشد کی اجازت
  سے گرمی کے موسم میں کشمیر چلے جاتے تھے اور جاڑے کے موسم میں لاہور آ کر اپنے پیرو مرشد کی خدمت میں
  رہتے" — with permission, summers in **کشمیر** and winters at **لاہور** in his pir's service (p. 294,
  folio 290). A two-station annual pattern, licensed, and dated to after "جب آپ کا کام پایہ تکمیل کو پہنچ
  گیا".
- **A term of service quantified, twice.** Thirty [OCR?] years in the pir's service (p. 293, folio 289) and
  three years of being ignored before the first question (p. 293, folio 289).
- **A household with neither kitchen nor lamp.** "آپ نہایت عسرت سے گذارا کرتے تھے' آپ کے گھر میں نہ کھانا
  پکتا تھا اور نہ چراغ روشن ہوتا تھا" — he lived in great **عسرت** (straits): in his house no food was
  cooked and no lamp was lit (p. 294, folio 290). The chunk's only description of a saint's domestic
  economy.
- **A Mughal prince as a named khalifa, and as the addressee of doctrinal letters.** "شہزادہ دارا شکوہ آپ
  کے ممتاز خلیفہ ہیں" (p. 294, folio 290); his teachings are cited from "مکتوب بنام شہزادہ دارا شکوہ"
  (p. 295, folio 291, حاشیہ 5). **Royal patronage entering a Lahore Qadiri seat at the level of formal
  succession, not merely of donation.**
- **Two khanqahs in one city competing for an arriving disciple, and palanquin-bearers deciding between
  them.** In Agra "دو بزرگ ہیں' جن سے مخلوق فیض یاب ہو رہی ہے" — two elders from whom the people were
  benefitting: Mir Numan, khalifa of Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi, and Amir Abu al-Ala; Mir Sayyid Muhammad
  "پالکی میں بیٹھ کر روانہ ہوئے اور کہاروں کو حضرت نعمان کی خانقاہ پر چلنے کی تاکید فرمائی" — set out in
  a **پالکی** and enjoined the **کہار** to go to Numan's khanqah, and "کہار آپ کو بجائے حضرت نعمان کی
  خانقاہ پر لے جانے کے حضرت امیر ابو" — the bearer, instead of taking him to Numan's khanqah, took him to
  Amir Abu… (p. 303, folio 299, where the chunk ends). **A bearer's choice between two seats is where the
  chunk breaks off.**
- **A dream-instruction to take up a second silsila.** Khwaja Naqshband's order is "اس سلسلہ کو بھی اخذ
  کرو" — take up **that silsila too** (p. 303, folio 299), i.e. plural affiliation authorised from within
  an existing one; the chapter calls the subject a "ابو العلائیہ سلسلہ کے بلند پایہ بزرگ" (p. 303,
  folio 299).
- **A shrine created by a state execution, with the whole apparatus named.** The charges against Sarmad are
  four and are listed as such, the first being that he denied the bodily miraj in a quatrain, the second
  that he was Dara Shikoh's "ہمدرد اور بہی خواہ", the third that he stayed naked "جو رسم شرع کے خلاف ہے",
  the fourth that "آپ لا الہ سے زیادہ نہیں کہتے تھے" (pp. 298–299, folios 294–295). Aurangzeb sent the
  **قاضی القضاۃ ملا قوی** to investigate (p. 299, folio 295); an assembly was convened in which "علاوہ
  اورنگ زیب کے علماء فضلاء عصر بھی موجود تھے" (p. 299, folio 295); the emperor set the test himself —
  "محض برہنگی وجہ قتل نہیں ہو سکتی' اس سے کہو کہ کلمہ طیبہ پڑھے" — mere nakedness cannot be a ground for
  killing, tell him to recite the kalima (p. 299, folio 295); the ulama ruled the act kufr, required
  repentance, and on refusal "علماء نے فتوی دیا کہ قتل جائز ہے" — issued a **fatwa that killing was
  permissible** (p. 300, folio 296). The next day he was taken to the "قتل گاہ" (p. 300, folio 296). **The
  shrine below the Delhi Jami Masjid is thus the direct product of a documented imperial proceeding** —
  the fullest account of state involvement in any chunk of this book so far.
- **Influence at court, before the reversal.** "شہزادہ دارا شکوہ آپ کا معتقد تھا' غرض شاہی دربار میں آپ کا
  کافی اثر تھا" — Dara Shikoh was his devotee, and in short he had considerable influence in the **شاہی
  دربار** (p. 298, folio 294); after the defeat, Dara's associates "اپنے آپ کو خطرے میں پاتے تھے" and
  Sarmad chose not to leave (p. 298, folio 294).
- **Printing and circulation of a saint's verse, with publisher named.** "یہ رباعیات شائع ہو چکی ہیں"
  (p. 301, folio 297), and حاشیہ 7 records that "رباعیات سرمد کا انگریزی میں ترجمہ ڈاکٹر ظہور الحسن شارب
  نے کیا ہے۔ انڈین انسٹی ٹیوٹ[OCR?] آف آرٹس اینڈ لیٹرس کا سلسلہ مطبوعات نمبر 2 ہے' اسماء پبلیکیشن
  اجمیرو[OCR?] نے شائع کیا ہے" — an English translation by Dr Zahur ul-Hasan Sharib, no. 2 in the
  publication series of the Indian Institute [OCR?] of Arts and Letters, issued by Asma Publication,
  Ajmer [OCR?] (p. 302, folio 298).
- **A jogi's elixir refused and a river's sand substituted** (pp. 290–291, folios 286–287) — the economic
  frame is explicit in the jogi's complaint "افسوس میری ساری عمر کی کمائی آپ نے جمنا میں لٹائی", you have
  squandered my whole life's earnings in the Jumna, and in the measure given, "ایک رتی بھر تانبے پر ملنے
  سے تانبا سونا ہو جاتا ہے", one **رتی** rubbed on copper turns it to gold.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Bloodshed at court perceived from the khanqah.** Under "کرامات" the author relates that sitting in his
  khanqah he suddenly told Amir Nur al-Ala "کچھ ایسا معلوم ہوتا ہے کہ شاہجہاں بادشاہ کے دربار میں اس وقت
  خون ریزی ہو رہی ہے", and "تھوڑی دیر کے بعد نواب صداقت خاں کے قتل کی خبر سارے شہر میں پھیل گئی" — a
  little later the news of Nawab Sadaqat Khan's killing spread through the whole city (p. 289, folio 285,
  حاشیہ 12 = اذکار الاحرار).
- **A man dead in sama' raised by a glance.** The author relates that Mulla Umar, in a state during سماع,
  "اسی حالت میں اپنی جان شیریں جان آفریں کے سپرد فرمائی"; brought before the shaykh, "آپ نے ان پر ایک نگاہ
  ڈالی' ملا عمر اٹھ کر بیٹھ گئے۔ اور پھر حالت وجد میں رقص کرنے لگے" — he sat up at one glance and began to
  dance in wajd (p. 289, folio 285).
- **A rutting elephant halted by a look.** The author relates that against his disciples' advice to take a
  side-lane he said "بابا ابو العلاء اپنی راہ جاتا ہے' وہ اپنی راہ جائے"; when the "بدمست ہاتھی" came
  before him "آپ نے اس کی طرف بغور دیکھا' ہاتھی ایک دم رک کر کھڑا ہو گیا" and he walked past it
  (pp. 289–290, folios 285–286).
- **The same elephant put to work as a ferry** at Raj Ghat after he spoke to it at the khanqah door
  (p. 290, folio 286) — see Practices.
- **River sand that turned copper to pure gold.** The author relates that he threw the jogi's box of اکسیر
  into the Jumna and, on hearing that elixir looks "خاک سی", told him "جمنا کی یہ ریت سب خاک ہے' جتنی
  چاہیے لو"; "سادھو کو یقین نہ آیا' پھر بھی اس نے تھوڑی سی ریت بطور آزمائش لے کر تانبے پر ملی' تانبا زر
  خالص ہو گیا" — the sadhu tried a little sand on copper and the copper became pure gold, and he became
  his devotee (pp. 290–291, folios 286–287).
- **A prayer said to guarantee a lineage.** Under "پیرو مرشد کی دعا" the book reports Mian Mir's answer
  that he had prayed "ملا شاہ کے بارے میں' جس سے میرا طریقہ روشن ہو گا" (p. 293, folio 289).
- **A night passed in two breaths, and thirty sleepless years.** Printed under سیرت, not under karamat:
  "پوری رات دو سانس میں گذرا دیتے تھے۔ تیس سال تک آپ مطلق نہیں سوئے" (p. 294, folio 290).
- **An unspoken request answered before it was made.** Under "کشف و کرامات" the author relates that Dara
  Shikoh had formed the intention of asking to be made a neighbour in the hereafter as he was in this
  world, and that Mulla Shah, "بغیر ان کے کچھ کہے ہوئے ان کا ہاتھ پکڑ کر", took his hand and said "اے عزیز
  میں نے اپنے کسی مرید اور دوست سے اس قسم کا مصافحہ نہیں کیا' اور میں کہتا ہوں کہ انشاء اللہ تعالی آخرت
  میں بھی تیری مدد کروں گا" (p. 296, folio 292, حاشیہ 7 = سکینۃ الاولیاء اردو ترجمہ ص 133).
- **Foreknowledge of his own burial place.** The book states as the reason Sarmad did not flee Delhi:
  "کیونکہ وہ جانتے تھے کہ دہلی ان کا مدفن ہو گا" — because he knew Delhi would be his burial place
  (p. 298, folio 294).
- **A severed head reciting the kalima.** Under "کرامت": "شہادت کے بعد آپ کے سر سے تین بار لا الہ الا اللہ
  کی آواز سنائی دی۔ آپ کے سر نے صرف کلمہ ہی نہیں پڑھا' بلکہ کچھ دیر حمد باری تعالی میں بھی مصروف رہا" —
  three times, and it then continued a while in the praise of God (p. 301, folio 297). **The book places
  this immediately after the refusal to say "الا اللہ" at the trial** (pp. 299–300, folios 295–296) without
  commenting on the pairing.
- **A reign without rest, presented as consequence.** "اورنگ زیب نے آپ کی شہادت کے بعد قریب اڑتالیس سال
  حکومت کی' لیکن کبھی چین و سکون میسر نہ ہوا" — he ruled about forty-eight years after the martyrdom but
  never had ease, spent much of it in Deccan wars "اور وہیں انتقال کیا" (p. 301, folio 297).
- **A dream that sent a man across India.** Under "بشارت": Khwaja Naqshband appeared and ordered him to
  Akbarabad, withholding the elder's name (p. 303, folio 299).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The book stages a disagreement between three named tazkirah-writers and refuses to settle it.** "تذکرہ
  نویسوں میں اختلاف ہے کہ یہ واقعہ کہاں ہوا" — the tazkirah-writers differ over where this happened; then
  "علی قلی خاں داغستانی نے اس واقعہ کا سورت میں ہونا لکھا ہے" (حاشیہ 3), "آزاد بلگرامی نے لکھا ہے کہ یہ
  واقعہ عظیم آباد پٹنہ میں ہوا", and Shir Khan Lodhi's Persian "در اثنائے تجارت شہر تتہ افتاد [OCR?]' بر
  ہندو پسر عاشق است" (حاشیہ 4 = مراۃ الخیال) with an Urdu gloss (p. 298, folio 294). **The compiler prints
  three places for one event and adjudicates none of them** — the most methodologically explicit passage
  in the chunk. Note that his own narrative has already committed to Thatta three lines earlier ("جب ٹھٹھہ
  پہنچے", p. 298, folio 294), i.e. to Shir Khan Lodhi's version, before the disagreement is announced.
- **He does the same with Sarmad's origin**, reporting an Iranian family, a Christian and a Jewish
  possibility side by side and deciding nothing (p. 297, folio 293), and supplying a demographic note of
  his own — "ایران میں ارمنیوں کی کافی تعداد تھی' جس میں سے کچھ عیسائی تھے اور کچھ یہودی" (p. 297,
  folio 293) — which is the nearest thing in this chunk to the author's own reasoning.
- **He passes an explicit judgement on a legal proceeding.** "آپ پر جو فرد جرم لگائی گئی' وہ سیاسی آمیزش
  سے خالی نہ تھی" — the charge-sheet laid against him was not free of political admixture (p. 298,
  folio 294). He then lists the four charges neutrally and lets the emperor's own remark about nakedness
  stand (p. 299, folio 295). **This is the author editorialising, and the only place in the chunk where he
  does.**
- **The source canon shifts entry by entry and is declared page by page.** The Abu al-Ala entry runs on
  "اسرار ابو العلی" (حواشی 1–8, 13), "رسالہ فنا و بقاء" (حواشی 9–11) and "اذکار الاحرار" (حواشی 4, 12)
  (p. 291, folio 287); باب 56 on "سکینۃ الاولیاء (اردو ترجمہ)" (حواشی 1–4, 7) plus two مکتوب (حواشی 5–6)
  (p. 296, folio 292); باب 57 on "مراۃ الخیال" and "ریاض الشعراء" (حواشی 1–6) plus a modern publication
  note (حاشیہ 7) (pp. 301–302, folios 297–298). **Nothing in the chunk is presented as the compiler's own
  inquiry except the three interpretive moves above.**
- **He cites a manuscript by its holding library.** حاشیہ 2 reads "ریاض الشعراء (قلمی نسخہ اشیاٹک[OCR?]
  سوسائٹی آف بنگال کے کتب خانہ میں موجود ہے۔)" — a manuscript copy present in the library of the Asiatic
  [OCR?] Society of Bengal (p. 301, folio 297). The only such locating citation in the chunk.
- **Doctrine is printed as doctrine, in the saint's own voice, with source lines.** The three-fold fana of
  باب 55's subject is set out under its own sub-headings with "آپ فرماتے ہیں کہ" and a footnote for each
  stage (pp. 286–288, folios 282–284), and the definition of a Sufi that closes it is repeated verbatim
  among the اقوال (pp. 288–289, folios 284–285). **Repetition inside one entry, not error** — worth
  knowing before quoting.

## Citable passages

- "بابا ابو العلاء اپنی راہ جاتا ہے' وہ اپنی راہ جائے۔" — refusing to leave the road for a rutting
  elephant: "Baba Abu al-Ala goes his own way; let it go its way." (p. 290, folio 286)
- "سادھو جی! انسان تو خود اکسیر ہے' ایسی صورت میں دوسری اکسیر کی تدبیر کرنا انسان کی تحقیر ہے۔" — "Sadhu
  ji, man is himself the elixir; to contrive another elixir is to degrade man." (p. 290, folio 286)
- "ملا شاہ کے بارے میں' جس سے میرا طریقہ روشن ہو گا۔" — Mian Mir, asked for whom he had prayed: "About
  Mulla Shah, through whom my tariqa will be illumined." (p. 293, folio 289)
- "آپ نے 1069ھ میں وصال فرمایا۔ مزار پر انوار لاہور میں واقع ہے۔" — "He passed away in 1069 AH; his
  luminous mazar is located in Lahore." (p. 294, folio 290)
- "ابھی تو میں نفی میں مستغرق ہوں' مرتبہ اثبات تک نہیں پہنچا ہوں' اگر الا اللہ کہوں گا تو جھوٹ ہو گا۔" —
  Sarmad at the trial: "I am still immersed in negation, I have not reached the station of affirmation; if
  I said *illa Allah* it would be a lie." (p. 300, folio 296)

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry, and the entry's chapter number and heading are outside it.** Page 286
  (folio 282) begins at the sub-heading "بیعت و خلافت"; the next chapter heading printed is "باب 56"
  (p. 292, folio 288). **Do not assign a chapter number to the first entry from this chunk.** The subject
  is named inside the chunk only obliquely — in his own quoted speech as "بابا ابو العلاء" (p. 290,
  folio 286), in the footnote source title "اسرار ابو العلی" (p. 291, folio 287), and by باب 58's
  statement that the two Agra elders of that era were Mir Numan and "امیر ابو العلی" (p. 303, folio 299).
  The full form of his name and his titles must come from the previous chunk.
- **The chunk closes mid-sentence.** باب 58 breaks off at "کہار آپ کو بجائے حضرت نعمان کی خانقاہ پر لے
  جانے کے حضرت امیر ابو" (p. 303, folio 299). The rest of the entry and its حواشی (marker 1 appears in the
  text) fall outside this chunk. **Nothing here is the outcome of the palanquin episode.**
- **shrine-of-mian-mir: what the mapping rests on, and what it does not.** The book names "حضرت میاں میر"
  and stations him at **لاہور** repeatedly and unambiguously as the seat where bai'at is given and service
  performed (pp. 292–294, folios 288–290), and the line is called **Qadiri** (p. 294, folio 290) —
  matching the row's "Lahore, Punjab" and "Qadiri". **But the book gives him no mazar, no death year, no
  burial locality and no silsila statement of his own**, and the row's "Alam Ganj (Dharampura)" and "1635"
  are untouched. The row's figure is given as "Mian Mir (Mir Mohammad of Sind)"; **the chunk offers no
  personal name, no Sindhi origin and no alternative** to check that against. A human should confirm that
  the book's Lahore murshid of Mulla Shah is the row's saint before any date from the row is read back
  into this chunk.
- **خواجہ غریب نواز is named twice and must not be mapped.** He commands both the bai'at and the marriage
  (p. 286, folio 282) and is given **no town, no shrine, no silsila and no dates**. The known trap applies:
  the book's خواجہ غریب نواز is not `khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz` (a Miani Sahib, Lahore row for a
  saint who died in 1981). Leave unmapped.
- **حضرت ملا شاہ has no archive row and is the strongest candidate this chunk produces.** A dated Lahore
  mazar ("1069ھ", "مزار پر انوار لاہور میں واقع ہے", p. 294, folio 290), a stated Qadiri affiliation, a
  named pir already in the archive, a named khalifa (Dara Shikoh), a diwan, and a Badakhshan origin — none
  of which the 169 rows carry.
- **حضرت سرمد شہید likewise has no row, and must not be mapped to one.** His mazar is "دہلی جامع مسجد کے
  نیچے" (p. 300, folio 296) — India. The Pakistani places in his entry (**سندھ**, **ٹھٹھہ**) are a trade
  route and a love episode, not a shrine (pp. 297–298, folios 293–294).
- **A khalifa list and a chapter subject that may or may not be the same man.** Abu al-Ala's khalifas
  include "حضرت میر سید کالپوری" (p. 286, folio 282); باب 58's subject is "حضرت میرسید محمد کالپوی"
  (p. 303, folio 299), called a "ابو العلائیہ سلسلہ کے بلند پایہ بزرگ". The nisba is spelled **کالپوری**
  in one place and **کالپوی** in the other, and the personal name "محمد" appears only in the second.
