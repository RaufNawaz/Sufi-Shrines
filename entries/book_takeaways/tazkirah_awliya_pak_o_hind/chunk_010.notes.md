باب 35 حضرت شاہ عالم (p. 195, folio 191); باب 36 حضرت شیخ سعد الدین خیر آبادی (pp. 196–197,

which breaks off at the head of its خلفاء list (pp. 208–210, folios 204–206).

**No archive row is mapped in this chunk, and the `## Shrines and figures in the archive` heading is
therefore omitted.** Every shrine the chunk names is placed by the book at **جونپور (Jaunpur),
سرکھیج[OCR?] (Sarkhej), بتوہ[OCR?] (Batwa) near احمد آباد, احمد آباد (Ahmedabad), بہرائچ (Bahraich),
لکھنؤ (Lucknow), خیر آباد (Khairabad), مانکپور (Manikpur), فیض آباد (Faizabad), ناگور (Nagaur) or
کتھیل[OCR?]** — none of them an archive location. The only Pakistani places the chunk names,
**ملتان** and **ٹھٹھ[OCR?]**, appear solely as stops on one saint's itinerary and carry no shrine
claim (p. 209, folio 205). Three figures here bear names that overlap archive rows and are **not**
mapped: see Doubts, which sets out each case.

**The chunk's value for this project is heavily institutional and economic.** It contains the
clearest endowment statement so far encountered in this book — a house, a well and a garden made
waqf — and a fully narrated case of a royal cash offering refused on principle, then accepted on the
express condition that it be spent on building two shrines, with the money's actual destination
named. It also gives a mufti establishment measured in five hundred palanquins, a general langar, a
mazar-service vocation, and two territorial "ولایت" grants.

**Folio relation**: every non-blank page in this chunk prints a `[folio N]` marker, from p. 185 →
folio 181 to p. 210 → folio 206. Pages 202 and 204 are marked `[blank page]` and print no marker, so
**folios 198 and 200 never appear**. Folio numbers below are only those the chunk prints.

  اصفہان", meeting many perfect dervishes (p. 209, folio 205). Arrival in Hindustan: he reached
  **ٹھٹھ[OCR?]** and stayed one year, where "ملا سید محمد مدرس کو بیعت کیا اور خرقۂ خلافت سے سرفراز
  فرمایا"; from Thatta to **ملتان**, "وہاں حمید خان نے آپ کا شاندار استقبال کیا", where Hamid Khan
  gave him a splendid reception; from Multan to **لدھیانہ**; from Ludhiana to **پائل (سرہند کے
  قریب)**, Payal near Sirhind; from Payal to **کتھیل[OCR?]**, which he made "اپنی رشد و ہدایت کا
  مرکز" (pp. 209–210, folios 205–206). His three sons **حضرت شاہ عمادالدین، حضرت شاہ موسیٰ ابو
  المکارم and حضرت نور الدین** were "صاحب کشف و کرامات" and "ریاضت مجاہدہ اور تزکیہ نفس میں بے
  نظیر" (p. 209, folio 205). Death: "آپ کی[OCR?] وفات 19 جمادی الثانی 921ھ کو واقع ہوئی" (p. 210,
  folio 206, حاشیہ 2 — outside this chunk). "آپ کا مزار مبارک کتھیل[OCR?] میں مرجع خاص و عام ہے" —
  his mazar at **کتھیل[OCR?]** is a resort of high and low (p. 210, folio 206). **His silsila is not
  named as an order; his pir's nisba is "قادری" (p. 209, folio 205).**
- **Rulers and officials named** (no archive rows): **سلطان فیروز**, who "آپ کا معتقد و منقاد تھا",
  was Shaikh Ahmad Khattu's devotee and obedient to him (p. 188, folio 184); **ظفر خان**, "جو بعد میں
  سلطان مظفر کے لقب سے مشہور ہوا' اور گجرات کا بادشاہ ہوا", who later became famous as Sultan
  Muzaffar and king of Gujarat, also a devotee who "آپ کی بہت تعظیم و تکریم کرتا تھا" (p. 188,
  folio 184); **تیمور**, whose army arrested and then released him (p. 189, folio 185); **ملک عین
  الدین مانکپوری**, a donor (p. 198, folio 194); **غیاث الدین خلجی**, "منڈو کے بادشاہ", king of
  **منڈو** (pp. 205–206, folios 201–202); **حمید خان** at Multan (p. 209, folio 205).
- **حصہ پنجم (Part Five) opens with a Persian couplet and nothing else** (p. 203, folio 199): "چشم
  بند و گوش بند و لب بہ بند / گر نہ بینی سر حق بر من بخند[OCR?]".
- **Places named**: **دہلی** (pp. 187, 188, 189; folios 183, 184, 185), **کھٹو** (p. 187, folio 183),
  **حرمین شریف** (p. 187, folio 183), **گجرات** (pp. 188, 190, 205; folios 184, 186, 201),

  (p. 191, folio 187), **لکھنؤ** (p. 193, folio 189), **خیر آباد** (p. 196, folio 192), **مانکپور**

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A saint's whole estate made waqf, itemised: house, well and garden.** "آپ کے پاس جو جائیداد تھی،
  یعنی مکان کنواں اور باغ وہ آپ نے سرور عالم صلی اللہ علیہ و سلم کے نام پر وقف کر دی تھی" — the
  **جائیداد** (property) he had, namely **مکان** (house), **کنواں** (well) and **باغ** (garden), he
  made **وقف** in the name of the Prophet (p. 205, folio 201). **The most direct endowment statement
  in this chunk**: a named set of assets, a named dedicatee, and the term "وقف" the book's own. The
  book states no administrator, no income and no beneficiary beyond the dedication itself.
- **A royal cash offering refused, then accepted under an express building condition, with the
  destination named.** Sultan Ghiyath al-Din Khalji "نے آپ کو نذرانہ اور تحائف پیش کیے آپ نے قبول
  کرنے سے انکار کر دیا" — presented نذرانہ and تحائف, which Khwaja Husain Nagauri refused (p. 206,
  folio 202). His son wished them accepted; the saint knew it "کشف سے" and said (حاشیہ 1 = اخبار
  الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 377): "یہ سانپ ہیں، سانپ کو بھی کسی نے پالا ہے" — these are snakes; has
  anyone ever reared a snake? Seeing the son's wish persisted, he enjoined: "اگر اس میں سے کچھ لے کر
  حضرت خواجہ بزرگ اور اپنے دادا کے روضہ کو بناؤ تو لے لو، کیونکہ اس باب میں اپنے پیر شیخ کبیر سے
  سنا ہے کہ تمہارے ہاتھ زر لگے گا، جس کو تم اپنے مشائخ کے روضوں پر صرف کرو گے" — if you take some of
  it to **build the روضہ** of Hazrat Khwaja Buzurg and of your grandfather, take it, because in this
  matter I heard from my pir Shaikh Kabir that **gold will come to your hand which you will spend on
  the روضے of your mashaikh**. "چنانچہ نذرانہ قبول کیا گیا اور حضرت خواجہ غریب نواز اور حضرت صوفی
  حمیدالدین ناگوری کے مزارات پر وہ رقم خرچ کر دی" — accordingly the نذرانہ was accepted and the sum
  **spent on the mazars of Khwaja Gharib Nawaz and of Sufi Hamid al-Din Nagauri** (p. 206, folio 202).
  **The clearest shrine-finance narrative yet found in this book**: royal money, a doctrinal objection
  to receiving it, a licensing prophecy from the pir, an earmark to construction only, and two named
  shrines as the actual destination. The sum is **(not stated)**.
- **Service at a mazar as a stated vocation, and departure by the buried saint's own order.** At Ajmer
  "آپ اجمیر آئے اور دربار خواجہ غریب نواز میں مدتوں حاضر رہے۔ خواجہ غریب نواز کے مزار مبارک کی خدمت
  میں مشغول رہے، عبادت و مجاہدات کرتے رہے" — he came to **اجمیر**, remained present for long periods
  at the **دربار** of Khwaja Gharib Nawaz, and was occupied in the **خدمت of his mazar mubarak**, doing
  worship and mujahadat (p. 205, folio 201). He left only on the buried saint's authority: "خواجہ
  غریب نواز کا حکم پا کر آپ اپنے وطن تشریف لے گئے", having received Khwaja Gharib Nawaz's حکم he went
  to his homeland and there took up "تعلیم و تلقین" (p. 205, folio 201). The chunk gives the darbar
  no custodian, no urs and no revenue.
- **A general langar, and total disbursement of income.** Of Shaikh Ahmad Khattu: "جو کچھ آتا' وہ سب
  خرچ کر دیتے تھے۔ آپ کا لنگر عام تھا" — whatever came in he spent entirely; **his langar was عام**,
  open to all (p. 188, folio 184). No quantity, no funding source and no kitchen office are named.
- **A saint relocated between provinces at a ruler's request, and the shrine that resulted.**
  "سلطان مظفر کی استدعا پر آپ دہلی سے گجرات تشریف لائے اور سرکھیج[OCR?] میں بودو باش اختیار فرمائی"
  — at Sultan Muzaffar's **استدعا** (request) he came from **دہلی** to **گجرات** and took up residence
  at **سرکھیج[OCR?]** (p. 188, folio 184); and it is at Sarkhej that "آپ کا مزار… ہے" (p. 188,
  folio 184). **A royal invitation is the book's whole explanation of where this shrine stands.**
- **A نذرانہ refused, an identical sum from a different donor accepted, and the criterion stated.**
  In Husam al-Din Manikpuri's father's house "تین روز کا فاقہ تھا", there was a three-day fast; "ایک
  شخص نے نذرانہ پیش کیا' آپ نے واپس کر دیا", a man offered a نذرانہ and he returned it, at which "گھر
  والوں کو یہ بات ناگوار ہوئی", the household took it ill; "ملک عین الدین مانکپوری نے آپ کو اسی قدر
  نذرانہ بھیجا جتنا کہ وہ شخص لایا تھا", Malik Ain al-Din Manikpuri then sent him **the same amount**
  as the first man had brought, "آپ نے وہ نذرانہ قبول کیا" — he accepted that one — and told his
  household "خدا کا شکر ہے' جس نے پاک پیسہ دیا", thanks be to God who gave **clean money** (p. 198,
  folio 194). **A donor-screening rule stated in the book's own terms, with the sum held constant so
  that only the giver differs.**
- **Copying the Quran as a livelihood.** Of Maulana Jalal al-Din Manikpuri: "قرآن شریف لکھ کر گزارہ
  کرتے تھے" — he subsisted by writing out the Quran (p. 198, folio 194). The chunk's only named trade.
- **A mufti establishment measured in palanquins, its opposition to a newcomer, and its collapse.**
  "کتھیل[OCR?] میں مفتیوں کا اقتدار تھا، ان کی پانسو پالکیاں نکلا کرتی تھیں" — at **کتھیل[OCR?]** the
  **muftis held the اقتدار** (power), and **پانسو پالکیاں** (five hundred palanquins) used to go out
  for them; they set themselves to oppose Shah Kamal in every way, "بہت سے لوگ مفتیوں کے بہکانے سے آپ
  کے مخالف ہو گئے", many people turned against him at their instigation, "وہ طرح طرح سے آپ کو اذیت
  پہنچانے لگے", and began to torment him (p. 210, folio 206). After his angry words (see Legends)
  "مفتیوں کا اقتدار گرنا شروع ہوا، یہاں تک رفتہ رفتہ سب نیست و نابود ہو گئے" — the muftis' power
  began to fall until gradually all were annihilated; thereafter he taught "بلاروک ٹوک", without
  hindrance, and "آپ کا حلقہ ارادت روز بروز بڑھتا گیا", his circle of devotion grew day by day
  (p. 210, folio 206). **A contest between an established juristic establishment and an incoming
  Sufi over one town, with the establishment quantified.**
- **"ولایت" as a territorial grant, twice.** Shah Kamal's pir, pleased with his attainments, "آپ کو
  ہندوستان کی ولایت عطا فرمائی کہ ہندوستان جا کر تادم آخر رشد و ہدایت میں مشغول رہیں" — granted him
  the **ولایت of ہندوستان**, to go to Hindustan and remain occupied in guidance to his last breath
  (p. 209, folio 205). Of Shah Mina the book says flatly "آپ لکھنؤ کے صاحب ولایت ہیں" — he is the
  **صاحب ولایت of لکھنؤ** (p. 193, folio 189), with no grantor named.
- **Khilafat conferred in transit, at a one-year halt.** At **ٹھٹھ[OCR?]** Shah Kamal stayed one year
  and there "ملا سید محمد مدرس کو بیعت کیا اور خرقۂ خلافت سے سرفراز فرمایا" — took Mulla Sayyid
  Muhammad Mudarris' bai'at and honoured him with the **خرقۂ خلافت** (p. 209, folio 205). The chunk
  says nothing about what became of that seat.
- **Two shrines described as "مرجع خاص و عام".** Shaikh Darwesh Muhammad's mazar at **فیض آباد**
  (p. 201, folio 197) and Shah Kamal's at **کتھیل[OCR?]** (p. 210, folio 206). This phrase — a resort
  of high and low — is the closest the chunk comes to a statement of visitor traffic; **no urs, no
  mela, no visitor number and no offering is recorded anywhere in this chunk.**
- **Burial position used as the locating device for a shrine.** Shah Ajmal's "مزار بہرائچ میں مولوی
  شاہ نعیم اللہ کے مزار کے قریب واقع ہے" — his mazar is at Bahraich **near** the mazar of Maulvi Shah
  Na'im Allah (p. 191, folio 187). The book gives no other address for it.
- **A khanqah courtyard as the site of a named austerity.** Shah Mina "جاڑے کے موسم میں اپنے کپڑوں
  کو پانی سے تر کرکے خانقاہ کے صحن میں بیٹھ کر عبادت میں مشغول ہوتے تھے" — in the cold season he
  wetted his clothes with water and sat in the **صحن of the خانقاہ** at worship (p. 193, folio 189).
  The chunk's one use of the word خانقاہ.
- **A hujra occupied for six months at a time, and a named practice.** Shah Kamal "کو شغل میت سے
  کافی دلچسپی تھی، اس شغل میں کئی کئی مہینے گزر جاتے تھے" — had much interest in **شغل میت**, and
  many months at a time would pass in that practice; "آپ اپنے حجرے سے چھ چھ مہینے باہر تشریف نہیں
  لاتے تھے", he would not come out of his **حجرہ** for six months at a stretch (p. 210, folio 206).
- **A chilla after the pir's death, quantified.** "اپنے پیرو مرشد کی وفات کے بعد آپ نے ایک چلہ
  کھینچا' اور ایک کھجور روز کھاتے تھے۔ اس طرح آپ نے چالیس روز تک چالیس کھجوریں کھائیں" — after his
  pir's death Shaikh Ahmad Khattu drew a **چلہ** and ate one date a day: **forty dates over forty
  days** (p. 187, folio 183).
- **A relic in royal keeping, and a saint summoned to it.** Sultan Ghiyath al-Din Khalji of **منڈو**
  "نے آپ کو کئی مرتبہ بلایا، لیکن آپ تشریف نہیں لے گئے" — summoned Khwaja Husain Nagauri several
  times and was refused each time; then a **موئے مبارک** of the Prophet "کہیں سے آیا", came to the
  Sultan from somewhere; "سلطان نے آپ کو خبر کرائی، آپ منڈو تشریف لے گئے اور موئے مبارک کی زیارت سے
  مشرف ہوئے" — the Sultan sent him word, he went to Mandu, and was honoured with the ziyarat of the
  relic (pp. 205–206, folios 201–202). **A relic accomplishes what repeated royal summons could not.**
- **A ruler's family grave visited and prayed over on request.** The Sultan "آپ کی آمد اپنے لئے باعث
  خیر[OCR?] و برکت سمجھی، وہ آپ کو اپنے والد کی قبر پر لے گیا، آپ نے اس کے عرض کرنے پر اس کے لئے
  دعائے مغفرت کی" — reckoned his coming a cause of خیر[OCR?] and برکت, took him to **his father's
  grave**, and at his petition he made a **دعائے مغفرت** for the dead man (p. 206, folio 202).
- **Ordinary conveyance and self-performed labour, recorded as a virtue.** "آپ کے پاس ایک چھکڑا تھا۔
  جو آپ کی سواری کے کام آتا تھا۔ اس چھکڑے کو خود ہانکتے تھے اور بیلوں کو جو اس میں جوتے جاتے تھے۔
  خود ہی چرانے لے جاتے تھے" — he had a **چھکڑا** (bullock cart) that served as his conveyance, drove
  it himself, and himself took the yoked **بیل** (bullocks) out to graze (p. 205, folio 201). Printed
  under the heading "ساز سامان", alongside the waqf.
- **A quantified gift of food.** After Husam al-Din Manikpuri's involuntary verse, one man brought
  food and "ایک اور شخص نے ایک من ارد کی دال آپ کو پیش کی" — another presented him **one من of urad
  dal** (p. 200, folio 196). The chunk's one measured commodity.
- **Daily recitation quotas, three of them.** Surah Yasin forty-one times daily (p. 198, folio 194);
  Husam al-Din Manikpuri "ہر روز بلاناغہ پندرہ سپارے پڑھتے تھے", fifteen siparas every day without a
  break (p. 200, folio 196); and his own rule that "اگر کوئی مقام قطبیت میں پہنچے تو بھی قرآن شریف کی
  تلاوت ترک نہ کرے' کم از کم ایک سپارہ ہر روز پڑھے" — **even one who reaches the station of قطبیت**
  should not give up Quran recitation, and should read at least one sipara a day (p. 199, folio 195).
- **Rules of discipleship, stated as rules.** "مرید کو ارادت کے بعد پرانے حریفوں کے ساتھ نشست و
  برخاست نہیں کرنی چاہئے' کیونکہ وہ اس کو راستے سے بہکا دیں گے اور اس کے کام میں خلل آئے گا۔ اور
  دہلیز میں نہ بیٹھے" — after taking ارادت a murid must not sit and rise with his old companions, who
  will lead him astray and disrupt his work; and he should not **sit in the دہلیز** (threshold),
  because "شیطان صفت لوگ" will come and mislead him (pp. 198–199, folios 194–195, حاشیہ 2 = رفیق
  العارفین). On pir and murid, the patch simile: murids resemble their pirs "جیسے کپڑے میں پیوند"; the
  true murid who walks by his pir's word is "جیسے سفید کپڑے میں سفید پیوند", a white patch on white
  cloth that washes clean with the cloth, so the فیض reaching the pir reaches him and he attains
  **برخورداری**; the merely conventional murid — "رسمی مرید" — is "جیسے سفید کپڑے میں سیاہ پیوند", a
  black patch on white cloth, who receives فیض but gains little from it (p. 199, folio 195, حاشیہ 3 =
  رفیق العارفین). Of رسمی مرید: "اگر وہ نیک ہیں تو ان کی وجہ سے جانے جائیں گے اور اگر بد ہیں تو ان کے
  طفیل ان کو بخشش دیں گے' یہ دولت کم نہیں ہے' بہر حال پیر ضرور ہونا چاہیے" — **in any case one must
  have a pir** (p. 199, folio 195; on an apparent inversion in this passage, see Doubts).
- **A malfuzat collection as the khalifa's own office, named and titled.** Shah Mina's prominent
  khalifa Shaikh Sa'd al-Din Khairabadi "نے آپ کے حالات ملفوظات جمع کئے ہیں" (p. 193, folio 189), and
  his own entry names the resulting text: within "مجمع السلوک", his commentary on رسالہ مکیہ[OCR?],
  "اپنے مرشد حضرت شاہ مینا رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے حالات و ملفوظات بھی قلم بند کئے ہیں" (p. 197, folio 193).
  **The chunk documents, in two chapters, how one of its own sources came into being.**
- **A dispute over study between pir and murid, and the pir's ruling.** Shaikh Sa'd al-Din repeatedly
  submitted to Shah Mina (حاشیہ 1 = اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 396): "حضرت مخدوم کو معلوم ہے کہ اس
  کتاب کے الفاظ کی تصحیح کے لئے طبع بندہ کافی ہے۔ اور معانی کا سمجھنا خود ان کے احوال شریف کا خاصہ
  ہے' اب ملاؤں سے پڑھنا کس لئے" — my own faculty suffices to correct this book's wording, and
  understanding its meanings is a property of your own noble state; **why then read with mullahs?**
  Shah Mina answered: "بابا دیانت نہیں ہے کہ علم کے باوجود ترک تعلم کریں اور اپنے علم پر اکتفا کریں"
  — it is not honest to abandon learning because one has knowledge and rest content with one's own
  (p. 196, folio 192). **A formal ruling against a Sufi's exemption from madrasa study.**
- **A mosque as a named place of worship.** In Delhi, Shaikh Ahmad Khattu "مسجد خان جہاں میں عبادت
  میں مشغول رہتے" — was occupied in worship in **مسجد خان جہاں** (p. 187, folio 183). The chunk gives
  the building no date and no patron beyond the name.
- **Sleep-prevention devices as regular practice.** Shah Mina "رات کو دیوار پر بیٹھ کر عبادت میں
  مشغول رہتے تھے' تاکہ اگر نیند آئے تو دیوار پر سے نیچے گر پڑیں"; and if he worshipped sitting on the
  ground "اپنے چاروں طرف کانٹے رکھ لیتے تھے", he placed **thorns** on all four sides of himself so
  that he would fall on them (p. 193, folio 189).
- **Nothing on custodianship, Auqaf, land revenue, building dates or architecture.** The word "سجادہ
  نشین" does not appear in this chunk; no shrine is given a builder, a date of construction or a
  managing family, and the one construction event recorded (the نذرانہ spent on two rauzas, p. 206,
  folio 202) names no builder and no year.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A boy carried off by a windstorm to the dervish who would train him.** Under "بچپن کا ایک واقعہ"
  the author reports that Shaikh Ahmad was playing with his companions at Delhi when "آندھی کا ایک
  طوفان آیا۔ اس طوفان نے آپ کو گھیر لیا اور وطن سے دور کسی جگہ پھینک دیا" — a windstorm surrounded him
  and flung him down somewhere far from home; he wandered many days; at **کھٹو** lived a dervish
  **بابا اسحاق مغربی**, whom he met, who kept him, and in whose shade he was trained (p. 187,
  folio 183). **The book presents this, not a journey, as how he came to his pir.**
- **A plainly dressed stranger who out-answers a teaching scholar at Samarqand.** The author relates
  that at **سمرقند** he entered a mosque where a scholar was lecturing to many students, sat quietly
  to one side in very ordinary dress, and corrected a student who "حسامی[OCR?] پڑھ رہا تھا' غلط
  اعراب پڑھے", was reading حسامی[OCR?] with wrong vowel-marks; the scholar, testing him, "آپ سے علم
  اصول کے متعلق چند سوالات کئے", asked several questions on اصول, "آپ نے ہر سوال کا جواب دیا", and was
  greatly astonished (p. 188, folio 184, حاشیہ 1 = اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ ص 332). Asked "تم نے
  باوجود اتنے علم کے ایسے حقیرے کپڑے اور ٹوپی پہن رکھی ہے", he answered: "ایک تو علم' دوسرے اگر اچھے
  کپڑے پہنوں تو نفس بد خوئی کرے اس درویش نے خاص کر خود کو اس لباس میں پوشیدہ رکھا ہوا ہے" (p. 188,
  folio 184).
- **A fifteen-day warning of Timur's attack, and forty loaves in prison.** Under "کرامات" the author
  reports that Shaikh Ahmad Khattu was at Delhi when Timur attacked; "حملے سے پندرہ روز قبل آپ نے
  لوگوں کو آگاہ کیا کر دیا تھا" — he had warned people **fifteen days before** the attack, and "آپ کے
  بعض مرید آپ کے حکم سے جونپور چلے گئے", some of his murids went to **جونپور** at his order, while he
  himself stayed at Delhi; Timur's army arrested him, and "جب تیمور کو آپ کی بزرگی و عظمت کا علم ہوا'
  اس نے آپ کو رہا کیا اور آپ سے معذرت کا خواہاں ہوا" — when Timur learned of his greatness he released
  him and sought his pardon; and while he was in confinement "غیب سے چالیس روٹیاں آتی رہیں" — **forty
  loaves kept coming from the unseen**, so that neither he nor his معتقدین suffered for food (p. 189,
  folio 185).
- **A stone given three natures at a word.** Under "کرامات" the author relates that Qutb Alam went
  into water, something struck his foot, and at the blow he said "جس چیز سے چوٹ لگی ہے' کیا یہ پتھر
  ہے یا لوہا ہے یا لکڑی"; "چنانچہ اس پتھر میں تینوں چیزیں پیدا ہو گئیں" — accordingly all three came
  to be in that stone: "پتھر کی صفت بھی اس میں ہے لوا[OCR?] بھی لوہا لکڑی بھی" (p. 190, folio 186; on
  the doubled word see Doubts).
- **A son killed by his father's angry word.** The author reports that when Shaikh Qawam al-Din heard
  his estranged son was coming to ask pardon he said "میں نہیں چاہتا کہ وہ نابرخوردار میرے سامنے
  آئے", and "ان کا یہ فرمانا تھا کہ ان کے لڑکے شیخ محمد… بیمار ہوئے اور راہی ملک بقا ہوئے" — his
  saying it was enough: the son fell ill and died (p. 192, folio 188).
- **A replacement son requested of a servant, and granted.** The author reports that after that death
  Shaikh Qawam al-Din told his special servant Shaikh Qutb al-Din his wish: "میں چاہتا ہوں کہ تمھارے
  ہاں ایک لڑکا پیدا ہو' اور اس کا نام محمد مینا ہو اور فرزندوں کی جگہ نعم البدل کا حکم رکھتا ہو" — I
  wish a boy to be born in your house, named Muhammad Mina, to stand as a **نعم البدل** in place of
  sons; "چنانچہ جب پیدا ہوئے تو آپ کا نام شیخ محمد رکھا گیا" (pp. 192–193, folios 188–189).
- **A born saint who would learn only the first letter.** Under "کرامات" the author reports that Shah
  Mina was "مادر زاد ولی", a saint from birth; put in the مکتب at five, he said "الف" when told, but
  refused "ب", and when pressed answered "الف پڑھ لیا ہے' یہی کافی ہے۔ ب کی کوئی ضرورت نہیں"; he then
  expounded so many "معنی' حقائق' مضمرات و متعلقات" of "الف" that his teacher and those present were
  astonished (pp. 193–194, folios 189–190).
- **A cry of hunger, a verse, and food within the hour.** The author relates that on a day of فاقہ in
  Husam al-Din Manikpuri's house his child came crying from hunger, his heart was struck, and
  involuntarily from his tongue came "اے عجب چوں توئی ہمچو منی راز بس[OCR?]"; "ابھی کچھ دیر نہ ہوئی
  تھی کہ ایک شخص خدمت میں حاضر ہوا اور کھانا پیش کیا" — barely any time had passed before a man came
  and presented food, and another a maund of urad dal; "آپ کو افسوس ہوا کہ اتنی سی بات کے لئے آپ کی
  زبان سے ایسا کلمہ کیوں نکلا", **and he regretted having uttered such a word over so small a matter**
  (p. 200, folio 196).
- **Memorised learning erased at bai'at and replaced.** The author reports that after being honoured
  with bai'at a marked change came over him: "آپ کو بہت کتابوں کے متن یاد تھے۔ وہ سب بھول گئے" — he
  had the texts of many books by heart and forgot them all; and after discipleship "آپ کو ایسا علم
  حاصل ہوا کہ جس سے ہر چیز بخوبی سمجھ میں آتی تھی", such knowledge came to him that everything was
  well understood, so that he said "اگر کوئی چاہے تو ہدایہ کو سلوک میں لکھ دوں" — if anyone wishes, I
  will write out the **ہدایہ** in terms of سلوک (p. 200, folio 196).
- **A relic that came into his hand at a glance — reported as hearsay.** "کہا جاتا ہے کہ آپ کے دیکھتے
  ہی موئے مبارک آپ کے ہاتھ میں آ گیا" — **it is said** that as soon as Khwaja Husain Nagauri looked,
  the موئے مبارک came into his hand (p. 206, folio 202). **The one item in this chunk the book marks
  as hearsay in its own words.**
- **A son's unspoken wish read by kashf.** The author reports that his son's thought about accepting
  the نذرانہ "آپ کو کشف سے معلوم ہوئی", became known to him by kashf, and he answered it aloud
  (p. 206, folio 202, حاشیہ 1 = اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 377).
- **A saint's career predicted over him as an infant.** The author reports that حضرت فضیل[OCR?] قادری
  came to the house, was delighted at the sight of the child, and told his father: "ہادئی کامل و ولی
  عادل تمہیں ودیعت ہوا ہے، اس کی تربیت صحیح طور پر کیجئے، کیونکہ یہ بچہ اولیاء کے زمرے میں مراتب
  عالیہ پر فائز ہو گا، اس کی پرواز سدرۃ المنتہی تک ہو گئی، اس کا علم وسیع ہو گا، اور عمر دراز ہو گی"
  — a perfect guide and just wali has been given into your trust; raise him properly; this child will
  attain high ranks among the awliya, his flight will reach **سدرۃ المنتہی**, his knowledge will be
  wide and **his life long** (p. 208, folio 204).
- **A child found in meditation who vanished from his father's sight.** The author relates that when
  Shah Kamal was absent from home as usual his father went searching, found him seated in مراقبہ
  under a tree in a forest, and "آپ کو اسی حالت میں روحانی قوت کے ذریعے معلوم ہوا کہ آپ کے والد ماجد
  وہاں تشریف لائے ہیں" — in that state he knew by spiritual power that his father had come; he rose,
  his father followed him, "آپ تھوڑی دور چل کر غائب ہو گئے", and after a short distance he vanished
  (p. 209, folio 205).
- **An establishment destroyed by a sentence.** The author reports that when the muftis would not
  desist "ایک دن آپ کو غصہ آ ہی گیا اور آپ کی زبان مبارک سے یہ الفاظ نکلے" — one day anger did come
  over him and these words left his blessed tongue: "مفتیان کی جڑ اللہ شہ کمال نے پٹی"[OCR?]; after
  which their اقتدار began to fall "یہاں تک رفتہ رفتہ سب نیست و نابود ہو گئے" (p. 210, folio 206).
- **A death found, then postponed, then completed during the washing.** The author reports that Shah
  Emad al-Din, passing his father's hujra, peeped in and saw him "بے حس و حرکت لیٹے ہیں"; the door
  was taken down, and on going near "جب آپ کو دیکھا تو مردہ پایا نبض غائب تھی", they found him dead
  with the pulse gone. "غسل دیتے وقت آپ نے حرکت کی اور غسال سے فرمایا کہ 'ہمارے مرنے کی خبر تمام شہر
  میں پھیل گئی ہے'" — while being washed he moved and said to the **غسال**: the news of my death has
  spread through the whole city. The ghassal replied that it was so, "یہ سن کر آپ نے فرمایا 'اچھا ہم
  جاتے ہیں'۔ یہ کہا اور جان شریں جاں آفرین کے سپرد فرمائی" — hearing which he said "very well, we are
  going", and surrendered his soul (p. 210, folio 206, حاشیہ 1 — the note itself falls outside this
  chunk).
- **Three sons described as possessors of karamat, without a single instance.** "آپ کے تینوں صاحب
  زادوں… صاحب کشف و کرامات تھے" (p. 209, folio 205).
- **A generic statement in place of a karamat.** Of Shah Alam: "آپ صاحب کرامت بزرگ تھے" — he was a
  saint possessed of karamat (p. 195, folio 191). **باب 35 carries no کرامات heading and no instance**;
  this sentence, under سیرت, is all the book gives.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The compiler is working almost entirely from one Urdu-translated source, and says so page by
  page.** "اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ)" is cited eight times in this chunk: ص 372 (p. 186, folio 182),
  ص 332 (p. 189, folio 185), "234" without a "ص" (p. 190, folio 186), ص 328 twice and ص 329 (p. 194,
  folio 190), ص 325 (p. 195, folio 191), ص 396 (p. 197, folio 193), ص 376 (p. 200, folio 196) and
  ص 377 (p. 207, folio 203). Beside it stand only "تحفۃ المجالس" (p. 189, folio 185), "انوار العارفین
  (فارسی) ص 457" twice (p. 194, folio 190), "رفیق العارفین" twice (p. 200, folio 196) and "سالک
  السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 425" (p. 201, folio 197). **Where اخبار الاخیار is all he has, the entry
  shrinks to a page**: باب 33 and باب 38 are single pages, and باب 33 carries no footnote marker at
  all (p. 191, folio 187).
- **Entry length tracks the source, not the saint.** باب 34 (Shah Mina) runs three pages with four
  حواشی drawn from two works (pp. 192–194, folios 188–190); باب 37 (Husam al-Din Manikpuri) runs
  three pages where "رفیق العارفین" supplies the teachings (pp. 198–200, folios 194–196); باب 32 and
  باب 35, both Gujarat Bukhari sayyids, get one page each on one citation each (pp. 190, 195;
  folios 186, 191). **Absence of a heading is the book's silence, not a gap in the transcription** —
  باب 31 simply has no death date under "وفات" (p. 188, folio 184).
- **He marks hearsay once and only once.** Everything in this chunk is asserted flatly except the
  relic coming into Khwaja Husain Nagauri's hand, prefaced "کہا جاتا ہے" (p. 206, folio 202). The
  chunk contains **no variant accounts and no visible disagreement between sources.**
- **Each باب opens with a one-line epigram on the saint, in the compiler's own voice.** "حضرت قاضی
  شہاب الدین دولت آبادی باکمال درویش تھے" (p. 185, folio 181); "حضرت شیخ احمد کھٹو سر آمد اولیائے
  روزگار تھے" (p. 187, folio 183); "حضرت قطب عالم' عالم میں مشہور ہوئے" (p. 190, folio 186); "حضرت
  شاہ عالم سے عالم کی زینت ہے" (p. 195, folio 191); "حضرت شاہ مینا واقف رموز ربانی ہیں" (p. 192,
  folio 188). **These lines carry no citation and are the only places the compiler speaks unsourced.**
- **A laqab explained by a whole narrative in one chapter and merely listed in another.** باب 34
  gives a "لقب کی وجہ تسمیہ" heading and spends a full page on it, including a lexical remark —
  "'مینا' ایسا ہی لفظ ہے جیسے 'میاں' کسی کو عزت اور پیار سے پکارتے ہیں" (p. 192, folio 188) — while
  باب 40 lists "القاب" and offers nothing (p. 208, folio 204).
- **The grid of Urdu sub-headings is applied unevenly and sometimes misapplied.** Across the chunk:
  خاندانی حالات, والد ماجد, نام, لقب, القاب, لقب کی وجہ تسمیہ, ولادت, تعلیم, تعلیم و تربیت, پرورش,
  بیعت و خلافت, ریاضت و عبادات, زیارت حرمین شریف, سلاطین سے تعلقات, گجرات میں آمد, ہندوستان میں آمد,
  سیر و سیاحت, ساز سامان, نذرانہ, سیرت, فرمان, تعلیمات, اقوال, کرامات, وفات, خلیفہ, خلفاء, مرید,
  مریدین. **Two headings do not match their contents**: "وفات" in باب 31 gives a mazar and no death
  (p. 188, folio 184), and "شادی اور اولاد" in باب 40 names three sons but no marriage and no wife
  (p. 209, folio 205).

## Doubts

- **No archive row is mapped, and that is a decision, not an omission.** Every mazar this chunk
  locates is placed outside the archive's rows, and the two Pakistani towns it names carry no shrine
  claim: **ملتان** appears only as a stop where "حمید خان نے آپ کا شاندار استقبال کیا" (p. 209,
  folio 205), and **ٹھٹھ[OCR?]** only as a one-year halt where a khilafat was conferred (p. 209,
  folio 205). Nothing in the chunk engages any Multan row, and the archive has no Thatta row.
- **"حضرت سید جلال الدین بخاری" could be either of two archive rows, and the book is inconsistent
  about whether he is one person or two.** باب 32 makes him a single figure: "مخدوم جہانیاں جہاں گشت
  حضرت سید جلال الدین بخاری", whose پوتا (grandson) Qutb Alam is (p. 190, folio 186); باب 33 confirms
  the identification, "حضرت سید جلال الدین بخاری المعروف بہ مخدوم جہانیاں جہاں گشت" (p. 191,
  folio 187); but باب 35 makes them **two generations**: "مخدوم جہانیاں جہاں گشت کے بیٹے حضرت سید جلال
  الدین بخاری", Sayyid Jalal al-Din Bukhari the **son** of Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht, of whom Shah
  Alam is the پر پوتا (p. 195, folio 191). The name matches **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht**
  (Uch Sharif, Bahawalpur; "Sayyid Jalaluddin (Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht)"; died 1384; Suhrawardi)
  element for element, and the bare name also matches **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-
  jalaluddin-bukhari** (Uch Sharif; "Sayyid Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari"; died 1291; Suhrawardi).
  **He is left unmapped on two grounds**: the chunk gives him **no mazar, no town, no death year and
  no age** (only that Shah Ajmal was his murid in "چشتیہ جمانیہ[OCR?] و قادریہ و سہروردیہ", p. 191,
  folio 187, where سہروردیہ agrees with both rows and the other two do not appear in either), and the
  book's own genealogy contradicts itself between باب 32/33 and باب 35. **A human should settle which
  Uch row, if either, is meant, and should also settle باب 35's generation count**, since on باب 32's
  reckoning Shah Alam is the great-grandson of Jahaniyan himself, while باب 35's wording makes him
  one generation further down. All the material is recorded above with page references, ready to be
  carried across.
- **"خواجہ غریب نواز" here must not be mapped to `khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz`.** The chunk's
  Khwaja Gharib Nawaz is the saint whose **دربار and مزار مبارک are at اجمیر** (p. 205, folio 201) and
  on whose mazar the نذرانہ money was spent (p. 206, folio 202). The archive row of that name is
  "Darbar Hazrat Khawaja Feroz-ud-Din Gharib Nawaz Chishti Nizami", Miani Sahib Graveyard, Lahore,
  died November 27, 1981 — a different person in a different country and century. **The title alone
  is the whole of the resemblance.** No Ajmer row exists in the 169.
- **"حضرت شاہ کمال کتھیلی[OCR?]" must not be mapped to `shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal`**, and the chunk's
  own statements exclude it: he arrives in Hindustan at ٹھٹھ[OCR?], passes through ملتان and لدھیانہ
  to **پائل (سرہند کے قریب)** and thence to **کتھیل[OCR?]**, which he makes his centre, and "آپ کا
  مزار مبارک کتھیل[OCR?] میں" is where the book puts his shrine (pp. 209–210, folios 205–206). The
  archive row is at "Muslim Town / Samanabad (near Ichhra), Lahore", with no death year and silsila
  Qadiri; the chunk's saint is born "7 شوال 835ھ", dies "19 جمادی الثانی 921ھ", and has a pir with
  the nisba "قادری" (pp. 208–210, folios 204–206). **Name and Qadiri nisba agree; town, death year
  and everything else do not.** The nisba is printed "کتھیلی[OCR?]" in the chapter heading and opening
  line and "کتھیل[OCR?]" five times thereafter, all flagged.
- **The chunk closes mid-entry.** باب 40 breaks off at "آپ کے مشہور خلفاء حسب ذیل ہیں۔" (p. 210,
  folio 206): **the khalifa list itself is absent**, and so are the حواشی for باب 40, whose markers 1
  and 2 both appear in the text. Nothing here is the end of Shah Kamal's entry.
- **The chunk opens cleanly.** p. 185 (folio 181) begins with the heading "باب 30" and the saint's
  name, so no entry is left unnamed at the top.
