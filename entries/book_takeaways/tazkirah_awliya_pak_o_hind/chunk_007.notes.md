# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 007 — pp. 129–147

**The tail of one entry, the whole of باب 22, and the opening of باب 23, with the "حصہ چہارم" divider
between them.** The chunk runs: the closing pages of an entry whose chapter heading falls outside the
chunk, on a saint the book places at **پانی پت (Panipat)** (pp. 129–132, folios 125–128);
باب 22 حضرت سید جلال الدین بخاری — مخدوم جہانیاں جہاں گشت (pp. 133–142, folios 129–138); the divider
"حصہ چہارم" with a Persian couplet (p. 143, folio 139); and باب 23 حضرت میر سید اشرف جہانگیر سمنانی,
which continues past the end of the chunk (pp. 145–147, folios 141–143).

**Four archive rows are engaged, and they cluster in one family and two towns** — Uch and Multan. باب 22
is the first entry in this worker's pages to match an archive row as its own subject: the book gives the
figure's name, his birth and residence at **اوچہ (Uch)**, and his bai'at "سلسلہ سہروردی میں", all of which
agree with `shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht` (p. 133, folio 129; p. 135, folio 131). His
grandfather, his grandfather's pir and the Multan shaykh who governs his schooling supply three more.

**Everything else in the chunk is in India and is not mapped**: the Panipat saint of pp. 129–132, and
Ashraf Jahangir Simnani, whose khanqah, garden and mazar the book puts at **کچھوچھ (Kachhauchh)**, a qasba
of the Jaunpur area (p. 147, folio 143).

**The chunk is unusually rich for this project.** It carries an open langar with a daily headcount, urs
both attended and held, a sajjada nashini voluntarily handed on, a whole village bought and renamed to
clear its land revenue, a state office attached to a named khanqah, a sultan's offering refused and a
minister's offering redirected, a madrasa lodging in a shaykh's gift, a shaykh's private boat, and a
khanqah with an adjoining named garden.

**Folio relation**: every page that prints a marker prints folio = PDF index − 4 (p. 129 → folio 125
through p. 147 → folio 143). Page 144 is marked `[blank page]` and prints no marker, so folio 140 never
appears.

  "(170) ایک سو ستر سال سے زیادہ ہوئی" — more than **one hundred and seventy years** (p. 130, folio 126,
  حاشیہ 9). Five sons and two daughters; the sons "حضرت خواجہ عبدالقادر' خواجہ ابراہیم' خواجہ شبلی [OCR?]'
  خواجہ کریم الدین' خواجہ عبدالواحد", and "آپ کی لڑکیوں کی شادی کرنال کے شیخ زادوں میں ہوئی" — the
  daughters married into the **shaikhzadas of کرنال (Karnal)** (p. 129, folio 125, حاشیہ 8). His book is
  "زاد الابرار", called "آپ کے علمی ذوق کی آئینہ دار" (p. 130, folio 126, marker "11 [OCR?]"). His
  shrine, langar, urs and succession are under Practices.
- **حضرت شمس الدین ترک** (p. 129, folio 125): pir of the above, "پیر روشن ضمیر"; he is quoted at the top of
  the chunk — "اپنی نعمت اس لڑکے کی پیشانی میں تاباں دیکھتا ہوں", I see my gift shining in this boy's
  forehead — and his gaze makes the disciple dismount and lay his head at his feet, whereupon he is told
  "گھوڑے پر سوار ہو اور گھوڑے کو پھیرو" (p. 129, folio 125). **No mazar, no death date, no silsila name,
  no town** is given for him in this chunk.
- **سلطان فیروز شاہ / سلطان فیروز تغلق** (pp. 129, 130, 137, 138; folios 125, 126, 133, 134): he is the
  chunk's recurring ruler under two spellings. On hearing of the Delhi healing he said "زہے طالع من کہ در
  عہد ما ایں چنیں اولیائے عظام ہستند" — happy is my fortune, that in our age there are such great awliya
  (p. 129, folio 125); he travelled to Panipat for "قدم بوسی", presented "گلہائے عقیدت", and his
  **نذرانہ** was refused (p. 129, folio 125); at Panipat he asked "یا شیخ! شما خدائے عزوجل را دیدہ اید"
  (p. 130, folio 126); he is Makhdum Jahaniyan's devotee (p. 137, folio 133); and his wazir is Khan Jahan
  (p. 138, folio 134).
- **سلطان محمد تغلق شاہ** (p. 136, folio 132): in his reign the office of Shaikh al-Islam and the masnad of

- **وزیر خان جہاں** (p. 138, folio 134): Firoz's minister, who had imprisoned a **محرر** (clerk's) son;

- **علامہ قاضی بہاء الدین, شیخ موسیٰ, مولانا مجد الدین** (p. 134, folio 130): the teachers of Makhdum
  Jahaniyan at Uch and Multan.
- **شیخ جمال الدین خنداں اونچی / حضرت جمال الدین خنداں** (pp. 134, 141; folios 130, 137): named as "حضرت
  شیخ صدر الدین عارف کے خلیفہ" and visited by the seven-year-old Makhdum Jahaniyan with his father
  (p. 134, folio 130); estranged from and then reconciled with that father (p. 134, folio 130); **and his

  stated)**. See Doubts on his nisba.
- **شیخ عفیف الدین عبداللہ المطری / شیخ عبداللہ المطری, "شیخ مدینہ"** (pp. 135, 136; folios 131, 132): at

  "صحاح", "عوارف" and "رسائل سلوک" (p. 136, folio 132).
- **امام عبداللہ یافعی, "شیخ مکہ"** (pp. 135, 136–137; folios 131, 132–133): at Mecca, "ان سے تربیت حاصل
  کر کے کامیاب و کامران ہوئے" (p. 137, folio 133).
- **حضرت نصیرالدین چراغ دہلوی** and **حضرت نظام الدین اولیاء** (pp. 135, 137; folios 131, 133): both appear

- **حضرت میر سید اشرف جہانگیر سمنانی** (باب 23, pp. 145–147, folios 141–143), **whose khanqah, garden and
  mazar the book places at کچھوچھ (Kachhauchh) in the Jaunpur area — India, and not mapped**: "قدوۃ
  الاخیار"; of the royal house of **سمنان**, his father "سلطان ابراہیم" being "سمنان کے بادشاہ"; mother
  "خدیجہ بیگم"; one sister (p. 145, folio 141). Before his birth "حضرت خواجہ احمد یسوی [OCR?] کی روح پاک"
  told his mother a boy would be born "جو اپنے نور ولایت سے دنیا کو روشن کرے گا" (p. 145, folio 141).
  Birth: "آپ نے 688ھ میں اس عالم کو روشنی بخشی" — **688 AH** (p. 145, folio 141). Name "سید اشرف"; laqab
  "جہانگیر", given by his pir (p. 145, folio 141, حاشیہ 1). He memorised the Quran at seven "اور ساتھ قرات
  بھی سیکھی", and finished the exoteric sciences at fourteen (p. 145, folio 141). His father died

  علاء الدین بنگالی سے اپنا حصہ لے" (p. 146, folio 142). Under the heading "تخت سے دست برادری" [as
  printed] he abdicated, "عنان حکومت سلطان محمود کے سپرد فرمائی" and left for Hindustan with his mother's

  "علماء خاموش ہو گئے" (pp. 146–147, folios 142–143). At **ظفر آباد** "شیخ کبیر آپ سے بیعت ہوئے", which
  Haji Chiragh-e Hind resented; his curse failed and "شیخ کبیر جوان نہیں مرے' بلکہ کافی ضعیف ہو کر مرے"

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **An open langar with a daily headcount — the chunk's single most usable institutional line.** Of the
  Panipat saint: "لنگر آپ کا عام تھا۔ ایک ہزار آدمی روزانہ آپ کے یہاں کھانا کھاتے تھے" — **his langar was
  عام (open to all), and one thousand persons ate at his place daily** (p. 130, folio 126, حاشیہ 10).
  No source of the food, no endowment and no manager is named.
- **Urs both attended and held, in the saint's own lifetime.** "اعراس میں شریک ہوتے تھے اور خود بھی عرس
  کرتے تھے" — he took part in **اعراس** (urs gatherings) and **himself held an urs** (p. 130, folio 126).
  The chunk's only urs statement anywhere, and it describes urs as a practice of the living shaykh, not
  of a shrine after him.
- **A shrine at Panipat described as a public ziyaratgah.** "مزار پر انوار پانی پت میں زیارت گاہ خاص و عام
  ہے" — his luminous mazar at **پانی پت** is a **زیارت گاہ خاص و عام**, a place of visitation for high and
  low (pp. 129–130, folios 125–126).
- **Sajjada nashini handed on voluntarily, and forty khalifas.** "آپ کی وفات کے بعد خواجہ ابراہیم سجادہ
  نشین ہوئے۔ انہوں نے اپنی خوشی سے خواجہ شبلی کو اپنے بجائے سجادہ نشین مقرر کیا۔ آپ کے چالیس خلفاء ہیں" —
  after his death **Khwaja Ibrahim became sajjada nashin, and of his own pleasure appointed Khwaja Shibli
  sajjada nashin in his place**; he has **forty khalifas** (p. 130, folio 126, حاشیہ 10). The famous ones
  are listed: "خواجہ عبدالقادر' خواجہ ابراہیم' خواجہ شبلی' خواجہ کریم الدین' خواجہ عبدالواحد' شیخ زینا
  [OCR?]' شیخ احمد قلندر' شیخ عبدالحق ردولوی' شیخ بہرام" (p. 130, folio 126). **A documented in-family
  transfer of the gaddi from one brother to another, by the holder's own act** — rare in this book so far.
- **A whole village bought, renamed, and its land revenue cleared — the chunk's central economic
  episode.** Passing a village he found the inhabitants leaving with their goods; asked why, "انہوں نے عرض
  کیا کہ گاؤں میں ژالہ زدگی کی وجہ سے فصل خراب ہو گئی ہے' لگان ادا کرنے سے قاصر ہیں' حاکم سخت ہے" — the
  crop was ruined by **ژالہ زدگی** (hail damage), they could not pay the **لگان** (land revenue), and the
  **حاکم** was harsh. He offered: "اگر اس گاؤں کو ہمارے ہاتھ فروخت کرو اور ہمارے نام پر اس کا نام جلال
  آباد رکھو تو تمہارا لگان بھی ادا ہو جائے اور تم کو اچھی خاصی رقم بچ جائے" — **if you sell this village
  into our hands and name it جلال آباد after our name, your revenue will be paid and a good sum will be
  left over for you**; "وہ لوگ راضی ہو گئے" (p. 131, folio 127). The purchase price is realised by the
  iron-to-gold karamat; "انہوں نے لگان ادا کیا' باقی روپیہ اپنے خرچ میں لائے اور اس گاؤں کا نام "جلال
  آباد" رکھا" — they paid the revenue, spent the rest, and named the village Jalalabad (p. 131,
  folio 127). **A sale, a renaming after the purchaser, a revenue liability and a named harsh official,
  all in one transaction** — the most transferable economic fact in the chunk.
- **A sultan's offering refused at the shrine town.** Firoz Shah reached Panipat, "آپ کی خدمت بابرکت میں
  حاضر ہوا اور گلہائے عقیدت پیش کئے۔ اس کا پیش کردہ نذرانہ قبول کرنے سے آپ نے انکار فرمایا" — presented
  flowers of devotion, and **the saint refused to accept the نذرانہ he offered** (p. 129, folio 125,
  حاشیہ 7).
- **A minister's offering accepted and redirected to the victim.** Khan Jahan, Firoz's wazir, had
  imprisoned the son of a **محرر** (clerk); Makhdum Jahaniyan "انیس بار سفارش کرنے تشریف لے گئے" — went
  **nineteen times** to intercede and was not received; on the twentieth Khan Jahan sent word "اے سید تم
  کو غیرت نہیں آتی", to which he replied "اے عزیز! میں جتنی بار آتا ہوں' مجھ کو ثواب ملتا ہے۔ میں چاہتا
  ہوں کہ مظلوم کو تیرے ہاتھ سے چھڑاؤں' تاکہ تو بھی درجہ ثواب کو پہنچے"; Khan Jahan came "نگے سر' گلے میں
  رسی ڈالے ہوئے", sought pardon, became a murid, "محرر کے بیٹے کو خلعت اور گھوڑا دے کر رہا کر دیا", and
  presented a **نذرانہ** to the saint — "آپ نے وہ نذرانہ محرر کے بیٹے کو دے دیا", **which he gave to the
  clerk's son** (p. 138, folio 134, حاشیہ 14 = سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 434). A **خلعت** (robe of
  honour), a horse and a redirected nazrana, with a count of twenty visits.
- **A state religious office attached to a named khanqah, held in a named jurisdiction, then resigned on a
  shaykh's order.** "سلطان محمد تغلق شاہ کے عہد میں آپ کو منصب شیخ الاسلامی و مسند خانقاہ محمدی پیش کیا
  گیا۔ آپ نے عہدہ قبول فرمایا۔ کچھ عرصے تک منصب شیخ الاسلامی و مسند خانقاہ محمدی پر سیوستان اور اس کے
  مضافات میں فائز رہ کر اپنے فرائض بحسن و خوبی انجام دیتے رہے" — the **منصب شیخ الاسلامی** (office of
  Shaikh al-Islam) **and the مسند خانقاہ محمدی** (seat of the Khanqah-e Muhammadi) were offered to him; he
  accepted, and held them for a time **at سیوستان (Sehwan) and its مضافات (environs)**, discharging his
  duties well; then "حضرت شیخ رکن الدین ابو الفتح رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے حکم سے ملازمت سے مستعفی ہو گئے" — **he
  resigned the post on the order of Rukn al-Din Abu al-Fath** (p. 136, folio 132). **A salaried state
  office, a named khanqah as its seat, a named provincial jurisdiction, and a shrine shaykh's veto over
  it.**
- **Lodging in a madrasa held at a shaykh's pleasure.** "ملتان میں آپ شیخ رکن الدین ابو الفتح کی اجازت سے
  مدرسے میں رہتے تھے" — in Multan he lived in the **مدرسہ** by Rukn al-Din Abu al-Fath's **اجازت**
  (p. 134, folio 130). A shrine shaykh disposing of residence in a teaching institution.
- **A shaykh's private boat used to move a student between two shrine towns.** "انہوں نے آپ کو اپنی خاص
  کشتی پر سوار کرا کے اونچہ بھجوایا" — Rukn al-Din put him aboard **his own special کشتی (boat)** and sent
  him to Uch, carrying a message that reconciled the quarrel (p. 134, folio 130). Multan–Uch as a river
  route, and a boat as shrine property.
- **A Jami Masjid cleared by the town's governor during i'tikaf.** "ایک سال ماہ رمضان میں آپ نے اوچہ کی
  جامع مسجد میں اعتکاف فرمایا۔ ایک دن اوچہ کا حاکم جس کا نام سومرہ تھا' جامع مسجد میں آیا۔ آپ کے پاس بہت
  لوگ جمع ہو گئے' اس نے سب کو جامع مسجد سے باہر نکال دیا" — in a Ramadan he kept **اعتکاف** in the **جامع
  مسجد of اوچہ**; the **حاکم of Uch, named سومرہ (Sumra)**, came in and **turned everyone out of the Jami
  Masjid** where a crowd had gathered around him (p. 141, folio 137, حاشیہ 16). **A named local officer
  dispersing a shaykh's congregation in the congregational mosque of the shrine town.**
- **A mazar prescribed as the site of a cure, with a named procedure.** After Sumra was driven mad, "سومرہ
  کی ماں اس کو آپ کے پاس لائی اور آپ سے معافی کی خواستگار ہوئی۔ آپ نے اس کو ہدایت فرمائی کہ سومرہ کو غسل
  دے کر اور کپڑے پہنا کر حضرت جمال الدین خنداں کے مزار پر لے جائے۔ اور پھر آپ کے پاس لائے" — he instructed
  the mother to **bathe him, clothe him, take him to the mazar of Jamal al-Din Khandan, and then bring him
  back**; "اس کی ماں نے ایسا ہی کیا", and after his apology "آپ کی دعا سے وہ بالکل اچھا ہو گیا" (p. 141,
  folio 137, حاشیہ 16). **A three-step therapeutic circuit running through a third saint's tomb** — the
  clearest ritual prescription in the chunk.
- **A khanqah built and a named garden laid out beside it.** At Kachhauchh, "کچھوچھ میں ایک خانقاہ تعمیر
  کروائی۔ اور ایک باغ لگایا اور اس کا نام روح آباد رکھا" — he **had a خانقاہ built** and **laid out a باغ
  (garden) which he named روح آباد** (p. 147, folio 143); after his travels he "کچھوچھ آ کر خانقاہ میں
  رہنے لگے" (p. 147, folio 143). **A dated-by-sequence building act with a named garden** — the chunk's
  only construction statement.
- **The shaykh's own account of what initiation is.** "آپ جب کسی کو مرید کرتے تو فرماتے کہ "میں صرف وکیل
  ہوں"" — whenever he made someone a murid he would say **"I am only a وکیل (deputy)"** (p. 138,
  folio 134).
- **Two kinds of khirqa distinguished, one of them explicitly a relic.** "خرقہ دو طرح کا ہے۔ خرقہ تصوف اور
  خرقہ تشبہ خرقہ تصوف خرقہ صحبت ہے اور خرقہ تشبہ خرقہ تبرک کو کہتے ہیں' اس میں صحبت کی شرط نہیں" — the
  **خرقہ تصوف** is the robe of **صحبت** (companionship); the **خرقہ تشبہ** is what is called the **خرقہ
  تبرک** (robe of blessing), **in which companionship is not a condition** (p. 138, folio 134). **The
  book's own doctrinal warrant for relic-robes handed out without discipleship** — directly relevant to
  how khirqas circulate in this chunk (pp. 135, 146; folios 131, 142).
- **Three conditions laid down for being a shaykh at all.** "مشیخیت کے لئے تین شرطیں ہیں۔ اگر یہ تینوں نہ
  ہوں تو مشیخیت درست نہیں۔ اول شرط یہ ہے کہ وہ تین علم یعنی شریعت' و حقیقت کا عالم ہو۔ دوسری شرط یہ ہے کہ
  اس زمانے کے علماء اس کو قبول کریں اور اس کے معتقد مرید ہوں۔ تیسری شرط یہ ہے کہ اس کو سوائے حق تعالیٰ کے
  اور کوئی طلب نہ ہو" — for **مشیخیت** three conditions, without all three of which it is not valid:
  learning in the three sciences; **that the ulama of the age accept him and his devotees be his murids**;
  and that he seek nothing but God (p. 139, folio 135). **The second condition makes scholarly recognition
  constitutive of shrine authority.** The list promises three sciences and names two — see Doubts.
- **A named daily wird.** "یاحیی یاقیوم اسم اعظم ہے" — Ya Hayy, Ya Qayyum is the Greatest Name (p. 140,
  folio 136).
- **A saint's karamat described as redistribution.** "یہ کہا جاتا ہے کہ حضرت مخدوم علاؤ الدین علی احمد
  صابر کی کمائی حضرت شیخ جلال الدین کبیر الاولیاء نے لٹائی" — it is said that **the earnings (کمائی) of
  Makhdum Ala al-Din Ali Ahmad Sabir were lavished away (لٹائی) by Shaikh Jalal al-Din Kabir al-Auliya**
  (p. 130, folio 126). A transfer of spiritual capital stated in the language of money.
- **Sama and hunting as habits of a shrine's founder.** "سماع سنتے تھے۔ شکار کا شوق تھا" — he listened to
  sama and was fond of hunting (p. 130, folio 126); compare the sama contradiction recorded for Makhdum
  Jahaniyan (p. 137, folio 133).
- **A named chain of teaching materials at Medina.** "صحاح", "عوارف" and "رسائل سلوک" were the lesson texts
  given nightly after tahajjud (p. 136, folio 132); and out of respect, leading the prayer in the
  Prophet's mosque in the imam's absence, "آپ بپاس ادب اس جگہ سے جہاں سرور عالم صلی اللہ علیہ وسلم کھڑے
  ہوتے تھے۔ ایک صف پیچھے کھڑے ہوئے" — he stood **one row behind** the Prophet's standing-place (p. 136,
  folio 132).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A gaze that made a rider dismount.** The author reports that as soon as Shams al-Din Turk said "اپنی
  نعمت اس لڑکے کی پیشانی میں تاباں دیکھتا ہوں" and his eye fell on him, "آپ بے اختیار ہو گئے۔ گھوڑے سے
  اترے اور سر نیاز ان کے قدموں پر رکھا" (p. 129, folio 125).
- **A life extended by a gift of years, and Delhi reached in a moment.** The author relates under "کشف و
  کرامات" that he knew of Makhdum Jahaniyan Jahan Gasht's illness "بنور باطن", reached **دہلی** "بقوت
  روحانی ایک ساعت میں", found him "حالت نزع میں", prayed for his health, "اپنی عمر کے چند سال ان کو دے
  کر" gave him some years of his own life, and returned to Panipat in a moment (p. 131, folio 127). The
  same event is told once earlier as Firoz Shah's information (p. 129, folio 125, حاشیہ 7) — see Doubts on
  the two wordings.
- **Iron turned to pure gold to buy a village.** The author relates that he had the villagers fetch "لوہا
  اور لوہے کا سامان", gather wood, burn the wood and throw the iron in, and come back in the morning; he
  left the village that night, and "صبح کو جب گاؤں والے وہاں گئے تو ان کو دیکھ کر حیرت ہوئی کہ سارا لوہا
  زر خالص تھا" — all the iron was **pure gold** (p. 131, folio 127).
- **A jogi's philosopher's stone thrown into the river and multiplied.** The author relates that a jogi
  sitting with closed eyes by the river opened them and gave him a "سنگ پارس"; he threw it into the river;
  told to fetch his own stone back and no other, the jogi "وہاں ہزاروں اس قسم کے پتھر پائے", found
  thousands of the same kind, brought out a second in concealment, was named for it, laid both before him
  "اور آپ کا مرید ہوا" (p. 131, folio 127).
- **A silsila continued by his prayer.** "آپ کی دعا سے حضرت جمال الدین ہانسوی کا سلسلہ جاری ہوا" — by his
  prayer the silsila of Jamal al-Din Hansvi continued (p. 132, folio 128).
- **A cell turned to gold, and alchemy refused.** The author relates that an "کیمیاگر" (alchemist) came to
  one of his sons, saw his "فقر و فاقہ" and promised to teach him alchemy; when the son mentioned it,
  "آپ نے حجرے کی دیوار پر تھوکا' حجرہ سونے کا ہو گیا", he spat on the wall of the **حجرہ** and it turned to
  gold, and told his son "کیمیائے سعادت سیکھنا بہتر ہے" — better to learn the alchemy of felicity
  (p. 132, folio 128, حاشیہ 12 = سیر الاقطاب (فارسی) ص 209).
- **An estatic absence broken by a threefold call.** The author reports that in his last years his
  **استغراق** was such that at prayer time "آپ کے کان میں تین مرتبہ حق' حق' حق کہا جاتا' تب آپ کو ہوش
  ہوتا" — "Haqq, Haqq, Haqq" had to be said three times in his ear before he came to himself (p. 130,
  folio 126).
- **Sanctity conferred by a look, stated as a general property.** "جس پر آپ کی نظر پڑتی' وہ ولی ہو جاتا۔
  آپ کے فیض کا چشمہ جاری تھا" — whoever he looked at became a wali; and "آپ جو فرماتے' وہی اور ویسا ہوتا"
  (p. 130, folio 126).
- **God's shadow seen, but not God.** Asked by Firoz Shah "یا شیخ! شما خدائے عزوجل را دیدہ اید" — have you
  seen God? — he answered "دیدن حق سبحان تعالیٰ بدیں چشم سرہ [OCR?] امر شرع شریف محالست و لیکن سایہ حق
  تعالیٰ را دیدہ ام", glossed "(دیکھنا حق سبحان تعالیٰ کا اس چشم سرہ [OCR?] امر شرع شریف محال ہے' لیکن
  میں نے سایہ حق تعالیٰ کو دیکھا ہے)" — impossible with this eye under the holy law, **but I have seen the
  shadow of the Truth** (pp. 130–131, folios 126–127).
- **A child's death foretold from a distraction in prayer.** The author relates that his four-year-old
  youngest son played near his musalla during the چاشت prayer and broke his concentration; afterwards he
  told "سید شمس الدین": "مجھے اس لڑکے کا زندہ رہنا مشکل نظر آتا ہے' کیوں کہ عین نماز میں میری طبیعت اس کی
  طرف مائل تھی"; "ظہر کے وقت اس لڑکے کو بخار ہوا اور اسی رات اس کا انتقال ہو گیا" — the boy took fever at
  Zuhr and died that night (pp. 140–141, folios 136–137).
- **Rain prayed for, then prayed away.** The author relates, on the authority of تاریخ فرشتہ (فارسی)
  ص 416, that in Delhi people distressed by drought sought his prayer; "اتنی بارش ہوئی کہ لوگوں نے عاجز ہو
  کر بارش کے بند ہونے کی آپ سے دعا کرائی' آپ نے دعا کی اور بارش رک گئی" — so much rain fell that they had
  to have him pray for it to stop, and it stopped (p. 141, folio 137).
- **A governor driven mad and restored.** The author relates, on the authority of سالک السالکین (جلد دوم)
  ص 436, that on being told "اے سومرہ! کیا تو دیوانہ ہو گیا ہے کہ درویشوں کو تنگ کرتا ہے", "آپ کا یہ
  فرمانا تھا کہ سومرہ دیوانہ ہو گیا" — Sumra went mad at the word; and after the mazar circuit and his
  apology, "آپ کی دعا سے وہ بالکل اچھا ہو گیا" (p. 141, folio 137).
- **A gift absorbed by embrace.** "آپ کے متعلق یہ کہا جاتا ہے کہ" — it is said of him — "آپ جب کبھی کسی سے
  معانقہ فرماتے تو جو نعمت اس کے پاس ہوتی' اسی وقت جذب کر لیتے"; the book then glosses this as a matter of
  attention and service, "یعنی آپ اس قدر توجہ اور خدمت سے کام لیتے کہ وہ شخص بے اختیار ہو کر آپ کو اپنی ہر
  نعمت دے دیتا" (pp. 134–135, folios 130–131, حاشیہ 3 = اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 296).
- **A greeting answered from the grave at Medina.** The author reports that he said "السلام علیک یا جدی"
  and "وہاں سے آپ کو جواب ملا: "وعلیک السلام یا ولدی"" — the reply came from there (p. 136, folio 132,
  حاشیہ 6 = سیر العارفین).
- **A birth announced by a saint's spirit.** The author reports that before Ashraf Jahangir's birth
  "حضرت خواجہ احمد یسوی [OCR?] کی روح پاک نے آپ کی والدہ ماجدہ کو مطلع کیا تھا" that a boy would be born
  who "اپنے نور ولایت سے دنیا کو روشن کرے گا" (p. 145, folio 141).
- **A dream initiation and a visit from Khizr.** The author reports that he was honoured with the ziyarat
  of "حضرت اویس کرخی" in a dream, who taught him "ذکر اویسیہ", and that "حضرت خضر علیہ السلام" then came
  and directed him to Hindustan (p. 146, folio 142).
- **A curse that failed.** The author reports that Haji Chiragh-e Hind cursed Shaikh Kabir for taking
  bai'at elsewhere, "لیکن آپ کی دعا شیخ کبیر کے شامل حال تھی۔ شیخ چراغ ہندی کی بد دعاء کا کوئی اثر نہیں
  ہوا اور شیخ کبیر جوان نہیں مرے' بلکہ کافی ضعیف ہو کر مرے" (p. 147, folio 143).
- **Dates eaten with their stones.** The author reports that at seven, given "چھوارے" from a "خوان" before
  Shaikh Jamal al-Din Khandan, he ate them "مع گٹھلیوں کے" and explained "اس میں بھی برکت ہے اور پھینکنا
  بے ادبی ہے" — there is blessing in these too, and to throw them away is disrespect (p. 134, folio 130).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The two complete entries rest on two different source sets, and the book says so footnote by
  footnote.** باب 21's twelve حواشی are **all** "سیر الاقطاب (فارسی)", pages 197–211 (p. 132, folio 128).
  باب 22's sixteen draw on four works: "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم)" for most of the biography, "اخبار
  الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ)" for the birth and the Firoz visits, "سیر العارفین" for two episodes, and "تاریخ
  فرشتہ (فارسی)" for the Delhi rain (p. 142, folio 138). **The Delhi rain is the only karamat in this
  chunk sourced to a chronicle rather than a tazkira.**
- **The book records a contradiction about sama rather than resolving it.** "سماع سنتے تھے۔ بعض کہتے ہیں
  کہ سماع نہیں سنتے تھے" (p. 137, folio 133, حاشیہ 11) — the second clause is left standing beside the
  first. Compare the flat "سماع سنتے تھے" of the Panipat saint (p. 130, folio 126).
- **The compiler marks unsourced report with a formula.** "یہ کہا جاتا ہے کہ" introduces the claim about
  Sabir's earnings (p. 130, folio 126) and "آپ کے متعلق یہ کہا جاتا ہے کہ" the characterisation of the
  embrace (p. 134, folio 130) — both carried here as the book carries them, not as fact.
- **A gloss that interprets rather than translates.** The embrace claim, that he absorbed whatever gift a
  person had, is immediately turned by the book's own "یعنی" into a social explanation — attention and
  service so great that the person gave everything up willingly (p. 135, folio 131). **The book is
  visibly softening its source.**
- **The entries run on the same fixed grid of Urdu subheadings as earlier chapters** — خاندانی حالات,
  والد, ولادت, نام, کنیت, لقب, لقب کی وجہ تسمیہ, تعلیم و تربیت, بیعت و خلافت, ملازمت, سفر, وفات, خلفاء,
  سیرت, تعلیمات, اقوال, کشف و کرامات (pp. 133–141, folios 129–137) — applied at very different lengths:
  باب 22 runs to twenty-odd headings over ten pages, while باب 21's surviving tail uses seven over four
  (pp. 129–132, folios 125–128). **Absence of a heading is the book's silence, not a gap in the
  transcription** — which is why "مزار" is recorded above as (not stated) for Makhdum Jahaniyan.
- **A saint's teaching is used to license the book's own practice of collecting khirqas.** The doctrine
  that the **خرقہ تبرک** carries no condition of companionship (p. 138, folio 134) sits nine pages after
  the list of eighteen figures from whom the same saint took khirqa, two of them in dreams (pp. 135–136,
  folios 131–132). The book does not draw the connection.

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the subject's chapter heading is outside it.** Pages 129–132
  (folios 125–128) carry the close of an entry whose "باب" heading and first pages fall in the previous
  chunk; its footnote markers **1–6 are not in these pages** (the first marker present is 7, p. 129,
  folio 125), while the حواشی block on p. 132 (folio 128) lists twelve. The subject is named in the
  running text only as "حضرت شیخ جلال الدین کبیر الاولیاء", inside a "یہ کہا جاتا ہے" clause about someone
  else's earnings (p. 130, folio 126), and implied by "ہمارے نام پر اس کا نام جلال آباد رکھو" (p. 131,
  folio 127). **Confirm the name against the chapter heading in chunk 006 before attributing anything on
  pp. 129–132 to him.**
- **He must not be mapped to any archive row.** The book puts his mazar at **پانی پت (Panipat)** (pp. 129–130,
  folios 125–126) and his daughters' marriages at **کرنال (Karnal)** (p. 129, folio 125) — both in India.
  The 169 rows carry no Panipat or Karnal site, and no row should be reached for on the strength of the
  name "جلال الدین".
- **حضرت میر سید اشرف جہانگیر سمنانی must not be mapped either.** Every institution the book gives him —
  the khanqah, the باغ "روح آباد", the mazar "مرجع خاص و عام" — is at **کچھوچھ**, "جو جونپور کے علاقے میں
  ایک قصبہ ہے" (p. 147, folio 143), in India. His passage through **اوچ** (p. 146, folio 142) is a visit,
  not a residence, and does not attach him to any Uch row.
- **باب 23 is unfinished in this chunk.** The text ends at the mazar sentence (p. 147, folio 143); footnote
  markers 1, 2 and 3 appear but **the حواشی block for باب 23 falls outside these pages**, as do any
  خلفاء, سیرت and تعلیمات sections. Nothing here is the end of his entry.
- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam is mapped here for the first time, on one sentence, and a human should
  confirm it.** The identification rests on "ملتان میں آپ شیخ رکن الدین ابو الفتح کی اجازت سے مدرسے میں
  رہتے تھے" (p. 134, folio 130), which places him at **ملتان** with authority over a madrasa — the row's
  location is "Multan City, Multan, Punjab" and its figure "Shah Rukn-e-Alam (Rukn-ud-Din Abul Fath)",
  matching the book's name including the kunya. **But the chunk gives him no death year, no silsila, and
  no town for his own mazar** — the Eid-night sentence names three mazars without naming their town
  (p. 133, folio 129). chunk_004 left this figure unmapped for want of exactly this locational statement;
  the mapping is offered here with that history on the record.
- **The mazar-town of the Eid-night vigil is not printed, and carries an [OCR?] flag mid-list.** "پھر صدر
  الدین عارف اور [OCR?] حضرت رکن الدین ابو الفتح کے مزارات پر حاضر ہوئے" (p. 133, folio 129) — the flag
  sits on the word "اور" joining the two names, so it is not certain whether a third name or a title is
  lost there. Multan for these mazars comes only from the grandfather's migration sentence earlier on the
  same page, not from this one.
- **The book's death year for Makhdum Jahaniyan and the row's differ, and no conversion is made here.**
  The book prints "10' ذی الحجہ 788ھ" (p. 137, folio 133, حاشیہ 10); the archive row carries **1384** CE.
  Both values are recorded side by side above. **Do not compute either from the other** — a human with the
  calendar tables should settle whether these are the same year.
- **Makhdum Jahaniyan's shrine is never located in this chunk.** The row's Uch Sharif is supported by his
  **birth** at اوچہ (p. 133, folio 129), his residence and i'tikaf there (p. 141, folio 137) and his
  journeys "اوچہ سے دہلی" (p. 137, folio 133) — but the وفات heading gives a date and nothing else
  (p. 137, folio 133). No urs, no sajjada nashin, no endowment for him anywhere in these pages.
- **Uch is spelled three ways within ten pages** — "اوچہ" (pp. 133, 137, 141; folios 129, 133, 137),
  "اونچہ" (p. 134, folio 130) and "اوچ" (p. 146, folio 142) — and Jamal al-Din Khandan's nisba is printed
  "اونچی" (p. 134, folio 130) where chunk_004 read "اوچھوی [OCR?]". **No archive row carries this figure's
  name**, and the town of the mazar used for Sumra's cure (p. 141, folio 137) is not stated; leave him
  unmapped, but note that his tomb is an active healing site in this book.
- **"شیخ جلال الدین سرخ بخاری" is printed without "پوش".** The row reads "Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari"
  (p. 133, folio 129). The mapping rests on Bukhara → Multan → **اوچہ** by his pir's order, plus the name;
  **no death year and no silsila are stated for him.**
- **The same event is told twice with a one-letter difference that changes its sense.** The Delhi healing
  is introduced on p. 129 (folio 125) as "ان کی صحبت کے لئے دعا فرمائی" — prayed for his **صحبت**
  (company) — and told in the karamat section on p. 131 (folio 127) as "آپ نے ان کی صحت کے لئے دعا فرمائی"
  — for his **صحت** (health). **Neither word is OCR-flagged**, so the discrepancy may be in the print;
  quote whichever page you cite and do not harmonise.
- **A list of three that names two.** "وہ تین علم یعنی شریعت' و حقیقت کا عالم ہو" (p. 139, folio 135) —
  three sciences are announced and only شریعت and حقیقت are named, with طریقت apparently dropped; the
  passage is **not** OCR-flagged. Quote it as printed.
- **A duplicated clause in the شریعت/طریقت/حقیقت passage.** "اور جسم کا معصیت سے شریعت ہے' اور دل کا نگاہ
  رکھنا کدورت بشریت سے طریقت ہے۔ اور جسم کا معصیت سے شریعت ہے' اور دل کا نگاہ رکھا کدورت بشریت سے طریقت
  ہے" (p. 139, folio 135) — the clause is printed twice, the second time with "رکھا" for "رکھنا", and the
  passage is not flagged. **Do not quote this passage as a continuous text.**
