# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 001 — pp. 1–34

**The book's front matter and its first entry.** Four title pages, a publisher's colophon, a dedication,
two devotional poems, a two-page table of contents, the author's own preface, and then Chapter 1 in
full — **حضرت شیخ علی ہجویری**, "Hazrat Shaikh Ali Hujwiri", which is the archive's **data-darbar**
(pp. 21–29, folios 17–25) — plus the opening of Chapter 2 on **حضرت شیخ جلال الدین تبریزی**[OCR?],
Shaikh Jalal al-Din Tabrizi, breaking off mid-sentence (pp. 33–34, folios 29–30). One archive row is
matched. The chunk's other value is threefold: the author names himself **sajjada nashin of two named
shrines** on his own title page (p. 7); the contents list gives a **complete locator map of all seventy
chapters** with printed page numbers (pp. 15–16); and the Hujwiri entry carries **two death years, two
accounts of his arrival in India, and a twenty-item footnote apparatus** (pp. 21–29, folios 17–25).

**Folio markers appear only from p. 21 onward, and where they appear the relation is folio = PDF
index − 4**: p. 21 → folio 17, p. 22 → 18, p. 23 → 19, p. 24 → 20, p. 25 → 21, p. 26 → 22, p. 27 → 23,
p. 28 → 24, p. 29 → 25, p. 31 → 27, p. 33 → 29, p. 34 → 30. **Pages 1–20 (all the front matter) carry
no `[folio N]` marker at all**, so facts from the title pages, colophon, contents and preface are cited
by PDF page only. p. 30 is blank and prints no folio.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **data-darbar** (حضرت شیخ علی ہجویری رحمۃ اللہ علیہ, "Hazrat Shaikh Ali Hujwiri", باب 1, chapter 1,
  pp. 21–29, folios 17–25):
  - **Titles.** "سلطان الطریقت ہیں۔ گنج حقیقت ہیں۔ برہان الشریعت ہیں" — he is Sultan al-Tariqat, Ganj
    al-Haqiqat, Burhan al-Shariat (p. 21, folio 17).
  - **Descent.** "آپ سادات حسنی سے ہیں" — he is of the **Hasani sayyids**; his lineage runs "چند
    واسطوں سے", through a few intermediaries, to "حضرت سیدنا زید شہید رضی اللہ عنہ ابن امیر المومنین
    حضرت سیدنا امام حسن" — Sayyidna **Zaid Shahid** son of Amir al-Muminin **Imam Hasan** (p. 21,
    folio 17). The number of intermediaries is **(not stated)**.
  - **Family and place of origin.** "آپ کے آباؤ اجداد غزنی کے رہنے والے تھے" — his forefathers were
    residents of **غزنی**, Ghazni; his grandfather's name was "حضرت علی", Ali (p. 21, folio 17). His
    father's name: "آپ کے والد ماجد کا نام عثمان ہے" — **عثمان**, Usman (p. 21, folio 17, حاشیہ 1,
    citing کشف المحجوب اردو[OCR?] ترجمہ ص 2 and سفینۃ الاولیاء فارسی ص 164). This agrees with the
    archive row's "Ali ibn Usman al-Hujwiri".
  - **Birth.** "آپ غزنی میں پیدا ہوئے" — he was born in **Ghazni** (p. 21, folio 17). Year of birth:
    **(not stated)**.
  - **Name, and what "Hujwiri" means.** "آپ کا نام 'علی' ہے۔ **ہجویر شہر کا نام نہیں ہے، بلکہ غزنی کے
    ایک محلے کا نام ہے۔** اس لئے آپ 'علی ہجویری' کہلاتے ہیں" — his name is Ali; **Hujwir is not the
    name of a city but the name of a quarter (محلہ) of Ghazni**, and this is why he is called Ali
    Hujwiri (p. 21, folio 17). The book asserts this without citing a source.
  - **Kunya** "ابو الحسن", Abu al-Hasan (p. 21, folio 17, حاشیہ 2, citing خزینۃ الاصفیاء).
  - **Laqab.** "آپ 'داتا گنج بخش' کے لقب سے پکارے جاتے ہیں" — he is called by the laqab **داتا گنج
    بخش**, Data Ganj Bakhsh (p. 21, folio 17); "بعض لوگ تو آپ کو 'گنج بخش' کے لقب سے پکارا کرتے ہیں",
    some call him simply Ganj Bakhsh (p. 22, folio 18).
  - **The book's account of how the laqab arose** — see Legends; the couplet is under Citable passages.
  - **Training.** "آپ علوم ظاہری کی تحصیل سے فارغ ہو کر علوم باطنی کی طرف متوجہ ہوئے" — having finished
    acquiring the outward sciences he turned to the inward (p. 22, folio 18). His teacher (استاد) is
    named as **شیخ ابو القاسم**, Shaikh Abu al-Qasim, whose saying on attendance upon a murshid the
    book quotes at length: a murshid "وہ ہوتا ہے جو کہ غواص ہو نہ کہ متصل [OCR?]", is one who is a
    **diver** and not متصل[OCR?]; and one should take bai'at only "اگر اپنے میں قوت پائے", if one finds
    strength in oneself, "اور اپنے میں قوت نہ ہو تو دونوں خراب ہوں گے", and if not, both will be ruined
    (p. 22, folio 18, حاشیہ 4, citing نفحات الانس فارسی ص 291).
  - **Bai'at and khilafat, with a three-link chain.** "آپ شیخ ابو الفضل بن حسن ختلی کے مرید ہیں" — he is
    the murid of **شیخ ابو الفضل بن حسن ختلی**, Shaikh Abu al-Fazl bin Hasan Khuttali (حاشیہ 5,
    سفینۃ الاولیاء فارسی ص 164); "وہ مرید حضرت خضری [OCR?] کے، اور وہ مرید حضرت شیخ شبلی کے ہیں" — he
    the murid of **حضرت خضری**[OCR?], and he the murid of **حضرت شیخ شبلی**, Shaikh Shibli (p. 22,
    folio 18, حاشیہ 6, same source). **No silsila is named as an order anywhere in the entry** — the
    archive row's `silsila` field is likewise empty, and the book supplies nothing to fill it.
  - **Travels and other affiliations.** "آپ نے خراسان، ماوراء النہر، مرو، آذربائیجان وغیرہ کی سیر و
    سیاحت فرمائی" — **خراسان** Khurasan, **ماوراء النہر** Transoxiana, **مرو** Marw, **آذربائیجان**
    Azerbaijan and others; he met many darwishes and drew benefit from the spiritual graces of
    **حضرت شیخ ابو القاسم گرگانی**, **حضرت شیخ ابو سعید ابو الخیر** and **حضرت شیخ ابو القاسم قشیری**
    (p. 22, folio 18, حاشیہ 7, citing کشف الاسرار اردو ترجمہ ص 16).
  - **The command to go to Lahore, in a dream** — see Legends and Citable passages. His objection and
    the pir's reply are at p. 22 (folio 18).
  - **Two accounts of how he reached India, left standing side by side.** "بعض لوگوں کا خیال ہے کہ آپ
    سلطان مسعود بن سلطان محمود غزنوی کے لشکر کے ساتھ ہندوستان تشریف لائے" — some think he came to
    Hindustan with the army of **سلطان مسعود بن سلطان محمود غزنوی**, Sultan Masud son of Sultan Mahmud
    Ghaznavi; "اور بعض لوگ اس سے متفق نہیں، ان کا کہنا ہے کہ آپ شیخ احمد جماوی [OCR?] سرخسی اور شیخ
    ابو سعید ہجویری کے ہمراہ ہندوستان تشریف لائے" — and some do not agree, saying he came in the
    company of **شیخ احمد جماوی**[OCR?] **سرخسی** and **شیخ ابو سعید ہجویری** (pp. 22–23, folios 18–19,
    حاشیہ 8, citing فوائد الفوائد اردو ترجمہ ص 27). The book adjudicates neither.
  - **Arrival at Lahore and the funeral he met.** He stayed outside the city because night had fallen;
    entering in the morning he saw a bier, and on enquiry learned "کہ حضرت شیخ حسن زنجانی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ
    کا جنازہ ہے", that it was the janaza of **حضرت شیخ حسن زنجانی**, Shaikh **Hasan** Zanjani (p. 23,
    folio 19, حاشیہ 9, citing کشف الاسرار اردو ترجمہ ص 16). See Doubts on Zanjani and the archive.
  - **Settlement at Lahore.** "آپ نے لاہور میں مستقل سکونت اختیار فرمائی اور تعلیم و تلقین اور رشد و
    ہدایت میں مشغول ہوئے" — he took **permanent residence in لاہور, Lahore**, and occupied himself in
    teaching, talqin and guidance (p. 23, folio 19).
  - **Death, with two years given.** "آپ نے **465ھ** میں اس جہاں فانی سے سفر دار الاخرت فرمایا" — he
    journeyed to the abode of the hereafter in **465 AH** (حاشیہ 10, citing سفینۃ الاولیاء فارسی ص 16);
    "بعض کے نزدیک آپ کا وصال **456ھ** میں ہوا" — in the view of some his passing was in **456 AH**
    (p. 23, folio 19, حاشیہ 11, citing کشف الاسرار اردو ترجمہ ص 16). Day and month: **(not stated)**.
    Age at death: **(not stated)**. Both years are printed in Western digits in this chunk. See Doubts
    for the relation to the archive row's `1072`.
  - **The shrine.** "مزار پر انوار لاہور میں فیوض و برکات کا سرچشمہ ہے" — the radiant mazar **in
    Lahore** is a fountainhead of graces and blessings (p. 23, folio 19). **No quarter, street, locality
    or building description is given, and no urs date, no custodian, no endowment and no visitor
    numbers.** This is the entry's only statement about the shrine as a place.
  - **Character.** "آپ قطب زمانہ تھے" — he was the **qutb of the age**; engaged in zikr and fikr,
    muraqaba and muhasaba, ibadat and riyazat; "دنیاوی آلائش سے پاک و صاف تھے", clean of worldly taint;
    "تصرفات آپ کے بے شمار ہیں", his tasarrufat are countless (p. 23, folio 19).
  - **Works, six titles listed.** "آپ نے بہت سے کتابیں لکھی ہیں" — he wrote many books:
    **منہاج الدین**, **البیان الاہل العیان**[OCR?], **اسر الخرق و المونیات**[OCR?], **کشف الاسرار**,
    **الرعایۃ بحوق اللہ**[OCR?], **کشف المحجوب** (p. 23, folio 19, حاشیہ 12, citing کشف الاسرار اردو
    ترجمہ ص 12). Three of the six titles are OCR-flagged.
  - **Poetry.** "آپ شاعر بھی تھے۔ آپ کا تخلص 'علی' ہے۔ آپ صاحب دیوان ہیں" — he was also a poet, his
    takhallus is **علی**, and he is the possessor of a diwan, as he says himself: "میں نے بیت اور اشعار
    بہت سے کہے ہیں اور ایک دیوان بھی کہا...." — I have composed many verses and couplets and also
    composed a diwan (p. 23, folio 19, حاشیہ 13, citing کشف المحجوب اردو ترجمہ ص 3). A Persian **غزل**
    attributed to him is printed in full at p. 24 (folio 20), seven couplets, of which five carry
    `[OCR?]` flags on the rhyme word — not reliable enough to quote (see Doubts).
  - **Teachings, five headed sections plus ten aqwal** (pp. 24–27, folios 20–23): نفسانی غرض, self-
    interest, which lifts the blessing from any work (حاشیہ 14, کشف المحجوب ص 48، 49); صوفیوں کی صحبت,
    the company of the Sufis, a long fourfold analysis of the motives of those who seek it, ending on
    those who "اپنی آفتوں کو ان کی اصلاح میں چھپائیں اور ان کا جامہ پہنیں", hide their own afflictions
    in the Sufis' correction and wear their garb (حاشیہ 15, کشف المحجوب ص 222); مجاہدہ نفس, the
    struggle against the self, which "رسول اللہ صلی اللہ علیہ وسلم نے … جہاد پر بزرگ رکھا ہے", the
    Prophet held greater than jihad; فقیر, on rulers — see Practices; دس چیزیں, ten things that devour
    ten (حاشیہ 17); and آٹھ کلمات, eight sayings selected from Luqman Hakim — see Other saints.
  - **Wird.** "اللہ تعالیٰ کے اسماء حسنیٰ میں سے اسم **یا حبیب** اور **یا لطیف** کے معنوں کو اپنے وجود
    میں رکھ لے اور ان کو اپنا ورد بنا لے" — take the meanings of the names **Ya Habib** and **Ya Latif**
    into your being and make them your wird (p. 27, folio 23, حاشیہ 19, citing کشف الاسرار اردو ترجمہ
    ص 4). The only wird prescribed in the chunk.
  - **The mosque he built at Lahore** — the entry's single karamat, and the only building in the chunk:
    see Practices and Legends (pp. 27–28, folios 23–24).
  - **The footnote apparatus.** The chapter's twenty numbered حواشی are printed together on a single page
    at the end (p. 29, folio 25) and cite six works only: **کشف المحجوب** (اردو[OCR?] ترجمہ),
    **سفینۃ الاولیاء** (فارسی), **خزینۃ الاصفیاء**, **کشف الاسرار** (اردو ترجمہ; once "ردو[OCR?] ترجمہ"),
    **نفحات الانس** (فارسی), **فوائد الفوائد** (اردو ترجمہ). Every substantive claim about the saint
    except the Hujwir-is-a-quarter etymology and the mazar sentence carries one.
  - Nothing in this chunk on the urs, the langar, the sajjada nashini, the Auqaf, the endowment, the
    buildings of the darbar, or any dispute over it. **(not stated)** throughout.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **حضرت شیخ جلال الدین تبریزی**[OCR?], Shaikh Jalal al-Din Tabrizi (باب 2, chapter 2, pp. 33–34,
  folios 29–30, entry incomplete): "سلطان الاصفیاء ہیں", he is Sultan al-Asfiya. Murid of **حضرت شیخ
  ابو سعید تبریزی**, Shaikh Abu Said Tabrizi (حاشیہ 1); after his pir's death he attached himself to
  **حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین سہروردی**, Shaikh Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi, at **بغداد**, Baghdad, and served
  him as described under Practices (p. 34, folio 30, حاشیہ 3). He travelled much with **حضرت بہاء الدین
  زکریا**, Bahauddin Zakariya — "آپ کا اور حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کا سیرو سیاحت میں بہت
  ساتھ رہا" — Zakariya occupying himself in worship in each city while Tabrizi went out to see it and
  meet its darwishes; at **نیشاپور**, Nishapur, he went to **حضرت فرید الدین عطار**, Fariduddin Attar
  (حاشیہ 5), and told Zakariya on his return "کہ حضرت فرید الدین عطار کو سب سے بہتر پایا", that he found
  Fariduddin Attar the best of all the darwishes. **The entry breaks off mid-sentence** as Zakariya asks
  him a second question (p. 34, folio 30). No dates, no shrine, no silsila as an order.
- **حضرت بہاء الدین زکریا** (p. 34, folio 30): named only as Tabrizi's travelling companion. No town,
  no death year, no shrine. See Doubts — this is **not** enough to map to `shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya`.
- **حضرت شیخ حسن زنجانی / خواجہ حسن زنجانی** (pp. 22–23, folios 18–19): the book says he was already
  present in Lahore before Hujwiri's arrival, and that his janaza was passing as Hujwiri entered the
  city. No grave site, no death year, no silsila. See Doubts.
- **حضرت خواجہ معین الدین حسن چشتی سنجری**, Khwaja Moinuddin Hasan Chishti Sanjari (p. 21, folio 17,
  حاشیہ 3; and p. 18): the book has him perform اعتکاف, itikaf, at Hujwiri's mazar when he came to
  Lahore, and recite the couplet that gave the laqab. In the preface the author says he wrote a separate
  book, **"معین الہند"**, *Muin al-Hind*, in tribute to him (p. 18).
- **حضرت خواجہ قطب الدین بختیار کاکیؒ**, Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki, titled **قطب الاقطاب** (p. 9):
  the book's dedicatee — "کی بارگاہ میں یہ چند اوراق بصد عجز و نیاز پیش کرتا ہوں", I present these few
  leaves at his court with all humility, closing "گر قبول افتد زہے عز و شرف" and signed "شارب". No
  location or dates given for him.
- **حضرت مخدوم سماء الدین سہروردیؒ (مہرولی نئی دہلی)**, Makhdum Sama al-Din Suhrawardi of **Mehrauli,
  New Delhi**, and **حضرت نواب گدڑی شاہ بابا رحمۃ اللہ علیہ (اجمیر شریف)**, Nawab Gudri Shah Baba of
  **Ajmer Sharif** (p. 7): named on the title page as the two shrines of which the author is sajjada
  nashin. Both are in India. See Practices.
- **لقمان حکیم**, Luqman Hakim (p. 26, folio 22): in a saying Hujwiri reports, "میں چار سو پیغمبروں کی
  خدمت بجالایا' اور ان سے آٹھ ہزار کلمے میں نے حاصل کئے' ان میں سے آٹھ کلمے میں نے ایسے انتخاب کئے
  ہیں" — I served four hundred prophets and obtained eight thousand sayings from them, of which I chose
  eight (حاشیہ 18).
- **حضرت نظام الدین اولیاء**, Nizamuddin Auliya (p. 33, folio 29, حاشیہ 2): quoted as the authority for
  the khanqah-and-cash story about Abu Said Tabrizi under Practices.
- **حضرت حیدر / فاتح خیبر / شاہ قنبر / قاسم کوثر** (p. 13): the addressee of the author's Persian
  petition-poem "مددے!", "Help!" — "بے مددگار منم حضرت حیدر مددے", helpless am I, Hazrat Haidar, help.
  Signed شارب, who calls himself "غلامے بدرت استادہ", a slave standing at your door.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The author declares himself the sitting sajjada nashin of two named shrines, on the title page.**
  Under his name and degrees the title page prints: "بانی صدر: دی سوسائٹی آف مسٹکس" — founder-president
  of **The Society of Mystics** — and then, on its own line, "**سجادہ نشین**", sajjada nashin, followed
  by two shrines: "حضرت مخدوم سماء الدین سہروردیؒ (مہرولی نئی دہلی)" — **Makhdum Sama al-Din
  Suhrawardi, Mehrauli, New Delhi** — and "حضرت نواب گدڑی شاہ بابا رحمۃ اللہ علیہ (اجمیر شریف)" —
  **Nawab Gudri Shah Baba, Ajmer Sharif** (p. 7). **This is the single most important institutional fact
  in the chunk**: the book's compiler is not an outside historian but a serving custodian of two Indian
  dargahs, holding **two sajjada nashini seats at once**, and he prints that office as his credential
  ahead of his doctorate. Every judgement the book makes about shrines should be read against it. His own
  address is "شارب انسٹی ٹیوٹ آف اسلامک لٹریچر، شارب ہاؤس، جھالرہ، اجمیر شریف" — the Sharib Institute
  of Islamic Literature, Sharib House, **جھالرہ, Jhalra, Ajmer Sharif** (p. 18).
- **A mosque built at Lahore by Ali Hujwiri, with a disputed qibla.** "لاہور میں سکونت اختیار کرنے کے
  بعد آپ نے ایک مسجد تعمیر کرانا شروع کی۔ مسجد کی سمت قبلہ رو نہ ہونے کی وجہ سے علماء نے اعتراض کیا" —
  after settling in Lahore he began to have a mosque built, and the **ulama objected** that its direction
  was not qibla-facing; "اس مسجد کی محراب جنوب کی طرف معلوم ہوتی تھی", the mihrab appeared to face
  **south**; he answered none of the objections and the work continued (p. 27, folio 23). When it was
  finished "آپ نے علماء و مشائخ کو بلایا' سب نے وہاں نماز پڑھی' آپ نے امامت فرمائی" — he summoned the
  **ulama and mashaikh**, all prayed there and **he led the prayer** (p. 28, folio 24). The resolution is
  under Legends (حاشیہ 20, سفینۃ الاولیاء فارسی ص 364). A named saint commissioning a building, a formal
  clerical objection to its orientation, and a public inaugural congregation — the only construction in
  the chunk, and **no date, cost, patron or site within Lahore is given**.
- **A doctrine that a faqir must avoid rulers, argued from the ruin of his provision.** Hujwiri: "فقیر کو
  چاہیے کہ بادشاہوں اور حاکموں کی آشنائی و معرفت کو سخت خطرناک سانپوں اور بڑے بھاری اژدہاؤں کی معرفت و
  آشنائی سمجھے اور جانے۔ کیوں کہ جب بادشاہ کا تقرب فقیر کو حاصل ہوا تو جانے کہ اس کا زاد اور توشہ برباد
  ہو گیا" — a faqir should reckon acquaintance with **kings and rulers** as acquaintance with venomous
  snakes and great dragons, because once a faqir gains a king's favour let him know his provision and
  viaticum are ruined; and "جب ترکی ٹوپی تو نے سر پر رکھ لی تو اس سے کچھ فقیری حاصل نہیں ہوتی", putting
  a Turkish cap on your head gains you no faqiri (p. 25, folio 21, حاشیہ 16, citing کشف الاسرار اردو
  ترجمہ ص 3). **The chunk's clearest statement of principled refusal of state patronage**, and it comes
  from the saint of Data Darbar himself.
- **A ruler's cash smuggled into a khanqah, detected by its effect on worship, and the ground dug up.**
  Reported on the authority of **حضرت نظام الدین اولیاء** about Jalal al-Din Tabrizi's pir **شیخ ابو
  سعید تبریزی**: he was so complete a renouncer that debt often fell on him but "کسی سے کوئی چیز نہ
  لیتے", he took nothing from anyone; once **three days** passed with no food cooked in the **خانقاہ**,
  he and his companions breaking their fast on **watermelon** alone. Hearing this, "وہاں کے حاکم", the
  local **hakim**, said "وہ ہماری چیز قبول نہیں کرتے۔ نقدی لے جاؤ اور شیخ کے خادم کو دے دو" — he does
  not accept our things; take cash and give it to the shaikh's **khadim** — with instructions to spend it
  "تھوڑا تھوڑا", little by little, and never mention it to the shaikh. The **شاہی (حاکم کے) نوکر**, the
  royal/ruler's servant, duly did so. On the day the money was spent the shaikh found none of his usual
  relish in worship, interrogated the khadim, who could not conceal it, and asked "کون شخص لایا تھا اور
  کہاں کہاں قدم رکھا تھا", who brought it and where he set foot. His order: "جہاں جہاں اس نے قدم رکھا'
  وہاں سے مٹی کھود کر پھینک دو" — wherever he set foot, dig up that earth and throw it away — "اور اس
  خادم کو بھی اسی قصور کے عوض خانقاہ سے نکال دیا", and he expelled the khadim from the khanqah for that
  fault (p. 33, folio 29, حاشیہ 2). A khanqah's economy, a ruler's attempt to endow it covertly through a
  servant, the staff dismissal that followed, and the physical purification of the ground: the chunk's
  richest single passage on the politics of shrine income.
- **Offerings ranked by the giver's poverty, at Baghdad.** When Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi returned from
  Hajj, "جو بھی آتا کچھ نہ کچھ لے کر آتا", everyone who came brought something; an **old woman** with
  nothing took "ایک درم", one **dirham**, from the hem of her old chador and presented it, "انہوں نے
  قبول کیا اور انہوں نے اس درم کو سب تحائف کے اوپر رکھا", he accepted it and placed that dirham **on top
  of all the gifts**. He then invited those present to take whatever they wished, and each took what he
  wanted; told "تم بھی کچھ لے لو", you too take something, Jalal al-Din took the old woman's dirham from
  the top of the pile, and the shaikh said "یہ تو سب کچھ لے گیا", this one has taken everything (p. 34,
  folio 30, حاشیہ 4). A shrine-side account of nazrana in which the smallest offering is the greatest.
- **Annual Hajj from Baghdad, and a disciple's portable kitchen.** "حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین سہروردی رحمۃ
  اللہ علیہ ہر سال بغداد سے حج کے لئے جاتے تھے" — he went **every year** from **Baghdad** for the Hajj;
  when he grew old he could no longer eat cold food, so Jalal al-Din "انگیٹھی اور دیگچی اپنے سر پر رکھ کر
  چلتے تھے' لیکن سر کو نہ ہلنے دیتے تھے", walked with a **brazier and cooking pot on his head** without
  letting his head move, and served hot food whenever it was asked for (p. 34, folio 30, حاشیہ 3).
- **A saint's saying against sama.** Among Hujwiri's aqwal: "مبتدی[OCR?] کو چاہیے کہ وہ **سماع** کے
  نزدیک نہ جائے اور اس سے کنارہ کش رہے" — a beginner[OCR?] should not go near **sama** and should keep
  away from it (p. 27, folio 23). Relevant to any claim about musical practice at the darbar, and it is a
  restriction, not a licence.
- **Charity argued from the grave.** "دولت کو ایک عذاب جان اور اس کو اہل فاقہ لوگوں کو دیدے اور تصدق
  کر' کیونکہ آخر قبر میں کیڑے کھائیں گے اور اگر تو نے یہ بخشش میں دی دے تو وہ تیرے دوستدار رہیں گے" —
  reckon wealth a torment, give it to the starving and give it as **sadaqa**, for in the end worms will
  eat it in the grave, whereas if you give it away as a gift they will remain your friends (pp. 26–27,
  folios 22–23). A distribution ethic stated as the saint's own.
- **Print and trade data for this edition** (p. 8, colophon): first edition **"بار اول | اکتوبر
  1999ء"**, October 1999; print run **"تعداد | 1100"**, 1,100 copies; printers **"حاجی محمد حنیف
  پرنٹرز"**; publishers **"چوہدری غلام رسول"** and **"میاں جواد رسول"**; price **"80/=روپے"**[OCR?].
  Points of sale: **"اسلام بکڈپو دوکان نمبر 12 گنج بخش روڈ لاہور"** — Islam Book Depot, shop no. 12,
  **Ganj Bakhsh Road, Lahore** — and **"ملت پبلی کیشنز۔ فیصل مسجد اسلام آباد"** — Millat Publications,
  **Faisal Masjid, Islamabad**. A hagiography of Data Ganj Bakhsh sold from a road named after him and
  from the national mosque: the book's own distribution runs through shrine and mosque space.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The couplet that made the laqab.** The author reports that when Khwaja Moinuddin Hasan Chishti
  Sanjari came to Lahore "تو آپ کے مزار مبارک پر اعتکاف فرمایا", he performed itikaf at Hujwiri's
  blessed mazar, and on leaving recited the couplet "گنج بخش فیض عالم مظہر نور خدا / ناقصاں را پیر کامل
  کاملاں را رہنما"; "اس روز سے آپ داتا گنج بخش مشہور ہوئے", from that day he became famous as Data Ganj
  Bakhsh (pp. 21–22, folios 17–18, حاشیہ 3, citing کشف الاسرار اردو ترجمہ ص 8). The book gives the laqab
  a **posthumous** origin, conferred at the tomb by another saint.
- **The dream that assigned Lahore.** "مرشد کے حکم سے ہندوستان تشریف لائے" — he came to Hindustan by his
  murshid's command; the author gives the incident thus: one night he saw his pir in a dream saying "تم
  کو لاہور کا قطب بنایا گیا ہے۔ لاہور تمہارے سپرد کیا گیا ہے۔ لاہور جاؤ" — you have been made the
  **qutb of Lahore**, Lahore has been entrusted to you, go to Lahore. He objected in the dream itself
  that Khwaja Hasan Zanjani was already there, "ان کی موجودگی میں میرا لاہور جانا بے کار ہے", and the
  pir replied there was no need for argument and he should set out quickly (p. 22, folio 18). No source
  is footnoted for the dream.
- **The wisdom of the command shown by a funeral.** Meeting Hasan Zanjani's bier on the morning he
  entered Lahore, "اب آپ کو معلوم ہوا کہ پیرو مرشد کے فرمان میں کیا مصلحت تھی" — now he understood what
  the expediency in his pir's command had been (p. 23, folio 19, حاشیہ 9).
- **The mosque whose qibla was proved by lifting the veils.** After the congregation had prayed, the
  author reports, he asked those present to consider where the Kaaba lay; "آپ نے توجہ فرمائی' حجابات اٹھ
  گئے' سب نے دیکھا کہ کعبہ سامنے اور مسجد کی سمت صحیح ہے" — he directed his attention, the veils were
  lifted, and all saw the **Kaaba in front of them** and the mosque's direction correct (p. 28, folio 24,
  حاشیہ 20, citing سفینۃ الاولیاء فارسی ص 364). The book's only karamat of Hujwiri, and it settles an
  architectural dispute by vision rather than by instrument.
- **A ruler's coin detected by its taste in worship.** It is reported on Nizamuddin Auliya's authority
  that Abu Said Tabrizi knew the hakim's money had entered his khanqah only because "اس روز شیخ صاحب کو
  طاعت میں جو ذوق اور آرام حاصل ہوا تھا' نہ ہوا", that day he did not obtain the relish and ease in
  obedience that he used to (p. 33, folio 29, حاشیہ 2). Full account under Practices.
- Of Hujwiri generally the author says only "تصرفات آپ کے بے شمار ہیں" — his tasarrufat are countless
  (p. 23, folio 19) — and names none beyond the mosque.

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The book's stated plan: a third volume of remainders.** The author gives the series explicitly: "میں
  نے 'معین الہند' لکھ کر خواجہ خواجگان حضرت خواجہ معین الدین حسن چشتیؒ کو خراج تحسین پیش کیا۔ پھر 'دلی
  کے بائیس خواجہ' لکھ کر دلی کے برگزیدہ پیران عظام[OCR?] کو نذر عقیدت پیش کی۔ **یہ کتاب اس سلسلے کی
  تیسری کڑی ہے**" — he wrote *Muin al-Hind* as tribute to Khwaja Moinuddin Hasan Chishti, then *Dilli ke
  Bais Khwaja*, "Delhi's twenty-two Khwajas", for the elect great pirs[OCR?] of Delhi; **this book is the
  third link in that chain** — and its criterion of inclusion is negative: "اس کتاب میں ان اولیائے کرام
  کے حالات شامل ہیں جن کے حالات 'معین الہند' اور 'دلی کے بائیس خواجہ' میں جگہ نہ پا سکے" — it contains
  the circumstances of those awliya whose circumstances **could not find a place** in the first two books
  (p. 18). **Decisive for how the archive uses this book**: its coverage is the residue of two earlier
  volumes, not a survey, so an absence here is not evidence of anything.
- **Seventy saints, and the categories of material promised.** "اس کتاب میں نہایت اختصار کے ساتھ ہفتاد
  یعنی ستر اولیائے کرام کے حالات" — with the greatest brevity, the circumstances of **haftād, that is
  seventy**, awliya — followed by the list of what is recorded for each: "حالات طیبات، خوارق عادات،
  غرائب و اقعات و حکایت[OCR?]، نادر اسرافات[OCR?]، ترک لذات، مراقبات و مشاہدات، اور ورد و وظائف و
  معمولات، کلمات طیبات و تعلیمات، کشف و کرامات، مجاہدات و عبادات و ریاضات، مکشوفات اور مقامات" (p. 18).
  **Note what is absent from the list: no shrine, no urs, no custodianship, no endowment.** The author's
  own programme is biographical and devotional, not institutional — and the Hujwiri entry bears that out,
  giving the mazar a single sentence. The contents list's seventy chapters (باب ۱ to باب ۷۰, pp. 15–16)
  agree with the stated count.
- **A claim of priority.** "یہ کتاب اپنی نوعیت کی پہلی کوشش ہے" — this book is the **first attempt of
  its kind** — followed by "اگر میری یہ کتاب ان اولیائے کرام نے قبول فرمائی تو یہ میری محنت کا بہترین
  صلہ ہوگا", if these awliya accept this book of mine it will be the best reward of my labour (p. 18).
  The claim is asserted without argument, and the reward sought is the saints' acceptance, not the
  reader's.
- **The author's theory of what a wali is, stated as social doctrine.** The preface's opening pages argue
  that the awliya "ذہنی غلامی سے آزاد ہیں۔ فائدے کی امید اور نقصان کا خوف ان کو محکوم اور غلام نہیں کر
  سکتا", are free of mental slavery, and that "انہوں نے ایک ایسے قانون حیات کی بنیاد ڈالی جس کی رو سے
  انسان کا انسان سے محبت نہ کرنا ایک '**جرم**' ہے" — they laid the foundation of a law of life under
  which for one human not to love another is a **crime**; the object of that law is "ایک ایسے سماج کی
  تشکیل", the formation of a society in which love, humanity, service, sympathy, brotherhood, **مساوات**
  equality, sacrifice, truth, sincerity, forbearance, gratitude and submission are supreme (p. 17). A
  modern egalitarian reading of sainthood placed at the head of a traditional tazkirah.
- **Death years reported in parallel, not adjudicated.** For Hujwiri the book prints 465ھ with one source
  and then 456ھ as the view of "بعض", some, with another, and takes no side (p. 23, folio 19). The same
  even-handedness governs the two arrival accounts (pp. 22–23, folios 18–19). **This author reports
  variants; he does not correct them** — unlike the edited sibling tazkirah, there is no editorial hand
  here overruling the text.
- **One unsourced etymological judgement.** The statement that Hujwir is a quarter of Ghazni and not a
  city (p. 21, folio 17) is the only assertion in the chapter offered flatly, with no footnote, against
  twenty footnoted ones.
- **A hagiography with a citation apparatus, and its narrow base.** Chapter 1's twenty حواشی rest on six
  works only, four of them read in **Urdu translation** ("اردو ترجمہ") and two in Persian (p. 29,
  folio 25). Nine of the twenty point to a single book, کشف الاسرار (اردو ترجمہ), and three to
  سفینۃ الاولیاء at three different pages (ص 16، 164، 364). The book is a compilation from printed and
  translated tazkirahs, and says so page by page.

## Citable passages

- "گنج بخش فیض عالم مظہر نور خدا / ناقصاں را پیر کامل کاملاں را رہنما" — the couplet the book makes the
  origin of the laqab: "Ganj Bakhsh, grace of the world, manifestation of God's light; perfect pir to the
  deficient, guide to the perfect." (p. 22, folio 18)
- "تم کو لاہور کا قطب بنایا گیا ہے۔ لاہور تمہارے سپرد کیا گیا ہے۔ لاہور جاؤ۔" — the pir in the dream:
  "You have been made the qutb of Lahore. Lahore has been entrusted to you. Go to Lahore." (p. 22,
  folio 18)
- "مزار پر انوار لاہور میں فیوض و برکات کا سرچشمہ ہے۔" — the entry's whole statement about the shrine:
  "The radiant mazar in Lahore is a fountainhead of graces and blessings." (p. 23, folio 19)
- "فقیر کو چاہیے کہ بادشاہوں اور حاکموں کی آشنائی و معرفت کو سخت خطرناک سانپوں اور بڑے بھاری اژدہاؤں کی
  معرفت و آشنائی سمجھے۔" — "A faqir should reckon acquaintance with kings and rulers as acquaintance with
  venomous snakes and great dragons." (p. 25, folio 21)
- "جہاں جہاں اس نے قدم رکھا' وہاں سے مٹی کھود کر پھینک دو۔" — the shaikh on the ruler's servant who
  carried cash into his khanqah: "Wherever he set foot, dig up the earth there and throw it away."
  (p. 33, folio 29)

## Doubts

- **PROVENANCE OF THE FILE, recorded once.** (a) A publisher's rights line: "**جملہ حقوق محفوظ ہیں**",
  all rights reserved, heading the colophon, with first edition **October 1999** and print run **1100**
  (p. 8) — this is a modern in-copyright print. (b) A **handwritten ownership inscription in ink** across
  the upper half of the inner title page: "فقیر عبد ال[illegible] دوم [illegible] نقشبندی [OCR?]", Faqir
  Abd al-[illegible], with "نقشبندی", Naqshbandi, in the name — **the owner's name is not recoverable
  from the transcription**. (c) A **handwritten number at an angle in the upper corner** of the same
  page: "28.0102 [OCR?]", which looks like an accession or shelf number but is not identified as one
  (p. 5). (d) A **rectangular publisher's stamp printed over the endpaper pattern**: "پروگریسو بکس
  [OCR?]   ۲۴ بی اردو بازار لاہور [OCR?]" with the number "7352795" (p. 3). **The two publisher
  addresses and the two telephone numbers in this chunk do not agree**: the p. 3 stamp gives **۲۴ بی
  اردو بازار لاہور** and **7352795**, while the p. 7 title page gives **۳۰-بی، اُردو بازار، لاہور
  [OCR?]** and **فون: ۶۳۵۲۶۹۵ [OCR?]**. A human must decide which is this copy's imprint before either
  is cited.
- **THE PREFACE DATE CONTRADICTS THE EDITION DATE BY THIRTY-FOUR YEARS.** The preface is signed
  "**۴، اپریل ۱۹۶۵ء [OCR?]**", 4 April 1965, at Ajmer Sharif (p. 18); the colophon says "**بار اول |
  اکتوبر 1999ء**", first edition October 1999 (p. 8). Both are in the chunk. The book therefore either
  reprints a 1965 preface in a 1999 Lahore edition described as the first, or one of the two dates is
  wrong; the preface date is additionally `[OCR?]`. **Nothing should be dated from this book without
  saying which of the two years is meant**, and the 1999 Lahore imprint must not be treated as the
  book's date of composition.
- **THE TITLE IS GIVEN IN THREE FORMS.** Cover: "تذکرہ اولیائے پاک و ہند" (p. 1). Both inner title
  pages: "**جدید** تذکرۂ / تذکرہ اولیائے پاک و ہند" with the subtitle "**خُم خانۂ تصوف / خُم خانۂ
  تصوّف**" (pp. 5, 7) — i.e. *Jadid*, "New", plus a subtitle *Khum Khana-e Tasawwuf* that appears
  nowhere else. Colophon: "تذکرہ اولیاء پاک و ہند" (p. 8). The word **جدید** and the subtitle are absent
  from the cover and the colophon. Cite the form with its page.
- **PDF page 17 and printed page 17 are different pages, and must never be conflated.** The contents
  list sends باب ۱ to printed page **۱۷** (p. 15), and printed 17 is the `[folio 17]` marker at **PDF
  p. 21**. PDF p. 17 is the first page of the دیباچہ, in the unfoliated front matter. Every printed page
  number in the contents list is a **folio**, i.e. PDF index − 4 as far as this chunk can show.
- **CONTENTS-LIST NAMES THAT RESEMBLE ARCHIVE ROWS — LOCATORS ONLY, NONE OF THEM MAPPABLE FROM THIS
  CHUNK.** The contents (pp. 15–16) gives name and page only, with no town, date or silsila, so not one
  of the following may be attached to a row on its strength; each must wait for the chunk that carries
  the actual entry:
  - "باب ۷ حضرت شیخ بہاؤالدین زکریا ۴۴" resembles **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (Multan, 1267,
    Suhrawardi). His appearance in the body of the chunk (p. 34, folio 30) is as Tabrizi's travelling
    companion, with **no town, no death year and no shrine** — so still not mappable.
  - "باب ۸ حضرت بابا فرید الدین مسعود گنج شکرؒ ۵۲" resembles **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar**
    (Pakpattan, 1266).
  - "باب ۱۷ حضرت شیخ رکن الدین ابو الفتح ۱۰۹" resembles **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**, whose archive
    figure is "Shah Rukn-e-Alam (Rukn-ud-Din Abul Fath)" — but the contents gives no Multan.
  - "باب ۵۰ حضرت مادھو لال حسین ۲۴۵" resembles **madho-lal-hussain**; "باب ۵۱ حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی ۲۵۱"
    resembles **darbar-abul-muali-qadri**; "باب ۵۳ حضرت میاں میر ۲۶۵" resembles **shrine-of-mian-mir**;
    "باب ۶۰ حضرت سلطان باہو ۳۰۹" resembles **garh-maharaja-shorkot**. All four are Lahore or Jhang rows
    and all four are plausible, but the contents says nothing but the name.
  - "باب ۲۲ حضرت سید جلال الدین بخاری ۱۲۹" **could be either of two archive rows at Uch Sharif** —
    **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari** (d. 1291) or **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht**
    (Sayyid Jalaluddin, d. 1384), whose archive names share both "Sayyid Jalaluddin" and "Bukhari".
    Do not pick one.
  - "باب ۶۷ حضرت خواجہ محمد سلیمان ۳۵۹" resembles
    **darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a**, but the contents gives no Taunsa and no
    date; the name alone fits many.
  - "باب ۵۴ حضرت شاہ بلاول ۲۷۴" bears on **shah-noorani-shrine-syed-bilawal-shah-noorani** (Syed Bilawal
    Shah Noorani, Khuzdar) only through the element **بلاول**. The book's name has no "Noorani" and no
    Balochistan. Do not map.
- **TWO CONTENTS-LIST NAMES ARE ACTIVE TRAPS FOR THIS ARCHIVE.**
  - "**باب ۷۰ حضرت حاجی وارث علی شاہ ۳۷۸**" (p. 16) **must not be mapped to mausoleum-of-waris-shah.**
    The archive row is **Waris Shah** the poet at Jandiala Sher Khan, Sheikhupura, d. 1798, Chishti. The
    contents names **حاجی وارث علی شاہ**, Haji Waris Ali Shah — a different name with the honorific
    **حاجی** and the element **علی**. The book gives no town and no date. A name-similarity match here
    would put the wrong person in a Pakistani row.
  - "**باب ۴۰ حضرت شاہ کمال کیتھلی ۲۰۴**" (p. 16) **must not be mapped to shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal.**
    The nisba is **کیتھلی**, of Kaithal; the archive row is **Baba Shah Kamal** at Muslim Town /
    Samanabad, **Lahore**, Qadiri. The book gives no Lahore, no date and no order. Leave unmapped. (The
    sibling tazkirah's chunk 012 flagged the identical trap for the identical name.)
- **حضرت شیخ حسن زنجانی is left unmapped, and the reason should be checked by a human.** The chunk names
  him twice, as "خواجہ حسن زنجانی" and "حضرت شیخ حسن زنجانی", places him **in Lahore** before Hujwiri's
  arrival, and has his janaza met at the city gate (pp. 22–23, folios 18–19). The archive's
  **shrine-of-miran-hussain-zanjani** is Syed **Miran Hussain** Zanjani at **Chah Miran, Lahore**, died
  1042. **The given name differs — حسن, Hasan, not حسین/میران حسین — and the chunk gives no grave, no
  quarter, no death year and no silsila for him**, only that he was living in Lahore and died there. Per
  the archive's mapping rule (location of the shrine not stated) he is recorded under *Other saints*
  instead. A human should decide whether the book means that row's figure; if it does, this chunk
  supplies a **name variant** worth recording on the row.
- **The Hujwiri death year and the archive row.** The book gives **465ھ** and, as a minority view,
  **456ھ** (p. 23, folio 19); the archive row **data-darbar** carries **1072**. The chunk never gives a
  Christian-era year for anything, so the correspondence between "465ھ" and "1072" is **not something
  this chunk establishes** and is not asserted here. Record the book's two Hijri years with their
  sources, and let a human settle the era. The minority reading **456ھ**, nine years earlier, is the real
  variant and should not be dropped.
- **Chapter numbering in the contents list breaks down at the end of حصہ پنجم.** After "باب ۴۷ حضرت شیخ
  حمزہ دہر سوی[OCR?] ۲۳۰" the next two entries are printed "**باب ۴۱**[OCR?] حضرت شیخ سلیم چشتی ۲۳۲" and
  "**باب ۴۲**[OCR?] حضرت شیخ جلال الدین تھانیسری ۲۳۷", and the following part opens at "باب ۵۰" (p. 16).
  Both numbers are OCR-flagged and both repeat numbers already used earlier in the same part. The page

  not. Whether the print or the transcription is at fault cannot be told from the chunk.
