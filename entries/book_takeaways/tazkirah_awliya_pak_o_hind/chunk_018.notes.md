# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 018 — pp. 341–359

**The tail of the Bulleh Shah entry, the opening of "حصہ ہشتم" (Part Eight), and chapters 64 to 66.**
The chunk runs: the closing two-thirds of an entry whose heading lies outside this chunk, whose subject
the book identifies only by his takhallus "بلھے شاہ" and whose mazar it places at **قصور** (Kasur)
(pp. 341–343, folios 337–339); the divider "حصہ ہشتم" with one Persian couplet (p. 345, folio 341);
باب 64 حضرت خواجہ نور محمد مہاروی (pp. 347–350, folios 343–346); باب 65 حضرت سید عبداللہ قادری
(pp. 351–353, folios 347–349); and باب 66 حضرت شاہ نیاز احمد, breaking off inside a numbered list of
اذکار و اشغال (pp. 354–359, folios 350–355).

**Two archive rows are engaged, both by the first entry** — the Kasur shrine of the saint whose
takhallus is given as بلھے شاہ, and his pir, whom the book places at **لاہور**. Chapters 64 to 66 are
stationed by the book at **مہارو / مہار**, **دہلی**, **رام پور**, **سرہند** and **بریلی** — none of
which the archive carries — and none of their subjects is mapped. Three figures inside those chapters
carry names that match archive rows (Sultan Bahu, Khwaja Muhammad Sulaiman, Hafiz Muhammad Jamal
Multani) and are **deliberately left unmapped**: each appears as a bare name in a list or an anecdote,
with no town, no date and no silsila. See Doubts.

**The chunk's institutional yield is unusually high**: a daily cash stipend, a priced four-item
condition for bai'at, a jagir and a named village gifted by a Nawab, an offering for mosque
construction refused, a relic installed on a mazar by testamentary instruction, a chronogram engraved
on a mazar door, a wilayat withdrawn and restored, a six-months-here-six-months-there residence rule,
an initiation timed to an urs, and a saint's ruling on whether a تعزیہ may be made.

**Folio relation**: folio = PDF index − 4 on every page that prints a marker (p. 341 → folio 337
through p. 359 → folio 355). Pages 344 and 346 are marked `[blank page]` and print no marker, so
folios 340 and 342 never appear. Recorded as observed; not used to supply any folio.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** (the book gives no chapter heading and no personal name inside this chunk;
  its only naming of him is "آپ کا تخلص بھی بلھے شاہ تھا" — his takhallus too was Bulleh Shah, p. 343,
  folio 339):
  - **Death and mazar, in one sentence**: "آپ کا وصال 1171ھ میں ہوا۔ مزار قصور میں واقع ہے" — his
    wisal took place in **۱۱۷۱ھ** (1171 AH) and his **mazar is situated at قصور** (Kasur) (p. 343,
    folio 339). The book gives no month, no day and no weekday, and states no CE year. On the archive
    row's "1757", see Doubts.
  - **Pir and initiation**: he was sent to "حضرت شاہ عنایت اللہ قادری رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" and took bai'at
    at his hand in **لاہور**, receiving the **خرقہ خلافت** afterwards — "آپ کو بیعت سے مشرف فرمایا اور
    بعدہ خرقہ خلافت عطاء کیا" (p. 342, folio 338).
  - **Two residences before Kasur**: after some time in his pir's service he "چناب کے کنارے رہنے لگے
    اور عبادت و مجاہدہ میں مشغول ہوئے" — settled on the bank of the **چناب** (Chenab) and occupied
    himself with worship and mujahada (p. 342, folio 338); "آپ نے پھر قصور میں سکونت اختیار کی" — he
    then took up residence at **قصور** (Kasur), busied in "تعلیم و تلقین و تبلیغ" (pp. 342–343,
    folios 338–339).
  - **His wilayat withdrawn and restored**: "آپ کے پیرو مرشد آپ سے کسی بات پر خفا ہو گئے۔ انہوں نے آپ
    کی ولایت سلب کرلی" — his pir became displeased over some matter and **stripped him of his
    wilayat**; he went to **گوالیار** (Gwalior), attended at the mazar of Shaikh Muhammad Ghaus
    Gwaliari, and "کچھ دنوں کے بعد آپ کے پیرو مرشد آپ سے خوش ہو گئے اور ولایت آپ کو عطا کی" — after
    some days his pir was pleased and granted him the wilayat again (p. 343, folio 339).
  - **Music and sama'**: "آپ کو سماع کا بہت شوق تھا۔ فن موسیقی سے بخوبی واقف تھے۔ آپ شاعر بھی تھے" —
    he was greatly fond of **سماع**, well acquainted with the art of music, and was also a poet (p. 343,
    folio 339); at Gwalior he "فن موسیقی میں کمال حاصل کیا", attained mastery in the art of music
    (p. 343, folio 339). "اپنے پیرو مرشد سے والہانہ عشق تھا" — he had a rapturous love for his pir
    (p. 343, folio 339).
  - **Personal name: (not stated)** in this chunk. **Father's name: (not stated)** — the father acts
    repeatedly (pp. 341, 343; folios 337, 339) but is never named. **Silsila: (not stated)** as an
    order-name for him; it is carried only by his pir's nisba "قادری" (pp. 341–342, folios 337–338).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A daily cash stipend assigned in a vision.** On parting, Sayyid Abd al-Hakim told the subject
  "ہمارے پاس تمہاری امانت تھی' جو آج ہم نے تمہارے سپرد کر دی۔ آج سے تم کو دس روپیہ روز ملا کریں گے' اب
  یہ ضروری ہے کہ تم مرشد کامل کی تلاش کرو" — your trust was with us and today we have handed it over to
  you; **from today you will receive ten rupees a day**; now it is necessary that you seek a perfect
  murshid (p. 341, folio 337). The source of the ten rupees, and whether it is a cash grant or a
  spiritual figure of speech, is **(not stated)**. The chunk's only recurring-income figure.
- **A priced condition on bai'at, in four items.** "حضرت عنایت اللہ قادری رحمۃ اللہ علیہ نے آپ سے
  فرمایا کہ اس شرط پر بیعت کریں گے کہ پہلے وہ پانسو روپے نقد' پانسو روپے کا ایک گھوڑا' پانسو روپے کے
  طلائی کنگنوں کی ایک جوڑی اور پانسو روپے کی ایک پوشاک لے کر آئیں" — he would give bai'at on the
  condition that the man first bring **five hundred rupees in cash, a horse worth five hundred rupees,
  a pair of gold bangles worth five hundred rupees, and a suit of clothes worth five hundred rupees**
  (p. 342, folio 338). **The most transferable economic statement in the chunk: a shrine-founder's
  initiation fee, itemised and priced.** The items are later delivered exactly (p. 342, folio 338) and
  the printed figure "پانسو" is not OCR-flagged.
- **A father's outfitting of a disciple.** "آپ کے والد نے بخوشی آپ کو اجازت دی۔ کچھ روپے اور دستار آپ
  کو دی۔ حضرت عنایت اللہ قادری کی خدمت میں پیش کریں" — the father gladly gave permission and gave him
  **some rupees and a دستار** (turban) to present at Inayat Allah Qadiri's service (p. 341, folio 337).
- **Wilayat as a grant that can be revoked.** "انہوں نے آپ کی ولایت سلب کرلی" — the pir **stripped him
  of his wilayat**; and after some days "آپ کے پیرو مرشد آپ سے خوش ہو گئے اور ولایت آپ کو عطا کی" — was
  pleased and **granted the wilayat** back (p. 343, folio 339). No document, seal or witness is named.
- **A whole field made over as نذر.** After the cattle-and-snake episode, and after the field proved
  "پہلے سے زیادہ شاداب" (greener than before), "جیون خاں نے وہ سارا کھیت آپ کے والد کو نذر کر دیا" —
  Jiwan Khan **gifted the entire field to the saint's father** (p. 343, folio 339). **A land transfer
  to a saintly family, with the donor named and the occasion given.**
- **An initiation timed to an urs.** "حضرت سلطان المشائخ کے عرس کے روز آپ حضرت مولانا فخر الدین فخر
  جہاں کے دست حق پرست پر بیعت ہوئے" — on the **day of the urs of Hazrat Sultan al-Mashaikh** he took
  bai'at at the hand of Maulana Fakhr al-Din Fakhr Jahan (p. 347, folio 343, حاشیہ 6 = انوار العارفین
  فارسی ص 455). **The urs is named only by title; the saint's personal name and the place of the urs
  are (not stated).** The book adds that this was the first bai'at the Maulana gave in Delhi (p. 347,
  folio 343).
- **A six-months-and-six-months residence rule.** "حضرت مولانا نے چونتیس سال دہلی میں قیام فرمایا۔ آپ
  چھ ماہ اپنے وطن مہارو میں اور چھ ماہ اپنے پیرو مرشد کے ساتھ دہلی میں رہتے تھے" — the Maulana resided
  **thirty-four years in Delhi**, and Nur Muhammad Maharvi spent **six months in his home town مہارو
  and six months with his pir in Delhi** (p. 348, folio 344). A quantified circuit between a provincial
  seat and a capital khanqah.
- **A pilgrimage to Pakpattan, with no shrine named.** "حضرت مولانا کے ساتھ آپ پاک پٹن گئے تھے" — he
  went with the Maulana to **پاک پٹن** (Pakpattan) (p. 348, folio 344). The chunk names no shrine, no
  saint and no occasion there — see Doubts.
- **Bai'at delegated with full force.** Of Hafiz Sharaf al-Din, made a murid on his behalf, the pir said
  "بیعت ایشاں بیعت من است" — their bai'at is my bai'at (p. 348, folio 344, حاشیہ 10). A transferable
  formula for delegated initiation.
- **A jagir and a named village granted by a ruler.** At Rampur, "نواب فیض اللہ خاں صاحب والی رام پور
  آپ کے حلقہ ارادت میں داخل ہوئے"; after taking bai'at he pressed the saint to settle there, and on his
  insistence "نواب صاحب نے آپ کو جاگیر پیش کی۔ آغا پور گاؤں آپ کو نذر کیا" — the Nawab **presented him
  a jagir and gifted him the village of آغا پور** (pp. 351–352, folios 347–348, حاشیہ 4 = انوار
  العارفین فارسی ص 522). **The clearest endowment statement in the chunk: a state grant of revenue and
  a named village to a living saint, in exchange for residence.**
- **A mosque built, collapsed and rebuilt, and the ruler's building fund refused.** "رام پور میں آپ نے
  ایک عالی شان مسجد تعمیر کرائی" — he had a **grand mosque** built at Rampur; warned by ilham that the
  structure under construction would fall, he cleared the masons and labourers and it fell; "آپ نے از
  سر نو مسجد کی تعمیر کی", he rebuilt it from scratch; "نواب فیض اللہ صاحب نے مسجد کی تعمیر کے واسطے
  روپیہ نذر کرنا چاہا' لیکن آپ اس بات پر راضی نہ ہوئے اور نواب صاحب کی نذر قبول نہیں فرمائی" — the
  Nawab wished to offer money for the mosque's construction but the saint **would not agree and did not
  accept the Nawab's نذر** (p. 352, folio 348). **A ruler's construction money refused by the same
  saint who took his jagir — the sharpest institutional contrast in the chunk.**
- **Offerings received and spent to nothing.** "فتوحات بکثرت آتی تھیں۔ آپ اس میں کچھ نہیں بچاتے تھے۔ سب
  غرباء فقراء اور مساکین پر خرچ کر دیتے تھے" — **futuhat** came in abundance, he saved nothing of it,
  and spent it all on the poor, the fuqara and the destitute (p. 353, folio 349, حاشیہ 8). No amount is
  given.
- **A chronogram engraved on the mazar door.** "آپ کے مزار مبارک دروازے پر آپ کی تاریخ وفات کندہ ہے" —
  **his date of death is engraved on the door of his mazar**, and the five printed couplets are given,
  closing with the figure "(1207 ہجری)" (p. 352, folio 348, حاشیہ 6 = کرامات نظامیہ ص 27). The verse
  "بیکشنبہ[OCR?] دہ و چار از محرم" gives the day as **ten and four of Muharram**, agreeing with the
  prose "14 محرم 1207ھ" on the same page; the weekday word is OCR-flagged.
- **A relic installed on a mazar by testamentary instruction.** "آپ کی وصیت کے مطابق جو قدم شریف آپ
  بغداد سے اپنے ہمراہ لائے تھے' وہ آپ کے مزار پر نصب کر دیا گیا" — in accordance with his **وصیت**
  (will), the **قدم شریف** he had brought with him from **بغداد** was **installed on his mazar**
  (p. 352, folio 348). A dated relic transfer, a testamentary clause and a fixture of shrine
  architecture in one sentence.
- **A mazar described as a general destination.** "مزار فیض آثار رام پور میں مرجع خاص و عام ہے" — the
  mazar at Rampur is a **resort of high and low** (p. 352, folio 348). The chunk's only visitor
  statement, and it carries no number.
- **A palanquin as a public honour, twice.** At Delhi the named notables "آپ کی پالکی کو ان بزرگوں نے
  اپنے کاندھے پر رکھا" — placed his **پالکی** on their own shoulders (p. 351, folio 347); and generally
  "جس شہر میں آپ تشریف لے جاتے' وہاں کے لوگ آپ کی پالکی کو کاندھا دینا اپنے لئے باعث سعادت سمجھتے تھے"
  — in whatever city he entered, the people counted shouldering his palanquin a cause of blessedness
  (p. 353, folio 349, حاشیہ 8). The same instrument reappears in باب 66, where a **کہار**
  (palanquin-bearer) of Fakhr Jahan is received at Shah Niaz Ahmad's khanqah (p. 357, folio 353).
- **A named wage, paid double.** Settling accounts with all the labourers at evening, Sayyid Abdullah
  Qadiri gave the one who had dug under his musalla "دوگنی مزدوری یہ کہہ کر دی کہ آدھی مزدوری دن کی ہے
  اور آدھی مزدوری رات کی ہے" — **double wages**, half for the day's work and half for the night's
  (p. 353, folio 349). Construction labour paid daily, in cash, at a site with no visible funds.
- **A form of bai'at invented to avoid a conflict of claims, and named.** Fakhr Jahan took Shah Niaz
  Ahmad into training "لیکن بیعت نہیں کیا" because his maternal grandfather had already placed his hand
  on the boy's; on the boy's insistence he initiated him "اس طرح سے … کہ ہاتھ پر ہاتھ تو نہیں رکھا'
  بلکہ اپنا دامن پکڑا کر بیعت فرمایا اور اس کا نام 'بیعت طالبی' رکھا" — not hand upon hand, but by
  having him grasp his **دامن**, and he **named it "بیعت طالبی"** (p. 355, folio 351). **A named
  variant rite, created to manage a competing prior claim on a disciple.**
- **Succession documents: an Arabic خلافت نامہ and a دستار.** Sayyid Abdullah Baghdadi "خلافت سے آپ کو
  سرفراز فرمایا' عربی میں خلافت نامہ لکھ کر آپ کو دیا اور اپنی دستار آپ کو مرحمت فرمائی" — gave him
  khilafat, **wrote him a certificate of succession in Arabic**, and bestowed **his own turban** on him
  (p. 356, folio 352). No seal or countersignature is mentioned.
- **A hand-over of ritual technique, counted.** At the same initiation he "ذکر نفی و اثبات کے باون
  طریقے آپ کو بتائے" — taught him the **fifty-two (باون) methods** of the dhikr of negation and
  affirmation (p. 356, folio 352). The later list heading counts these as **51** — see Doubts.
- **Sajjada nashini by primogeniture.** "آپ کے بڑے صاحب زادے صاحب حضرت شاہ نظام حسین رحمۃ اللہ علیہ آپ
  کے سجادہ نشین" — his eldest son became his **سجادہ نشین**, with a long list of "مقتدر خلفاء" beside
  him (pp. 356–357, folios 352–353). The term is the book's own.
- **A khanqah's floor protocol, reversed for a palanquin-bearer.** A کہار of Fakhr Jahan's palanquin
  came to Shah Niaz Ahmad's **خانقاہ**; "آپ اس کو لینے کی غرض سے خانقاہ کے صحن تک تشریف لے گئے' اس کہار
  کے ہاتھوں کو بوسہ دیا اور نہایت عزت و احترام کے ساتھ اس کو اپنی مسند کے پاس بٹھایا' اس کو نذر پیش کی
  اور رخصت کیا" — he went out to the **courtyard** to receive him, kissed his hands, seated him beside
  his own **مسند**, **presented him نذر**, and dismissed him (p. 357, folio 353, حاشیہ 5). **A shrine
  head paying the offering instead of receiving it.**
- **A saint's ruling on whether a تعزیہ may be made.** Asked about the تعزیہ he answered "اگر بنا نہیں
  ہو گا تو میں اجازت تعزیہ بنانے کی نہیں دوں گا۔ اس واسطے کہ کماحقہ' اس کی تعظیم نہ ہو سکے گی اور جو
  بن گیا ہو گا تو جتنا ممکن ہو گا' تعظیم کروں گا" — if one has not been made **I will not give
  permission to make a تعزیہ**, because it cannot be honoured as it deserves; but one already made I
  will honour as far as possible (pp. 358–359, folios 354–355, حاشیہ 8). **A named ritual object placed
  under the shaykh's permission, with a reason of ritual capacity, not of legality.**
- **A ruling on سماع, given as an evasion.** Asked "سماع کے متعلق آپ کا کیا حکم ہے؟" he answered "ڈھولک
  کی آواز ایسی کان میں بھری ہے کہ دوسری بات سنائی نہیں دیتی" — the sound of the **ڈھولک** so fills the
  ear that nothing else can be heard (p. 358, folio 354). The book prints no further gloss.
- **A ruling on cursing Yazid.** "آج تک اللہ تعالٰی نے میری زبان کو اس نام خبیث سے بچایا ہے… میں تو لعن
  یا غیر لعن اس کا نام ہی نہیں لیتا' اتنی دیر حسین حسین کیوں نہ کہوں" — he will not utter the name at
  all, whether in cursing or otherwise, and would rather say "Husain, Husain" for that long (p. 359,
  folio 355).
- **A nightly and daily office, timed.** "آپ رات کو بارہ بجے کے قریب اٹھتے' وضو کرتے اور تہجد پڑھتے۔
  تہجد سے فارغ ہو کر خاندانی وظیفہ پڑھتے۔ بعد ازاں بارہ تسبیح ضرب کی ادا کرتے۔ صبح کی نماز پڑھ کر تھوڑی
  دیر آرام کرتے" — rose about **twelve at night**, performed wudu and **tahajjud**, then the
  **خاندانی وظیفہ** (family litany), then **twelve tasbih of ضرب**, then the dawn prayer, then rested
  a little (p. 357, folio 353, حاشیہ 7). The phrase "خاندانی وظیفہ" names a litany as family property.
- **A numbered inventory of اذکار و اشغال, broken off.** Under "اذکار و اشغال" the book lists what
  reached him from the mashaikh beyond the current usage, headed "ذکر نفی اثبات 51 طریقے سے" and
  numbered (1) to (38), ending "شغل جامع" where the chunk stops (pp. 359, folio 355, حاشیہ 9). Named
  items include "اشغال خاندان قادریہ", "پاس انفاس کئی طریقوں سے", "ذکر جاروب", "ذکر حدادی", "ذکر سلطان
  الاذکار", "شغل غوثیہ", "شغل کشف القبور و القرآن", "شغل غوطہ", "شغل فوارہ", "شغل طاقچہ", "شغل
  سرگوشی", "شغل کاسہ" and "شغل سفر در وطن"; several carry `[OCR?]` flags — see Doubts. **A khanqah's
  practice-repertoire itemised as an inheritance, and the first item is named for the Qadiri house.**
- **A mosque as a residence.** Sayyid Abdullah Qadiri "دہلی پہنچ کر جامع مسجد میں قیام فرمایا" (p. 351,
  folio 347); Sayyid Abdullah Baghdadi likewise "جامع مسجد دہلی میں مقیم ہیں" (pp. 356, folio 352).
  The Delhi Jami Masjid houses incoming saints twice in this chunk.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Milk that lit the breast.** The book relates that Sayyid Abd al-Hakim drank a little of the bowl of
  milk and gave the rest to the subject; "دودھ پیتے ہی آپ از خود رفتہ ہو گئے۔ آپ کا سینہ روشن ہو گیا" —
  on drinking it he passed out of himself and his breast became illuminated (p. 341, folio 337).
- **A living man located by muraqaba.** The book relates that the father, asked where the elder was now
  to be found, "مراقبہ کرکے آپ کو بتایا کہ وہ بزرگ موضع ساندہ میں رونق افروز ہیں" — performed muraqaba
  and told him the elder was gracing the village of Sanda (p. 341, folio 337).
- **A masked horseman at the Ravi who never came out of the river.** Under the heading "غیبی امداد"
  (unseen help) the book relates that as the despairing subject sat on the bank of the **راوی** having
  resolved to drown himself, a masked man on horseback arrived, asked him to hold the horse and guard
  his things while he bathed, handed over "ایک تھیلی جس میں پانچ سو روپے تھے' طلائی کنگنوں کی جوڑی'
  پوشاک اور گھوڑا" — a purse of five hundred rupees, the pair of gold bangles, the suit and the horse —
  and went into the river; he waited long, and when the man did not emerge assumed he had drowned and
  went to the city, where people cried out that the clothes and horse were Shah Inayat Allah's (p. 342,
  folio 338). **The four items match the four-item condition exactly**; the book draws no conclusion
  and Shah Inayat Allah merely "آپ کو دیکھ کر مسکرائے", smiled on seeing him (p. 342, folio 338).
- **A cobra's hood as a parasol, and a field left greener.** Under "کرامت" the book relates that the
  field's owner, searching for the sleeping boy, "دیکھا کہ آپ محو خواب ہیں اور ناگ سانپ اپنے پھن کا
  سایہ کئے ہوئے آپ کے پاس کھڑا ہے" — found him deep in sleep with a cobra standing beside him making
  the shade of its hood; the owner reported him dead, the father came with some men, "سانپ غائب ہو گیا'
  آپ اٹھ بیٹھے", the snake vanished and he sat up; and on inspection "واقعی کھیت پہلے سے زیادہ شاداب
  تھا", the field was truly greener than before (p. 343, folio 339).
- **Thoughts read as they came and went.** Under "کشف و کرامات" the book relates of Nur Muhammad
  Maharvi "ہر آنے جانے والے کے مافی الضمیر کو آپ بتا دیتے تھے" — he would tell every comer and goer
  what was in his mind (p. 349, folio 345).
- **Presence at a funeral a hundred kos away.** The book relates that his murid Ghulam Husain died "سو
  کوس کے فاصلہ پر" and "آپ کو مولوی غلام حسین کے جنازہ کے ہمراہ لوگوں نے دیکھا" — people saw him
  accompanying the bier (p. 349, folio 345).
- **A mosque's collapse foretold and its workmen cleared.** The book relates that while the Rampur
  mosque was under construction Sayyid Abdullah Qadiri received "الہام و مکاشفہ حضرت غوث الاعظم کی
  طرف" that the building would fall; "آپ فوراً خلوت سے باہر آئے اور معماروں اور مزدوروں کو باہر آنے کی
  تاکید فرمائی' جب معمار اور مزدور باہر آگئے' مسجد گر گئی" — he came straight out of seclusion and
  ordered the masons and labourers out, and when they were out the mosque fell (p. 352, folio 348).
- **A musalla that yielded money, and a night's wage paid for a theft attempt.** Under "کرامت" the book
  relates that he had no visible wealth, but drew what was needed from beneath the **مصلے** on which he
  sat; a labourer, thinking all the money buried there, dug the ground by night and found nothing,
  levelled it again and told no one; the next day the saint again drew from beneath the musalla, and at
  the evening reckoning gave that labourer double wages, "آدھی مزدوری دن کی ہے اور آدھی مزدوری رات کی
  ہے" — half for the day and half for the night; the man was ashamed and begged forgiveness (p. 353,
  folio 349).
- **A bai'at accepted in a dream by Piran Pir, and a face shown.** The book quotes Fakhr Jahan telling
  Shah Niaz Ahmad "میاں! آج شب میں حضرت جناب پیران پیر قدس سرہ العزیز نے تمہاری بیعت اپنے دست مبارک پر
  قبول فرمائی اور مجھ کو ایک صورت دکھلائی ہے اور فرمایا کہ اپنی خاص اولاد میں سے ان کو بھیجتا ہوں بظاہر
  ران[OCR?] کے ہاتھ پر تکمیل کرا دینا" — last night Piran Pir accepted your bai'at at his own blessed
  hand, showed me a face, and said he is sending one of his special progeny, at whose hand the
  completion is to be effected outwardly (p. 355, folio 351, حاشیہ 1).
- **A rebuke delivered from the unseen after six months.** The book relates that about six months passed
  with no such elder arriving, until one morning Fakhr Jahan brought the news that Piran Pir says "ہمارے
  فرزند مرسلہ کو آج تین روز دہلی پہنچے گزرے اور تم ان سے غافل ہو" — three days have passed since our
  sent son reached Delhi and you are heedless of him (p. 355, folio 351, حاشیہ 2); men were sent out,
  the elder was found in the Jami Masjid, and his description made Fakhr Jahan certain (p. 356,
  folio 352).
- **A recognition on sight.** The book relates that Sayyid Abdullah Baghdadi, on seeing Shah Niaz Ahmad,
  said at once "انہیں کی صورت مجھے دکھلائی گئی تھی' جن کے لئے میں بھیجا گیا ہوں" — his was the face
  shown to me, for whom I have been sent (p. 356, folio 352, حاشیہ 3).
- **A ghazal that induced a state.** The book relates that when Shah Niaz Ahmad recited his ghazal
  beginning "امشب انست کہ زد حلقہ جہاں بردرما" to Mirza Jan Janan Mazhar Shaheed, "حضرت مرزا صاحب پر
  وجدانی کیفیت طاری ہوئی" — an ecstatic state came over Mirza Sahib (p. 358, folio 354).
- **A fire set for the sake of a glance** — narrated by Nur Muhammad Maharvi about Sultan Bahu, as
  evidence in a debate on نفی وجود rather than as a karamat: he burnt his own hut so that the crowd
  would gather and the boy step out of the house, "اور سلطان باہو نے اس بہانے سے اپنے محبوب کو دیکھ لیا"
  (p. 349, folio 345).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Every chapter in this chunk is footnoted to a named earlier work, and the canon is four titles**:
  "تکملہ سیر الاولیاء (فارسی)" (p. 350, folio 346, حواشی 1، 5، 7، 9، 10), "انوار العارفین (فارسی)"
  (p. 350, folio 346, حواشی 2، 3، 6، 8، 11; p. 353, folio 349, حواشی 2، 4، 5، 7، 8), "مناقب المحجوبین
  [OCR?]" (p. 350, folio 346, حاشیہ 4) and "کرامات نظامیہ" (p. 353, folio 349, حواشی 1، 3، 6). **The
  compiler cites at the level of the single fact** — قوم, father, birth, name, laqab, bai'at, children,
  death, khalifas each carry their own marker in باب 64 — and nothing in the chunk is presented as his
  own inquiry.
- **He prints two reasons for one journey and chooses neither.** Of Sayyid Abdullah Qadiri's coming to
  India: "ہندوستان میں آپ کے تشریف لانے کی دو وجوہات بتائی جاتی ہیں" — two reasons are *said* to be
  given; the first (grief) is footnoted to انوار العارفین, the second (a commission from Ghaus al-Azam
  to initiate Shah Niaz Ahmad) to کرامات نظامیہ (p. 351, folio 347). **The passive "بتائی جاتی ہیں"
  carries the attribution, exactly as the variant formula "یہ بھی کہا جاتا ہے" does elsewhere in the
  book.**
- **Two chapters are built to justify a third.** باب 65's second reason for Sayyid Abdullah Qadiri's
  journey is the initiation of Shah Niaz Ahmad (p. 351, folio 347), and باب 66 then narrates that
  initiation at length from the other side (pp. 355–356, folios 351–352), including the same figure's
  arrival at the Delhi Jami Masjid. **The compiler has arranged consecutive chapters to interlock; the
  two accounts agree on the mosque, the sending and the recognition.**
- **A saint's erotic anecdote used as doctrinal evidence.** The Sultan Bahu story is introduced not as
  karamat but as an argument: "ایک دن مسئلہ نفی وجود پر بحث ہوئی۔ آپ نے سلطان باہو کے عشق کا قصہ بیان
  فرمایا" — a debate arose on the question of the negation of existence and he related the tale of
  Sultan Bahu's love (p. 349, folio 345). **The book records the use and supplies no interpretation of
  it.**
- **A distinction between ulama and ahl-Allah, given as the saint's own reasoning.** Asked why the ulama
  do not honour unbelievers while the ahl-Allah honour believer and unbeliever alike, though shariat and
  haqiqat do not conflict, he answered "وجہ یہ ہے کہ علماء کی نظر ان کے کفر پر پڑتی ہے اور اہل اللہ کی
  نظر مظہریت اور حقیقت پر پڑتی ہے نہ کہ کفر پر" — the ulama's gaze falls on their unbelief, the
  ahl-Allah's on manifestation and reality, not on unbelief (pp. 348–349, folios 344–345, حاشیہ 11).
- **Rulings reproduced verbatim as answers, in numbered order.** باب 66's "تعلیمات" is set out as
  "پہلا سوال / دوسرا سوال / تیسرا سوال" with each answer in quotation marks and one footnote covering
  the set (pp. 358–359, folios 354–355, حاشیہ 8). **The compiler reports the sama', تعزیہ and Yazid
  answers without comment, including the evasive one.**
- **Entries follow the same grid of Urdu sub-headings as elsewhere in the book**, applied unevenly:
  باب 64 runs خاندانی حالات، والد ماجد، ولادت، نام، لقب، تعلیم و تربیت، بیعت و خلافت، پیرو مرشد کی
  خدمت، شادی اور اولاد، وفات، خلفاء، سیرت، تعلیمات، اقوال، کشف و کرامات (pp. 347–349, folios 343–345);
  باب 66 adds دستور العمل، علمی ذوق، شعر و شاعری، اذکار و اشغال (pp. 357–359, folios 353–355). **The
  absence of a heading is the book's silence, not a gap in the transcription** — باب 65, for instance,
  has no ولادت and no نام heading at all (pp. 351–353, folios 347–349).

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the subject is never given a personal name here.** Pages 341–343
  (folios 337–339) continue an entry whose chapter number, heading and نام/ولادت sections lie in the
  previous chunk. The **only** naming in this chunk is the sirat line "آپ کا تخلص بھی بلھے شاہ تھا"
  (p. 343, folio 339), i.e. the takhallus, with "بھی" ("too") implying the name as well. The mapping to
  **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** therefore rests on that takhallus plus "مزار قصور میں واقع ہے" (p. 343,
  folio 339) — town and name both agreeing with the row — but the row's personal name "Abdullah Shah
  Qadri" is **not** in this chunk. A human reading the previous chunk should confirm the heading.
- **Death year: book "1171ھ" (p. 343, folio 339) against the row's "1757".** Recorded as printed, with
  no conversion. The book gives no CE year, no month and no day; the row's value is not addressed by the
  book.
- **The Shah Inayat mapping is the weaker of the two and should be checked.** For
  **shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri** the book gives: residence at **لاہور** (p. 341, folio 337), the nisba
  **قادری** (pp. 341–342, folios 337–338), and the role of pir to the Kasur saint (p. 342, folio 338).
  It gives **no mazar, no death year, no silsila-name beyond "قادری", and no locality within Lahore**.
  The row reads "Shah Inayat Qadiri", "Mozang Chungi, Queens Road, Lahore", "c. 1728", "Qadri
  Shattari". The book prints the name as **"شاہ عنایت اللہ قادری"** and **"عنایت اللہ قادری"** — with
  اللہ, where the row has none. The town agrees and the order partly agrees; the identification is one
  statement short of secure.
- **Do not conflate the book's two "Inayat" figures.** باب 66 names an ancestor of Shah Niaz Ahmad as
  **"حضرت شاہ ایت اللہ علوی"** [so printed, not OCR-flagged] who left the throne of Bukhara and came to
  **ملتان** (p. 354, folio 350). This is a different man from شاہ عنایت اللہ قادری of Lahore, and the
  printed form is one syllable short of عنایت اللہ. Record as printed; do not emend.
- **سلطان باہو is not mapped, and this is deliberate.** He resembles **garh-maharaja-shorkot** (archive
  location "Garh Maharaja (Shorkot), Jhang, Punjab"; principal figure "Sultan Bahu"; died 1691; Sarwari
  Qadiri). The chunk gives him **no town, no district, no mazar, no death year and no silsila** — he
  appears only as the subject of a single anecdote told by another saint in a debate (p. 349,
  folio 345). Bare name; left open.
- **حضرت خواجہ محمد سلیمان is not mapped.** He resembles
  **darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a** (Taunsa Sharif; died 1850;
  Chishti-Nizami). The chunk gives him nothing but a place in Nur Muhammad Maharvi's khalifa list
  (p. 348, folio 344) — **no nisba, no town, no date**. Bare name; left open.
- **حافظ محمد جمال ملتانی is not mapped, though the name matches element for element.** He resembles
  **shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani** (archive location "Near Aam Khas Bagh, outside Daulat
  Gate, Multan City, Punjab"; principal figure "Hafiz Muhammad Jamal Multani"; died 1811; Chishti). The
  book's "حافظ محمد جمال ملتانی" carries the row's nisba, and he stands in the khalifa list of a saint
  whose own chain the chunk traces to Fakhr al-Din Fakhr Jahan of Delhi (p. 348, folio 344). **But the
  chunk gives him no shrine statement, no death year and no silsila-name** — a nisba inside a list is
  not a placement. **This is the likeliest of the three to be a genuine match and a human should settle
  it.**
- **پاک پٹن is named with no shrine and no saint attached.** "حضرت مولانا کے ساتھ آپ پاک پٹن گئے تھے"
  (p. 348, folio 344) is the whole statement. The archive's **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** is at
  Pakpattan; the chunk does not connect the visit to it, to any mazar, or to any occasion, so nothing is
  mapped and nothing should be inferred from the trip.
- **"بی بی غریب نواز" is a laqab of Shah Niaz Ahmad's mother, and nothing else.** "آپ کی والدہ ماجدہ کا
  نام بی بی لاڈو ہے۔ ان کو بی بی غریب نواز کے لقب سے پکارا جاتا ہے" (p. 354, folio 350). Given that
  "غریب نواز" is one of this book's established traps, note explicitly: **this figure is a woman of
  Sirhind and Delhi in باب 66, is not connected here to Ajmer or to Lahore, and must not touch
  khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz.**
- **The book's باب 66 subject bears the name that appears inside an archive row's silsila field.** The
  row **wasif-ali-wasif** records allegiance to "Hazrat Shah Niyaz Ahmad Chisti Qadri"; the chunk's
  "حضرت شاہ نیاز احمد" has takhallus "نیاز" (p. 358, folio 354), died "6 جمادی الثانی 1250ھ" and has
  his mazar at **بریلی** (p. 356, folio 352), and his initiation here is Qadiri via Baghdad (p. 356,
  folio 352) with no order-name printed. **This is not a mapping** — the row's principal figure is
  someone else entirely — but a human may want to check whether the row's pir-name refers to this man.
- **Grandfather named two ways, neither flagged.** The mother is "صاحب زادی سید مولانا سعد الدین کی"
  (p. 354, folio 350), while the man who placed his hand on the boy's is "سعید الدین رضوی" and "مولانا
  سعید الدین رضوی" (p. 355, folio 351). **سعد الدین** and **سعید الدین** both stand as printed.
- **The count of dhikr methods contradicts itself.** "ذکر نفی و اثبات کے باون طریقے آپ کو بتائے" —
  fifty-two methods (p. 356, folio 352) — against the list heading "ذکر نفی اثبات 51 طریقے سے" —
  fifty-one (p. 359, folio 355). Neither figure is OCR-flagged.
- **The heir's name contradicts itself across two lines.** "حضرت شاہ نظام حسین" is named the sajjada
  nashin (p. 356, folio 352) and "حضرت شاہ نظام الدین حسین" heads the khalifa list on the next page
  (p. 357, folio 353). Do not reconcile.
- **A doubled sentence in the ازواج و اولاد paragraph.** "لڑکی کا انتقال کے بعد آپ نے دوسری شادی کی۔
  دوسری بیوی سے ایک لڑکی اور دو لڑکے پیدا ہوئے۔" is printed twice in succession at p. 356 (folio 352),
  the second time running straight on into "لڑکی کا انتقال صغر سنی میں ہو گیا". Read as one
  second marriage with one daughter and two sons; the duplication is a transcription or print artefact
  and the page should be re-checked before the family is described.
- **The subject's own name in باب 66 is OCR-flagged.** "آپ کا نام نامی راز احمد[OCR?] ہے" (p. 354,
  folio 350) against the laqab "نیاز احمد" on the same page. **Do not state his personal name from this
  chunk.**
- **Two spellings of the home town of Nur Muhammad Maharvi, neither flagged.** The father settled at
  **"مہار"** (p. 347, folio 343); his own وطن and his mazar are at **"مہارو"** (p. 348, folio 344,
  twice). His nisba throughout is "مہاروی". His birthplace **"کوٹ چوتالہ [OCR?]"** is flagged (p. 347,
  folio 343) and should not be used as a place-name without the page image.
- **باب 66's حواشی block falls outside this chunk.** Markers 1 to 9 appear in the text (pp. 355–359,
  folios 351–355) with **no حواشی block transcribed**, because the chapter breaks off mid-list. None of
  باب 66's quotations can be attributed to a named source from this chunk alone.
- **The chunk closes mid-list.** باب 66's اذکار و اشغال list stops at "(38) شغل جامع" (p. 359,
  folio 355); the remaining items, the حواشی, and anything after them are outside this chunk. Nothing
