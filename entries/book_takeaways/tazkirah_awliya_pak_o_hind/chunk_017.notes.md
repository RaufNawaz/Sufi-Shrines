# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 017 — pp. 325–340

**The tail of one entry, the whole of باب 62, and the opening of باب 63.** The chunk runs: the closing
two-thirds of an entry on a saint the book calls only **"میران" / "میران جی"**, whose mazar it places at
**کہرام** (Kahram) and whose death it dates "5 رمضان المبارک 1131ھ" (pp. 325–331, folios 321–327,
with its thirteen-item حواشی block on p. 331, folio 327); **باب 62 حضرت شاہ نظام الدین اورنگ آبادی**,
whose mazar it places at **اورنگ آباد** (Aurangabad) (pp. 332–339, folios 328–335, حواشی on p. 339,
folio 335); and **باب 63 حضرت بلھے شاہ**, breaking off mid-sentence inside a dream narrative (p. 340,
folio 336).

**One archive row is engaged, and its identification is one statement short** — `mazar-of-bulleh-shah`,
on the book's own "عبداللہ شاہ" / "بلھے شاہ" pair plus **قصور** (Kasur) as the only town it gives him.
One row is **actively excluded**: the book's حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی lives at "اینتھہ [OCR?]", not Lahore,
and is the living pir of a man who died 1131 AH, where `darbar-abul-muali-qadri` has Lahore and 1024 AH.
Everything else here is stationed at **کہرام, ملوی [OCR?], اینتھہ [OCR?], سرہند, دہلی, اورنگ آباد,
کاکوری, دکن, شکر کھیڑہ** or **مدینہ منورہ**, none of which the chunk connects to an archive location.

**The chunk's value for this project is heavily institutional and economic**: an offering-sharing rule
that sends the surplus upward to the pir's seat, an open langar, an imperial farman routed through a
provincial governor, a نذرانہ from the throne accepted with difficulty, Friday offerings treated
differently from the rest of the week, an urs held in the saint's own khanqah in his lifetime, a
territorial ولایت assignment, and two named mosques as a saint's successive residences — one of them
stated to stand near his mazar.

**Folio relation, as observed on this chunk only**: every page prints a marker and folio = PDF index − 4
throughout (p. 325 → folio 321 … p. 340 → folio 336). No page in this chunk is marked `[blank page]`,
so the run is unbroken here. Do not project this relation beyond the chunk.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **An offering-sharing rule that routes the surplus upward to the pir's seat, printed beside an open
  langar.** Of the Kahram saint: "نذرانہ جو آتا' اس میں سے خادموں کے خرچ کے واسطے نکال کر باقی اپنے
  پیرو مرشد کو پیش کرتے' آپ کا لنگر عام تھا" — whatever **نذرانہ** came, he took out of it the
  **expenses of the خادم** (servants/attendants) and presented **the remainder to his pir**; his
  **langar was عام**, open to all (p. 329, folio 325). **This is the most transferable institutional
  fact in the chunk**: a named order of claims on shrine income — attendants first, the parent seat
  second — with a general kitchen alongside it, at a shrine whose own upkeep is otherwise undescribed.
- **Friday's offerings treated differently from the rest of the week.** Of Shah Nizam al-Din
  Aurangabadi: "چلتے وقت ہر شخص کو کچھ نہ کچھ دیتے' جمعہ کے علاوہ جو نذرانہ آتا' وہ آپ محتاجوں کو دے
  دیتے تھے" — when walking he gave something to everyone, and **whatever نذرانہ came apart from Friday
  he gave away to the needy** (p. 336, folio 332). **What became of Friday's offerings is (not
  stated)** — see Doubts; the sentence establishes only that Friday was excepted from the give-away rule.
- **An imperial farman routed through a provincial governor, and a throne-offering accepted with
  difficulty.** In a rainless year of Muhammad Shah's reign "بادشاہ کو آپ کا نام بتایا گیا کہ اگر آپ
  دعا کریں تو یقیناً بارش ہو" (p. 328, folio 324); "محمد شاہ نے سرہند کے حاکم کے نام ایک فرمان جاری
  کیا' جس میں اس نے آپ کو دہلی لانے کی تاکید کی اور ایک عریضہ آپ کی خدمت میں بھیجا۔ آپ نے معذرت چاہی'
  جس وقت آپ کو خط ملا' وہاں خوب بارش ہوئی' محمد شاہ نے آپ کو نذرانہ بھیجا' جو آپ نے مشکل قبول فرمایا" —
  Muhammad Shah issued a **فرمان** in the name of the **حاکم of سرہند** enjoining that the saint be
  brought to **دہلی**, and sent an **عریضہ** to him; he excused himself; rain fell where he was at the
  moment the letter reached him; the emperor **sent him a نذرانہ, which he accepted with difficulty**
  (p. 329, folio 325). **A shrine's refusal of a summons to the capital, and a qualified acceptance of
  royal money.**
- **A royal distribution in kind, and a prayer for dynastic continuity refused on jurisdictional
  grounds.** "ایک مرتبہ محمد شاہ نے نواب روشن الدولہ کو آپ کی خدمت میں بھیجا' آپ کی خدمت میں کچھ مٹھائی
  اور کچھ کپڑے محتاجوں کو تقسیم کرنے کے لئے بھیجے اور آپ سے اس امر کی دعا چاہی کہ اس کی اولاد میں ہمیشہ
  سلطنت رہے" — the emperor sent Nawab Roshan al-Daula with **sweets and cloth to be distributed to the
  needy** and asked for a prayer that **sovereignty remain always in his progeny**; the answer was that
  Muhammad Shah's sovereignty came by Nizam al-Din Auliya's intercession, which covered "دو پشت", two
  generations, "اس میں کس کی مجال کہ دخل دے" (p. 329, folio 325). **A saint's shrine as a channel for
  imperial almsgiving, and a stated limit on what intercession can be asked to do.**
- **An urs held in the saint's own khanqah during his lifetime, with qawwals singing Arabic verse.**
  "ایک روز آپ کی خانقاہ میں عرس ہو رہے تھے' قوال عربی کے اشعار گا رہے تھے" — one day an **عرس** was
  taking place in his **خانقاہ** and the **قوال** were singing **Arabic** verses (p. 337, folio 333).
  The saint stopped a debate there with "وقت بحث کا نہیں ہے' سماع سننے کا ہے" — the time is not for
  dispute but for hearing **سماع** (p. 337, folio 333). **The chunk's only use of the word عرس, and it
  is of a living saint's khanqah, not of a death anniversary at a tomb.**
- **A sama assembly run behind a closed door, with a standing exclusion rule and its override.** At
  Shaikh Kalimullah's khanqah in Delhi "اس وقت مجلس سماع گرم تھی۔ دروازہ بند تھا۔ حضرت کا یہ دستور تھا
  کہ اجنبی شخص کو مجلس سماع میں شرکت کی اجازت نہیں دیتے تھے" — the sama assembly was in full swing, the
  door was shut, and it was his **دستور** not to allow a stranger to take part (p. 332, folio 328). A
  knock was investigated by a deputed attendant who "آپ کا نام نامی و احوال گرامی معلوم کرکے" reported
  name and circumstances, and admission was granted (p. 333, folio 329, حاشیہ 2); the objection
  "دخل اجنبی در مجلس سماع دستور حضور نیست" was overruled (p. 333, folio 329, حاشیہ 3). **A documented
  door-policy at a khanqah, and the shaykh's personal power to suspend it.**
- **ولایت as a territorial assignment, with a residence order attached.** "آپ کے پیرو مرشد حضرت شیخ
  کلیم اللہ شاہجہاں آبادی نے دکن کی ولایت آپ کے سپرد فرمائی اور ہدایت فرمائی کہ اورنگ آباد میں جا کر
  مستقل سکونت اختیار کریں" — his pir entrusted him with the **ولایت of the دکن** and directed him to
  take **permanent residence at اورنگ آباد** (p. 335, folio 331). The shrine at Aurangabad is thus
  presented as the outcome of an assignment made at Delhi.
- **The regalia of khilafat, itemised, and delivered at the disciple's town rather than the pir's.**
  The Kahram saint's pir had ordered him at parting "کہ اینتھہ نہ آئیں' جب مناسب ہو گا' وہ خود بھی
  کہرام آئیں گے" — not to come to Aintha; when it was fitting, he would himself come to Kahram; "کچھ
  عرصے کے بعد آپ کے پیر روشن ضمیر کہرام میں رونق افروز ہوئے اور آپ کو پیراہن' کلاہ' جامہ اور چادر عنایت
  فرمائی" — after some time his pir came to Kahram and bestowed **پیراہن (shirt), کلاہ (cap), جامہ
  (robe) and چادر (sheet)**; he demurred "بصد [OCR?] عاجزی" with "بندہ کو اس لباس کے پہننے کی لیاقت
  نہیں ہے", was answered "میں کہتا ہوں اور تم عذر کرتے ہو", wore the dress at command, and "آپ کے پیرو
  مرشد نے آپ کو خلافت سے سرفراز فرمایا" (p. 328, folio 324, حاشیہ 11). **A four-item investiture list
  and a visitation in the reverse direction.**
- **Two mosques as a saint's successive residences, the second located by reference to his mazar.**
  "آپ کہرام [OCR?] پہنچے اور محمد فاضل قانون گو کی مسجد میں رہنے لگے' کچھ عرصے اس میں قیام کیا' پھر ایک
  دوسری مسجد میں جو آپ کے مزار کے قریب ہے' رہنا شروع کیا" — he reached Kahram and began living in
  **محمد فاضل قانون گو's mosque**, stayed some time, then began living in **another mosque, which is
  near his mazar** (p. 327, folio 323). **The chunk's one topographical statement about the Kahram
  shrine's setting**, and it is made in the book's present tense about a mosque, not about the tomb.
- **Assigned service at a dervish's establishment, with a term and a task.** At موضع ملوی [OCR?] "آپ
  کے سپرد خدمت تھی کہ بھاڑ کے لئے لکڑیاں جمع کیا کریں" — the service assigned to him was to **gather
  firewood for the بھاڑ** (oven), over "قریب ایک سال", about a year (p. 325, folio 321).
- **Building and repair work performed as service.** A شہتیر was made "اپنے گھر کی چھت پاٹنے کے لئے", to
  roof the dervish's house, and the murids were ordered to lift it (p. 325, folio 321); and at the pir's
  establishment "آپ کے پیرو مرشد کے حجرے کی چھت خراب ہو گئی۔ سب مریدوں نے چھت کی مرمت کی' لیکن چھت ٹھیک
  نہیں ہوئی" — the roof of the pir's **حجرہ** went bad, all the murids repaired it and it did not come
  right, and the disciple was called out of **چلہ** to do it: "آپ نے گھاس اکھاڑی' پھر مٹی اور پانی ڈال
  کر چھت کو کوٹنا شروع کیا", he pulled up the grass, then put on mud and water and began pounding
  (p. 330, folio 326). **The chunk's only description of shrine-precinct fabric maintenance, and it is
  mud-and-grass roofing done by disciples.**
- **A murid's stipend of food, and the saint's rate of consumption.** "آپ نے ایک شخص سے کھانے کے واسطے
  فرمایا' وہ روزانہ آپ کے واسطے کھانا لاتا تھا' لیکن آپ چھ سات روز کے بعد ایک روٹی پانی میں تر کر کے
  تناول فرماتے تھے" — he told a man about food, and the man brought food for him daily, but he ate one
  roti soaked in water after six or seven days (p. 327, folio 323).
- **A counted khalifa establishment.** "آپ کے بیالیس خلیفہ تھے" — he had **forty-two khalifas**, of whom
  the famous are listed (p. 329, folio 325, حاشیہ 12). For Shah Nizam al-Din Aurangabadi: "آپ کے ایک
  لاکھ سے زیادہ مرید تھے" — **more than one lakh murids** — with eleven famous khalifas named and the
  sajjada nashini held by his son (p. 335, folio 331). **The only two membership figures in the chunk,
  and both are round.**
- **A quantified daily zikr obligation, with a penalty attached to the dervish's food and cloak.** "آپ
  اپنے مریدوں کو ذکر اسم ذات جہر کے ساتھ تلقین کرتے تھے" — he instructed his murids in the **ذکر اسم
  ذات** aloud (جہر); and "فقیر کو چاہیے کہ ایک لاکھ مرتبہ ذکر اسم ذات کیا کرے' اگر چالیس مرتبہ ہر روز نہ
  کرے تو اس کو لقمہ درویشی و دلق حرام ہے" — a faqir should perform the zikr of the ism-e zat **one lakh
  times**; if he does not do it **forty times each day**, the **morsel of dervishhood (لقمہ درویشی) and
  the دلق (patched cloak) are حرام to him** (p. 330, folio 326). **The clearest statement in the chunk
  of a devotional quota tied to a material entitlement**; on the two numbers, see Doubts.
- **A sleep rule for disciples.** "آپ اپنے مریدوں کو آدھی رات سے زیادہ سونے کی اجازت نہیں دیتے تھے" — he
  did not allow his murids to sleep more than half the night (p. 330, folio 326).
- **A nightly office and a shajra-directed فاتحہ.** Shah Nizam al-Din Aurangabadi "پاس انفاس کی بہت
  تاکید فرماتے ہیں", and "سوتے وقت سو مرتبہ تہلیل پڑھے اور اپنے اہل شجرہ کی ارواح پاک کو فاتحہ کا تحفہ
  پیش کرنا چاہیے" — at sleeping time one should recite the **تہلیل a hundred times** and present the
  **gift of فاتحہ to the pure souls of one's اہل شجرہ**, the people of one's spiritual genealogy
  (p. 337, folio 333). **A named transfer of merit along the silsila's own roll.**
- **Zikr jahr defended against any time-restriction.** "ذکر جہر کسی وقت بھی منع نہیں ہے' بلکہ ہر وقت
  مامور و ماجور ہے" — zikr aloud is forbidden at no time; rather it is at all times commanded and
  rewarded (p. 336, folio 332, حاشیہ 12 = نظام القلوب); and among his اقوال, "جو فائدہ میں نے ذکر جہر
  میں دیکھا ہے' کسی چیز میں نہیں پایا", and "ذکر خلو معدہ کے وقت کرنا چاہیے' خصوصا مبتدی کو اس کا لحاظ
  رکھنا ضروری ہے" — zikr should be done on an empty stomach, especially the beginner (pp. 336–337,
  folios 332–333).
- **Two signet rings, with their inscriptions.** Under the heading "سبع [OCR?] مہر": one نگیں carried
  "ذکر مولٰی از ہمہ اولٰی", and the other "در رعایت دلہا بکوش   نظام دیں بدنیا مفروش" (p. 336,
  folio 332, حاشیہ 11 = تکملہ سیر الاولیاء (فارسی) ص 96). **Neither is glossed.**
- **A marriage arranged out of the khanqah.** After the jogan took bai'at, "اس کی خواہش کے مطابق اس کا
  نکاح سعید بیگ سے کر دیا" — her نکاح with the murid Saeed Beg was performed in accordance with her own
  wish (p. 338, folio 334).
- **A maxim on the pir's obligation, printed as a standalone "فرمان".** "پیر کو مرید شناس چاہیے" — a pir
  must be a knower of murids (p. 330, folio 326).
- **Employment as a schoolmaster's deputy and as a household tutor.** "خلیفہ مکتب کے فرائض بحسن و خوبی
  انجام دیئے" (p. 325, folio 321, حاشیہ 7) and the faujdar's engagement of him to teach his son on the
  journey to his posting (p. 325, folio 321) — the chunk's two paid or quasi-paid offices before the
  saintly career begins.
- **A family's staged migration, as the setting of a shrine's origin.** Bullhe Shah's father moved from
  **اچ** to **ملکوال** and thence to **پانڈو [OCR?]** (p. 340, folio 336), and Bullhe Shah himself went
  to **قصور** to study under Hafiz Ghulam Murtaza (p. 340, folio 336).
- **The mazar's present-tense function, stated for both completed entries.** Kahram: "مزار پر انوار
  کہرام میں حاجت روائے خلق ہے" — the luminous mazar at Kahram is the **fulfiller of the people's needs**
  (p. 329, folio 325). Aurangabad: "مزار اقدس اورنگ آباد میں زیارت گاہ خاص و عام ہے" — the most holy
  mazar at Aurangabad is a **زیارت گاہ for خاص و عام**, elite and commons (p. 335, folio 331).
  **Neither entry states an urs date, a custodian after the first generation, an endowment, a land
  grant or a visitor number.**

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **A beam no one could lift, lengthened at a touch.** The author relates that the شہتیر was so heavy
  "کہ کسی سے نہیں اٹھا' سب زور کرکے رہ گئے", and that he lifted it alone onto the wall; "وہ شہتیر کسی قدر
  چھوٹا تھا' آپ کا ہاتھ لگنے سے وہ پورا ہو گیا" — the beam was somewhat short and became complete at the
  touch of his hand (p. 325, folio 321).
- **Three days senseless on parting from the pir.** The author reports that on reaching ملوی [OCR?] he
  "تین دن بے ہوش رہے۔ آپ کے منہ سے کف جاری تھا", lay three days unconscious with foam issuing from his
  mouth, and came to after three days (p. 326, folio 322).
- **A single beard-hair located by kashf across a journey.** Under the heading "کشف" the author relates
  that he learned "کہ آپ کے پیرو مرشد حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی کا داڑھی کا ایک بال بوریہ پر گرا ہے", that a
  hair of his pir's beard had fallen on the mat; he travelled to Aintha, found it, and kept it (p. 327,
  folio 323). The book frames the outcome as a rebuke, not a credential (حاشیہ 9).
- **A fowl roasted three days that cleansed the heart.** "آپ کے پیرو مرشد نے آپ کو ایک مرغ مرغن تین روز
  تک بھون کر کھلایا' اس کے کھانے سے آپ کو صفائی قلب حاصل ہوئی" — his pir fed him a fattened fowl roasted
  for three days, and from eating it he obtained **صفائی قلب** (p. 327, folio 323).
- **Kahram to Aintha between noon and sunset, and a river crossed dry-shod.** The author reports that he
  set out after the messenger and "آفتاب غروب [OCR?] ہونے سے ذرا پہلے آپ اینتھہ پہنچ" — reached Aintha
  a little before sunset — having left Kahram "آج ہی دوپہر", that very noon; asked how he crossed the
  دریا, he answered "پانی کے اوپر چلا آیا اور پاؤں میرے تر نہیں ہوئے", I came over the water and my feet
  were not wetted (pp. 327–328, folios 323–324). **The book immediately prints the pir's corrective:
  "اسباب ظاہر کی رعایت ضروری ہے", and records that the return journey was made "کشتی سے", by boat**
  (p. 328, folio 324).
- **Rain at the moment the emperor's letter arrived.** "جس وقت آپ کو خط ملا' وہاں خوب بارش ہوئی" — at the
  time he received the letter it rained well there (p. 329, folio 325). The saint is not said to have
  prayed.
- **A muraqaba that read the term of an earlier saint's intercession.** "آپ نے مراقبہ کیا اور فرمایا کہ
  محمد شاہ کو حضرت نظام الدین اولیاء رحمۃ اللہ علیہ [OCR?] کی سفارش سے سلطنت ملی ہے" and that the
  intercession ran to two generations (p. 329, folio 325).
- **A station manifested at every stroke of the roof-beater.** "جتنی بار کوٹتے تھے' ہر بار ہر ضرب پر ایک
  مقام ظاہر ہوتا تھا" — each time he pounded, at every stroke a **مقام** was manifested; his pir had
  said beforehand "میران جی سے چھت درست ہو گی" (p. 330, folio 326).
- **A boy of ten restored, with the death timed and the corpse produced.** The author relates that a
  murid of موضع نوندھن [OCR?] shut his dead son's نعش in a کوٹھری and offered food; the saint refused it
  until the boy should come and eat; pressed, the murid admitted "لڑکا آپ کے آنے سے دو ساعت پہلے مر گیا'
  اس کے نعش کوٹھری میں رکھی ہے"; the saint said "لڑکا مرا نہیں ہے' جا کر دیکھو' اگر سوتا ہو تو جگا کر
  لاؤ"; the man found the boy breathing, shook him, and the boy sat up and came out and kissed the
  saint's feet (pp. 330–331, folios 326–327). **The single most elaborate karamat in the chunk.**
- **A Medina disciple overcome at the sight of Shaikh Kalimullah.** The author relates that a murid of
  Shaikh Yahya Madani came from Medina and "حضرت شیخ کو دیکھتے ہیں اس پر وجدانی کیفیت طاری ہوئی اور وہ
  بے ہوش ہو گیا", fell into a state of ecstasy and became unconscious on seeing the Shaikh (p. 333,
  folio 329, حاشیہ 4); the variant account repeats it with the detail that this came after Yahya
  Madani's death (p. 334, folio 330).
- **A pir at Medina naming an unborn disciple, his appearance and his opening couplet.** The author
  relates on حاشیہ 7 that Shaikh Yahya Madani told Kalimullah "کہ اس شکل و شباہت و صورت کا ایک شخص ان
  کے… پاس آئے گا' اس کا نام نظام الدین ہو گا۔ اور وہ عین دعوت الی اللہ کے وقت مندرجہ بالا شعر پڑھے گا"
  — a man of such form would come, his name would be Nizam al-Din, and he would recite that very couplet
  (p. 334, folio 330).
- **A stranger's two false names, real name, residence and education told out.** The author relates that
  the saint said "فقیروں سے جھوٹ بولنا اچھی بات نہیں", then later "نہ تمہارا نام عبداللہ ہے اور نہ
  عبدالغنی", and then told his real name, residence and education, "وہ شخص شرمندہ اور حیران ہوا اور آپ
  کا معتقد و منقاد ہوا" (p. 337, folio 333).
- **A sandal-mark on the tents as a dated sign of victory.** The author relates that the saint did
  muraqaba and said "کہ کل صبح بروز پنج شنبہ ان کے ڈیروں پر صندل کا نشان ظاہر ہو گا' یہ اشارہ ان کے لئے
  کافی ہے' یہ فتح کی علامت ہے'" — tomorrow morning, on **پنج شنبہ** (Thursday), a mark of **صندل** would
  appear on their tents; "ایسا ہی ہوا", so it happened, and Nawab Asaf Jah gained the victory (p. 337,
  folio 333).
- **A jogan who came of her own accord after being shown to the saint.** The author relates that the
  other murids contrived to bring the saint to where the jogan was staying, "وہ جوگن آپ کے سامنے پیش کی
  گئی"; the next day, at the saint's order, she approached Saeed Beg and said "کہ وہ حضرت کی خانقاہ جانا
  چاہتی ہے", she wished to go to the Hazrat's khanqah, and there she took bai'at (p. 338, folio 334).

  بیٹھے ہوئے ہوا میں اڑ رہے ہیں", and that the throne came down and the ancestor asked "تم کون ہو؟",

## Arguments and interpretations

- **Every entry here is footnoted page by page to a short, closed list of named earlier works, and the
  compiler presents nothing as his own inquiry.** For the Kahram entry the canon is **"انوار العارفین
  (فارسی)"** (حواشی 1–3, 5–7, 9–12) and **"بستان معرفت"** (حواشی 4, 13), cited at pp. 413–420 and
  pp. 109, 114 respectively (p. 331, folio 327). For باب 62 it is **"تکملہ سیر الاولیاء (فارسی)"**
  (حواشی 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 11, at pp. 94–106), **"مناقب فخریہ (فارسی)"** (حواشی 2, 4, 6, 9, at pp. 4–5),
  **"خلاصتہ القوائد"** (حاشیہ 8, no page), and the subject's own **"نظام القلوب"** (حاشیہ 12, no page)
  (p. 339, folio 335).
- **The compiler cites his subject's own book as the authority for his subject's teaching.** حاشیہ 12 of
  باب 62 is simply "نظام القلوب۔", supporting the dictum on ذکر جہر (p. 336, folio 332; p. 339,
  folio 335), and the book separately states "آپ کی کتاب 'نظام القلوب' اذکار و اشغال کے متعلق ہے"
  (p. 336, folio 332). **Here the tazkirah's source is the saint, not an intermediate hagiographer.**
- **He prints two accounts of one bai'at without choosing between them.** "آپ کی حضرت شیخ کلیم اللہ شاہ
  جہاں آبادی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ سے بیعت ہونے کی تفصیل اس طرح بھی بیان کی جاتی ہے" — the detail of his
  bai'at is **also related in this way** — and the second version (footnoted to خلاصتہ القوائد) turns on
  the Shaikh's "یہ بھی ایک علم ہے اور یہ علم تم کو بھی تعلیم کیا جائے گا" rather than on the couplet and
  the prophecy of the first (p. 334, folio 330). **The two versions differ on the trigger, on whether
  Shaikh Yahya Madani was alive, and on what was being read at the time.** The book leaves both standing.
- **A karamat is printed and then deflated by the book's own next sentence.** The dry-shod river crossing
  is immediately followed by the pir's "اسباب ظاہر کی رعایت ضروری ہے" and by the return crossing made by
  boat (p. 328, folio 324); the beard-hair kashf is introduced under its own heading and then answered
  with "میران! یہ فقر نہیں ہے' فقر دوسری چیز ہے" (p. 327, folio 323). **The compiler records the
  wonder and the correction with equal weight and does not adjudicate.**
- **A point of tafsir is argued out in the saint's assembly and the saint's reading is given the last
  word.** On "لا یمسہ الا المطھرون" an ālim present held "اس جگہ معنی انشاء میں ہے", the sense is
  prescriptive — "چاہئے کہ نہ چھوئیں قرآن شریف کو' لیکن پاک لوگ (جو حدث اکبر و اصغر سے پاک ہوں)", those
  pure of major and minor ritual impurity; the saint answered "کیا ضرورت ہے کہ اخبار کو معنی انشاء میں
  حمل کریں" — what need to carry a statement into the prescriptive sense — and read it instead as
  "مس نہیں کرے (قرآن و ادراک معنی و اسرار کے کو) مگر پاک لوگ (کہ پاک ہوں آلائش بشری سے)", none touches
  it — the Quran and the grasp of its meanings and secrets — but the pure, pure of **human defilement**
  (pp. 329–330, folios 325–326, حاشیہ 13 = بستان معرفت ص 114). **The dispute is over ritual versus moral
  purity as the condition of access to scripture, and the book sides with the saint by giving him the
  closing speech.**
- **Titles and epithets are asserted flat, without the explanatory narrative the earlier chapters gave
  them.** "حضرت شاہ نظام الدین اورنگ آبادی مصدر [illegible] یزدانی ہیں" (p. 332, folio 328) and
  "حضرت بلھے شاہ قدوۃ الملاں [OCR?] تھے" (p. 340, folio 336) each stand as a single opening sentence
  with no وجہ تسمیہ.
- **The fixed grid of Urdu sub-headings is applied at very different densities.** باب 62 runs about
  twenty headings over eight pages — خاندانی حالات, ولادت باسعادت, نام, تعلیم و تربیت, دہلی میں آمد,
  دربار کلیمی میں باریابی, بیعت و خلافت, فرمان مرشد, اورنگ آباد میں قیام, شادی اور اولاد, وفات, خلفاء,
  سیرت پاک, علمی ذوق, سبع [OCR?] مہر, تعلیمات, ذکر جہر, کمال عشق, اقوال, اوراد و وظائف, کشف و کرامات
  (pp. 332–338, folios 328–334) — while باب 63 has reached only four (والد ماجد, نام, کنیت, تعلیم و
  تربیت, تلاش حق, جد امجد کی زیارت) before the chunk ends (p. 340, folio 336). **Absence of a heading is
  the book's silence, not a gap in the transcription.**

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-entry and the saint is never named.** Pages 325–331 (folios 321–327) complete an
  entry whose heading and chapter number fall before p. 325. The subject is referred to inside the
  chunk only as "میران" and "میران جی", always in another speaker's mouth (pp. 326, 327, 330;
  folios 322, 323, 326) — the book's own narration uses only "آپ". **Do not attribute anything on
  pp. 325–331 to a named saint until the preceding chunk is read.** Everything above is recorded under
  Other saints with its page references, ready to be carried across once the name is in hand.
- **The one archive row whose figure-name overlaps this unnamed saint is excluded on two counts.**
  `shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari` has "Hazrat Meeran Muhammad Shah Bukhari (Mauj Darya)", Lahore,
  died 1604, Suhrawardi. The chunk's saint has his mazar at **کہرام** and died "5 رمضان المبارک 1131ھ"
  (p. 329, folio 325) — a different town and a book-year that cannot be reconciled with the row's CE
  year without a conversion this worker does not make. **Not mapped, and the resemblance is name-only.**
- **`darbar-abul-muali-qadri` is actively excluded, and a human should record the exclusion.** The row
  reads "Darbar Abul Muali Qadri", Lahore, "Hazrat Syed Muhammad Khair ul Deen, known as Shah Abul Muali
  Qadri", "16 Rabi ul Awal 1024 AH". The chunk's **حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی** lives at "اینتھہ [OCR?]"
  (pp. 326, 327, 328; folios 322, 323, 324) — never at Lahore — and is the **living pir** who initiates,
  summons, corrects, invests with khilafat and travels to Kahram to see a disciple who died in 1131 AH
  (pp. 326–329, folios 322–325). Under the mapping rule the differing location alone forbids the map;
  the chronology compounds it. **Two men of the same name, or a different Shah Abu al-Maali.**
- **`mazar-of-bulleh-shah` is mapped, and the mapping is one statement short.** In favour: the book gives
  **two** name elements that both appear in the row — "آپ کا نام عبداللہ شاہ ہے" and "آپ کی کنیت بلھے
  شاہ ہے" (p. 340, folio 336) — and the only town it attaches to him personally, **قصور** (Kasur), is the
  row's location (p. 340, folio 336); the whole family geography it gives (اچ, ملکوال, پانڈو [OCR?],
  قصور) sits on the Pakistan side, with nothing placing him in India. Against: **the chunk states no
  mazar, no death year, no silsila and no pir** — the entry breaks off before بیعت — and Kasur is named
  as his **place of study**, not as his shrine town. The row's "1757" and "Qadiri" are unaddressed.
  **A human should confirm this on the continuation of باب 63 in the next chunk before any fact here
  enters an entry.**
- **The chunk closes mid-sentence.** باب 63 breaks at "انہوں نے آپ کا حسب نسب دریافت فرمایا' آپ"
  (p. 340, folio 336). The rest of the dream, the entry's بیعت, وفات, مزار and خلفاء headings and its
  حواشی block all fall outside this chunk; **no footnote markers are printed on p. 340 at all**, so
  باب 63's footnote numbering cannot be checked from here.
- **Footnote markers 1–6 of the first entry have no anchors in this chunk.** The حواشی block on p. 331
  (folio 327) lists **thirteen** items, but the only markers in the transcribed text are 7 (p. 325),
  8 (p. 325), 9 (p. 327), 10 (p. 328), 11 (p. 328), 12 (p. 329) and 13 (p. 330). Items 1–6 belong to
  pages before p. 325. **باب 62's block is complete and fully anchored**: twelve items on p. 339
  (folio 335), markers 1–12 all present in the text of pp. 332–336.
- **A footnote page number out of sequence and OCR-flagged.** In the first entry's block, حاشیہ 8 reads
  "انوار العارفین (فارسی) ص 45 [OCR?]" where its neighbours run 413, 413, 413, 414, 414, 415, 416, 417,
  417 and "414٬420" (p. 331, folio 327). **"ص 45" is very likely a lost digit but is recorded as
  printed.** حاشیہ 12's "ص 414٬420" prints two page numbers separated by a mark.
- **Chapter numbering, as far as the chunk shows it, is unbroken.** "باب 62" appears at p. 332
  (folio 328) and "باب 63" at p. 340 (folio 336). The opening entry's chapter number is **not printed
  in this chunk**; do not assume it is 61.
- **The saint's own zikr quota carries two numbers that do not agree, and neither is OCR-flagged.**
  "فقیر کو چاہیے کہ ایک لاکھ مرتبہ ذکر اسم ذات کیا کرے' اگر چالیس مرتبہ ہر روز نہ کرے تو اس کو لقمہ
  درویشی و دلق حرام ہے" (p. 330, folio 326) sets **one lakh** and then makes the penalty turn on
  **forty a day**. As printed, the sentence does not say whether the lakh is a lifetime total, a
  completion target, or a figure from which the forty is a minimum. **Quote the sentence whole; do not
  reconcile the two numbers.**
- **باب 62's dates do not close, and both of the disagreeing figures are flagged.** Birth "1060ھ"
  (p. 332, folio 328), death "12 ذیقعدہ 1242ھ [OCR?]" and age at death "بیاسی [OCR?] سال" (p. 335,
  folio 331). Birth plus eighty-two lunar years does not reach 1242; the stated age and the stated death
  year cannot both stand with the stated birth year. **The death year is the more load-bearing figure and
  it is the one carrying an [OCR?] flag.** No year should be taken from this entry without re-reading the

- **The pir's nisba is spelled four different ways inside one entry.** "حضرت شیخ شاہ جہاں آبادی [OCR?]"
  (p. 332, folio 328), "حضرت شیخ کلیم اللہ شاہجہانی آبادی" (p. 332, folio 328), "حضرت شیخ کلیم اللہ
  آبادی" (p. 333, folio 329), "حضرت شیخ کلیم اللہ شاہ جہاں آبادی" (p. 334, folio 330), and in the
  variant account "قطب حضرت شاہ جہانیاں [OCR?] آبادی" (p. 334, folio 330). **The last of these is the
  worrying one**: read literally it names a different person from the Kalimullah of the same paragraph,
  and the form "جہانیاں" is precisely the element that this book elsewhere attaches to Makhdum Jahaniyan
  Jahangasht. **Treat "شاہ جہانیاں [OCR?] آبادی" as a corrupt spelling of the pir's own nisba, flagged,
