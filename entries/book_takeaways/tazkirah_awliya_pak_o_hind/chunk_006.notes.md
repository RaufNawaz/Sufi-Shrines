# tazkirah_awliya_pak_o_hind — chunk 006 — pp. 110–128

**Five chapters and two fragments.** The chunk opens mid-entry in what the chapter numbering makes
**باب 16** (an unnamed "حضرت قلندر صاحب", pp. 110–112, folios 106–108), then runs **باب 17** حضرت شیخ
رکن الدین ابو الفتح (pp. 113–120, folios 109–116), **باب 18** حضرت مولانا فخرالدین مروزی (pp. 121–123,
folios 117–119), **باب 19** حضرت خواجہ حسام الدین سوختہ (p. 124, folio 120), **باب 20** حضرت شیخ صدر
الدین احمد طبیب دلہا (pp. 125–126, folios 121–122), and breaks off inside **باب 21** حضرت شیخ جلال
الدین محمد کبیر الاولیاء (pp. 127–128, folios 123–124). Only **باب 17** is a Pakistani shrine; the rest
are Delhi, Panipat and a place the transcription flags. The chunk's real value for this project is
**باب 17**: a succession narrated as the physical transfer of a turban and a khirqa, a shrine at Multan,
and the most explicit statements of state cash, refused gifts and a spend-nothing-back nazrana policy
so far in this book.

**The folio relation is constant across the chunk**: folio = PDF index − 4, p. 110 → folio 106 through
p. 128 → folio 124, every marker reading exactly that.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** (حضرت شیخ رکن الدین ابو الفتح رحمۃ اللہ علیہ, باب 17, pp. 113–120,
  folios 109–116):
  - **Identification rests on the book's own statements, not the name.** "آپ حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا
    رحمۃ اللہ علیہ ملتانی کے پوتے ہیں" — he is the grandson of Shaikh Baha al-Din Zakariya Multani
    (p. 113, folio 109); his mazar is at Multan (p. 116, folio 112); he inherits "حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین
    سہروردی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کا خرقہ" — the khirqa of Shaikh Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi — from his father
    (p. 114, folio 110). Grandfather, town and the Suhrawardi khirqa together agree with the row.
  - The author's opening encomium: "حضرت شیخ رکن الدین ابو الفتح خورشید سپہر ہدایت ہیں" — he is the sun
    of the sky of guidance (p. 113, folio 109).
  - **Father.** The rubric "والد ماجد" gives "آپ کے والد ماجد کا نام رکن الدین [OCR?] ہے" — his father's
    name is Rukn al-Din[OCR?] (p. 113, folio 109) — but the narrative twice names the father **حضرت شیخ
    صدر الدین عارف** (pp. 114, folio 110). See Doubts.
  - **Mother.** "آپ کی والدہ ماجد بی بی راستی رابعہ عصر اور حافظہ کلام اللہ تھیں' وہ حضرت بہاء الدین
    زکریا رحمۃ اللہ علیہ ملتانی کی مرید تھیں" — his mother **Bibi Rasti**, the Rabia of the age and a
    hafiza of the Quran, was a murid of Baha al-Din Zakariya Multani; "روزانہ ایک قرآن ختم کرنا ان کا
    معمول تھا", completing one Quran daily was her practice (p. 113, folio 109). She is thus both
    daughter-in-law and murid of the grandfather — the text calls him her "خسر", father-in-law (p. 113,
    folio 109).
  - **Birth**: "آپ نے 9 رمضان 649ھ کو بروز جمعہ اس عالم کو زینت بخشی" — **9 Ramadan 649 AH, a Friday**
    (p. 113, folio 109).
  - **Naming.** "آپ کے دادا حضرت بہاؤ الدین زکریا رحمۃ اللہ علیہ نے آپ کا نام رکن الدین رکھا" — his
    grandfather named him Rukn al-Din (p. 113, folio 109). **Kunya**: "ابو الفتح". **Laqab**: "آپ کا لقب
    'فضل اللہ' ہے" — his laqab is **Fazl Allah** (p. 113, folio 109). The chunk never uses the title
    "رکن عالم" / Rukn-e-Alam: see Doubts.
  - **Education** under his grandfather's supervision (p. 114, folio 110).
  - **Bai'at and khilafat**: "آپ اپنے والد ماجد حضرت شیخ صدر الدین عارف کے مرید اور خلیفہ اعظم ہیں" —
    murid and **khalifa azam** of his father Shaikh Sadr al-Din Arif; after his father's passing he
    became **sajjada nashin** and occupied himself with teaching, talqin and guidance (p. 114,
    folio 110).
  - **Relations with Nizam al-Din Auliya.** Great affection; he came "دو بار سلطان علاء الدین کے عہد
    میں اور تین بار سلطان قطب الدین کے عہد میں" — twice in Sultan Ala al-Din's reign and three times in
    Sultan Qutb al-Din's (p. 114, folio 110). In Qutb al-Din's reign Nizam al-Din came out from his
    khanqah as far as "حوض خاص علائی [OCR?]" to receive him, and they then met "اس وقت کی جامع مسجد
    میں", in the Jami Masjid of the time (p. 114, folio 110; repeated p. 116, folio 112). He came from
    **Multan to Delhi** to visit Nizam al-Din in his illness, and **led his funeral prayer**, after which
    "آپ ملتان تشریف لے گئے", he went to Multan (p. 115, folio 111).
  - **A question of doctrine put by his brother.** Shaikh Imad al-Din Ismail asked "حضرت محمد مصطفٰی
    صلی اللہ علیہ وسلم کی ہجرت میں کیا حکمت تھی؟" — what was the wisdom in the Prophet's hijra; Rukn
    al-Din's answer turned on the "اصحاب صفہ" and Nizam al-Din's on the poor of Medina who could not
    otherwise reach the Prophet's company (p. 115, folio 111).
  - **No children**: "آپ کے کوئی اولاد نہیں تھی" (p. 116, folio 112).
  - **Death**: "وفات سے تین ماہ قبل گوشہ تنہائی اختیار کر لی تھی۔ 16' رجب 735ھ کو جوار رحمت میں داخل
    ہوئے" — three months before his death he took to seclusion; he entered the neighbourhood of mercy on
    **16 Rajab 735 AH** (p. 116, folio 112; footnote 9 → "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 527", p. 120,
    folio 116). No CE equivalent is given: see Doubts on the archive's 1335.
  - **Shrine**: "مزار پر انوار ملتان میں مرجع خاص و عام ہے" — the luminous mazar at **Multan** is a
    resort of high and low (p. 116, folio 112). No quarter, no building date, no urs, no custodian named
    for the shrine itself (not stated).
  - **Sirat, quantified by age.** Bound to prayer and fasting from **seven**; prayed in congregation;
    kept tahajjud, ishraq, chasht and nawafil; fasted in **Ashura of Muharram** besides Ramadan; zikr
    khafi and jali, muraqaba and muhasaba among his practices (p. 116, folio 112). "کشف قلوب' طے ارض و
    طے لسان میں دس سال کی عمر سے ممتاز تھے' کمالات صوری و معنوی آپ کو پچیس سال کی عمر سے حاصل تھے" —
    distinguished in kashf-e qulub, tai-e arz and tai-e lisan from the age of **ten**, and possessed of
    the formal and inward perfections from **twenty-five** (p. 116, folio 112).
  - Habit: "خوراک آپ کی کم تھی' لیکن کھانا اچھا کھاتے تھے" — he ate little but ate well (p. 116,
    folio 112).
  - **Successor and khalifas**: under Practices.
  - **Teachings** (رubric تعلیمات): on "صورت اور صفت", form and attribute — judgement rests on attribute,
    not form, and its verification appears only in the abode of the hereafter, where each person is
    gathered in the form answering to his attribute (p. 117, folio 113, footnote 14 → "مجمع الاخبار");
    and on "اعمال پر متابعت", holding the limbs back from what the sharia forbids or dislikes, in word
    and deed, and avoiding idle assemblies and the company of "بطالوں" (pp. 117–118, folios 113–114).

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (حضرت شیخ بہاء الدین زکریا رحمۃ اللہ علیہ ملتانی — named throughout
  باب 17, not the subject of an entry here):
  - The town agrees with the row by the book's own nisba, "ملتانی", used at every mention (pp. 113–114,
    folios 109–110); the Suhrawardi affiliation is carried in the family khirqa of Shaikh Shihab al-Din
    Suhrawardi (p. 114, folio 110). **No death year, no shrine statement, no urs and no custodianship
    for him appear in this chunk** (not stated).
  - He is Rukn al-Din's **grandfather**, Bibi Rasti's **pir** and **father-in-law** (p. 113, folio 109).
  - **He names the child and predicts him before birth**: standing for his pregnant daughter-in-law he
    said "یہ تعظیم اس بچے کی ہے۔ جو تیرے پیٹ میں ہے۔ وہ چراغ خاندان و شمع دو دمان کا ہو گا" — this
    respect is for the child in your womb; he will be the lamp of the family and the candle of two
    houses (p. 113, folio 109, footnote 1 → "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 527").
  - **He conducts the succession in person**: the turban episode at p. 114 (folio 110) is his act and his
    words — see Practices.
  - He supervised Rukn al-Din's "تعلیم و تربیت" (p. 114, folio 110).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **A succession enacted as the handing over of a turban, and a khirqa worn at the accession.** At four
  years old, his grandfather's "دستار" lay on the bed-leg; playing, he lifted it onto his own head; his
  father Sadr al-Din Arif forbade him and told him to take it off; Baha al-Din Zakariya answered: **"بابا
  صدر الدین! تم اس کو منع نہ کرو' کیوں کہ اس نے دستار بطور حق کے اپنے سر پر رکھی ہے' میں نے یہ دستار اس
  کو دی"** — do not forbid him, for he has set the turban on his head **as of right**; I have given him
  this turban. And then the closing of the circle: "اپنے والد کی وفات کے بعد جب آپ سجادہ نشین ہوئے تو آپ
  نے وہی دستار سر پر رکھی اور حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین سہروردی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کا خرقہ جو آپ کو والد سے ملا
  تھا' زیب تن فرمایا" — on becoming **sajjada nashin** after his father's death he put **that same
  turban** on his head and donned the **khirqa of Shaikh Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi** which had come to him
  from his father (p. 114, folio 110, footnotes 2–3 → "سیر العارفین"; "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 527").
  Two heritable objects and a grandfather's pre-emptive designation over the sitting successor: the
  clearest account in this book so far of how a Multan sajjada was transmitted.
- **The succession after him, and the khalifa list as an institutional record.** "آپ کی وفات کے بعد آپ
  کے بھائی حضرت عماد الدین اسمٰعیل آپ کے سجادہ نشین ہوئے" — his **brother** Imad al-Din Ismail became
  sajjada nashin, he himself having no children; six "ممتاز خلفاء" follow (p. 116, folio 112, footnote 10
  → "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 523"). A lateral succession, not a lineal one.
- **State cash on a scale, and the protocol of the third gate.** "سلطان علاء الدین آپ کا بہت معتقد تھا'
  آپ جس دن دہلی میں رونق افروز ہوتے تو وہ **دو لاکھ ٹنکہ** بطور شکرانہ آپ کی خدمت میں بھیجتا اور جس روز
  دہلی سے روانہ ہوتے **پانچ لاکھ ٹنکہ** پیش کرتا' اس کی درخواست منظور کرکے جب آپ اس سے ملنے جاتے تو وہ
  **تیسرے دروازے پر** آپ کا استقبال کرتا" — Sultan Ala al-Din sent **two lakh tankas** as thanksgiving on
  the day he arrived in Delhi and presented **five lakh tankas** on the day he left; when Rukn al-Din
  granted his request and went to meet him, the sultan received him **at the third gate** (p. 115,
  folio 111). Sultan Qutb al-Din likewise "بہت اعتقاد رکھتا تھا اور آپ کی بہت تعظیم و تکریم کرتا تھا"
  (pp. 115–116, folios 111–112). A named sum, twice, attached to arrival and departure, with a court
  protocol for the reception.
- **And the money did not stay.** "جو نذرانہ آتا' آپ اس کو خرچ کر دیتے تھے۔ اس میں سے کچھ نہیں بچاتے
  تھے۔ سلطان علاء الدین جب آپ دہلی آتے **لاکھوں ٹنکے** آپ کو پیش کرتا' آپ وہ سب رقم خرچ کر دیتے تھے" —
  whatever **nazrana** came he spent; he saved nothing out of it; when he came to Delhi Sultan Ala al-Din
  presented **lakhs of tankas** and he spent the whole sum (p. 117, folio 113). Read with the preceding
  line: "جو حاجت مند آپ کے پاس آتا' خالی ہاتھ نہیں جاتا' چونکہ آپ لوگوں کی حاجت پوری کرتے تھے۔ اس واسطے
  لوگوں میں آپ **'قبلہ حاجات'** مشہور ہوئے" — no needy person left empty-handed, and for that he became
  famous among the people as the **Qibla of needs** (pp. 116–117, folios 112–113). **A shrine treated as
  a conduit rather than an endowment: cash in, cash out, no accumulation stated.** No land, no jagir, no
  waqf is mentioned anywhere in this chunk (not stated).
- **A gift refused at the feet and diverted to a brother.** After food at Nizam al-Din's, "حضرت نظام
  الدین اولیاء رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے خاص خادم **اقبال** نے **ریشمی کپڑوں کے چند تھان اور سو اشرفیاں** ایک
  باریک کپڑے میں رکھ کر آپ کے قدموں پر رکھیں' آپ نے قبول نہیں کیا۔ حضرت محبوب الٰہی نے وہ تھان اور
  اشرفیاں آپ کے بھائی شیخ عماد الدین اسمٰعیل کے سپرد کیں" — Nizam al-Din's special khadim **Iqbal** laid
  **several bolts of silk cloth and one hundred ashrafis**, wrapped in a fine cloth, at his feet; he did
  not accept them, and Mahbub-e Ilahi handed them to his brother Imad al-Din Ismail instead (p. 115,
  folio 111, footnote 6 → "اخبار الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 141"). Refusal between two shrines, with a named
  servant carrying the gift and a named relative accepting it — a transfer, not a rejection.
- **The kulah as an instrument of policy, and an objection from the ranks.** A rich man came, became a
  murid and repented of his sins; Rukn al-Din bestowed a **کلاہ** on him; "ایک درویش نے اس پر اعتراض
  کیا", a darwish objected. The answer: **"اے عزیز! اگر وہ بوجہ ایک کلاہ کے گناہ سے باز آجائے اور اس کی
  جہت [OCR?] سے بخشا جائے تو میں کس لئے اس کو کلاہ نہ دوں"** — if by means of one kulah he gives up sin
  and is forgiven on that account, why should I not give him a kulah (p. 117, folio 113, footnote 13 →
  "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 523"). An internal dispute over admitting the wealthy, decided for
  admission.
- **Public deportment as a technique.** "کسر نفسی کا یہ عالم تھا کہ جب **پالکی** میں نکلتے تو آپ کے
  دونوں ہاتھ باہر نکلے ہوئے ہوتے" — such was his humility that when he went out in a **palanquin** both
  his hands hung outside it, as he himself said, so that perhaps the hand of some forgiven person might
  touch his and he too be forgiven (p. 117, folio 113). Also: he took great care of "علماء' فضلاء اور
  درویشوں", honoured them and spared no pains in entertaining them (p. 117, folio 113).
- **A standing food dole, counted.** A devotee "کو آپ کے یہاں سے **چار قرص** ملا کرتے تھے" — used to
  receive **four loaves** from his place; in Mecca he complained that here he got none, and thereafter
  "چار قرض [sic] اور دو پیالے سالن برابر ملتے رہے", **four loaves and two bowls of curry** continued to
  reach him (p. 119, folio 115, footnote 15 → "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 533"). The only quantified
  ration in the chunk; the second spelling is flagged under Doubts.
- **A khanqah with cells.** "آپ کا ایک مرید **خانقاہ کے حجرہ** میں عبادت میں مشغول تھا" — a murid of his
  was occupied in worship in a **hujra of the khanqah** (p. 119, folio 115). Rukn al-Din rules on him
  that hajj is not obligatory "کیوں کہ وہ ایک فقیر ہے", because he is a faqir (p. 119, folio 115) — a
  shrine officer deciding a legal obligation against travel and for residence.
- **A shrine chamber inside another shrine.** Fakhr al-Din Maruzi's mazar is "دہلی میں حضرت محبوب الٰہی
  کی درگاہ کے اندر **چبوترہ یاراں** میں" — in Delhi **within** Mahbub-e Ilahi's dargah, in the **Chabutra-e
  Yaran** (p. 122, folio 118). A named interior precinct for companions, and the chunk's only statement
  of shrine topography at that level of detail.
- **An annual urs, the chunk's only one.** Of Husam al-Din Sokhta: "مزار فیض آثار سانبھر [OCR?] میں مرجع
  خاص و عام ہے' **آپ کا عرس ہر سال ہوتا ہے**" — the mazar at Sanbhar[OCR?] is a resort of high and low,
  and **his urs is held every year** (p. 124, folio 120). No month, no day, no duration and no organiser
  are given (not stated).
- **Copying the Quran as a livelihood inside a khanqah circle.** Of Fakhr al-Din Maruzi: "قرآن مجید کی
  **کتابت** کرکے گزارہ کرتے تھے" — he subsisted by the **katabat** of the Quran (p. 122, folio 118),
  while resident at Ghiyaspur in Nizam al-Din's service (p. 121, folio 117). Self-support, not stipend.
- **Sama as the setting of an intercession.** Nizam al-Din told the childless trader Shaikh Shihab "سماع
  کے وقت آنا", come at the time of **sama**; when he came, "حضرت محبوب الٰہی نے **عین حالت وجد میں** اپنی
  پیٹھ ان کی پیٹھ سے ملا دی" — in the very state of **wajd** he joined his own back to the man's back
  (p. 125, folio 121). The practice is named, dated to no year, and treated as efficacious.
- **A khirqa cut from the shaykh's own jubba for an infant.** "حضرت محبوب الٰہی نے آپ کو گود میں لیا اور
  اپنے **جبہ مبارک** سے ایک **خرقہ** اپنے ہاتھ سے تیار کیا اور آپ کو پہنایا" — he took the newborn in
  his lap and with his own hand made a khirqa out of his own blessed jubba and clothed him in it
  (p. 125, folio 121). Affiliation conferred before any training, and then the boy formally handed to
  Nasir al-Din Chiragh Dehlvi for it (p. 125, folio 121).
- **Treasure found and immediately dispersed.** Sadr al-Din Ahmad Tabib Dulha "نے اس مقام سے خزانہ نکالا'
  اور **راہ خدا میں لٹا دیا**" — took the treasure out of that spot and squandered it **in the path of
  God** (p. 126, folio 122). The same spend-it-all pattern as Rukn al-Din's nazrana policy, in a Delhi
  entry.
- **A saint's household economy before renunciation.** Jalal al-Din Muhammad had a **کھیت**, a field, and
  filled his skirt with **غلہ**, grain, kept for a horse (pp. 127–128, folios 123–124); the text is
  explicit that he was born into a prosperous family and spent heavily on housing and dress (p. 127,
  folio 123), which the entry then contrasts with the torn collar and forty years on the road (p. 128,
  folio 124).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The saint seen sitting in the form of a lion, and told to go to the jungle.** The author reports, on
  the authority of "سیر الاقطاب (فارسی) ص 190'191", that a murid of Shams al-Din Turk Panipati passing
  the Qalandar Sahib's house "دیکھا کہ آپ **شیر کی صورت** میں بیٹھے ہیں"; Shams al-Din Turk ordered him
  to go back, convey a salaam, and if he saw him in lion form again to deliver the message "کہ **شیر کی
  جگہ جنگل میں ہے**", that a lion's place is in the jungle; on hearing it "آپ وہاں سے شیر کی صورت میں
  اٹھے اور **باگھوٹے [OCR?]** میں جا کر قیام فرمایا", he rose from there in lion form and took up
  residence at Baghote[OCR?] (p. 111, folio 107, footnote 16).
- **Baha al-Din Zakariya stands for an unborn child.** It is related (footnote 1, "سالک السالکین (جلد
  دوم) ص 527") that when his pregnant daughter-in-law came to salute him "آپ سات ماہ کے اپنی والدہ کے
  شکم مبارک میں تھے" — the child being seven months in the womb — and he rose in her honour, explaining
  that the honour was for the child (p. 113, folio 109).
- **The turban taken at four years old "بطور حق"** — reported with two citations (footnotes 2–3) and
  treated by the book as the designation of the successor (p. 114, folio 110). See Practices.
- **Four loaves delivered in Mecca every Friday night.** It is said that a darwish told the stranded
  devotee "آپ ہر جمعہ کی رات کو یہاں آتے ہیں" — that Rukn al-Din comes here every Friday night — and
  showed him the spot; he found and saluted him, and afterwards the ration continued (p. 119, folio 115,
  footnote 15).
- **The Mughals scattered by an army of angels.** The author reports that when the Mughals came near
  Multan and people asked for prayers, he did muraqaba and then said "مغل چلے گئے", the Mughals have
  gone; reaching the riverbank they dispersed, and asked the reason he said "خدا تعالٰی نے **فرشتوں کا
  لشکر** بھیج دیا اور ان کو منتشر کر دیا" (p. 119, folio 115, footnote 16 → "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم)
  ص 533").
- **A dream of hajj diagnosed as satanic.** It is related that a murid dreamed he was told to go on hajj;
  Rukn al-Din, on seeing him, said at once "وہ خواب **شیطان** کی وجہ سے تھا۔ مشغولی سے محروم کرنا چاہتا
  ہے" — the dream was Satan's, meaning to deprive him of his occupation (p. 119, folio 115, footnote 17).
- **A bowl of water broken rather than drunk, and a rebuke for breaking it.** Of Fakhr al-Din Maruzi, on
  the authority of "سیر الاولیاء ص 298'299": thirsty and alone, he saw a bowl full of water appear; "آپ
  نے اس پیالے کو توڑ دیا او کہا کہ **کرامت کا پانی نہیں پئیں گے**", he broke it saying he would not
  drink the water of a karamat; when he told Nizam al-Din, "حضرت محبوب الٰہی نے فرمایا کہ وہ پانی پینا
  چاہیے تھا", he said the water ought to have been drunk (p. 122, folio 118).
- **A comb out of a wall.** Needing a comb with no one to fetch one, "دیوار میں سے ایک کنگھی بر آمد
  ہوئی" (p. 122, folio 118, footnote 2 → "ایضا").
- **A child conceived from a back touched in ecstasy.** It is related (footnote 1 → "سالک السالکین (جلد
  دوم) ص 423") that Nizam al-Din, in wajd during sama, joined his back to the aged trader's and said
  "جا! خداوند تعالٰی تجھ کو نیک فرزند عطا کرے گا" — go, God will grant you a righteous son — and "اسی روز
  آپ کی والدہ ماجدہ حاملہ ہوئیں", his mother conceived that same day (p. 125, folio 121).
- **The fairies, the letter and the digging dog.** The author reports that fairies took Sadr al-Din Ahmad
  away to treat a **پریزاد**, who recovered; they gave him a letter and told him to show it to a certain
  dog in a lane outside the city; the dog rose, went out of the city, stood at a spot and began digging,
  "آپ اشارہ سمجھ گئے", he understood the sign, and took a treasure from there (p. 126, folio 122,
  footnote 2 → "سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 534").
- **The horse that spoke, and a prayer for offspring by the grain.** It is related that Bu Ali Qalandar
  told the boy to ask the horse whether it wanted the grain; "گھوڑا گویا ہوا", the horse spoke: "میرا
  پیٹ بھرا ہے' حضرت مخدوم مجھ کو دانہ کھلا کر مجھ پر سوار ہوئے تھے", my belly is full, the Makhdum fed
  me grain before mounting me. Pleased, Bu Ali Qalandar said "یہ غلہ میں نے تجھ کو بخشا اور میں نے خدائے
  عزوجل سے دعا مانگی ہے کہ جس قدر یہ غلہ ہے' اس کے ہر دانہ کے برابر تجھ کو **اولاد و امجاد [OCR?]** نصیب
  ہو" — I have granted you this grain, and have prayed that offspring be given you equal to every grain
  of it — "چنانچہ ایسا ہی ہوا", and so it came to pass (p. 128, folio 124, footnote 4).
- **A blessing that ends a worldly life.** Bu Ali Qalandar, seeing him mounted, said "**زہے اسپ وزہے
  سوار**", glossed by the book as "کیا خوش قسمت گھوڑا' اور کیا خوش قسمت سوار ہے"; hearing it "آپ پر
  وجدانی کیفیت طاری ہوئی", an ecstatic state came over him, he dismounted at once, tore his collar and
  took the road to the jungle (p. 128, folio 124, footnote 5).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The book's method is a fixed rubric, and it is worth knowing which fields it always fills.** Each
  chapter is a numbered **باب** opening with a one-line encomium, then labelled rubrics in a near-constant
  order: خاندانی حالات، والد (ماجد)، والدہ ماجدہ، پیشین گوئی، ولادت، نام، کنیت، لقب، تعلیم و تربیت، بیعت
  و خلافت، سلاطین سے تعلقات، شادی اور اولاد، وفات، خلفاء، سیرت، تعلیمات، اقوال، کشف و کرامات (pp. 113–119,
  folios 109–115, fullest in باب 17). **"سلاطین سے تعلقات" is a standing rubric of its own** (p. 115,
  folio 111) — this book asks about state relations as a matter of course, which is why the tanka figures
  are there at all. Shorter chapters simply drop rubrics: باب 19 has seven lines and no silsila field
  (p. 124, folio 120).
- **Everything is footnoted, and the notes are gathered at the chapter's end under "حواشی".** باب 16 ends
  with sixteen notes (pp. 111–112, folios 107–108) and باب 17 with seventeen (p. 120, folio 116). The
  author does not argue with his sources in this chunk; he cites them and passes on.
- **The source base, as this chunk shows it.** Persian and Urdu tazkiras and one hagiographic
  compendium in Urdu translation: **سیر الاقطاب (فارسی) ص 190, 190–191**; **معارج الولایت**; **اخبار
  الاخیار (اردو ترجمہ) ص 141, 142, 251, 271, 272**; **مناقب فریدی**; **اشرف المناقب**; **سیرت نظامی
  ص 363, 263**; **تذکرہ شمس العارفین صدر الدین محمد ترک بیابانی ص 18**; four notes reading only
  **"مکتوب"** (pp. 111–112, folios 107–108); **سالک السالکین (جلد دوم) ص 423, 523, 527, 528, 530, 533,
  534**; **سیر العارفین**; **انوار غوثیہ**; **مجمع الاخبار**; **سیر الاولیاء ص 298'299** (pp. 111–112,
  120, 123, 124, 126; folios 107–108, 116, 119, 120, 122). **سالک السالکین (جلد دوم)** carries most of
  باب 17 and باب 20, including the death date and the khalifa list — a single source doing the archival
  work for the Multan shrine here.
- **A Persian document reproduced with a facing Urdu translation.** Nizam al-Din's letter to Fakhr al-Din
  Maruzi on "محبت رب العالمین" is printed in Persian and then under the rubric "ترجمہ" in Urdu
  (pp. 121–122, folios 117–118). The doctrine: love of the Lord of the Worlds is of two kinds, **محبت
  ذات** (a مواہب, a bestowal, to which the servant's effort has no relation) and **محبت صفات** (a
  مکاسب, an acquisition); the method of acquiring it is "ودام ذکر … مع تخلیہ القلب عما سواہ"; it needs
  **فراغ**, and four things bar it — **خلق و دنیا و نفس و شیطان** — repelled respectively by عزلت,
  قناعت, and petition to God. The printed Urdu does not render all of this: see Doubts.

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens inside an entry and the saint is not named in it.** باب 16 is already running at
  p. 110 (folio 106); the only name the chunk gives him is the parenthetical "آپ (حضرت قلندر صاحب)"
  (p. 111, folio 107). The chapter heading, his full name, his dates and his shrine all fall **before**
  this chunk. **Do not name him from the surrounding figures** — Shams al-Din Turk Panipati appears in his
  karamat as a separate person, and a "بو علی قلندر" appears only much later in باب 21 (pp. 127–128,
  folios 123–124). A human must read the preceding chunk for the heading.
- **The chunk closes inside باب 21.** The last sentence is cut: "انہوں نے آپ کو دیکھتے ہی حاضرین سے
  فرمایا" — seeing him, he said to those present — and the speech does not follow (p. 128, folio 124).
  Jalal al-Din Muhammad Kabir al-Auliya's bai'at, death date and shrine are **not in this chunk** (not
  stated here).
- **Rukn al-Din's father is given two different names on adjacent pages.** The rubric says "آپ کے والد
  ماجد کا نام **رکن الدین [OCR?]** ہے" (p. 113, folio 109) — which would make father and son homonyms —
  while the narrative twice names the father **حضرت شیخ صدر الدین عارف** (p. 114, folio 110), once in the
  turban story and once under "بیعت و خلافت". The rubric reading is **OCR-flagged**; the narrative reading
  is not. Do not carry the rubric value without this note.
- **"حاجی شیخ صدر الدین عارف" appears in the khalifa list** (p. 116, folio 112) — the same name as the
  father and pir. The book does not remark on it, and the chunk gives no way to tell whether this is a
  second person of the same name or a duplication.
- **The archive's death year and the book's are not the same kind of statement.** The row
  **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** carries `figure_died` = **1335**; the chunk gives only **"16' رجب
  735ھ"** (p. 116, folio 112) and no CE equivalent anywhere. I have not converted it. A human should
  reconcile the two rather than assume agreement.
- **The chunk never calls him Rukn-e-Alam.** The row names the figure "Shah Rukn-e-Alam (Rukn-ud-Din Abul
  Fath)"; the book gives name **رکن الدین**, kunya **ابو الفتح**, laqab **"فضل اللہ"** (p. 113, folio 109)
  and no "رکن عالم". The identification rests on the grandfather, the Multan mazar and the Suhrawardi
  khirqa, not on the title.
- **The chunk names no silsila for Rukn al-Din.** The row reads Suhrawardi; the book's only order-bearing
  statement is the inherited **خرقہ** of Shaikh Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi (p. 114, folio 110). That is the
  basis of the map and should be cited as such, not as the book calling him Suhrawardi.
