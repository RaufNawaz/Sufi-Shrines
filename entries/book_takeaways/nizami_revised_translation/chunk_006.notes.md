# nizami_revised_translation — chunk 006 — pp. 93–107

Coverage: end of the Third Discourse (Astrologers), the author's autobiographical anecdote (p. 93); the opening of the FOURTH DISCOURSE "On the Science of Medicine, and the right direction of the Physician" (p. 93), the excursus on the physician (pp. 94–98), and Anecdotes XXXII–XXXVI (pp. 95–107). Voices: Nizami-i-'Arudi (the author) for the discourse and anecdotes; Browne for the footnotes (some reporting Mirza Muhammad's views).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)
- Author's autobiographical anecdote (Nizami's voice): the Prince asked when "this deliverance" would come; Nizami "worked out the Ascer^dant [text-layer?] [Ascendant]" and foretold that "To-morrow at the time of the afternoon prayer the messenger will arrive"; at the call to the afternoon prayer a courier brought news that the consignment had come, "consisting of fifty thousand dinars, sheep, and other things", with "'Izzu'd-Din Mahmud Hajji, the steward of Prince Husamu'd-JDawla [text-layer?] [Husamu'd-Dawla] wa'd-Din" in charge of the convoy (p. 93, folio 75).
- "my lord Shamsu'd-Dawla wa'd-Din" was next day "invested with the King's dress of honour, and released"; he recalled Nizami's earlier "prognostication in Herat" and "twice filled my mouth" with gold, then filled his sleeve with gold (p. 93, folio 75).

- Avicenna, named "Abu 'All [text-layer?] [Abu 'Ali] al-Husayn ibn 'Abdu'llah ibn Sina (Avicenna)", "that Proof of the Truth", "most accomplished of the moderns"; his "Qdntin [text-layer?] [Qanun]" holds that the movement of contraction of the pulse "can be gauged, though with difficulty, in thin subjects" (p. 94, folio 76). - Anecdote XXXVI: "Abu'l-' Abbas Ma'mun Khwarazmshah" and his Minister "Abu'l-Husayn Ahmad ibn Muhammad as-Suhayli"; at the court: "Abu 'All ibn Sina, Abu Sahl-i» Masfhi [text-layer?], Abu'l-Khayr ibnu'l-Khammar, Abu Nasr-i-'Arraq and Abu Rayhan al-Biruni" (p. 103, folio 85). ## Practices, institutions, economy
- Court patronage, royal reward: the Prince filled the author's mouth with gold twice and his sleeve (p. 93, folio 75).
- Ar-Razi's honorarium: "a horse fully caparisoned, 3. cloak [text-layer?] [a cloak], turban and arms, and a slave- boy and a handmaiden", and "in Ray from the estates of al-Ma'mun a yearly allowance of two thousand dinars in gold and two hundred ass-loads of corn" (p. 103, folio 85). Earlier the Amir "sent a thousand dinars and one of his own private horses fully caparisoned" (p. 101, folio 83).
- Public response to the Amir's recovery: "his servants and retainers and people rejoiced greatly, and gave alms, and offered sacrifices, and held high festival" (p. 103, folio 85).
- Medical practice: at Gurgan "people began to bring him their water in the morning for him to look at, and he began to earn an income" (p. 106, folio 88). Diagnosis by pulse ("systole,- a diastole, and a pause"; "the pulse is of ten sorts") and urine (p. 94, folio 76).
- Prescribed curriculum (author): elementary — Hippocrates' "Aphorisms" (Fusiil [text-layer?]), Hunayn ibn Ishaq's "Questions", ar-Razi's "Guide" (Murshid), the Commentary of "Nih'" [text-layer?]; intermediate — Thabit ibn Qurra's "Thesaurus", ar-Razi's Mansuri, Abu Bakr "Ajwi'n(" [text-layer?]'s "Direction", Ahmad [ibn] Faraj's "Sufficiency", Sayyid Isma'il Jurjani's "Aims" (p. 96, folio 78); advanced — Galen's "Sixteen Treatises", ar-Razi's "Continens", "Kdmilus-Sindfaty" [text-layer?] [Kamilu's-Sina'at], Abu Sahl Masihi's "Hundred Chapters", the Qanun, the "Dnakhira-i-Kkwdrazm-shdht" [text-layer?] [Dhakhira-i-Khwarazmshahi] (p. 97, folio 79).
- A physician is "worthy of confidence" once he "hath mastered the first volume of the Qdmin [text-layer?] [Qanun], and hath attained to forty years of age" (p. 98, folio 80).
- Prayer practice (Browne's footnote): each prayer has "fard", "sunnat", "ndfila"; "The sunnat portion comes first" (p. 96, folio 78).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)
- Abu Bakr Daqqaq's report (as heard by the author): after all remedies failed, he recited the Fatiha "breathing it in that direction", citing "Quran, xvii, 84"; the patient "This very hour ... obtained relief", which Daqqaq attributed to "the blessing of the Fdtiha [text-layer?] [Fatiha]", "a draught ... issued from the Divine Dispensary", and he says it proved beneficial "in many cases" (pp. 95–96, folios 77–78).
- Avicenna's diagnosis of love at Gurgan by the patient's pulse fluttering at the names of district, street, house and girl (pp. 106–107, folios 88–89).
- Abu Sahl foresaw his own death from his Ascendant: "Henceforth only the intercourse of souls will exist between us" (p. 105, folio 87).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)
- Author: medicine maintains, restores and embellishes health (p. 93, folio 75). The physician needs tender disposition, wise nature (Logic) and acumen; "all these indications depend on Divine help and Royal patronage" (p. 94, folio 76). Logic illustrated by disease as genus, fevers as species (quotidian, tertian, double tertian, quartan) (pp. 94–95, folios 76–77). If he fails, "let him turn to God" (p. 95, folio 77); "the physician should be of good faith, and should venerate the commands and pro- hibitions ofthe 'Holy Law" (p. 96, folio 78).
- Author praises the Qanun ("could Hippocrates and Galen return to life, it were meet that they should do reverence to this book") and condemns the author of "the Rectification of the Qdniin" [text-layer?] (p. 97, folio 79); Aristotle ordered philosophy after "four thousand years"; only Avicenna in "fifteen centuries" reached his doctrine (pp. 97–98, folios 79–80).
- Author: "every King who would choose a physician must see that these conditions ... are found in him" (p. 98, folio 80).
- Browne (footnote): Anecdote XXXV "is, howeve-,% [text-layer?] a tissue of errors, for this Mansur came to the throne at least thirty years after the death of the great physician ar-Razi, who died either in A.H. 311 or 320 (A.D. 923-4 or 932)"; Mansur I "reigned A.H. 350-366 (A.D. 961-976)"; the Oxus refusal, as Mirza Muhammad pointed out to Browne, "appears to be based" on an incident in the life of "the geographer Abu Zayd al-Balkhi" (p. 101, folio 83).
- Browne (footnote): Anecdote XXXIV "is told by al-Qifti ... and Ibn Abi Usaybi'a ... of the physician Bukht-Yishu' and the Caliph Harunu'r-Rashid"; a versified rendering is in Jami's "Chain of Gold", "composed in A.D. 1485" (p. 100, folio 82).
- Browne (footnote): "We have it on Avicenna's own authority that he arrived in Jurjan just too late to see Qabus, who had been deposed ... and put to death in 403/1012-1013" — contradicting the anecdote (p. 107, folio 89). Qabus "reigned A.H. 366-371 (A-.D. 976^-981) [text-layer?] and again A.H. 388-403 (A.D. 998-1012)" (p. 106, folio 88).
- Browne (footnote): Ma'mun II "was killed in 407/1016-17"; Mirza Muhammad considers "Suhayli" (not "as-Sahli") correct; Suhayli "died at Surra man ra'a in 418/1027-8" (p. 103, folio 85).
- Browne (footnote) dates as printed: Qanun "printed at Rome, A.D. 1593", Latin at "Venice in A.D. 1544" (p. 94, folio 76); Hunayn "bjirn [text-layer?] A;H.
