  - Abu'l-Faraj-i-Runi: "Rtina [text-layer?] [Runa], from which he took his nisba, was a place near Lahore, not, as asserted in the Ta'rikh-i-Guzida, in Khawaran of Kl/urasan [text-layer?] [Khurasan], nor, as the Majma'u'l-Fusaha states, near Nishaptir [text-layer?] [Nishapur]" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - Runi "was a younger contemporary" of Abu Hanifa-i-Iskaf and Rashidi, "for he survived into the reign of Sultan Ibrahim's son Mas'tid III [text-layer?] [Mas'ud III] (A.H. 492-508 = A.D. 1099-1114)", so Taqiyyu'd-Din-i-Kashi "is evidently mistaken in placing his death in A.H. 489 (A.D. 1096)" (p. 134, folio 116 – p. 135, folio 117).
  - Mas'ud-i-Sa'd-i-Salman: "The poet's family came originally from Hamadan in Persia, but he himself was born at Lahore, of which, in several passages in his poems, he speaks as his native place" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - Panegyrist of "Five Kings of the House of Ghazna … Ibrahim, Mas'tfd III [text-layer?] [Mas'ud III], Shirzad, Arslan and Bahramshah", reigns over "96 lunar or 93 solar years (A.H. 451-547 = A.D. 1059-1152)" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - Special patron: "Sultan Ibrahim's son Mahmud, who was made governor of India in A.H. 469 (A.D. 1076-7)", an event the poet says al-Biriini [text-layer?] [al-Biruni] foreshadowed "fifty years earlier" in his Tafhim; "the earliest date explicitly mentioned by Mas'ud-i-Sa'd" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - "He was probably born about A.H. 440 (A.D. 1048-9) and died about A.H. 515 (A.D. 1121-2)" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - "In A.H. 480 (A.D. 1087—8)" he shared his master's disgrace and "was imprisoned in different fortresses for ten years"; freed at the intercession of Abu'l-Qasim, one of Sultan Ibrahim's courtiers, he "returned to his father's estate in India" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - In "A.H. 492 (A.D. 1098-9)" Mas'ud III gave the government of India to his son Shirzad with "Qiwamu'l-Mulk Abu Nasr Hibatu'llah of Pars" as commander-in-chief, who "made him governor of Jalandar, a dependency of Lahore" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - Imprisoned again "in the fortress of Maranj for another period of eight or nine years"; released "about A.H. 500 (A.D. 1106-7)" at the intercession of "Thiqatu'l-Mulk Tahir ibn 'Ali ibn Mushkan"; "Being now sixty years of age … he retired from public life and spent the remainder of his days in seclusion" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - 'Uthman Mukhtari of Ghazna, Mu'izzi and Sana'i "(who first collected and edited his poetry) bear witness to his pre-eminence" (p. 135, folio 117).
  - Mirza Muhammad's long critical notice on this poet was translated by Browne in "the J.R.A.S. for 1905 (pp. 693-740) and 1906 (pp. 11-51)"; summarized in "the Persian notes (pp. 142-150 and 178-182)" (p. 135, folio 117) — external references, not pages of this book.
  - Rashidi (poet) is referred to "by his contemporary and rival Mas'iid-i-Sa'd-i-Salman [text-layer?] [Mas'ud-i-Sa'd-i-Salman]" in two qasidas (p. 134, folio 116).

## Practices, institutions, economy
- Endowment: Amir Isma'il the Samani bought the Ju-yi-Muliyan estates and "made palaces and gardens … most of which he conferred on the Mawalis, and which are still endowments" (Narshakhi quoted) (p. 139, folio 121).
- Burial: Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Muhtaj "was buried at Chaghaniyan"; his son "buried with his father at Chaghaniyan"; grandson also "buried at Chaghaniyan" (p. 140, folio 122).
- Funerary dress: a dying nobleman (al-Mafarrukhi's History of Isfahan) wants a shroud of Miqradi fabric, "unwilling to appear before God less sumptuously clad" (p. 126, folio 108).
- Court patronage: Zinati "receiving a handsome present for his verse" (p. 133, folio 115); Mas'udi incurred Sultan Mas'ud's anger by verses warning against the Saljuqs (p. 134, folio 116); poets imprisoned with their patrons (Mas'ud-i-Sa'd, p. 135, folio 117).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)
- Mirza Muhammad corrects the author: Mas'ud/Sanjar confusion in Anecdote VIII (p. 126, folio 108); Bughra Khan substituted for Ilak Khan in Anecdote XI (p. 130, folio 112).
- Browne: divergence on the merit of Rudaki's verses between the author and Dawlatshah indicates "a complete change in the canons of taste … and, it must be admitted, a change for the worse" (p. 139, folio 121).
- Repeated criticism of the Majma'u'l-Fusaha for "astonishing confusions and chronological errors" (p. 134, folio 116; p. 136, folio 118).
