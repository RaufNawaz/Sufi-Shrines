## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)
- Razi (end of his entry, Note XXVII): Bodleian MS. "Mkrsh 156" [text-layer?] has six leaves "(ff. 239b-245b)" with clinical reports of "some two dozen of Razi's own cases"; these "quite bear out Razi's high repute as a clinical observer" (Notes, Mirza Muhammad as abridged by Browne) (p. 171, folio 153).
- Abu'l-Khayr ibn Khammar ("Abrfl-Khayr ibn Khammdr" [text-layer?]): philosopher, physician and logician "born at Baghdad in A.H. 331 (A.D. 942-3)"; date of death "unknown", but per Anecdote XXXVI he survived "A.H. 408 (A.D. 1017-8) in which Sultan Mahmud conquered Khwarazm"; "a Christian, apparently of Persian ancestry"; translated from Syriac into Arabic (p. 171, folio 153).
- Abu Nasr ibn 'Arraq ("Abti Nasr ibn lArrdq" [text-layer?]): contemporary of al-Biruni, descended from "the old kings of Khwarazm"; that family "seems to have been destroyed or dispersed by Sultan Mahmiid [text-layer?] of Ghazna" (p. 171, folio 153).
- Abu Sahl Sa'id ibn 'Abdu'l-'Aziz an-Nili: "a poet as well as a physician" (p. 172, folio 154).
- 'Ali ibnu'l-'Abbas al-Majusi ("Haly Abbas"): court-physician to 'Adudu'd-Dawla, "died in A.H. 384 (A.D. 994-5)"; author of the Kamilu's-Sina'a ("Kdmt-Ms-Sinfra" [text-layer?]), printed at Cairo "in 1294/1877" and "lithographed at Lahore in 1283/1866"; Latin translation printed at Venice "A.D. 1492" and Lyons "A.D. 1523" (p. 172, folio 154).
- Abu Sahl-i-Masihi ("Aim Sahl-i-Masihi" [text-layer?]): of Gurgan/Jurjan, "one of Avicenna's teachers", patronised at the court of Ma'mun Khwarazmshah; Wustenfeld gives "390/1000" as his death year, "but it is not clear on what authority" (p. 173, folio 155).
- Avicenna ("Shaykh Abti ''AH ibn Sind" [text-layer?] — = ibn Sina, false friend for Sind): "born in August, A.D. qSo [text-layer?]" in "a village near Bukhara"; at "the early age of ij [text-layer?]" treated the Samanid prince Nuh ibn Mansur; minister to Shamsu'd-Dawla at Hamadan; served 'Ala'u'd-Dawla at Isfahan; died "of colic on the march to Hamadan (where his tomb still exists) in 428/1037, at the age of 58 lunar years" (p. 173–174, folios 155–156).
- Avicenna's disciples: Bahmanyar, died "458/1066"; Abu Mansur ibn Zila, died in "440/1048-9"; Abu 'Ubayd al-Juzjani, met him in Jurjan "^403/10 1 2-3" [text-layer?] and edited his scattered writings after his death (p. 175–176, folios 157–158).
- Sayyid Isma'il Jurjani: "probably the first Muslim physician who used the Persian language chiefly or exclusively in writing on scientific subjects"; entered service of Qutbu'd-Din Muhammad Khwarazmshah "in 504/1110-1111"; died at "Ivierv" [text-layer?] "in or about the year 531/1136-7" (p. 176, folio 158).
- The Ma'muni Khwarazmshahs (Note XXIX): Ma'mun ibn Muhammad, d. "387/997"; 'Ali ibn Ma'mun, married Sultan Mahmud's sister; Abu'l-'Abbas Ma'mun, murdered by his nobles "in 407/1016-7"; Mahmud then annexed Khwarazm "in 408/1017-8" (p. 179–180, folios 161–162).
- 'Ala'u'd-Dawla (Note XXX): ruled Isfahan "from 398/1007-8 to 433/1041-2" (p. 180, folio 162).
- Shaykh 'Abdu'llah Ansari (Note XXXI) — a Sufi of Herat, no archive row: born "Sha'ban 2, ^96 [text-layer?] (May 4, 1006)", died "towards the end of A.H. 481 (March, 1088)"; Hanbali, "accounted a leading Jjiifi [text-layer?]"; known in Persia as "Khwaja 'Abdu'llah Ansari"; lectured on the lives of the saints from as-Sulami's Tabaqatu's-Sufiyya, which a disciple recorded "in the ancient language of Herat", the basis of Jami's Nafahatu'l-Uns (p. 181, folio 163).
- India: Hindus "have systems of horary astrology, called Salyana and Arudha"; "Indian Jyoshis" (W. Gornold, Note XXXII) (p. 184–185, folios 166–167).

## Practices, institutions, economy
- Court patronage of learning: Abu Sahl-i-Masihi was one of "the numerous m^n [text-layer?] of learning who found patronage and protection at the Court of Ma'mun" (p. 173, folio 155); al-Biruni "spent seven years (A.H. 400-407 =A.D. 1010-1017) at the Court of Abu'l-'Abbas, where he held various offices" (p. 180, folio 162).
- Political submission via the khutba: Mahmud ordered Abu'l-'Abbas Ma'mun "to recognize him as his ov^r-lord and insert his name *ri the khutba" [text-layer?] (p. 180, folio 162).
- Book culture: Avicenna "would often give the original to the person who had asked him to write it without keeping a copy" (Browne) (p. 176, folio 158); the Khuffi was named from khuff, "a boot", written in two narrow volumes a traveller could carry "in each of his riding-boots" (Browne) (p. 177, folio 159).
- Astrology (Note XXXII, W. Gornold): computation of the "Part of the Unseen" and "Part of Fortune"; the horoscope date "28 Safar, A.H. 511 (i [text-layer?] July, A.D. 1117)" judged "correctly taken" (p. 182–183, folios 164–165).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)
- A qasida "ascribed to" Avicenna "foretells with extraordinary prevision the Mongol invasion, the sack of Baghdad" and the battle of 'Ayn Jalut "in A.D. 1260", "based on astrological considerations" (Browne) (p. 174–175, folios 156–157).
- Ansari's opponents produced "a little copper image" claiming he worshipped it; the King, convinced of his innocence, "dismissed him with honour and punished his detractors" (Mirza Muhammad, on the authority of adh-Dhahabi) (p. 181, folio 163).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)
- The Notes reject the author's claim: "There is no justification for our author's assertion that Abii Nasr ibn 'Arraq was the nephnw [text-layer?] of Khwarazmshah" (p. 171, folio 153).
- Browne: the Kamilu's-Sina'a "is, in my opinion, far superior in style, arrangement and interest to Avicenna's Qanun" (p. 173, folio 155).
- Browne questions Mirza Muhammad's view that al-Majusi himself was a Magian ("how could he have been called 'Ali"); Mirza Muhammad replied with instances of non-Muslims bearing Muslim names (footnote) (p. 172, folio 154).
- Note XXX: two errors in Anecdote XXXVIII — Avicenna was minister to Shamsu'd-Dawla, not 'Ala'u'd-Dawla, and the latter lived at Isfahan, not Ray (p. 181, folio 163).
- Mirza Muhammad: Qadi Ahmad-i-Ghaffari confuses the Ma'muni dynasty with the Farighuni family (p. 180, folio 162).
- Gornold: the horoscope "calculation was retrogressive and … the observation was not made at the time of birth", since a partial solar eclipse is not mentioned (p. 183, folio 165).
