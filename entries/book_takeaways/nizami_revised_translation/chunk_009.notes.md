## Practices, institutions, economy
- Court patronage of poets: Tahir ibn Fadl "a generous patron of poets" (p. 141, folio 123); Qilij Tamghaj Khan Ibrahim celebrated for "his generosity to men of letters", prose works dedicated to him (p. 145, folio 127).
- Observatory: per Ibnu'l-Athir (under "A.H. 467 (A.D. 1074-5)"), Nizamu'l-Mulk and Sultan Malikshah assembled astronomers incl. "'Umar ibn Ibrahim al-Khayyami" and fixed the Naw-ruz; "A great amount of wealth was expended upon it"; it remained in use "until the King died in A.H. 485 (A. 1)^1092-3 [text-layer?])", then disused (pp. 154–155, folios 136–137).
- Astrology (Note XXIV): khaby and damir ("divination and thought-reading") per al-Biruni's Tafhim — "Herein are astrologers speedily put to shame" (p. 149, folio 131); computation of Sahmu's-Sa'adat ("Part of Fortune") and Sahmu'l-Ghayb ("Part of the Unseen") (p. 149, folio 131); Kad-khuda and Haylaj, "two indications of the length of life of the child" (p. 150, folio 132); Ralph Shirley, editor of the Occult Review, in letters "dated May 22, May 31 and October 25, 1920" (pp. 150–152, folios 132–134).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)
- Mirza Muhammad (as quoted) condemns arbitrary emendation: the Majma'u'l-Fusaha's author "deliberately falsified the text of Azraqi's poems", changing "Tughanshah ibn Muhammad" to "Tughanshah Ibn Mu'ayyad"; such emendations "are not only an encouragement to ignorance, but a betrayal of the trust" (pp. 141–142, folios 123–124).
- Note XXI: Nizami, "in recounting what professes to be a personal reminiscence", commits "several grave historical and chronological errors" — six enumerated (name, relationship, title, "Alp Ghazi", battle "near Ray, not Ramadan", date "nearly a century before"); "We are driven to suppose that in this passage the original text has been tampered with" (pp. 143–144, folios 125–126).
- Three-point argument that Azraqi died at least forty years before the date Taqiyyu'd-Din gives ('Awfi says he was "antecedent to Mu'izzi"; no mention of Malikshah or Sanjar; his father's contemporaneity with Firdawsi) (p. 142, folio 124).
- On al-Kindi: "How our author can have represented him as a Jew is incomprehensible" (p. 148, folio 130).
- On the Chahar Maqala as a source: its author "was personally acquainted with 'Umar and wrote [lines scrambled] some thirty years after his death" — apparently "the oldest account of him yet discovered" (p. 153, folio 135).
- Browne in first person: received a letter from Professor Bevan "On Nov. 12,^ [text-layer?] 1912" about a new MS of al-Athar al-Baqiya (p. 146, folio 128); collated the Mirsad passage with an MS "transcribed in A.H. ,768 (A.D. 1367)" (p. 153, folio 135); "I believe that I was the first to call attention" to the Jami'u't-Tawarikh notice (p. 155, folio 137); placed Shirley's notes after the Arabic/Persian explanations (p. 148, folio 130).
