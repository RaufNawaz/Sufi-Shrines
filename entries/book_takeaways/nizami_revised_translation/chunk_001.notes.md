## Practices, institutions, economy
- Courtly patronage: the author says the custom is for an author to praise his patron in the preface; he instead lists the favours God granted the king (p. 20, folio 2). Kingship is ranked next after "the Prophetic Function", "naught else than a Divine gift" (p. 21, folio 3).
- Publication: Gibb series list gives the Chahar Maqala as vol. "XI i, 2" — Persian text "IQIO [text-layer?] [1910], 125.* [text-layer?] [12s.]", English translation "1921, 155. [text-layer?] [15s.]" (p. 7, folio not stated). Title page: Cambridge University Press for the Gibb trustees, Luzac & Co., "1921" (p. 5, folio not stated).
- Editorial method (Browne): Mirza Muhammad's notes, "200 pages" in the original, were "rearranged and greatly compressed" — shorter ones as footnotes, longer ones under "thirty-two headings" (p. 12, folio x); Persian page breaks marked by Arabic numbers in the translation (p. 12, folio x).
- Medicine/physiology (author): the four plant forces (Attractive, Retentive, Assimilative, Expulsive) and three faculties incl. "the Reproductive Faculty" (p. 24, folio 6); five external and five internal senses located in brain ventricles (pp. 25–26, folios 7–8); the "Animal Spirit" in the heart, "Psychic Spirit" in the brain, "Natural Spirit" in the liver (p. 26, folio 8).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)
- Author: the nasnas, "a creature inhabiting the plains of Turkistan", carries off a solitary man, "and it is even said that it will conceive from him" (p. 27, folio 9).
- Abu Rida's report (Anecdote I): a naked, beautiful woman on a sand-hill fled faster than a horse; the Turkish muleteers called her "a wild man, such as they call nasnds" (p. 27, folio 9).
- Author: "the vine flees from the bind-weed" (p. 24, folio 6).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)
- Browne: Nizami's poetry "was not ... of the highest order" and "far inferior to his prose", "almost unequalled in Persian" (p. 13, folio xi).
- Browne: the book holds "the only contemporary account of 'Umar Khayyam, and the oldest known account of Firdawsf [text-layer?]", and composition is placed "with certainty between the years 547/1152 and 552/1 157 [text-layer?], and with great probability in the year 55I/II561 [text-layer?]" (p. 14, folio xii).
- Browne, citing Mirza Muhammad: "Fifteen such blunders" of history by the author, some "of the grossest character"; not all ascribable to copyists "since the point of the story is in several cases dependent on the error" (p. 14, folio xii).
- Author (cosmography/evolution): water evaporated by the sun bared "one quarter of the earth's surface" — the "Uncovered" or "Inhabited Quarter" (p. 23, folio 5); kingdoms linked successively: clay to coral, thorn to date-palm and vine, maggot to nasnas, then man with "the capacity for abstract concepts" (pp. 24–27, folios 6–9).
