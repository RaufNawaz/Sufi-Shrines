## Practices, institutions, economy
- Political theory (the author): "the king, therefore, is the lieutenant of the Imam, the Imam of the Prophet, and the Prophet of God" (p. 29, folio 11); kings need "the Secretary, the Poet, the Astrologer and the Physician" (p. 30, folio 12).
- Plan (the author): four Discourses, each with philosophical considerations then "ten pleasing anecdotes" (p. 30, folio 12).
- Secretary's qualifications and reading list (Qur'an, Traditions, Arabic and Persian works incl. Rudagi, "the Epic of Firdawsi", 'Unsuri) (p. 31–33, folios 13–15).
- Taxation (Anecdote VI): Lamghanis complain against a tax-gatherer "on account of a maund of chaff or a single egg"; Ahmad-i-Hasan remitted "that year's taxes" and a second year; on the third petition he wrote "The tax is a running sore: its cure is its discharge" (p. 38, folio 20; p. 39, folio 21).
- Patronage: Iskafi's salary increased after the pigeon despatch (p. 36, folio 18); the Caliph gave a distracted scribe "means sufficiently ample" (p. 37, folio 19); Fadl scattered wax pearls each naming a village, and sent the finder "the title-deeds of that village" (p. 40, folio 22).
- Communication: carrier-pigeon followed by courier; Iskafi's despatch "As for Makan, he hath become as his name" (p. 36, folio 18).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)
- The author: Prophets reach knowledge "without master or book", Philosophers by study (p. 28, folio 10).
- The author: the Qur'an's eloquence proves "this Word did not proceed from the mouth of any created being" (p. 43, folio 25).
- Browne (footnotes): "Nuh ibn Mansur" seems "an error ... for Mansur ibn Nuh"; "chronological difficulties ... considerable" in Anecdotes II–III (p. 33, folio 15; p. 34, folio 16); "Sanjar is, however, a mistake for Mas'iid [text-layer?] [Mas'ud] ibn Muhammad ibn Malikshah" (p. 41, folio 23).
