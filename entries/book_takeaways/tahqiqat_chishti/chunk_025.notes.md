# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 025 — pp. 281–291

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 281) on a Sikh place of worship ("یہ مکان سکھوں کا بڑا عبادت گاہ ہے"). **Its heading is in the previous chunk (024)**, so this chunk does not name it. Four titled sections follow. The first is "احوال مکان مزار حضرت پیر غازی المشہور بہ پیر غیب" (pp. 281–282), a grave beside the Data Ganj Bakhsh precinct that is kept by Data's mujawirs, with its healing custom and the story of how it was found. The second is "حال تکیہ سکندر شاہ" (pp. 282–283), a faqir's takia and its Eid-night charas gathering. The third is "الاحوال گنبد قاسم خان" (pp. 283–284), the building and a short life of Qasim Khan, an amir of Akbar. The fourth is "احوال مزار پُر انوار حضرت میاں میر لاہوری رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" (pp. 284–291), which runs to the end of the chunk: Mian Mir's family, birth and death, his pir Shaikh Khizr, his practice and karamat, the disciples Mian Natha and Nimat Allah, Shah Jahan's and Dara Shikoh's visits, and a life of Dara Shikoh. The chunk breaks off mid-sentence on Dara's planned red-stone road from the shrine to the Lahore fort. Five archive ids are mapped: shrine-of-mian-mir (most of the chunk), data-darbar, samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh (person rule), and shah-jamal and shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal (the toponym Ichhra, on a flagged reading).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (حضرت میاں میر لاہوری; "مزار پُر انوار حضرت میاں میر"; also "حضرت میاں میر بالا پیر"):

  - Family as printed: "حضرت کا نام قاضی سائیں دتا اور والدہ کا نام فاطمہ بنت قاضی قاداں[OCR?] تھا".   - Birth: "مولد انکا شہر سیستان میں سال نو سو ستاون[OCR?] ہجری" (p. 284).
  - Death: "وقت نماز ظہر ساتویں ربیع الاول [illegible]ھ". Age: "اٹھانوے[OCR?] سال" (p. 284).
  - A chronogram (qita) for his birth and death, attributed to Mufti Ghulam Sarwar ("مفتی غلام سرور"), is quoted. Its text is damaged (p. 284).
  - A second qita is quoted, with Dara Shikoh's "کتاب سکینۃ الاولیا" named in the lead-in. The lead-in also names "فتح اللہ [illegible]", one of Mian Mir's murids, in connection with a chronogram after his death. The passage is too damaged to say who wrote which qita (p. 284).
  - A garbled passage connects the start of his Qadiri path with "ملتان[OCR?]" and with his mother. He went looking for a pir in "کوہستان سیستان" (p. 284).
  - He waited three days and nights ("تین[OCR?] شبانہ روز") at a place he took to be a wali's dwelling. Shaikh Khizr then came, and Mian Mir took bai'at with him (p. 285).
  - After some years in Shaikh Khizr's service he reached "تکمیل ولایت", and with his pir's permission ("حسب الاجازت پیر روشن ضمیر") he set out for Lahore ("روانہ لاہور ہوئے") (p. 285).
  - Practice: he stayed awake all night, and he had mastered "حبس نفس[OCR?]" so far that a whole night often passed in one or two breaths. When age and weakness came, he passed the night in four breaths. "حضرت ملا شاہ صاحب" is said to confirm this (p. 285).
  - "کرامات ان حضرت کی لاتعد و لاتحصی ہیں" (his karamat are countless); only a few are written down (p. 285). They are listed under Legends.
  - His murids number "ہزارہا آدمی" (thousands) (p. 289).
  - Dara Shikoh, "خلف شاہجہان بادشاہ", was a murid of Mulla Shah, Mian Mir's murid. Dara had the "روضہ عالیہ" built and wrote Mian Mir's life in detail in "کتاب سکینۃ الاولیا" (p. 289).
  - "کئی مرتبہ شاہجہان بادشاہ بھی حضرت کی خدمت میں حاضر ہوتا تھا" (Shah Jahan also came to him several times) (p. 289).
  - Mulla Shah is described as "مرید و خلیفہ [illegible] حضرت میاں میر بالا پیر کی" (p. 291).
  - Dara's "ارادت کاملہ" was to Mian Mir, even though he was Mulla Shah's murid. Dara often attended him and built the rauza: "حضرت کا روضہ[OCR?] بھی اوسی نے بنوایا" (p. 291).
  - Dara intended a road of red stone ("ایک سڑک سنگ سرخ کی") "حضرت کے روضہ مقدس سے تا قلعہ لاہور", for his own coming and going, and always went barefoot ("پا برہنہ") on that road. The chunk breaks off here (p. 291).

- **shah-jamal**, **shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal** (the book's "موضع اچھرہ[OCR?]"):

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Sikh place (p. 281)**:
  - Greatly valued under Ranjit Singh.
  - Revered by "تمام اہل ہنود" as well as Sikhs.
  - Tied to the adjoining "مکان پانچویں بادشاہی" (p. 281).
- **Pir Ghaib — fabric**:
  - A raised pakka platform of five steps ("پانچ سیڑھیاں[OCR?]"), with a grave in lime plaster ("چونہ گچ") (p. 281).
  - Lamp niches ("طاقچہ[OCR?] چراغدان") around it, and at the head a tall round brick lamp-stand with niches for lamps (p. 281).
  - North of it, a well and the trace of a roofless, decayed mosque ("نشان مسجد بوسیدہ بے سقفہ") (p. 281).
- **Pir Ghaib — repairs**:
  - The well and mosque were repaired by "کرامت[OCR?] نام شخص ٹھیکہ دار" over "سات[OCR?] [margin] [illegible] سال" (p. 281).
  - Before that, the platform was only "رکھنہ[OCR?]" and was repaired from time to time (p. 281).
- **Pir Ghaib — custody and income**:
  - Held by Data Ganj Bakhsh's mujawirs, who take its income (p. 281).
  - "جنب[OCR?] مسجد[OCR?] میاں علاوالدین علی محمد اہر[OCR?] قابض ہیں" (p. 281).
  - "بہت آدمی واسطے زیارت کے آتے ہیں اور بہت آمدنی ہوتی ہے" (many people come for ziyarat and there is much income) (p. 281).
  - "ہمیشہ ایک دو عورتیں مجاورہ یہاں بیٹھی رہتی ہیں" (one or two women mujawiras always sit here) (p. 282).
  - "تمام لوگ اس خانقاہ کو بزرگ کرکے مانتے ہیں" (p. 282).
- **Pir Ghaib — healing custom**:
  - For a swollen or aching throat ("گلے پر درد ورم[OCR?] یا سوج"), people come, take up "[OCR?] پیالہ[OCR?] مٹی کا" and pass the clay over the place (p. 281).
  - When cured, they bring "مصری", "شکر" and "زر نقد[OCR?]" as nazr (p. 281).
  - "ہمیشہ غلاف وغیرہ پارچات بطور نذر آتی ہیں" (ghilafs and cloths always come as offerings) (p. 281).
- **Pir Ghaib — surroundings**:
  - A small and a large cistern ("حوضچہ[OCR?]") nearby have collapsed (p. 282).
  - A mosque in "شیریں محل" and a running well ("چاہ رواں") are mentioned. The mujawirs' rights over the well are discussed in a garbled passage that mentions "عہد مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ" (p. 282).
  - "اب عرصہ پچاس سال سے ویراں ہو کر نام و نشان اسکا گم ہو گیا" (desolate for fifty years, its name and trace lost) (p. 282).
- **Takia Sikandar Shah**:
  - Trees: pipal, kikar and others (p. 282).
  - A faqir's kotha with two kothris; a platform with the sign of a mosque; a pakka well with a wheel ("چاہ پختہ چرخی دار") (p. 283).
  - An old tree said to be fifty or sixty years old ("عرصہ پچاس ساٹھ سال"), with a brick-and-plaster platform and the grave under it (p. 283).
  - Custody: "پیر محمد شاہ گیلانی صاحب" is mukhtar and appoints the sitter (p. 283).
  - "بروز رویت ہلال عید تمام چرس خوار لوگ جمع ہوکر سوا روپیہ[OCR?] کی چلم" (on the day the Eid moon is sighted, all the charas smokers gather for a chillum of a rupee and a quarter[OCR?]) (p. 283).
- **Gunbad Qasim Khan — building**:
  - Four doors, one on each side, and a stair on the north (p. 283).
  - "اب مدت سے" (for a long time now), four brick pillars have been set inside the dome and a roof laid on them, so that it has become two-storeyed (p. 283).
  - A broken dalan on the east (p. 283).
  - An upper walkway ("غلام گردش"), "بارہ در[OCR?]", "آٹھ[OCR?] محرابی در" and skylights ("روشندان") (p. 283).
  - "سفیدی دیواروں پر عہد انگریزی میں ہوئی[OCR?]" (the walls were whitewashed in the English period) (p. 283).
  - An old stair in the south-east corner is now closed (p. 283).
  - From outside it looks three-storeyed ("سہ منزلہ") (p. 283).
- **Mian Mir — rauza**: built by Dara Shikoh (pp. 289, 291). Dara planned a red-stone road from the rauza to "قلعہ لاہور" (p. 291).
- **Mian Mir — graves nearby**:
  - Mian Natha's grave is outside the enclosure wall (p. 288).
  - "نعمت اللہ حاجی حرمین" is buried "حضرت میاں میر کے سامنے" (p. 289).
- **Mian Mir — offerings**: a Mughal places "مبلغ پچیس[OCR?] روپیہ" before him as nazr, and he gives it at once to a poor Mughal (p. 289).
- **Dara's purse story ending**: the purse ("ہمیانی") ended up in "بادشاہ کے بیت المال" (p. 291).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Pir Ghaib**:
  - A gate and wall by the river ("لب دریا") were built by day and fell by night (p. 282).
  - A "شاہ" (the name is illegible) gathered ulama and fuqara. They judged that a wali's grave must lie there, the grave was found, and it was named "پیر غیبی[OCR?]" because it appeared in this wondrous way. Hence "پیر غیب کی قبر" (p. 282).
- **Mian Mir**:
  - Guests from his homeland ("[illegible] بھابی[OCR?] حضرت کی وطن سے آئی") came when he had no food or money. He prayed in the garden, and an unknown man ("شخص ناشناس") brought a tray of food sent by God and offered cash too. Mian Mir took the food, refused the cash, and fed everyone present (p. 285).
  - Dara Shikoh as eyewitness ("خود دیدہ"): a hunter shot a dove ("قمری") in the garden, and Mian Mir passed his hand over it and it flew off alive. The hunter tried again, his arm was seized with pain, he repented and vowed to harm no creature, and Mian Mir healed the arm (p. 286).
  - Shah Jahan said on the road to Dara: "اگر یہ پیر تمہارا کامل ہے تو آج ہمکو انگور تازہ کھلا دیگا". Mian Mir brought a tray of grapes from his cell although "موسم انگور تازہ کا نہ تھا" (p. 289).
  - From Sakinat al-Auliya: a naked darvish complained that the nazr belonged to all darvishes. Mian Mir said he was a greedy faqir with "ایکسو[OCR?] بانکیس[OCR?] روپیہ آٹھ آنہ" hidden in his waistband, and that the money would be lost (pp. 289–290).
  - The purse story continues: the darvish left his purse at the hammam. Mian Mir sent him to a darvish in a boat on the river, who knew "براہ کشف" (by unveiling) that Mian Mir had sent him and who had hundreds of purses. The man got his purse back but died two days later of grief. Others fought over it and poisoned each other, the last was executed "بحکم حاکم", and the purse went to the royal treasury (pp. 290–291).
- **Mian Natha**:
  - His eye pain was cured at Mian Mir's word (p. 286).
  - "مشہور ہے کہ پتھر[OCR?] اور درخت میاں نتھا[OCR?] سے ہمکلام ہوتے تھے". He prayed that no tree should speak to him, and from that day the speaking stopped (p. 286).
  - A dome told him to wait because rain was coming, and the rain began (p. 287).
  - From Sakinat al-Auliya: he told a dead, rotten rat ("چوہا[OCR?] مردہ") in a lane to get up and go, and it came alive (p. 287).
  - The date palms of "موضع اچھرہ[OCR?]" recited tasbih (p. 287).
  - He offered to scatter a storm cloud. Mian Mir was displeased ("خود فروشی اور اظہار کرامات کرتا ہے") and said "کار حکیم حکمت سے خالی نہیں ہوتا" (pp. 287–288).
- **Nimat Allah Sirhindi**: a father said his son had been robbed of the trade money. Nimat Allah told the boy that he had buried it under a wall, and the money was dug up (p. 288).
- **"حاجی المست[OCR?] [illegible] صاحب"** (Sakinat al-Auliya): he told a man whose slave-girl had fled to wait for a "بہلی یعنی گاڑی" and ask for her without asking whose it was. She was found in it (pp. 288–289).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is heavy throughout: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags, several `[margin]` notes, and the qitas on p. 284 and the Pir Ghaib story on p. 282 are badly broken. Every quotation keeps its flags. Folio markers (277–282, 285) are printed but not cited, per the brief.
- **Opening Sikh place (p. 281)**: its heading is in chunk 024. It is not mapped to any gurdwara row.
  - "مکان پانچویں بادشاہی جو شہر قریبہ[OCR?] قلعہ ہے" is not mapped either. The chunk does not name the guru of the "fifth padshahi", and identifying him would be general knowledge. Check against chunk 024.
  - "گورو صاحب" is unnamed in this chunk.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**: person rule only. Ranjit Singh is named purely as a period ("عہد") (pp. 281, 282), and nothing is said about the samadhi. Weigh accordingly.
- **shah-jamal, shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal**: place-level, from the toponym "موضع اچھرہ[OCR?]" (p. 287). The place name is itself flagged `[OCR?]`, and the material is Mian Natha's former retreat and its tasbih-reciting date palms, not either shrine. Weigh accordingly.
- **data-darbar**: the attachment is through the Pir Ghaib grave's location and custody (p. 281). It says nothing about Data's own shrine beyond the mujawirs' possession.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** "لاہور" appears only as where Qasim Khan was killed and buried (p. 284), Mian Mir's destination (p. 285), Mian Natha's origin "لاہوری" (p. 286) and "قلعہ لاہور" (p. 291). None is material on the city as such, so the 35 `lahore` ids are not attached.
- **"قلعہ لاہور" (p. 291)** is not mapped to the `lahore fort` rows (loh-temple-lava-temple, samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh). It is only the planned end-point of Dara's road, and the material belongs to Mian Mir's shrine. This is recorded so the consolidator can weigh it against ruling (a).

The word is flagged, and the sentence ("اول حضرت میاں میر صاحب کے ملتان[OCR?] سے [illegible] طریقہ قادریہ") is too garbled to say what it states about Multan, or whether the word is Multan at all.
