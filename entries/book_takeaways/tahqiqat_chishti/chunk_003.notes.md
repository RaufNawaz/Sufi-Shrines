# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 003 — pp. 33–47

This chunk ends the author's front matter and opens the shrine survey. It runs: the author's account of his own murshid and his Chishti initiation (p. 33); the "حال" of Hazrat Miskin Shah, including a rain miracle at Delhi involving Shah Ghulam Ali Naqshbandi Mujaddidi (pp. 34–36); a Persian verse "شجرہ عالیہ چشتیہ نظامیہ" composed by the author (pp. 36–37); "واجب العرض", which gives the book's name as "تحقیقات چشتی", followed by chronogram qit'as for its composition (pp. 37–38); a "فائدہ" on the Sufi path, its obstacles (عقبہ) and the books the author read (pp. 38–43); a "خمس" in praise of Ali al-Murtaza (pp. 43–45). Then the section titled "آغاز احوال قبور و مزارات و مقبرہ ہائے بزرگان لاہور" begins with Madho Lal Husain (pp. 45–47), and a new section on Shaikh Bahlul, "مرشد حضرت لال حسین", opens at p. 47 and breaks off. Damage is very heavy throughout, and almost all of pp. 33–44 is devotional prose and verse with no site material. Two archive rows are touched by name; the Lahore-in-general bullet rests on one section heading.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Madho Lal Husain's khanqah has a "سجادہ نشین", who keeps a manuscript بیاض (p. 46).
- The author describes the Sufi path as a series of عقبہ, obstacles (pp. 41–43). They are knowledge, tauba, عوائق, and the hindrances that follow.
  - The four عوائق are دنیا, خلق, شیطان and نفس. Their remedies are ترک دنیا, ترک دوستی اہل دنیا, fighting Shaitan, and taking the bridle of تقویٰ (p. 42).
  - The next four hindrances, beginning with rizq and fear of outcomes, are met by توکل, تفویض, صبر and رضا (p. 42).
  - The path then reaches بواعث, then عجب and حسد, then حمد و شکر (pp. 42–43).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- At Delhi, after two years of drought, Shah Ghulam Ali found Miskin Shah sitting by the حوض in the midday heat. Miskin Shah said "میں خدا [illegible]". Told to have mercy on his servants, he looked at the sky, and within an hour clouds came and it rained (pp. 34–35). The rain was so heavy that Shah Ghulam Ali judged him a "صاحب کمال" (p. 35).
- The khams credits Ali with uprooting Khaibar "ایک انگلی سے" (p. 44).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author says earlier masters wrote many books on the path. He writes this risala because it records the lives of the "مردانِ راہِ خدا", and he sets down what he gained from blessed elders and from reading Sufi books (p. 40).
- He presents the Madho Lal Husain material as drawn from Shaikh Pir Muhammad's "حقیقت الفقرا" ("یوں تحریر فرماتے ہیں") (p. 45).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory**: `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags are on nearly every line. Every year in the chunk is illegible or flagged: "۱۸۵۰[OCR?]" (p. 33), the death years of Miskin Shah and Niyaz Ahmad (p. 36), the chronogram years (p. 38), "۵۰[OCR?]" and a 3–4 numeral marginal note (p. 45), "۹۳۵[OCR?]" (p. 46). None may be used without checking the PDF. `[folio N]` markers (28, 30–40, mostly flagged) are present but deliberately not cited.
- **Lahore-in-general bullet**: place-level. It rests only on the section heading at p. 45.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar**: person-level, mapped on a bare name in a verse shajara and in a chronogram invocation, under the 18 Sept person rule. The chunk says nothing about the shrine, its place or any date.
- **madho-lal-hussain**: the book's "باغبان پورہ" and "باغ شالامار" match the row's location. The caste name "ڈھاڈا[OCR?]", the descent name "کلشتر[OCR?]" and the father's name "عثمان[OCR?]" are all flagged. The birth chronogram is partly illegible, and the referent of "۹۳۵[OCR?]" (p. 46) is unclear.
- **Whose handwriting is the بیاض in?** "بدست خط خاص اونکے" (p. 46) may mean the saint's hand or the sajjada nashin's.
- **Baz Ahmad vs Niyaz Ahmad**: Miskin Shah goes to "مولوی باز احمد[OCR?]" (p. 35), but the death date on p. 36 is given for "مولوی نیاز احمد", and the shajara has "نیاز احمد[OCR?]" (p. 37). These are probably one man misread, but this is not resolved here.
- **"لاہور" on p. 33** sits inside an illegible passage about the author's murshid. Its sense (e.g. arrival in Lahore) cannot be read, so it is not used for any fact.
- **"مدرسہ مزنگ[OCR?] لاہور[OCR?]"** (p. 38) names a madrasa's locality. Per the brief it is not a toponym bullet, and it is not mapped to the Mozang rows.
- **The author's name**: the chronograms are signed "سرور" and "غلام سرور[OCR?]" appears (pp. 37–38). Whether this is the author's full name is not stated clearly in this chunk.
