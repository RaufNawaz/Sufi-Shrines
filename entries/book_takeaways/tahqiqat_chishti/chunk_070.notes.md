# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 070 — pp. 762–771

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 762) with the tail of a description of an old mosque whose heading is in the previous chunk: its Arabic and Persian inscriptions, its two-aisled plan and its present use for teaching. It then runs through these titled sections in order. "حال حضرت سعداللہ شاہ امام مسجد ہذا" (pp. 762–764) gives the line of imams of that mosque down to Saadullah Shah and his chosen successor Ghulam Ghaus. "حال تکیہ سبحان شاہ والہ واقع موضع اچہرا" (p. 764) is a short takia notice with a faqir's chain. "حال شہید گنج" (pp. 764–767) describes the Shahid Ganj complex outside the Delhi Gate: an old mosque and a hammam of Dara Shikoh's khansaman Abdullah Khan, Sikh samadhs, the "دربار صاحب المشہور شہید گنج", the story of the martyrs and of Mir Mannu's death and tomb. "حال منی سنگہ" (pp. 768–770) covers Bhai Mani Singh, the site's grants under the Maharaja and the British, its sajjada line and its present occupants and shop rents. "سمادہ صاحب سنگہ" (pp. 770–771) covers the samadh of Bhai Sahib Singh and a dispute over who controls it. "مسجد ستارہ بیگم المعروف قدسیہ بیگم" (p. 771) begins a description of a three-domed mosque and breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk. Folio markers are printed but are not cited here (brief rule).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

  - In the time of "مہاراجہ کلان" the Mani Singh site was much talked about; "he" came often, and while that rule lasted a "سدابرت" of twenty-five rupees a day ("پچیس روپیہ یومیہ") ran there (p. 768). The passage is damaged ("جیلیں [OCR?] سامی [OCR?]", "مدد [OCR?] [illegible]").
  - "مہاراجہ کا معمول تہا" that from every district[OCR?] ("ہر انگو [OCR?] ضلع") a hundred rupees was set aside as an offering ("بطور تصدق [OCR?] نذر [OCR?]"), distributed through[OCR?] Bhai Ram Singh; five rupees a day of it went on bhang (p. 768). The Maharaja is not named in this sentence.
  - Jiwan Singh, sajjada of the Mani Singh site, prospered: "خدا نی اسکی مرادین برلائین کہ مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ حاکم ہوگیا" (p. 769).
  - Bhai Sahib Singh sat at his place "ابتدای عملداری مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ میں"; "مہاراجہ کلان بہی بار ہا" came to pay respects ("آستانہ بوسی [OCR?]") (p. 770).

- **gurdwara-dash-mesh-pita** (the book's "سری گورو گوبند سنگہ صاحب بہادر", "گورو گوبند سنگہ صاحب"; also "دسوین بادشاہ"):

  - Origin of the bhang custom, as the people of the site explain it: in Guru Gobind Singh's time, when Muslims were [damaged], he ordered the Sikhs to drink bhang so that fear would not overcome them, and the faqirs who used it much made it a habit (p. 768, heavily flagged).   - At the Shahid Ganj darbar, on one of two wooden takhtposh is kept a granth "دسوین بادشاہ کا" (p. 766; the other granth's name is garbled: "گرنتہ [OCR?] آدہ [OCR?]").

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- Old mosque (heading in chunk 069): Arabic inscriptions including "ان المساجد للہ فلا تدعو مع اللہ احد" in the north mihrab; a Persian chronogram ending "سنہ ١١ [OCR?]"; signed "کتبہ میر مرتعی [OCR?]" (p. 762). Two-aisled under one roof, eastern aisle "٥ [OCR?] گز" wide, western "٩ [OCR?] - گز"; painted plaster flaking; a north window now only one gaz above ground through raised flooring (p. 762). Founder named as "اقرار [OCR?] خان [OCR?] بانی مسجد" (p. 762). Location (mohalla) (not stated) in this chunk.
- Imams of that mosque: Ghulam Habib, appointed by the founder, paid with shop rents and "ابراہیم آباد" (yearly income about eight hundred rupees); buried in the mosque (pp. 762–763). Then his son-in-law Hafiz Khairullah, "قوم ہاشمی"; then Hafiz Ilahi Bakhsh, who died "سنہ ١٢٢٤ [OCR?] ہجری" and is buried in a corner of the mosque inside the enclosure of the khanqah of "مولوی غلام حسین صاحب مرحوم جد امجد راقم" (p. 763). His sons: Saadullah Shah, Abdullah Shah (became majzub and died), Zuhur Ali Shah ("مرد فقیر سلسلہ جلالیین [OCR?] خادم بخاریان", children brought to him for "دم جہاڑ [OCR?]") (p. 763).
- Saadullah Shah: present imam, childless, skilled in Persian, teaches boys; sold his own house to repair the mosque floor; has had Ghulam Ghaus son of Shaikh Imam Bakhsh "المشہور [OCR?] تعویزیہ" registered as his successor by a "تملیک نامہ رجسٹری سرکار" (p. 763). Imam Bakhsh: long a resident of Lahore and imam of a mosque built by the author's grandfather ("مبنیہ جد امجدم"), with hundreds of murids (pp. 763–764).
- Takia Subhan Shah, north of Ichhra ("اچہرا"): grave of Subhan Shah, an unroofed[OCR?] unbaked room, six-foot mud wall; present faqir "سوہلے شاہ"; chain as printed: Sohle Shah ← Lal Shah ← Nur Shah ← Fazil Shah ← "محمد لاہوری [OCR?]" ← "مالک رحمن [OCR?]" ← "نوت [OCR?] صاحب" (p. 764).
- Shahid Ganj ("شہید گنج"), outside the Delhi Gate on its east side, opposite the sarai of "محمد سلطان ٹہیکہ دار سرکار انگریزی" (p. 764). The area was known by Dara's name "کیونکہ حویلیان محمد دارا شکوہ ... کی یہان ہی تہین" (p. 764, damaged).
  - Old mosque built by "عبداللہ خان خانسامان داراشکوہ"; a large domed hammam "مبنیہ داراشکوہ" built under Abdullah Khan "سنہ ١٠٥٦ [OCR?]"; the mosque "سنہ ١٠٤٦ [OCR?]", because Abdullah Khan, appointed "کوتوال لاہور", held court at the "نخاس" (p. 765). The Nakhas's tiled gateway ("کاشی کار مثل دروازہ مسجد وزیر خان") stood early in British rule and was demolished (p. 765). Rent of the hammam now taken by Ganda Singh and Gurdit Singh (p. 765).
  - The mosque now: Sikhs closed its arches and fitted wooden panels; "ایک چہوٹا سا سمادہ" has come up there (p. 764). Above it a three-storey house where the bed of "کاہن سنگہ" is kept; he died about six years ago and Sikhs revere the bed (p. 764).
  - Samadh of "بابا سائین داس [OCR?]", died about fifty years ago; mirrors in its dome; music on the first of the month and at "سنگرانت [OCR?]", gatherings of "اوداسی [OCR?]", karah prashad (pp. 765–766).
  - "دربار صاحب المشہور شہید گنج": domed samadhs of "بہای [OCR?] دلوال [OCR?] سنگہ" and "بابا جہان [OCR?] سنگہ سرگیانی [OCR?]" (p. 766); the martyrs' samadh, a four-arched dome with two white "مرمیان [OCR?]" (p. 767); samadh of "دونا [OCR?] سنگہ ملوہی [OCR?]" with circumambulation path, red-stone screens and two marble graves, his and his wife's (p. 767).
  - Martyrdom account (pp. 766–767): "میرمو [OCR?] صاحب" / "میر منو صاحب", out of religious bigotry, began killing Sikhs and offered "پچیس [OCR?] روپیہ" for a Sikh's head; the execution place was by the kotwali; "بہای تارا سنگہ" and another Sikh are named as engaged in highway robbery[OCR?] (p. 766); two men were seized, told to accept Islam, refused, were flayed and salted (pp. 766–767).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Mani Singh / Shahid Ganj grants under the Sikhs: a "گیارہ سو روپیہ کی جاگیر" in "علاقہ ترن تارن ضلع امرتسر"; revenue-free wells at "موضع چوکاک [OCR?] وال و مظفرپور [OCR?]", "شرقپور [OCR?] ضلع لاہور" (a well of fifty rupees), near "پل مالی [OCR?]", and a table: "موضع سکیاں [OCR?]" chah "٥ [OCR?]", "علاقہ نجاس [OCR?]" one well, "بیلہ [OCR?] بہای دستی [OCR?] رام" one well (p. 768).
- Under British rule a jagir was released to Jiwan[OCR?] Singh for life, to continue to his heirs, but with one-fourth of income paid as nazrana; his death "سمت ١٩١ [OCR?] بکرماجیتی" (p. 769, damaged).
- In Sikh times the site also got dues from Lahore grain markets: one chhatank per bullock-load, half a ser per cow[OCR?]-load, half a pao per camel-load, and two pai from every traveller; travellers still give something voluntarily (p. 769).
- Sajjada line as told by the site's people: Jagga Singh (devotee who swept and lit lamps; built an unbaked structure; read the granth) died "سمت ١٨٣ [OCR?] اٹہارہ سو تیس"; son Jiwan Singh succeeded, died "سمت ١٩ [OCR?]"; now his son Ganda Singh is sajjada nashin (p. 769).
- Residents listed by name: Ganda Singh; Lachhman Singh "گرنتہہ خوان"; Sukha Singh cook; Asa Singh son of Ganda Singh; Prem Singh grandson; Ram Singh; "انکولی [OCR?] کشن"; Bhai "ایسرہ [OCR?]" and Bhai Gulab, two "شبد خوان" on two rupees a month with food and clothing (p. 769).
- Bhang: water and bhang offered to passers-by at all hours from a cistern and four bhang kundis; Sikh-era spending was large; now "اکبر [OCR?] و بیس روپیہ" is still spent (p. 765). Asa Singh's job is to prepare bhang day and night for travellers (p. 769).
- Shops and mills: Lal Singh "چہیرو [OCR?]" son of Ganda Singh holds the two mills ("خراس") and shops, one mill grinding langar flour (p. 769); shop rents of eight annas a month, holders named; four shops let rent-free to named persons including "بلس [OCR?] سنگہ مہتہ ساکن امرتسر" (p. 770).
- Sahib Singh samadh custody: chela Sahjan Singh was made sajjada and built the samadh; then Bhai Charhat Singh; now his widow "مسمات نند کور" lives there, sons Dyal Singh and Bishan Singh in the "فوج سرکار"; she has sent for her elder son to become sajjada nashin (pp. 770–771).
- Dispute: Ganda Singh, sajjada of Shahid Ganj, "ہاتہہ ڈالتا ہی" on the Sahib Singh samadh, claiming Sahib Singh was a chela of his father Jiwan Singh and the place belongs to Shahid Ganj; no one confirms it, all say "گنڈا سنگ ناحق دست اندازی کرتا ہی" (p. 771).
- Old mosque (pp. 762–763): no income now, since the mohalla has four other mosques; its congregation is only six houses and a haveli (p. 763).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author judges the Sikh account of the bhang custom "قرین قیاس" (p. 768).
- On Mir Mannu's death the author quotes "بترس از آہ مظلومان کہ ہنگام دعا کردن * اجابت از در حق بہر استقبال می آید" (p. 767).
- On the tomb-digging the author blames "تعصب مذہبی" and greed ("ان خام طمعون کو تو بہانہ ہی چاہیئی تہا") (p. 767).
- The author sides against Ganda Singh's claim: Sahib Singh "کچہہ علاقہ جیون سنگہ سی نتہا" (p. 771).

## Citable passages

- "پہر بعہد مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ یہہ تمام عمارت طیار ہوئی" — then, in Maharaja Ranjit Singh's time, this whole building was completed (p. 767).
- "عہد مہاراجہ شیر سنگہ مین قبر اسکی کہدوا گئی" — in Maharaja Sher Singh's reign his [Mir Mannu's] grave was dug up (p. 767).
- "بترس از آہ مظلومان کہ ہنگام دعا کردن * اجابت از در حق بہر استقبال می آید" — fear the sigh of the oppressed, for when they pray, the answer comes out from God's door to meet it (p. 767).
- "گنڈا سنگ ناحق دست اندازی کرتا ہی" — Ganda Singh interferes without right (p. 771).
- "سیکڑون کوس سے لوگ انکی درشن کیواسطی آیا کرتی تہی" — people came hundreds of kos for his [Sahib Singh's] darshan (p. 770).

## Doubts

- Damage inventory: heavy `[OCR?]` throughout and several `[illegible]`; worst at p. 762 (inscriptions), p. 766 (martyrdom story and Panjabi verse, largely unreadable) and p. 768 (bhang passage). Quotations above carry their flags.
- Shahid Ganj: the archive has `gurdwara-shaheed-ganj-singh-singhnian` (name match "Shaheed Ganj", Lahore) and `gurdwara-shaheed-bhai-taru-singh` (Lahore). The chunk names "شہید گنج" outside the Delhi Gate and "بہای تارا سنگہ" (p. 766), but it does not say this site is either row, does not mention women or children, and prints "تارا" not Taru. Left unmapped under "Other saints…". A consolidator should decide whether the book's Shahid Ganj is either row.
- samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh: the person rule applies. "مہاراجہ کلان" (pp. 768, 770) and the bare "مہاراجہ" (p. 768) are identified with Ranjit Singh only through context (p. 770 has both names in one sentence; p. 768 does not name him). Nothing here concerns the samadhi itself.
- gurdwara-dash-mesh-pita: person-level mapping (Guru Gobind Singh is its principal figure). The mention is biographical and has nothing to do with the Sukkur site.
- Internal contradictions: the hammam is dated "سنہ ١٠٥٦ [OCR?]" and the mosque "built after the hammam" is dated "سنہ ١٠٤٦ [OCR?]" (p. 765). Jagga Singh's death is "سمت ١٨٣ [OCR?]" in digits but "اٹہارہ سو تیس" in words (p. 769). The second martyr is "گلاب سنگہ" (p. 768) and later "گلزار [OCR?] سنگہ" (p. 769). Mir Mannu is printed "میرمو [OCR?]" (p. 766) and "میر منو" (p. 767).
- Partial dates: "سنہ ١١ [OCR?]" (p. 762), "١٢ [OCR?]" (p. 768), "سمت ١٩ [OCR?]" (p. 769) and "سمت ١٩١ [OCR?]" (p. 769) are printed incomplete and must not be filled in.
- Place names left unmapped (each names a location only and gives no material about the place): "اچہرا" (toponym_map ichhra → shah-jamal, shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal; it is the location of a non-archive takia, so per the brief it is not a toponym bullet); "نواح قصور" (Kasur → mazar-of-bulleh-shah; only where Mir Mannu rode); "شرقپور [OCR?] ضلع لاہور" (Sharaqpur → shrine-of-pir-sher-muhammad; only the site of a revenue-free well).
