# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 033 — pp. 365–372

The chunk opens with the damaged last lines of a section whose heading is in the previous chunk (032). The lines concern a woman ("تنو[OCR?] ہی صاحبہ") whose husband's name was learned by oral report (p. 365). Four titled sections follow. The first is "احوال تکیہ حضرت سید بہاول شاہ گیلانی بن سید اسماعیل گیلانی" (pp. 365–366), a takia west of Mozang on the Ferozepur road. The second is "احوال حلقہ حضرت شاہ محمد مقیم صاحب حجرہ[OCR?] والہ رحمتہ اللہ علیہ" (pp. 366–371). It describes Shah Muqim's mound south of that takia, then gives long accounts of Bahawal Sher, of Shah Muhammad Muqim and of Sayyid Jamal Allah "حیات المیر", with karamat and two chronograms by Mufti Ghulam Sarwar. The third is "احوال تکیہ[OCR?] ولن شاہ[OCR?]" (p. 371), a takia with an akhara, wells, inscribed graves and resident faqirs. The fourth is "احوال مکان گدی[OCR?] شاہ" (pp. 371–372). It tells of Abu al-Khair's madrasa "حیر گدہ[OCR?]", the settlement "ٹھٹھی[OCR?]", the Sikh plunder of Lahore and a family of maulvis, and it runs to the end of the chunk. Three archive rows are touched by name: Mauj Darya Bukhari, Shah Abu al-Maali and Shah Kamal. There is also one Lahore-in-general passage.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Bahawal Sher**:
  - During an i'tikaf in "کو مستان[OCR?]", his skin seat stuck to his body. The passage is garbled (p. 367).
  - At Hund Dholan[OCR?], where the "دریا بیاس[OCR?]" then flowed (toward "ملتان", the text says, though it is now dry), the women of the Dhol[OCR?] zamindars made him move several times (p. 367).
  - He threw his staff ("سونٹہ[OCR?]", which he had named "شیہان[OCR?] یعنی شیر") into the river and told the river to go away. The zamindars' children threw clods toward the river at his word (p. 367).
  - The river moved a kos away, and a high mound ("ٹیلہ بلند[OCR?]") appeared, on which he settled (p. 367).
  - Garbled sequel about two sticks or trees, one green and one half-dry ("نیم خشک"), said to last until qiyamat (p. 368).
  - After his death, a murid who visited the grave against an order went blind ("انداہ[OCR?] ہو گیا") (p. 368).
  - When his tomb was being built, the building kept collapsing, or the builder kept going blind. The text is heavily garbled (p. 368).
- **Shah Muhammad Muqim**:
  - Bahawal Sher appeared to him in a dream, coming out of the grave, and sent him to Hayat al-Mir in Lahore (p. 369).
  - In a gathering in Lahore a man doubted a claim about women (the passage is garbled). Shah Muqim read the man's thought "نکسف[OCR?] باطن" and told him to look at a tree, where Shah Muqim appeared on every leaf (p. 370).
  - He had a field of carrots ("گا حروں[OCR?]") pulled for his mare. Next day the owner came, and Shah Muqim told him "غم[OCR?] نکر کیونکہ حق بحق دار عاید ہو گیا ہے" (do not grieve, for the right has reverted to the rightful owner) (p. 370).
- **Hayat al-Mir**: given eternal life by Ghaus al-Azam, and to live until Jesus returns (pp. 369–370).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is heavy throughout. Pp. 366–368 and the Gaddi Shah section (p. 372) are barely continuous. The Bahawal Sher burial and tomb story and the stick/tree story (p. 368) are largely unreadable. Every quotation above keeps its flags. Folio markers are printed (357–362, then "??1", 342, 343, which do not run consistently) and are not cited, per the brief.
- **Opening fragment (p. 365)** belongs to a section headed in chunk 032. Its subject is unplaceable here.
- **Lahore bullet: place-level.** The Sikh plunder of Lahore after Abdali, the "حاکم[OCR?] لاہور" and "اندروں حصار بلدہ لاہور[OCR?]" (p. 372) are attached to all 35 `lahore` ids per ruling (a). The attachment is place-level, not site-specific; weigh accordingly.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** appears only in the place-level Lahore bullet. Shah Chiragh is not named in this chunk. The held identity question (whether "Shah Chiragh" conflates two men) is not touched and is not resolved. **langer-makhdoom** is not named.
- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari** is mapped by the person rule. The book names him only as husband of the Sahiba whose khanqah adjoins Bahawal Shah's grave. Her name is damaged ("نبا[OCR?] بہو یعی[OCR?] دی[OCR?]") (p. 365). It is a bare name; weigh accordingly.
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** is mapped on the book's own words: "شاہ ابو المعالی", tomb "لاہور میں", author of "تحفہ القادریہ" (p. 369). The chunk gives no other identifier. The same section also names a different-seeming Shah Abu al-Maali, son of Shah Nur[OCR?] and father of Shah Muqim (pp. 366, 369). The chunk does not say whether they are one man or two. Only the Lahore-tomb author is mapped. A consolidator should check.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal** is mapped on a bare, flagged name: "حضرت سید آنگ[OCR?] شاہ کمال" (p. 368). The chunk gives no place or date for him, and the sentence's subject is garbled. Weigh accordingly.
- **Mozang is not given a toponym bullet.** "موضع مزنگ" (p. 365) is named as the location of Bahawal Shah's takia and of a resident. Per the brief, a mohalla named as a location is not a toponym bullet. So gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-mozang and shrine-of-shah-inayat-qadiri are not attached by toponym; they are covered only by the Lahore bullet.
- **"میانی" (p. 371)** is not mapped to the `miani sahib graveyard` rows. The chunk says only "میانی", with no "صاحب" and no "graveyard". Identifying it would need knowledge from outside the chunk.
- **Pakpattan, Multan and Peshawar are not mapped.**
  - "ضلع گوگیرہ تحصیل پاکپتن[OCR?]" (p. 366) is the administrative location of Hujra Shah Muqim, a non-archive site.
  - "تا ملتان" (p. 367) describes the old course of the Beas.
  - "ضلع پیشور" (p. 372) is where a maulvi's jagir lay.
  - None of these is material on that place's own tradition, so shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar, the 8 `multan` ids and the 10 `peshawar` ids are not attached. This is recorded for the consolidator to weigh against ruling (a).
- **"سید جمال اللہ حیات المیر"** is not mapped to shah-jamal. The chunk makes him a grandson of Ghaus al-Azam, born "سنہ پانسو[OCR?] بائیس[OCR?]", with nothing that ties him to that row's figure.
- **"فرید الدین لاہوری"** (p. 371) is not mapped to shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar. The book calls him "لاہوری" and names him on a slab dated "۱۲۶۵ [OCR?]".
- **Bahawal Sher's death date**: "یوسو[OCR?] تہ ہتر[OCR?] حجری[OCR?]" (p. 368) is too damaged to read as a number. A separate "پانچویں[OCR?] سنہ ۹۵ [OCR?]" on p. 366 may concern his death, but the sentence is garbled. Do not compute.
- **Section heading word "حلقہ"** (p. 366) is printed without a flag. The body text calls the place "ٹیلا[OCR?]" and the site "حجرہ شاہ محمد مقیم".
- **Shah Muqim's dates**: "سنہ ۱۰۵۵ [OCR?]" (p. 371) is from the chronogram line and is a flagged numeral. "دو مریس[OCR?]" (p. 370) is unclear.
- **Other flagged numerals**: "سنہ ۱۰۳۶ [OCR?]" (Abu al-Khair, p. 371); "بانیس[OCR?] سال" (Ilahi Bakhsh, p. 365); "سو[OCR?] چالیس[OCR?]" trees (p. 371); "جہتر[OCR?]" villages (p. 372). All are carried as printed.
- **Wallan Shah's death**: "بعہد[OCR?] [illegible] سنگ[OCR?]" (p. 371). The ruler's name is illegible, so it is not read further.
- **"معنی[OCR?] غلام[OCR?] مستر[OCR?]" and "تحریۃ الاصحیا[OCR?]"** (p. 368) are carried as printed. "معنی[OCR?] غلام سرور صاحب لاہوری[OCR?]" is glossed as Mufti Ghulam Sarwar only because p. 371 prints "مفتی غلام سرور صاحب".
- **Two darbars on the site but not in the index** (Shah Gohar Peer; Mian Qurban Ali Shah): neither is named in this chunk.
- **No province-level material** in this chunk. "سرکار" (pp. 365, 371) is carried as printed and not mapped.
