# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 043 — pp. 471–481

The chunk opens mid-way through a discourse on sama (listening to music and song). **The heading of that discourse is in an earlier chunk**, and pp. 471–479 continue it: the legality of sama argued from hadith (Aisha watching play in the mosque, the Eid daf), the kinds of sama by the listener's state, the "five causes" that make sama haram, the subheading "آثار و آداب سماع" (p. 475) on the three maqams of sama (understanding, wajd, movement), Quran versus song, and the subheading "در باب آداب و آثار سماع" (p. 478) on time, place and company. A short section follows, "احوال شاہ دمی[OCR?] کھوی[OCR?]" (p. 479), on a jhok south of Lahore that is described as a halting place of Hazrat Shah Abul Maali[OCR?] on his way from Sher Garh. It covers the grave of his grandson, a Madari faqir resident, a tank, a well and a land grant (pp. 479–480). The chunk ends with the opening of "احوال مقبرہ سید عبدالحلیم صاحب رحمتہ اللہ علیہ" (p. 481), which is very heavily damaged and breaks off. Two archive rows are mapped, both from the jhok section: darbar-abul-muali-qadri (by the person rule) and shergarh.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Sama: the rulings (in order)**:
  - **Proof from hadith.** Aisha watched "باری[OCR?]" (play) in the mosque at the Prophet's invitation. "یہ روایت تمام کتب احادیث صحیحہ میں موجود ہے", and five rulings are drawn from it (p. 471). On Eid the Prophet told Abu Bakr "آج انکو معاف رکھیو کیونکہ روز عید ہے", so "دف و سرود بھی جائز ہے" (p. 472).
  - **Love of a woman or boy.** Sama heard to feed such love, or songs on "زلف و خال و خد", is "حرام محض". If the love is for one's lawful wife, it is halal (p. 472).
  - **A praiseworthy quality** has four kinds (pp. 472–473):
    - hajis' songs in praise of Mecca;
    - nauha and grief, though grief over a loss is not permitted;
    - lawful joy (nikah, walima, aqiqa, circumcision, return from a journey), with the Medina welcome "طلع البدر علینا" quoted;
    - "اصل الانواع": love of God reaching the bounds of ishq. Wajd is defined as the states that come "عالم غیب سے" in sama.
  - **The "five causes" of haram sama** (pp. 474–475):
    - a woman singer (behind a screen and without fitna it is "مباح");
    - "چنگ و رباب و بربط" and mazamir. Tabl and daf, even with jalajil, are not haram, but "طبل مخنثان حرام";
    - words that are obscene, satire or "طعن اہل دین", e.g. "اشعار روافض";
    - a young listener ruled by lust;
    - common people making it a habit. Occasional sama is permitted.
  - **The three maqams of sama** (pp. 475–478):
    - first, understanding ("فہم", printed "قسم" in the list), with a warning that misunderstanding God's state is kufr;
    - second, wajd, of two kinds, "احوال" and "مکاشفات";
    - third, "حرکت اور رقص اور کپڑی[OCR?] پہاڑنا[OCR?]". When it is shown off for people it is "حرام بلکہ میں نفاق". The most perfect listener "سماع سنے اور ساکن رہے".
  - **Dance permitted.** "زنگیوں نے مسجد میں رقص کیا", and Ali danced from joy at "انت منی و انا منک" (p. 478).
  - **Quran versus song.** The book says many fainted on hearing the Quran, and cites "احیاء العلوم". It gives three reasons why song moves listeners more: not every verse fits a state, verses are over-familiar, and the heart needs melody (pp. 477–478).
  - **Conditions: "زمان ۔ مکان ۔ اخوان"** (pp. 478–479):
    - not at the time of prayer or a meal;
    - a suitable place;
    - only people of sama present, with no worldly people and no "کو دکاں[OCR?]".
  - **Etiquette** (p. 479):
    - Sit with head bowed, and do not look at one another.
    - When one person rises in wajd, the others conform. The book calls this "بدعت حسنہ ... جیسے کہ ہمارے تراویح".
- **The jhok of Shah Abul Maali — site and grounds** (p. 480):
  - Shisham, kikar and ber trees.
  - To the south, "ایک تالاب کلان تخمینا آٹھہ[OCR?] کنال زمین میں پکہ[OCR?] خشتی" (a large tank of fired brick on about eight[OCR?] kanals). It fills "موسم برسکال میں" (in the rainy season), and its sides have decayed.
- **The jhok — waqf, well and land grant** (p. 480):
  - Sohni[OCR?] Shah planted (?) trees ("بیڑیاں بھی نذے[OCR?] لگا کر") and made them waqf. He says no one should seize them after him.
  - He built "ایک چاہ کلان چرخ چوب والہ" (a large wooden-wheel well) with his pir's permission.
  - "سولہ[OCR?] کہا[OCR?] نو[OCR?] زمین" is granted "وعدہ سرکار سے" (by agreement with the sarkar), "تا قیام جھوک و اگذار[OCR?]" (revenue-free for as long as the jhok stands).
  - The faqir takes its income and gives a traveller one meal.
- **Madari malangs**: they do not cut their hair, keep ash in it, wrap it like a turban and do not comb it. They are called "دھاری[OCR?] فقیر" (p. 480).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Junaid's disciple.** A young man in Junaid's company wept aloud in sama. Junaid barred him unless he stopped. He practised full restraint, but one day in sama gave such a cry that "شکم اوسکا پھٹ گیا جس سے مر گیا" (his belly burst and he died) (p. 478).
- **Shah Abul Maali[OCR?].** By the blessing of his feet, each halting place became blessed and settled (p. 479). This is attributed to him as the book states it.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author defends sama for the qualified.**
  - Deniers of sama and of the sufis' states are excused as ignorant ("بے خبری سے معذور") (p. 473).
  - Feigned wajd and the self-styled sufis who gaze at boys are condemned (pp. 475, 478).
- **Sama of the Quran.** The author argues it is greater in itself, yet song moves the listener more, and gives three reasons (pp. 477–478).

## Citable passages

- "پس اس سے معلوم ہوا کہ دف و سرود بھی جائز ہے" — "So from this it is known that daf and song are also permitted." (p. 472)
- "کاملترین وہ شخص ہے کہ سماع سنے اور ساکن رہے" — "The most perfect is the one who hears sama and stays still." (p. 478)

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is heavy throughout: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags. p. 475 (the complaint about self-styled sufis), pp. 477–478 (Quran versus song) and almost all of p. 481 are barely legible. Two illegible lines are marked on p. 481. Every quotation keeps its flags. Folio markers (465–474) are not cited, per the brief.
- **The sama discourse's heading** is in an earlier chunk, so its owning section cannot be placed from this chunk.
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri is mapped by the person rule on a flagged name**, "شاہ ابوالمعالے[OCR?]". The chunk does not say "Qadri" and does not mention his tomb. The Sher Garh and Shaikh Daud[OCR?] link comes from the chunk itself (p. 479). Weigh accordingly.
- **shergarh** is mapped by name and place: "شیر گڑھ" and "حضرت شیخ داؤد[OCR?]". The chunk does not say "Bandagi" or "Kirmani", and does not say that Daud is buried at Sher Garh. "مرتد[OCR?]" is read as مرشد (p. 479).
- **The jhok section's title**, "شاہ دمی[OCR?] کھوی[OCR?]", is carried as printed. The text calls the place "جھوک", so it may be "Shah ... Jhok", but that is not repaired.
- **Distance and place near the jhok**: "کمارہ[OCR?] پہر[OCR?]" and "عاصلہ[OCR?] مار[OCR?] کوس[OCR?]" are unreadable for the figure (p. 479).
- **Faqir's name.** It is printed once as "موہنی[OCR?] شاہ" and afterwards as "سوہنی[OCR?] شاہ" (p. 480).
- **Land and numbers.** "سولہ[OCR?] کہا[OCR?] نو[OCR?]" and "آٹھہ[OCR?] کنال" are flagged numerals and units (p. 480). On p. 481, "آٹھ[OCR?] سو[OCR?] چالیس[OCR?]" appears in a broken context, and it is not stated whether it is a date. Do not compute.
- **Internal tension on p. 474.** "سماع کا سننا ایسی لڑکوں سے کہ جو محل فتنہ ہوں مباح ہی" reads as permitting sama from boys who are a source of fitna. The context argues the opposite, so a negation may be lost in transcription. It is carried as printed.
- **"میاں میر کو جاتا[OCR?] ہے" (p. 481)** is not mapped to shrine-of-mian-mir. In a broken sentence it names a destination (place or shrine, not stated), and nothing is said about the saint or his shrine. It is recorded here for the consolidator.
- **"حضرت شاہ شمس الدین[OCR?]" (p. 481)** is not mapped to shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari. The chunk gives no "Sabzwari" and no Multan, and the context is a Lahore building.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** Lahore appears only as a direction and a destination (pp. 479–480), so the 35 `lahore` ids are not attached. There is no province-level material.
- **Held identity questions** (shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, langer-makhdoom) and the two darbars not in the index (Shah Gohar Peer; Mian Qurban Ali Shah): none is named in this chunk.
