# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 014 — pp. 167–175

This chunk is wholly about the shrine of Ali Hujwiri, "Ganj Bakhsh". It opens mid-section and carries no section heading of its own. P. 167 begins mid-sentence ("[illegible] بنایت[OCR?] …"), in a description of a building whose dome is said to have been rebuilt "از سرِ نو بنیاد و سابقہ پر", anew on the former foundation. The heading for this section must be in the previous chunk (013); see Doubts. The subject is fixed by the chunk itself: the waqf inscription on p. 167 names "مزار قدوة السالکین میر[OCR?] علی ہجویری[OCR?] در لاہور", and p. 170 describes "مزار پر انوار حضرت شاہ میر علی ہجویری". In order, the chunk covers:
- the buildings and graves of the complex, including a chabutra associated with Khwaja Mu'in-ud-Din Chishti (p. 167);
- the Qur'an-reading dalan and the Persian waqf inscription in its Qur'ans, which lists Qur'ans and paid reciters sent to many other mazars (pp. 167–169);
- the large Qur'ans and their donors, among them Ranjit Singh (p. 169);
- the tomb of Nawab Shaikh Imam-ud-Din Khan and its inscriptions (pp. 169–170);
- the saint's mazar, its marble work and its chronograms (pp. 170–171);
- the surrounding buildings put up by mujawirs, the urs, the mela and the Sikh-period grant (p. 171);
- the mujawirs' origin and their share system (p. 172);
- Hujwiri's genealogy and spiritual chain (p. 172);
- Nawab Mir Momin Khan, nazim of Lahore, his family and an anecdote about him (pp. 172–174);
- a long genealogy of the mujawir family (pp. 174–175).

The chunk breaks off mid-sentence on the shares of absent mujawirs (p. 175).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Recitation endowment. Qur'ans were endowed so that reciters would recite and send the merit to the Prophet. Stipended qaris ("قاری موظف") were appointed at each mazar listed (pp. 167–169).
- Food at the urs. "تقسیم نان و نعم شرایف[OCR?]" at the urs (p. 167). At the urs, "حلوا و دال سنخو[OCR?] معہ گوشت" is distributed, and the khadims distribute offerings ("نذریں") (p. 171).
- Sikh-period grant: "سرکار سے بعہد مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ ایک ہزار[OCR?] روپیہ سالیانہ", with some sweets (p. 171).
- Mujawirs. "۲۲[OCR?] آدمی مجاور علی الدوام حاضر رہتے ہیں": 22[OCR?] mujawirs are always in attendance (p. 172). They descend from the convert Shaikh Mahdi: "اوسکی اولاد حال جا دم و مجاور ہیں" (p. 172).
- Division of income. The income is divided among the mujawirs by head. A child born to a mujawir receives a share, and the share of one who dies lapses. There are rules for one who goes away to another land (p. 172).
- Equal shares. "جسقدر زن و مرد مجاوران خدم علیہی[OCR?] سوجو[OCR?] دین سب سبب برابر حصہ پاتے بین": all mujawirs, men and women, take equal shares. A newborn's share begins, and a dead person's share stops (p. 175). The chunk breaks off on the rule for absentees (p. 175).
- Mujawir genealogy. The book traces the line from Shaikh Mahdi through Shaikh Lutfi and a series of "…اللہ" names to Shaikh Latif Allah, whose sons were Shaikh Sulaiman and Usman (p. 174). Both branches are traced down to living members ("موجود ہیں"), among them Shaikh Muhammad-ud-Din, Shaikh Ahmad Din, Sadr-ud-Din, Amir Din, Ghulam Nabi, Ghulam Muhammad, Shaikh Abid, Fateh Din and Karim Bakhsh (pp. 174–175). The book closes Shaikh Sulaiman's line with "ذکر اولا شیخ سلیمان … کا ختم ہوا" (p. 175).
- Buildings put up by mujawirs at their own expense: dalans, kothas, a roadside mosque and a diwankhana (p. 171).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Origin of the mujawirs, as the book tells it. When the Hazrat arrived, a man (damaged: "حاکم[OCR?]"?) "مسلمان ہوا", and his name was given as "بیچ مہدی[OCR?]" (p. 172). The fuller account: he came to the Hazrat's service, became Muslim, and "حضرت یہ نام اسکا شیخ مہدی کہا", the Hazrat named him Shaikh Mahdi (p. 174).
- Mir Momin Khan and the sweeper-woman. During his nizamat a beautiful "کمہترانی[OCR?]" wanted union with him, and he paid her no attention (p. 173). In the end she said she wanted a son of his likeness. He answered "میں ہی تیرا بیٹا", I am your son (p. 174). The passage is very heavily damaged. The teller appears to be "جہد امجد فدوی", but this is unclear from the transcription (p. 173).

- Damage inventory: pervasive. Nearly every line carries several `[OCR?]` or `[illegible]` flags.
