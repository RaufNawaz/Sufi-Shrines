# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 051 — pp. 564–575

The chunk opens mid-section, under the running margin "حال باعث قبضہ جوگیان اس مکان تھان بھیرو" (pp. 564–566). This is the jogis' possession of the Than Bhairo house, and **its heading is in the previous chunk (050)**. It covers the Jwala Nath / Ram Nath dispute and court case, the origin of the Mast Nath panth, two naked jogis (one buried alive), and the Hardwar gaddi. A section "احوال وفات جوگیان" follows (pp. 567–569), on jogi death, burial and kriya customs. Then comes "حال مکان سماد مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ سرگ باسی" (p. 569), a building-by-building description of Ranjit Singh's samadh and the adjoining samadhs of Kharak Singh and Nau Nihal Singh (pp. 569–574). It contains an inset "حال دیوی کی اشنان" (pp. 570–571) retelling a Devi story from the Markandeya Puran. The chunk ends with the opening of "حال مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ" (p. 575), a Sikh political history (the tenth Guru, Banda, Ahmad Shah Abdali, the twelve misls), which breaks off at the third misl. The chunk maps to one site row (the Samadhi), the Lahore place-level bullet, and person-level rows for Gorakhnath, Shiva, Guru Gobind Singh and Hanuman.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Jogi death and burial customs** ("احوال وفات جوگیان", pp. 567–569):
  - A dying jogi is bathed. After the breath ("پران[OCR?]") leaves, the body is washed and bound (p. 567).
  - Burial is in a pit with a side niche "بطور لحد[OCR?] گور مسلمانان" (like a Muslim grave's lahd) (p. 568).
  - The procession is held with "بہت دھوم دھام[OCR?]" and is called "سواری" (p. 568).
  - "اگر بہت غریب ہو تو لاش کو [illegible] پانی[OCR?] میں بہا دیتے ہیں" (if very poor, the body is set afloat in water) (p. 568).
  - The cloth is given to a faqir (the Shiv ji explanation is under the Shiva rows) (p. 568).
  - The body is seated facing north ("منہ کو بطرف شمال"). On the right are placed a lota of water, a langota, the "کنگہ[OCR?] مہا دیو" thread, a large wheat loaf, and two bowls, one of water and one of milk (p. 568).
  - Afterwards all the jogis bathe and the chela serves sweets. On the third day there is a meal for all jogis (p. 568).
  - Later the "کریا کرم" is held: jogis gather and keep vigil all night, and dishes such as "مچھلی[OCR?] یا پکوڑا یا کھیر" are cooked (p. 568).
  - Seven gaddis are set up, the sixth being Gorakh Nath's and the seventh "میلہ کی", with khairat given. Someone receives the office of "گورو شہ[OCR?] یعنی سربراہت[OCR?]", and "حسب المقدور لنگر[OCR?] پایا جاتا ہے" (p. 569).
- **Jogi institutions**:
  - A gaddi-nashin succession at Than Bhairo (p. 564).
  - Payments to the jogis, and a court suit dismissed (pp. 564–565).
  - Adjudication at the Hardwar Kumbh (p. 566).
- **Samadhi custodianship and funding**:
  - The income is collected in a kothri (p. 570).
  - Custodian Lala Narsingh Das[OCR?], with one servant present (p. 570).
  - Three paid servants at Kharak Singh's samadh (p. 574).
  - British-government expense and yearly repairs (p. 574).
  - Shops in the gateway (p. 569).
- **Samadhi architecture**: dimensions, materials (red stone, marble, brick, lime plaster, mirror work, gilded domes and kalas) and Hindu images (Hanuman, "گنین[OCR?]", Devi) as listed under the row (pp. 569–574).
- **Sati at Kharak Singh's samadh**: markers for ranis "جو ساہتہ[OCR?] ستی ہوئی" and "لونڈیان[OCR?]" (p. 574).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **"گورو جی"** found the lost she-camel and made the camel-man a chela, "مست" (pp. 565–566).
- **Mast Nath's earrings**: the jogis rioted ("بلوہ[OCR?]") because he did not wear the "مندروں" in his ears as jogi custom requires. "انہوں نے اپنے منہ سے مندروں نکال کر اوسیوقت کانوں[OCR?] میں [illegible] باوجودیکہ اونکا کان دریدہ[OCR?] نہ تھی" (he took the earrings out of his mouth and [put them] in his ears at once, though his ears were not pierced). People's devotion grew from this (p. 566).
- **Hira's chela** was buried alive with his companion (p. 566). The book reports this as an event, not a karamat.
- **Devi legend** (inset "حال دیوی کی اشنان", from "کتاب مارکنڈے پران[OCR?]"):

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is very heavy: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags on almost every line, and two `[illegible: 1 line]` gaps (p. 571; p. 572). The samadh description on pp. 572–574 and the history on p. 575 are badly garbled, with many letter-substitution forms ("نہرہ[OCR?]", "بریان[OCR?]", "سکہ[OCR?]" for what may be other words). Every quotation above keeps its flags, and none is repaired. Folio markers (560, 563–565, 556–560, several flagged) are printed but not cited, per the brief.
- **Opening section (pp. 564–566)** is headed in chunk 050. The location of the Than Bhairo house is (not stated) in this chunk; Lahore is named only as where people came and went. The 35 `lahore` ids are therefore not attached to it.
- **Lahore place-level bullet**: the attachment is place-level rather than site-specific; weigh accordingly. It rests on the old-city-wall line (p. 572, garbled) and on the misl material naming "ضلع لاہور" and "شرقی لاہور" (p. 575). The Wazir Khan baradari line (p. 575) is a named site that is not a row, carried in the same bullet only because it sits in the misl passage.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**: mapped on the section heading and margins.
