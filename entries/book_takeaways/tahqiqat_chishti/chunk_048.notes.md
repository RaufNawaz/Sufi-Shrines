# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 048 — pp. 529–539

The chunk opens with the end of a section whose **heading is in the previous chunk (047)**. That fragment covers a Qadiri family: Mir Adil, "المشہور مسمی[OCR?] نبیہو[OCR?] شاہ", a living Sayyid Haidar Ali Gilani and his chain of bai'at (p. 529). Three titled sections follow. "احوال تکیہ کامل شاہ" (pp. 529–531) is a Madari takia near "موضع بابو سا پور[OCR?]", with its graves, its sajjada nashins and a Madari chain. "حال تکیہ سیلانی شاہ" (pp. 531–534) covers a Madari faqir's takia near "موضع بابو سابو", three kos from Lahore: a burial dispute settled by the Lahore tahsildar, the muafi and its sanad, the trees, the faqir's life and a story about "موراں". "حال حضرت شاہ مدار صاحب رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" (pp. 534–538) gives the author's account of Shah Madar from named books: the veil, the Tayfur Shami chain, Uwais Qarani and the Uwaisi path, the lineage, the death date, the urs at "مکن پور[OCR?]" and Dara Shikoh's estimate of murids in Hindustan. The last section, "جوگیان" (p. 539), describes a building known as "پیہرو[OCR?]" and breaks off mid-description. Two archive rows are touched, both only by the person rule: Ranjit Singh named as the era, and Hujwiri cited as an author.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Sailani Shah and "موراں"**: freed from captivity "قدرت الٰہی سے" after her vow, she submitted to him (p. 533).
- **The curse on the Hindus' mauza** (attributed in the chunk to the faqir; the pronoun is unclear):
  - "جہان تم … وہاں می دریا آ پہونچیگا اور تمہارا موضع گرا لیگا". The book says this was accepted, and the village has been lost seven times since (p. 533).
- **Shah Madar's veil**: whoever looked on his face would go blind or "مجذوب و مسحور" (enraptured or spellbound), so he always kept a veil on (p. 535).
- **Shah Madar's fasting**: "طعام نہ کھایا اور تبدیل لباس نہ کیا" (he ate no food and did not change his clothes) (p. 535).
- **Tayfur Shami**: a contemporary of Isa who was told he would live until the Prophet's mission (p. 535).
- **Shah Madar's karamat in Hindustan**: "عجیب و غریب کرامات اوسمو[OCR?] ظہور میں آتی ہیں" (wondrous karamat appear there), in the Dara Shikoh passage (p. 538).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's method for the Madari chain**:
  - No Madari faqir remembers the shajra, so he copies what his own inquiry yielded ("از روئے تحقیقات فدوی کو منتج[OCR?] ہوا") under the Sailani Shah section (p. 531).
  - For Shah Madar he draws on named books: "مذکرہ[OCR?] العارفین", "مصالح الولاء[OCR?]" and a title read "سماجار[OCR?] الاحیار[OCR?]" (p. 534).
- **Sources cited in the Shah Madar section**:
  - "صراط المستقیم" (p. 535).
  - "کشف النعمات[OCR?]" (p. 535).
  - "کشف المحجوب" (p. 537).
  - "معارج الولایت" (p. 537).
  - "سفینۃ الاولیاء" (p. 538).
  - "اخبار الاخیار[OCR?]" (pp. 538–539).
- **The Uwaisi path**:
  - Whoever received teaching from Uwais in a dream, or was taught that way by a teacher, "سلسلہ اویسیہ میں داخل ہوا" (entered the Uwaisi silsila). Benefit from any earlier elder is likewise called "فیضیاب برکات اولیہ" (p. 537).
  - Shah Madar received this from Uwais, so his chain reaches the Prophet "فقط ایک واسطہ سے" (through only one link) (p. 537).
- **Dara Shikoh's division of Hindustan's murids** (p. 538):
  - Of four parts, two are murids of Ghaus al-Azam, "شرفاء زیادہ اور اجلاف کم" (more nobles than commoners).
  - One part are murids of Shah Madar, more "جن ملاں[OCR?]" and fewer ashraf.
  - The rest are split between the Chishtis and the Suhrawardis.
  - The passage is partly garbled. The text then turns to Abd al-Haqq Muhaddith Dihlawi's "اخبار الاخیار[OCR?]" and breaks off at the page end (pp. 538–539).

- **Opening fragment (p. 529)**: its heading and subject are in chunk 047.
