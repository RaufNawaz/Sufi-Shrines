# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 047 — pp. 517–528

The chunk opens mid-section. Its first pages (pp. 517–520) describe a Sikh "سمادہ" (samadh) compound: an enclosure, a baoli-type well, the samadh building, a naqqara, Guru pictures and the people who live there. **That section's heading is in the previous chunk (046)**, and this chunk never says whose samadh it is. Titled sections then follow in this order: "احوال تکیہ روڈی شاہ" (p. 520), "تکیہ مطہر شاہ فقیر جلالیہ" (p. 520), "حال گورستان حضرات سادات گیلانی اجداد حضرت پیر محمد شاہ گیلانی" (p. 521), "احوال مزار سید بہاوالدین[OCR?] مرحوم ولد بزرگوار[OCR?] حضرت سید بہاول شاہ گیلانی" (p. 522), "خانقاہ سید میر میران بن سید مبارک حقانی بن سید محمد غوث وچی[OCR?] والا[OCR?]" (p. 522), and "حال مکان تکیہ انبلی[OCR?] والہ" (p. 522). The last section has a sub-heading "تشریح قبور و غیرہ متعلقہ مکان ہذا[OCR?]" (p. 523) and a "شجرہ طیبہ" (p. 525). It runs to the end of the chunk, which stops mid-account of Sayyid Fazil and his sons (p. 528). The Gilani material ties the Lahore family to "اوچ شریف". Pages 522–526 are very badly damaged, and much of them cannot be read with confidence.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Samadh — fittings and decoration** (pp. 518–520):
  - "تقصوریں[OCR?]" (pictures) on all eight sides (p. 518).
  - Mirrors fitted in the dome. In the outer ivan is "نقارہ کلاں", which "ہر روز شام کو بجایا جاتا ہے" (is beaten every evening) (p. 519).
  - A marble railing, coloured painted wooden panels, and a marble floor in part (p. 519).
  - The ceiling is "منقش … کار طلائی بطور شیش محل" (painted, with gold work, in the manner of a Shish Mahal) (p. 519).
- **Samadh — the cloth and objects on it**:
  - A cover lies always on the samadh, "کبھی سفید کبھی رنگین[OCR?] کبھی زرکار[OCR?]" (sometimes white, sometimes coloured[OCR?], sometimes gold-embroidered[OCR?]) (p. 519).
  - "تین شیر [read: تیر?] ایک مور" are always kept on it (p. 519).
  - There is a gold-brocade canopy (p. 519).
- **Samadh — repair by the Raja of Nabha**: "اب عرصہ ایک سال ہوا کہ راجہ صاحب والی نابہہ[OCR?] نے فرش اندرونی اسکے معہ اجارہ مرمت کرا دی" (about a year ago the Raja of Nabha[OCR?] had the inner floor repaired). His name is written in Gurmukhi as a memorial (p. 519).
- **Samadh — upper storey**:
  - Twelve brick steps lead up to the upper storey (p. 519).
  - There are four corner domelets and "انیس[OCR?] گنبدیان خورد" (nineteen[OCR?] small domes), some with broken finials (p. 519).
  - There is a hidden chamber "بطور خفیہ خانہ" (p. 519).
  - The main dome has a gold finial ("کلس طلائی") (p. 520).
- **Samadh — residents and staff**:
  - A niche holds pictures of the Gurus "وغیرہ دسویں گورو" (up to the tenth Guru) (p. 520).
  - Residents include "سنت سکھ" (p. 520).
  - "ایک قوال جو دو وقت گاتا ہے اوسکو اٹھارہ[OCR?] روپیہ سال" (a qawwal who sings twice a day, paid eighteen[OCR?] rupees a year) (p. 520).
- **Land disputes at the takias**:
  - Takia Mutahhar Shah's faqir complains that land was seized by a municipal committee member (p. 521).
  - Takia Tamarind is owned by Sayyid Muhammad Shah Gilani's line but held by Sayyid Nizam al-Din Gilani. Muhammad Shah is quoted as saying "مکان تو … ہمارے بزرگان کا ہے … ہم تو کہتے ہیں کہ وہی مالک بن جاویں" (the house belongs to our elders … we say let him be the owner) (p. 523).
- **Gilani graveyard**: "انکیس[OCR?] قبریں خام" (twenty-one[OCR?] unbaked graves) in the enclosure, and a ruined state "بریدہ[OCR?] و آوارہ" (p. 521).
- **Land grants**:
  - Akbar's offer of a thousand bigha was refused by Sufi Ali (p. 523).
  - Akbar offered "ہزار بیگہہ[OCR?] زمین" in "علاقہ[OCR?] فیروز پور" to the ancestor from Uch (p. 525).
  - A village ("ایک موضع") was granted by "عالم با و ساہ" (read: Alamgir Badshah?) in Sayyid Fazil's time (p. 528).
- **Respect for the Gilani sadat**: "اکثر اہل اسلام بلکہ ہر باشندان[OCR?] لاہور انکا ادب دل و جان سے کرتے ہیں" (most Muslims, indeed all the residents[OCR?] of Lahore, respect them with heart and soul) (p. 524).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **"لکھی[OCR?] محلہ"**: in Akbar's time a man bought "دہ[OCR?] لاکھہ[OCR?] روپیہ کا زعفران" (ten[OCR?] lakh rupees' worth of saffron) and had it mixed in. From that day the place was known as Lakhi Mohalla. The story is heavily garbled (pp. 522–523).
- **The flag**: during the Lahore disturbance the hazrat's household raised a flag so that the Sikhs would see the house was one of "پیران عظام سادات ذوی الاکرام" (great pirs and honoured sayyids) (p. 525).
- **The donated haveli**: a woman's gift of a haveli worth 2000 rupees "نذر حضرت غوث الاعظم" was taken as divine provision ("قدرت الٰہی سے"). It was said "جو مسلمان اس جھنڈے کے سامنے آدمی کا مورد آفرین و فرحت حضرت غوث الاعظم ہو گا" (p. 525).
- **The ancestor's coming**: an order reached him from "الشیخ عبد القادر جیلانی الحسنی" (in a passage too garbled to say how) to go to Multan (p. 526).
- **Hazrat Muhammad Ghaus**: "کرامات حضرت شاہ محمد غوث کی ہزار ہا معروف" (his karamat are known by the thousands) (p. 527).
- **Sayyid Fazil**: he took his mother's words literally and never stepped out of his house for life (p. 528). He was "قائم اللیل" (one who keeps the night vigil) (p. 528).

## Citable passages

- "ایک قوال جو دو وقت گاتا ہے اوسکو اٹھارہ[OCR?] روپیہ سال" — "A qawwal who sings twice a day gets eighteen[OCR?] rupees a year." (p. 520)
- "یہ بڑا معمر آدمی سلسلہ جلالیہ کا فقیر تھا" — of Rodi Shah: "He was a very old man, a faqir of the Jalaliya order." (p. 520)
- "بوقت ویرانی شہر یہ محلہ اجڑ گیا اور اونکی حویلی بھی مسمار ہو گئی" — "When the city fell to ruin this mohalla was deserted and his haveli too was demolished." (p. 523)
- "حضرت شاہ چراغ صاحب و عبد القادر ثالث کہ جنکو مزارات نواح لاہور میں زیارت گاہ خلق اللہ ہیں" — "Hazrat Shah Chiragh and Abd al-Qadir the Third, whose mazars near Lahore are places of pilgrimage for God's creatures." (p. 527)
- "این خانہ تمام آفتاب است" — a Persian saying, "This house is all sunlight", applied to the Gilani family (p. 523).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is very heavy throughout. The Gurmukhi on p. 517 is almost entirely `[illegible]`. Pages 522 (Mir Miran section), 524, 526 and 528 are mostly runs of `[OCR?]` tokens that cannot be construed. Every quotation above keeps its flags. Folio markers (512–522) are printed but not cited, per the brief.
- **The samadh of pp. 517–520 is unnamed in this chunk.** Its heading is in chunk 046. It is NOT attached to samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh as a site. Ranjit Singh is mapped only by the person rule, because he is named as an era or agent (pp. 518, 519, 520). Bare name; weigh accordingly.
- **Guru rows (Nanak 18, Hargobind 6, Arjan 3, Ram Das 1)** are attached by the person rule from pictures in the samadh niche (p. 520) and flagged Gurmukhi words (p. 517). This is person-level, not site-specific, and the evidence is a bare name. gurdwara-panjvi-chati-patshahi appears under both the Hargobind and the Arjan bullets.
- **"تین شیر"** (p. 519) is printed "شیر" (lion). It is read as "تیر" (arrows) only because the next sentence speaks of "تیر و کمان" and "یہ تیر". This is not silently repaired.
- **Gurmukhi samvat "੧੮੭੧੮[OCR?]"** (p. 517) has five digits as printed. Do not convert.
- **Lahore-in-general bullet**: place-level. It rests on city-level statements: the city's ruin (p. 523), the disturbance in Lahore (p. 525), the uproar in the whole city (p. 525) and Akbar in Lahore (p. 525). Named takias, gates (Mochi, Lahori) and graveyards are NOT treated as Lahore-in-general.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh: held identity question, not resolved.** The book names "حضرت شاہ چراغ صاحب" as a descendant of Abd al-Qadir Sani of the Uch line, with a mazar "نواح لاہور" (p. 527). Whether this is the man of the archive row, or whether "Shah Chiragh" conflates two men, is held for review. Mapped as named.
- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari: uncertain.**
  - On p. 523, "موج دریا سنجھار[OCR?] کی زد میں آکر مٹا[OCR?]" could mean literally "erased by the river's current" rather than naming the saint.
  - On p. 525, "حضرت موج دریا سنجار[OCR?]" carries the honorific and reads as the saint, but the sentence is garbled.
  - "سنجھار/سنجار[OCR?]" is unexplained. No "Bukhari" or other identifier is given. Weigh accordingly.
- **Uch Sharif (5 ids): place-level.** The material is the Gilani ancestor's origin, the mazar of Hazrat Muhammad Ghaus at Uch and its upkeep, and the authority of "سادات اوچ" in matters of descent (pp. 525–527). Much of p. 526 is garbled. bahawalpur rows are not separately added; they are a subset of the Uch rows.
- **Not mapped (recorded for the consolidator, against ruling (a)):**
  - **"گورستان میانی"** (pp. 523, 525): the location of Sayyid Badr al-Din's non-archive grave. It is not mapped to the 4 `miani sahib graveyard` rows (ghazi-ilm-din-shaheed, khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz, tahir-bandagi-qadri, wasif-ali-wasif). The book writes "میانی", not "میانی صاحب", and gives only a grave location.
  - **"کوہات"** (p. 523): a family-residence mention, so the `kohat` row (darbar-ghamkol-sharif-zinda-pir) is not attached.
  - **"ملتان"** (p. 526): a single event in a non-archive person's life, so the 8 `multan` rows are not attached.
  - **"دروازہ موچی"** (p. 520): a location only. darbar-abul-muali-qadri is not attached.
- **No province bullet.** "ملک پنجاب[OCR?]" (p. 521) describes where a family is famous, and "فنانشل[OCR?] کمشنر پنجاب" (p. 521) is an office title. Neither is a statement about Punjab in general.
- **Hazrat (Shah) Muhammad Ghaus of Uch** (p. 527) bears the same name as the Lahore saint of chunk 015. This chunk does not identify the two, and neither does this note.
- **Dates** are all flagged numerals. Do not compute:
  - "نو سو[OCR?] اٹہتر[OCR?]" (p. 526).
  - Sayyid Jafar's birth and death in words (pp. 527–528).
  - "سوبارہ[OCR?]" (p. 528).
  - "بارہ سو دس[OCR?] ۱۲۱[OCR?]" (p. 528): the words and digits disagree as printed.
  - "۱۲۵۵[OCR?]" (p. 528).
- **"عالم با و ساہ"** (p. 528) is read tentatively as Alamgir Badshah. It is not confirmed.
- **Two darbars on the site but not in the index** (Shah Gohar Peer; Mian Qurban Ali Shah): neither is named in this chunk.
