# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 016 — pp. 184–192

**The chunk opens mid-section.** Pages 184–186 are the end of an account of **حضرت شاہ بلاول (Hazrat Shah Bilawal)**. That section's heading is in the previous chunk (015), so his name, dates and the start of his account are not here. These pages give miracle stories involving Shah Jahan and Dara Shikoh, a contrast drawn with Mian Mir, a description of his tomb, a removal of his coffin, and his urs (pp. 184–186). At p. 186 a new section begins: "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت شیخ طاہر بندگی و مفصل احوال مقابر گورستان خطہ میانی بنچ [OCR?] ڈھیرا [OCR?]", the section on the tomb of Hazrat Shaikh Tahir Bandagi with a detailed account of the tombs of the Miani Panj Dhera graveyard. It covers his life and conversion story, his death and its chronogram, a Persian petition attributed to him, and a building-by-building description of his enclosure (pp. 186–190). It then gives the history of the graveyard's name and a tabulated list of its named sites, which breaks off in a description of a mosque and the graves of Malik Ali Kotwal (pp. 190–192). Pp. 188, 190 and 192 are very heavily damaged; much of them can't be used.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **tahir-bandagi-qadri** (the book's "حضرت شیخ طاہر بندگی", also "شیخ طاہر لاہوری" and "شیخ طاہر"):

  - The author cites "کتاب تذکرۃ الاحبا [OCR?]" as his source for Shaikh Tahir's account (p. 186).
  - He was a murid of "حضرت شیخ احمد سرہندی کہ [OCR?] المجدد الف ثانی [OCR?]". He first taught the Mujaddid's sons "حضرت شیخ محمد معصوم اور شیخ محمد سعید [OCR?]" (p. 187).
  - Conversion story, as the book tells it: the Mujaddid told his murids that one of those present would become a kafir. When asked, he named "حضرت [illegible] نقشبندی [OCR?]". Later, at Sirhind ("بمقام سرہند"), he fell in love with a "ماہ پیشانی [OCR?]". He wore the zunnar ("زنار [OCR?]"), drew the qashqa ("قشقہ کھینچ کر"), sat in the idol-temple ("بتخانہ میں جا بیٹھے"), and in the end changed his appearance, "ہندو ہو کر", and lived in the temple (p. 187).
  - The couplet attributed to him in this episode: "کافرِ عشقم مسلمانی مرا در کار نیست ٭ ہر رگِ من تار گشتہ حاجتِ زنار [OCR?] نیست" (p. 187).
  - The Mujaddid's sons grieved that "ہمارا اوستاد کافر ہو گیا" and asked their father to intervene, because of "حقِ اوستادی". The Mujaddid's prayer was answered. Shaikh Tahir came to his senses, repented, became a murid and reached perfection, and "قطبیت لاہور کی [illegible] حضرت مجدد سے اونکو عطا ہوئی" (p. 187).
  - When he came to Lahore, "ہزار ہا" (thousands of) people came to him (p. 187).
  - His practice: he accepted no cash, goods, "نذر و نیاز [OCR?]" from anyone. He always copied books of hadith and tafsir himself and sold them, and lived on the income from that hard labour (p. 187).
  - Death: "بروزِ پنجشنبہ ششم [OCR?] ماہ محرم سنہ ۱۰۴۱ [OCR?]", and "بمقام میانی مدفون ہوئی" (p. 187).
  - "مفتی [OCR?] غلام سرور" composed a "قطعہ تاریخ وفات" (chronogram verse) on him (p. 187). The qita follows on p. 188 but is almost entirely flagged (p. 188).
  - A second source is named, "کتاب تذکرہ محدودیہ[OCR?]" (p. 188). The book then copies an "عرضی" (petition) in Persian, from "احقر العبد مرتب[OCR?] محمد طاہر[OCR?]". In it the writer says he set out for Lahore ("متوجہ لاہور شدم"), and it mentions "حضرت خواجہ بزرگ[OCR?]", "حضرت غوث الاعظم[OCR?]" and "قادریہ و چشتیہ" (p. 188). Nearly every word is flagged.
  - "دفا[OCR?] حضرت شیخ طاہر مدگی[OCR?] کہ عہد شاہجہان میں وقوع میں آئی": his death fell in Shah Jahan's reign (p. 188). His age and the regnal year follow but are unreadable ("بارسال[OCR?] سندیلوس[OCR?] ساہجہانی[OCR?]") (p. 188).
  - Title: "حضرت لال کمال شاہ[OCR?] ساکن کیتھل[OCR?]" gave him the title "طاہر بندگی", "سو اب تک طاہر بندگی مشہور ہیں" (p. 189).
  - "اولاد حضرت کی باقی نہیں": none of his descendants remain (p. 189).
  - Two smaller graves next to his are of his wives ("زوجہ" printed "و رو وجہ[OCR?]"): "ماہ خاتم[OCR?] دختر مرزا امان اللہ[OCR?]" and "عصمت النسا[OCR?] دختر سید شہ[OCR?]" (p. 189).
  - While Shaikh Tahir lived, he resided inside the hisar of Lahore in "محلہ شیخ اسحاق[OCR?]". The text then gives a location by "حویلی[OCR?]... خوشحال سنگہ[OCR?]" and a bazaar, but that part is damaged (p. 191).

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (the book's "میاں میر صاحب", "حضرت میاں میر"; he appears only as a foil in the Shah Bilawal account):
  - The page opens with someone (the subject is in the damaged line, with "شاہجہان" in context) having gone to "[illegible] مست میاں میر صاحب". Mian Mir did not accept the offering, but Shah Bilawal did (p. 185).
  - Shah Bilawal's explanation: "میاں میر صاحب ملکِ صفات میں اور عالمِ تجرید و تفرید میں لگا نہ [OCR?]", and "انکا توجہ [illegible] دنیا کی طرف بالکل نہیں ہے". Shah Bilawal, by contrast, has the poor, travellers and dervishes to provide for, so he needs money (p. 185).
  - The same day Shah Jahan went to Mian Mir and asked why his offering ("دو روپیہ پیشکش [OCR? context]" printed "آپکے [illegible] دو روپیہ پیشکش بندہ کا") was refused when Shah Bilawal accepted it. Mian Mir answered: "وہ ولئ کامل دریا کی مانند میں اور میں بجائے راہ [illegible] ہوں". If something falls into a river, the river is not made [illegible] (p. 185).
  - Shah Jahan then gave thanks that his reign held "ایسے اولیائی کامل و ولی اکمل" (p. 185).

- **tahir-bandagi-qadri**, **ghazi-ilm-din-shaheed**, **khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz**, **wasif-ali-wasif** (the book's "گورستان خطہ میانی بنچ [OCR?] ڈھیرا [OCR?]" / "قبرستان میانی پنج ڈھیر[OCR?]", i.e. the Miani graveyard as a place):
  - Place-level section title, as above (p. 186).
  - The book's account of the name: in Akbar's reign the place was a village ("کانون[OCR?]" / "گانون[OCR?]") where only learned men lived. Because Punjabis call ulama "میاں", the village was called "میانی" (p. 191).
  - Later, when its ulama gained nearness to the king, it was given another name, printed "شاہ[OCR?]..." (p. 191).
  - Its chief at that time was "حافظ جان محمد صاحب والد حضرت ابو محمد قادری". The ulama shared an "انعام[OCR?]" among themselves (p. 191).
  - After his death Abu Muhammad Qadiri was made "افسر". A split arose among the ulama "کسی مسئلہ شرعی میں", many left, and "یہ موضع ویران ہو گیا" (p. 191).
  - "حضرت میر علی[OCR?]" was brought from "گمہٹ[OCR?]", appointed by the king, and settled there. Its "ریاست" was shared between Abu Muhammad and Mir Ali, and the place was resettled (p. 191).
  - "پیر علیمہ[OCR?] صاحب" had the coffins of "پانچ بزرگ[OCR?]" brought from "گمہٹ[OCR?]" and buried here. Because in Punjabi a grave mound is called "ڈھیر[OCR?]", the place was named "میانی پنج ڈھیر[OCR?]" (p. 191).
  - East and south of one enclosure, "تمام قبرستان کنجر[OCR?]" (p. 191).
  - The chunk closes with a tabulated list of named sites in the graveyard (see Other) (p. 192). A related passage, too damaged to construe, mentions "محمد شاہ[OCR?]", "یہ موضع" and "حصہ چند[OCR?] قبرستان میانی" (p. 192).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Shah Bilawal's urs** is held at his tomb. People of Lahore ("لاہور کے کتنے [OCR?] قبیل [OCR?] لوگ") come and set off various things. The text mentions "آتشباری [OCR?]" (fireworks) and "چکہ چلانے [OCR?]", and the custom is described as "رسم" (p. 186).
- **Shah Bilawal's tomb**: north-facing gates, a dalan, a raised three-step platform ("چبوترہ بڑا ملبند [OCR?] تین زینہ والہ"), a well with a wooden wheel ("چاہ چرخ چوب"), and the tomb under a dome ("گنبد") (pp. 185–186). The details are heavily flagged.
- **Shaikh Tahir's enclosure** (p. 189):
  - A wall of man's height ("قد آدم") made of lime plaster ("چونہ گچ"). One gate is in the middle of the north wall and a second on the south side, used for coming and going (p. 189).
  - Two walls carry "آٹھہ آٹھہ پنجرہ خشتی" (eight brick lattices each) (p. 189).
  - A dalan of white, painted, lime-plastered work is the "جائے[OCR?] نشست گدی نشین یعنی سجادہ نشین جناب سید شہ[OCR?] بخش[OCR?] رسول[OCR?]". There is an upper room ("بالاخانہ بطور[OCR?] شہ نشین"), and "یہاں بموسم گرما سجادہ نشین ٹھہرتا[OCR?] ہے": the sajjada nashin stays here in the hot season (p. 189).
  - A central masonry platform ("چبوترہ پختہ چونہ گچ") with steps holds the tomb, "جو چونہ گچ سفید". A large tree ("پہروان[OCR?]") has a branch reaching over the grave (p. 189).
  - "مزار حضرت پر ہمیشہ غلاف سفید رنگ کا پڑا رہتا ہے": a white cover always lies on the grave, with a lamp niche ("چراغدان") at the head (p. 189).
- **Enclosure wall, Sikh period**: a passage ties an enclosure wall to "ملازم راجہ[OCR?] دہیاں سنگہ[OCR?]" and "سمت[OCR?] ۱۸۹۹[OCR?]" (p. 190). Who built it, and which wall, is not legible.
- **Chilla house**: west of Hakim Ali's mosque is a "کوٹہ[OCR?]" known as a "مکان جلہ[OCR?]", where visiting faqirs "طالب جلد[OCR?]" stay. One of its two south doors is now closed and the other open (p. 191).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Shah Bilawal makes out-of-season "ساگ جو لائی [OCR?]" appear for Shah Jahan, "حضرت کی کرامت سی" (p. 184).
- Shah Bilawal sends a cloud: when Abu Talib ("ابو طالب [OCR?]") asked for rain on his jagir, a cloud appeared over the saint's head. He told it to go and rain on Abu Talib's jagir. Abu Talib noted the day and hour, and news came that it had rained there at that very time (p. 185).
- The Mujaddid foresees that one of his murids will become a kafir, and his prayer brings Shaikh Tahir back (p. 187), as the book attributes it to "کتاب تذکرۃ الاحبا [OCR?]" (p. 186).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author reports from his own site visits ("فدوی نے بر سرِ موقع مکان جا کر دیکھا") (p. 185). For Shaikh Tahir he cites two named tazkiras, "تذکرۃ الاحبا [OCR?]" (p. 186) and "تذکرہ محدودیہ[OCR?]" (p. 188), and Mufti Ghulam Sarwar's chronogram (p. 187).
- He explains the name "میانی پنج ڈھیر" from Punjabi usage ("میاں" for ulama, "ڈھیر" for a grave) (p. 191).

## Doubts

- **Opening fragment**: pp. 184–186 continue the Shah Bilawal section, whose heading is in chunk 015. He is not in shrine_index.tsv. "شمالا باغ" (p. 185) is only a road reference, and **madho-lal-hussain** is not mapped from it.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir** is mapped on the principal-figure name "Mian Mir" only. The chunk says nothing about his shrine, and all of the material comes from the Shah Bilawal anecdote.
- **Miani graveyard, place-level**: the book names it "میانی" / "میانی پنج ڈھیر[OCR?]", not "Miani Sahib". The four-id bullet rests on the index's own placement of tahir-bandagi-qadri in "Miani Sahib Graveyard" and on the book burying Shaikh Tahir "بمقام میانی" (p. 187). Treat it as place-level, not site-specific, and weigh it accordingly.
- **Death date of Shaikh Tahir**: printed "ششم [OCR?] ماہ محرم سنہ ۱۰۴۱ [OCR?]" (p. 187). shrine_index.tsv's figure_died for tahir-bandagi-qadri reads "8 Muharram 1040 AH". Both the day and the year disagree, and both of the book's numerals are flagged. Check against the PDF; do not reconcile.
- **"Bandagi" spellings**: "مدگی[OCR?]" (p. 188) and "بندہ[OCR?]" (p. 191) are read as Bandagi from context only.
- **Silsila**: the chunk makes him the Mujaddid's murid and gives the label "نقشبندی [OCR?]" next to an illegible name (p. 187). The index row says Qadri. The chunk gives no silsila for Tahir himself, so none is recorded above.
- **"خانقہ سید چراغ شاہ[OCR?]"** (p. 192) is listed as a site **inside the Miani graveyard**. It is not mapped to **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** (The Mall; "Shah Chiragh"), because the name order and location differ and the name is flagged. This is a held identity question; the consolidator should look at it. Likewise "میراں شاہ[OCR?]" (p. 192) is not mapped to **shrine-of-miran-hussain-zanjani-zanjani-sahib**.
- **"حضرت خواجہ ویریکیح[OCR?] شکر"** (p. 188) in the Persian petition might be a garbled honorific, but it is unreadable and is not mapped to any row.
- **"پنجابی" usage** (p. 191) is a statement about language, used to explain one place's name. It is not treated as Punjab province-level material.
- **"سمت[OCR?] ۱۸۹۹[OCR?]"** (p. 190) is quoted as printed. The era and the conversion are not stated, and it is not converted here.
- **No Lahore-in-general material**: the chunk mentions Lahore only as the location of named sites, Shaikh Tahir's qutbiyat, or the people attending an urs. No city-level bullet is made.
- **Damage inventory**: very heavy throughout. The p. 188 qita and Persian petition, most of p. 190, and the p. 192 table and mosque description are nearly all `[OCR?]`. P. 184 has many `[illegible]` gaps inside the Shah Jahan/Dara Shikoh story, so what the hoped-for sign about Dara Shikoh was, and its outcome, can't be read. Folio markers (178–186) appear but are not cited.
