# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 004 — pp. 48–60

This chunk is hagiography, almost all of it about Hazrat Husain (Lal Husain) and his circle, and it is very heavily damaged. Pages 48–57 continue an account that began before the chunk: the travels of a holy man, named Shaikh Bahlul from p. 49 on, through Medina, Baghdad, Mashhad and a cave on Kuh Banjshir[OCR?]. There a majzub sends him to Lahore to guide the boy Husain. The account then follows Husain's training, his twelve years at the khanqah of Pir Ali Ganj Bakhsh Hujwiri, his turn to the *malamatiyya* path and Bahlul's death. At p. 57 a new section opens, "احوال حضرت شیخ مادہو قدس سرہ العزیز" (the account of Hazrat Shaikh Madho), about the Brahmin boy Madho and Husain's love for him. At p. 59 another begins, "در ذکر کرامات و خوارق عادات حضرت لال حسین رحمتہ اللہ" (on the miracles and wonders of Hazrat Lal Husain). It breaks off mid-sentence at p. 60. Every page carries a printed folio marker. Following the brief, none of them is cited.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **madho-lal-hussain** (the book's "حضرت حسین", "حضرت لال حسین", "حسین فقیر[OCR?]"; and "حضرت شیخ مادہو" / "مادہو"):
  - A majzub orders Bahlul: "یہاں سے براہ لاہور کو جاؤ اور [OCR?] حسین کو راہ ہدایت[OCR?] دکھلاؤ", go from here by way of Lahore and show Husain the path of guidance (p. 49).
  - When Bahlul came, "حضرت لال حسین دہ سالہ تھی[OCR?]" (Lal Husain was ten years old). He was memorising the Quran with Maulvi Abu Bakr, "ساکن [illegible]" (resident of [illegible]) (p. 50). He had memorised the seventh sipara and begun the eighth (p. 50).
  - His maktab was in his own mohalla, and "وہ محلہ [illegible] دروازہ ٹکسالی[OCR?] لاہور متصل[OCR?] [illegible] دریا" (that mohalla [illegible] Taksali[OCR?] gate, Lahore, adjoining [illegible] the river) (p. 50). See Doubts.
  - Bahlul sends Husain to fetch water from the river for his ablution, prays that he be made "فقیر عارف", and stays "کچھ[OCR?] مدت" (some time) in Lahore to train him (p. 50).
  - Bahlul has Husain lead the *tarawih* prayers in Ramadan. On the seventh day Husain says he has recited all he knew (p. 50). After the encounter at the river he completes the Quran in Ramadan (pp. 51–52).
  - A date for Bahlul's coming to Husain is given in a verse attributed to "حضرت پیر محمد صاحب حقیقت الفقر[OCR?]": "سال پنجاہ و پنج و نہ صد بود" (the year was nine hundred and fifty-five). No era is stated (p. 52). A second chronogram, marked "لمحررہ[OCR?]" (by the writer), has its date illegible (p. 52).
  - On leaving, Bahlul tells Husain that the mazar of Pir Makhdum Ali Ganj Bakhsh Hujwiri is "یہاں لاہور میں" (here in Lahore), and commits him to Hujwiri's service (p. 52). See **data-darbar**.
  - His ascetic routine: practice at the river, "دائم الصوم اور قایم اللیل" (always fasting and standing in prayer at night) (p. 53). He kept the dawn and *ishraq* prayers at Hujwiri's khanqah and mazar[OCR?] and always prayed in congregation. "بارہ برس" (twelve years) at that khanqah from morning to evening[OCR?] (p. 53).
  - Later routine: completing the Quran until *chasht*, a short *qailula*[OCR?] (midday nap), then teaching *tafsir*[OCR?] in madrasas until *asr*. At night he stood in the river completing the Quran (p. 54).
  - He studied *tafsir* with "شیخ سعد اللہ", reading "تفسیر مدارک[OCR?] و نسفی[OCR?]" (p. 54). A verse calling the world "لہو و لعب" (play and pastime) led him to strike his feet, dance, and dispute its meaning with his teacher (pp. 54–55). He then left the madrasa dancing (p. 55).
  - "اس روز سے آپ نے طریقہ ملامتیہ اختیار کر لیا تا لوگ اوسے نفرت کریں" (from that day he took up the *malamatiyya* way, so that people would despise him) (p. 56). A chronogram (*qita*) for this has its year illegible, and his age then is "[illegible: numeral word][OCR?] برس" (p. 56).
  - He spent nights with wine and music. Bahlul heard that Husain had gone "خطہ[OCR?] صلاح سے باہر" (beyond the bounds of right conduct), came to Lahore, watched him, and was satisfied that "حضرت حسین باحق واصل ہیں" (Husain is joined with God) (p. 56).
  - After Bahlul's death Husain "بدستور لاہور میں مرجع خلق عام رہے[OCR?]" (remained, as before, the resort of the common people in Lahore). Drunk, he often danced to *chang* and *rabab*, though the text says he did this "واسطے اخفائے[OCR?] حال" (to conceal his state). He completed the Quran every night in the first watch (p. 57).
  - Madho: "حضرت مادہو ایک حسین لڑکا ذات کا برہمن تہا" (Madho was a handsome boy, a Brahmin by caste), "ساکن قصبہ شاہدرہ[OCR?]" (resident of the qasba of Shahdara[OCR?]) (p. 57). Husain fell in love at first sight, rode there at once, and circled Madho's house at night (p. 57).
  - "سولہ[OCR?] برس" (sixteen[OCR?] years) passed, and Husain became "بدنام" (notorious) for this love. Madho then turned to him, and they drank and slept together (p. 58).
  - Madho's relatives feared he would become Muslim and resolved to kill Husain. "آخر کار مادہو ظاہر بہی مسلمان ہو گیا" (in the end Madho outwardly too became Muslim) (p. 58).
  - On Basant Panchami[OCR?], near Holi, Madho threw colour and *gulal* on Husain, colouring his beard. Husain went into ecstasy and danced, and kept Basant every year for life (p. 58).
  - Companions of Husain's majlis, as printed: "میاں[OCR?] شعبان", "میاں[OCR?] ابراہیم[OCR?]", "ملا محمد[OCR?]", "یعقوب[OCR?]", "شاہ[OCR?] جہاں[OCR?]", "محمد[OCR?] اسلام[OCR?]", "شہاب[OCR?] الدیں", "صالح[OCR?]" (pp. 58–59).
  - Jahangir: "حضرت نور الدیں جہانگیر بادشاہ" is named at the head of the miracles section. Someone was appointed to write down what Husain said and report it, and a book of his "حالات" (circumstances) is mentioned. Nearly all of the passage is illegible (p. 59).
  - Madho "بصدق[OCR?] دل مسلمان ہو گیا" (became Muslim in sincerity of heart) on the day of the Ganga miracle (p. 59). His age is given as "[illegible: numeral] سال[OCR?]" (p. 59). Husain gave him wine with his own hand, "کدورت کفر اوسکے[OCR?] دل سے رفع کی" (removing the turbidity of unbelief from his heart) (p. 60).
  - At "بابو یورہ[OCR?]", which the text says belonged to a "[illegible] بابو[OCR?]" who was one of Husain's intimates, Husain made Madho "ولی کامل" (a perfect saint) through drink (p. 60).
  - Madho entered the service of "راجہ مان سنگہ[OCR?]" and went with him to Delhi. "بحکم اکبر شاہ" (by order of Akbar Shah) the Raja went on the Deccan[OCR?] campaign and took Madho with him. Afterwards the Raja told Madho "آج سی[OCR?] میں آپ کا مرید ہوں" (from today I am your disciple) (p. 60).

- **data-darbar** (the book's "جناب حضرت پیر مخدوم علی گنج بخش ہجویری رحمۃ اللہ علیہ"; "مخدوم شیخ پیر علی"):
  - Bahlul to Husain: "یہاں لاہور میں جناب حضرت پیر مخدوم علی گنج بخش ہجویری رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کے مزار پر انوار ہے" (here in Lahore is the light-filled mazar of Pir Makhdum Ali Ganj Bakhsh Hujwiri) (p. 52). Persian verses follow in his praise (pp. 52–53).
  - Bahlul says that whoever comes to Hujwiri's door finds the way of truth, and that the disappointed who come with a sincere heart obtain their wish. Husain should stay at "دروازہ فیض انداز[OCR?]" (his door of grace), and Hujwiri will be his "مربی" (guide/trainer) on the path (p. 52).
  - Husain kept his prayers at "حضرت پیر علی گنج بخش ہجویری کے خانقاہ و مزار[OCR?]" (the khanqah and mazar[OCR?] of Hazrat Pir Ali Ganj Bakhsh Hujwiri) and served "بارہ برس" (twelve years) at the khanqah (p. 53).
  - A radiant figure appears to Husain and gives his name in verse: "گفت نامم [OCR?] رحمت[OCR?] ازلی ٭ ہست مخدوم شیخ پیر علی". He tells Husain that for twelve years' service he has become "ولی[OCR?] کامل" (pp. 53–54).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- Shaikh Bahlul ("حضرت شیخ بہلول"), Husain's guide (pp. 49–57). His home seat: "اپنا مقام چنیوٹ[OCR?] سے[OCR?] سات میل اس طرف ہے" (his own place is seven miles this side of Chiniot[OCR?]) (p. 53). The chunk mentions his "رحلت" (death), but the chronogram dates in "قطعہ" verses are illegible. One date is attributed to "پہر محمد[OCR?] [illegible] حقیقت الفقر[OCR?]" (p. 57).
- Bahlul's pilgrimage stops (pp. 48–49), in order: the mazars of Imam Baqir and Ja'far Sadiq, Bibi Fatima al-Zahra, the graves of the Companions and of Uthman ibn Affan, then Medina, where he spent six months at the Prophet's rauza. Next came Baghdad, for "شیخ عبد القادر جیلانی", where he swept the shrine a full year. Then the rauza of "امام اعظم", the [illegible] of "موسی امام کاظم", and Mashhad. Last was "کوہ بنجشیر[OCR?]", the cave of a majzub near "غار اصحاب کہف" (the cave of the Companions of the Cave), who is described as of "سالکان مسلک سلسلہ قادریہ" (wayfarers of the Qadiri order) (p. 48).
- The anonymous majzub of the cave, with two glances, "جلالی[OCR?]" and "جمالی[OCR?]". "نام اوس شیخ مجذوب[OCR?] کا کسکو معلوم نہیں" (no one knows that majzub's name), because Bahlul told no one. People called him "مرد حق" (pp. 49–50).
- Maulvi / Hafiz Abu Bakr, Husain's Quran teacher (p. 50).
- Shaikh Sa'd Allah ("شیخ سعد اللہ"), Husain's tafsir teacher, called "عالم عامل اور ولی[OCR?] کامل" (p. 54).
- Pir Muhammad, author of "حقیقت الفقر[OCR?]", quoted for dates (pp. 52, 57).
- Raja Man Singh ("راجہ مان سنگہ[OCR?]") and Akbar Shah ("اکبر شاہ"): the Deccan[OCR?] campaign (p. 60). Jahangir: see p. 59 above.
- Places named only as settings: Shahdara[OCR?] (Madho's qasba, p. 57); "بابو یورہ[OCR?]" (p. 60); Delhi and the Deccan[OCR?] (p. 60); the Ganga (p. 59).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Basant at the mazar, present tense: "الی الان[OCR?] بروز بسنت انکی مزار پر سرود و سماع و رقص و رنگ اندازی ہوتی ہی" (to this day on Basant there is singing, *sama*, dancing and throwing of colour at his mazar) (p. 58). This attaches to **madho-lal-hussain**. Its origin is given as Husain's yearly keeping of Basant after Madho coloured him (p. 58).
- Hindu observances as the book describes them: Basant Panchami[OCR?] celebrated "حسب رسم و معمول" (by custom) with dancing, near Holi (p. 58). Bathing in the Ganga is called meritorious ("غسل کا ثواب[OCR?] عظیم") (p. 59).
- Husain taught *tafsir* in madrasas (p. 54). He led *tarawih* (p. 50).
- At p. 59, apparently under Jahangir, a person was appointed to record Husain's words and inform [illegible]. This is too damaged to state further.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- The majzub's jalali glance burned to ash whatever it fell on, and his jamali glance made things green. Anyone before him in that state became a perfect saint. His jamali glance resolved all of Bahlul's difficulties (p. 49).
- At the river, a "[illegible] پوش[OCR?]" figure gave Husain water from a jug. Afterwards the unlettered Husain recited the whole Quran, "بسم تا والناس" (from Bismillah to wal-Nas) (pp. 51–52).
- Hujwiri appeared to Husain in radiant form after twelve years' service and declared him a perfect saint (pp. 53–54).
- Husain threw his tafsir into a well. When his fellow students were upset, the book came out of the well "خشک آب نارسیدہ[OCR?]" (dry, untouched by water), and "تمام شہر میں اس کرامت کا چرچا پھیلا" (the whole city talked of this miracle) (pp. 55–56).
- Husain had Madho put his foot on his footstep and close his eyes. After one step they were at the Ganga. Madho bathed, met his parents, returned, and that day became Muslim (p. 59).
- In the Deccan[OCR?] battle, Madho called on Husain inwardly. Husain, who was in Lahore, told his companions he would be back soon and reached Madho "بزور کرامت" (by force of miracle). The Raja saw "فوج قلندران[OCR?]" (an army of qalandars) in the sky, the enemy was defeated, and Husain returned to Lahore and told his companions (p. 60).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author names his sources inline: "حقیقت الفقر[OCR?]" of Pir Muhammad (pp. 52, 57); "کتاب بہاریہ[OCR?] میں تحریر ہے" (it is written in the book Bahariya[OCR?]) for the well miracle (p. 55); "خاصہ شیخ [illegible] لکھتے ہیں" (Shaikh [illegible] writes) (p. 57). He also gives his own chronogram ("لمحررہ[OCR?]", p. 52). He presents Husain's drinking and music as concealment of his state (p. 57). He does not dispute any of it.

## Doubts

- Damage inventory: heavy `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` on every page. Most Persian verses are largely illegible (pp. 51, 53, 57). All chronogram years are illegible (pp. 52, 56, 57), as are Husain's age at the malamati turn (p. 56) and Madho's age (p. 59). The opening of the miracles section (p. 59) is mostly illegible. The only legible date is "سال پنجاہ و پنج و نہ صد بود" (p. 52). It is written in words, inside a verse, with no era stated. Do not convert it.
- The p. 50 phrase "متصل[OCR?] [illegible] دریا ہی[OCR?] راوی[OCR?] [illegible] راوی[OCR?] کہتا ہے": "راوی" may be the river's name or "narrator" (as in "راوی کہتا ہے", the narrator says). I have not resolved it, and the river is not named in these notes.
- No Lahore-in-general bullet. Lahore appears only as the setting of events, and "دروازہ ٹکسالی[OCR?]" only as a location for Husain's mohalla, so ruling (a) was not triggered.
- Chiniot[OCR?] appears only as a distance reference for Bahlul's seat (p. 53). The chunk says nothing about Chiniot itself or about Makhdoom Burhan-ud-din, so **langer-makhdoom** was not mapped. A consolidator may weigh this.
- Madho is mapped under **madho-lal-hussain** because the row name and principal figure combine both men. The row's principal figure is Shah Hussain, and the book's own title for Madho is "حضرت شیخ مادہو".
- Madho's conversion is told twice. He "ظاہر بہی مسلمان ہو گیا" (outwardly too became Muslim) (p. 58), then "بصدق[OCR?] دل مسلمان ہو گیا" (became Muslim in sincerity of heart) on the Ganga day (p. 59). The two may be sequential (outward, then inward) or a contradiction.
- The Quran teacher is "مولوی ابو بکر" and "حافظ ابو بکر" on the same page (p. 50). I take these as one man, but the chunk does not say so.
- The p. 48 traveller is unnamed on that page. He is identified as Shaikh Bahlul from p. 49 by continuity. The start of his account is outside this chunk.
- "حضرت شیخ بہلول" and Pir Muhammad of "حقیقت الفقر[OCR?]": I added no dates or silsila. The chunk links the majzub to the "سلسلہ قادریہ" (p. 48) but gives no silsila for Bahlul or Husain.
