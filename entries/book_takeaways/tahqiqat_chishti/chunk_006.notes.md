# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 006 — pp. 73–84

This chunk sits entirely inside the book's account of Hazrat Hussain of Lahore (the book's "حضرت حسین", also "مادھو لعل حسین"). It opens mid-way through a Persian eulogy and the author's statement that Hussain's karamat are countless (p. 73). Next comes a section headed "حکایت", a story, about Shaikh Arzani[OCR?], Hussain's fellow disciple, who is sent from Lahore to Thatta and has a tomb there (pp. 73–76). The author then turns to the buildings and fairs at Hussain's khanqah, with a long first-person digression on a blessing given to him at an urs and on the Basant fair under the Sikh Maharaja and under the British (pp. 76–80). Further karamat about Akbar's vazir and a mulla follow, then the author's own sources (pp. 81–82). The chunk ends with two titled sections: "وفات حضرت مادھو لعل حسین رحمۃ اللہ علیہ", which lists Hussain's sixteen disciples and their tombs (pp. 82–83), and "بیان سجادہ نشینان حضرت حسین مرحوم", the succession of sajjada nashins, which breaks off mid-sentence at p. 84. The chunk maps to two archive rows. No Lahore-in-general bullet was needed (see Doubts).

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **madho-lal-hussain** (the book's "حضرت حسین"; section title "حضرت مادھو لعل حسین"; at p. 80 "حضرت شیخ مادھو[OCR?] و جناب حسین", called "عاشق و معشوق"):
  - The author says Hussain's karamat are "لا تعد و لا تحصی", beyond counting, and that he could not finish writing them in a lifetime (p. 73).
  - **Shaikh Arzani story.** "شیخ ارزانی[OCR?]", Hussain's "ہم پیر بھائی[OCR?]", challenges him. Arzani flies up high, and Hussain catches him from the sky, so that he falls to the ground unconscious (pp. 73–74). Hussain then vanishes, appearing on "ایک کنگرہ عرش", and Arzani cannot find him anywhere. Arzani apologises and calls himself Hussain's servant (p. 74). Arzani hears that Hussain has died and comes to Lahore (p. 74). (Details under Legends.)
  - Persian verses at pp. 74–75 stage a dialogue with Hussain speaking "از تہِ خاکِ گور", from beneath the grave's earth. The verses use "جولاہ" (weaver), and Hussain answers "شیرِ راہِ خدا با کم من / نہ چہ روباہ زیرِ خاکم من" (p. 74).
  - Arzani stays at the shrine and takes up residence there. He goes to "مزارِ حسینی کی قدموں" (p. 75). Then "حضرت [illegible] مرحوم" appears in [illegible]'s dream and says that a man named Shaikh Arzani lives in "میری خانقاہ" and should be sent to Thatta (pp. 75–76).
  - **Akbar's farman.** "اکبر بادشاہ", "بتعمیلِ حکم حضرت حسین", issued a farman to the "ناظمِ لاہور" to send Shaikh Arzani, who lived at "حضرت حسین [illegible] خانقاہِ عالی جاہ", to "شہرِ ٹھٹھہ[OCR?]", and the nazim did so (p. 76). As he left, Arzani "مزارِ مبارک کو بغل میں لیکر روئے", wept holding the blessed tomb (p. 76).
  - The author introduces "حضرت حسین کی خانقاہ پر مجموعہ عمارات", the group of buildings at the khanqah, and promises an account of them (p. 76). The detailed list does not appear in this chunk.
  - He says the devotion of Hindus and Muslims to the shrine is such that "[illegible] دم نہیں مار سکتا" (p. 77).
  - **Two fairs ("دو میلو[OCR?]")**: one is "چراغوں کا مسلہ[OCR?]", the chiraghon fair. Thousands of people come for ziyarat, and there is no room to put a foot down (p. 77). Crowds stretch toward "[illegible] دروازہ / لاہور" (p. 77). (Numbers under Practices.)
  - The author is writing on the day of the urs: "آج تاریخِ سلخ [illegible] حضرت کا عرسِ مبارک" (p. 77).
  - **Basant under the Sikhs.** Under "علمداری سکھان[OCR?]", on Basant the "مہاراجہ صاحب بہادر" ordered all nobles, chiefs and troops to wear basanti (yellow) dress, with tents put up "میان مزار پر انوار". Troops in basanti lined the way "در قلعہ تا مزار پر انوار", from the fort to the tomb (p. 78). The Maharaja's procession left the fort and came to the mela with "چار پانچ سو گھوڑا" and "دو رجمنٹ[OCR?] پیدل" (p. 79). He dismounted and went with the chiefs to the khanqah, and a salute was fired. The offering was "گیارہ سو[OCR?] روپیہ نقد معہ دو شالہ بسنتی", with halwa and preserves[OCR?] (p. 79). He gave robes of honour, attar, ambergris and gulal were thrown, and dancing women from Lahore attended (pp. 79–80). The nazr collected went to servants and troops as a Basant prize, and the procession went back into the fort near sunset (p. 80). (Details and flags under Practices.)

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Fair crowds.** "ساٹھ[OCR?] ستر[OCR?] ہزار آدمی" attend (p. 77). Riders and camels come separately (p. 77). District hakims and officers are present (p. 77).
- **Liquor at the fair.** "دن رات میں ہزار ہا روپیہ کی شراب فروخت ہو جاتی ہے", thousands of rupees' worth of liquor sold day and night. People wear fine clothes (p. 77).
- **Basant under the Sikhs** (pp. 78–80). Basanti dress was ordered for the whole court and army, with tents at the mazar and troops lining the route from the fort (p. 78). The procession had 400–500 horses and two regiments[OCR?] of infantry, and the Maharaja made cash offerings[OCR?] (p. 79). The offering was 1100[OCR?] rupees with a basanti shawl, and robes of honour were given (p. 79). The nazr was given out as a Basant prize to servants and troops. A month's pay to the troops is mentioned with "موقوف[OCR?]"; the meaning is unclear (p. 80).
- **Revenue-free grants.** "معاف و واگذار" under "فرامین شاہان[OCR?] جغتائی[OCR?]" (p. 80).
- **Under British rule.** Prizes on Basant by order of the hakims. A prize for "آسمان[OCR?]" is mentioned in a damaged context. There is a prize for an Arab-bred mare "بہ رنگ سبزہ[OCR?]", with "قیمت مبلغ تیرہ سو[OCR?]". A "کمیٹی[OCR?]" is credited (p. 80).
- **The author's tabarruk.** From the cowries in his lap he counted "تین آنہ کی کوڑیاں[OCR?]" and "پونہ آنہ یعنی تین[OCR?] پیسے[OCR?] ڈبل[OCR?]". He means to buy a "چوآنی" with them, keep it at home, and hang it on the neck of the son he prays for (p. 78).
- **Custodianship.** For the succession see the madho-lal-hussain bullet. The author holds the Shah Jhangi[OCR?] site by gift with "بند[OCR?] و قبالہ" (p. 83).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Hussain catches the flying Shaikh Arzani out of the sky, then vanishes to "ایک کنگرہ عرش" where Arzani cannot find him (pp. 73–74). The book attributes this to Hussain.
- Hussain speaks "از تہِ خاکِ گور", from inside the grave, to a man who calls him a weaver (verse, p. 74).
- Hussain, after his death, appears in a dream ([illegible] recipient) and has Arzani sent to Thatta by royal farman (pp. 75–76).
- Akbar's vazir is sent to punish Hussain for un-shar'i conduct. Hussain holds his own beard, the intoxicants are not found ("از قسم شراب[OCR?] وغیرہ سکرات موجود نہیں"), and the vazir becomes "مرید با اخلاص" (p. 81). The source named is "واردات [illegible]".
- A mulla who comes to punish Hussain opens his eyes to find himself in the city of [illegible]. He lives there a long time. Then a dancing "نقشبند[OCR?]" faqir in the bazaar turns out to be Hussain, who brings him back to the mosque (p. 81). The source named is "[illegible] شطحیات".

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author answers why Hussain abandoned two pillars of Islam ("روزہ و نماز"), and says that hostility came from Qazi Khan[OCR?]'s envy (p. 81). The text is heavily damaged.
- **The author's sources.** A Persian verse work by "حضرت سیّد محمد[OCR?]". He also names a book he had wanted to see for years, which he could finally study after becoming a munshi in a "سرکار عالی دفتر" (p. 82).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** The chunk has very heavy `[illegible]` and `[OCR?]` damage on every page. The Persian verse (pp. 73–75) and pp. 79–81 are worst. Every quoted word above keeps its flag. Folio markers appear at pp. 73, 74, 75 and 77 and none at p. 76. No folio is cited, per the brief.
- **Hussain's death date** "سن ایک ہزار آٹھ[OCR?]", the month "جمادی الاخری[OCR?]", Madho's conversion "سن ایک ہزار ایک[OCR?]" and his age "تہتر[OCR?]" / "۷۳[OCR?]" are all OCR-flagged (p. 83). Madho's death year is illegible (p. 83). Arzani's death "ایک ہزار پندرہ[OCR?]" is flagged (p. 76). None has been converted.
- **Disciple categories are inconsistent.** P. 82 gives "با اسمِ عرس" and "شاہ عرس[OCR?]" for the first, then "شاہ عرب[OCR?]" for the rest. The third group is "خانگی[OCR?]" at p. 82 but "خاکیوں" at p. 83. Which tomb belongs to the second Diwan is ambiguous across the p. 82/83 page break: p. 82 puts it inside the enclosure, while p. 83 opens with "دیوان بخشو[OCR?] لعل[OCR?]" at Bijapur[OCR?].
- **The p. 84 boy's name** is printed "میر احمد[OCR?]" and then "لور احمد[OCR?]". Who made the prophecy is illegible. The succession order after Wilayat Shah[OCR?] is partly illegible, and "خاند شاہ[OCR?]" / "عظمت شاہ[OCR?]" are flagged.
- **data-darbar placement.** The turban and cowrie passage (p. 78) names the urs of "علی گنج بخش ہجویری", but the "دستارِ بسنتی" and the following Basant digression point to Hussain's shrine. It is unclear whether "حسن[OCR?] علی شاہ[OCR?] سجادہ نشین" is the Data Darbar's or Hussain's sajjada nashin. A human should check the PDF before this goes to either entry.
- **The Sikh Maharaja is never named** in pp. 78–80, so nothing is mapped to **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**. Do not identify him from general knowledge.
- **Madho.** The archive row's figure is "Shah Hussain (Madho Lal Hussain)". The book treats Madho as Hussain's successor and a separate person, so Madho's facts are kept under the same row as shrine history. A consolidator should keep the two men apart.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** Lahore appears only as the location of named sites, the "ناظمِ لاہور", "دروازہ / لاہور" in the crowd description, and the railway station. None of this is material about the city itself, so ruling (a) was not applied.
- **Province bullet**: province-level, weigh accordingly. The p. 84 instance concerns a boy in "ضلع امرتسر[OCR?]", which is outside Pakistan, so it is recorded only as wording. The p. 80 instance is badly damaged.
- **Thatta, Bijapur, Delhi, Wazirabad[OCR?], Shahdara[OCR?] and Rasulpur** have no matching archive rows.
