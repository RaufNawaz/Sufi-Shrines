# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 072 — pp. 783–794

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 783) inside the account of the Shalamar garden east of Lahore (its heading is in the previous chunk). It covers the name "شالامار" and its Kashmir model, a chronogram, the seven component gardens and their buildings, gates and towers (pp. 783–785), then "حال وجہ تعمیر باغ ہذا", the dream-and-construction story, the garden's decline under Lehna Singh and its restoration by Ranjit Singh (pp. 785–787). Next come "مکان نگاہ خانہ" (p. 787) and the land statement "شرح زمین محدودہ شہلا باغ" (p. 788). Then "مکان تکیہ مائی بہاگی شاہ" (pp. 788–791) gives the life of the majzuba Mai Bhagi, her relations with Ranjit Singh's court, her takia, graves and trees. The last section, untitled in the chunk and beginning "چندرات" (pp. 791–794), describes the Hindu place of worship Chandrat near Ichhra, its jogi custodians and a Suryavanshi genealogy down to Rama and Lachhman, and the Sita–Lachhman legend that explains the site. The chunk breaks off mid-sentence in the Chandrat succession (p. 794). Folio markers are printed but are not cited here (brief rule).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- Shalamar / "شہلا باغ", Lahore: the name comes from a Kashmir story. A jackal was caught by a hunting dog, and in Kashmiri "گیدڑ کو شالا کہتی ہین" and "مار" is a kind of hunting dog. The source given is the "سفرنامہ" of "لالہ امین چند صاحب" (p. 783). It lies "لاہور سے شرق رویہ بفاصلہ چار میل انگریزی". Shah Jahan built it on the Kashmir plan in "سنہ [illegible] ہجری" (p. 783). The chronogram ends "گفتا کہ بگو نمونہ خلد برین", date "سنہ ۱۰ [illegible]" (p. 783).
- Its seven gardens as printed:
  - Angoori (held by Pandit Brij Nath).
  - Inayatabad (held by "سرداران سندہانوالیہ[OCR?]").
  - Inside "شالا باغ" there are three: Faiz Bakhsh, Hayat Bakhsh (with tank, baradaris, Sawan-Bhadon) and Farah Bakhsh ("پائین باغ").
  - Mahtabi Bagh, now separate and "غیر آباد مقبوضہ سرکار", whose walls were auctioned "بوقت سرکار انگریزی" to "میلا رام".
  - The seventh, Gulabi Bagh, now gone: "اب اسکا نام و نشان نہین".
  - Pages: pp. 783–785.
- Shalamar fittings: a marble takht kept as "یادگار شاہجہان", a marble waterfall called "مرغان[OCR?] بیجالی[OCR?] چادر", Shah Jahan's hammam with hot and cold sections, and the Sawan-Bhadon, named because its falling water sounds like rain, with lamp niches behind the water (pp. 783–784). Gates on Hayat Bakhsh had elephant-sized doorways with kashi work (p. 784). Major Magar[OCR?], Deputy Commissioner, cut a new large door in the south wall (p. 785).
- Zeb-un-Nisa, "دختر عالمگیر": she used to come to watch the waterfall and composed a ruba'i on it, which is quoted (p. 784).
- Origin story ("بعضون کا مقولہ قرین قیاس ہے"): Shah Jahan, going to visit Jahangir's tomb, spent the night at "شاہدرہ باغ دلکشا" and dreamt of a garden called "ارم". "نواب علی مردان خان" brought a canal from the Ravi "علاقہ شاہ پور[OCR?]". "نواب فاضل خان" was "میر عمارت", and a mason "حالی[OCR?]" drew the plan. Hali's grave is west of the graveyard of "مہر گنگا", with a cross-reference to the book's section on Baghbanpura. Mehr Ganga, owner of the land, was made gardener (pp. 785–786).
- Decline: the garden flourished until "عہد محمد شاہی" and then fell into ruin in the "بادشاہ گردی". "حافظ محمد عظیم اللہ", a gardener descended from Mehr Ganga, hid a jasper tank. "سعید[OCR?]", "فی الاصل شیعی[OCR?]", informed Lehna Singh, who had the tank and all the stone fountains torn out and sold. The author condemns this ("کیسے بیحوصلہ گرسنہ چشم حاکم تہی") (p. 786).
- Nigah Khana ("اب تمام لوگ اسکو نگار خانہ کہتے ہین"), east of Shalamar: troops entered by one gate and left by the other while the king reviewed them from a baradari. "سر ہنری[OCR?] کرنیل لارنس صاحب" rebuilt the fallen baradari. An alternative explanation is a grave-shaped structure placed there "تاکہ موت یاد رہی" (p. 787). Present entrance on "سڑک امرتسر"; stable, shops and a marble khwabgah (pp. 787–788). A well called "دریائی[OCR?] کہوہی[OCR?]" after a mango tree "دریائی[OCR?]" (p. 788).
- Takia of Mai Bhagi Shah: east of Shalamar, "متعلقہ موضع میران دی کہوہی" (p. 788).
  - Her origin and early life: "قوم راجپوت مناس[OCR?]", of "موضع سنگو تار[OCR?] علاقہ بٹالہ". After the famine she travelled with mahants "سندوک[OCR?] داس و آتما رام و پریتم داس" to Amritsar, and her mother "جیبان[OCR?]" died on the way. At twelve she came to Lahore, inside the Delhi Gate (p. 789).
  - Her life in Lahore: she was a courtesan, first with "جانی[OCR?] نمدمال[OCR?]" and later with "راجو[OCR?] بہل[OCR?] کہاری". She ran liquor shops inside Delhi Gate and Shah Alami Gate, and the book lists daughters and a son "نور محمد" (p. 789).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Shalamar land statement "شرح زمین محدودہ شہلا باغ": bigha figures for each garden, all [illegible] (p. 788).
- Mai Bhagi's devotion and gifts: she struck seekers with bricks ("اسیٹین[OCR?] مار کر"), and devotees took being chased by her as good fortune (p. 790). Crowds of "لاکہوں لوگ" came day and night, and nazr included the Maharaja's 500 rupees and the 10,000-rupee building (p. 790). Earlier she spent "دو سو روپیہ" of her own to level the takia land and revived an old well (p. 788).
- Takia land and lawsuit: "تین[OCR?] بگہہ مزروعہ" was released ("واگذار") from the sarkar. A claim by "سادات شمسی" to the place "باجلاس مسٹر اجرٹن[OCR?] صاحب بہادر" was dismissed (p. 791). Tree table: "شرینہہ[OCR?] ۱۵[OCR?]", "کیکر ۱۲[OCR?]", other counts [illegible] (p. 791).
- Chandrat observances: a mela at night "ہر غرہ ماہ"; Hindu men and women from Lahore and nearby villages come to "ماتہا ٹیکنے" and offer "چڑہاوی[OCR?]"; a lamp burns all night in the niche (pp. 791–792).
- Chandrat land: "ساٹہہ[OCR?] بیگہ" attached, "تیس[OCR?] بیگہہ بصیغہ انعام بنام مقام", the rest revenue-free for Booti Nath's lifetime (p. 792).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Shah Jahan saw the Shalamar garden in a dream at Shahdara, as reported by "بعضون کا مقولہ" (pp. 785–786).
- Mai Bhagi, "مجذوبہ صاحب کمال": the book attributes karamat and "خوارق" to her. Ranjit Singh's partial recovery followed her eleven-day stay (pp. 789–790).
- Lachhman rested at Chandrat after leaving Sita, and Sita came to Valmiki's place in Lahore, "حسب عقیدہ اہل ہنود" (p. 794).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author judges the dream origin of Shalamar "قرین قیاس" over the Kashmir-copy explanation (p. 785). He prefers the "remember death" derivation of "نگاہ خانہ" over the troop-review one, citing the similar building he saw at Lahore Fort (p. 787).
- He blames Lehna Singh's plundering for Ranjit Singh's coming to Lahore and praises Ranjit Singh's restoration ("آفرین باد برین ہمت مردانہ") (pp. 786–787).
- He calls Ranjit Singh's removal of the marble "سنگدلی" (p. 783).
