# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 057 — pp. 636–645

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 636) on the end of a tomb account whose heading lies in the previous chunk. The book says that after much searching the tomb was found to be that of "حضرت شاہ پیر [illegible]", a Gilani sayyid, and gives his chain back to Ghaus al-Azam. Then come five titled sections. The first is "احوال باغ زیب النسا کہ فی زمانہا موضع نواں کوٹ مشہور ہے" (p. 636). It covers the garden's founding story, Zeb-un-Nisa's marble tomb and its stripping under Ranjit Singh, and the surviving gateway, baradari and burjis (pp. 636–639). The second is "حال خانقاہ حاجی عبد الکریم صاحب چشتی" (p. 639). It covers the enclosure, the graves, the sajjada nashin, the urs, the wells and the karamat (pp. 639–641). After it come the Takia al-Shahid and "حال شاہ رستم غازی", with the tomb of his murshid Abd al-Khaliq (p. 641). Next is the history of the village of Nawan Kot and its Mehr landholding family, Nawan Kot Khurd and the 1861[OCR?] census (pp. 641–643). Then "حال زیب النسا بیگم" covers her pen name, her verses and anecdotes with Aqil Khan (pp. 643–645). The chunk ends just after the heading "احوال مہر حکم الدین[OCR?]" (p. 645). Only one archive row is touched: Maharaja Ranjit Singh, by the person rule. No Lahore-in-general material appears.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** (مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ; "حضوری باغ"):
  - "جب عملداری مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ ہوئی", he had Zeb-un-Nisa's marble tomb taken down ("تمام اس مقبرہ کو اتروا کر") and a baradari built with it in a garden. "چنانچہ اب تک وہ بارہ دری حضوری باغ میں یادگار[OCR?] [illegible] مہاراج صاحب [illegible] موجود ہے" (so that baradari still stands in the Hazuri Bagh as a memorial[OCR?] of the Maharaja) (p. 637).
  - Inside the tomb, the floor of white marble and black stone in inlay ("بطور گلکاری") now survives only on the west and one other side. "بقیہ رنجیت سنگھ اوکھاڑ کر لے گیا" (Ranjit Singh tore up and took away the rest) (p. 637).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Opening tomb (p. 636), section heading in the previous chunk**:
  - A dome once "پیلو[OCR?] رنگ", now "لو[OCR?] رنگ". There is a platform to its east (p. 636).
  - Local tradition is that at the time of the city's settlement "یہاں محلہ [illegible] تھا" (p. 636).
  - "بہت تلاش سے معلوم ہوا کہ یہ مقبرہ حضرت شاہ پیر [illegible] کا ہے", described as "ولی کامل سید گیلانی صحیح النسب". One "عبد السعادوم[OCR?]" is buried in the same tomb (p. 636).
  - His murshid was "حضرت شاہ عالم المشہور[OCR?] [illegible]". The chain then runs through "حضرت شاہ جمال[OCR?]", Shaikh Ahmad, Shaikh Hamid Gilani, Sayyid Abd al-Razzaq and others to "حضرت غوث الاعظم محی الدین گیلانی" (p. 636).
  - Among his sajjada nashins, "حضرت سید عبد الحکیم گیلانی[OCR?]" became a wali kamil. The book says his account appears elsewhere in the book (p. 636).
- **Zeb-un-Nisa's garden, now the village of Nawan Kot** (باغ زیب النسا / موضع نواں کوٹ):
  - The village adjoins the city of Lahore, "دو کوس[OCR?]" off (p. 636).
  - Founding story, damaged. A garden known as "چوہڑی[OCR?]" was being built by a "مائی[OCR?] دایہ". Zeb-un-Nisa resolved to give a garden to whoever first met her with a prayer for her welfare, and she gave it accordingly (pp. 636–637).
  - The gateway of that garden is "چوبرجی[OCR?]". After that, the present garden, in which Nawan Kot now stands, was built, with a marble tomb in it (p. 637).
- **Zeb-un-Nisa's tomb** (مقبرہ زیب النسا):
  - Square and four-sided, with three openings on each side. It formerly had twelve doorways, four arched and eight square, with marble lattices (p. 637).
  - Now partly bricked up, with a path cut through by someone living wall-to-wall with the tomb (p. 637).
  - Inside, a mud platform holds a grave covered with a green ghilaf. Its size is "تیرہ[OCR?] گز طول" (p. 637).
  - The dome's stones were torn out (p. 637). The corners have marble columns (p. 637).
- **The garden's surviving buildings**:
  - A large gateway, now blocked on the outside, with a roofed deorhi[OCR?], sitting rooms, cells and shah-nishins (p. 638).
  - Two of the original four corner burjis survive. They are octagonal with eight doorways, reached by "بارہ زینہ", and have "برنگ[OCR?] بسنتی" domes. The southern one keeps its colour and the northern one is decayed (pp. 638–639).
  - A room at the north-west corner is linked to "شاہ قریشی" (p. 638).
- **Munshi Umar al-Din** ("عمر الدین صاحب", "قوم ارائیں[OCR?]"):
  - He lives in a house on the north side of the garden. The passage is badly damaged (p. 638).
  - He took service at the start of "عملداری سرکار [illegible]" and rose (p. 638).
  - He built mosques, "سات مسجدیں" (seven mosques) as read (p. 638).
- **Haji Abd al-Karim Chishti** (حاجی عبد الکریم صاحب چشتی):
  - Family: he is said to be among the descendants of "حضرت عبد الصمد[OCR?] صاحب" (p. 640).
  - His sons, as named on the graves: "دیوان یحیی" (eldest), "آلہ نور[OCR?]" (middle) and "عبد الحق" (youngest) (p. 639).
  - His son is named "شیخ یحیی لاہوری" and described as "صاحب کمال" (p. 640).
  - Death: "وفات اُنکی دوم ذی قعدہ سن ۱۱۳۲[OCR?] ہے" (p. 641). Whose death this is, is ambiguous; see Doubts.
- **Takia al-Shahid** ("تکیہ الشہید[OCR?]"):
  - It stands outside Nawan Kot, in the south-west corner (p. 641).
  - It has a dalan, a brick cell with a mosque, and graves of "شاہ دلبو[OCR?]" and "شاہ سید[OCR?]" (p. 641).
  - The graves were made pakka by "سید فضل شاہ" (p. 641).
  - "گامون شاہ[OCR?] مالگذار سرکار" lives there (p. 641).
- **Shah Rustam Ghazi** (حضرت شاہ رستم غازی):
  - His place is on a high mound west of Nawan Kot (p. 641).
  - Informants are "امام بخش نمبردار و کمال شاہ پٹواری" (p. 641).
  - Dating: "زیب النسا سنہ ایک ہزار چوبیس[OCR?]", read in a garbled clause about his time. Zeb-un-Nisa had his tomb built of red stone (p. 641).
  - Two basement chambers. In the first are the graves of Rustam Ghazi and of a son "نامعلوم الاسم". In the second are the graves of one of his "قبیلہ" and of his mother (p. 641).
  - Imam Bakhsh, "معمر سفید ریش", says there were two red-stone domes and that their stone was taken away (p. 641).
  - "جانے شاہ قرشی[OCR?]" now lights the lamp and sweeps (p. 641).
- **Abd al-Khaliq** (حضرت عبدالخالق), "مرشد حضرت رستم غازی":
  - His brick grave is in "باغیچہ حضرت عبدالخالق" on the mound, on a platform whose khasra number is "۱۵۵۹[OCR?]" (p. 641).
  - "تاریخ وفات معلوم نہیں" (p. 641).
- **Sayyid Fazl Shah** (سید فضل شاہ): described as "دفتری فنا نشلی[OCR?]". He fitted the woodwork of the deorhi. The book says he composed "پنجابی زبان میں شعر عاشقانہ" and an "آٹھ ماہے[OCR?]" (p. 641).
- **Nawan Kot village history** (from "میاں غلام فرید ولد میر ماماں[OCR?] نمبردار و امام بخش نمبردار"):

  - Mehr Muhkam al-Din of "موضع کھیل[OCR?] متعلقہ تحصیل چوہرہ[OCR?]", with his brothers Mehr Shah Di and Abd al-Rahim, came to Lahore. At that time "سوہا سکہ[OCR?]" was among the "حاکمان لاہور" (p. 642).
  - "سنہ اٹھارہ سو": "سوہا سکہ[OCR?]" gave "پانچ سو روپیہ مدد خرچ" to settle the village (p. 642).
  - The three brothers are "ولدان مہر عظمت [illegible]" (p. 642).
  - A long genealogy of the brothers' descendants follows. It is heavily flagged (pp. 642–643).
- **Nawan Kot Khurd**: about twenty years ago, the numbardars Imam Bakhsh and "ہامان[OCR?]" settled a second village on their own land and named it "نوان کوٹ خورد". It is now owned by Mehr Imam Bakhsh and Ghulam Farid (p. 643).
- **Takia "تلیاں والا"**: north of the great northern gate. "الہی بخش فقیر" lives there (p. 643).
- **Shahdara**: in exchange for this land, Zeb-un-Nisa gave the landholder land "شاہدرہ کے متصل", where he settled a "پتیالہ[OCR?]" that still exists (p. 643).
- **Zeb-un-Nisa Begum** (زیب النسا بیگم):

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Haji Abd al-Karim's khanqah — enclosure and fittings**:
  - Mud and brick enclosure, "تخمیناً پانچ کنال", with a west gate (p. 639).
  - A dalan with two cells (p. 639).
  - Two large lemon trees "حضرت عبد الکریم صاحب نے اپنے ہاتھ سے لگائے" (p. 639).
  - An octagonal brick platform holds four graves: the saint and his three sons (p. 639).
  - A second platform holds four graves: a "فرزند خواجہ عبد الکریم", "عظیم شاہ خادم اعلی حضور", "غلام شاہ سجادہ نشین" and Ghulam Shah's son (p. 639).
  - Lemon, kikar, orange (سنترہ) and peach (آڑو) trees grow there (p. 639).
- **Haji Abd al-Karim's khanqah — custodianship and urs**:
  - Sajjada nashin: "نور الدین مالک مکان". Living with him are "رجب بخش[OCR?] اللہ شاہ" and "مائی مبارک بی بی دختر غلام شاہ" (p. 639).
  - Urs: "بیست و ہفتم[OCR?] ماہ رجب یا ماہ رجب بشب معراج شریف" (p. 639).
- **Haji Abd al-Karim's khanqah — wells and revenue**:
  - A well with land is "سرکار سے تا قیام خانقاہ معاف" (revenue-free from the sarkar for as long as the khanqah stands) (p. 639).
  - A second well has been out of use for three months. While a new trough was being dug, another well was found (p. 639).
  - Something, possibly a well or spring, was built by "دیوان[OCR?] یحیی", murid of Haji Abd al-Karim (p. 640).
- **Nawan Kot village — houses and shops**:
  - Houses: "کل مکانات مسکونہ پانسو دو حبس[OCR?] میں دو سو ستانوں[OCR?] گھر آباد ہیں" (p. 643).
  - Shops: "سا تہہ[OCR?] دوکانیں", and six are derelict (p. 643).
- **Nawan Kot village — census**:
  - "بوقت مردم شماری مرقومہ یارہ ہوئیں دسمبر سنہ ۱۸۶۱[OCR?] اٹھارہ سو سا ٹھہ عیسوی" (p. 643).
  - Residents: "چار سو ستر"; men "دو سو اونیس"; women "ایک سو بیتالیس" (p. 643).
- **Nawan Kot village — residents and wells**:
  - Residents include Arains, labourers, "مہتم[OCR?] و تیلی و چوکیداران" and weavers (جولاہہ گری) (p. 643).
  - Wells: three attached to the village. There are also "چاہ آب نوشی بارہ" (twelve drinking wells), large and small (p. 643).
- **Nawan Kot village — customs**: whoever builds a new house in the village pays "ایک دو ر[OCR?] روپیہ بطور نذر" to the owning numbardars (p. 643).
- **Zeb-un-Nisa's land acquisition**: the land belonged to "دو سو[OCR?] زمیندار". He refused to sell, so she gave him land near Shahdara in exchange (p. 643).
- **Ranjit Singh period**: the tomb's marble was removed and its floor stripped. See the archive bullet (p. 637).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Haji Abd al-Karim**:
  - On the way to Hajj he was stranded without water in a desert. He prayed, and a spring of water appeared. He declared something about whoever becomes his murid. The text is heavily damaged (p. 640).
  - A man on the day of Hajj regretted not being in Mecca. The saint told him to do wuzu and close his eyes. He found himself in Mecca, performed the Hajj and was brought back the same way (p. 640).
- **Shaikh Yahya Lahori, or the khanqah (attribution unclear)**: a thief who stole there went blind whenever he went outside and saw again whenever he came back in. In the morning he confessed. Something was put on his eyes and "وہ ولی کامل ہو گیا" (pp. 640–641).
- **Abd al-Khaliq**: "سب لوگ بیان کرتے ہیں کہ یہاں رات کو خود بخود دیہ[OCR?] آتش روشن ہو جاتا ہے" (all people say that a lamp[OCR?] lights itself here at night) (p. 641).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is heavy throughout: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags on every page. Pp. 642–643 (the Mehr genealogy) and pp. 644–645 (the Persian verses and the Aqil Khan anecdotes) are largely garbled. The verses are not glossed. Every quotation keeps its flags. Folio markers (635–638, 439[OCR?]–442[OCR?]) are printed but not cited, per the brief. Their jump from 638 to 439 is noted only.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** is mapped by the person rule on Ranjit Singh's acts (p. 637). The chunk says nothing about the samadhi itself. The "حضوری باغ" baradari is named as the destination of the marble. Weigh accordingly.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** Lahore appears only as a reference point ("شہر لاہور سے لگا ہوا", p. 636), in the settlement-era mohalla phrase at the opening tomb (p. 636), and as "حاکمان لاہور" (p. 642). None of this is material on the city as such. So the 35 `lahore` ids are not attached.
- **"قلعہ لاہور" (p. 638)** occurs in a garbled clause about Umar al-Din ("سیر[OCR?] قلعہ لاہور"). It is not mapped to the `lahore fort` rows.
- **"حضرت شاہ جمال[OCR?]" (p. 636)** is a flagged name inside a Qadiri-Gilani chain. The chunk does not identify him with the Shah Jamal of Ichhra, so **shah-jamal** is not mapped. This is recorded for the consolidator to weigh against the person rule.
- **The opening tomb's saint** is "حضرت شاہ پیر [illegible]", also written "شاہ پیر ور[OCR?]". His name is incomplete, and his death place or date is "[illegible]" (p. 636).
- **Whose death is "دوم ذی قعدہ سن ۱۱۳۲[OCR?]" (p. 641)?** It follows directly on the thief story, which itself follows a mention of "شیخ یحیی لاہوری". It may refer to the thief, to Shaikh Yahya or to Haji Abd al-Karim; the chunk does not settle it.
- **Census, p. 643**:
  - "۱۸۶۱[OCR?]" in digits against "اٹھارہ سو سا ٹھہ" ("eighteen hundred sixty") in words.
  - Total "چار سو ستر" against men "دو سو اونیس" and women "ایک سو بیتالیس", which do not add up to the total as printed.
  - All of these are flagged or uncertain numerals. Do not compute.
- **Other flagged numerals**: "سنہ ایک ہزار چوبیس[OCR?]" (p. 641), "سنہ اٹھارہ سو" (p. 642), "تیرہ[OCR?] گز" (p. 637), "۱۵۵۹[OCR?]" (p. 641) and the house count "پانسو دو" with "دو سو ستانوں[OCR?]" (p. 643). All are carried as printed.
- **"سوہا سکہ[OCR?]" / "سو بہا نگہ[OCR?]" (pp. 642, 645)** is a garbled name of one of the "حاکمان لاہور". It is not identified.
- **The Aqil Khan name** varies: "عاقل خان", "عامل جاں[OCR?]", "یا قفل خان[OCR?]" and "عاقل نہاں[OCR?]" (pp. 644–645). It is carried as printed.
- **Zeb-un-Nisa's father** is given only as "حضرت عالیجاہ[OCR?] بادشاہ" (p. 643). No name is given.
- **Two darbars on the site but not in the index** (Shah Gohar Peer; Mian Qurban Ali Shah): neither is named in this chunk. The held identities (shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh, langer-makhdoom) do not occur.
