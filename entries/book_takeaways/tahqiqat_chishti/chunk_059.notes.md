# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 059 — pp. 655–663

The chunk opens mid-section (pp. 655–659), and **the section's heading is in the previous chunk (058)**. This section is about the "مکان جہنگر شاہ" (the makan of Jhangar Shah) outside "مستی دروازہ" and the faqirs called "شہری[OCR?] شاہی". It begins with a heavily damaged origin story of a guru ("گورو مہررائو[OCR?]") and his disciple, who is named in several forms ("حیدرا مل", "حیندرامل[OCR?]", "تپدرامل[OCR?]"). It then gives the chain of chelas, Jhangar Shah's audience with Nawab Khan Bahadur, the stipend paid in Ranjit Singh's time, a description of the samadh building and the sect's practices. Six titled sections follow: "حال مکان علی رنگریز" (p. 659), "حال مقبرہ محمد صالح و شیخ عنایت اللہ رحمہ" (p. 660), "حال [illegible] شاہ کمال رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" (p. 661), "احوال شوالہ بیرون موری دروازہ" (p. 661), "احوال مکان شیر علی [illegible] باہر[OCR?] دروازہ تکسالی[OCR?]" (p. 662) and "احوال[OCR?] شیخ[OCR?] علی شاہ" (pp. 662–663). The chunk breaks off mid-sentence in the succession dispute after Rahim Shah. Damage is extreme on pp. 656–658 and 660, where much of the text is unreadable.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Jhangar Shah's makan — stipend**: one rupee a day "بیطور وہرم[OCR?] ارنہہ[OCR?]" and one paisa a day per house[OCR?] or shop in Ranjit Singh's time. One damaged passage reads "اب تمیسر[OCR?] ہو دن فقط شہر ہی" (now … only from the city) (p. 657).
- **Jhangar Shah's makan — buildings**:
  - A platform "طول سات قدم اور حرس[OCR?] پانچ قدم" (seven paces long and five[?] wide) with a tree on it (p. 658).
  - Pictures in the walls, including one of a figure "شمشیر بر سوار" (riding, sword in hand) on one side and "کشن راس مندل" to the south (pp. 658–659).
- **The samadh of Jhangar Shah — dome**:
  - The domed samadh is on the west side, with a circuit for tawaf, "مقام طواف جیکو ہندوہ پر و کہنا[OCR?] کہتی" (a place of circumambulation, which Hindus call parikrama[OCR?]) (p. 659).
  - The dome is "اندر سی ہشت پہلو اور با ہر سے مربع" (octagonal inside, square outside), with eight arches and pictures of deities ("تصویریں دیو تالو نگی[OCR?]") (p. 659).
  - The floor is of red stone ("فرش سنگ سرخ") (p. 659).
- **The samadh of Jhangar Shah — inner dome and history**:
  - Inside is a small inner dome of marble whose square base is "ڈیڑہ گز" (one and a half gaz) on each side. It has four arched openings and a circuit between the small and large domes (p. 659).
  - In Nawab Khan Bahadur's time the samadh was only "چبورہ[OCR?] ترہ[OCR?] خشتی" (a brick platform) (p. 659).
- **Worship and begging customs of the sect**:
  - On Sunday they wash their two "ڈونڈ[OCR?]" with water and eat sweet food (p. 659).
  - A conch is sounded twice daily ("دو وقتہ شنکہ[OCR?] سجیا[OCR?]"), and a lamp of "روغن زرد" (ghee) was kept burning on the samadh (p. 659).
  - Begging: they strike their two sticks together ("دو ڈنڈے" also appears on p. 657), call out phrases and couplets, and some recite "مناقب حضرات اولیا اللہ" (praises of the awliya) (pp. 657, 659).
  - Many blacken their faces ("منہ اپنا سیاہی سے کالا کر لیتا ہے"). The gaddi nashin wears a "کفنی" (shroud-cloth) round his neck (p. 659).
  - They describe themselves as "سند[OCR?] و سلمان میں مشترک او صلح کل" (shared between Hindu[OCR?] and Muslim[OCR?], and at peace with all) (p. 659).
- **Ali Rangrez**: an urs is held once a year, and the dyers of Lahore are attached to the khanqah (pp. 659–660).
- **Shah Kamal's tomb**: care by sweeping and lamp-lighting by Nihal Shah (p. 661). The building's state is described under the archive bullet above.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The guru's disciple (Haidra Mal / Hindra Mal)**:
  - He carried two small bundles ("دو پوتلیاں") filled with sugar and with earth past the doorkeepers, promising them shares of his reward, and bowed to the bundles before the guru (p. 655).
  - The earth bundle turned to sugar and the pebble bundle to rupees: "مٹی والی پوتلی شکر تر ہی اور کنکر من کی پوتلی روپیہ نگلکو[OCR?]" (p. 655).
  - Given halwa to give "جو سب سے بڑا پیارا ہو", he ate it himself, because no one dearer was in his sight. The guru said "واہ واہ" (pp. 655–656).
- **A test with a sparrow** ("کنجشک"): he was asked whether a sparrow held in the hand was alive or dead (p. 657).
- **Bullet story at Jhangar Shah's makan**: a damaged account of a tree and a well, and of a ball fired from "ملعہ[OCR?]" (the fort[?]). The outcome cannot be read (p. 658).
