# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 076 — pp. 829–840

This chunk is a run of Mughal dynastic history. It contains no shrine survey material. It opens mid-narrative (p. 829) in Jahangir's reign: Mahabat Khan's victory, Nur Jahan's flight toward Lahore, Mahabat Khan's fall and his alliance with Asaf Khan to enthrone Shah Jahan. It continues with Jahangir's death after returning from Kashmir to Lahore, a Persian chronogram, and his burial at Shahdara (pp. 829–830). The next titled section, "ذکر سلطنت شاہجہان بادشاہ غازی" (pp. 830–836), covers Shah Jahan's accession, the revolt of "لودی", his buildings (Shahjahanabad, the red-stone fort, the Jama Masjid, the Taj Mahal, the Shalimar garden at Lahore) and the war of succession among his four sons. The last section, "سلطنت اورنگ زیب بادشاہ" (pp. 836–840), runs through Dara Shikoh's flight via Lahore, Sind and Thatta[OCR?], his capture and killing, Shuja's defeat, Muhammad Sultan's imprisonment, Shah Jahan's death in captivity, and the deaths of Sulaiman Shikoh and Murad. It breaks off mid-sentence at p. 840 on an embassy from "شاہ عباس بادشاہ ایران". Folio markers are printed but are not cited here (brief rule).

  - Jahangir, ill on his way back from Kashmir, came to Lahore and died there ("جب وہان سے واپس ہوکر لاہور مین آئے تو ... خیمہ زن ملک عدم ہوگئے"). The date as printed is "تاریخ [illegible] ماہ نومبر سنہ ۱۶۲[illegible]ء [OCR?] مطابق سنہ [illegible]۳۶ [OCR?] ہجری" (p. 830).
  - Jahangir was buried "شہر [OCR?] لاہور کے شمال رویہ لب دریای راوی موضع شاہدرہ مین" (north of the city of Lahore, on the bank of the Ravi, in the village of Shahdara) (p. 830).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- Jahangir ("شہنشاہ جہان شاہ جہانگیر", "نورالدین محمد"): death chronogram attributed to "کشفی", ending "جہانگیر از جہان رفت" (p. 830). He is called "نیک مزاج اور رحم دل متلون المزاج" (p. 830). His tomb at Shahdara, Lahore, is said to be described separately in this book: "حال مقبرہ مرقد حضرت مرحوم کا علیحدہ درج کتاب ہذا ہے" (pp. 830, 833).
- Mr Thomas ("مسٹر ٹامس" / "طامس صاحب"), envoy of "جیمس اول شاہ انگلنڈ", attended the court and got leave for a "کوٹھی" at Surat (p. 830).
- Nur Jahan, Mahabat Khan, Asaf Khan: their struggle, Mahabat Khan's flight and his meeting with Asaf Khan (p. 829). Asaf Khan is called Nur Jahan's real brother (p. 830).
- "لودی", who claimed Afghan descent, revolted against Shah Jahan. His women killed themselves in his house, he escaped across the flooded Chambal, and he was killed by a bullet in Malwa with "چوتیس [OCR?] ۳۴ [OCR?]" men left (pp. 830–832).
- Shah Jahan's buildings: he named Delhi "شاہجہان آباد", built a city wall, a red-stone fort and the Jama Masjid. "پادری ہیبر [OCR?] صاحب" is quoted praising the fort (p. 832). The Taj Mahal at Agra is described as "سنگ مرمر کا ایکسو نوی گز مربع" with a dome diameter of "۳۳ [OCR?] گز", built at a cost of "پچہتر لاکھ روپیہ" (p. 833).
- Shalimar Bagh, Lahore: its full description, together with that of Jahangir's tomb, is said to be "علیحدہ درج کتاب ہذا" (p. 833).
- Shah Jahan's sons (p. 833): Dara Shikoh ("علم دوست اور فقیر کا طالب", but hasty), Shuja (Bengal), Murad (Gujarat), and Aurangzeb Alamgir ("محدث و فقیہ ... پابند شرع").
- War of succession: the Narmada crossing and defeat of Jaswant Singh (p. 834); the battle near Agra, where Dara dismounted from his elephant and lost (p. 835); Shah Jahan's confinement in the fort (p. 836); Murad seized at a feast (p. 836).
- Battle against Shuja near Allahabad: Mir Jumla told Aurangzeb not to get down from his elephant, while Shuja did get down and lost (p. 837).
- Dara's last campaign near Ajmer, the false letter, his flight to Gujarat and then to "ٹہٹہ [OCR?]", his wife "نادرہ بانو", his betrayal by "خان [OCR?] [illegible]" (later named "خان جہان"), and his killing (pp. 837–838). The date as printed is "بروز جمعہ یکم ماہ محرم سنہ یکہزار [OCR?] [illegible] [illegible]" (p. 838).
- Muhammad Sultan married Shuja's daughter, was undone by Aurangzeb's planted letter, and was imprisoned in Gwalior fort, where he died after "سات برس" (p. 839). Shuja fled to Arakan and was killed there by its raja (p. 839).
- Shah Jahan lived "آٹہہ برس" in captivity (p. 839). Sulaiman Shikoh was handed over by "راجہ سری نگر"; dates are illegible ("سنہ [illegible: numeral] ... گیارہوین جمادی الاول سنہ [illegible: numeral] ہجری"). He, Sipihr Shikoh and Murad's younger son died at Gwalior (p. 840). Murad tried to escape on a rope, was betrayed by a Hindu woman's cry, and was executed after a blood-claim (p. 840).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Jahangir "فقرای صوفیہ اور مست فقیرون سے بہت خوش اعتقادی کے ساتھ پیش آتا تھا" (treated Sufi faqirs and "mast" faqirs with great devotion) (p. 830).
- Shah Jahan at first punished Hindus severely for idol-worship. Later he came to treat Muslims and Hindus alike ("مسلمان اور ہندؤن کو یکسان سمجھنے لگا"); the passage is partly illegible (p. 832).
