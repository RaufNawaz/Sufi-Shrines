# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 064 — pp. 702–711

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 702), in the tail of an account of "حضرت شاہ گدا" (Shah Gada), whose heading is in the previous chunk. This tail covers Qazi Muhammad Fazl[OCR?]'s visit to him and Mian Mir / "شاہ میر"'s part in it, a Hindu of Lahore who became Muslim and built a mosque and well, and Shah Gada's khadim Lakh[OCR?] Shah. The titled sections then follow in order:
1. "حال شاہ جمال صاحب خادم[OCR?] حضرت شاہ گدا" (p. 703).
2. "احوال مزار حضرت شاہ حسین زنجانی" (p. 705).
3. "ذکر مکان سید علی شاہ المشہور بہ[OCR?] جنگلی[OCR?] چراغ شاہ" (pp. 705–708), with a tree table, Ranjit Singh's clearing of the riverbed, a healing story and a long Persian versified Qadiri shajra.
4. "احوال عیدگاہ بانیہ[OCR?] حضرت نور الدین جہانگیر[OCR?]" (pp. 708–709), covering the Eidgah's architecture and Jahangir's endowment.
5. "ذکر مکان مرگ ننینی[OCR?]" (pp. 709–710), with graves, a mela and a Qadiri shajra.

The chunk ends with a building description (pp. 710–711): a place said to lie between "میانین[OCR?] لاہور" and the garden of a Nawab's mother. Its heading is not printed in this chunk, and its account breaks off mid-sentence at p. 711. Folios are not cited (brief rule).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Shah Gada and the Qazi**: Shah Gada is found half-sunk in earth and water. After the moustache-trimming, the Qazi says Shah Gada's face became luminous, and Shah Gada answers "آپ کا چہرہ بھی نورانی ہو گیا" (p. 702).
- **Shah Gada and Shah Jamal**: Shah Gada prays that Shah Jamal be given "چشم بینا". Shah Jamal is at once seized with remorse and falls at his feet (p. 703).
- **Shah Gada and the purse**: Shah Gada tells the Hindu that the lost purse is in his own dhoti. The Hindu calls him God ("خدا"), and on Shah Gada's word he becomes Muslim on the spot (p. 704).
- **Shah Mir's knowledge**: Shah Mir knows from his own place what has happened to the Qazi (p. 702).
- **Syed Ali Shah and the flood**: it is said ("مشہور ہے") that when the river rose, his house stayed safe. He refused offers (damaged) to be carried away from the flood, saying "خدا ہمارا [illegible] ہی". The river did not reach the place (heavily damaged) (p. 706).
- **Syed Ali Shah's healing**: Qazi Muhammad Bakhsh relates that his parents' children all died young and he fell mortally ill at five or six. His parents took him to Syed Ali Shah, whose prayer healed him. Syed Ali Shah then made him his son and murid (p. 707).

## Doubts

- **Mapping summary**: three ids are mapped (shrine-of-miran-hussain-zanjani-zanjani-sahib, shrine-of-mian-mir, samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh).
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet**: no passage in this chunk treats Lahore in general. "لاہور" appears only as a person's residence (p. 704), as an arrival point (p. 705), in a damaged flood passage (p. 706) and as a location phrase (p. 710). So there is no 35-id toponym bullet.
- **No province-level material.**
- **shrine-of-miran-hussain-zanjani-zanjani-sahib**: the book names him "شاہ حسین زنجانی", while the index has "Syed Miran Hussain Zanjani". I mapped on the name "زنجانی". The location "موضع میران دی کھوئی[OCR?]" is flagged; I do not identify it with Chah Miran. "شاہ حسین" here is not Madho Lal Hussain (madho-lal-hussain). Both year numerals (arrival and death) are illegible.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir**: "میاں میر صاحب" is printed once (p. 702). Thereafter the text says "حضرت شاہ میر" / "شاہ میر صاحب" / "حضرت میر صاحب" (pp. 702–703). The chunk does not state that these are the same man; I bulleted the "شاہ میر" material under Mian Mir only provisionally. The consolidator should weigh this, as the p. 702 passage is heavily damaged.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**: person-level mapping. The book says "راجہ رنجیت سنگھ" and "مہاراجہ صاحب"; nothing about the samadhi.
- **Not mapped, Syed Ali Shah "جنگلی[OCR?] چراغ شاہ"**: the book names him Syed Ali Shah, Gilani, north of the fort in the parade ground. The index's shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh is Syed Abdul Razzaq (Shah Chiragh) on The Mall. The names and places differ, so I did not map him; he is under "Other". This touches the held identity question on shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh (whether "Shah Chiragh" conflates two men), so the consolidator should weigh it. "جنگلی" itself is flagged [OCR?].
- **Not mapped, Shah Jamal of Rohtas**: the book's "شاہ جمال", khadim of Shah Gada, has his mazar at Rohtas (p. 703). This is not the index's shah-jamal (Syed Shah Jamal Uddin Naqvi Bukhari, Lahore). The name matches, but the book places him elsewhere.
- **Not mapped, "خواجہ ایاز[OCR?]"**: he supervised the Eidgah bazaar under Jahangir (p. 709). Per the book's own period this is not Malik Ahmad Ayaz (darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz), so I did not map him. The name is flagged.
- **Shah Gada**: Shah Gada's identity and section heading are in the previous chunk. He is not in the index.
- **Garbled passages**: the Persian verse shajra (pp. 707–708) is very heavily garbled from mid-p. 708 on, and no names past "غوث اعظم … محی الدین" are reliable. The Marg Naini shajra (p. 710) also has many flagged names. The p. 711 building heading is not printed in this chunk.

The worst pages are p. 706 (the Jangli Chiragh Shah buildings, land, tree table and flood legend, mostly illegible) and pp. 708–711 (architectural descriptions where nearly every technical word is flagged). Year numerals are illegible at p. 705 (twice).

- **Possible text-internal inconsistency**: "قاضی افضل" (p. 702) vs "قاضی محمد فضل[OCR?]" (pp. 702–703). It may be the same person, but the chunk does not say so.
