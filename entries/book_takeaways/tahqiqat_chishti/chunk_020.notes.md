# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 020 — pp. 225–235

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 225): its first lines are the tail of an account of a well and takia made blessed by the prayer of a "شاہ" whose name and heading are in the previous chunk (chunk 019); the fragment covers the well's healing reputation and lists the takia's trees. It then runs through three titled sections in order. First, "احوال مسجد قدیمہ [illegible] لاڈو[OCR?]", the old mosque of Mai Lado outside the city wall between the Shah Alami[OCR?] and Mochi[OCR?] gates (pp. 225–228), with its founder, endowment, decline, restoration, urs and the shajra of its present faqir. Second, "احوال مکان محلہ بابا فرید جسکو لوگ فریدانہ کہتی ہیں", the mound in Lahore called Farid Astana (pp. 228–235), which turns into a long life of Baba Farid Ganj Shakar (anecdotes, the origin of the name Ganj Shakar, his death, sayings, his tomb at Pak Pattan and a chronogram) and then returns to the Lahore mound's mela and surroundings. Third, "حال سائیں قطب شاہ صاحب" (p. 235), which begins the life of Sain Qutb Shah and breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk. Folio markers are printed but are not cited here (brief rule).

  - Chishti chain as the book gives it: Farid's pir is Khwaja Qutb-ud-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki[OCR?], whose mazar is in "شاہ جہان آباد[OCR?]", and Qutb-ud-Din's pir is Khwaja Mu'in-ud-Din Chishti (p. 228). ## Practices, institutions, economy

- Mai Lado mosque: Mai Lado started a dars there, with Friday sermons (p. 227). Under Sikh rule ("باعث حکومت سکھا شاہی") a "سادہ مہد و" lived in the mosque; when "علمداری[OCR?] ... انگریزی[OCR?]" came, it was freed from him and the brick enclosure built (p. 226). When the city became desolate the mosque was abandoned "حتیٰ کہ اسپر قبضہ ہندو کا ہو گیا" (p. 227).
- Mai Lado mosque urs: twice a year, in "ربیع [margin: الاولی]" and in "رجب", held by Mehr Shah, with "باج راگ رنگ" and distribution to faqirs (p. 226).
- Farid Astana: "حسب الحکم سرکار وہ ٹیلہ گرایا جانا [illegible]" (p. 228); much of the SW corner has been razed and old graves demolished, with decayed bones visible (p. 229).
- Farid Astana mela: yearly on "پنجم محرم"; barbers attend; murids shave their heads and eat "کہل گوٹر"; stalls sell it; black thread ("پٹرا[OCR?]" in Panjabi) is sold and worn as nazr; on Ashura, Shab-e Barat and every Thursday men and women bring flowers and offer salam (p. 235).
- Pakpattan: crowds at the door said to give safety; "لاکہہ در لاکہہ آدمی" (p. 234).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- The opening well heals whoever bathes there, through the Shah's prayer; "اوس روز سی یہ کرامت آپ کی مشہور ہوئی اور اب تک جاری ہی" (p. 225).
- Farid put on a new shirt, took it off and gave it to his brother Najib-ud-Din Mutawakkil, saying the old one was sweeter (p. 229).
- Farid's daily fare: a cup of sherbet and two buttered rotis, of which he took little and gave away the rest; his godri too short to cover both head and feet (p. 230).
- He rebuked a servant for buying salt on credit ("قرص"): "عین اسراف[OCR?]" (pp. 230–231).
- Told a son was dying, he replied in Persian "بندہ مسعود چکند اگر تقدیر حق دہد..." (p. 231).
- Origin of "Ganj Shakar", from Akhbar al-Akhyar: under his pir's order to fast (tay), he ate bread a man brought, vomited, and was told it was the food of a drunkard[OCR?]; on the next fast, pebbles he put in his mouth turned to sugar, three times; his pir approved (pp. 231–232).
- From Siyar al-Auliya: a merchant who said his sugar was salt found it salt at Delhi; Farid turned it back to sugar; Persian verses on this from Akhbar al-Akhyar[OCR?] (p. 232).
- Chilla hung upside down for forty days in the well of a jami mosque, tied to a tree by servants (place wording damaged) (p. 232).
- In his last years "سایہ گم ہو گیا تہا", compared with the Prophet having no shadow (p. 232).
- Ate a wooden roti for twelve years; his mother tasted it; he then fasted twelve more years (badly damaged) (p. 234).

## Doubts

- **Opens mid-section.** The healing well and takia on p. 225 belong to a "شاہ" whose heading is in the previous chunk; the subject cannot be named from this chunk.
- **Damage class**: heavy `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` throughout; pp. 232–235 are especially damaged (Persian verses, the Pakpattan door passage, the wooden-roti legend, the chronogram). Only legible parts are reported.
- **Dates kept as printed**: Farid "سنہ ۶۶۳[OCR?] ہجری" (pp. 234, 235); death night "تحم[OCR?] محرم" (p. 232) against the Lahore mela on "پنجم محرم" (p. 235). These are not reconciled here. Mai Lado's death is given as Alamgir's first regnal year, "پنجم[OCR?] محرم" (p. 227). Ahmad Yar "سنہ بارہ سو اکہتر[OCR?]" (p. 227). Qutb Shah "سنہ ۱۲۳۶[OCR?]" (p. 235).
- **Farid Astana is a Lahore chilla site, not the Pakpattan shrine.** It is under Other, and only Farid's own deeds are mapped to shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar. Do not carry the Lahore mela into the Pakpattan entry.
- **data-darbar** is mapped on the bare title "حضرت داتا صاحب" in a damaged sentence (p. 228); the chunk gives no other name. Person-level, weigh accordingly.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir** and **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht** are bare names (shajra head and ancestor) (pp. 227, 235). Nothing is said about either shrine.
- **"شہ جمال" in Mehr Shah's shajra (p. 227) is left unmapped.** It is a bare name in a chain under Mian Mir, and the chunk does not identify him with Shah Jamal of Lahore (shah-jamal). A consolidator should check.
- **Mehr Shah's affiliation is doubled**: he is the faqir of "میاں مہر رضا[OCR?]" and also "خادم پیر احمد یار" (p. 227).
- **"حضرت خواجہ سلیمان صاحب سنگر والی[OCR?]" (p. 235) is not mapped** to darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a. The chunk does not say Taunsa, and the place word is flagged.
- **Toponyms not applied under ruling (a)**, because each only places a person or event and is not material about the place: "کوٹ مٹہن[OCR?]" (Qutb Shah's bai'at, p. 235; would match mithankot-kot-mithan); "اُچ شریف" (birthplace, p. 235); "ضلع گجرات" (Mehr Shah's village, p. 227); "ضلع گوجرانوالہ[OCR?]" (Ahmad Yar, p. 227); "انارکلی" (garbled, p. 228). Shah Alami and Mochi gates name a location only.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** The one city-level clause, "جب شہر ویران ہو گیا" (p. 227), is about the mosque's decline, so it was not made a place-level bullet.
- **No province bullet.** "تمام ملک ہندوستان و پنجاب" (p. 234) is about Farid's devotees, not about Punjab, and sits in a heavily flagged passage.
- **Author's own affiliation**: p. 230 seems to say the author's pir's silsila is Nizamiyya ("ہماری ہر دستگیر میں سلسلہ کمر میں کا سی نظامیہ ہے"), but the line is too garbled to cite.
- Mai Lado's relation to Shah Jahan ("حرمول کی [illegible]") and the Shah Jahan naming story (p. 226) are too damaged to state more than recorded.
