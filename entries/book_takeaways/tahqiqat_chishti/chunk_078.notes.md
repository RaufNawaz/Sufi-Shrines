# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 078 — pp. 852–863

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 852) in a dynastic history of the later Mughals: the tail of the account of Ahmad Shah Abdali's invasions (Delhi, the Marathas, Panipat), his accession and death chronograms (pp. 852–853), then "ذکر شاہ عالم ثانی بادشاہ" (p. 853), "اکبر شاہ ثانی بادشاہ" (p. 854) and "ذکر سراج الدین ابوظفر بہادر شاہ بادشاہ" with a damaged "تاریخ جلوس و وفات" (p. 854); these last pages are very heavily damaged. It then turns to Lahore-area monuments: "احوال مقبرہ حضرت نورالدین جہانگیر بادشاہ غازی مرحوم [OCR?]" with a long "تشریح عمارت" (pp. 855–861) covering the tomb, its chambers, roof, minarets, enclosure, garden, wells, the dak bungalow in the gate, and the adjoining sarai and mosque; "حال مقبرہ آصف خان برادر نور جہان بیگم" (pp. 861–862); a description of Nur Jahan Begum's tomb (p. 863); and "حال مجاوران وخدام [illegible]" of the Jahangir tomb, which breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk (p. 863). None of the monuments described at length is an archive row; archive ids are reached only through Lahore as a place, Multan as a place, Ranjit Singh by name, and Sakhi Sarwar by name.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Jahangir tomb, cost and neglect: "لاکہا روپیہ" spent; long neglected because of "تنزل سلطنت چغتائی و بادشاہ گردی" (p. 855).
- Roof over the grave: there was once a second marble grave-cover on the roof, open to the sky; under Bahadur Shah, "حسب الحکم کسی ملا کی", a roof was built over it so that rain [of mercy] would fall; when rain damaged it, Lehna Singh had a wooden roof put up; Kharak Singh repaired it; "اب تک وہ سقف چوبی [illegible] موجود ہے" (p. 855). The roof "جو بہادر شاہ نے توڑ کر بنائی تہی" is now blackened (p. 859).
- Architecture, as printed (all measures flagged in the source): octagonal grave chamber of marble with inlaid floors of "سنگ مرمر و سنگ عرم [OCR?] و سنگ موسی" (p. 856); gateway openings with sizes in gaz and feet; painted dados in "لاجوردی و سبز" (p. 856); forty cells ("چالیس کوٹہریان") around the gallery, some held by mujawirs, tabulated as "شرف دین مجاور | قطب الدین مجاور | امام الدین مجاور | نظام الدین مجاور | مہتاب [OCR?]" (pp. 856–857); outer arches 50 ft high; platform "دو سو ساٹہہ [OCR?] فٹ" square, five ft high (p. 857); brick pavement "تین سو چوبیس فٹ" (p. 858); roof "۲۱۳ [OCR?] فٹ" by "۲۱۵ [OCR?] فٹ"; central raised platform "۷۵ [OCR?] فٹ" (p. 859); four octagonal four-storey minarets, storeys given in feet; minaret height figure "۴۱ [OCR?] فٹ" (pp. 859–860).
- Enclosure and garden: four corner towers; garden of "ایکسو بیگہ", thirty bigha built (roads, fountains), the rest cultivated; four Persian-wheel wells (by Lehna Singh, Faqir Aziz-ud-Din, Raja Faiz Talab[OCR?], and Mr Wedderburn[OCR?] in the name of a mujawir) (p. 858). The east wall has fallen "بصدمہ دریا"; parts of the south wall and a corner tower fallen (p. 860). Square tanks with fountains, mostly silted (pp. 860–861).
- State involvement: two notices, one English and one Persian, forbid any "صاحب انگریز یا کوئی رئیس" from entering with shoes; the author praises the government ("سرکار ہماری کیسا خوب [OCR?] مراتب شاہان سلف کا رکہتی ہے") (p. 857). The government repaired the floor with lime (p. 857) and the minarets under Wedderburn (p. 860). The west gate is now a dak bungalow: "ایک روپیہ یومیہ", with a chaprasi, income going to the government (p. 860). Since English rule repairs have been made: "تخمینا تیس [OCR?] ۳ [OCR?] ہزار روپیہ" spent (p. 863).
- Sarai: two two-storey gates (north and south); an octagonal well without a wheel; 25+25 cells on the west and east, 21+21 on north and south; size "ایکسو تئیس [OCR?] گردن [OCR?]" by "ایکسو اکاسی [OCR?] گردن [OCR?]" (p. 861). Sakhi Sarwar's sang halts here (see above) (p. 861).
- Custodianship of Jahangir's tomb, "زبانی مجاورین": Shah Jahan gave gardening and mujawiri to "میان غلام محمد قوم آرائین", with furnishings worth "لاکہا روپیہ"; two Quran-reciting hafiz per cell; a jagir of "موضع منگل [OCR?] و [illegible]" and revenue-free land of the wells; hafiz paid from the royal treasury; later a king "واحد [OCR?] شاہ" supported them; the Sikh period ruined the furnishings (text breaks off) (p. 863).
- Removals by Sikhs: marble from Jahangir's tomb to Amritsar (p. 855); Asif Khan's marble gate and mosque floor (pp. 861–862); Nur Jahan's tomb stripped (p. 863).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author says the historical account of Jahangir needs no detail since it is in all histories of India, but the building had to be described, so he gives it in detail (p. 855).
- He reads the ruins as a lesson ("مشاہدہ سے ان مکانات کی عبرت آتی ہے") and blames those who "نام ونشان یادگار شاہان سلف کا مٹا دیا", contrasting the English government's repairs (p. 863).

## Citable passages

- "یہہ مقام مقبرہ دریای راوی کی کنارے پر شہر لاہور سے بطرف شمال بفاصلہ چار میل واقع ہے" — this tomb stands on the bank of the Ravi, four miles north of Lahore city (p. 855).
- "جب حضرت سخی سرور کا سنگ [OCR?] جاتا ہے تو ایک رات یہاں مقام کرتا ہے" — when Hazrat Sakhi Sarwar's sang passes, it halts here one night (p. 861).
- "بباعث تعصب مذہبی ... اکہڑوا کر بمقام دربار سری امرتسر در رام باغ نصب کرائی" — out of religious prejudice he had them pulled up and set in the Ram Bagh at Amritsar (p. 855).
- "اگر فردوس بروی زمین است ٭ ہمین است و ہمین است" — if there is paradise on earth, it is this (quoted of the tomb) (p. 855).
- "مشاہدہ سے ان مکانات کی عبرت آتی ہے" — seeing these buildings teaches a lesson (p. 863).
