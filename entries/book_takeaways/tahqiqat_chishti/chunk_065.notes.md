# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 065 — pp. 712–721

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 712). Its first pages continue a building-by-building description of Begumpura, Nawab Khan Bahadur's estate; the section heading is in the previous chunk. The description covers the chabutra with the graves of the Nawab and his family, a domed "مقام قدم رسول", the garden with its dalans and kothris, a kashi-work mosque with an inscribed marble slab, and a tank, and it says repeatedly that "گلاب سنگھ" damaged or altered the buildings (pp. 712–714). It then runs through three titled sections in order. First, "احوال نوَاب جاں[OCR?] بہادر" (pp. 714–719) is a political narrative of the Mughal governors of Lahore: Abdus Samad Khan, Zakariya Khan (Khan Bahadur), Shahnawaz Khan and Yahya Khan, Nadir Shah, Ahmad Shah Abdali, Nawab/Khwaja Abdullah Khan and Mir Mannu (Mu'in-ul-Mulk). Second, "حال اولاد نواب خان بہادر مرحوم" (pp. 719–721) is a dense family register of the Nawab's descendants that ends with their dispersal from Lahore and the coming of Ranjit Singh. Third, "حال بیگم پورہ" (p. 721) covers Begumpura under Ranjit Singh's rule and breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk. The damage is very heavy throughout. Many sentences are unreadable, and only facts with a readable core are carried below.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- Begumpura ("بیگم پورہ"): the Nawab's estate. "تسمیہ اسکا باسم بیگم پورہ" (it was named Begumpura) after the Nawab's mother, who is said to have had it built (heavily flagged) (p. 714). Buildings described (pp. 712–714):
  - a domed "مقام قدم رسول" where the Nawab had set up a Qadam Rasul, "مگر افسوس کہ اب موجود نہیں" (sadly no longer there) (p. 712);
  - a chabutra with the graves of Nawab Khan Bahadur and others (p. 712), and graves of begums (p. 714);
  - dalans, balconies and kothris, partly collapsed, some built or altered by "گلاب سنگھ" (pp. 712–713);
  - a large kashi-work mosque with four minarets, which "گلاب سنگھ" hid by raising a building around them (p. 713);
  - a marble slab inscribed "لا الہ الا اللہ محمد رسول اللہ", with "عجلوا بالصلوٰۃ قبل الموت" on the north side and "عجلوا بالصلوٰۃ قبل الفوت" on the south side (p. 713);
  - a large tank with a fountain, and a marble platform in the garden (p. 713).
  - Under Ranjit Singh it became an artillery post. "اب بھی" (even now) a garden enclosure stands, and inside a smaller garden is a square tomb, "یہ مقبرہ ملک زمانی[OCR?] کا ہے", whose account is in the section on Hazrat Ishan. Malika Zamani is described as "شہ[OCR?] مغفر[OCR?] زادی[OCR?] شاہجہاں کی" (p. 721).
- Nawab Khan Bahadur, i.e. Zakariya Khan: the book equates the two names, "ذکریا خان المشہور[OCR?] خان بہادر" (p. 719).
  - His father was Abdus Samad Khan, a Naqshbandi pirzada descended from "حضرت ایسان" (p. 714).
  - He complained against the deputy Mir Momin Khan and the Rajputs of "اولیا پور" (chief "محمود خان", a robber) over a robbed Iranian merchant, went to Shahjahanabad under "محمد معزالدین جہاندار[OCR?] شاہ", and after Farrukhsiyar's accession was made Nazim of Lahore (pp. 715–716).
  - Farrukhsiyar's dates as printed: "تاریخ ولادت ۱۸ ــ رجب [illegible] روز پنجشنبہ سنہ ۱۱۹۵۰[OCR?] ــ اور وفات ۸ ــ ربیع الاول شنبہ[OCR?] ۱۱[OCR?]" (p. 715).
  - He arrested Mahmud Khan and restored the merchant's goods (p. 716). He married a daughter of "قمرالدین خان" (p. 716).
  - He built a haveli for "مسماۃ[OCR?] گلو", whom he married; "اب ... حویلی اہلو والہ مشہور[OCR?]" (it is now known as the Ahluwalia haveli) (p. 715).
- Shahnawaz Khan and Yahya Khan, the Nawab's sons, fought each other. Yahya Khan's haveli stood where "باغ نوابان ملتان" is; Shahnawaz lived in "سلیم پورہ" (p. 716). Shahnawaz captured Yahya; Yahya fled "معرفت ڈاک" to "حیدرآباد" in the Deccan, where his descendants still live. A village "یحیی[OCR?] نگر[OCR?]" was named after him (p. 716).
- Nadir Shah's invasion: the battle at "کرنال", Qamar-ud-Din Khan as commander, "قتل عام" in Delhi, and the taking of the Koh-i-Nur and the Darya-i-Nur ("نور و دریای نور") (p. 717). A Persian couplet is quoted: "سر بگردد بر نگردد سر نوشت   این سخن با آب زر باید نوشت" (p. 717).
- Nawab/Khwaja Abdullah Khan: called "برادر حقیقی نواب خان بہادر" (the Nawab's real brother) and "خام طمع بد مزاج" (greedy and ill-tempered) (p. 717).

  - His horse bolted and dragged him to death. He was buried "[?] محرم المحرم شہ ۱۱۶۷[OCR?] کو متصل مقام شہید گنج", and "مقبرہ اسکا تا دم تحریر موجود ہی" (his tomb still stands). Later, under Sikh rule, "مقبرہ میر مسنو کا اوکہاڑا[OCR?] گیا" (Mir Mannu's tomb was dug up) (p. 719).
  - The book attributes the Sikh enmity toward the Nawab's descendants to Mir Mannu's connection with the family (p. 721).
- Ahmad Shah, emperor at Delhi, was blinded and killed by "نواب غازی[OCR?] ... الدین خان"; "عالم گیر[OCR?] ثانی[OCR?]" succeeded (p. 719).

Khwaja Inayatullah[OCR?] is said to have died "سنہ ۱۲۰۰[OCR?]" aged "سو برس کامل" (a full hundred years), honoured by all as a family elder (p. 719).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Begumpura revenue: "راجہ سنا[OCR?] چند" gave it to his Brahmins, with the "معاملہ" (assessment) fixed at "ایک سو رپیہ سالانہ" (one hundred rupees a year). Later the Brahmins were given a jagir in the Kohistan instead (damaged) (p. 721).
- Settlement of Begumpura under Ranjit Singh: zamindars "مراد بخش اور خدا بخش" of Baghbanpura were ordered to settle and cultivate it, and Khuda Bakhsh was made "نمبردار" (p. 721).
- The Nawab's descendants were dispossessed: "اولاد خان بہادر نواب کی اپنی املاک سی بید خل[OCR?] رہی" (the Nawab's descendants remained ousted from their own properties). Yet "قبر و مسجد" (the tomb and mosque) survived, which the book puts down to the Nawab's "نیک کمائی" (honest earnings) (p. 719).
- Building damage under Sikh rule by "گلاب سنگھ" is noted throughout the Begumpura description (pp. 712–713, 719, 721).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- None as such. The book's own explanation that the tomb and mosque survived because of the Nawab's "نیک کمائی" (honest earnings) is recorded under Practices (p. 719).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author blames the exile of the Nawab's descendants on the link with Mir Mannu: "اگر میر منو اور نواب صاحب میں رابطہ[OCR?] ... نہ ہوتا تو ... اولاد کو جلا وطن ہونا پڑتا" (if there had been no tie between Mir Mannu and the Nawab, the descendants would not have had to go into exile) (p. 721).
- The author's judgement on Muhammad Shah: "عیش و عشرت میں مشغول" (absorbed in pleasure) while Nadir advanced (p. 717).

## Citable passages

- "نادر شاہ نی لاہور کو خوب لوٹا" — "Nadir Shah thoroughly plundered Lahore" (p. 717).
- "حکومت نواب عبداللہ[OCR?] نہ چکی[OCR?] رہی نہ چلہا[OCR?]" — a proverb on Nawab Abdullah's rule during the Lahore famine (p. 717).
- "جہان کسی سکہہ[OCR?] نے موقع پایا حاکم ہو بیٹھا" — "wherever a Sikh found the chance, he set himself up as ruler" (p. 719).

- Damage inventory: the whole chunk is saturated with `[OCR?]`, plus several `[illegible]` stretches (p. 712; p. 714 "about six words"; p. 715 in Farrukhsiyar's date; p. 721).
