# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 026 — pp. 292–302

The chunk carries no new "فصل" heading of its own. It opens mid-narrative (p. 292) with a damaged passage about red stone and Alamgir's "مسجد بادشاہی" in Lahore. This leads into the story of Dara Shikoh, Mian Mir's prophecy that he "would be king", his betrayal and death, and Alamgir's vision as told by a fakir of Mulla Shah's circle (pp. 292–293). Next comes an account of Mulla Shah, whose tomb lies in the south-west corner of Mian Mir's rauza: his origin, his discipleship, his austerities, Dara Shikoh's anecdotes about him, and his death date and chronograms (pp. 293–295). From p. 295 to the end the chunk is about the tomb complex of Mian Mir. It covers the site at Hashim Pura, the enclosure, the urs and mela, Maharaja Ranjit Singh's attempt to strip the tomb's stone and his fall from his horse, Mian Mir's name, descent, family, practices and karamat (largely from Dara Shikoh's "سکینۃ الاولیا"), his khalifas, the sajjada nashin line and the farman appointing Muhammad Sharif, the building history (Dara Shikoh and Alamgir), the Qadiri shajra, and a long architectural survey of the dalans, mosque, well, garden and graves (pp. 295–302). The chunk breaks off in a list of sajjada nashins' names (p. 302).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Mian Mir — enclosure** (p. 295):
  - A high, pakka "چار دیواری" (enclosure wall).
  - Inside it on the west, a "مسجد عالیشان پختہ تین گنبد والی" (a grand three-domed mosque), white and spacious.
  - The entrance gate is on the south. An earlier north gate has been "مسدود[OCR?]" (blocked) for some time and is sometimes opened on the urs.
  - Outside the south gate, a fakir's kotha.
  - The whole floor inside is red stone.
- **Mian Mir — chabutra.** In the middle is a marble chabutra, "پانچ فٹ اونچا" (five feet high), with "سات[OCR?] زینہ" (seven[OCR?] steps). At the mela, visitors and spectators sit on it in the hot season (p. 295).
- **Mian Mir — urs and mela** (pp. 295–296):
  - Held on "ہفتم ربیع الاول".
  - Shops line both sides from the gate to the chabutra. "صد ہا" (hundreds of) halwai and fruit stalls run out behind the mosque, with a "می فروشی[OCR?]" stall, falooda makers and toy sellers (p. 295).
  - There are "اکھاڑی[OCR?]" of charas-smokers and water carriers. Hundreds of carts, ekkas, camels, dolis and ponies gather (p. 296).
- **Mian Mir — the mela in decline.** The mela ground was here, but people began building houses on part of it, and "یہ میلہ [illegible] تمام ہونی لگا" (p. 296). The passage is damaged.
- **The Ranjit Singh episode — public opinion.** People said a king should not pull down the buildings of former kings but repair old buildings. The present "صاحبان عالیشاں دام اقبالہم و حشمتہم" repair old buildings so they remain as "یادگار" (memorials) (p. 296).
- **Mujawirs and revenue** (p. 297):
  - After Ranjit Singh's repentance, the mujawirs became rich.
  - "آجکل بہی معافیان" (revenue-free grants) exist today.
  - Hundreds of nobles brought offerings daily, and beggars sat there and went home with goods in the evening.
- **Custodianship.** The appointment by Dara Shikoh's farman, and the "جاگیر متعلقہ مقبرہ", are under the archive bullet (pp. 298–299). The sajjada nashins' graves are inside the enclosure. The other graves are of brick and lime (p. 299).
- **Building history.** Dara Shikoh built the stone parts, and Alamgir the brick dome and the remainder (p. 299).
- **Mian Mir — dalans and the west side** (p. 300):
  - The walls carry arcades of "پانچ محراب".
  - The wall is "تخمیناً پانچ گز" (about five yards) high.
  - A great lime-plastered dalan stands on the west.
  - A dalan for the present sajjada nashin Mahbub Shah is dated "عرصہ چودہ برس سی" (for fourteen years) (p. 300).
  - Further dalans with cells are for the sajjada nashin's use (p. 300).
  - A mosque on the north wall is attributed to Alamgir's building (p. 300).
- **Mian Mir — mosque** (p. 301):
  - Brick, with "پانچ گنبد", five arches and white "کلس".
  - Two eight-sided burjis.
  - Marble panels "آدہ آدہ گز" (half a yard each).
  - A brick minbar.
  - The courtyard is "دس[OCR?] گز" by "تیس[OCR?] گز".
  - Steps of red and black stone.
  - Whitewashed seven years ago by Nur Hasan[OCR?], who had the north bay closed off into a hujra (p. 301).
- **Mian Mir — north dalan.** Its base is "مبنیہ دارا شکوہ[OCR?]" in red stone, and the upper work is Alamgir's. On the urs [illegible] is prepared there, and there are "دو تنور" (two ovens) (p. 301).

- **Mian Mir — the east dalan.** A dalan directly east of the tomb was built "سات برس سی" by Mahbub Shah[OCR?] (p. 301).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Alamgir's vision** (p. 293), shown him by the fakir of Mulla Shah's circle:
  - The Prophet rides in procession with Imam Hasan on his right and Imam Husain on his left. Dara Shikoh's hand is in Imam Husain's.
  - All say "دارا شکوہ شہید[OCR?] ہو کر[OCR?] شاہ بہشت ہوا".
  - Alamgir sees himself carrying a basket of filth on his head. The fakir says the basket is "دنیا کا جاہ" (worldly rank).
- **Mulla Shah's lamp.** He had a lamp fetched from a neighbour when Dara visited, saying the house was lit only because of Dara's coming (as Dara writes) (p. 293).
- **Mulla Shah and Dara's question.** Dara could not bring himself to ask Mulla Shah about the vision of God ("رویت ذات حق تعالی"). The Prophet answered him in a turning of attention. The next day Mulla Shah told Dara that his doubt had been settled, though only God knew of it (p. 294).
- **Ranjit Singh's fall.** He fell from his horse "لیلی" when he ordered the stripping of Mian Mir's tomb, and read it as punishment for disrespect (pp. 296–297).
- **Mian Mir at Ghar Hira.** The servant "میان نتہا[OCR?]" found Mian Mir missing from his bed one night (from Dara Shikoh's "سکینۃ الاولیا"). Mian Mir told him, in confidence, "ہم رات کو ہر شب غار حرا مین حاضر ہوا کیا کرتی ہین". An hour's worship there is worth a thousand elsewhere (pp. 297–298).
- **Dara's cure.** Dara writes that in an illness the doctors gave up hope. Shah Jahan sought Mian Mir's help. Mian Mir breathed over water and gave it to him to drink, and he recovered completely (p. 298).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author, through the fakir's speech, reads Mian Mir's prophecy to Dara Shikoh as being about inner kingship ("بادشاہ ملک[OCR?] باطن"), not the throne of Hindustan (p. 292).
- The author reports with approval the view that kings should repair, not pull down, the buildings of earlier kings. He praises the present "صاحبان عالیشاں" for doing so (p. 296).
