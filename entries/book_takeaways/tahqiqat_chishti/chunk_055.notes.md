# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 055 — pp. 615–624

The chunk opens in the middle of a section on the Jalali faqirs and the tomb of a "حضرت دریام شاہ صاحب" / "سید دریائی بخاری" (pp. 615–617). **That section's heading is in the previous chunk (054).** The section covers the faqirs' dress and branding seal ("مہر"), their origin story as Lahore's present faqirs tell it, the tomb's platforms, graves and trees, and its present Bukhari Sayyid custodians. Two titled parts follow: "چہل تنون کے حال کی تشریح" (the story of the Chihil-tan, the Forty Bodies, pp. 617–620) and "شجرہ خاندان جلالیہ حسب بیان سادات بخاری" (p. 620), then two "فائدہ" notes (p. 620). After these comes the author's own account, drawn from "تحفۃ الفقرا", of how the branding began with Sayyid Jalal Bukhari and Wadud[OCR?] Haqqani, ending in his harsh verdict on the faqirs (pp. 621–623). Three short tomb sections close the chunk: "احوال متبرکہ جانی خان مرحوم" (pp. 623–624), "ذکر مقبرہ پیر سید سراج الدین کیلانی" (p. 624) and "حال مقبرہ حضرت شاہ بخاری" (p. 624). The last of these breaks off mid-description. The only archive mapping is one place-level Rawalpindi bullet.

- **Chihil-tan (چہل تن)**:
  - Origin: "حضرت دحیہ کلبی" complained of having no children. Hazrat Ali gave him a taweez for his wife, forty times in all. She had not used them, and when he scolded her she dissolved all forty at once and drank them. She bore forty children, "اکتالیس فرزند" by another phrase, and one was kept while the rest were left in a basket in the jungle (pp. 617–618).
  - They were nourished in "ایک گمد[OCR?]". When "وبا" (plague) struck Medina, the Prophet was told to bring them (p. 618).
  - At the door, "میں محمد ہوں" was refused. On Ali's advice the Prophet said "عبدہ محمد رسول اللہ", the door opened and forty boys came out. Strips torn from his turban made their loincloths, and the turban stayed whole (p. 618).
  - Their names "known in Hindustan" are listed (pp. 618–619), ending "۴۰ [OCR?]" (p. 619).
  - The plague left Medina when they entered. The people complained of their wild behaviour, and the plague returned once they were sent away. Ali brought them back, and at the city gate a goat ("بکری") was the plague, which they caught and ate. Its tail was left in the hand of "کلاک علی[OCR?]" (p. 619).
  - The Prophet made "محمد و باتلی[OCR?]" his khadim and told them to take bai'at of one another (p. 619).
  - The book reports: "تمام اہل اسلام کا اعتقاد ہے کہ اب تک وہ چہل تن زندہ ہیں". They move each day to a different direction by the lunar date, and travel toward the side where they are is forbidden (pp. 619–620).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Jalali faqirs' insignia** (p. 615):
  - A horn ("سینگ [OCR?]") which, when blown, gives the call "قطب قطب قطب" three times.
  - A "قلابہ" (worn ring or band).
  - A rope worn as a belt.
  - A wool turban called "سیلی".
- **Their origin as told** (p. 615):
  - The "قلابہ" stands in for a "چوٹر ہی" (a bangle worn by women) that Khatun-e Qiyamat gave Sultan Ahmad.
  - The seli turban stands in for a "پرانندہ [OCR?]" she gave.
- **The branding seal (مہر)**:
  - The faqirs' story ties it to Sayyid Jalal's tandoor and the Prophet's dream: "تب سے یہ رسم مہر جاری ہو" (p. 616).
  - The author's version, from "تحفۃ الفقرا": Sayyid Jalal found Wadud[OCR?] Haqqani's followers holding many "اشرفیاں", the cause of their hardness of heart. He had each take an ashrafi, and the coins were then distributed (p. 622).
  - Wadud Haqqani was told to test each seeker: first refuse him, then brand ("داغ") his arm "حسب الارشاد حضرت سید جلال" (p. 623).
  - "الی الیوم اس سلسلہ جلالیہ میں مروج ہے" (it is current in this Jalaliyya silsila to this day) (p. 623).
  - Now "بوقت بیعت بجائے اشرفی پیسہ گرم کر کے بازو پر لگاتے ہیں" (at bai'at they heat a paisa in place of an ashrafi and apply it to the arm) (p. 623).
- **Money**:
  - No faqir keeps money with him (p. 623).
  - Those who stay in worldly work send their yearly surplus as nazrana to Hazrat Wadud[OCR?] Haqqani's mazar (p. 623).
- **Two silsilas**: from Wadud Haqqani "دو … سلسلہ جاری ہوئے ایک چہل تنی اور دوسرا جلالیہ" (two silsilas began, one Chihil-tani and one Jalaliyya) (p. 621).
- **Fire-dhamal**:
  - People with an illness or difficulty vow a goat or the like as nazrana to the Chihil-tan (p. 621).
  - When the wish is granted, faqirs are called. They light many coals in memory of "تنور حضرت سید جلال" and extinguish the fire by doing "دھمال" on it barefoot, and only then is the nazr taken (p. 621).
- **Pir/murshid distinction ("فائدہ")**:
  - Today there is a pir and a murshid. The murshid's follower is a "طالب" and the pir's a "مرید" (p. 620).
  - One who stays in worldly work is a murid, and one who abandons the world as a seeker of God is a talib (p. 620).
- **Jani Khan's tomb — fabric and ownership**:
  - A deorhi of the old garden survives to the south. "از عرصہ دو سال" (two years ago) it was bought "بقیمت ایک سو ایک روپیہ" (for one hundred and one rupees) by someone named "شیر از سینہ دار[OCR?]" (p. 623).
  - The building is plastered, rectangular, with arches, and its dome is partly fallen (p. 623).
  - A "میندار[OCR?]" (zamindar?) has put "تورٹھی[OCR?]" inside it (p. 623).
- **Siraj-ud-Din Gilani's tomb**:
  - "[illegible] کو یہاں چراغ ملتا ہے" (a lamp is lit here [illegible]) (p. 624).
  - The residents of "پورہ اعوان[OCR?]" and the zamindars tell of his many karamat (p. 624).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Chihil-tan: their forty-fold birth from forty taweez, the door that opened at "عبدہ محمد رسول اللہ", the turban that stayed whole, the plague that left and returned, and the goat that was the plague (pp. 617–619). All of this is attributed to "وہ کہتی میں" (they say), meaning the present Jalali informants (p. 617).
- The Chihil-tan are believed still alive (p. 619).
- Wadud Haqqani's followers became "صاحب کمال" after giving up their ashrafis (p. 622).
- Siraj-ud-Din Gilani: a "کریم بخش[OCR?]" reports that "رات کو اس مقبرہ سے آواز و ذکر لا الہ الا اللہ کا آتا ہے" (at night the sound of the zikr "la ilaha illallah" comes from this tomb) (p. 624).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author says he set down the faqirs' oral stories only because they are "زبان زد عوام الفقرا" and "دلچسپ" (current among common faqirs, and interesting). He then searched "کتب معتبرہ" and gives the summary from "تحفۃ الفقرا" as the real account (p. 621).
- He judges that the claim "یہی فقیر جلالی چہل تنی ہے" is "داخل سخن ہندی مجہولان پنجاب" (among the sayings of the ignorant of Punjab) (p. 623).
- He says that in earlier times the faqirs of this silsila were "عارفین با اللہ", while "اب کی زمانہ" (in the present day) they are "مجہول مطلق اور یہ حیوان لایعقل" (p. 623).
- He adds sarcastically that if branding the arm produced gnosis, "مضایقہ ندارد" (there would be no objection), and closes with "کار پاکان را قیاس از خود مگیر" (p. 623).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.**
  - Damage is heavy throughout, with dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags.
  - Pp. 616 and 622 also carry long stretches of unflagged garble (e.g. "ایک شہر آدمی", "خاداراں بہال"). Those passages are summarised only where the sense is recoverable, and nothing is quoted as a reading.
  - Every quotation keeps its flags.
  - Folio markers on pp. 621, 622 and 624 ("619", "620", "622") are printed but not cited, per the brief.
- **Opening section.** The heading for the Jalali / "دریام شاہ" section is in chunk 054. Pp. 615–617 should be joined to it at consolidation.
- **Sayyid Jalal Bukhari is NOT mapped.**
  - "پیر شیر شاہ سید جلال بخاری ابوج والہ" (p. 615) could be either of two rows:
    - **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari** (Sayyid Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari).
    - **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht** (Sayyid Jalaluddin, Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht).
  - Both are at Uch Sharif. The chunk gives no date or other identifier.
  - "ابوج[OCR?] والی" / "ابوج والہ" is flagged once and is not printed as "اوچ", so it is not read as Uch and the 5 `uch sharif` ids are not attached.
  - Under the protocol's two-row rule this is left for the consolidator. It is the main placement question in this chunk.
- **"حضرت دریام شاہ" / "سید دریائی بخاری" is not mapped.** The chunk does not identify him with shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari ("Mauj Darya") or with any other row. The resemblance of the name is not evidence. Check the heading in chunk 054.
- **Rawalpindi mapping is place-level, weigh accordingly.** The phrase is "کوہستان نواح راولپنڈی" (the hill country *around* Rawalpindi), not the city. It is the scene of Wadud Haqqani's twelve years of austerity, as reported from "تحفۃ الفقرا". The 6 `rawalpindi` ids are attached by ruling (a), but no site is named.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.**
  - "فقرای موجودہ لاہور" (p. 615) is about a faqir community, not the city.
  - "ناظم لاہور" (p. 624) is Jani Khan's office, inside a damaged sentence.
  - The 35 `lahore` ids are therefore not attached. The consolidator may weigh this.
- **Punjab province bullet is province-level, weigh accordingly.** It is a single dismissive phrase. The book does not mark the unit's boundaries.
- **Bare names not mapped**:
  - "شاہ جمال مجبر[OCR?]" in the faqir chain (p. 615) is not shah-jamal; nothing identifies him.
  - "سلطان ایبک[OCR?]" in the Chihil-tan name list (p. 618) is not tomb-of-qutbuddin-aibak.
  - "ملتان شام[OCR?]" (p. 616) is a personal name in a chain, not the Multan toponym.
- **Jani Khan's death date conflicts internally.** The digits "۱۱۹۴[OCR?]" and the words "گیارہ سو باویں[OCR?]" are printed side by side (p. 624). They do not obviously agree, and both are flagged. Do not compute.
- **The ruler "ابو الفدا[OCR?] [illegible] شاہ بادشاہ"** in whose reign the tomb was built (p. 623) is unreadable. The book names "محمد شاہ بادشاہ" separately as Jani Khan's patron.
- **Siraj-ud-Din's death** "دہم محرم سنہ[OCR?] بارہ سو[OCR?] [illegible]" (p. 624): the year is incomplete.
- **"متبرکہ"** in the Jani Khan heading and text (p. 623) is printed unflagged. It may stand for "مقبرہ", but it is carried as printed.
- **"چودہ[OCR?] سال"** (fourteen years before khilafat, p. 621) and "ایک لاکھ" (followers, p. 621) are numerals from a single reading.
- **Two darbars on the site but not in the index** (Shah Gohar Peer; Mian Qurban Ali Shah): neither is named in this chunk.
