# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 009 — pp. 107–119

This chunk is part of the book's tomb-by-tomb survey of Lahore and its outskirts. It opens mid-entry with the close of the account of Hazrat Mauj Darya Bukhari's tomb (its inscription, eight door verses and the graves and mosques around it) (pp. 107–108). It then runs through five titled sections in order: "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت عبدالرزاق شاہ چراغ گیلانی" (p. 108); "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت عبد اللہ شاہ صاحب قادری" (p. 110); "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت شاہ ابو اسحاق قادری" (p. 113); "ذکر مقبرہ حضرت شیخ موسیٰ" (p. 117); and "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت شیخ عبد الجلیل المشہور [illegible]" (p. 118), whose heading runs onto p. 119 and whose entry breaks off mid-sentence at the end of p. 119 ("جب وہ بی بی فوت"). Most entries combine an eyewitness description of the building ("کمترین نے خود ... دیکھا"), custodians and observances, a karamat, and chronograms, several by Mufti Ghulam Sarwar. Damage is very heavy on every page; most dates survive only as margin numerals flagged [OCR?].

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Mauj Darya Bukhari: no sajjada nashin; a dalan and two kothris are the mutawallis' sitting place (p. 107).
- Shah Chiragh: sajjada nashin of the saint's offspring, "ثنا [OCR?] شاہ" (p. 110); a faqir "موصی [OCR?] شاہ" holds the urs and takes the income as khadim (p. 110); urs in "ربیع الثانی [OCR?]" (p. 108); a weekly Thursday "خوب [illegible] بجتی ہے" (p. 109); land lost since the Sikh period, "بعہد سکھان", before British rule (p. 109); an adjoining mosque taken into an accounts office (pp. 108–109).
- Abdullah Shah Qadri: daily mela "[illegible] الاول[OCR?] کی آٹھویں سے گیارہویں[OCR?] تک", attended by murids from near and far; "تیسرے روز بلوچ لوگ ساکنان مزنگ[OCR?]" hold his urs (p. 111); the nazr, fatiha and income are taken by "بلوچ برادران سردار خان", and "سردار خان [illegible] فتح علی [illegible]" is now owner of the place (p. 111); a faqir lives in a room there and weavers ("بافندہ[OCR?]") work there (p. 111).
- Abdullah Shah's complex as described by the author: a mosque with two four-storey minarets with stairs counted per storey (pp. 110–111); a baradari with twelve doors, a sarai[OCR?], a large tank (حوض), a hammam (p. 110); a talab north of the mosque, a running well to the east, a garden with a few trees (p. 111); the enclosure later built by Sardar Khan (p. 112).
- Shah Abu Ishaq: plaster (گچ) work inside that the author admires — "عقل کام نہیں کرتی" how the craftsman did it (p. 114); interior inscriptions in white: Sura Tabarak (Mulk), the names "اللہ محمد ابو بکر [illegible] عثمان علی", Sura Ikhlas "بخط ثلث", divine names "یا قائم[OCR?] یا قہار[OCR?] یا ساتر و یا غفار" (pp. 114–115); a government school in the west corner, "مدرسہ سرکاری[OCR?]", with "میاں فرید الدین[OCR?] صاحب" as teacher (p. 115).
- Shah Abu Ishaq observance: in Muharram from the fifth date, every year; Quran reciters gather; fatiha over meat; "قوالی راگ [illegible] کی یہاں بالکل ممانعت ہے اور کبھی وقوع میں نہیں آئی", qawwali and rag are entirely forbidden here and have never happened (p. 116).
- Abd al-Jalil: the deorhi rebuilt by "سردار [illegible] سنگھ" under the supervision of Ghulam Muhyi-ud-Din Shah Qureshi, and the enclosure built by him "سال بارہ سو[OCR?] [illegible] ہجری", with Mufti Ghulam Sarwar's chronogram "بنائی از غلام محی الدین گفت" and "سنہ ۱۲۶۴[OCR?]" (p. 119); a new dalan, two kothris, a brick well; a faqir "حاجی شاہ[OCR?]" now sits there; a lamp-niche (چراغدان) at the head of the grave (p. 119).
- Shaikh Musa: tomb built by "اکبر[OCR?] بادشاہ کی والدہ" (p. 117).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Shah Chiragh's name: on his birth Abdul Qadir Salis said "یہ ہمارے [illegible] کا چراغ ہوگا", he will be the lamp of our [illegible] (p. 108).
- Abdullah Shah Qadri, as told by Mulla Muhammad[OCR?]: a shaikh came seeking help; the saint turned the mosque's dust[OCR?] into "سرخ خالص سونا", told him "یہ کیمیا ہے ... اسی خدا کی عبادت کر", the gold was sold and ropes and [illegible] bought and distributed so that no mosque in the city lacked them; the shaikh later tried alchemy at home with fire and lost even his coins, and the saint rebuked him, "اگر ایسی تعلقات کی طرف متوجہ ہوگے تو خدا کو کب پاؤ گے" (pp. 112–113).
- Shah Abu Ishaq: after his death a murid going to Arabia ("عرب") to trade called on him; the merchant[OCR?] prospered, came to Lahore and built the tomb (p. 116).
