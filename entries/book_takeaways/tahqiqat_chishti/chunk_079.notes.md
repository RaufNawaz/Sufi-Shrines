# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 079 — pp. 864–873

This is the book's last chunk. It opens mid-section (p. 864) with the end of an account of a "مقبرہ" whose heading and identity are in the previous chunk (chunk 078). This fragment covers its mujawirs, their genealogy, their dispute over the garden and the offerings, and the lapsed urs. Then come three titled sections in order. First, "حال سمادہ سردار جواہر سنگہ وراجہ سوچیت سنگہ" (pp. 866–871). It starts with Ranjit Singh's dera of granthis and gives a long eyewitness account of the Lahore court killings (Raja Hira Singh, Pandit Jalla, Sardar Jawahar Singh). It then describes the building of Jawahar Singh's samadhi, its custodian Bhai Budh Singh, the adjoining samadhi of Raja Suchet Singh and a shivala built by Rani Jindan's servant Maskalan. Second, "حال نولکہہ" (pp. 871–872), on Nawab Ali Mardan Khan's garden near the Lahore railway station. Third, "خاتمہ" (p. 872), a printer's afterword on the book and its author. It is followed by chronograms for the printing and for the author's death (p. 873). Folio markers are printed but are not cited here (brief rule).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Opening tomb, garden and offerings dispute:
  - Previously all offerings were split equally every day. When the settlement (بندوبست) came, the mujawirs quarrelled. "محکمہ بندوبست" appointed two arbitrators, "دیال شاہ کہتری ساہوکار ساکن شاہدرہ" and "عبد اللہ چودہری لونگہ [OCR?]" (p. 864).
  - The arbitrators split the garden into three shares (p. 865).
  - Hikmatullah then set ten-day turns (نوبت) for the offerings. Each holder keeps up to four rupees, and anything above that is shared (p. 865).
  - Hikmatullah and Jawaya live in Shahdara and practise as hakims ("حکمت کرتی [OCR?]"). Imam Din, Qutb-ud-Din, Sharafdin and Nizam-ud-Din attend day and night and sweep (p. 865).
  - During Hikmatullah and Jawaya's turn the door is kept locked. The author judges this improper because "صاحبان عالیشان وغیرہ رئیسان ورعایا" come to visit (p. 865).
  - The others complain that offerings from veiled women or notables go inside unseen, and that their living depends only on this tomb's income. The author is sure the matter will reach the court (p. 865).
- Dera of granthis, pay and allowances:
  - Officers got jagirs and the rest got "اٹہہ آنہ [OCR?]" a month with grain (p. 866). The Maharaja proclaimed that anyone bringing 25 granths would get rank (p. 866).
  - Ajitans got "نو نو [OCR?] روپیہ" a month besides their jagirs (p. 867).
  - Jawahar Singh's grant set five granthis per granth, with one rupee a day each for food plus "۵ [OCR?]" rupees pay. It added five rupees a day for "کڑاہ پرشاد", with the remainder for oil, lamps and flowers (p. 867).
  - That grant is dated "تاریخ سنکرانت ماہ اسوج سمت انیس [OCR?] سو دو [OCR?]" (p. 867).
- Jawahar Singh's samadhi, endowment:
  - A jagir for "سداورت" for any "مسافر غریب سادہو سکہہ سنت", a sabil, and a water-carrier at "للعہ [OCR?]" a month (p. 870).
  - Two rupees a day, of which one and a quarter rupees a day went to karah prashad offered as ardas. "سنت رام جمعدار کہاران" was ordered to offer it daily with his own hand (pp. 870–871).
  - A jagir village worth three hundred rupees (p. 870), confiscated in 1859[OCR?] and later restored (pp. 870–871).
- Suchet Singh's samadhi is kept up with some support from the Maharaja of Jammu and Kashmir (p. 871).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author's judgements:
  - Locking the tomb door is "مناسب نہین" (p. 865).
  - The Pandit Jalla rising happened "کیونکہ وہ نہایت بدچلن ظالم تہا" (p. 868).
  - The court killings are a lesson ("سبحان اللہ مقام عبرت ہی") (p. 867).
- Afterword's claims (Fazl-ud-Din): the book is unique and the first full work on Lahore's antiquities, and it is even-handed across religions (p. 872).

## Citable passages

- "چارون طرف لاہور کے جسقدر مزار ہین بزرگان سلف کی یادگار ہین اون سب کی حالات" — "of all the tombs around Lahore, memorials of the elders of the past, the accounts of them all" (p. 872).
- "جامع احوال بزرگان ہنود و اہل اسلام ہے" — "it gathers the lives of the holy men of the Hindus and the people of Islam" (p. 872).
- "صلح کلی کی یہہ حالت ہی کہ ہر ایک مذہب والی کی قدر و منزلت کی رعایت ہی" — "its universal peace is such that the standing of the followers of every religion is respected" (p. 872).
- "قبل از اختتام عمارت سمادہ اختتام سلطنت ہو گیا" — "before the samadhi building was finished, the kingdom was finished" (p. 870).
- "ہمارا گذارا صرف اسی مرقد کی آمدنی پر منحصر ہے" — the mujawirs: "our livelihood depends only on this tomb's income" (p. 865).
