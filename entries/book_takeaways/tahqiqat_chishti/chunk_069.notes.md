# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 069 — pp. 752–761

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 752). Its heading is in the previous chunk (068). The first part describes the kashi work and inscriptions of a mosque whose Persian foundation text names "فدوی آل محمد صالح" and the year "در سال ہزار و ہفتاد و نہ ہجری" (p. 753). It then covers that mosque's imams and repairs, and a small old mosque beside it inside the Mochi gate, rebuilt by Haidar Shah (pp. 753–754). Next comes an untitled-in-heading section, "دوسری مسجد عبد اللہ خان نائب فدائی خان کوکہ", on a mosque inside the Taksali gate (pp. 754–755). Then "حال مسجد چینیان والی" (p. 755), which leads into the long life "حال جناب حضرت مولوی فخر الدین صاحب مرحوم" (pp. 755–759), the pir of the author's grandfather Ghulam Husain. The chunk then gives a short notice of Ghulam Husain and "تشریح عمارت مسجد چینی [OCR?] والہ" (pp. 759–760), and begins "تشریح عمارت موجودہ حال مسجد حسینی [OCR?]" (pp. 760–761), which breaks off mid-sentence in an inscription at the end of p. 761. Folio markers are printed but are not cited (brief rule).

  - At Pak Pattan, Fakhr-ud-Din sat as a dervish in a narrow, dark hujra "جسکا طول دو گز اور عرض ڈیڑہ گز تہا" (p. 756).
  - The sajjada nashin there at that time is named as "حضرت دیوان محمد سبحان". Fakhr-ud-Din went to meet him (p. 756). For the apple episode, see Legends.
  - The marble chronogram on Ghulam Husain's grave in Lahore calls him "گل بستان [OCR?] شیخ پاک پٹن" (p. 759). This is a literary reference only.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Mosque of Muhammad Salih** (its heading is in chunk 068):
  - The kashi panels carry divine names, Ayat al-Kursi, the 99 names, the names of the four caliphs, hadith and durud. Persian verses are copied from the north arch (pp. 752–753).
  - A large chenar[OCR?] tree in the courtyard has damaged the kashi, and two hemistichs are broken (p. 752).
  - The foundation text reads: "بحسن سعی فدوی آل محمد صالح در سال ہزار و ہفتاد و نہ ہجری صورت اتمام یافت" (p. 753).
  - The founder's house stood east of the mosque. A "شاد نامی دلال پشمینہ" now lives in it (p. 753).
  - Imams: Muhammad Salih appointed "حافظ عبد الرحیم قریشی" as imam and mutawalli. He was followed by his son Hafiz Bahla, then Hafiz Muhammad[OCR?], then Hafiz Rahim Bakhsh. The present imams are "غلام ہی اور غلام پیشین [OCR?]", who earn their living by other work (p. 754).
  - "بعہد مہاراجہ شیر سنگہ مسمی کریم اللہ دریائی باب [OCR?]" repaired it. The book cites the inscription on the door lintel (p. 754).
- **Small old mosque inside the Mochi gate**, adjoining the above: "مشفقی حیدر شاہ صاحب" rebuilt it in "سنہ [illegible] ہجری". It has three shops below, a well with a wheel ("چاہ چرخی دار"), a large dome, two half-domes, eight small domes and two small minarets. Inscriptions include "بانی مسجد حیدر شاہ سنہ [illegible] ہجری" and the fish/bird saying about the believer and the hypocrite in the mosque (pp. 753–754). Muhammad Salih[?] leads prayers here too. The text is "اس مسجد کی بہی امامت محمد صالح امام کرتا ہی". A court "اقرار نامہ" settles the shop rents (p. 754).
- **Mosque of Abdullah Khan, naib of Fidai Khan Koka**:
  - Location: "اندرون دروازہ ٹکسالی متصل شہر پناہ لاہور", four gaz inside the gate (p. 754).
  - Fabric: kashi work, three domes, three shops below. The north dome and half the middle arch have fallen. Two of four minarets remain (p. 754).
  - In Ranjit Singh's time, "شیر سنگہ نامی افسر توپخانہ قوم ہندو [OCR?]" seized it, "اسکا قبضہ ابہین تک چلا آتا ہی" (p. 754).
  - Neighbours: "چبوترہ خانقاہ و سائین [OCR?] عمارت شاہ سر [OCR?] احمد شاہ درانی", whose account is said to be under Begumpura. Government kothis nearby house "مسٹر ولیم ارمسٹرانگ صاحب" and "مسٹر ایچ شین [OCR?] صاحب ... سپرنٹنڈنٹ پولیس لاہور" (p. 754).
  - The author wishes the Muslims of Lahore would petition for its release and repair (pp. 754–755).
  - Founder, found "بدقت تمام": "عبد اللہ خان کابلی", naib muhtamim of the Badshahi mosque under Fidai Khan Koka. He built it during the building of the Badshahi mosque (p. 755).
- **Masjid Chiniyan wali** ("مسجد چینیان والی"; also "مسجد چینی [OCR?] والہ"):
  - Founder: "نواب سرافراز خان جو اجلہ امرائی عالمگیر سے تہا" (p. 755). The present imam Hafiz Sa'dullah Shah says the old east door bore "مسجد مبنیہ اقرار [OCR?] خان مرحوم" (p. 759).
  - Location: in "محلہ چابک سواران", wall-to-wall with the corner of "حویلی میانخان". Its three arches resemble those of the Wazir Khan mosque (p. 755).
  - Graves: Hafiz Ilahi Bakhsh, imam and father of Sa'dullah Shah; and in the adjoining enclosure the author's grandfather (pp. 755, 759).
  - Later history: there were once two shops, then three half-sunk ones seen "عرصہ تیس سال کا" ago, now wholly sunk. The east chini door is demolished, and a barber's shop and hammam stand there, sold by the imam (p. 759). The mosque was "غیر آباد" until the grandfather's burial. A margin note reads "۱۲۶۰ [OCR?]" (p. 759).
  - Fabric: double-storeyed under the roof ("زیر سقف دو درجہ"), with a staircase in the west wall and a hidden hujra for worship (p. 759).
- **Same mosque, under "تشریح عمارت موجودہ حال مسجد حسینی [OCR?]"** (pp. 760–761):
  - A hujra on the south houses "میان رکن شاہ بہاکری داماد شیخ امام بخش تعویزیہ". His son Muhammad Ghaus has been appointed imam "منجانب سعداللہ شاہ" (wording damaged) (p. 761).

- **Maulvi Fakhr-ud-Din** ("حال جناب حضرت مولوی فخر الدین صاحب مرحوم"; source named: "کتاب فخریہ", plus the author's own hearsay) (pp. 755–759):
  - Father and descent: son of "حضرت نظام الدین مرحوم اورنگ آبادی خلیفہ حضرت کلیم اللہ جہان آبادی", of the line of "شیخ شہاب الدین سہروردی". Born "[illegible: year]" at Aurangabad. Kalimullah sent fine cloth and fixed his name (p. 755).
  - Father's death and tomb: his father died "سنہ [illegible] ہجری" and passed the blessing to him "سینہ کو سینے لگایا". The father's mazar at Aurangabad is a ziyaratgah (p. 756).
  - Disciples: Nur Muhammad Mahl[OCR?] was the first to take bai'at, in "سنہ [illegible]". Others followed, including "مولوی غلام حسین" (p. 756).
  - Travels: he went to Ajmer and Shahjahanabad (p. 756). Later he taught at Nawab Ghazi-ud-Din Khan's madrasa outside the Ajmeri gate, and was known as "محبوب الٰہی" (p. 757).
  - Khalifas: a list of 30+ names is given, headed by "حضرت عبد اللہ شاہ صاحب" and including "مولوی نیاز احمد", whose khalifas are active "ہرات [OCR?] مین" (p. 757).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Muhammad Salih mosque: the imams declared before a court ("اقرار نامہ ... عدالت سی") that they claim nothing from the shop rents. The rent belongs to the "مالی [OCR?]". They get "ایک دو آنکو [OCR?]", and the rest goes on rope and buckets (p. 754).
- Taksali-gate mosque: it has been in the possession of a Hindu[OCR?] artillery officer since Ranjit Singh's time. The author proposes that the Muslims of Lahore petition for its release, believing "سرکار رعایا پرور" would grant it (pp. 754–755).
- Chiniyan mosque: the author's father ("والدم [OCR?] مولوی [OCR?] محمد [OCR?] صاحب") paid for repairs of the floor and fabric with "صدہا روپیہ اپنی گرہ اور زر چندہ" (p. 759). He also built a long sabil with a spout, and the author's maternal grandfather built a well with steps (p. 761). The imam sold the old door site (p. 759).
- Fakhr-ud-Din's way of life (pp. 757–758):
  - He refused grants from amirs and sultans, apart from "دو قطعہ" (wording damaged).
  - Gifts from the Delhi king's visit were distributed to faqirs.
  - He visited a sick sweeper himself.
  - Late in life he kept a doli and let others ride.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- At the age of five, Fakhr-ud-Din received five coffee beans from the Prophet's court in a dream. He found them at his bedside on waking. He shared two with his father, who had learned of it "بروز باطن" (p. 755).
- His father asked a majzub at Aurangabad not to turn the child majzub: "ہم نے اس سی بڑے بڑے کام لینی ہین" (p. 755).
- At Pak Pattan, Diwan Muhammad Subhan's cure needed apples that were unobtainable in that season. The Diwan was pleased "اس معاملہ سے" and became "معتقد حضرت کی کرامت". The book does not explain the mechanism legibly, and the passage straddles pp. 756–757.
- On the road, Fakhr-ud-Din said he felt anxiety from the Deccan. Two days later news came that Nawab Nizam-ul-Mulk had been killed (p. 757).
- At a sama' gathering, a murid's spiritual state was taken from him by Fakhr-ud-Din's rebuke (p. 758; heavily flagged).

## Citable passages

- "اکثر اوقات حضرت پیر علی گنج بخش صاحب رحمۃ اللہ علیہ کی خانقاہ عالیجاہ پر مشغول رہا کرتے": he was often occupied at the khanqah of Pir Ali Ganj Bakhsh (p. 756).
- "ہمیشہ سال بسال باراد [OCR?] آستانہ بوسی کیواسطی پاک پٹن مین حاضر ہوا کرتی تہی": every year he went to Pak Pattan to kiss the threshold (p. 756).
- "سبحان اللہ کار دنیا تمام خواب وخیال ہی": the author's comment on the seized Taksali-gate mosque (p. 754).
- "چہبیس سال برابر شب بیدار رہی شام کی وضو سی صلوۃ صبح ادا فرماتی رہی": he kept awake for twenty-six years, praying the dawn prayer on the evening's ablution (p. 759).

## Doubts

- Damage inventory: the chunk is heavily damaged throughout, with dense [OCR?] flags in the Persian inscriptions and chronograms (pp. 752–753, 758–761). Almost every year in the Fakhr-ud-Din life and on the Ghulam Husain tomb is "[illegible: year, superscript numerals]". No birth or death year for Fakhr-ud-Din, his father, Nur Muhammad or Ghulam Husain is legible.
- Legible dates are few:
  - "سال ہزار و ہفتاد و نہ ہجری" is written in words and unflagged (p. 753).
  - The margin "۱۲۶۰ [OCR?]" (p. 759), "سمت [OCR?] ۱۹ [OCR?]" (p. 760) and "سنہ ٧١٠ [OCR?]" on a Chiniyan mosque arch (p. 761) are all flagged. I have not reconciled any of them.
- data-darbar: the book's name is "پیر علی گنج بخش", not "داتا" or "ہجویری". I mapped it on "علی ... گنج بخش" matching the index's principal figure. A consolidator should confirm.
- shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar: the material is about Pak Pattan, mapped by toponym, plus the poetic "شیخ پاک پٹن" (p. 759). The saint is not named in this chunk, and the attachment is place-level.
- darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a: both "سکنہ" and "تونسہ" are flagged, and the sentence is garbled (p. 758). Weigh accordingly.
- samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh: this is a bare regnal-dating mention under the person rule. Nothing is said about the samadhi.
- Lahore bullet: the attachment is place-level. The material is the visit to "تمام مزارات" and a poetic line. The non-archive mosques and mohallas (Mochi gate, Taksali gate, Chabuk Sawaran) are kept under "Other saints…", not as toponym material.
- Masjid Chiniyan founder: the book gives "نواب سرافراز خان" (p. 755), while the imam reports a door inscription reading "اقرار [OCR?] خان" (p. 759). This is an internal discrepancy, with an OCR flag on the second name.
- "مسجد حسینی [OCR?]" (p. 760): I treat this as the same building as Masjid Chiniyan, because p. 761 speaks of "سعداللہ شاہ امام مسجد ہذا" and of "چاردیواری خانقاہ". That is a reading of the chunk, not a stated identity.
- Muhammad Salih "امام" at the Haidar Shah mosque (p. 754) versus Muhammad Salih the founder of the adjoining mosque (p. 753): the text later says "محمد صالح مرحوم". Whether these are the same person is not stated.
- "مولوی نیاز احمد" (p. 757) is not mapped. The index's wasif-ali-wasif row names a "Shah Niyaz Ahmad" only in its silsila column, not as principal figure, and nothing in the chunk links the two.
- No province-level material in this chunk.
