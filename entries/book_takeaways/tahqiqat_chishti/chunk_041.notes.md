# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 041 — pp. 449–459

The whole chunk continues one section about Hazrat Muhammad Ismail, "المشہور میان وڈا" (known as Mian Wadda) (p. 450). **Its heading is in the previous chunk (040)**, and no new section title appears between p. 449 and p. 459. In order, the chunk covers: his teaching by the river at "موضع لنگے[OCR?]" and the flight of his students (p. 449); his move to Lahore and his chilla at the mazar of Hujwiri (p. 449); his karamat and his shajra (pp. 450–451); the story of his pupil and teacher Hajji Muhammad (pp. 451–453); his childhood, his brothers and Muhammad Salih (pp. 453–455); the sajjada-nashin line and a counter-claim to the khanqah (pp. 455–456); the langar and teaching of the present family (pp. 456–457); a description of the khanqah, its mosque and the nearby tomb of Sayyid Mahmud (pp. 457–459); and the start of an account of a Sikh raja killed at that spot, which breaks off mid-sentence (p. 459). Mian Wadda is not an archive row. Three archive rows are touched: Data Ganj Bakhsh (Hujwiri) directly, and Bahauddin Zakariya and a "Makhdoom Burhan al-Din" as names in the shajra.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **data-darbar** ("مزار پر انوار حضرت پیر علی گنج بخش ہجویری رحمۃ اللہ علیہ"):
  - Sayyid Mahmud Sahib advised Mian Wadda, who was unhappy in Lahore and wanted to leave, to keep "ایک چلہ یعنی چالیس روز" (a chilla, that is, forty days) "معتکف" (in retreat) at this mazar. He was told that "[illegible] کلی عطا ہو گی" would be granted (p. 449).
  - "آپ نے و سیا ہی کیا پھر تو صفای کلی حاصل ہو گئی" (he did so, and then he gained complete purity). Students then gathered round him (p. 449).
- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** ("محمد و م بہاء الدین ذکریا ملتانی"):
  - Named in Mian Wadda's shajra (spiritual chain) as the murshid of "محمد و [illegible] الدین متقی[OCR?]", and as the murid of "حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین عمر سہروردی" (p. 450).
- **langer-makhdoom** ("حضرت مخدوم برہان الدین"):
  - Named in the same shajra: Mian Wadda's murshid was "مخدوم شیخ عبدالکریم", whose murshid was "محمد و طیب[OCR?]", whose murshid was "حضرت مخدوم برہان الدین", whose murshid was "محمد و [illegible] الدین متقی[OCR?]" (p. 450).
  - Nothing else is said about him: no place, tomb or date (p. 450).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Mian Wadda**:
  - In a famine, an old woman brought one loaf. It passed from student to student and came back to him. Pleased, he said they were now free of bodily ties and could fly like birds, and "تم سب کے سب اڑ جاؤ" (all of you fly away). They all flew to their homes and became "عمارف[OCR?] کامل" (perfect gnostics[OCR?]) (p. 449).
  - "تین سو[OCR?] ساٹھہ[OCR?]" (three hundred and sixty[OCR?]) students became hafiz and flew to their homelands (p. 454). See Doubts on the number.
  - A man asked to learn the Quran in a day, because his wife, a hafiz, refused him. After prayer Mian Wadda cast his glance to the right, and everyone there became "حافظ قرآن", "حافظ [illegible]" or "ناظرہ خوان". He said this faiz would continue after him, and to this day a slow-witted person takes "کہاس[OCR?]" (grass[OCR?]) from his grave and the Quran comes to him quickly (p. 450).
  - Hajji Muhammad received an ilham to keep his promise. Mian Wadda embraced him, and he gained much faiz (p. 452).
  - A faqir between Parvezabad and Ganj Pura drew the baraka out of passing holy men by meeting their eyes, and drained Hajji Muhammad. Mian Wadda made him give it back and said he would have no children and his grave would be moved. "آخر کار ایسا ہی ہوا" (in the end it happened so) (p. 452). The subject of the reburial that follows (pp. 452–453) is ambiguous; see Doubts.
  - As a child he disputed a word's pronunciation with his teacher. The teacher dreamed that an angel showed him the "لوح محفوظ", which proved the boy right, and he told the parents "یہ لڑکا تمہارا صاحب کمال اور ولی کامل ہے" (your boy is accomplished and a perfect wali) (p. 453).
  - At "موضع لنگے[OCR?]", no crowd could lift a new beam for a mosque. He struck it with his staff, which went into it, and the beam rose by itself. "اب تک اوس شہتیر میں شرب[OCR?] عصا کا سوراخ موجود ہے" (the hole from the staff's blow is still in the beam) (p. 454).
  - He knew in advance that Muhammad Salih was coming, saying "آج ہمارا مالک ... یہاں آوہ[OCR?] لگا" (p. 455).
  - A damaged story on p. 455: something about prayer, repentance and the Quran (p. 455).
  - Through Ahmad Din: a merchant of Delhi brought his sick daughter. Something cried "میں حل گیا" (I am burnt) and the girl was cured. Mian Wadda explained that had he read the whole name, all "[OCR?]" in it would have burnt. The passage is heavily garbled (p. 457).
- **Muhammad Khalil**: at Multan, Mian Wadda's prayer drew him into ecstasy. He set off for Lahore and the mosque moved with him. "تمام شہر میں غلغلہ پڑ گیا کہ ایک درویش لاہوری مسجد کو اپنے ساتھہ لیئے جاتا ہے" (an uproar went through the city that a Lahori dervish was taking the mosque with him). The "زاہدان[OCR?] ملتان[OCR?]" (ascetics of Multan) could not stop it. A famous man of Multan learned by muraqaba whose brother Khalil was, and petitioned Mian Wadda inwardly, and the mosque was left there (p. 454).
- **Hajji Muhammad's reburial**: the night after the move, something happened to his grave by Mian Wadda's. The account is garbled. People took it as a sign of his tie to Mian Wadda (p. 453).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author's stance on the ownership dispute**: "لیکن کمترین کو ان خر خشون[OCR?] سے کیا حاصل اگر انکو کچھہ شکایت ہو گے تو دروازہ عدالت" (but what does this humble one gain from these quarrels; if they have a complaint, the court's door is open) (pp. 456–457).
- **The author's praise of the present family** (p. 456):
  - Ahmad Din is "نہایت خلیق کم گو" (most courteous, of few words), and Sharaf al-Din was "نہایت شریف".
  - He supports this with the verse "کسے راکہ بابا فلاطون بود ..." (one whose father is a Plato).
  - He also says "اکثر و سائر نواح خانقا" (most people around the khanqah) state the old possession by the sajjada nashins. A clause with "لایق اعتماد نہین" (not trustworthy) follows, but its subject is damaged.
- **The author's reserve on the sajjada claim**: he says reliable persons do not confirm it (p. 450).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is very heavy, especially pp. 451–455, 457 and 459, where whole sentences are strings of `[OCR?]` tokens. Every quotation above keeps its flags. Folio markers (441, 442, 444, 445, 446, 448, 451, 452) are printed but are not cited, per the brief. There is a `[margin]` note on p. 456 that is illegible.
- **Section heading in chunk 040.** Mian Wadda's section begins in the previous chunk. This chunk gives no title for it.
