# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 061 — pp. 674–682

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 674) with the tail of a description of a shivala lying south of a takia (a marble image of Shivji, a Kali Devi shrine, a resident woman), whose heading is in the previous chunk. It then runs through these titled sections in order: "مکان چوہڑیان" (p. 674); "حال تکیہ پٹ رنگان" (p. 674); "احوال مزار حضرت پیر مکی[OCR?] صاحب" (pp. 674–675); "حال مقبرہ حضرت شاہ دمی[OCR?] شاہ" in the Company Bagh (pp. 675–676); "باغیچہ محمد شاہ کمیدان سردار بہادر کا" (p. 676); "ٹہاکر[OCR?] دوارہ باوا ہر جو داس" outside the Mori gate (pp. 676–677); "حال مشریہ[OCR?] اللہ", a Shiva-linga dome with samadhs (p. 677); "مکان بہلی[OCR?] صاحب" beside the railway station enclosure (p. 677); "مکان گرنتہہ[OCR?] صاحب" with the samadh of Diwan Bhawani Das and the Nag Devta (pp. 677–678); "حال ناگ دیوتا" (pp. 678–679); and "حال گورو[OCR?] سری چند" (pp. 679–682), a long account of Sri Chand son of Baba Nanak, the list of the ten Sikh gurus, karamat stories set in Lahore before Aurangzeb, and the chela lines of the "ٹہلی صاحب" seat, which breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk. ## Practices, institutions, economy

- Tahli Sahib / Sri Chand samadh: the mahant's house was built a full hundred years ago ("سو برس کامل"); lamps lit daily and daily "سنگرانت[OCR?]", "یعنی حلوا تقسیم" (p. 679).
- Present gaddi-nashin: "با واحمنا[OCR?] داس مہنت" (p. 679). The earlier samadh of Sri Chand was small; in "شمست[OCR?] ۱۸۹۸[OCR?] اٹہارہ اٹہانوین" [samvat 1898] Jamna[OCR?] Das built the grand samadh with a well (p. 679).
- The tahli tree was cut by the railway sahibs but has become green again; the place is known as "ٹہلی[OCR?] صاحب" after it (p. 679).
- Land: under Ranjit Singh a daily rupee[OCR?] and a well with land were revenue-free; that land has now gone to the railway, leaving "صرف اٹہارہ کنال" north of the enclosure (p. 679).
- Later grants (p. 682, damaged): "موضع [OCR?] [illegible] ایک من", "موضع کہووال[OCR?]".
- Sikhs are of two kinds: the descendants ("سیدی[OCR?]" [Bedi]) and the chelas; the Bedis are held as elders, with a role at marriage, death and birth (damaged) (p. 682). A rite: a Sikh brings an offering before the guru's samadh and the guru cuts five hairs with scissors ("پانچ بال کتر لیا ہے") (p. 682).
- Pir Makki urs, mela, and revenue-free land: see that row (p. 675). Shah Dami Shah urs and Thursday gatherings (pp. 675–676).
- Company Bagh built under English rule at a cost of about a lakh of rupees (p. 675).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Nag Devta: serpents once troubled people until the road was closed; a man took to puja; a large snake appeared in his dream, said "مین بادشاہ[OCR?] ہوں", and promised no harm if a place were made for them; whoever worships there will get what he asks of God (p. 678).
- Sri Chand by karamat drew back the three-year-old Dharam Chand as Lakhmi Das departed toward the sky (p. 679).
- Sri Chand's quilt ("گوڈر[OCR?]") could not be lifted onto the royal elephant; he came to Lahore on foot (p. 680).
- He turned a bitter well in Lahore sweet: "تیرا بالی[OCR?] کہا راتشیریں[OCR?] ہو حائیگا"; the well is said still to exist within the samadh (p. 680).
- The qazi's goat: slaughtered without breaking a bone, revived when Bhai Kamliya poured the well's water on it, but with three legs, because the qazi had eaten the fourth (p. 681).
- The killer mare of the royal stable: Sri Chand said she had been the disobedient wife of a certain Sikh, reborn in this form; she bowed at the guru's feet (p. 681).

## Doubts

- Damage inventory: `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` on most content words in every page; pp. 676 (Shah Dami Shah continuation, Muhammad Shah's garden) and 682 (grants, chela lines, Sikh rites) are largely unreadable, and only fragments are reported. Every quoted word above carries its flag.
- **peer-makki**: mapped by name ("حضرت پیر مکی[OCR?] صاحب"). The book places the grave in the south-east corner of the Company Bagh near the Taksali gate, while the index row has Bhati Gate area (near Data Darbar) and principal figure Syed Azizuddin; the book gives no personal name and itself says his history is unknown, offering only a speculative identification with Sa'd-ud-Din. Consolidator should check the identity.
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri**: rests on one flagged "ابو المعالی[OCR?]" in a near-illegible line (p. 676); weak.
- **data-darbar**: name forms are heavily garbled ("سر[OCR?] کنجش[OCR?] بجو بری[OCR?]", "وا نا گنج بخش[OCR?]"); readings in brackets are mine.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**: person-rule mapping from Ranjit Singh named as patron/ruler; nothing on the samadhi itself.
- Guru Nanak (18 rows), Guru Arjan Dev (3), Guru Hargobind (6), Guru Ram Das, Guru Gobind Singh: person-level attachments, not site-specific; for all but Nanak the evidence is a bare name in a list. The book says Ram Das was "خلف گورو امرداس[OCR?]" (p. 680); carried as printed.
- "حاجی شاہ المعروف چراغ شاہ", mujawir of Shah Dami Shah (p. 675), is not mapped to **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh**; this is a living keeper, not that saint.
- "گورو دہ مد[OCR?] صاحب" (p. 680), the guru summoned by Aurangzeb, cannot be identified from the garbled text.
- Dates as printed, unverified: "۱۸۹۸[OCR?]" samvat (p. 679); "۱۲[OCR?] ربیع الثانی سنہ[OCR?] [illegible] ہجری" (p. 675); Shah Dami Shah's death date "نزین[OCR?] شمشت[OCR?] شبان[OCR?]" is unreadable (p. 675).
- Sri Chand's village "موضع با اُلہ[OCR?] ملح[OCR?] گورو دا سیورہ[OCR?]" (p. 680): heavily flagged; no province mapping made.
- Ruling (a): no Lahore-in-general material in this chunk; all places are named takias, gardens, gates and samadhs, so no 35-id Lahore bullet. No province-level material.
