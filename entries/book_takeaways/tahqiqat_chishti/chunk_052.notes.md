# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 052 — pp. 576–588

The chunk falls inside the book's long Sikh-history excursus, and every page carries a margin running head rather than a new "فصل" heading. It opens mid-list of the Sikh misls, from the 4th (آہلوالیہ) to about the 12th (pp. 576–577), under the margin head "حال مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ". It then gives "مختصر حال سکھ خاندان", the ancestry of Ranjit Singh's family from "کالو" down to Charat Singh and Mahan Singh (pp. 577–579). Next comes "مختصر حال و واقعات مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ" (p. 579), his reign to the Jammu and Kangra episodes (pp. 579–581); p. 581 is almost entirely illegible. After that the book gives short accounts of Ranjit Singh's courtiers: the Jammu rajas Gulab Singh, Dhian Singh and Suchet Singh, Jamadar Khushal Singh, Diwan Dina Nath (pp. 582–583), and "حال خاندان فقیر صاحبان" (pp. 584–586). Last comes "ہمہ احوال بعد وفات مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ [illegible] عملداری صاحبان انگریزی" (p. 586): Kharak Singh, Chet Singh's murder, Nau Nihal Singh's death and the coming of Sher Singh (pp. 586–588). The chunk breaks off mid-sentence at p. 588. No shrine, tomb or saint with an archive row has a section here. The mappings are by person (Ranjit Singh → his samadhi row), by toponym (Lahore, Multan, Gujranwala, Uch Sharif) and by province (Punjab).

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE punjab (book's word: "پنجاب"; refers to: the Sikh period, Ranjit Singh's reign and after his death, as the book states):
  - In "اٹھارہ سو پانچ[OCR?]", "جسونت راؤ ہولکر اور … امیر خان پنجاب کو آدمیں[OCR?]". The verb is garbled, so whether they "came to Punjab" is not certain (p. 580).
  - On Ranjit Singh's death, Kharak Singh sat on "کرسی حکومت پنجاب" (the seat of government of Punjab) (p. 586).
  - The Faqir family's mohalla is called "قطب یونان[OCR?]" by "لاہور میں تمام لوگ بلکہ پنجاب" (all people in Lahore, indeed Punjab) (p. 585).
  - Dhian Singh's promise to Sher Singh: "انشاء اللہ تعالیٰ عنقریب آپ حاکم پنجاب ہو نگے" (God willing, you will soon be ruler of Punjab) (p. 588).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Ranjit Singh's ancestors traded as shopkeepers: "نمک اور تمباکو بیچنا فروخت" (selling salt and tobacco) in the villages (p. 578).
- Court offices and pay:
  - Dhian Singh's office "دیوان [illegible]" controlled entry to the darbar (p. 582).
  - Starting pay was "ایک روپیہ یومیہ" (one rupee a day) in the cavalry (p. 582).
- Land and money:
  - Gulab Singh bought Kashmir for "پچھتر لاکھ روپیہ" (p. 582).
  - British pensions and annuities went to Dina Nath's heirs: "[illegible] ہزار روپیہ سالیانہ"; "ایک لاکھ روپیہ" on deposit paying a monthly sum (pp. 583–584).
  - A jagir "سالیانہ[OCR?]" goes to a raja's family, in a heavily damaged passage (p. 583).

- The Faqir family ran a "مدرسہ" (p. 585).
