# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 017 — pp. 193–201

This chunk is a run of short site entries, most of them headed "متعلقہ گورستان میانی" / "متعلق قبرستان میانی" / "متعلقہ زمین میانی" (belonging to the Miani graveyard / Miani land). **It opens mid-section (p. 193): the heading of the first subject is in the previous chunk (016).** The fragment describes a mosque and graves, among them the grave of "ملک علی کوتوال" (Malik Ali Kotwal), with two versions of how he and his family came to be killed under Akbar (pp. 193–195). Then follow, in order as titled: "تکیہ فقیر ولایتی" (p. 195); "باغیچہ رانی گل حکیم[OCR?] صاحبہ" (pp. 195–197); "گنبدی چشمہ[OCR?] متعلقہ گورستان میانی" (p. 197); "قبور و قایہ[OCR?] ہامون شاہ[OCR?]" (p. 198); "مقبرہ پیر زمردی[OCR?] صاحب" (p. 198); "قبر مولوی حاجی لاہوری" (p. 198); "قبر زوجہ الا روضہ[OCR?] فراسین[OCR?]" (p. 198); "حال چار دیواری قبر با بندی طوالف" (p. 199); "حال مسجد [illegible] میاں محمد سلطان" (p. 199); "حال قبر نواب سعادت یار خان مرحوم والی معزول بہاولپور[OCR?]" (pp. 199–200); "حال مکان حاجی نور حما[OCR?] صاحب مرحوم" (pp. 200–201); "حال قبر حضرت محمد[OCR?] عارف[OCR?] چشتی المشہور میاں وڈا" (p. 201); and "حال قبر نظام شاہ و مجذوب[OCR?]", which breaks off at the end of p. 201. Damage is very heavy throughout; p. 200 in particular is mostly `[illegible]`. Only one archive row is touched by name (and that by name-match), plus one Lahore-in-general bullet.

In the mosque courtyard are three graves: "قبر غربی ملک علی کوتوال کے اور دو تیر[OCR?] اوسکے بھائی اور بیٹی کی" — the western grave is Malik Ali Kotwal's, the other two his brother's and daughter's (p. 193). ## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Graveyard land cultivated (p. 193).** "گور کن لوگ یہاں کہتے ہیں کہ سردار خان مثنی[OCR?] ... زمین کو ... کاشت کرا لی ہے ۔ مگر فی الاصل یہ زمین متعلقہ گورستان مہمانی[OCR?] ہے" — the gravediggers say Sardar Khan [OCR?] had this land cultivated, but in origin it belongs to the graveyard "Mehmani[OCR?]" (p. 193).
- **The gravediggers as the author's source.** The first account of Malik Ali "بالا ... گورکن کے دریافت ہوا تھا", was learned from the gravedigger; then "اب کتاب تحقیقات چشتی[OCR?] سے حال اسکا اور[OCR?] معلوم ہوا" (p. 194). See Doubts.
- **Rani Gul Begum's garden: plan and fittings (pp. 195–197).** Wooden railing on the north, mud wall on the south; a brick room in the north-west corner with a long stable ("طویلہ") (p. 195); a well with a waterwheel ("چاہ ... چرخ"), a cistern and bath-house, a large plastered platform with flower-pots, a painted two-storey gateway on the west with a balakhana (p. 195); a tablet and verse chronograms (p. 196); a large plastered, painted dalan with a central dome and four domes around (p. 197).
- **Garden building date, as printed.** In the first chronogram the margin carries "۱۲ ۴۳[OCR?]" (p. 196); a second "ایضاً" carries margin "۱۹[OCR?]" (p. 196). A line names the masonry: "بدست کاری و معماری علام[OCR?]" (p. 196). A measurement "مساحہ ۱۳ ۔ ۱۹ ۔ ۱[OCR?]" and a line naming "خواہر شاہ" are on the mihrab inscription (p. 196).
- **An old mosque in the garden repaired** — "ایک مسجد قدیمی تہی[OCR?] ... انکی مرمت ... کرائی" (p. 196); seven graves on a platform; an order of "حکم کریم اللہ" and the rani with margin "۶۲[OCR?]" (p. 196, too damaged to state what was ordered).
- **Rani Gul Begum's tomb.** In the middle of the dalan "قبر رانی گل بیگم"; a black-stone platform with marble lines, "دو گز عرض میں تین گز طول دو فٹ ارتفاع" (p. 197). The grave was made in her lifetime ("پہلے بجین[OCR?] حیات بنوایا تہا"), and "اب یہ قبر سردار خانصاحب[OCR?] بنوائی ہے" (p. 197).
- **Custody of the garden.** "ہمیشہ دروازہ بند رہتا ہے" — the gate is always closed; the key ("کٹہیہ[OCR?]") stays with the gardener (p. 197). Four gardeners are servants of the Rani: "ایک یا مان[OCR?] دوسرا شمیر[OCR?] تیسرا[OCR?] محمد دین چوتھا[OCR?] جیم بخش[OCR?]" (p. 197). A door in the north wall "ہمیشہ بند ہے" (p. 197).
- **Vows and offerings at Pir Zumurrdi[OCR?] (p. 198).** "جو کوئی آکر منت مانے ہے کہ اگر فلاں کام میرا ہو جاوے تو میں ... کی مدر دوں[OCR?]" — whoever makes a vow that if such-and-such a task is done he will give an offering; sweet rotis ("شیریں روتیاں[OCR?]") are brought; women come ("اکہر[OCR?] وں[OCR?] یہاں آتی ہیں"); "عرس ان حضرت کو" is mentioned in a broken line (p. 198).
- **Mian Muhammad Sultan's mosque (p. 199).** White lime plaster, three-arched, a central dome with smaller domes and four corner turrets; a sabil "واسطہ پانی پینے[OCR?]"; inscriptions "لا الہ الا اللہ[OCR?] اللہ محمد الرسول اللہ" over the middle mihrab, a Persian couplet, and "ابو بکر و عمر و عثمان و حیدر" (p. 199). The marble sabil reads "فی سبیل اللہ ... محمد سلطان ٹھیکہ دار" with "شنبہ[OCR?] ۱۲[OCR?]۸[OCR?]۹[OCR?] ھ[OCR?]" (p. 199).
- **Payment to mujawirs.** When Muhammad Sultan comes, "ایک رویہ مجاوران کو دیجاتا ہے" — one rupee [as printed "رویہ"] is given to the mujawirs (p. 199).
- **Haji Nur's house (pp. 200–201).** Arched gateway in the south wall, four mihrabs "بطور مصلا[OCR?]", a brick platform "پچیس[OCR?] فٹ طول اور بیس[OCR?] فٹ عرض" next to a canal ("متصل نہر"), jamun and pipal trees, a well and bath-house "احمد بخش و محمد بخش[OCR?]" are named in connection with (p. 201).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Malik Ali and Abdullah, version 1 (the gravediggers', pp. 193–194).** Abdullah would not salute Akbar; the emperor had a low doorway built in the Diwan-e Khas so that he would bow and could be said to have prostrated; Abdullah put his feet in first and said he would not salute; Akbar had him chained and handed to "ملک علی کوتوال لاہور[OCR?]" (p. 193). The kotwal's dealings with him in prison are garbled (a "لڑکی", daughter, is mentioned) (pp. 193–194). Abdullah was killed; Akbar then ordered "کوتوال و معہ تمام عیال و المعال[OCR?] اوسکے قتل کر دیا" — the kotwal killed with all his family, and ordered tombs built for them (p. 194).
- **Version 2 ("from the book Tahqiqat-e Chishti[OCR?]", pp. 194–195).** Akbar wrote to Malik Ali to bring Hazrat Husain in chains; "قدرت الہی سے" the chain broke ("خبر میں بار ٹوٹ گیا", flagged) (p. 194). Akbar ordered Malik Ali to report Abdullah's words at the gallows ("بوقت دار کشی[OCR?]") letter for letter; Malik Ali did so; Akbar was angered and Malik Ali was killed; "حضرت حسین دہلی کو گئے" (p. 195).
- **Haji Nur's loan to Shahjahan (p. 200).** Shahjahan needed "چار کڑور[OCR?] روپیہ" and could find no lender in the city; Haji Nur lent it; when it was to be repaid he said "اب یہ روپیہ تیرا حلال ہے مجھ پر حرام" — this money is now lawful for you, forbidden to me (p. 200).
- **Mian Wadda's voice and appearance (p. 201).** "گاہ گاہ لوگوں کو قبر سے آواز آتی ہے کہ [illegible] مجھ سے بچ کے جاو" — at times people hear a voice from the grave; "اکثر لوگوں نے ان کو چشم ظاہر دیکھیا[OCR?] بھی ہے" — many have seen him with their own eyes, "جمیل اور سفید لباس میں" (p. 201).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author on the courtesan's grave (p. 199):** he is astonished ("میں حیران ہوں") that courtesans deceive people in life and do not stop deceiving after it; a passer-by seeing the walled takia and painted grave thinks "کسی بزرگ کی ہوگی", but on inquiry the truth comes out that "یہ کسی کنجری کی قبر ہے" (p. 199).
- **The author weighs two sources on Malik Ali:** the gravedigger's oral account, then a written one (pp. 194–195), and cross-refers to his own entry on Hazrat Husain's khanqah (p. 195).

## Doubts

- **Opens mid-section.** P. 193 begins inside an entry whose heading is in chunk 016; the subject (a mosque with Malik Ali Kotwal's grave and a second mosque) cannot be named from this chunk alone.
- **Damage inventory (ruling (b)).** Several hundred `[OCR?]` and many `[illegible]` flags; pp. 194, 196 and 200 are largely unreadable; all verse on pp. 196–197 is heavily flagged. Directional words ("رویہ" vs "یہ" / "ویہ") are often broken, so compass positions above are approximate where flagged. Every quotation above keeps its flags.
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri is a name-match.** The book says "حضرت شیخ طاہر", never "Bandagi" or "Qadri"; the match rests on the name and the Miani-graveyard headings. **shrine-at-odero-lal-udero-lal-teerath-asthan** also has "Sheikh Tahir" as a principal figure (Sindh); not mapped there, but the consolidator should weigh the name match.
- **Lahore bullet is place-level**, weigh accordingly; its material is a city title ("دارالسلطنتہ") inside a heavily flagged line and a passing mention of Shahjahan staying in Lahore.
- **Miani graveyard not mapped as a toponym.** Most headings say "متعلقہ گورستان/قبرستان/زمین میانی", but under the brief they only locate non-archive tombs. The one statement about the graveyard's own land (p. 193) spells it "مہمانی[OCR?]". The four Miani rows (**ghazi-ilm-din-shaheed**, **khawaja-feroz-ud-din-gharib-nawaz**, **tahir-bandagi-qadri**, **wasif-ali-wasif**) could take that p. 193 statement if a human confirms the reading; left for the consolidator.
- **"حضرت حسین" not mapped to madho-lal-hussain.** The chunk never says "Shah", "Madho Lal" or gives a site; identifying him would be outside knowledge. The book's own cross-reference ("حضرت حسین کے خانقاہ کے حال", p. 195) should settle it when that section is noted.
- **"میاں [illegible] میر تاجی[OCR?]" (p. 200) not mapped to shrine-of-mian-mir.** The gap is illegible and must not be filled.
- **Bahawalpur not mapped.** "بہاولپور[OCR?]" appears only in Nawab Saadat Yar Khan's title and in a list of his people (pp. 199–200), not as material about the place; the word is flagged.
- **The book cites "کتاب تحقیقات چشتی[OCR?]" as a source inside itself (p. 194).** Either the transcription has mis-read another title or the text cites a version of itself; a human should check the PDF.
