# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 002 — pp. 17–32

This chunk is the book's front matter, not yet its shrine survey. It opens mid-narrative (p. 17) on a family of Lahore maulvis, the marriage of a daughter to a boy of unknown origin, and a maulvi's death and mosque tomb (pp. 17–19). It then gives the life of Maulvi Ghulam Husain, first named Ghulam Ali: his bai'at to Hazrat Fakhr-ud-Din, son of Khwaja Nizam-ud-Din Aurangabadi, his stay at Ajmer, and his return to Lahore (pp. 19–21). Next comes the service of the author's father, Maulvi Ahmad Bakhsh, at a Maharaja's darbar and the villages he was granted (pp. 21–23), and a section titled "حلیہ مبارک حضرت مولوی غلام حسین کا" on Ghulam Husain's appearance, habits, death, burial at "مسجد چینی والی [OCR?]" and chronograms (pp. 23–26). After that comes a section titled "حال مؤلف کتابِ ہذا عفی عنہ" on the author, his books and the commissioner's order to write this survey (pp. 27–29). The chunk closes with the book's sources, its questionnaire of what is recorded for each site (pp. 29–31), Mufti Ghulam Sarwar's share in the material (pp. 31–32), and the author's residence and murshid (p. 32). The damage is very heavy throughout.

- At midnight Ghulam Husain recited loudly. "تمام اہل محلہ بیدار" (the whole mohalla woke), and in each heart a longing for God arose (p. 24), as the author relates.
- After his death something [illegible] happened to his body ("پائی [OCR?] مبارک [illegible]"), and those present were astonished. A maulvi said "یہ معاملہ [illegible] حضرت [illegible] شبلی [OCR?] پر بھی ہوا تھا" (pp. 25–26).
- The renaming to "غلام حسین" is presented as foreknowledge: Fakhr-ud-Din's own death chronogram yields "[illegible] حسین" (p. 20).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author places the book as "فی الاصل تواریخِ [OCR?] اولیا", a history of saints at root, despite its survey of buildings (p. 29).
- He records that material went both ways between this book and Mufti Ghulam Sarwar's own book (pp. 31–32).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory**: `[illegible]` runs through almost every line, and `[OCR?]` sits on most names, places and all numerals in this chunk. Every quotation above keeps its flags. Folio markers appear on most pages but are not cited (book rule). Pages 23 and 30 carry no folio line.
- **Lahore bullet**: the attachment is place-level, weigh accordingly. The material is the survey's commission and scope, not any one site.
- **Multan bullet**: the attachment is place-level. The event (a conquest of Multan) is about the city, and its agent is illegible or unnamed. A consolidator should weigh whether a conquest notice belongs to the Multan rows at all.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar is person-level**. The chunk mentions no shrine, tomb or Pakpattan, only a descent and adoption claim for the author's family (p. 24). The adopted man's name is OCR-flagged ("مرزا عنایت اللہ بیگ [OCR?]").
- **The unnamed "مہاراج" / "مہاراجہ صاحب" / "سرکار"** (pp. 22, 27) is never named in this chunk, so it is not mapped to **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**. Do not identify him from general knowledge.
- **"سید جلال بخاری [OCR?]"** (p. 25) is a bare, OCR-flagged name. It could match more than one archive row whose principal figure is a Sayyid Jalal(uddin) Bukhari, and nothing places him, so it is left unmapped.
- **"کشف المحجوب"** (p. 29) appears in the source list as a book title only. No author is named, so it is not mapped to **data-darbar** (identifying its author would be general knowledge).
- **Possible pen-name conflict**: the author is "المتخلص حسینی [OCR?]" (p. 27), yet his pen name in poetry is said to be "چشتی" (p. 24).
- **Ghulam Husain's names**: one chronogram reads "غلام حسنین [OCR?]" (p. 26), against "غلام حسین" elsewhere.
