# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 027 — pp. 303–312

The chunk opens mid-section, inside the account of the tomb of Hazrat Mian Mir. **The section heading is in an earlier chunk.** Pp. 303–305 continue that account with a list of sajjada nashins, the graves around the tomb, a detailed description of the marble and brick tomb with its chronogram ("قطعہ تاریخ", p. 303), its mirror work and grave coverings, the shop, well and khadims' graves in the precinct, and the urs mela and the Wednesday melas of the rainy season. Pp. 306–310 give Mian Mir's last illness, death and burial, much of it attributed to Dara Shikoh's writing, then his birth and siblings, Jahangir's and Shah Jahan's meetings with him (with two Persian letters headed "قصہ اول" and "عریضہ دوم", pp. 308–309), and the story of Nadira Begum and the baradari outside the enclosure wall. A new, badly damaged heading on p. 310 introduces the tomb of "حضرت ملا شاہ" and the garden that became "موضع میا میر". It runs to the end of the chunk (pp. 310–312) as a survey of the garden wall, its burjis and the kothas and residents inside, and it breaks off mid-sentence. One archive row is mapped: **shrine-of-mian-mir**.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Mian Mir — tomb decoration**:
  - Marble with flower work and black-stone inlay ("گلکاری سنگ سیاہ"), with inscribed arched slabs and four marble columns at the platform corners (p. 303).
  - "کار کاشی" under the arches and red lattices ("پنجرہ") in the arches (p. 303).
  - "لفظ اللہ بخط عربی" on the east and west (p. 303).
  - Mirror work ("کار صورت آئینہ") in the dome (p. 304).
- **Mian Mir — grave furnishings**:
  - Coverings: "گلبدن [OCR?] دارائی کمخواب اطلس [illegible] گوٹہ [OCR?] کناری دار" (p. 304).
  - A cloth fixed to the ceiling, "ایک ہانڈی بلوری کلاں" (a large crystal lamp) and wax candles "کبہی ہی کبہی نہ" (sometimes, sometimes not) (p. 304).
  - A garbled phrase "سیر عرس بہی آٹہہ یوم [OCR?]" (p. 304).
- **Mian Mir — shop rent to the sajjada nashin**: a shop in the outer wall, rent "سجادہ نشین کو دیا" (p. 304).
- **Mian Mir — urs mela (p. 305)**:
  - There is a yearly urs mela. Most people spend the night there, with "تمام رات راگ ناچ رنگ".
  - "صدہا روپیہ نذر کا جمع ہوتا ہی" (hundreds of rupees of offerings are collected).
  - Courtesans perform mujra there without fee, which they call "مجرا جوگی".
  - The devout spend the night in worship, while "ہزار ہا عیاش" waste money and drink ("می نوشی [OCR?]").
  - "اب بباعث اجرای ریل و قریب چہاونی [OCR?] میا میر" (now, because of the railway and the nearby Mian Mir cantonment[OCR?]) there are huge crowds.
  - The next day there is a qawwali majlis from morning to evening, and food is cooked in many places.
- **Mian Mir — rainy-season melas (p. 305)**: in Sawan and "بہادہون [OCR?]" a mela is held at the mazar every Wednesday night ("ہر ایک بدہ کی شب") for two months. Thousands attend, and these melas are no smaller than the urs. "اوس رات دروازہ شہر بہی کہلا رہتا ہی" (that night the city gate also stays open).
- **Mian Mir — the maidan (p. 305)**: there was formerly a large ground by the tomb, and "میدان میا میر" was used proverbially. It has shrunk because of "سٹرک ریلوی ملتان [OCR?] و کوٹہی گین [OCR?] صاحب وغیرہ".
- **Baradari and tank (p. 310)**:
  - Formerly a tank surrounded the baradari. The tank has collapsed, and its bricks were dug up by "محمد سلطان ٹہیکہ دار" when "جہا ولی [OCR?] میا میر" was built.
  - "دو سو بچاس [OCR?] روپیہ" (two hundred and fifty[OCR?] rupees) were given as the price to "محبوب شاہ سجادہ نشین حضرت میا میر" by "[illegible] صاحب بہادر [OCR?] یشی [OCR?] کمشنر لاہور".

  - Wall height "ساڑہی چار [OCR?] گز", length "ایک سو [OCR?] میسکی [OCR?] گز", width "اکہتو [OCR?]" (p. 310).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Mian Mir's birth**: a "ہاتف غیب" answered his mother's prayer after tahajjud. Attributed by Dara Shikoh to Mian Mir himself (p. 307).
- **Dara Shikoh's dream**: Dara Shikoh saw Mian Mir's death in a dream at Akbarabad, and four days later the news confirmed it (p. 307).
- **Dara Shikoh's cure**: Dara Shikoh was ill and physicians had given up. Mian Mir blew on water and gave it to him, and he was cured "فی المحال [OCR?]". This is told in Dara Shikoh's voice (p. 309).
- **Nadira Begum**:
  - As a child she brought Mian Mir water for wudu at Zuhr. When she was "گیارہ [OCR?] برس", he told her not to come any more.
  - Fearing she had erred, she prayed to be covered ("پردہ پوس"), and she died at midnight.
  - Mian Mir had her buried there, and the baradari is linked to her.
  - Related "زبانی محبوب شاہ [OCR?] و سجادہ نشین" (pp. 309–310).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author notes the change in the tomb's distance from Lahore, "آدہ کوس" (half a kos) then against "مین [OCR?] کوس" now, and remarks that Darapur and Alam Ganj can no longer be traced (p. 307).
- The author reports conflicting ages for Mian Mir without choosing between them (p. 306).
- The author judges the urs night harshly: worship by the devout against "ہزار ہا عیاش" who squander money (p. 305).

- "میا میر سر دفتر عارفان ٭ کہ خاک درش رشک اکسیر شد" — "Mian Mir, chief of the gnostics, the dust of whose door became the envy of the elixir." (p. 303)
