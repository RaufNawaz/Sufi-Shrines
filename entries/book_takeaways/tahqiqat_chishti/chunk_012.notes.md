# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 012 — pp. 145–156

This chunk opens on the last lines of an entry whose subject is not named in the chunk: a man who "شعر[OCR?] اچھا کہتا ہے", whose house looks ruined from outside but is fine within and is "نئی عمارت" with nothing worth writing (p. 145). Most of the chunk is then an account of the **Rasul Shahi** (رسول شاہیان) faqirs. It runs from their origin with Ni'mat Allah and Hazrat Daud Misri, through Sayyid Rasul Shah of Alwar, Maulvi Mutahhar/Muzaffar Husain "Shah Hanif", Fidai Husain and Tawakkul Husain, to the section "ذکر حضرت نور حسین صاحب" on the order's living faqirs in Lahore (pp. 145–153). Inside that account the author prints chronograms for this book, "تاریخ تصنیف" and "قطعہ تاریخ طبع" (p. 153). The account ends with "نقل شجرہ فقرائے رسول شاہی موجودین لاہور", the order's chain of masters, which passes through four Multan/Uch figures who have archive rows (p. 154). The last section is "فصل در بیان مقبرہ حضرت عبد الرزاق", a detailed description of a tomb, its mosque, garden and tenants. It breaks off mid-sentence at the end of p. 156 (pp. 154–156). Damage is very heavy throughout, and pp. 146, 150 and 156 are only partly readable.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** (the book's "حضرت شاہ چراغ صاحب قادری سلطان پوری" on p. 151, and "حضرت عبد الرزاق" beside "کوٹھی شاہ[OCR?] چراغ" on pp. 154–156):
  - In the section on Nur Husain, the book says that in Lahore Nur Husain's love for faqirs was "بدرجہ کمال" and that "حضرت شاہ چراغ صاحب قادری سلطان پوری" looked on him with "بدرجہ غایت نظرِ مہربانی", the utmost kindness (p. 151). Nothing more is said of this Shah Chiragh in this passage.
  - Section heading: "فصل در بیان مقبرہ حضرت عبد الرزاق" (p. 154). The opening lines are badly damaged. They place the tomb in relation to "کوٹھی شاہ[OCR?] چراغ" and say the walls around the tomb are new, so that the tomb looks new ("دیواری نئی ہے ... نئی دکھلائی دیتی") (p. 154).
  - "نام انکا عبد الرزاق [illegible] سید سہروردی[OCR?]" (p. 154). The words around "سہروردی[OCR?]" are illegible, so whether it names his order or his descent is not clear. The text also has "عہدِ سلاطین [illegible]" and a fragment with "بخاری[OCR?]" (p. 154).
  - Death, exactly as printed: "وفات انکی سال ایک ہزار چوہ[OCR?] رہی یہ نوع[OCR?] میں آئی" (p. 155). The number is damaged, so it cannot be read as a year (see Doubts).
  - After his death, "سربلان[OCR?]" raised a large sum by "چندہ" (subscription) and built the tomb. Because there was so much money, a large mosque adjoining the tomb ("مسجد کلاں متصل مقبرہ") was built with what was left (p. 155). The mosque's fabric, staff and garden are under Practices.

- **shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht** (the book's "حضرت مخدوم جہانیاں جہاں گشت"):
  - Named only as a link in the Rasul Shahi chain of masters ("نقل شجرہ فقرائے رسول شاہی موجودین لاہور"). The chain makes "سید براہو[OCR?] بخاری" his disciple, and makes him a disciple of "حضرت سید [illegible]" (p. 154).

- **shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari** (the book's "سید جلال الدین بخاری"):
  - Named only as a link in the same chain, placed after "حضرت سید [illegible]" and before "حضرت سید رکن الدین ابو الفتح ملتانی", i.e. as the latter's disciple (p. 154).

- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** (the book's "حضرت سید رکن الدین ابو الفتح ملتانی"):
  - Named only as a link in the same chain, as master of "سید جلال الدین بخاری" and disciple of "شیخ بہاؤ الدین ذکریا ملتانی" (p. 154).

- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** (the book's "شیخ بہاؤ الدین ذکریا ملتانی"):
  - Named only as a link in the same chain, as disciple of "حضرت شیخ شہاب الدین سہروردی" (p. 154). The chain then refers the reader back to where "اس کا سلسلہ [illegible] سہروردی" was already recorded ("مرقوم میں ہوچکا ہے وہاں [illegible] اسامی دیکھیے") (p. 154).

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE punjab (book's word: "پنجاب"; refers to: the time of writing, "دم[OCR?] تحریر", as the book states):
  - The Rasul Shahi sect is "چنداں[OCR?] کثرت سے نہیں", not numerous. The book says "تمام پنجاب میں بجز اس مکان کے کہ نہیں": in all Punjab they have no house except this one. At the time of writing only "چار فقیر اس سلسلہ کے موجود ہیں", four faqirs of this order, and they have no other house, takia or khanqah. The book then names one more house near Lahore (see Other saints) (p. 145).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Rasul Shahi practice: bhang and wine.** Daud Misri gives Ni'mat Allah a "کاسہ بنگ[OCR?]" (p. 145). Rasul and Rahm[OCR?] Ali Shah "ہر روز ہمراہ حضرت بنگ و شراب پیا کرتے تھے" (p. 147). Mutahhar Husain is given "ایک ایک پیالہ بنگ و شراب" (p. 148). Of the Lahore group: "یہ لوگ شراب خواری کو عبادت جانتے ہیں", they regard wine-drinking as worship (p. 152).
- **Prostration to the master**: Rasul's disciples "ان حضرت کو سجدہ کرنے لگو[OCR?]", and the Arab sayyid calls it "غیر شرعی" (p. 147). Mutahhar's companion prostrates to Rasul (p. 148). The Madari challenge "رسول شاہی [illegible] کو سجدہ کرتے ہو اسکا کیا ثابت" is answered with "یا تو ہمکو سجدہ کر اور یا طوق لعنت" (p. 150).
- **Malamati self-description**: "یہ لوگ کہتے ہیں کہ خاکساران مہندہ[OCR?] ہم ہی لوگ ہیں". Wine-drinking and the "[illegible] کی صفائی" are practised "واسطے[OCR?] ملامتِ خلق ... تاکہ خلق ہمسے متنفر ہو", so that people turn away from them (p. 151). "[illegible] کی صفائی" also appears at initiations (pp. 146–148, 152); the object of the shaving/cleansing is illegible or OCR-flagged each time ("سر و کی[OCR?] صفائی", p. 146).
- **Dress and ash**: Ni'mat Allah removed his fine clothes and rubbed the dust of the saint's feet on his face "بطور بھبوت[OCR?]" (p. 145). The Lahore faqirs "بدن پہ خاکستر ملتے رہتے ہیں", rubbing white ash on face and body. They always keep a handkerchief ("رومال") on the face and wrap a square cloth "صافہ مربع کیارہ[OCR?] گرہ کا". In summer they carry a fan (p. 153). "ایک لنگوٹی[OCR?]" is given at bay'at (p. 147); "لنگوٹ [illegible]" also appears (p. 152).
- **Wazifa**: verses by Fidai Husain "جو ان خاندان رسول شاہی میں بطور وظیفہ مشہور ہیں" (p. 149).
- **Annual observance at Alwar**: "اتبکس[OCR?] ہر سال نزار[OCR?] مبارکہ[OCR?] بہ سیلہ[OCR?] بربی[OCR?] و ہوم[OCR?] دعا[OCR?] سے ہوتا ہے", with gatherings of "سماع و رقص" and "تقسیم طعام گونا گون" (p. 149). This is badly damaged; whether it describes an urs is not certain.
- **Sikh-period patronage and its decline.** In Lahore "امیرانِ حالیہ[OCR?] سکھاں" were devoted to Nur Husain. A sum of "دو سو روپیہ[OCR?] ماہواری[OCR?]" for wine was given "بطور مدد ماہیانہ[OCR?]" (p. 152). Now, under "سرکارِ گردوں وقار انگلشیہ[OCR?]", those sardars' support "کم آتا". The faqirs "سوال بھی کسی سے کچھ نہیں کرتے", they ask no one for anything (p. 152).
- **Nur Husain's house in Lahore**: "مکان [illegible] طولانی چوسہ[OCR?] گج [illegible] باغچہ" (p. 152). He has "دو خادم اور ایک بھائی حاضر باش" (p. 152). "ہزار[OCR?] روپیہ خرچ" is mentioned in damaged context. The text breaks off at "وقت تعمیر اسکے حکم سرکا[OCR?] رہوا[OCR?] اہا[OCR?] کہ اگر" (p. 156).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Ni'mat Allah's conversion.** Daud Misri bids Ni'mat Allah, "بدرجہ کمال دیندار و متشرع", to drink the bowl. He drinks it, strips and rubs ash on his face, and abandons his trade (p. 145). His servants plead that they cannot go home without their master (pp. 145–146).
- **Ni'mat Allah enters Rasul.** "آنحضرت کالروح فی حسد[OCR?] انکی جسد مبارک میں سمائی", his spirit entered Rasul's blessed body. The book attributes the half-verse "کرم نمائو فرود آ کہ خانہ خانہ تست[OCR?]" to Rasul in reply (p. 146).
- **The Arab sayyid and the gold coin.** He wrote a taʿwiz in his hookah's chilam and drew out "اشرفی". Rasul told him "خاک[OCR?] بنا", make dust. His art then failed, and Rasul made him unconscious with one glance for a week (p. 147).
- **Mutahhar Husain's illumination.** He found Rasul playing with boys, was struck senseless by his glance, and became "واصل با اللہ" (p. 148).
- **The Madari faqir.** On hearing Fidai Husain's answer, "وہ لا حواس ہوگیا" (p. 150).
- **Raising the dead.** "یہ لوگ مشہور کرتے ہیں کہ حضرت فدائی حسین صاحب نے علی احمد ... کو زندہ کیا تھا و اللہ اعلم". The author attributes the claim to the Rasul Shahis and reserves judgement (p. 151).
- **The Raja of Alwar.** Attributed to the Lahore Rasul Shahis ("یہ حضرت کراماتِ اپنے پیر کی بہت سی بیان کرتے ہیں"), with "و اللہ اعلم". Through the saint's prayer "راجہ الور کو دو دفعہ ... فرزند ہوا". When the Raja doubted, the saint said in anger that if the child was born by his prayer, let it die. The text resumes damaged at "وہ مر گیا" (pp. 153–154).
- **Alwar's view of Rasul and Shah Hanif.** "الور کے ہندو و مسلمان کہتے ہیں کہ بابا رسول است [illegible] اور مولوی صاحب خدا ہیں" (p. 149). The illegible gap affects the sense; do not use without the PDF.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author justifies including the Rasul Shahis because the house "نواح لاہور میں واقع ہے لہذا حال انکا لکھنا ضرور و مناسب" (p. 145). He says plainly that no written source was found and that the account is oral, from Nur Husain and his circle (p. 145).
- He reports the order's antinomian practice without endorsing it. The miracle claims carry "و اللہ اعلم" (pp. 151, 153). He reports that Delhi people called Fidai Husain "غیر شرع کا [illegible] خراب کنندہ نام فقرا" (p. 151).

## Doubts

- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** Lahore is named only as the location of specific houses or people: the Rasul Shahi house "نواح لاہور" (p. 145), Nur Husain in Lahore (pp. 151–152), poems famous "لاہور وغیرہ میں" (p. 153), and "رئیسِ شہر لاہور" in the chronogram (p. 153). None of this is material about the city itself, so ruling (a) is not triggered.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh: held identity question.** Mapped as the book names him, unresolved. The chunk gives "شاہ چراغ صاحب قادری سلطان پوری" (p. 151) and a tomb of "عبد الرزاق" near "کوٹھی شاہ[OCR?] چراغ" (p. 154). It does not itself say these are one man, and it does not say that Abdul Razzaq is called Shah Chiragh. The link rests on the OCR-flagged "شاہ[OCR?]" and the row's principal_figure. The order word near Abdul Razzaq is "سہروردی[OCR?]" (p. 154), while the index row says Qadiri and p. 151 says "قادری". Both readings are on record; a human must check the PDF.
- **Abdul Razzaq's death number is unreadable.** As printed: "سال ایک ہزار چوہ[OCR?] رہی یہ نوع[OCR?] میں آئی" (p. 155). It is not converted or repaired here.
- **Shajra mappings are bare names** (person-rule, ruling of 18 September). They cover shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht, shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari, shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam and shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya (p. 154). The chunk gives no site, date or place for any of them, so weigh them accordingly. "سید جلال الدین بخاری" is matched to the Surkh-Posh row by name. The Jahaniyan row's principal figure is also a "Sayyid Jalaluddin", but the chain lists the two as separate links, so the book itself distinguishes them. The link between Jahaniyan and Jalal-ud-Din is "حضرت سید [illegible]", so the chain cannot be read continuously there.
- **Province bullet** (Punjab, p. 145): province-level, weigh accordingly. The period is the author's present ("دم[OCR?] تحریر"); the book gives no year.
- **Mutahhar vs Muzaffar Husain.** "مولوی مطہر حسین" (p. 148, and the shajra p. 154) and "مولوی مظفر حسین صاحب المشہور شاہ حنیف[OCR?]" (p. 149) occupy the same place in the story. They may be one man under a transcription variant. Not resolved.
- **"صاحبزادہ حضرت محمد شاہ بادشاہ"** (p. 148) is followed by an illegible gap. In the same passage "محمد شاہ بادشاہ" takes the throne (p. 148). Whether the book makes Mutahhar Husain a son of the emperor cannot be read from the transcription. Do not use.
- **Fidai Husain's birthplace is given two ways**: "میرٹھ[OCR?]" (p. 149) and "مولد دہلی" (p. 151). This may be an internal contradiction or an OCR problem.
- **The Rasul Shah story's place and era words are all OCR-flagged.** "شہر الور[OCR?]" (first mentions, pp. 145–146), "ہریا[OCR?] در پور سادات[OCR?]", "عہد شاہجہانی[OCR?]" (p. 145). Later mentions of "الور" are unflagged (pp. 147–151, 153).
- **Rasul Shah's age "سو برس[OCR?]"** sits between illegible gaps (p. 148). All chronograms (pp. 148–149, 151, 153) are heavily damaged, and none is decoded to a year here.
- **"اتبکس[OCR?] ہر سال نزار[OCR?] مبارکہ[OCR?]"** (p. 149) may describe an annual observance at the tomb, but the key words are all flagged.
- **Pages 146, 150 and 156 are largely unreadable.** The Persian verses on pp. 146 and 150 are fragmentary and are not glossed. The p. 156 description of the tomb enclosure, its tenants and its finances contains dozens of flagged tokens, and only the clearer phrases are carried above.
- **The subject of the p. 145 opening fragment** (the poet whose house looks ruined) belongs to the previous chunk and is not placeable from here.
- **Damage inventory (ruling b).** Vision transcription of scribal Nastaliq. Every page carries dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags, and `[margin]` markers appear on pp. 149–151, 153 and 155. The flags have been carried onto every quoted word. Folio markers are present (e.g. p. 145 = folio 139) but are not cited, per the brief.
