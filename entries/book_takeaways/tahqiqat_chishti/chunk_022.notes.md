# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 022 — pp. 247–257

This chunk opens mid-section. Pages 247–254 continue a description that began in the previous chunk: a walled Hindu complex, the "چوبارہ" (chaubara) of "جیجو بھگت" (Jiju Bhagat), whose mahant and gaddi-nashin is "با وا ہر بھجن[OCR?] داس" (Baba Har Bhajan[OCR?] Das). **This section's heading is in the previous chunk (021) and is not seen here.** The account covers the samadhs, mandir, gaddi, wells and garden plots, then the history of the gaddi, the sect's book and rules, Jiju Bhagat's death year with a Persian chronogram, a theft, and the complex's grants and income. It ends "فقط" (p. 254). Two titled sections follow: "احوال مکان چلہ حضرت میاں میر" (the chilla of Hazrat Mian Mir in Sadr Bazar Anarkali, pp. 254–255) and "احوال مکان چوکی سخی[OCR?] سرور المشہور بہ[OCR?] لکہہ[OCR?] داتا" (the chauki of Sakhi Sarwar, known as Lakh Data, in Anarkali, pp. 255–257). The Sakhi Sarwar section moves from the Lahore mela to the saint's family story and breaks off at the bottom of p. 257 in the middle of the goats miracle ("بیٹا تم نے اچھا کیا کہ مال اپنا").

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Jiju Bhagat** "سی جو بارہ میں آب میں [illegible] بیٹھی تھو اور ادے[OCR?] دی میں سماگئے". The book says she sat in the chaubara and merged ("سماگئے") into the spot (p. 247). It repeats "اوسی میں سماگئی" (p. 251).
- **The foundation water:** when they dug the foundation of Baba Thakar[OCR?] Das's samadh in "سمت ۱۹[OCR?]" [margin: ۱۱[OCR?]], water was found at the foundation ("پانی ہانڈی تک") (p. 247).
- **Sakhi Sarwar and the goats** (p. 257), told by the book as his story:
  - Ten[OCR?]-year-old Sayyid Ahmad asks to graze goats and is given some. In the jungle he leaves them, spreads his prayer-mat under a tree and reads the Quran.
  - Forty[OCR?] faqirs come who have eaten nothing for twelve[OCR?] years. He gives them his five[OCR?] goats, then his brothers' flock and others' flocks, and they make kebabs.
  - The herdsmen complain to Zain-ul-Abidin, who promises double compensation. He goes to the jungle and finds the faqirs eating.
  - He tells his son "بیٹا تم نے اچھا کیا کہ مال اپنا". The text breaks off here, and the story continues in chunk 023.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author rejects the Anarkali chauki's claim to be a place of Sakhi Sarwar's seclusion. He says it was built by Bharai[OCR?] people out of greed (p. 255).
- He condemns the mela: "اوس روز شیطان کا بازار بڑا گرم ہوتا ہے" (p. 255).
- He contrasts the low-caste devotion with his own view of the saint: "اگر وہ اصل میں یہ حضرت تری[OCR?] بزرگ سید کامل ہند[OCR?]" (p. 256). The wording is damaged.
- He marks his survey as first-hand (Jiju Bhagat chaubara, p. 251). For Mian Mir's chilla he relies on oral report from the sajjada nashin (p. 255).

## Doubts

- **Opening fragment:** pp. 247–254 continue the Jiju Bhagat chaubara section, whose heading is in chunk 021. Its location within Lahore is not stated in this chunk. Not mapped to any archive row: no row's name or principal figure matches Jiju Bhagat, Dadu or the Dadu-panthi gaddi.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir:** mapped by the person rule. The material is about a chilla in Sadr Bazar Anarkali, which the book itself does not connect to the tomb site. Nothing here should be cited as being about the shrine at the row's location. "وفات ... اسی حکمہ[OCR?] بر واقع ہوئی" (death at this spot) and the following "خانہ[OCR?] ... اوٹھا[OCR?] کر لیکئی[OCR?]" are flagged, so check the PDF before using them.
- **sakhi-sarwar:** mapped by the person rule (principal figure "Sultan Sakhi Sarwar (Lakhdata)"; the book's "لکہہ[OCR?] داتا"). The Lahore chauki is not the archive row's site. The book does not name the place of the mazar where lakhs gather. "موضع شاہ[OCR?] سکوٹ[OCR?]" and "لگا[OCR?] ہا[OCR?]" are flagged and are not identified here.
- **Multan** appears only as a distance and route reference in the Sakhi Sarwar family story (p. 256), not as material on Multan in general. The 8 Multan ids are therefore **not** attached; weigh this if a consolidator disagrees.
- **Anarkali / Lahori Darwaza** appear as locations of non-archive sites (the chilla, the chauki, the mela route). Following the brief, they are not toponym bullets.
