## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Sain Nizam Shah — سائیں نظام[OCR?] شاہ** (pp. 202–204): a "فقیر مستان[OCR?] مجذوب", an ecstatic faqir. "تمام ہندو و مسلمان انکا ادب و آداب بجا[OCR?] کرتے تھے", all Hindus and Muslims paid him respect. He lived in various places, first near "قلعہ گوجر سنگ[OCR?]" and then around the city. He settled where there was an old mosque and had a building put up there (p. 203). His death is given as "ہجری ۱[OCR?]۲[OCR?]۶[OCR?]۹[OCR?]", with two chronogram qit'as by "مفتی غلام سرور" (p. 202). His tomb is "اس عالم کے گوشہ غرب[OCR?] و شمالی", in the north-west corner of the [enclosure?] of "شیخ امام الدین". After his death there were "ہزار ہا" (thousands) at his funeral, and "میلہ" (a mela) is mentioned (p. 204).
- **Bustan[OCR?] Shah — بستان[OCR?] شاہ** (p. 203): a majzub "فقیر قوم … افغان[OCR?]" who always sat outside "دروازہ شاہ عالمی[OCR?]" (Shah Alami gate). ## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Gravedigging as a hereditary trade with shared income**: one line of gorkans holds the work, the income is split "نصفاً نصف", and part is paid to the sajjada nashin (p. 209). This is detailed under the Miani bullet above.
- **Sikh-state patronage of a majzub**: an allowance from the Maharaja's treasury for Bustan[OCR?] Shah (p. 203).
- **A colonial court process against a faqir**: after a Mughal ("قوم مغلی[OCR?]") man was shut in Nizam Shah's "کاٹھہ[OCR?]", a complaint was lodged "بحکمہ[OCR?] صاحب ڈپٹی کمشنر بہادر ضلع لاہور", with the Deputy Commissioner of Lahore District, and the "کوتوال" was sent to summon him (pp. 203–204).
- **Funeral and mela**: thousands of Hindus and Muslims attended Nizam Shah's funeral, and a "میلہ" is mentioned (p. 204).
- **Rent on resettled land**: one[OCR?] rupee a year per house at Miani, about two hundred houses (p. 212).
- **Office of Qazi of Lahore**: held by Qazi Ghulam Muhammad, Chiragh Shah's ancestor (p. 206).
- **Architecture**: Nizam Shah's square platform with four kalas-topped turrets (p. 202); Chiragh Shah's mud enclosure, well with wheel ("چرخ") and garden (pp. 206–207); Hidayat Khan Baloch's well and bathhouse (p. 207); Shaikh Sa'di's masonry enclosure (p. 207).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Nizam Shah, on an Eid morning at "تکیہ سادھوان[OCR?]", called for "صفیں" (prayer rows). From this it was understood "کہ آج سلطنت میں ضرور کچھ فرق آ و لگا[OCR?]", that some change would surely come to the kingdom that day. Afterwards "راجہ مہر[OCR?] سنگہ" fled from his haveli with treasure, was pursued by "سردار جواہر سنگہ" (linked by a garbled word, "باسوں[OCR?]", with "مہاراجہ دلیپ سنگہ") and was killed together with "میاں سوچیت[OCR?] سنگہ". This is told on the authority of "جناب[OCR?] [illegible] ۔ اجمیر[OCR?] سنگہ" (p. 203).
- When the kotwal summoned Nizam Shah, he said "بھائی فقیر تو محکموں میں گرفتار[OCR?] نہیں", a faqir is not held by courts, and that he would go to the Sarkar's court himself. He died that night, and the city took it as a sign of his faqiri (p. 204).
- Shaikh Sa'di Lahori: the sick were told to say in his name "سعدی فرما تو دیں" and were healed, and anyone with a difficulty had it solved by his prayer (p. 208). The book attributes this to the "تذکرہ" of Sayyid Adam.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author draws on oral testimony and names it: "زبانی سید منصور سجادہ نشین" (p. 209), "زبانی گورکنان" (p. 211). He also cites a written "کتاب تذکرہ مصنف سید آدم[OCR?]" for Shaikh Sa'di (p. 208).

## Doubts

- **The chunk opens mid-section.** P. 202 begins partway through describing a building and Sain Nizam Shah's death and tomb, so his section heading is in chunk 017. "شیخ امام الدین", in whose enclosure his tomb lies, is presumably introduced there too.
- **Miani mapping is place-level.** The book writes "گورستان میانی" / "قبرستان میانی" / "خطہ میانی" / "سیانی[OCR?]". It never says "Miani Sahib", and the match to toponym_map's "miani sahib graveyard" (4 ids) is by place. Weigh accordingly. The book also does not distinguish this tract's boundaries from the later graveyard.
- **tahir-bandagi-qadri identity.** The book uses "شیخ طاہر بندگی[OCR?]" (flagged), "شیخ محمد طاہر صاحب" and "شیخ طاہر صاحب". The index's principal figure is "Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri". The mapping is made on name and Miani location. The chunk gives him no silsila and no date.
- **Mozang mapping is place-level and textually weak.** The founder passage on p. 207 is garbled ("پہ پر فرزیہ[OCR?] مزنگ کی ہو جہ بانی محلہ مزنگ کا نہا[OCR?] … مسمی فتح[OCR?] مزنگ"), and its subject (Shah Mir or someone named Fateh[OCR?] Mozang) cannot be fixed. It was mapped to the two "mozang" rows under ruling (a) because it is about the mohalla's founding, not just a location. A consolidator may decide this is mohalla-as-location and drop it.
- **Uch and Multan were deliberately not mapped.** "اوج[OCR?] / ابوج[OCR?]" (p. 207) is OCR-flagged and is only the ancestors' place of origin. "موضع احمد پور علاقہ ملتان" and "صوبہ ملتان" (p. 205) are a village in the Multan area and a Mughal-era suba named as a family's residence and service, not material on Multan's tradition. No Uch Sharif or Multan rows and no province bullet were added. A consolidator may revisit this under ruling (a).
- **Shah Chiragh is not mapped to shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh.** The "سید چراغ علو[OCR?] شاہ صاحب چشتی و سبزواری" of pp. 205–207 is a living contemporary (son of Sayyid Ahmad Shah, with living sons) at Miani. The row's principal figure is Syed Abdul Razzaq (Shah Chiragh) at The Mall. "مسجد چراغ شاہ" (p. 204) is unattributed. Both were left unmapped on name grounds. This touches the held identity question, which is recorded here and not resolved.
- **data-darbar rests on a flagged word**: "ہجویری[OCR?]" and the garbled link "حمیدیان[OCR?] … ہر یہ[OCR?]" (p. 207).
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar and samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh are person-level only.** Farid is a bare name at the end of a silsila chain. Ranjit Singh is a dating point and patron, and the samadhi is absent. Bustan Shah's patron is "سرکار میہا[OCR?] راجہ" without a personal name (p. 203).
- **Sikh-court names in the Nizam Shah legend are carried as printed** ("راجہ مہر[OCR?] سنگہ" etc.). No identification is offered.
- **Dates, all flagged or partial**: Nizam Shah "۱[OCR?]۲[OCR?]۶[OCR?]۹[OCR?]" (pp. 202, margin), with a margin figure "۶۶[OCR?]" (p. 202); Shaikh Sa'di "اکھر[OCR?] ارشاسی[OCR?]" under Alamgir (p. 208); the Chishti brothers "سال [illegible] ہزار[OCR?] دو سو[OCR?]" (p. 211); Bustan Shah "عرصہ دس سال" before writing (p. 203). None is converted.
- **"شاہ عالم بادشاہ عالمگیر کے بیٹی[OCR?]"** (p. 205) is printed this way and is internally odd. It is carried as printed.
- **Damage inventory**: dense `[OCR?]` on nearly every line, `[illegible]` across pp. 210 and 212 almost entirely, and margin notes illegible (pp. 205, 206, 212). Pp. 207 and 209 carry no folio line, and no folio is cited anywhere in these notes.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet**: the chunk's "Lahore" mentions are a district office (Deputy Commissioner), a qazi post and people's devotion to a faqir. None is about the city itself.
