# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 005 — pp. 61–72

This whole chunk is one continuous account of "حضرت حسین" (Hazrat Husain; also "حضرت لال حسین" and "حضرت حسین لاہوری", p. 68) and his companion "مادہو / مادھو[OCR?]" (Madho). It opens mid-narrative at p. 61, which carries Husain's fondness for "بابو پورہ[OCR?]" and his prophecy of his own burial. Then comes a run of karamat, introduced by "نقل ہے" and "کہتے ہیں". These are the rain at a village across the Ravi, Haji Yaqub and the Medina vision, the arrest ordered by Akbar and the kotwal Malik Ali, the wine cups before Akbar, the childless wife of Mulla Mir[OCR?], the Khan-e Khanan's Thatta campaign, Sa'ida's earache and the alchemist (pp. 61–69). The titled section "حال وفات حضرت حسین رحمتہ اللہ علیہ" (p. 69) gives his death, burial, chronograms and the mourning (pp. 69–70). It then covers Madho's twelve years with Raja Man Singh, the flood, the empty grave and the reburial (pp. 70–71), and finally Madho's succession, death and burial beside Husain (pp. 71–72). The chunk ends in a long Persian poem in praise of Husain quoted from "کتاب حقیقتہ الفقرا" (p. 72). The damage is very heavy throughout. Every page prints a `[folio N]` marker (p. 61 = folio 54 … p. 72 = folio 65). Following the brief, none of these folios is cited.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Sajjada. Husain prophesied a faqir "بسجادہ[OCR?] نشین" (p. 61). Madho sat as sajjada nashin at the mazar after thirteen years (p. 71). At Multan, the khanqah's "پیر سجادہ نشین مزار حضرت مخدوم" (p. 68).
- Offerings. The Khan-e Khanan gave "پانسو[OCR?] روپیہ" to Husain (p. 67). He gave "دو سو روپیہ نذرانہ" at the Zakariya khanqah, which was returned (p. 68).
- Burial and built fabric. Husain's grave was opened during a Ravi flood and his remains reburied "جہاں اب مزارِ مقدس واقعہ ہی" (p. 71). Madho's grave is "پہلوئے مزارِ حضرت حسین" (p. 72). Malik Ali's grave has a "حجرہ مسجد" (p. 64).
- Food offering. The villagers brought "نان مرغن با شیر و شکر", after which Husain's companions danced (p. 62).
- Dance and music. Husain "نغمہ و رقص کنان[OCR?]" (p. 62) and "رقص کنان" (pp. 63, 65).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Rain at the village across the Ravi. The villagers held his friends and threatened them. On his prayer, rain fell until the fields flooded and stopped when they begged. The village became his khadims (pp. 61–62).
- Haji Yaqub. With eyes closed he was shown Husain at the rauza in Medina. Husain vanished when his secret was told aloud, and Haji Yaqub then found him in tawaf at the Ka'ba in Mecca (pp. 62–63).
- The chains put on him by order of Malik Ali "اوسی وقت ٹوٹ گئی", broke at once, and broke again (p. 64).
- Akbar's test. From one bottle "آٹہ[OCR?] پیالہ" were poured, each yielding something different [illegible]. Shut in a hujra, he was also seen elsewhere in the palace. Akbar fell at his feet (pp. 64–65).
- Mulla Mir[OCR?]'s wife. The husband looked through the door and saw him "بشکل بچہ شیر خوارہ", in the form of a suckling infant, drinking at her breast. A son was born. He drank bottles of poisoned wine with no effect (pp. 65–66).
- Night errand. Madho [illegible] was sent to the Ravi at midnight and a dhobi was found there (pp. 66–67). The outcome is largely [illegible].
- Thatta. The 500 rupees were spent for the conquest. Husain told the Khan-e Khanan not to ask any other faqir, and the Multan sajjada nashin returned his offering after a dream (pp. 67–68).
- Sa'ida[OCR?]'s earache was cured by "وہ ٹکڑا کاغذ" (p. 68).
- The alchemist. Husain recounted the man's labours unseen. Where [illegible] fell, "وہ جگہ تمام طلا[OCR?] ہو گئی" (pp. 68–69).
- Empty grave and the bouquet of rihan (p. 71). Madho took Husain's likeness on becoming sajjada nashin (p. 71).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author names his written source: "کتاب حقیقت الفقرا[OCR?]" by "شیخ پیر محمد رضا[OCR?]" (pp. 63, 70, 72). For Malik Ali's grave he cites the oral account of gravediggers ("زبانی گور کنان[OCR?]"). He notes that they "وہ بیان کبہی[OCR?] اور طرح سی[OCR?] کیا تہا", that is, told it differently at another time (p. 64).
- "تنبیہ" (p. 70): the author explains why Husain sent Madho away, namely to spare him the grief of separation.

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Very heavy `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` density on every page. Pp. 61, 63–64 and 67 are the worst: whole clauses are lost, including the outcome of Malik Ali's death (p. 64) and the night-errand story (pp. 66–67). The Persian verses on pp. 69 and 72 are badly broken. All quotations above keep their flags.
- **Mapping to madho-lal-hussain** rests on the book's "حضرت حسین" / "حضرت لال حسین" / "حضرت حسین لاہوری", the pairing with "مادہو/مادھو[OCR?]", and the Lahore setting. The chunk never writes "شاہ حسین". A consolidator should weigh this. A different "حسین" boy, a prisoner awaiting hanging, appears on p. 64 and is not the saint.
- **Burial place "بابو پورہ[OCR?]"** (pp. 61, 71) is flagged every time. The index row gives Baghbanpura as the site's location, but the chunk does not say Baghbanpura. Do not equate the two without checking the PDF.
- **Dates and chronograms are not computed.** Husain's death year "ایک ہزار آٹھ[OCR?]" (p. 70). His age "[illegible: a number in words]" (p. 69). The obscure age/stage lines "عمر[OCR?] دہ[OCR?] سال نو[OCR?]" and "نانویں[OCR?] سال" (p. 69). The reburial chronogram (p. 71). Madho's birth line "۲۲[OCR?] … سہ[OCR?] ہشتاد[OCR?] … نہ صد[OCR?]" (p. 72). Madho's death "سنِ ایک ہزار [illegible: number word] ہجری … ذی الحجج کی بائیسویں[OCR?] … دو شنبہ" (p. 72). All need human reading from the PDF.
- **Timeline consistency.** Husain's p. 61 prophecy (Madho away "بارہ[OCR?] برس" after one year) agrees with the narrative of one year plus twelve years with Man Singh (p. 70) and with "تیرہ برس کامل" (p. 71). Recorded as observed, not checked against the original.
- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya.** The name is printed "بہاؤالدین[OCR?] ذکریا" (with ذ) and is OCR-flagged. The material concerns the khanqah's sajjada nashin of the day, "شیخ کبیر[OCR?] بالا پیر[OCR?]". The saint who spoke in the dream, "حضرت جلدی[OCR?]", is unplaced: the reading is flagged and I could not identify it.
- **Multan not mapped as a bare toponym.** "ملتان" occurs only as the place where the Khan-e Khanan reached the Zakariya khanqah (p. 68). That is site-specific, so the other Multan rows were not attached. Flag if the consolidator reads it otherwise.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** Lahore occurs only as the setting of Husain's life and as part of Malik Ali's title "کوتوال شہر لاہو[OCR?]" (p. 63). Neither is material about the city as such, so the 35 lahore ids were not attached.
- **"گورستان سیانی[OCR?]"** (p. 64, twice) is printed with س and flagged. If the true reading were "میانی", it might bear on the Miani Sahib graveyard rows. It is **not** mapped, because the printed form is not repaired.
- **"گوجر خان[OCR?]"** (p. 66) is used as a form of address to Mulla Mir[OCR?], not as a place.
- **"ملک ٹھٹھہ[OCR?]"** (p. 68) is not in the toponym map and no province bullet is made for it. It is the target of Akbar's campaign and is noted under Other.
- **Province bullet (punjab):** province-level, weigh accordingly. The book does not mark boundaries. The first "پنجاب" is flagged.
- **Source title variants:** "حقیقت الفقرا[OCR?]" (p. 63), "تحقیق[OCR?] الفقرا[OCR?]" (p. 69), "حقیقتُ الفقرا[OCR?]" (p. 70) and "حقیقتہ الفقرا" (p. 72). These are probably one work, but that is unverified.
- **Folios.** Folio markers run 54–65 on pp. 61–72 and are not cited, per the brief.
