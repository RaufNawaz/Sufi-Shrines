# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 013 — pp. 157–166

**The chunk opens mid-section.** Pages 157–158 continue an account whose heading is in the previous chunk (012). It covers a domed tomb and the mosque beside it, the management and funding of both, and then the life, burial and tomb-building of "سیّد عبد الرزاق" (p. 157), whose death chronogram closes the section (p. 158). Two titled sections follow in order. The first is "فصل[OCR?] در[OCR?] ذکر[OCR?] محمد سعید لاہوری" (p. 158), on the enclosure of Haji Muhammad Saeed Lahori west of the tomb of Shah Chiragh, which runs on into the long story of Shaikh Ashraf, Alamgir and Shah Muhammad Amir of Hujra Shah Muqim (pp. 159–163). The second is "فصل در بیان مقبرہ عالیہ حضرت علی مخدوم ہجویری غزنوی جلالی المشہور دا تا گنج بخش رحمتہ اللہ علیہ" (p. 163), on Data Ganj Bakhsh: his life, his arrival in Lahore, his mosque, his death-date chronograms and the buildings of the khanqah. That section is still running at the end of p. 166. Six archive rows are touched. The richest material is on **data-darbar** (pp. 163–166), followed by **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** (pp. 157–158). Damage is very heavy on pp. 158, 160, 162 and 164–166, and several passages there cannot be read for sense.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

  - From "سن اٹھارہ سو باون[OCR?] [margin] [illegible]" the mosque's imam has been Maulvi Ahmad Din, with Mulla Nur Ahmad as naib imam. Mian Rahim Bakhsh the merchant meets "تمام خرچ مسجد" (all the mosque's expenses) (p. 157).
  - The saint: death "بروز پنجشنبہ" (on a Thursday), in "سنہ ایک ہزار چوراسی[OCR?] ہجری[OCR?]" (p. 157).
  - "نام انکا اگرچہ سیّد عبد الرزاق ہی مگر سیّد لگی[OCR?] کرکے مشہور میں": his name is Sayyid Abdul Razzaq, but he is famous as "سیّد لگی[OCR?]" (p. 157).
  - "اول ارادت انکی سجادت[OCR?] حضرت موج دریا": his first discipleship was with Hazrat Mauj Darya. After Mauj Darya died, he always went to Mauj Darya's tomb ("مقبرہ") at night for worship. By day he rested at the place where his own tomb now stands, where a hujra and a dalan had been built ([illegible] in between) (p. 157).
  - He told people to bury him in that hujra. "حسب وصیت" (as his will directed), he was buried there, and "مدت بہر قبر خام رہی": for a long time the grave stayed unbuilt (kacha) (p. 157).
  - Tomb-building: a subscription was collected after the khanqah's mutawalli had a dream (see Legends). "عبد الغفور نامی ایک شخص معمار" (a mason named Abdul Ghafur) was appointed manager of the building. When the tomb was nearly finished, Abdul Razzaq came to the manager in a dream; the text breaks off (pp. 157–158).
  - Mosque (p. 158, very damaged): "ایک مسجد عالیشان وسیع[OCR?]" (a grand, wide mosque) was built with money ("روپیہ"). A "منشی حکم الدین" had repairs ("مرمت مسجد") done. The mosque is "سہت کشادہ" (very spacious) and "صدہا آدمی" (hundreds of people) fit in it. A "مولوی غلام رسول[OCR?]" is named. The last clause, "یہ مسجد و ہاں یو بکی مسجد ہوگئی ہی", cannot be read for sense (p. 158).
  - "تاریخ وفات حضرت عبد الرزاق" (the date of his death) is given as a qita by "غلام سرور صاحب", composed "وقت تصنیف کتاب" (at the time the book was written). The verse is almost entirely flagged (p. 158).
  - The next section places Haji Muhammad Saeed's enclosure "بطرف مغرب مقبرہ[OCR?] شاہ چراغ صاحب رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" (to the west of the tomb of Shah Chiragh Sahib) (p. 158).

- **data-darbar** (the book's "حضرت علی مخدوم ہجویری غزنوی جلالی المشہور دا تا گنج بخش", pp. 163–166):

In the morning he met people carrying "جنازہ حضرت حسین زنجانی" (Husain Zanjani's bier), joined the funeral, and saw to the shrouding and burial (p. 163).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Abdul Razzaq (Shah Chiragh) tomb and mosque (p. 157):**
  - The threat of entry as "مال[OCR?] نزول[OCR?]" was met by a merchants' subscription, with Mian Rahim Bakhsh as manager.
  - Rahim Bakhsh meets all the mosque's expenses and the imams' arrangement.
  - Study boys from the adjoining mosque use the dome.
- **Tomb-building by subscription after a dream:** "زر چندہ جمع ہوتا لگا", with a mason, Abdul Ghafur, as manager (p. 157).
- **Haji Muhammad Saeed:**
  - Mela: "اس تاریخ کو میلا بھی ہوتا ہی" (a mela is held on his death date). His followers Muhammad Bakhsh, Ghulam Muhammad and Mian Ilah Din say that formerly there was no mela here (p. 159).
  - Ownership: of his two daughters, one married Hafiz Muhammad Muqim Lahori and the other Hafiz Muhammad Murad. Muhammad Murad's descendants in Lahore are "محمد بخش حکاک" (engraver), Ghulam Muhammad "جوسب[OCR?] فروش" and Ilah Din of "قلعہ گوجر[OCR?] سنگ[OCR?]": "یہی لوگ مالک اسمکان کے میں" (these people are owners of this place) (p. 159). Later, "منشی احمد بخش ... کام حکاکی کا کرتا ہی اور اب یہ ہی مالک اس علاوہ[OCR?] کا ہی" (Munshi Ahmad Bakhsh, an engraver, is now its owner) (p. 160).
  - Revered from Kabul: "قبائل ... محمد خان والی کابل" and their relatives are his murids. "سردار سلطان محمد خان بہادر خان[OCR?] مرحوم", when in Lahore, often came to the mazar and offered "نذر و نیاز" (p. 159).
  - Revenue-free village: "مقام کوٹ کمان[OCR?] علاقہ جلال آباد میں ایک گاؤں[OCR?] بنام انکو معاف ہی" (a village at Kot Kaman[OCR?], Jalalabad tract, is held revenue-free in their name) (p. 159).
- **Shaikh Ashraf:** his tomb and mosque outside the Bhati[OCR?] gate were demolished by Ranjit Singh and he was reburied at the present place (p. 159).
- **Data Darbar:**
  - Mosque rebuilt in "۱۲۶۹[OCR?] ہجری" by Gulzar Shah (p. 163).
  - The gate for coming and going is "جنوب رو یہ پس پشت مسجد" (south-facing, behind the mosque), with a platform ("چبوترہ[OCR?]") built by a sahibzada (p. 165).
  - A north brick gate ("دروازہ شمالی خشتی پختہ"), "عرصہ تیس سال سے بنا ہوا" (built thirty years ago) (p. 165).
  - A kitchen and store ("با ورچی خانہ و گنجینہ") with three doors, where "عرس کے روز و ہان کہانا تقسیم فقرا ... طیار کیا جاتا ہے" (on the urs day food is prepared for distribution to faqirs). There are also "دو تنور" (two ovens) and seating for the mujawirs ("مجاوروں[OCR?]") (pp. 165–166).
  - Sikh-period structures and a haveli dated "شمت[OCR?] ۱۸۹۵" and "۱۲۴۱[OCR?] ہجری" (p. 166).
  - The author's doctrine of the qutb (p. 164): "حکومت ظاہری" (outer rule) is given to worldly rulers and "حکومت باطنی" (inner rule) to faqirs; "کوئی شہر اور کوئی ملک بغیر حکومت قطب نہیں رہی" (no city and no country is without a qutb's rule).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- A lion: "مشہور ہی کہ اوس وقت جمعرات کو دن شیر[OCR?] یہان آیا کرتا تھا" (it is said a lion came here on Thursdays), sweeping ("جا روبکشی[OCR?]") Abdul Razzaq's unbuilt grave. Reported as hearsay (p. 157).
- Mauj Darya appeared in a dream to the khanqah's mutawalli and said he had been ordered by Ghaus al-Azam to have Abdul Razzaq's tomb built. Whoever spent money on it would earn great reward ("بہت ثواب") (p. 157).
- Abdul Razzaq appeared in a dream to the building's manager to say he often comes to this place at night; the text breaks off (p. 157).
- At Haji Ibadat Allah's grave, "قسمت الٰہی سی" (by divine decree) five trees grew up and shade the grave (p. 158, damaged).
- Haji Muhammad Saeed and the mohalla: a karamat told on the authority of "غلام نبی ... رسالدار" about the people of mohalla Abdullah Wari, whom the Hazrat reassured ("تم کہ غم کرو اور مت مانگو") at a threatening arrival in Lahore. The passage is too damaged to state the outcome (p. 160).

The girl appeared and said the amir had made her a slave-girl ("کنیزک"), and that she had been in the bazaar when "یہ حضرت" took hold of her and told her to close her eyes; she found herself here. When her father opened his eyes, "اپنی لڑکی کو موجود پایا" (he found his daughter present) (pp. 160–161).
- Shaikh Ashraf and the crescent moon: Alamgir made the suit turn on whether the Ramadan/Eid crescent would be seen "today". Ashraf said yes; Shah Muhammad Amir said not before tomorrow. Ashraf, by "اسماء الٰہی" and "موکلاں" (divine names and spirit-agents), made the crescent visible to all, and "شیخ اسرف سچا" (Ashraf [was] true) (p. 162). The ending, including who received the betrothal ("ماطہ[OCR?] حضرت شاہ محمد امیر کو ملا"), is damaged. In his last years Ashraf turned to divine worship (pp. 162–163).
- Data Ganj Bakhsh and the qibla: after the ulama of Lahore objected to the qibla of his mosque, he gave them a feast and told them to look. "قبلہ بالمشاہدہ ... ظاہر نظر آتا" (the qibla was seen with their own eyes). They submitted, and "کرامت حضرت کا مشہور ہوگیا" (p. 164). Attributed via Dara Shikoh's Safinat al-Auliya (p. 163).

## Doubts

- **Chunk opens mid-section.** The heading of the Sayyid Abdul Razzaq account is in chunk 012. The domed building and adjoining mosque on p. 157 are assigned to his tomb **only by their sequence** in this chunk ("در حال ان حضرت صاحب مقبرہ صاحب کا" follows directly). Check against chunk 012.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh: held identity question, mapped as the book names him, not resolved.**
  - The chunk calls him "سیّد عبد الرزاق", famous as "سیّد لگی[OCR?]". It never itself says "Shah Chiragh" for him.
  - The mapping rests on (i) the name matching the row's principal figure and (ii) the next section's "مقبرہ[OCR?] شاہ چراغ صاحب" as a neighbouring landmark (p. 158).
  - Whether "Shah Chiragh" conflates two men is the brief's open question and is left open.
  - His death year is printed only in words, "ایک ہزار چوراسی[OCR?]" (p. 157).
- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari** is mapped by the person rule on the bare name "حضرت موج دریا", printed with three different flagged tails (p. 157). Weigh accordingly.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** is matched on the principal figure only. The samadhi is absent. Everything under that id is about demolitions and building at other sites (pp. 159, 166).
  - The p. 165 agent of demolitions at Data Darbar is printed "مہار[OCR?] ابہ[OCR?] زحمت سنگ". It may be the same man, but it is **not** mapped and must not be repaired without the PDF.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar** is mapped on "حضرت بابا فرید الدین گنج شکر ... پاک پٹن[OCR?]". The material is about the gaddi-nashins of his day, in a story set in Alamgir's time, not about the shrine (p. 161).
