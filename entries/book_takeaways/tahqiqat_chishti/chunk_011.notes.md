# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 011 — pp. 133–144

This chunk opens mid-section on the custodianship arrangements of a shrine whose name falls before the chunk begins: three sahibzadas share the tomb's income by turns, followed by the urs, two revenue-free villages and the faqirs posted as mujawirs (pp. 133–134). Four short titled sections follow: "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ فقیر تاج شاہ", the tomb of Faqir Taj Shah, with its inscribed verses and a chronogram (pp. 134–135); "فصل در ذکر حاطہ[OCR?] چہر[OCR?] نگاں[OCR?]", an enclosure with a domed mosque (pp. 135–136); and "فصل در بیان مقبرہ انار کلی", the tomb of Anarkali, its story and its present use as a church (pp. 136–137). The longest section, "فصل در ذکر مکان باغیچہ مولوی سید رجب علی خان صاحب بہادر سابق میر منشی [illegible] ممالک پنجاب", is about the garden house of Syed Rajab Ali Khan (pp. 137–143). It holds an old mazar of Syed Mahmud Bhakari[OCR?], an account of the family's descent and its massacre at Panj Graian, Rajab Ali's own first-person account of his career (the text calls it "نقل احوال مرسلہ", an account sent in and copied "مطابق اصل", as in the original), and a nasab-nama with a genealogical tree (p. 143). The chunk ends with "فصل در بیان مزار حضرت کالو چشتی" and "فصل در بیان تکیہ فقرائے رسول شاہی" (p. 144), which breaks off mid-sentence. Archive rows appear only as location references: Shah Abu al-Ma'ali's tomb and Bibi Pak Daman's mazar. There is also one passage on the city of Lahore as a whole.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Turn-based custodianship** (unnamed shrine): each sahibzada keeps the income of his turn. The other sahibzadas are not barred ("ممانعت[OCR?] نہیں") from receiving offerings (p. 133).
  - An offering brought by a murid who names his own sahibzada goes to that pir ("اوسی کے پیر کو پہونچتا ہے") (pp. 133).
- **Urs of the unnamed shrine**: held "بتاریخ سولہویں ربیع الاول" (p. 133). Oil, wick and cloth are given in shares ("حصہ وار[OCR?]"), and each family distributes "نان گوشت پلاؤ حلوا" at its own house (p. 133).
  - "بتاریخ ۱۵[OCR?] ـ ربیع الاول" the mazar is washed ("مزار کو غسل دیکر"), and the urs is held the next day with a night vigil and khatm (pp. 133–134).
- **Revenue-free land**: one village "کوٹ[OCR?] سیالاں علاقہ لاہور تحصیل چوبیاں[OCR?]", another "موضع جان پور[OCR?] علاقہ شیخوپورہ ضلع گوجرانوالہ", and land "مہلن[OCR?] والی" (p. 134).
  - These were remitted and released ("معاف و واگذار[OCR?]") by the former sarkar (p. 134).
  - Syed Imam Shah takes the Sialan village's income and spends it on the urs. Jan Pur and the land go to Syed Mahin Shah, "نصف[OCR?]", for his urs (p. 134).
- **Mujawirs**: each of the three heirs has appointed a faqir as mujawir, e.g. "قادری[OCR?] شاہ مقررہ سید امام شاہ". These faqirs have no connection with the family's offspring (p. 134).
- **Taj Shah's urs**: held on the death-date "روز دو شنبہ ہفتم ماہ بسیا[OCR?] کہ سمت ۱۹۰۱[OCR?] مطابق سنہ ۱۲۶۱[OCR?] بارہ سو سات[OCR?] ہجری" (p. 135).
  - Many people stay the night, and "رنگ[OCR?] ناچ بھی ہوتا ہی" (p. 135).
  - Money for the lamp ("چراغ") is also mentioned (p. 134).
- **Anarkali tomb, architecture and use**: the tomb is two-storeyed, with eight rooms on each storey, eight burjis and eight doors below and eight above (p. 137).
  - "اب سرکار کی عملداری میں" mirrored panels have been fitted (p. 137).
  - The cenotaph is kept for display ("واسطے نمائش") behind a small door that stays locked (p. 137).
  - The building was first brickwork "عہدِ اکبر کی"; it has now been whitewashed and a southern staircase built (p. 137).
  - "صاحب فرانسیس[OCR?]" once lived there (p. 137).
  - It is now "گرجا گھر": a red-stone cross about two feet long has been set on the central dome, and curtains hang at the doors (p. 137).
- **Endowment of the Sayyid's khanqah**: by British grace, per the report of "کرنیل سر منگمری[OCR?] لارنس[OCR?]", the revenue of Panj Graian is remitted and spent on "معارف خانقاہ سید مرحوم" (p. 139).
- **Shah Kalu Chishti's urs**: the saint left a bequest ("وصیت") for monthly expenses of a majlis and lamp-lighting (p. 144).
  - At the urs there are "طوائفاں[OCR?] و سماع قوالوں" and chiraghan in the evening, and halwa is distributed (p. 144).
- **Rasul Shahi faqirs**: they wear "رومال سفید", some white and some ochre ("گیروا[OCR?]"), and keep ash ("راکھ") (p. 144).
  - "شراب[OCR?]" appears in a damaged clause (p. 144). People show them respect ("ادب") (p. 144).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Syed Mahmud Bhakari[OCR?]: "کرامات انکی خیلے مشہور", his miracles are very famous, especially among Gujars; none are told (p. 137).
- Anarkali: the book reports a rumour, introduced with "سنا گیا", that her death was kept hidden out of fear, from which "معلوم ہوتا ہے کہ انارکلی مسموم ہوئی[OCR?]" (p. 136).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- On Anarkali's death the author sets the two accounts side by side ("کوئی تو کہتا ہے ... اور کوئی کہتا ہے"). He then leans to poisoning on the basis of the rumour (p. 136).
- The Rajab Ali Khan section is presented as his own account, copied "مطابق اصل" (as in the original), not the author's own composition (p. 138).

- "انارکلی ایک خوبصورت کنیز[OCR?] اکبر بادشاہ کی تھی" — "Anarkali was a beautiful slave-girl[OCR?] of Akbar Badshah." (p. 136)

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory**: heavy `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` throughout. Pp. 138–142 (the Rajab Ali narrative) are especially broken, and many clauses there are reported only in outline. Every quoted word keeps its flag.
- **The shrine at pp. 133–134 cannot be placed.** Its title falls before p. 133, and the chunk names the saint only as "حضرت". A consolidator should look in chunk 010.
- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri is mapped on a bare name used as a landmark** ("حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی", p. 134). The chunk gives no location for that tomb; weigh accordingly.
- **bibi-pak-daman**: "بی بی پاکداماں[OCR?]" carries an OCR flag (p. 139). Whether "خانقاہ حضرت بیوی[OCR?] صاحبان" (p. 137) is the same site is not stated.
- **Lahore-in-general bullet** (p. 142): place-level. The attachment is place-level, not site-specific.
- **Province bullet**: province-level, weigh accordingly. "ملک/ممالک پنجاب" is carried as printed; the chunk gives no boundary.
- **Anarkali (pp. 135–137) is not mapped by the "anarkali" toponym.** It is a named tomb that is not an archive row, and the book does not use the name as a locality for any archive row.
- **"علاقہ بہاولپور[OCR?]" (p. 138) is not mapped to the four Bahawalpur rows.** It records where a family settled and is not material on Bahawalpur's tradition; it is also OCR-flagged. This is a deviation the coordinator should confirm.
- **Panj Graian (near "پٹالہ[OCR?] ضلع گورداسپورہ"), Jalandhar, Ludhiana, Jagraon[OCR?], Kapurthala**: no archive rows. Panj Graian is not given a province (ruling (c)(4)).
- **"گنج سکھاں[OCR?]" and "شہید سکھاں[OCR?]" (p. 144)** might bear on a Lahore gurdwara row (e.g. Shaheed Ganj). The chunk does not say so, so they are left unmapped.
- **Taj Shah's death date contradicts itself**: "سنہ ۱۲۶۱[OCR?]" in figures against "بارہ سو سات[OCR?]" (1207) in words, alongside "سمت ۱۹۰۱[OCR?]" (p. 135). The age is "ایک سو[OCR?] دو برس" (p. 135). Check the PDF.
- **Rajab Ali Khan's dates do not fit together**: born "۱۸۷۲[OCR?]" (era not stated) (p. 140), yet appointed "۱۸۳۴[OCR?] ء" and resigned "۱۸۵۳[OCR?] ء" (p. 141). Also "سنہ ۱۲۳۳[OCR?] [margin] ۱۸[OCR?] ع" (p. 141). No conversion has been made.
- **Garbled names**: "دیوان حکم[OCR?]", "راجہ[OCR?] دنیا ماتھہ[OCR?]", "جرنیل بیچر[OCR?]", "کرنیل سر منگمری[OCR?] لارنس[OCR?]" and "عزیز الدین عالمگیر ثانی" are as printed; none is identified here. The Taj Shah chronogram's author "مفتی[OCR?] غلام سرمدی[OCR?]" is doubly flagged.
- **Folios** are printed on most pages, but folio 137 is missing at p. 143, which has a large image. Per the brief, no folio is cited.
