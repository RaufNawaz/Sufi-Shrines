# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 036 — pp. 394–403

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 394) inside an account whose heading is in the previous chunk (035). From the matching dates on p. 398, its subject reads as Shah Shahab al-Din, son of "حضرت موج دریا بخاری": a childhood story about his teacher, his death at "ٹبالہ [OCR?]" and burial on the road to Lahore, and the Sher Shah legend (pp. 394–396). The section continues with what the present sajjada nashin of Mauj Darya Bukhari told the author: Mauj Darya's two wives, sons and a long genealogy, the succession to the sajjada and a dispute over it (pp. 396–398). It then describes the khanqah of Shah Shahab al-Din, gives birth and death dates and chronograms (pp. 398–399), lists Mauj Darya's "صحیح النسب" descendants in Lahore and his silsila back through Bahauddin Zakariya (p. 399), and gives more genealogy (pp. 400–402). The second titled section, "احوال موضع کھوئی میراں" (p. 402), covers the village, its "کھوئی", and the khanqah of "حضرت سید [OCR?] حسین زنجانی" (p. 403), with lambardars, gardens and graves. The chunk ends on the heading "احوال خانقاہ حضرت حامد قاری علیہ رحمۃ اللہ الباری" and one line after it (p. 403).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author separates the sajjada nashins recorded by the sarkar (descended from the adopted Rahmat Shah) from Mauj Darya's "خاص" Bukhari descendants (p. 398). He also calls those in Lahore "سادات صحیح النسب" (p. 399).
- He gives the hadith "لعنۃ اللہ علی خارج النسب و داخل النسب [OCR?]" in the Sher Shah story, glossed in Urdu as a curse on whoever enters or leaves a lineage falsely (p. 395).
- He says he recorded Mauj Darya's descendants and lineage because "بہت لوگو کو اسکے دریافت کرنے کا شوق تہا" (p. 399).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is heavy throughout: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags, and the chronogram verses on p. 399 are almost wholly illegible. Every quotation keeps its flags. Folio markers are printed on pp. 394–400 ("385"–"39?", some unreadable) and none on pp. 401–403. No folio is cited, per the brief.
- **Subject of pp. 394–396.** The section heading is in chunk 035. The "حضرت" of the death, burial and Sher Shah stories is read as Shah Shahab al-Din because (1) he is the "صاحبزادی [OCR?]" of Mauj Darya in the opening story, (2) his death "اکہزار [OCR?] اکتالیس [OCR?]" (p. 394) matches "ایکہزار اکتالیس [OCR?]" / "۱۰۴۱ [OCR?]" for Shah Shahab al-Din (p. 398), and (3) "شاہ مصطفی" is called his son on both pages. Check against chunk 035.
- **Death day conflicts.** "۱۱ [OCR?] ماہ ذی الحج" (p. 394) against the chronogram's "۱۰ [OCR?] ماہ [illegible] ربیع [OCR?]" (p. 399). Both are flagged. Do not reconcile.
- **Mauj Darya's own dates (p. 398)** are attached to "حضرت فرزند علی [OCR?]" in a damaged sentence that also names Mauj Darya. Read as printed: birth "ایکہزار [OCR?] [illegible]", death "ایکہزار [OCR?] ایکسو [OCR?] گیارہ [OCR?]". Whether these are Mauj Darya's or a son's ("فرزند") is uncertain. They are all flagged numerals; do not compute.
- **Silsila wording (p. 399).** "حضرت سید صفی الدین [illegible] باپ کے خادم" makes a Safi al-Din Mauj Darya's father and pir. Elsewhere Safi al-Din is his eldest son (p. 396). This is carried as printed; it may be two men of the same name.
- **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam** is mapped on the flagged "رکن عالم [OCR?]" inside "مسمی [OCR?] دریا [illegible] رکن عالم [OCR?]". It is a bare name in a chain; weigh accordingly.
- **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya** is mapped by the person rule. It is a bare name in a silsila, and the chunk gives no site or date.
- **"سید جلال الدین"** in the silsila and **"سید جلال الدین حیدر"** in the genealogy are NOT mapped to shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari. The chunk gives no identifier beyond the name.
- **"زبان ملتانی" (p. 396)** names a language. The phrase is not Multan material, so the 8 `multan` ids are not attached.
- **shrine-of-miran-hussain-zanjani-zanjani-sahib** is mapped on "حضرت سید [OCR?] حسین زنجانی" (p. 403). The book does not write "میراں" with his name; it appears only in the village name "کھوئی میراں". The index row's location reads "Chah Miran", and the book glosses "کھوئی" as "چاہ خرد [OCR?]".
