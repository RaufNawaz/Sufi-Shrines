# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 066 — pp. 722–730

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 722) with the tail of an account of Begumpura (its section heading is in the previous chunk): a Nawab who came from Kabul to Lahore, the place's resettlement, a Sikh regiment quartered there, and a family's lost property under Sikh rule and the English courts. It then runs through these sections as titled in the book: "حال ٹبگا[OCR?] کبن[OCR?]" (pp. 722–723; a dome in Begumpura which the book identifies from a written source as a tomb, and where Maharaja Sher Singh first stayed); "احوال باغبان پورہ" (pp. 723–726; the founding of Baghbanpura and a long genealogy and land account of the family of Mehr Manga, darogha of "سنہلا باغ"); "حال مسجد قدیمہ اندرون باغبان پورہ" (p. 726); "حال گورستان مہر منگا" (p. 727); "حال مسجد خواجہ ایاز" (pp. 727–728); "احوال تکیہ نصر اللہ شاہ" (p. 728); "مقبرہ حضرت عبد الغنی صاحب مرحوم" (pp. 728–729); "حال مکرر آبادی باغبان پورہ" (p. 729, with a population table); "مقبرہ صادق خان المشہور مقبرہ نواب جعفر خان" (pp. 729–730); and "حال باغ مہابت خان المشہور محبت خان والا باغ" (p. 730), which breaks off mid-description at the end of the chunk. Damage is very heavy throughout; much of pp. 722, 726 and 730 cannot be reported. The chunk is about Baghbanpura and Begumpura, so it has very little direct archive-row material.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- Begumpura ("بیگم پورہ"): settled by the local zamindars (damaged); in "سمت ۱۸۹۵[OCR?]" a Sikh cantonment ("جہاں ولی[OCR?] سکھوں کی") was fixed there with a regiment of cavalry and guns ("سواران ... توباں[OCR?]") (p. 722). Plundered in Ahmad Shah's time (p. 724).
- A family of Begumpura (names illegible) lost hundreds of houses under the Sikhs; after the English ("عدالت انگریزی کا انصاف") they petitioned but were refused; the author laments that others own the property while heirs survive (pp. 722–722).
- "حال ٹبگا[OCR?] کبن[OCR?]" (heading damaged): a dome in Begumpura; "بعد تحقیقات کتاب عمرات[OCR?] الہند میں لکھا پایا کہ یہ مقبرہ یحیی خان اور محمد شاہ کا[OCR?]"; death "سنہ ۱۱۶۰[OCR?]" (p. 723). Maharaja Sher Singh first stayed in this dome when he came to Lahore, on "یوم ۱۲ صفر المظفر", and thought it auspicious; a chronogram was composed by "میان فریدالدین[OCR?] مزنگی[OCR?] عباسی", now teacher at "مدرسہ فرنگ[OCR?]": "...کہ کھڑک سنگھ حنبشت[OCR?] و شیر سنگھ نبشت" (p. 723).
- Baghbanpura (village): "مشرق قریہ[OCR?] لاہور بفاصلہ تین میل", very near and west of "باغ سنہلا[OCR?]"; to the south was "اسحاق پورہ", to the north "بابو پورہ", settled "بعہد شاہجہانی" (p. 723). Ishaqpura's houses reached the garden and spoiled its look, so it was removed by royal order (p. 723). Babupura was sold to "مہر حدیثکا[OCR?]" (also "مہر حدیثہا[OCR?]"), father of Mehr Manga[?], who named it "باغبان پورہ" and built houses; a walled graveyard from those buildings survives (p. 723).
- Mehr Manga (printed "مہر منگا", also "مہر سنگا[OCR?]", "مہر منگا[OCR?]"): darogha of "باغ دلکشا واقع شاہدرہ"; as a boy he came there and was allowed inside when the begums and princesses visited; through that acquaintance he was made darogha of "سہنلا[OCR?] باغ" after it was built, and had charge of it and other royal gardens until his death (p. 723). Family (pp. 723–726): elder son "مہر انور علی"; "حافظ لطف اللہ"; "حافظ عظیم اللہ", "حافظ محمد"; "حافظ رحیم اللہ" (made darogha of a garden in "قلعہ لاہور واقع نقارخانہ"), "رحیم بخش"; "مولوی قادر بخش" (made kar-mukhtar by "الہی بخش جبر نیل[OCR?]"); "کریم بخش"; "محمد بخش", "نبی بخش", "الہی بخش", "خدا بخش", "غلام مصطفے", "امام الدین", and further descendants (pp. 724–725). Hafiz Lutfullah died "سمت ۱۸۴[OCR?]" (digits incomplete) (p. 724).
- Mian Nabi Bakhsh: darogha of "سنہلا باغ" and numberdar of Baghbanpura "حسب منظوری گورنمنٹ بلا شرکت غیری"; has horses, carriages and servants (p. 725). Said to be in "خاندان چشتیہ" of "حضرت خواجہ سلیمان صاحب کر والا[OCR?]", "جو خلیفہ حضرت نور محمد بہبل[OCR?] کے تھی" (p. 725). He is the book's informant for the Khwaja Ayaz account ("زبانی میان نبی بخش") (p. 727).
- Kotli Abdur Rahman ("کوٹلی عبد الرحمن"): one kos from Baghbanpura; Abdur Rahman (description damaged) had a mosque and haveli there; later resettled by "جمعدار[OCR?] خوجال[OCR?] سگلر[OCR?]" keeping the old name (p. 726).
- Old mosque in Baghbanpura, "محلہ مسیت[OCR?] والہ": built "لعہد شاہجہانی" by "مسماۃ فاطمہ ست[OCR?] مہر سنگاہی[OCR?]" (damaged); imam now "ملاں کریم بخش" (p. 726).
- Graveyard of Mehr Manga: adjoining Baghbanpura on the north, with Mehr Manga's grave; Shah Jahan-period wall three gaz high; at its back is the wall of Khwaja Ayaz's mosque; a faqir lives in it; graves of Mehr Manga's brothers "مہر علی و محمد فاضل" and descendants; four bigha of land attached "معاف" (p. 727). West of it a tomb of the master-builder of "سنہلا باغ", now only a decayed grave-marker; the book calls this "مشہور" (p. 727).
- Mosque of Khwaja Ayaz (pp. 727–728): north of the graveyard, "بہت عمدہ عالیشان"; per Nabi Bakhsh, Khwaja Ayaz was an amir in "شاہجہان آباد" in Shah Jahan's reign, connected with "نواب علی مردان خان"; he sent several thousand rupees to Mehr Manga to build a garden north of "سنہلا باغ" (the "خواجہ کا باغ"); Mehr Manga built this mosque with the surplus (pp. 727–728). The garden later became government property; the mosque is held by Mehr Manga's descendants (p. 728). Square hauz with a former fountain; a marble slab inscription beginning "ملفوظ[OCR?] فی[OCR?] مسجد لمسجد[OCR?] اسس[OCR?] علی التقوی" and naming "بندہ درگاہ محمد ایاز", now broken (p. 728).
- Takia of Nasrullah Shah: east of the mosque, with his grave; settled by Mehr Manga; faqir "علی بخش" is its khadim (p. 728).
- Tomb of "حضرت عبد الغنی صاحب": in a corner of the takia, north of the garden and Baghbanpura; square brick platform, blue-tiled ("رنگ نیلا") work partly visible; a sweeper/caretaker "غلی[OCR?] بخش ساکن تکیہ نصر اللہ شاہ" (pp. 728–729).
- Tomb of Sadiq Khan, "المشہور مقبرہ نواب جعفر خان" (pp. 729–730): south of the road to "سنہلا باغ"; waist-high brick platform, square dome; faqir "صادق علیشاہ" lives there and has built a well. The book says it is really the tomb of "نواب صادق خان طہرالی[OCR?]", father of Nawab Jafar Khan, died "۱۲۴[OCR?]" (digits incomplete) "عہد شاہ جہانی" in Shawwal; "بعض لکھتی میں" it is the Jafar Khan of Alamgir's court (p. 730).
- Takia of "مولوی مرف[OCR?] الدین" / "مولوی شرف دین" near the tomb, about ten years old, with their graveyard (p. 730).
- Garden of Mahabat Khan, "المشہور محبت خان والا باغ": south of Baghbanpura; old gate on the west; "بارسی[OCR?] سوداگرون" added an upper storey with windows; a large arch where "تین ہزار آدمی نماز[OCR?]" could sit; breaks off (p. 730).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Land of Mehr Manga's family: two kinds, ancestral and self-acquired; "قریب چار صد بیکے[OCR?]" in Baghbanpura, shared half-and-half between the descendants of Azimullah and Hafiz Muhammad (p. 725). Shares in named wells ("چاہ بارہ دری والی", others damaged) and garden land measured in "نگہ[OCR?]"/"بیگہ[OCR?]", including "ایک سو چار بیگہ[OCR?]" (pp. 725–726).
- Karim Bakhsh also takes government contracts ("ٹھیکہ جات سرکاری") and practises some medicine (p. 725).
- Numberdari dispute: before the settlement Nabi Bakhsh and Karim Bakhsh shared numberdari equally; Karim Bakhsh says Nabi Bakhsh became sole numberdar "مہربانی عملہ بندوبست سے", though by "حکم شرعی" they were equal sharers (p. 729).
- Waqf: the book quotes "الوقف لا یملک[OCR?]" and says a mosque is waqf, yet this mosque is held privately (damaged) (p. 728).
- Resettlement (p. 729): after "سنہ ۱۱[OCR?] ہجری" (digits incomplete) Baghbanpura was wholly deserted; on the day "مہر محمد صالح[OCR?]", son-in-law of Hafiz Lutfullah, died ("بیسویں صفر"), Hafiz Muhammad was born; at the burial the notables urged Lutfullah to resettle; he proclaimed that whoever came within a set time and occupied land or buildings would own them; people came in crowds.
- Population as printed (p. 729): Muslim cultivator men "چار سو بیس[OCR?]"; women "تین سو ..." (damaged); non-cultivator men "آٹھہ سو بنانوین[OCR?] ۸۹۹[OCR?]", women "سات سو نوے"; Hindu cultivator men "اٹھارہ", women "دس", non-cultivators "ایک سو انتالیس[OCR?] ۱۳۹[OCR?]"; a houses/total figure "۹۷۰[OCR?]" (pp. 729). Totals do not reconcile as printed.
