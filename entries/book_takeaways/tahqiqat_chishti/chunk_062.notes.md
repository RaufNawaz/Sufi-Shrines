# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 062 — pp. 683–691

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 683) with the tail of an account whose heading is in the previous chunk: a "مکان سری چند" in Lahore. The visible text covers a sat-nam formula given to the author by "جمنا داس[OCR?] صاحب", a jagir from an emperor to "گورو مہر چند", Sri Chand's travels and arrival in Lahore, a tree grown from a planted datan twig, and Sri Chand's long life and celibacy (pp. 683–684). The book then runs through these titled sections in order: "حال مختصر موزوں[OCR?] سکھوں کے گورؤں کا", a short history of the ten Sikh gurus with an interposed "لطیفہ بطور جملہ معترضہ" (pp. 684–686); "حال مکان نہان[OCR?] ماتا[OCR?]", a Hindu place of worship with a baoli and baradari south of Lahore (pp. 686–687); "حال سرائے گولاوالہ[OCR?]", a Jahangir-era sarai near Ichhra[OCR?] (pp. 687–689); "حال آوہ[OCR?] بدہو کا", the brick-kiln of Budhu east of Lahore, with a curse story involving a murid of Mian Mir (pp. 689–690); and "احوال حضرت شاہ جمال قادری سہروردی رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" (pp. 690–691), which breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk. ## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- A twig used as a datan by "گورو [مہر] چند" was planted in the ground and by God's power became a large tree, still standing; "صاحبان" cut it down but it [grew] again (p. 683).
- Faqir Abd al-Haq, Mian Mir's murid, came cold in the rain to Budhu's kiln and asked for fire; refused and beaten out as "دیوانہ پاگل", he cursed: "تیرا یہ پڑاوہ[OCR?] خراب ہو جایا گا[OCR?]"; the bricks would not bake; Budhu fell ill, his father told him in a dream to seek pardon; he fell at the faqir's feet and was forgiven (pp. 689–690).
- Shah Jamal's dhamal ("یعنی رقص بحالت وجد عارفانہ") for three days at night: one storey of the building "جو اب موجود ہے[OCR?]" remained and "بقیہ پانچ منزلیں زمین میں غرق[OCR?] ہو گئیں" (p. 691).
- Shah Jamal gave the childless Khatri "دیوول[OCR?]" two melons for two sons; he peeled one, so one son would be Hindu and one Muslim ("ہمارا خادم"); the second, born after four years "مجنون", was named Shaikh Fakhr al-Din (p. 691).
- Shah Jamal ordered Fakhr al-Din to empty his house; once only earthen vessels remained, "فوراً وہ مکان گر پڑا" (p. 691).
