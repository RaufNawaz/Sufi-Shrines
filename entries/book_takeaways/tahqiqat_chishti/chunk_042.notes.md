# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 042 — pp. 460–470

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 460) with the end of a siege episode at the khanqah of "حضرت میان وڈا صاحب": Raja Hira Singh's Sikh army besieges Raja "سوحیت[OCR?] سنگھ" there, and he is killed. **The heading of that section is in the previous chunk (041).** A badly damaged p. 461 follows, with a ground plan of a shrine enclosure, wall measurements, a table of resident dervishes and a chronogram (pp. 461–462). Then come three titled sections. "حال مکان زرگران" (p. 462) is about the goldsmiths' graveyard and the grave of Alif Shah, and is followed by "حال الف شاہ صاحب مرحوم" (pp. 462–463). "حال تکیہ بہاگی شاہ" (pp. 463–467) is a hostile account of Bhago (Bhage) Shah, his murshids, his two shajras and the Naushahi way of "حال" (ecstatic states). Finally the author turns to sama: "احوال سماع معہ احکام علمائے طریقت" (pp. 467–470) and "بیان اباحت سماع" (p. 470). The chunk breaks off mid-sentence. Three archive rows are touched: Bibi Pak Daman by name, and Shah Sulaiman Nuri and Naushah Ganj Bakhsh as links in a shajra.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Alif Shah**: "عام مشہور ہے" (it is commonly said) that he came fighting as far as this spot ("تن [illegible] بر سر[OCR?] … لڑتا ہوا یہاں تک آیا"). When a woman looked at him in wonder, "توسر[OCR?] [illegible] ہوگئی[OCR?]". The author adds: "اگر یہ تذکرہ با واقعہ ہے تو یہ قبر بھی قدیمی ہے" (p. 462).
- **Ajmer**: anyone who refused an ijazat-nama "فی الفور برباد ہوا" (was ruined at once) (p. 468).
- **Ghaus al-Azam and Muin al-Din**: Ghaus al-Azam had food prepared for Khwaja Muin al-Din, who would not eat. When it became clear that Muin al-Din's inclination was towards sama, Ghaus al-Azam had an old qawwal found and sent to him. The saying is attributed: "جہاں محی الدین گیلانی [illegible] اور خواجہ [illegible] الدین ہیں ہر مجلس ہو وہاں سماع عبادت عظیم ہے" (pp. 468–469).
- **Nizam al-Din**: in a sama majlis he moved once. A divine command came that a calamity would fall in return. He prayed that it fall on himself, and "ہائی[OCR?] مبارک حضرت کا [illegible] خشک[OCR?] ہو گیا" (the hazrat's blessed [foot/leg?] dried up). It was pardoned after a long time (p. 469).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Hostile to Bhage Shah**:
  - The author calls Bhage Shah a "جعلساز" (forger) who invented a "جعلی شجرہ" (forged shajra) and wrote his own khadims' names into it (p. 465).
  - He says those who gather at Bhage Shah's place have no "خوف خدا" (fear of God): "اللہ اونکو ہدایت کرے" (may God guide them) (p. 464).
- **Doubts Naushahi ecstasy**: "جو لوگ پنج ارکان مسلمان کے بھی نہیں جانتے وہ کیونکر وجد و حالت میں آجاتے ہیں" (p. 466).
- **His own Chishti allegiance**: Muin al-Din is "جو ہمارے خاندان چشتیہ کے بزرگ[OCR?] پیر ہیں" (p. 468), and sama is "بدرجہ کمال" (at its fullest) in the Chishti silsila (p. 468).
- **The ulama are split**: some say haram, some halal, some mubah (p. 468). The author's elders taught that sama is "ایک پرندہ[OCR?]" that carries a person wherever their inclination lies (p. 468).
- **Ghaus al-Azam's ban**: by report ("مسموع ہو کہ"), he ordered "سماع بالکل نہ ہو", and the order was so obeyed that no musical instruments or qawwals remained (p. 468).
- **Against sama as play**: those who treat sama as a game, beating their heads for show, commit "بے ادبیاں[OCR?]" (p. 470).
- **On the lawfulness of sama**: the heart holds a divine secret, struck like fire from iron and stone. Sama increases whatever is already in the heart: for one with "آتش عشق باطل" it is "زہر قاتل" and "حرام مطلق". The "ظاہر پرست" call it haram because they deny that love of God can descend into the heart, and the author calls their view "غلط". He refers readers to "کیمیائے سعادت" (p. 470).
