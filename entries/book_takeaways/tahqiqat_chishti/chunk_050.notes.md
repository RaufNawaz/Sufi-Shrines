# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 050 — pp. 550–563

The chunk is entirely about jogis, apart from its last section. It opens mid-section (p. 550) on a jogi seat at "مقام کرانہ" and Raja Bharthari. **That section's heading is in the previous chunk (049).** The titled sections then run in this order: "حال راجہ گوپی چند کا" (p. 550); "حال راجہ سلوان متعلق حال جوگیاں[OCR?]" (p. 553), the story of Puran, Rani Luna and Raja Rasalu, set at Sialkot (pp. 553–559); "حال جعفر[OCR?] پیر متعلقہ[OCR?] مذہبہ[OCR?] جوگیان" (p. 559), on a line of Muslim jogis (pp. 559–561); "حال نوناتھہ چوراسی سدہ" and "نوناتھہ جو شاستر میں تحریر ہے" (p. 561), lists of the nine Naths and the jogi panths (pp. 561–562); "بیان دستور مقررہ باہمی جوگیان" (p. 562), on the jogi panchayat; and "حال بنیا و تھان بھیرو" (p. 562), on a Bhairo than at "موضع اچھرا", which runs on to the chunk's end (p. 563). pp. 551–553 and 555–557 are very heavily damaged. Four archive persons and one archive toponym are touched: Gorakhnath, Guru Nanak, Maharaja Ranjit Singh and Sialkot. There is no Lahore-in-general material.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- On the Gopi Chand story, the author prefers one version ("مگر اصح[OCR?] یہ ہے") and calls the version with the Guru Nanak exchange "قرین قیاس نہیں" (not plausible) (p. 552).
- Another account is called "غلط[OCR?]" in a damaged sentence (p. 553).
- The author rejects counting Machhandar Nath and Gorakh Nath among the nine Naths, because all the Naths were their chelas (p. 562).
- The nine Naths are given in two lists:
  - one apparently from jogi report (p. 561);
  - one from "بھاگوت" ("نوناتھہ جو شاستر میں تحریر ہے"), which traces a line through "نابھی[OCR?]", "رکھہ دیو[OCR?]" and "بھارت", after whom "بھارت کھنڈ" was named, and mentions nine "کھنڈ" (pp. 561–562).
- On Ja'far's followers: "ظاہر دیکھنے سے انہیں[OCR?] کچھ فرق معلوم نہیں ہوتا … مگر اتنا[OCR?] فرق ہے کہ یہ … مسلمان جوگی ہیں" (outwardly no difference is seen, except that these are Muslim jogis) (p. 560).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is heavy throughout, with dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags (including multi-line gaps). pp. 551, 553, 555, 557 and 559 are mostly illegible, and p. 563 is crossed by an ink stroke ("[image: a heavy diagonal ink stroke …]"). - **Opening fragment (p. 550)** belongs to a section headed in chunk 049. "کرانہ" is carried as printed and not identified.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** Lahore appears only as the place the queen returned to (p. 556), as part of "جو[illegible] لاہور وغیرہ" (p. 560), and as the location of a Bania's shop (pp. 562–563). None of these is material on the city as such, so the 35 `lahore` ids are not attached.
- **Ichhra is not mapped.** "موضع اچھرا" (pp. 562–563) is the location of a specific non-archive site (the Bhairo than / well), so the `ichhra` toponym rows (shah-jamal, shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal) are not attached. "رانی اچھراں[OCR?]" (pp. 554, 558) is a person, Puran's mother, not the place. The consolidator can weigh this against ruling (a).
- **"دروازہ شاہ عالمی"** (p. 562) is not mapped to darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz, whose location is "Shah Alam Market". The chunk names only a gate, as a shop location.
- **Peshawar is not mapped.** "بطرف[OCR?] پیشاور[OCR?] ایک راجہ" (p. 558) places a legendary raja "toward Peshawar", not material on Peshawar itself, so the 10 `peshawar` ids are not attached. Recorded for the consolidator.
- **"[illegible] کشمیر[OCR?]"** (p. 560) is not a province bullet: it is a damaged phrase attached to Sher Singh's name.
- **Sialkot mapping is place-level.** It rests on Raja Salwan's gaddi at Sialkot, Puran's well "موجود" there, and Puran's return there (pp. 553, 554, 557), every instance of "سیالکوٹ" flagged `[OCR?]`. The well is itself a specific non-archive site. Attachment is place-level, not site-specific; weigh accordingly.
- **Gorakhnath mapping is person-level** (2 rows, no site named). Some "گورو" / "گورو جی" references in the Puran section (pp. 556–558) are read as Gorakhnath only because "گورو گورکھ ناتھ" is named at pp. 555–556.

- **Ranjit Singh is a bare name**: "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ[OCR?]" (p. 560), in a broken sentence. The mapping is by the person rule only.
- **"پدران[OCR?]"** (pp. 556, 557) is read as Puran from context ("پدران[OCR?] نی تامل[OCR?] کرکے کہا کہ اے راجہ", p. 557). The printed form is kept.
