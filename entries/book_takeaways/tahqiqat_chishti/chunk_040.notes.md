# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 040 — pp. 437–448

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 437) on a saint known as "پیر روڑان والہ", identified by an informant as "میر عبد اللہ شاہ گیلانی", whose story involves Nawab Ali Mardan Khan. **That section's heading is in the previous chunk (039).** Titled sections follow in this order: "احوال گنبد نصرت خان المشہور مقبرہ نشتر خان" (p. 438), with a sub-section "حال نشتر خان" (p. 440); "حال مسجد نواب ذکریا خان مرحوم" (pp. 440–442), with its Persian inscriptions; "مقبرہ حضرت شاہ مسکین امری المشہور پیر عنبری" (p. 442); "حال عمارت حجیرہ[OCR?] سیّد رحمت اللہ" (pp. 442–443); "حال بارہ دری عمارت قدیمہ" (p. 443); "احوال مقبرہ شرف النسا بیگم" (pp. 443–444); "احوال مزار کریم شاہ مرحوم" (p. 444); "حال مقبرہ مولوی نظام الدین المشہور پیر مہکا[OCR?]" (pp. 444–446), which includes the author's own genealogy; "احوال تکیہ ملحقہ موضع خیر گڑہ المشہور گدی شاہ" (pp. 446–447), mostly a history of the village's numbardars; and "احوال مزار حضرت محمد اسماعیل المشہور میاں وڈا" (pp. 447–448). The chunk breaks off mid-story on p. 448. Three archive rows are touched: Madho Lal Hussain (Zakariya Khan's mosque), Mian Mir (Pir Ambari, Pir Mahka) and Langar Makhdoom (Mian Wadda), the last a held identity question.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Pir Roran-wala, healing practice**: anyone with [illegible] takes a pebble ("روڑا") from the grave, ties it in a thread round the neck, and on recovery brings a nadhr; the heap of pebbles keeps growing (p. 437). Zamindars say "اکثر شہر [illegible] انکی نذر مقررہ ہے" (p. 437).
- **Ali Mardan Khan's alms**: food distributed to the poor at the mazar, flowers "دو سیر" laid there (p. 438).
- **Zakariya Khan's mosque, state and repair**: plaster coming off in places; parts fallen and rebuilt by Munshi Ghulam Rasul; only the bath-house, courtyard, enclosure and arch traces remain of Zakariya Khan's building (p. 441). Kashi-tile inscription panels in blue, white and black described (p. 441).
- **Pir Mahka, cures**: those "مہکون[OCR?] والہ" who went to him were cured by his prayer; still, those who go and offer "جاروب و سہرہ گل" (a broom and a garland of flowers) are cured, "چنانچہ اتبک[OCR?] یہ منتا[OCR?] بماری[OCR?] ہے" (p. 445). The graves have [illegible] through "کم[OCR?] توجہی" (p. 445).
- **Baradari, property loss** (author's comment): the descendants of "نواب خان بہادر مرحوم" exist but through poverty cannot take possession or sue ("دیوانی مین نالش") (p. 443).
- **Khair Garh numbardari** (pp. 446–447):
  - "حبیب اللہ" (later "حبیب اللہ کمبو[OCR?]"), living near Lahore after the Abdali flight, asked for "مقام خیر گڑہ" because houses and walls with "بروج" stood there; the hakim granted it "حسب رواج ملک" (pp. 446–447).
  - He settled in "گوشہ لگنی[OCR?]" and took the northern-wall cells, still held by his descendants (p. 447).
  - Settlers: "شیخ [illegible]", "نوز[OCR?] محمد" in "برج لگنی[OCR?]", "مراد بخش جد کلان محمد بخش نمبردار حال" (p. 447).
  - Numbardars in succession: Habibullah; his son "رکن الدین"; "مہر وارث[OCR?]"; "مہر پیراج[OCR?]" "لعہد[OCR?] سکھاں جمعدار"; then under "تعلقداری انگریزی" "پیر بخش" alone; then "منجانب سرکار محمد بخش بیٹا اوسکا" (p. 447).
  - A claim "با جلاس جناب مسٹر را برٹ[OCR?] ایلیس[OCR?] [illegible] صاحب بہادر" was dismissed, with leave to complain at the next settlement ("عہد ومست[OCR?] ثانی") (p. 447).
  - Land: "تیرہ[OCR?] [illegible] مزروعہ ملحقہ اس موضع" (p. 447).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Pir Roran-wala**: an amulet ("تعویذ") cured a fever in Ali Mardan Khan's household at once (p. 437). He foretold Ali Mardan Khan's death in Lahore, and said the Agra dome was "جناب الہی میں منظور نہوا" (p. 437).
- **Pir Ambari**: "ہزار الکرامات[OCR?]"; the name "امری[OCR?]" explained from Mian Mir's word; the passage is garbled (p. 442).
- **Pir Mahka**: "صدہا کرامات" (p. 445).
- **Mian Wadda**: a student sent to fetch him found him reciting the Quran while [illegible] burned "قدرت الہی سی"; Makhdoom Abd al-Karim came, saw, and relieved him of the fire duty (p. 448). He carried all the neighbours' milk vessels on his head together, which convinced Makhdoom Abd al-Karim he was "ولی کامل" (p. 448).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Heavy throughout: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]`, two `[margin]` notes illegible, the Nusrat Khan architecture (pp. 438–440), the Pir Ambari section (p. 442), the Sharf-un-Nisa continuation (p. 444) and the Mian Wadda story (p. 448) partly unreadable. Transcriber brackets "[حافظ]" and "[آبا اجداد]" (p. 447) are carried as printed. Folio markers (433–440) are printed but not cited.
- **Opening fragment (p. 437)** continues a section headed in chunk 039; "پیر روڑان والہ" and "میر عبد اللہ شاہ گیلانی" are not identified with any row.
- **madho-lal-hussain**: the p. 440 phrase "مزار حضرت [illegible] و ہو [illegible] حسین[OCR?] صاحب" is damaged; the mapping rests on "حضرت مادھو لال حسین" (p. 442, twice) and "لال حسین" in the inscription (p. 441). The book does not name Baghbanpura or Shalimar.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir**: "میا میر[OCR?]" is flagged on p. 442; p. 444 reads "میا میر" unflagged after an `[illegible]`. Both are locative or discipular mentions only.
- **langer-makhdoom — held identity question, not resolved.** Mapped as the book names it ("موضع لنگر مخدوم عبدالکریم صاحب", p. 448). The book's makhdoom is "عبدالکریم"; the index row's principal figure is Makhdoom Burhan-ud-din, Chiniot. The chunk names no district or city, and the river's name is illegible. Weigh accordingly.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** Lahore appears only as a location ("لاہور سی[OCR?] ... چار کوس", p. 442), in a prediction about Ali Mardan Khan (p. 437), in titles ("خان اعظم لاہور", p. 440; "قاصیاں[OCR?] لاہور", p. 444; "حاکمان لاہور", p. 446). None is material on the city as such, so the 35 `lahore` ids are not attached. The Abdali plunder (p. 446) names no place legibly ("[illegible] بیگم [illegible] کر لوٹا").
- **No province bullet.** "ذکریا خان صوبہ پنجاب" (p. 441) is a governor's title in an inscription under "محمد شاہ", not a statement about Punjab; not mapped under ruling (c). Recorded here for the consolidator.
- **"قلعہ" (p. 447)** near Mian Wadda's khanqah is not mapped to the `lahore fort` rows: the qualifier is illegible.
- **Agra, Qandahar, Shahjahanabad, Gilan, Iran** (p. 437): no archive rows.
