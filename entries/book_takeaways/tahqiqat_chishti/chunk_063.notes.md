# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 063 — pp. 692–701

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 692) with the tail of an account of "حضرت شاہ جمال"; that section's heading is in the previous chunk. The tail covers the urs and income of his khanqah, the family of Sheikh Fakhr-ud-Din and Sheikh Amir Bakhsh "حوالدار[OCR?]", the line of sweepers (jarub-kash) and mujawirs, his chilla-places, the khanqah buildings and graves, a marble inscription, the legend of the sealed hujra, and his spiritual chain, ending with his death date (pp. 692–696). Then come three titled sections in order. First, "حال حضرت فتح شاہ مست رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" (pp. 696–697), followed by "حال عمارت موجودہ خانقاہ فتا شاہ[OCR?]" (p. 697). Second, "ذکر حضرت شاہ بوتراب المشہور [illegible] شاہ گدا[OCR?] رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" (pp. 698–701): his genealogy, Qadiri chain, a chronogram for Wajih-ud-Din Gujarati, his six khalifas, his sajjada line, a bhandara dispute, the khanqah buildings, an anecdote about Nur Jahan, and a list of trees. Third, "تتمہ احوال قاضی محمد افضل المشہور بہ[OCR?] فضل گدا" (p. 701), which breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk. Damage is heavy throughout, especially on pp. 692, 694, 696 and 700. Folio markers are printed but are not cited here (brief rule).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Shah Jamal khanqah: urs and repairs are funded from an assigned source. Thursday chadhat in cash and kind is shared among the keepers (p. 692). Income is about 300 rupees a year, and there is a dispute between the Fakhr-ud-Din family and the sweeper-mujawirs over attendance (p. 693). Chilla-places still receive vows (p. 693).
- Fata Shah[OCR?] khanqah: at the urs on "ہفتم شوال" (stated for the Gobindwal place) the custom is "روٹی گوشت با دال[OCR?]" for those present (p. 697). Land at the mazar, "زمین مزروعہ و غیر مزروعہ", has been "معافی[OCR?]" since earlier times (damaged) (p. 697). Thursday attendance and chadhat appear in a damaged line at the top of p. 697.
- Shah Gada: land at the site is "قدیم سے معاف"; its yield is split into five shares, among Kali Shah, "لڈو[OCR?] می[OCR?] شاہ", Khair Shah, Mamun Shah and one illegible name. Some sharers do not live there "مگر حصہ لیتی[OCR?] ہیں" (pp. 699).
- The book says the present faqirs of this place abstain, "بلکہ حقہ تک نفرت ہے" (p. 699).
- Bhandara dispute (pp. 699–700): Shah Gada's faqirs became so numerous that any bhandara host was strained by sharing with them. It was then proposed that hosts give Shah Gada's faqirs [a fixed share] (p. 699). At an urs, a khadim of Shah Gada complained to the sajjada nashin about his share and was slapped. Shah Gada's faqirs then looted the bhandara. A suit "سرکار میں نالش" was considered and dropped. A named elder (illegible) urged reconciliation; "شاہ محمد" accepted it but the "لال حسینی" faqirs did not (p. 700).
- Four years ago ("عرصہ چہار سال کا") disagreement among these faqirs led to a petition ("عرضی احوال") sent toward "اجمیر شریف". No answer came (p. 700). Now at the urs a few faqirs gather and give fatiha (p. 700).
- Shah Gada khanqah buildings: an old brick wall "عہد باستان[OCR?]", a well with a wooden wheel, kothas, dalans including a hexagonal one ("مسدس[OCR?]"), and a "تہہ گاہ[OCR?] مدورہ[OCR?]" (p. 700). Graves of khadims follow (Nadir Shah, one unnamed, "جب[OCR?] شاہ", Nasir-ud-Din[OCR?] Shah, Shah Wali[OCR?] Shah) (p. 701). There is a tahkhana, a one-dome mosque painted black, and a decayed well. ## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Shah Jamal's sealed hujra (p. 695). The book gives two versions:
  - In the first, he used to worship in the hujra and once went in for a chilla. After thirty days, before the chilla ended, the roof of the outer door [gave way]. The khuddam wanted to bring him out, but a voice from inside said "جو کچھ ہونا تھا سو ہو گیا" and told them to close the hujra door and build a grave-mark on top. This was done at once. The book says "تمام مشائخ لوگ متفق اللسان ہیں".
  - In the second, which "بعض اشخاص" relate, he went in at zuhr and came out at asr. One Thursday in Rabi al-Thani he told the khuddam to close the door from outside. After Sheikh Fakhr-ud-Din did so, he indicated in a dream that a grave should be built on top.
  - A damaged earlier passage (p. 694) also has "در بند کر دو[OCR?]".
- Fateh Shah Mast: someone came to him and his whole skin ("تمام پوست[OCR?]") [was affected]. At the saint's gaze ("نظر[OCR?]") his body became whole ("بدن سالم ہو گیا") (p. 696). The passage is heavily damaged.
- Fateh Shah Mast: the Lahore kotwal was overcome by longing at first sight and left everything to serve him (p. 696).
- Shah Gada and Nur Jahan: the elephant exchange, after which she left ashamed (p. 701).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- On Shah Gada and Ghaus al-Azam, the author reports a tradition ("روایات"; the preceding word is damaged, "[illegible] پنجاب[OCR?]") that Shah Gada was present "بحضور … غوث الاعظم". He rejects it: "مگر یہ امر درست نہیں کیوں کہ حضرت شاہ گدا صاحب [illegible] غوث اعظم سے بہت [illegible] ہوئی[OCR?] ہیں" (p. 700).
- On the faqirs' name and custom, the author says "بعد تحقیقات کاملہ بدقت تمام یہ معلوم ہوئی", and then gives the bhandara story (p. 699).
- On Fata Shah's devotees, the book infers them from the "شجرہ[OCR?]": "کی تحریر سے پایا جاتا ہے" (p. 697).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory:** the chunk carries heavy `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags on almost every line. Pages 692, 694, 696 and 700 are mostly illegible. All dates are partial: Shah Jamal's death year is illegible (p. 696), the inscription date "۱۲۹[OCR?]۹[OCR?]" is doubly flagged (p. 695), Fateh Shah's death is "ایک ہزار ایک سو[OCR?] [illegible]" (p. 696), Shah Gada's death year is illegible (p. 698), and the "بارہ سو [illegible]" dates are on pp. 692 and 694.
- **shah-jamal identity:** I mapped the subject by name ("حضرت شاہ جمال") as the book gives it. The section's heading and location are in the previous chunk, and this chunk does not itself name the khanqah's locality. The inscription calls him "شیخ جمال صاحب قادری", while the chain printed on p. 695 runs through "شہاب الدین سہروردی". That is an internal tension in the book, so I did not resolve it. The consolidator should confirm the mapping against chunk 062's heading.
- **A second Shah Jamal:** Shah Gada's khalifa "شاہ جمال حسینی[OCR?]", said to have been in Lahore (p. 699), is placed under Other saints. Whether he is the same as the archive's shah-jamal, or as the subject of pp. 692–696, is not stated. I did not map him.
- **Fateh Shah vs Fata Shah:** the section title spells the name "فتح شاہ مست", but the building heading and the chain spell it "فتا شاہ[OCR?]". It is not certain the same man is meant, though the flow of the text implies it.
- **"لال حسینی" faqirs (p. 700):** this is a faqir group label, not a named person. I did not map it to madho-lal-hussain, because the chunk makes no identification.
- **Toponyms with no bullet:** "شیخوپورہ" (p. 693) is named only as one of Shah Jamal's chilla-places, so I added no place-level bullet for mausoleum-of-waris-shah. "گجرات" (pp. 698–699) is tied to Wajih-ud-Din Gujarati, and the chunk does not say which Gujarat is meant, so I did not map it to shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai. "انارکلی" (p. 701) is the location of Mian Mir's chilla; per the brief it is not a toponym bullet.
