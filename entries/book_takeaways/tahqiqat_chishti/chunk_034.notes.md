# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 034 — pp. 373–384

The chunk opens in the middle of a section whose **heading is in the previous chunk (033)**. That section is about the place called "خیر گدہ" / "شاہ کی گدی", the mazar of "حضرت شاہ ابو الخیر", and how Shaikh Nur Muhammad drove out a man named Shah Hotli[OCR?]. It goes on to describe the enclosure, its mosque, the rooms and residents, the mazar and the graves around it, and ends with the author rejecting a popular link between Abu al-Khair and Abu al-Fazl and Faizi (pp. 373–376). The next section is titled "احوال روضہ حضرت خواجہ خاوند محمود المشہور بحضرت ایشان قدس سرہ" (p. 376). It covers the tomb west of Begumpura, the graves and mosques around it, the Nawab's endowment and fatiha distributions, the line of mutawallis, a failed petition to Sir Henry Lawrence[OCR?] for repairs, and grants and sanads (pp. 376–382). It then begins the saint's life: Bukhara, Samarqand, Herat, Qandahar[OCR?], Kabul and Kashmir (pp. 382–384). The chunk breaks off as the "بادشاہ کشمیر" orders him to leave Kashmir. PDF pp. 380 and 381 are suppressed duplicates and are not noted. **No archive row is mapped in this chunk** (see Doubts).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Hazrat Ishan — Bukhara and Samarqand** (p. 383):
  - He studied "علوم ظاہری" (the outward sciences) in the "مدرسہ [OCR?] سلطانی" and became "یگانہ [OCR?] آفاق" (unique in the world). His fatwa was accepted among the ulama.
  - The king of Bukhara came to serve him.
  - He memorised the Quran by "بارہ برس" (twelve years of age) and was "حافظ کل علوم" by "چودہ برس" (fourteen).
  - In Samarqand he stayed two years. "شاہ زمان [OCR?] مرزا [OCR?]", the ruler there, took bai'at. When a powerful ruler attacked Samarqand, he asked for a prayer. The saint prayed, and by its effect Shah Zaman defeated his enemy.
- **Hazrat Ishan — Herat, Qandahar and Kabul** (pp. 383–384):
  - "ہزاراں ہزار مریدان" (thousands upon thousands of disciples) came to him in Herat, Qandahar[OCR?] and Kabul (p. 383).
  - The ruler of Kabul came out "دو فرسنگ" (two farsangs) to receive him and lodged him in "سنبر [OCR?] باغ" (p. 383).
  - His Friday sermon in the Jami mosque caused uproar. A damaged phrase suggests two men gave up their lives: "دو آدمی [OCR?] مار [OCR?] می [OCR?] زور شور [illegible] جان [OCR?] سبھی [OCR?] تسلیم ہوگی [OCR?]". The king fell into wajd (p. 383).

  - He stayed there two years and sent khalifas toward "روم و شام و عراق و کوہ عور [OCR?]" (p. 384).
- **Hazrat Ishan — Kashmir** (p. 384):
  - His fame spread and people came in crowds to become murids.
  - "شیعہ ہائی [OCR?] کشمیر حو بہت متعصب مشہور ہیں" (the Shias of Kashmir, who are known as very bigoted) grew jealous and plotted to martyr him. They feared that if he stayed a few years no Shia Imamiyya would be left and all would become Sunni.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The author rejects the Abu al-Fazl link.** He rejects the popular claim that Hazrat Abu al-Khair was [connected to] "شیخ ابو الفضل [OCR?] شیخ مبارک [OCR?] [illegible] اکبر شاہ [OCR?]": "یہ بات غلط ہے [OCR?]" (this is wrong) (p. 376).
  - His evidence is a sketch of Abu al-Fazl's and Faizi's careers and deaths (p. 376).
  - He adds that [illegible] "عروج سے پہلے [OCR?] لاہور میں موت ہوکر دفن [OCR?] ہوچکا تھا" (had died and been buried in Lahore before [their] rise) (p. 376).
  - He states that "شہادت [OCR?] اسکی عمارت موجودہ خیر گدہ" (the existing Khair Gadah building is its evidence) (p. 376).
- **The author's sources for Hazrat Ishan**: "کتاب رضوای [OCR?] و عزو [OCR?]" (p. 382).
- **The author's judgement of Hazrat Ishan**: "بڑی [OCR?] بزرگ عابد [OCR?] زاہد متقی ولی اور قطب وقت" (a great elder, worshipful, ascetic, God-fearing, a wali and the qutb of his time), "اپنی وقت میں ثانی نہیں رکھتی [OCR?]" (without equal in his time) (pp. 382–383).
