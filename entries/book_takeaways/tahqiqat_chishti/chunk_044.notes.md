# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 044 — pp. 482–493

The chunk opens on p. 482 partway through a section whose heading is in the previous chunk (043). That section is about a reclusive Qadiri saint connected with "تکیہ ڈنڈی[OCR?]", and the part here gives his chain, his Lahore murids, his descendants and his death date. Seven titled sections follow, in this order:
- "احوال مزار حضرت شاہ فرید" (p. 482), a khanqah three kos from Lahore, with a ground plan on p. 486.
- "حال حضرت نوشہ صاحب رحمتہ اللہ علیہ" (pp. 483–490), which covers his two khalifas Pak Rahman and Pir Muhammad Sachiyar, their urs, and his birth, childhood, retreat and learning of the Quran, taken partly from "مناقب نوشاہیہ".
- "محبت[OCR?] شاہ مداری" (pp. 490–491), a takia at Ichhra.
- "احوال بارہ دری نواب وزیر خان مرحوم شاہجہانی" (pp. 491–493), with the inscriptions of the gun "بھنگیاں والی" set on its roof and the building's later uses.
- "احوال سمادہ جواہر سنگھ" (p. 493), outside Mochi Gate.
- "احوال تکیہ بطقان[OCR?] والہ[OCR?]" (p. 493). The chunk breaks off mid-sentence in this section.

Only one archive row is mapped: **ranmal-sharif**, by the person rule from Hazrat Naushah. There is no Lahore-in-general material. Pp. 483, 485, 487 and 489 are very heavily damaged.

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Pak Rahman — the name.** Crossing a river with Naushah, Abd al-Rahman slipped from the boat and was buried under a fall of earth from the bank. Naushah, "بحالت استغراق" (in absorption), went on. After forty days a khadim told him, and he came back. The water had drawn off and "زمین سفید پڑی ہے" (the ground lay white). He had it dug and Abd al-Rahman came out alive. Naushah said "تو تکالیف زمانہ سے پاک ہو", and from then he was known as "پاک رحمن" (p. 484).
- **The wedding money.** Naushah had no money for the 100 rupees the bride's side demanded, and every purse in the family was empty. A zamindar asked him to come to his wife, who had severe abdominal pain. She was cured at once. The zamindar, "مقدم اور بڑا مالدار", gave a hundred rupees in cash, a horse with saddle and bridle and a khilat (p. 484).
- **Before and after the birth** (pp. 486–487):
  - The father received repeated ilham that the unborn child would be a wali (p. 486).
  - Shah Sulaiman told the mother "تمام جہان اوسکے فیض سے بہرہ مند ہوگا" (the whole world will benefit from his grace) (p. 486).
  - A neighbour woman lifted the infant and saw a fearsome host ("مہیب[OCR?] لشکر"). The mother was told by ilham that the woman's heart could not bear it. The passage is garbled (p. 487).
- **The cattle.** The family's cattle suffered losses. Shah Sulaiman explained that angels always attend a friend of God, and that the cattle in the courtyard troubled them. The cattle were moved and the property was blessed (p. 487).
- **The father's return.** On returning from Mecca the father said he had received glad tidings at "بیت الحرم[OCR?]" that this child would be a wali. When the child was brought, he said there was no need to show him, as he already knew the boy's state (pp. 487–488).
- **The younger brother.** The mother favoured the younger son, Shaikh Ismail, and he was often ill. Shah Sulaiman said the favouritism was the cause. She ignored him and her hand swelled. Naushah repeated the warning, she promised to change, and she was cured at once (p. 488).
- **Retreat in the wilderness.** He refused food brought by a man who found him there, then accepted milk each night. The passage is heavily garbled (p. 489).
- **Learning the Quran.** He memorised the whole Quran in one night, and his teacher boasted of having such a wali as his pupil (p. 490).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is very heavy. Pp. 483, 485, 487 and 489 are largely unreadable word-salad, and the baradari's architecture (p. 491) is flagged nearly word by word. The Persian couplet on p. 489 is garbled. Every quotation above keeps its `[OCR?]`/`[illegible]` flags. Folio markers are printed but not cited, per the brief.
- **Opening section (p. 482)** is headed in chunk 043. The saint is not named in this chunk.
- **The p. 482 death date is ambiguous.** It is unclear whether "سنہ ایک ہزار ایک سو آٹھ[OCR?]" is reported by Sayyid Abd al-Hakim or by Sayyid Mehr Shah, and "آٹھ" is flagged. Do not compute.
- **"سید عبد الرزاق" in the p. 482 chain is NOT mapped to shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh.** It is a bare name in a silsila. The chunk does not say "Shah Chiragh" or give any place, so the held identity question is not engaged.
- **ranmal-sharif is mapped by person rule on the name "نوشہ/نوشاہ" alone.** The chunk never names Ranmal. The burial statement (pp. 483–484) is broken across the page and garbled, and it is unclear whose mazar is meant by "ضلع شاہ پور", "سہن پال[OCR?]", "رسول نگر[OCR?]" or "ضلع گجرات". Weigh accordingly.
- **"حضرت حاجی محمد [illegible] بو شاہ گنج بخش صاحب" in Shah Farid's shajra (p. 483)** may be Naushah, since it follows "پیر محمد" and precedes "حضرت شاہ سلیمان", matching the Naushah section. But the name is broken by "[illegible]", so it is not used for mapping.
- **"حضرت شاہ سلیمان" / "حضرت سلیمان قادری[OCR?]" (pp. 483, 486–488) is NOT mapped.** The name could be **purana-bhalwal** (principal figure "Hazrat Shah Sulaiman Nuri") or **darbar-hazrat-khawaja-shah-muhammad-sulaiman-taunsvi-r-a** (principal figure "Khwaja Muhammad Sulaiman Taunsvi"). The chunk gives no site, no "Nuri" and no date, so neither is picked.
- **Hazrat Shah Farid (pp. 482–483, 486) is NOT mapped to shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar.** The book places his khanqah at "موضع دہولن وال[OCR?]" three kos from Lahore and gives his chain through Pir Muhammad and Shah Sulaiman.
- **"شمس دین پاکپتن[OCR?]" (p. 485)** sits in an unparseable, flagged sentence. It is not mapped to the `pakpattan` row (shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar), because nothing can be read about Pakpattan. Recorded here for the consolidator.
- **Toponyms not mapped.** In each case the place appears only as a person's residence or as the location of a non-archive site, not as material on the place, which follows the chunk 015 practice (the brief says a mohalla or gate named as a location is not a toponym bullet):

  - "قصور" (p. 482). `kasur` → mazar-of-bulleh-shah.
  - "ضلع گجرات" (pp. 484–485). `gujrat` → shrine-of-hazrat-shah-daula-daryai.
  - "ضلع شاہ پور" (p. 484). `shahpur` → shah-yousuf.
  - "ضلع گوجرانوالہ" (p. 493). `gujranwala` → 2 gurdwara rows.
