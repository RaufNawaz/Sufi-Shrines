# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 021 — pp. 236–246

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 236). The opening is a first-person travel account: the narrator is not named on that page. He sets out, receives an instruction "لاہور کی طرف جاؤ" ("go towards Lahore"), and arrives in Lahore. The subject's own section heading is in the previous chunk (chunk 020). On p. 237 this account turns out to be about Syed Qutb Shah, the faqir of a takia in Lahore. Inside it the book gives a sub-section titled "حال حضرت احمد یار صاحب[OCR?]", on Qutb Shah's murshid Ahmad Yar (pp. 236–237). That is followed by Qutb Shah's shajra (p. 237), the author's own portrait of him and of his takia, including a land-settlement dispute (pp. 238–240), and the earlier history of the takia site under a faqir called "سرسنگی[OCR?]" (pp. 240–241). A new section titled "حال جو بارہ[OCR?] چہو بہگت[OCR?]" starts on p. 241. It covers a Bhatia money-changer saint, the book's spelling of whose name changes from p. 243 onward (see Doubts): his karamat (pp. 241–244), the site and its fairs (p. 244), and a detailed description of the site's samadhs, gaddi, Granth platform and Shiva lingam (pp. 244–246). The chunk breaks off mid-sentence at the end of p. 246 ("ایک اور"). Damage is very heavy throughout. Three archive rows are touched, all incidentally.

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Syed Qutb Shah (سیّد قطب شاہ, also "قطب شاہ صاحب", "شہ صاحب")**: the subject of the opening fragment. His heading is in chunk 020.
  - The p. 236 narrator, apparently Qutb Shah, intended to go to "اجمیر شریف[OCR?]" and asked for help from "علی کرم اللہ وجہہ". He was told to go towards Lahore. He stopped at "لو دیا[OCR?]" with a gathering of faqirs. He entered Lahore "[when] مہاراجہ شیر سنگہ تحت نشیں ہوئی تہو" (when Maharaja Sher Singh had taken the throne). He spent a night in "مسجد وزیر خاں", visited "مزار حضرت میر صاحب" in the morning, and met "سائیں طلا[OCR?] شاہ" (p. 236).
  - The book says he became Ahmad Yar's disciple ("بیعت کی") (p. 237). About 200 murids and attendants are now in Punjab (p. 237). His shajra is given as follows: Syed Qutb Shah, khadim of Ahmad Yar; Ahmad Yar of "مولو[OCR?] نور احمد"; then "فیض[OCR?] محمد"; "پیر محمد"; "شہ کریم"; "شاہ رحیم[OCR?]"; "شہ جمال[OCR?] لودی[OCR?]"; "سید سعید خا[OCR?]"; and finally "حضرت جناب جیا[OCR?] میر صاحب" (p. 237).
  - The author's portrait: he talks constantly of "قال اللہ و قال الرسول", of tawhid, and of the "کلام ملا صدرا" (p. 238). Many of Lahore's notables attend him (p. 238). In the Sikh period ("بعہد سکہاں[OCR?]") the people of the darbar were devoted to him (p. 238). He got a mosque "مسجد لاڈو دایہ[OCR?]" freed and lived there (p. 238). He married "ایک مائی پٹہانی ساکن ضلع مقصور[OCR?]" six or seven years ago (p. 239).
  - He left Lahore for Hajj about a year ago (p. 239). The book's assessment is that no one in "صلع[OCR?] لاہو" is now his equal. The author says he cannot vouch for the karamat himself (p. 240).
- **Ahmad Yar (حضرت احمد یار صاحب)**: lived at "کوٹ پہیرو[OCR?]", Gujranwala district ("صلع گوجرانوالہ"), and his caste is printed "سحار[OCR?]" (p. 236). He took bai'at with "حضرت مولوی لعم[OCR?] راہ[OCR?] صاحب" (p. 236). After eighteen years he had a vision of "عوث الاعظم[OCR?]" (p. 236). He stood in the river in winter and recited "قصیدہ بردع[OCR?]" every day and night (pp. 236–237). The book says he ate only once a month and sometimes went four months without food (p. 236).
- **Nawab Imam-ud-Din ("نواب امام الدین خالصہ[OCR?] صاحب بہادر مرحوم")**: built Qutb Shah a takia with a well near the "چہو[OCR?] بہگت[OCR?]" chaubara and the garden of Lala Ratan Chand (p. 237).
- **The faqir "سرسنگی[OCR?]" and the takia's earlier history**: in the Sikh period the takia was his (p. 240). He made it over to "کا می شاہ فقیر امام باڑہ" (Kami Shah, faqir of the imambara). He said money was buried there and became a Muslim near death (p. 241). His grave is beside the samadh, and a tree has grown on it (p. 241). The site was then given to Qutb Shah with the buried money. Qutb Shah's servant faqir "بوٹی شاہ" found the money ("اٹہارہ روپیہ", eighteen rupees, as heard) and used it up. Buti Shah stayed fourteen years, and hundreds came on Fridays (p. 241).
- **The bhagat of the chaubara (printed "چہو بہگت[OCR?]" pp. 241–242, "جیجو بھگت" p. 243 on)**: was of the "کریہ[OCR?] بہاٹیا" caste and a sarraf (money-changer). He kept his faqiri hidden (p. 241). His karamat are "اہل ہنود اور مسلمانوں میں مشہور" (p. 243). Named relatives with samadhs: "پدر جبرام بھگت", "مادر جبرام بھگت", "راکھو کھتری[OCR?]", "زن راکھو" (p. 245).
- **Jawahar Das (جواہر داس رمیس[OCR?])**: has a samadh in the south-east corner, with a marble Devanagari inscription and "संवत[OCR?]" (p. 246).
- **Marhis of "گو سد داس[OCR?]" and "کل داس[OCR?]"** (p. 246).
- **Places named only as locations**: "مسجد وزیر خاں" (p. 236); "دروازہ بہاٹی" (p. 237); "دروازہ شاہ عالمی", "سرائی لالہ رتن چند", "سڑک کے جو انارکلی کو جاتی ہے" (p. 241); "موضع ساندہ" (p. 241). See Doubts.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Qutb Shah's takia economy**: the book says he gets no jagir or stipend from the sarkar ("سرکار کی حاس[OCR?] سی انکو کجہہ جاگیر و منش[OCR?] نہیں") (p. 238). He neither begs nor lets his faqirs beg, and accepts what is given unasked (p. 239). His fixed daily expense is "چار آنہ" (p. 239). People bring charas, tea is made twice a day, a milch goat is kept, and one or two travelling faqirs are always fed (p. 239).
- **Sawan fair**: for some years in the month of Sawan he cooked "کہہیر[OCR?] یعنی شیر برنج" and held a mela that all faqirs, amirs and others attended. The city's Gujars gave him that day's milk. The custom has now been dropped (p. 239). A fatiha in the month "حب[OCR?]" is printed in a damaged phrase (p. 239).
- **Land-settlement dispute**: in the earlier settlement ("بندوبست سابقہ") his name was entered in the ownership column. Now "امام بخش نمبردار" has told the surveyors it was entered by mistake and that "فی الاصل یہ مکان تکیہ شاملات دہ ہی" (the takia is village common land). Qutb Shah intends to petition the settlement officer to have his name entered (p. 239).
- **Succession**: on leaving for Hajj he gave the house to "منشی سردار صا[OCR?] خلف پیر محمد شہ گیلانی" (pp. 239–240). "سائیں محبوب شاہ" is made sajjada nishin in a heavily damaged passage (p. 240).
- **Architecture of the takia**: Nawab Imam-ud-Din's building had one dalan, two rooms and a well (p. 237). A well bears an inscribed stone naming "سرسنگی[OCR?]" with "سمت ۱۸۹۰[OCR?]" (p. 240). There is a samadh with a small dome (p. 240). Qutb Shah's dalan has five windows (p. 241). A dome on the north side is described as "بنا بجہانی[OCR?]" and holds no grave (p. 241). Buti Shah built a deorhi and planted pomegranate, lasura, ber, kikar, date and other trees (p. 241).
- **Bhagat's site, gatherings**: there are great crowds, people stay "شنبہ[OCR?]" from dawn until evening, there are two days of sama', and qawwals receive something (p. 244).
- **Bhagat's site, architecture**: there are nine samadhs east of the gate, some old and some new, and not all names are known (p. 245). There are 3 kanal [OCR?] 7 marla [OCR?] of cultivated land and a large mela ground (p. 245). The gaddi building has a painted west wall and a red stone "پانچ فٹ طول اور تین فٹ عرض" set up "واسطے گرنتھہ رکھنی کو". The Granth is read there from time to time (p. 245). There are two gaddi seats, one used "بروز دوشنبہ", and the gaddi is occupied two days a week (p. 245). A Shiva lingam is fed by water dripping from a pot (p. 246). A second lingam stands in the garden (p. 246).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Qutb Shah's karamat: many believe in them. The author says he cannot report them (p. 240).
- Ahmad Yar, on Qutb Shah's faqiri: the book tells a story in which Ahmad Yar said true faqiri is not getting whatever one wants but emptying the heart of all but God (p. 237).
- The bhagat and the Mughal's ashrafi: the Mughal's wife took one gold ashrafi from the bag, and the Mughal beat the money-changer for it. The wife then suffered an affliction ("[OCR?]" heavy), and the Mughal came back to ask forgiveness (pp. 241–242).
- The bhagat and a woman's bad coin: he gave her full value out of pity for her circumstances (p. 242).
- The bhagat and his sister-in-law: he took her to bathe in the Ganga in a moment by having her shut her eyes, and she bought goods at the fair there. Her mother-in-law later came back from the Ganga and the story became known (p. 243).
- The bhagat and the sugar merchant: a merchant mockingly said his camels carried sand, and the sugar turned to sand. When the merchant asked forgiveness the bhagat prayed and it turned back to sugar (pp. 243–244).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory (ruling b)**: `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags are on nearly every line. Pp. 236, 238, 240 and 242 are the worst. P. 244 has long runs of `[illegible]`. P. 246 has an unread Devanagari inscription ("[illegible: دیوناگری کتبہ]") and unread numerals. Many sentences in the takia history (pp. 238–241) cannot be followed with confidence. No quotation above has been repaired.
- **Opens mid-section**: the subject's (Syed Qutb Shah's) heading is in chunk 020. The p. 236 first-person narrator is not named on that page, so his identification as Qutb Shah comes from context only.
- **data-darbar**: the mention is locational and biographical (Qutb Shah lodging beside the mazar, and a "kotha" of Data Sahib). It is not material about the shrine. "سائیں جلی شاہ" is printed "حبلی[OCR?] شاہ" two lines later.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir**: mapped on "حضرت میاں میر" (p. 244) only, and that sentence is broken by [illegible]. Two other names were **not** mapped: "مزار حضرت میر صاحب" (p. 236) and "حضرت جناب جیا[OCR?] میر صاحب" at the head of the shajra (p. 237). The chunk does not say either is Mian Mir, so a human should check them against the PDF.
- **"شہ جمال[OCR?] لودی[OCR?]" in the shajra (p. 237) is NOT mapped to shah-jamal.** The name is flagged, and the chunk gives no site or location.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**: matched on the principal figure only. The material is about the Sikh customs/revenue in a damaged sentence, not about the samadhi.
- **Toponyms not applied under ruling (a)**: "صلع گوجرانوالہ" (Ahmad Yar's home, p. 236) and "انارکلی" (a road named to locate the chaubara, p. 241) are only locators for a person and a site. They are not material on those places, so no Gujranwala or Anarkali rows were attached.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet**: the chunk names Lahore only as a destination or residence ("شریفان لاہور", "ساکنان لاہور", "صلع[OCR?] لاہو"). That is not material about the city itself, so no Lahore-in-general bullet was written. "دروازہ بہاٹی" and "دروازہ شاہ عالمی" are gates named as locations.
- **"یہاں پنجاب میں ... دو سو آدمی" (p. 237)** is a count of one saint's followers, not a statement about Punjab, so no province bullet was made.
- **The bhagat's name** is printed "چہو بہگت[OCR?]" in the section title and pp. 237, 241–242, and "جیجو بھگت" on pp. 243–244. The two forms are treated as one man only because the section continues.
- **Dates and numerals**: "سمت ۱۸۹۰[OCR?]" (p. 240). Also "اٹہارہ روپیہ", "چودہ برس[OCR?]" and "چار آنہ" (pp. 239, 241), and the land measures "تین کنال[OCR?]", "سات مرلہ[OCR?]" (p. 245). None was converted. The Devanagari samvat on p. 246 was not read.
- **"بنا بجہانی[OCR?]" (p. 241)** is a flagged reading for the dome's builder or period, and is not resolved here.
- **No folio marker on p. 239** (folios are not cited in any case).
- **The chunk ends mid-sentence** at p. 246, in the description of the bhagat's site.
