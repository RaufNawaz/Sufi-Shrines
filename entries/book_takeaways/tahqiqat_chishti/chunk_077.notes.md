# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 077 — pp. 841–851

This chunk is entirely a dynastic history of the later Mughal emperors, with no shrine, tomb or takia described. It opens mid-section (p. 841), continuing the account of Aurangzeb's reign whose heading falls in an earlier chunk: a quarrel with Shah Abbas of Iran over a letter, the rising of a woman "مسمات بسامی [OCR?]", the Deccan campaigns against Bijapur and Golkonda, and a long account of Sivaji and the Marathas (pp. 841–843), then Aurangzeb's death and the succession struggle (pp. 843–845). The titled sections that follow, in order, are "ذکر سلطنت شاہ عالم بادشاہ" (p. 845), which includes a passage on the rise of the Sikhs (p. 846); "ذکر معز الدین جہاندار شاہ" (p. 846); "ذکر بادشاہی فرخ سیر بادشاہ" (p. 847); "ذکر بادشاہی محمد شاہ بادشاہ" (pp. 847–851), which includes a comic anecdote about Nizam al-Mulk and the fool sent to keep him company (pp. 848–849), the breakaway of the Deccan and Oudh, and Nadir Shah's invasion (pp. 850–851); "ذکر سلطنت احمد شاہ بادشاہ" (p. 851); and "ذکر سلطنت عالمگیر ثانی بادشاہ" (p. 851), which breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk. Printed folio markers appear but are not cited (brief rule). Archive mapping is limited to place-level (Lahore, Multan) and person-level (Guru Nanak, Guru Gobind Singh) material.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author blames the Iran quarrel solely on the mir munshi's error (p. 841).
- Shah Alam is called "بڑا رحم دل اور لائق تمام بادشاہان سابق سے" and did not persecute "کسی مذہبی قوم" (pp. 845–846).
- Sikh hostility is attributed to "تکالیف مذہبی" under Aurangzeb (p. 846).
- On Oudh, the author praises "سرکار انگریزی": for misgovernment the Sarkar, wanting the welfare of the subjects, annexed the country and assigned a stipend to Wajid Ali Shah, now "بآرام تمام ... کلکتہ مین مشغول عیش وعشرت" (pp. 849–850).
- Source named: "سیر المتاخرین" (p. 843).

## Doubts

- Lahore bullet is place-level (ruling (a)); the material is dynastic history in which Lahore is a stop or a government seat, not about any site.
- Multan bullet is place-level; "ملتان کا صوبہ دار" refers to the Mughal suba of Multan, carried as printed; weigh accordingly.
- Guru Nanak bullet is person-level (all 18 rows); evidence is a single sentence on his birth in Babur's time. Guru Gobind Singh mapped to the one row whose principal figure he is, person-level; nothing about Sukkur or any site.
- Province bullet: province-level, weigh accordingly. The first Punjab statement names Multan and Lahore (also under those toponym bullets); the book's "پنجاب" here is the 18th-century unit, boundaries not stated.
- Sikhs as a community (p. 846, p. 851) not mapped to **gurdwara-singh-sabha** (principal figure "Sikh community (Singh Sabha)"): not a named person and nothing about Quetta; flagged for the consolidator.
- Name inconsistency: the Oudh nawab is "سعادت علی خان" (pp. 847, 850) and "نواب سعادت خان بہادر" / "سعادت خان" (pp. 849, 850).
- Aurangzeb's death year is "۱۱۱[illegible]" at p. 843 and "۱۱۱۸ [OCR?]" at p. 844; AD equivalent illegible.
- "جہاندار کے ایک بیٹی [OCR?]" (p. 851): Alamgir II described as a "بیٹی" (daughter?) of Jahandar, flagged; carried as printed.
- Damage inventory: heavy `[OCR?]` on proper names (سیوا جی, جنجی, ویلور, مدراس, میسور, بمبئی, بسامی, بندا) and on every AD date; `[illegible]` gaps in Aurangzeb's death date and age, Abdali's crossing year, Ahmad Shah's reign years; a marginal note at p. 843 is wholly illegible. Folio "[folio 846] [OCR?]" at p. 848 flagged (not cited).
