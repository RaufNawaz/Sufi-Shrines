# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 015 — pp. 176–183

The chunk opens mid-section with a badly damaged fragment (p. 176) about wells ("چاہ") near a mazar and something obtained from "سرکار سکھان". **The heading of that section is in the previous chunk (014)**, and the fragment is too damaged to name its subject. Two titled sections follow. The first is "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت شاہ محمد غوث قادری قدس سرہ" (p. 176), which covers the tomb, its mosque inscription, the saint's Qadiri lineage, his karamat, his death chronogram, the urs, the jagir and the Sikh-period attempt to demolish the tomb (pp. 176–179). The second is "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت شاہ بلاول قدس سرہ" (p. 179), which covers his birthplace, his chain of discipleship, the langar, chronograms by Mufti Ghulam Sarwar, his childhood and karamat, his daily routine and Shah Jahan's visits. It runs to the end of the chunk (pp. 179–183), and the chunk breaks off mid-sentence on Dara Shikoh. Neither saint with a section is an archive row. Three archive rows are touched in passing: Mian Mir, Data Ganj Bakhsh and Daud Bandagi's mazar at Sher Garh.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (حضرت میاں میر; "مقبرہ عالیہ حضرت میاں میر"):
  - Shah Muhammad Ghaus, in his own "رسالہ غوثیہ", is quoted as writing that when he came to Lahore he spent his first night at the tomb of Mian Mir: "جب میں لاہور میں آیا تو اول مقام مقبرہ عالیہ حضرت میاں میر شب باش ہوا" (p. 177).
  - That night Mian Mir appeared to him "یعنی خواب میں آئے" (that is, came in a dream), gave him "ایک شغل یعنی وظیفہ" and told him to practise it (p. 177).
  - The next morning Shaikh Hamid Lahori said "براہ کشف" (by unveiling) that the shughl Mian Mir had given "آج رات کو" (tonight) was enough and that nothing more from him was needed (p. 177).
- **data-darbar** (خانقاہ حضرت داتا گنج بخش):
  - Of Shaikh Hamid Lahori: "ہمیشہ مقفل[OCR?] خانقاہ حضرت داتا گنج بخش [illegible]". The sentence is cut by the illegible gap, so his exact relation to the khanqah is not certain (p. 177).
- **shergarh** (حضرت داود[OCR?] بندگی[OCR?]; "جنکا مزار شیر گڑھ میں ہے"):
  - Named in Shah Bilawal's chain: Shah Bilawal was a murid of Shaikh Shams-ud-Din, who was a murid of Shah Abu Ishaq, "وہ مریدِ حضرت داود[OCR?] بندگی[OCR?] کے جنکا مزار شیر گڑھ میں ہے", who was a murid of Sayyid Hamid. The chain then runs back through Sayyids and ends at Ghaus al-Azam Abd al-Qadir Jilani (p. 179).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Shah Muhammad Ghaus**:
  - A blind man and a mute ("گونگا[OCR?]") came to him. He passed his hand over the blind man's eyes and the man saw, and he told the mute to recite the kalima, which the mute did at once (p. 177).
  - The demolition story: Kanwar Nau Nihal Singh, coming back from the cremation of Maharaja Kharak Singh, was entering the fort by "دروازہ روشنائی" when a great stone fell from the gate onto his head. "دونوں صاحب" (both men) died that night. It became known that this was the saint's doing ("حضرت شاہ محمد غوث کی کنور نو نہال سنگھ پر لگی[OCR?]"), because he had ordered the demolition despite pleas that "ایک سید پیر کی مزار ہے" (pp. 178–179).
  - After that, "زیادہ تر لوگ انکا ادب کرتے ہیں" (most people pay him respect) (p. 179).
  - A garbled line mentions an eye-ache ("چشم درد[OCR?]") cure linked to the saint (p. 178).
- **Shah Bilawal**:
  - He read the "سپارۂ عمّ" without a teacher and finished half the Quran in one day (p. 181). His father said "یہ بہت[OCR?] کامل ولی ہوینگے" (he will be a very[OCR?] perfect wali) (p. 181).
  - He memorised the Quran in "چھہ[OCR?] مہینہ" (six[OCR?] months) (p. 181).
  - When he was ill, his father took a fal from the Gulistan and understood from it that his son would recover and he himself would die, which came true (p. 181).
  - On his way to buy ink, Shams-ud-Din stepped off a boat, put his hand on his head and called him into his company (p. 181).
  - A man climbed the tree under which the pir slept, broke branches and would not stop. At a glance from Shah Bilawal he fell dead. The pir woke and ordered him to rise, and he rose (p. 181).
  - A thief went blind in the khanqah and was found in a cell. He was fed and sent off with a gift, and years later he came back "دین[OCR?] محرم[OCR?]". The passage is heavily damaged (p. 182).
  - A garbled story about a birth in his mohalla and a building, or makan, raised near the mazar site (p. 182).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Damage is heavy throughout: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags, and the p. 176 fragment and the mosque couplets are almost unreadable. Several marginal `[margin]` notes are illegible. Every quotation above keeps its flags. Folio markers (170–177) are printed but are not cited, per the brief.
- **Opening fragment (p. 176)** belongs to a section headed in chunk 014. Its subject is unplaceable from this chunk. "الا رکلی[OCR?]" is not mapped to any Anarkali row.
- **No Lahore-in-general bullet.** Lahore appears only as the location of named saints, mosques and bazaars. The one city-level phrase, Kharak Singh's order about "رواجی[OCR?] شہر لاہور", is too damaged to say what it concerned. So the 35 `lahore` ids are not attached.
- **Peshawar is not mapped.** "قدیمی ساکن پشاور" (p. 177) is a non-archive saint's family residence, not material on Peshawar's tradition, so the 10 `peshawar` ids are not attached. This is recorded for the consolidator to weigh against ruling (a).
- **The "قلعہ" by "دروازہ روشنائی" (p. 179)** is not mapped to the `lahore fort` rows (loh-temple-lava-temple, samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh). The chunk says only "قلعہ", and the event concerns a gate.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh is not mapped.** In "ولی عہد مہاراجہ [illegible]" (p. 178) the maharaja's name is illegible, so Ranjit Singh is not named in the chunk.
