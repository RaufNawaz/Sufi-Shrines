# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 071 — pp. 772–782

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 772) with the tail of an account of a mosque in Lahore built by Dara Shikoh's wife; its heading is in the previous chunk. The fragment covers the mosque's later history under British rule: it was bought and sold, a printing-press man and the railway each held it, and a contractor, Muhammad Sultan, finally owned it. Then come four titled sections in order: "خانقاہ ابدی [OCR?] شاہ" (p. 773); "تکیہ مہتمان [OCR?] سرکی بند" (pp. 773–774); "جہگی بدہو شاہ" (pp. 774–775), with a long shajra and a tree table; and "ذکر مکان حضرت دوری شاہ رحمۃ اللہ علیہ" (pp. 775–782). The last section is the longest. It covers the saint's site, its loss under Diwan Kirpa Ram and its revival by Jhuggi Shah, a former khadim of the sajjada nashin of Madho Lal Husain. Jhuggi Shah then gave the site to the author, Nur Ahmad Chishti. The section also covers the reburial of the author's wife, mother and sister there in 1312 [OCR?] AH, with their chronograms, under the sub-heading "حال عمارت موجودہ" (p. 779). It ends with the Persian gift deed, "نقل قبالہ اقراری جہگی شاہ" (pp. 780–782), and its witnesses. The chunk closes with the opening of "احوال باغ شالا مار واقع لاہور" (p. 782), which gives etymologies of the name and breaks off mid-sentence. The text spells the saint's name in several ways ("دوری شاہ", "ڈوری شاہ", "دوڑی شاہ", "دوڑہی شاہ").

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **madho-lal-hussain** (the book's "حضرت مادہو لعل حسین"; in the deed "جناب شاہ لعل حسین صاحب"):
  - Sain Sharf Hasan, "المشہور جہگی شاہ", is called "خادم صولی [OCR?] شاہ سجادہ نشین حضرت مادہو لعل حسین" (p. 776). The deed calls him "خادم قدوۃ الفقرا سائین صولی [OCR?] شاہ سجادہ نشین مزار مبارک ... جناب شاہ لعل حسین صاحب" (p. 780).
  - When Diwan Kirpa Ram asked Jhuggi Shah to settle at Dori Shah's site, he first declined. He said he served the mazar of Madho Lal Husain ("ہم مزار حضرت مادہو لعل حسین کو کر [OCR?]"), where he had built at his own cost a house, "مکان ثبوت [OCR?] شاہ والہ", to live in (p. 777).
  - After Sikh rule began to falter, Jhuggi Shah sometimes went to the mazar of Madho Lal Husain to serve "مسمات مای صاحب جان [OCR?] محبوبہ ومنکوحہ صوفی شاہ مرشد خود" (the beloved and wife of his murshid, Sufi Shah). After she died and British rule was set up in Punjab, he returned to Dori Shah's site (p. 777).
  - The deed bars any claim on the property by "خادمان و یلار [OCR?] سجادہ نشینان شاہ علی حسین صاحب و یا کسی از سلسلہ فقیران حسین شاہی" (p. 781). See Doubts on "شاہ علی حسین".

- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** (the book's "حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی صاحب"; "روضہ حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی"):
  - Khanqah Abdi Shah stands on a small mound to the west of the "قبرستان حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی صاحب" (p. 773).
  - New burials in "گورستان متعلقہ روضہ حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی واقع ٹیلہ شہید گنج" have been banned and closed "بحکم سرکار". The author's mother, sister, wife and other relatives were buried there, and he had built pucca graves for them (p. 778).
  - Because of the ban, the author exhumed the bodies from "گورستان شاہ ابو المعالی صاحب" and reburied them at Dori Shah's site. The date as printed is "ششم ماہ شوال المکرم سنہ ۱۳۱۲ [OCR?] مطابق پنجم مارچ سنہ ۱۸۹۵ [OCR?] بروز یکشنبہ" (p. 779). The deed repeats the exhumation "از گورستان حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی مرحوم" (p. 781).

- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** (the book's "مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ صاحب"):
  - Named only as a period: "بعہد مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگہ صاحب", Diwan Kirpa Ram made his garden at Dori Shah's site and took over the khanqah's land (p. 776). The courtesan Naji is called "بعہد مہاراجہ بڑی نامور عورت" (p. 777). Nothing about the samadhi.

- **shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal** (the book's "حضرت شاہ کمال"):
  - A bare name in Badhu Shah's shajra: "...شیخ احمد مجدد الف ثانی [OCR?] خادم حضرت شاہ سکندر شاہ سکندر خادم حضرت شاہ کمال شاہ کمال خادم حضرت فضیل شاہ..." (p. 775). The book gives no place, tomb or date for him. See Doubts.

## Province-level material

- PROVINCE punjab (book's word: "پنجاب"; refers to: the setting-up of British rule, "عملداری سرکار گردون وقار انگریزی", as the book states):
  - British rule and a legal settlement ("بندوبست قانونی") were established "پنجاب مین". After this, Jhuggi Shah returned to Dori Shah's site and set about restoring it (p. 777).

  - Dara Shikoh married "ستارہ بیگم المعروف قدسیہ بیگم" in "سنہ [illegible]". She built the mosque with Dara's permission as a memorial, after a son was born to her by "سن پچاس [OCR?]". The bazaar from this mosque to "مسجد دای انگا" was endowed to it (p. 772).
  - Under early British rule it was bought by "مسٹر اوسولڈ [OCR?] ڈپٹی صاحب مہتمم مطبع لاہور کرانیکل" and made a kothi. When he left for Vilayat, he sold it to the railway for six thousand rupees. "صاحب ولیم لیوک [OCR?]" used to read there. Muhammad Sultan, a contractor, then bought it from the railway. People hoped he would return it to Muslims, but he did not, and it is let to tenants (p. 772).
  - Between this mosque and the Dai Anga mosque stood a mosque of "خانہ نور اللہ صاحب [OCR?]". "پادری مسٹر سی ڈبلیو فورمن صاحب متعینہ مشن امریکہ" bought it and built a kothi there. The railway later bought it as an office, and it is now being pulled down to clear the railway compound (p. 772).

- Dori Shah ("حضرت دوری شاہ") (pp. 775–782):
  - Location: "بطرف گوشہ ایشان مقام فرودگاہ ریلوی", near "باغ بہگت رام", on the old royal road "سی شالامار کو جاتا ہے" (p. 775).
  - Arrival and devotees: he came to Lahore in Shah Jahan's reign. Dara Shikoh was his devotee, and people then called him "مادہو والی [OCR?]" (pp. 775–776).
  - Death and succession: he died in "سن ایکہزار سجاس [OCR?]" (day and month [illegible]). "ہمراہ [OCR?] شاہ" became sajjada nashin. "صاحب خان جانان [OCR?] ناظم لاہور" released one hundred bighas of land to the khanqah (p. 776).
  - Next came Roshan Ali Shah, a majzub. He planted flowers and fruit trees, and four old "دل [OCR?]" trees of his remain (p. 776).
  - The place flourished until "عہد خان بہادر [OCR?]" and was then deserted "بحالت طوائف الملوکی" (p. 776).
  - Under Ranjit Singh, Diwan Kirpa Ram made his garden there and took the land. His workmen levelled the mazar. A fearsome figure in a dream told the Diwan that it was a saint's grave, so he came to the grave and appointed a faqir, Gulab Shah (pp. 776–777).

  - After the British settlement, the courtesan "مسمات ناجی [OCR?] طوایف", who had gone blind, came to live there. She spent her money on the urs and on rebuilding the khundi of the mazar, with a dalan, two kothis, an enclosure wall and two domed gates (p. 777).
  - Jhuggi Shah is "قدیم ساکن قصور قوم ککلو [OCR?] مادر زاد تارک الدنیا". The author says he is not a "ٹکر گدا" beggar but asks for a rupee or two when needed (pp. 777–778).
  - Dori Shah was in the service of "حضرت حاجی جمعیت [OCR?] صاحب مرحوم". The book cross-refers to its sections on Hajji Jamiat and "مکان قدم رسول" (p. 778).
  - Jhuggi Shah gave the place to the author by a registered hiba. The author promised him a place there for life, burial rites and fatiha (p. 778).
  - The building now: a three-arched mosque built by the author's wife; a bath-house; a well with a wheel; a deorhi; hujras; and an upper room with seven windows built by the author. An open court to the north holds Dori Shah's khundi, with Hamrah[OCR?] Shah's grave and the graves of the author's wife, mother and sister (pp. 779–780).
  - The adjoining land with some common graves is called by the zamindars "باب جہگی [OCR?]". The author holds that it is in fact khanqah land. He plans to build an enclosure and to fill in an old well that gives little water (p. 780).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Land grant: "سو بگیہہ زمین مزروعہ" was released to Dori Shah's khanqah by the nazim of Lahore "تا قیام خانقاہ" (p. 776). Under Sikh rule ("حسب رواج عملداری سکہان") the khanqah's land was taken over (p. 776).
- Patronage: under Diwan Kirpa Ram the urs and Thursday "مجری ناچ راگ" were paid for by the Diwan for his lifetime (p. 777). Later the urs was paid for by the courtesan Naji and by Jhuggi Shah's begging (p. 777).
- Custodians at Abdi Shah: there is no urs or mela, and the mason's sons keep the lamps (p. 773).
- State: the government offered Rs 300 for the sirki-bands' takia to be vacated (p. 774). Government order closing new burials at the Abul Muali graveyard (p. 778).
- Waqf and sale of the Dara-wife mosque: a bazaar endowed to it; sold for "چہہ ہزار روپیہ" to the railway (p. 772).
- Property transfer: a registered hiba of a khanqah site to the author (pp. 778, 780–782). Reburial was "بباعث تکمیل قبضہ خود" (p. 781), and the author notes "بہت سا روپیہ خرچ ہوگیا" (p. 779).
- Building dates: the mosque of the author's wife, "تعمیر یہہ سن بارہ سو چوہتر ہجری سی" (p. 778). Its two chronograms are carved in marble on the mosque front (p. 779).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Diwan Kirpa Ram had Dori Shah's mazar levelled. In a dream a fearsome figure told him it was a saint's grave, and in the morning he came to the grave and appointed a faqir (pp. 776–777). Attributed: "مشہورہ ہی".
- Karam Bakhsh sirki-band says the moon appears to him in dreams and visits on the first of each month (p. 773). Reported by the author from his own questioning; the author is sceptical.

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author regrets that the contractor Muhammad Sultan did not restore the Dara-wife mosque to Muslims ("ممکن تہا انکا نام نیک رہتا") (p. 772).
- The author calls Diwan Kirpa Ram's ruined garden "مقام عبرت" (p. 776).
- The author argues that the land the zamindars call "باب جہگی [OCR?]" is really khanqah land, because graves lie near the mazar (p. 780).
- The author is a party here: he is the recipient of the hiba and buried his own family at the site (pp. 778–782).

## Doubts

- Damage: there are heavy `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags throughout. The worst-hit passages are the deed (pp. 780–782), the witness list, which is mostly [illegible] (p. 782), and the chronogram verses (pp. 778–780). Several of Badhu Shah's dates are wholly [illegible] ("سن بارہ سو [illegible]", p. 774).
- Deed date versus contents: the deed is dated "۱۵ مارچ سنہ ۱۸۶۵ [OCR?] ء" (p. 782). Yet it records the reburial, which the book dates "سنہ ۱۳۱۲ [OCR?] مطابق پنجم مارچ سنہ ۱۸۹۵ [OCR?]" (p. 779), and the text calls the gift recent ("اب چند عرصہ سے", p. 778). One of these numerals is likely misread or misprinted. Not resolved.
- Wife Qudsiya Begum's death date: "بتاریخ سوم ذی الحجہ سنہ [illegible]" (p. 778) versus "۳ ذیقعد [OCR?] سنہ ۱۲۹۸ [OCR?] ہجری" (p. 780). The months differ.
- Mother's death date: "سنہ ۱۲۹۵ [OCR?] بارہ سو پچاسی [OCR?]" (p. 780). The digits (1295) and the words (1285) disagree.
- Chronogram for the reburial: Mufti Ghulam Sarwar's chronogram "مدفن مستورات" is said to give "ایکہزار دو سو اکاسی [OCR?]" (p. 780). This does not match the reburial date of 1312 [OCR?] given on pp. 779 and 780. Reported as printed.
- Dori Shah's death year: "سن ایکہزار سجاس [OCR?]" (p. 776) is garbled. Its reading is not attempted.
- The section on the Dara-wife mosque gives Dara's wife as "ستارہ بیگم المعروف قدسیہ بیگم" (p. 772). The author's own wife is also "قدسیہ بیگم" (pp. 778–780). These are two different women in the text; do not conflate them.
- Madho Lal Hussain: the deed's "شاہ علی حسین صاحب" (p. 781) may be a scribal or OCR variant of "شاہ لعل حسین" (p. 780). It is not mapped as a separate figure. Separately, Dori Shah himself is said to have been called "مادہو والی [OCR?]" (p. 776); this is about Dori Shah, and the word is flagged, so it is not attached to madho-lal-hussain.
- darbar-abul-muali-qadri: the book here says "شاہ ابو المعالی" without "Qadri", and places the graveyard at "ٹیلہ شہید گنج" (p. 778). The index row says the site is near Mochi Darwaza. The identification rests on the name; the consolidator should confirm.
- shrine-of-baba-shah-kamal: this is a bare name in a silsila chain (p. 775). Nothing ties this "حضرت شاہ کمال" to the Lahore (Ichhra/Samanabad) row; mapped under the person rule for weighing only.
- Not mapped: "سید عبد الرزاق پاک" in the same shajra (p. 775) shares the name of shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh's principal figure (Syed Abdul Razzaq). The book does not call him Shah Chiragh, and it makes him a khadim of Ghaus Azam. That row's identity question is held. It is left unmapped here; the consolidator should weigh it.
