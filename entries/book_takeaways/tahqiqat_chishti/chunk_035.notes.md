# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 035 — pp. 385–393

The chunk opens mid-section (p. 385), continuing the life of a Naqshbandi hazrat whose section is **headed in the previous chunk (034)**. Inside this chunk he is named only as "حضرت", once as "حضرت خواجہ" (p. 386), and in the death chronograms as "شہ محمود[OCR?]" / "شاہ محمود[OCR?]" (p. 389). The section covers Shia attempts on his life in Kashmir, his summons by Jahangir, a rain miracle, Shah Jahan's gift, a debate on sama' and the death of an objector, his removal to Lahore, his death in "۱۰۵۲" and chronograms (pp. 385–389). Three titled sections follow. "حال گلابی باغ" (p. 390) describes the gate and remaining buildings of the Gulabi Bagh at Begumpura, with its inscriptions and the story of its builder (pp. 390–392). "حال خانقاہ حضرت معصوم شاہ بیرون لاہوری دروازہ" (p. 392) gives the khanqah's location and two fire karamat (pp. 392–393). "احوال حضرت شاہ شہاب الدین جفرہ[OCR?] بن حضرت موج دریا بخاری[OCR?]" (p. 393) begins and breaks off mid-sentence at the end of the chunk. Two archive rows are mapped: Mian Mir (named in passing) and Mauj Darya Bukhari (as the father of the last section's saint).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **The opening section's hazrat**:
  - State support: Jahangir's "روزینہ معقول" (p. 386); Shah Jahan's "ایک لاکہ[OCR?] ٹکہ[OCR?]", divided between the Kashmiri khanqah building, his Lahore khanqah and takia, and the needy (p. 387).
  - His Lahore garden and khanqah were built before he arrived (p. 389).
  - Preaching: on "آٹہویں[OCR?]" days he preached from the pulpit in his khanqah mosque, "حو تا حال موحود ہی[OCR?]" (which still exists); "ہزارہا[OCR?]" unbelievers became Muslim and murids (p. 389).
- **Gulabi Bagh — gate** (p. 390):
  - A tall arched gate ("محرابی در بلند کلان") in two storeys, with a small arched window above "بطور دریچہ سیر گاہ" and an arched passage below.
  - Tile/inlay work given as "کا لسنی[OCR?]" throughout; floral painting ("منقش گلکار[OCR?]").
  - Two arched seats ("نشست گاہیں[OCR?]") east and west, "عرض ... سوا دو گز اور طول پونے تین گز".
  - Inscriptions in "خط حلی نستعلیق" and "بقلم حلی", including the kalima over the great arch, and a figure "۶۶ [OCR?]" (p. 390). Couplets under Citable passages.
- **Gulabi Bagh — deorhi, bara dari, hauz** (pp. 391–392): two arched dalans east and west, domed centre with brick floor, rooms ("کو ٹھہریاں[OCR?]", "حجرہ"), a bara dari towards the hauz with "چوسرگچ[OCR?]" plaster and painted flowers, rooms on east and west with a hauz on each side. Much is damaged; the passage is a building survey.
- **Gulabi Bagh — well and trees** (p. 392): "بفاصلہ[OCR?] بیس[OCR?] قدم" from the deorhi's north-east corner, a large well ("جاہ کلاں"); trees named "برنا[OCR?]", "گوندنی[OCR?]", "کابلی کیکر", "بڑ[OCR?]", "بیر[OCR?]".

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Opening section's hazrat**:
  - Changed into the form of a zamindar to disarm Nimat Ali (p. 385).
  - Kotwal Sikandar's sword hand dried up (pp. 385–386).
  - Rain: near "رحوڑ[OCR?]" the royal army was short of water and Jahangir suffered from heat; on the king's plea he knelt and prayed, a white cloud rose from the mountains before he finished, and it rained until all the "حمیر[OCR?] بالاب[OCR?]" filled; at the king's second plea he prayed again and it stopped (pp. 386–387).
  - The emperor's recovery from "ضیق النفس[OCR?]" after his prayer; the hazrat said something like "ایک شخص تم دونو سے ضرور ... ہو حا میگا[OCR?]" (garbled) (p. 387).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The hazrat's ruling on sama'** (p. 388), as the book reports it: "حضرات نقشبندیہ سماع ... نہیں ستہیں[OCR?]" but do not deny it either; "السماع لا املا[OCR?] مباح" — sama' is permitted for one who is a true lover of God and in whom sama' raises the thought of the divine essence, and "حرام" for a "فاجر" whose love is for a woman or anything other than God. Asked why Naqshbandis then avoid a permitted thing, he answered that "اولیاء اللہ در حالت سماع میں حان[OCR?] دیدہ[OCR?]" (saints have given up their lives in sama'), so the Naqshbandi elders neither listened nor denied (p. 388).
- **His stance** (p. 389): inclined to "اتباع ست[OCR?]" and averse to bid'a; he felt "لعرت[OCR?]" (aversion) towards anyone holding "وحدت وحودی[OCR?] و کلمہ ہمہ اوست" (p. 389).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Very heavy throughout: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags, especially pp. 385–389 (much of the Kashmir narrative), the chronograms on p. 389, the building survey on p. 391 and the karamat on p. 393. Many words are visibly misread dotting ("حب" for "جب", "حضرت" vs "حجرت", "عرب" for "غرب", "معتی/معنی/مقتی" for "مفتی"); every quotation keeps its flags and is given as printed. Folio markers (376, 378, 380, ?82, 38?) are printed but not cited, per the brief.
- **Opening section's subject is unnamed in this chunk.** Its heading is in chunk 034. He is called "حضرت خواجہ" (p. 386) and the chronograms read "شہ محمود[OCR?]" / "شاہ محمود[OCR?]" (p. 389); no fuller name and no identification is made here. He is not an archive row as far as this chunk shows.
- **shrine-of-mian-mir**: attached by the person rule on "میاں میر[OCR?] لاہوری" (p. 389), a flagged name in a passing mention. The chunk gives no other identifier. Note the book in the same paragraph says the hazrat disliked holders of "وحدت وحودی[OCR?]" while corresponding with Mian Mir on that question (p. 389); carried as printed, not reconciled.
- **shrine-of-mauj-darya-bukhari**: attached on "حضرت موج دریا بخاری[OCR?]" (p. 393), a flagged name, as the father of Shah Shihab-ud-Din; the book's cross-reference to its own Mauj Darya section supports the identification, but the name carries the flag.
