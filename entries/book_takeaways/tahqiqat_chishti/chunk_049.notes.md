# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 049 — pp. 540–549

The chunk opens mid-section (pp. 540–541) on a jogi/mahant establishment whose heading is in the previous chunk. It describes a mandir with an octagonal "سمادہ", a white dome over a mahant's samadh, the present gaddi-nashin "رام ناتھ[OCR?]" and his line of gurus, followed by an almost wholly illegible passage mentioning Sikhs, a tank and "امرتسر". Three titled sections follow. "احوال مفصل پتہ جوگیوں[OCR?] کا جہاں سے یہ جوگ نکلا" (p. 541) gives the jogis' origin legend: Gorakh Nath born from cow dung by Shiv ji's gift, Machhindar Nath from a fish, and the ear-splitting (pp. 541–543). The twelve chelas and twelve "پنتہہ" are listed (p. 544), and "رسم بنانی چیلہ کی جو جوگیوں میں رائج ہی" (p. 544) gives the vows and rules of the order (pp. 544–545). "احوال گورو گورکھہ ناتہہ صاحب" (p. 545) covers Gorakh Nath, the jogis' greetings and cries, and the story of Raja Bhartrihari and Raja Bikramajit, ending at "مقام تلہ[OCR?] گورکہہ ناتہہ" (pp. 545–549). The chunk breaks off mid-sentence on p. 549. Damage is extreme from p. 545 onward.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Opening establishment**: a building north of the samadh where "خرچ روز مرہ" (daily expenses) are handled (garbled). Many houses and dalans are around it (p. 540).
- **Becoming a chela** (p. 544):
  - The candidate asks a mahant. The mahant warns him of the hardships of this faqr and tells him to go and do worldly work.
  - If he persists, he is given two or three days' fast and then vows:
    - no trade ("بیوپار");
    - no "نوکری";
    - "تمہارہ نہ باندہی";
    - patience if abused;
    - no marriage;
    - guarding the ears.
  - "جسکا کان پھٹا ہو لڑ[OCR?] یا دہہ[OCR?] یا ٹوٹ جاوی تو اوسکو زندہ در گور کردیتے ہیں" (one whose split ear tears or breaks is buried alive).
- **Inheritance**: the chela becomes owner of the guru's property, and "کسی رشتہ دار کو دعوی و دخل نہیں" (no relative has a claim). Even a "لکھہ پتی" is called a faqir (p. 545).
- **Ear-splitting**: done by the guru or by another jogi "واقف علم کاں[OCR?] پہاڑی[OCR?]". A named chela is said to be appointed for this work at present, but the name is garbled (p. 545).
- **Greetings and cries**:
  - The jogis' salam and its reply "آدہ[OCR?] پرش[OCR?]" (pp. 545–546).
  - When begging in the lanes, a jogi calls "الکہہ الکہ", explained as "خدا الکہا[OCR?] یعنی بھیجو[OCR?]" (garbled) (p. 546).
- **The "روٹ"**: "ایک بڑی روٹی ہوتی ہے حو حوکی[OCR?] لوگ" distribute among people (p. 549).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Gorakh Nath born from cow dung by Shiv ji's gift (p. 542). Attributed as jogi tradition ("لوگ … روایت کرتی ہیں", p. 542).
- Machhindar Nath produced from a fish's mouth after twelve years. The book records that some kathas reject this (pp. 542–543).
- Parbati splits the ear at Shiv ji's order (p. 543).
- Bhartrihari and the sati test (pp. 546–547):
  - Bhartrihari sees a widow burn herself on her husband's pyre and tests his queen with false news of his death.
  - She dies.
  - He mourns at the cremation ground and wants to burn with her.
- Gorakh Nath breaks a pot, revives the queen together with many queens like her, then makes her vanish. The raja becomes his chela (pp. 547–548).
- Bhartrihari's people are refused a share of the "روٹ" and told to cut the mountain for it. Struck with a "پہوڑ[OCR?]", the mountain ("ہاڑ") is cut "قدرت آلہی سو". The book says it remains a place of visiting to this day and links a custom to "شیو راہ ہی" (Shivratri[?]). Heavily garbled (p. 549).
