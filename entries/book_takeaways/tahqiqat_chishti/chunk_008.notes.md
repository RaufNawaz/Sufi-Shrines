# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 008 — pp. 97–106

This chunk opens in the middle of the book's account of the khanqah of "حضرت حسین" and "حضرت مادہو" (the section heading falls before p. 97 and is not in this chunk), and describes in turn the buildings by the gaddi, a mosque built by a courtesan, the walled enclosure of graves of the sajjada nashins, the raised platform called "مقام تخت" with the two mazars, and the chiragdan (pp. 97–99). P. 100 is very heavily damaged: verses, then a section headed "حال بہشتی [OCR?] و دروازہ [OCR?]" with a story about a faqirni and Hazrat Hamid Shah [OCR?], and a story current in Baghbanpura. P. 101 adds a "فائدہ [OCR?]" note on the place outside Taksali [OCR?] gate where Hazrat Husain's mother's house stood and on the Madari [OCR?] faqir Sher Ali Shah under Maharaja Ranjit Singh. The chunk then opens a new section, "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت میران سید محمد شاہ موج دریا بخاری قدس سرہ" (p. 101), which runs to the end of the chunk: lineage, the Chittorgarh [OCR?] legend, Akbar's jagir and langars, the tomb and its graves, the fire-ordeal karamat, the two wives and Danala [OCR?], the death date and a chronogram, the urs, the Sikh-period stipend, and the descendants (pp. 101–106). Damage is heavy throughout and p. 100 is close to unreadable.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Gaddi and sajjada nashin seat at the Hussain khanqah (p. 97); a series of sajjada nashins buried in the enclosure (p. 98).
- Hussain khanqah urs: on the urs day people offer nazr at the takht (p. 99); ghilaf put on Madho's grave "بروز عرس و سیر" (p. 99); a lamp lit every day in the chiragdan (p. 99).

- Mauj Darya: Akbar's jagir of "[illegible] لاکھ [OCR?] روپیہ" (p. 102); income spent on faqirs and the poor (p. 102); three langars with hereditary servants (pp. 102–103).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Hussain khanqah door story: a faqirni "گلزار [OCR?] شاہ [OCR?]" living by the door is mocked in a village; she complains to Hazrat Hamid [OCR?]; the tale ends "وہ عورت مر [OCR?] بھی [OCR?] گئی واللہ اعلم"; most of it is illegible (p. 100).
- "تمام باغباں پورہ [OCR?] میں مشہور ہے" that at the sajjada nashini of Sain [illegible] Shah, two persons "حمید اللہ [OCR?] شاہ اور حیدر [OCR?] شاہ" were involved in an episode with rain "بہ امداد [OCR?] الٰہی"; a "شیخ [OCR?] بدردین [OCR?]" says "وہ روپیہ میں نے بھی دیکھا ہو" (pp. 100–101). Too damaged to reconstruct.
- Sher Ali Shah and "[illegible] شاہ", who "ہمیشہ ... شراب پیا کرتا تھا": "بفاصلہ اٹھ [OCR?] پہر" first Sher Ali Shah and then the other died; "یہ تذکرہ عجیبہ اب تک لاہور میں اکثر اشخاص بیان کرتے ہیں" (p. 101).
- Chittorgarh [OCR?]: Akbar could not take the fort; told that its conquest was "in the name" of Mauj Darya in Uch, he sent a sandni for him. The saint said that on his arrival all tents would fall and every lamp of the camp go out except the one at his own tent; a storm did this; Akbar went to him on foot; next morning the saint said the name of Allah three [OCR?] times by the fort "اوسی وقت قلعہ مفتوح ہو گیا" (pp. 101–102).
- Fire ordeal: Akbar's amirs objected to so large a jagir and asked for proof, saying a true sayyid does not burn in fire; a tandoor was heated in "قلعہ شاہی [OCR?]". The son Sayyid Shihab-ud-Din, refused entry by the guards, "بصورت شیر متمثل [OCR?] ہوکر" entered and frightened Akbar. The saint said a lowly servant could do this; Mian Murid leapt into the tandoor saying "اللہ اکبر", stayed there in zikr, and came out only when the saint called him, falling at his feet. From that day Shihab-ud-Din was called "سلطان شہاب الدین شہرہ [OCR?]" (pp. 103–104).
- Sweet water: the water was brackish; on Sayyid Abdur Rahim's word a well was dug and "پانی میٹھا نکلا اور پھر رفتہ رفتہ تمام موضع کا پانی میٹھا ہوگیا" (p. 106).
