# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 039 — pp. 425–436

The chunk opens mid-section on a mosque in the care of the sajjada nashins of "میان وڈا" (Hazrat Muhammad Ismail, known as Mian Wadda) and on its imam Mian Jan Muhammad, with a long ta'wiz story and a drawing of the ta'wiz (pp. 425–428). **The heading of that section is not in this chunk.** Titled sections then follow in this order: "در ذکر احوال مقبرہ ابو الحسن خان بن آصف خان مرحوم" (p. 428), covering the dome, its lightning damage, the family and a chronogram (pp. 428–429); "حال مقبرہ سردار خان بھٹ[OCR?] یعنی نقال[OCR?]" (pp. 429–430); "مقبرہ مخدومہ سیدہ حکم[OCR?] اہلیہ نواب ابو الحسن آصف خان[OCR?] مرحوم" (p. 430), which also carries a passage from Khulasat al-Tawarikh on Lahore's history (pp. 430–431); "احوال خانقاہ و مزار حضرت شاہ گھوڑی[OCR?] شاہ کہ نام اصلی اون کا جھولن[OCR?] شاہ" (pp. 431–436), with its buildings, graves, a full Chishti chain, the horse karamat, and a sub-account of the living Mahmud Shah Qadiri and his Qadiri shajra (pp. 435–436); and "احوال مقبرہ پیر روڑان والہ" (p. 436), which breaks off at the chunk's end. None of the saints or tombs with a section is an archive row. Archive mapping is the Lahore-in-general history (35 ids), Maharaja Ranjit Singh named as an era and a grantor, and Baba Farid named in a silsila chain.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Mian Wadda mosque**:
  - A brick enclosure, "۵۱[OCR?] گز" long and seven gaz wide, with Jan Muhammad's grave and two more graves of descendants (p. 425). A "چاہ چرخی دار" outside the door (p. 425).
  - Since Jan Muhammad's death the mosque has been "زیر متصرفہ سجادہ نشینان میان وڈا صاحب" (p. 425).
  - The present imam's tenure is under Ahmad Din; Hafiz Darwesh made him "متولی" of the mosque and khanqah (p. 427).
  - "اب میاں کوی عرس وغیرہ و تو نہیں ہوتا", but his descendants hold an annual fatiha in their village (p. 428).
- **The ta'wiz**: its ijazat has passed "سینہ بسینہ" in Jan Muhammad's family (p. 427). Ahmad Din keeps it hidden but gave it and its ijazat to the author, who publishes it. Practice: daily a chilla after "ایک ہزار درود شریف" offered to the Prophet, then writing the ta'wiz (pp. 427–428). The ta'wiz is drawn as an image with a numbered square (p. 428).
- **Abu al-Hasan's tomb**:
  - Large dome, two-storied inside, with eight [illegible] around (p. 428). Interior lime plaster; painted ceilings (pp. 428–429).
  - Lightning: "بروز پنجشنبہ ہفتم ساون سمبت ۱۸۹۹[OCR?] وقت ایک بجے دن"; "تخمیناً آٹھ دس آدمی محافظ" died and some were wounded (p. 429).
  - Shaista Khan placed hafiz Quran-readers at the tomb and made a waqf (p. 430). A large round brick well to its west, wide enough for "دس [illegible] چوب" to work; said to hold a secret passage ("راہ خفیہ[OCR?]") toward the tomb (p. 430).
- **Sardar Khan Bhat's tomb**: white, octagonal, four doors, four minarets, one fallen. The brick platform's bricks were taken by "خشت فروشوں" (brick-sellers) (pp. 429–430).
- **Ghore Shah's khanqah**:
  - Enclosure wall built by "محت[OCR?] شاہ" (p. 432). Platforms, dalans, a chaubara, a small mosque "مسمیٰ حاجی [illegible]" (pp. 432–433).
  - Land "ایک کنال [illegible] مرلہ" and a well granted by Ranjit Singh. The cultivator "شمس[OCR?]" takes "دو حصہ پیداواری[OCR?]" and gives one fixed share (p. 432).
  - Three graves on the main platform: "گھوڑی[OCR?] شاہ صاحب", "سید جھولن شاہ صاحب", "میران لطف شاہ" (p. 433).
  - Offerings: a heap of horses ("ڈھیر [illegible] گھوڑوں کا") lies west of the platform (p. 433). "گلی گھوڑی طلائی اور نقرئی اور اصلی لوگ وہان چڑھاتے ہین" (p. 435).
  - Custody: "مہتاب شاہ فقیر سجادہ نشین اور مالک" (p. 433).
  - Mela: "۱۲[OCR?] رجب کو میلہ ہوتا ہے", and something on "۲۳[OCR?] [illegible]"; the place is damaged ("موضع [illegible]") (p. 433). A gathering "ماہ رمضان کی [illegible: numerals] کو" at Miran Sayyid Wali's mazar (p. 434).
  - Urs on his death date, with faqirs and qawwali (p. 434).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **Mian Wadda's ta'wiz**: Jan Muhammad lived on what he earned lawfully. Mian Wadda gave him a ta'wiz to keep at home; the next day his house was [illegible] and he became "امیر کبیر" (p. 426).
- A man who for ten years had not eaten his fill was told by Jan Muhammad to recite "سبحان اللہ", then "والحمد للہ", then the full kalima of praise. He grew rich, and treasures buried in the ground called out to him "ہمکو اوٹھا لو" (pp. 426–427).
- Jan Muhammad returned the original ta'wiz to Mian Wadda after copying it, and kept its ijazat (p. 427).
- **Ghore Shah**: he loved horses. A murid brought him a wooden horse; he rode it in a state ("حالت") and said "اے گھوڑی دوڑ", and it ran. From that day he was known as Ghore Shah (pp. 434–435).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author distinguishes two Abu al-Hasans: the Asaf Khan Abu al-Hasan whose tomb is near Jahangir's, "جہانگیری", and this Abu al-Hasan son of Asaf Khan, "شاہجہانی" (p. 431).
- On publishing the ta'wiz: without ijazat from a "کامل" such books are of no use; since he has the ijazat, he shares its benefit and asks readers to remember him in prayer (p. 427).

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory.** Heavy throughout: dense `[OCR?]` and `[illegible]` flags; the ta'wiz drawing (p. 428), the Persian couplet (p. 426), the shajra verses (pp. 435–436) and most numerals are damaged. Every quotation keeps its flags. Folio markers are printed but not cited, per the brief.
- **Lahore-in-general bullet** (pp. 430–431): place-level, weigh accordingly. It is the book's Khulasat al-Tawarikh account of the city's history and of Ranjit Singh's cantonments; it is attached to all 35 `lahore` ids.
- **shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh** appears only within the Lahore-in-general head; the held identity question (whether "Shah Chiragh" conflates two men) is not touched or resolved here. "عبد رزاق[OCR?]" in Mahmud Shah's Qadiri shajra (p. 436) is not mapped to this row; the chunk does not say "شاہ چراغ".
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh**: person-level. Ranjit Singh is named as an era and as a grantor; the samadhi itself is not mentioned. Weigh accordingly.
- **shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar**: a bare name in a silsila chain, with "گنج شکر[OCR?]" flagged (p. 434). Person-level; weigh accordingly.
- **Lightning date**: "سمبت ۱۸۹۹[OCR?]" is placed "بعہد مہاراجہ رنجیت سنگھ" (p. 429). Flagged numeral; not converted or checked.
- **Ghore Shah's identity**: the heading makes Ghore Shah and Jhulan Shah one man (p. 431), and the text calls Muhammad Hafiz both "المشہور بہ جھولن شاہ" (p. 433) and "المشہور بہ گھوڑی شاہ" (p. 434).
