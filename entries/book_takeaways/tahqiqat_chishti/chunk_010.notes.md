# tahqiqat_chishti — chunk 010 — pp. 120–132

This chunk is a walk through sites outside the city's gates. It opens mid-account of a Shaikh Jauhar[OCR?], quoting a "تذکرہ قطب العالم" and a qita'a with a damaged date (p. 120). Then come short titled sections. "فصل در بیان حال مسافر خانہ میاں محمد سلطان" (p. 120). "فصل در بیان حال شیو و بالہ باوا گنگا برشت[OCR?]" (p. 121). "فصل در بیان حال علاقہ شہید گنج", with the tomb of Mir Mannu (pp. 121–122). "فصل در ذکر احوال سرائی میاں محمد سلطان وغیرہ" (pp. 122–123). "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ مخنث" (p. 123). The long section that runs to the end of the chunk is "فصل در ذکر مقبرہ حضرت شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی قادری المتخلص بغریب" (pp. 123–132). It covers the tomb, the graves and buildings around it, the large graveyard beside it (including the author's own family graves, and the enclosures of the Nawabs of Multan), the saint's birth and death dates and chronograms, two karamat, and a detailed division of the shrine's income among his heirs. Damage is very heavy throughout: almost every line carries `[illegible]`.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **darbar-abul-muali-qadri** (حضرت شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی قادری المتخلص بغریب; also "حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی"):
  - Section title gives his name as "شاہ خیر الدین ابو المعالی قادری" with the pen name "غریب" (p. 123).
  - Location: "یہ مکان باہر دروازہ موچی کے واقع ہے", this building is outside Mochi Gate, and its entrance door faces south (p. 123).
  - The dome is octagonal, "مشکل[OCR?] روضہ جناب پیر دستگیر قدس سرہ العزیز", shaped like the rauza of Pir Dastgir. There is also a door on the north side. The dome is "رنگ سفید منقش", painted white. A Qur'anic verse in Arabic script is over the south door. The wooden door is "ہرمچی[OCR?]" in colour with a green niche, and above it is a carved lattice (p. 123).
  - Inscription over the door: "ان اولیآء اللہ لا یموتون بل ینتقلون[OCR?]" (p. 123).
  - Stones "کچھ[OCR?] مرمت طلب ہو گئے ہے", some need repair. The tomb has "آٹھ[OCR?]" doors. In "سات[OCR?]" of them "کچہری کے لکھے" (court records?) are kept (p. 124).
  - Four pakka graves are on the platform inside: (1) Hazrat Shah Abul Maali; (2) "حضرت شاہ محمد باقر صاحب صاحبزادہ"; (3) "حضرت شاہ محمد رضا[OCR?] … جو خلف شاہ محمد فاضل[OCR?] صاحب والد حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی" (see Doubts); (4) a grave linked to "حضرت حاجی شاہ محمد الفضل[OCR?] صاحب" (p. 124).
  - Graves of his descendants are inside another enclosure in the dome. Names given: "مولی[OCR?] شاہ", "سمن[OCR?] شاہ", "شاہ سلطان", "شاہ محمد علی" (p. 124).
  - Comparison of tombs: at Shergarh[OCR?] there is "مقبرہ عالیشان حضرت داود بندگی[OCR?] کا", which "اسی مقبرہ کی موافق ہے", and the tomb is also "موافق[OCR?] مقبرہ حضرت پیر دستگیر محی الدین عبد القادر جیلانی کے". The book says "گویا یہ ہر سہ مقبرہ و مزار باہم مطابق ہین", all three tombs match each other (p. 124).
  - Construction: the tomb was begun "حین حیات", in his lifetime, but not finished. The dome was built after his death (p. 124).
  - Under the Sikhs "سجادہ والے", the sajjada-holders, rebuilt it again. A wall around the tomb was built "عرصہ سہ[OCR?] سال سے", about three years ago (p. 124).
  - The western staircase side of the rauza was left unfinished in his lifetime. Its completion is linked to "محمد باقر صاحب", but the passage is damaged (p. 125).
  - He is given as living in the era of "اکبر و جہانگیر و شاہ جہان" and "میان میر صاحب [illegible] یہ حضرت ہم عہد", a contemporary of Mian Mir (p. 125).
  - A red-stone well platform was built "۲۰ بیست سال سے", 20 years ago. A brick near the well has "اللہ سبحانہ[OCR?]" on it (p. 125).
  - On the platform are graves of "۸[OCR?] نواسون" (his daughters' sons) and of "محمد صوفی", described as his murid and "حاکم یا شہزادہ[OCR?]". West of the entrance is the grave of "محمد شاہ". To the east is the grave of "شاہ حسن", one of his murids, known as "حضرت حسن شاہ برہان پور[OCR?]" (p. 125).
  - "مسجد شرق رویہ مین جو کنواں کے وہ بھی خود حضرت نی بنوایا", the saint himself had the well in the eastern mosque built (p. 125).
  - Dalans to the south: one was given to "میان عظیم[OCR?] شاہ". In another sits "سید مراد[OCR?] … نبیرہ حضرت", a descendant of the saint. One dalan was built in "سنہ ۱۲۳[OCR?] ھ" (p. 125).
  - In an old dalan "سید امام شاہ" used to sit. The dalan used by another descendant is linked to "مسمات نوران طوایف[OCR?]" (p. 125).
  - A two-door kotha was built by "سید مہر شاہ صاحب و سید عالم شاہ"; "اوکا فقیر" sits in it (p. 125).
  - Many houses belong to his descendants, and there is a graveyard around the tomb (p. 125).
  - A kotha west of the tomb is where "اول وقت [illegible] غسل حضرت کا ہوا ہے", the saint's ghusl was performed (p. 128).
  - Birth, quoted from "صاحب [illegible] الاولیا": "تاریخ دہم ماہ ذی الحجہ روز عید اضحی روز دوشنبہ[OCR?] سنہ ۹۶۰[OCR?] نہصد[OCR?] و شست[OCR?] ہجری میں تولد ہوی لاہور" (p. 130).
  - Death: "سولویں ماہ ربیع الاول [illegible] شنبہ[OCR?]". The year is not legible (p. 130).
  - Chronograms were sent for "اندراج کتاب ہذا" by "حضرت سید مراد [illegible] شاہ", a descendant. They are printed as qita'at, mostly illegible. One hemistich reads "رحلتنش گفتا معالی خیر دین" (pp. 130–131).
  - "وفات حضرت کی عہد جہانگیر بادشاہ مین وقوع مین آئی", he died in Jahangir's reign, when "بعد وفات اکبر بادشاہ کے گیارہ[OCR?] سال" had passed. The age at death is illegible (p. 131).
  - His karamat are "صد ہا مشہور". Those quoted are taken from "صاحب سفینہ" and "تذکرة العارفین" (p. 131). See Legends.
- **shergarh** (book: "شیر گڑھ[OCR?]", "حضرت داود بندگی[OCR?]"):
  - The tomb of "حضرت داود بندگی[OCR?]" at Shergarh[OCR?] is called "مقبرہ عالیشان". The book says it matches the tomb of Shah Abul Maali, and that both match the tomb of Pir Dastgir Abd al-Qadir Jilani (p. 124).
  - The same damaged passage names "مزار شیر گڑھ[OCR?]" and "مزار سید جلال الدین [illegible] کوٹھال[OCR?]" alongside a "سید رحمت اللہ" (p. 124).
  - A "شیخ داؤد" is named in the damaged chronogram passage (p. 130).
- **shrine-of-mian-mir** (book: "میان میر صاحب", "حضرت میاں میر[OCR?]"):
  - Named as a contemporary of Shah Abul Maali (p. 125).
  - Mulla Shah, "مرشد دارا شکوہ", is called the one "جسکا ذکر خیر احوال حضرت میاں میر[OCR?] مین لکھا جا چکا", already written up in the book's account of Mian Mir (p. 131).
  - The name also appears as a place: "کوٹھی لاٹ صاحب بہادر و میاں میر[OCR?]", near the Shivdwala of Ganga Barisht[OCR?] (p. 121). See Doubts.
- **gurdwara-shaheed-ganj-singh-singhnian** (book: "شہید گنج", "سمادہ[OCR?] سکھو[OCR?]"):
  - "علاقہ شہید گنج" contains the tomb of Mir Mannu. In his time many Sikhs were killed, and there was a general order to seize Sikhs and kill them at once (p. 121).
  - Near "سرائی میاں محمد سلطان[OCR?]", outside Delhi Gate, there are two mosques to the east. Sikhs use one of them, where "گرنتھ[OCR?]" is read, and "اس [illegible] نام شہید گنج[OCR?] مشہور[OCR?]" (p. 122).
  - There is "ایک مکان کلاں سمادہ[OCR?] سکھو[OCR?] کی". It was built because "میر[OCR?] مسعود[OCR?]", a "قاتل[OCR?] قوم سکھو[OCR?]", killed Sikhs one day, and they died at this place. "نام اس سمادہ[OCR?] کا شہید گنج رکھا" (p. 122).
  - Granth is read there and "بھنگ[OCR?] بکثرت خرچ ہوتی ہی". Formerly, "بعہد مہاراجہ صاحب", a "فیض عام" (public distribution) was kept up there (p. 123).
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** (book: "مہا راجہ[OCR?] رنجیت[OCR?] سنگہ"; the samadhi itself is not mentioned):
  - "مہا راجہ[OCR?] رنجیت[OCR?] سنگہ نے ملتان کو فتح کیا". In that conquest Nawab Muzaffar Khan "شہید ہوی", and the rest of the Nawab's family were captured and brought to Lahore (p. 130).

  - An old mosque nearby has been bought by the same sahib, who has started building on it. A church and domes have been built "عیسوی[OCR?] لوگوں کے رہنے کیواسطے" (p. 121).

- **Tomb of a mukhannas** ("مقبرہ مخنث"):

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Shah Abul Maali melas**: "یہاں سال ہر میں ایک دفعہ ۷[OCR?] ربیع الاول کو جو تاریخ وفات حضرت کی ہی میلہ ہوتا". There are also melas at Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha (p. 126).
- **Descendants in turns**: on the northern platform "نوبت بہ نوبت صاحبزادہ حضرت کے بیٹھتی ہین", the saint's descendants sit in rotation (p. 125).
- **Custodianship under the Sikhs**: "بعہد سکھاں … سجادہ والے پھر دوبارہ اسکو تعمیر کرایا" (p. 124).
- **Shrine income and heirs** (pp. 131–132):
  - "وارث حضرت کر دو قسم": the heirs are of two kinds, "پوترین[OCR?]" (sons' line) and nawasas (daughters' sons) (p. 131).
  - Named living descendants: "سید عالم شاہ" (p. 131); "سید مہر شاہ", who "لا ولد ہے" (p. 132).
  - Named nawasa heirs: Sayyid Chiragh Shah and others, "ولدان سید غازی شاہ"; Sayyid Hakim Shah, Sayyid Haidar Shah, Jahangir Shah, Muhammad[OCR?] Shah; Fateh Shah and Sardar Shah, "برادران سید نوٹی[OCR?] شاہ مرحوم"; "معصوم شاہ"; "منٹو[OCR?] شاہ بن امیر شاہ"; Karam Shah; "سید ملال[OCR?] شاہ"; Ramzan Shah (p. 132).
  - Turns at the shrine: "چودہ[OCR?] روز نو اسہ[OCR?] اور دہ [illegible] روز شیرہ[OCR?] مقررہ پر حاضر رہتی ہیں". Whatever comes in on those days is theirs, "[illegible] گفت و گرفت نہین ہوئی", with no dispute (p. 132).
  - Shares: "مدر و چڑھاوہ" (offerings) are divided "خواہ کوئی غیر حاضر ہو یا حاضر", whether or not a person is present (p. 132).
  - A third share goes to "سید پیر و سہا[OCR?] ور شاہ پسران سید حاکم شاہ" (p. 132).
  - The other two parts are split into "سات[OCR?]" shares. A seventh goes to the sons of Nauti[OCR?] Shah (p. 132).
  - The remaining six are split into "پانچ[OCR?]": one share to "دختران معصوم شاہ"; one to Chiragh Shah and "[illegible] ور شاہ ساکنان لاہور"; one to Sayyid Pir Shah; one to "حسن علی[OCR?] شاہ"; and one is halved between "سید حاکم شاہ پسر حیدر شاہ ساکن کوٹلی [illegible] خاں" and the late Hakim Shah's line (p. 132).
  - Some shares are handed to "زوجہ سید پیر شاہ" (p. 132).
  - The book says of one line "معلوم نہیں", not known (p. 132).
- **Shahid Ganj samadh**: Granth reading, heavy use of bhang, and a former "فیض عام" under "مہاراجہ صاحب" (p. 123).
- **Votive clay horses**: at Ghore Shah's grave "اب بھی وہان لوگ مٹی سے گھوڑے بطور نذر چڑھاتی ہین". Horses are on the wall and grave platform (p. 123).
- **Funding by begging**: Ganga Barisht[OCR?] paid for the Shivdwala by begging. The ex-commissioner of Lahore granted the land (p. 121).
- **Colonial reuse of older buildings**:
  - The mukhannas tomb is now an office with verandas, next to the postmaster's office (p. 123).
  - Railway sahibs use a mosque near the Sarai (p. 122).
  - An old mosque was bought and a church built near the Shivdwala (p. 121).
  - Court papers ("کچہری کے لکھے") are kept in seven[OCR?] doors of Abul Maali's tomb (p. 124).
- **Municipal control of the graveyard**: see the Shahid Ganj mound above (p. 127).
- **Why the graveyard is so crowded**: "اس مقام کو اگر محشر[OCR?] القبور کہا جاوے تو بجا ہو گا". The reason given is that it was near the city and everyone wanted their family's graves close by, so as not to have to go far (p. 130).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- **The rosary for Mulla Nimatullah[OCR?]**: the saint's servant brought him a fine tasbih. Mulla Nimatullah thought that if the saint were a true wali "صاحب کشف" he would give it to him. When Nimatullah left, the saint called him over: "یہ تسبیح لیلو تمہاری نذر ہی". The book cites "صاحب سفینہ" and "تذکرة العارفین" (p. 131).
- **The white turban of Mulla Shah**: Mulla Shah wondered whether Ghaus al-Azam knew of his devotion. That night he dreamt he stood bareheaded in a desert, and Ghaus al-Azam gave him a white turban. In the morning Shah Abul Maali's servant summoned him, and the saint gave him a white turban, saying "یہ وہ ہی دستار ہے جو [illegible] تمکو حضرت غوث الاعظم نی تمکو بخشی تہی" (p. 131).
- **The headless martyrs**: "یہ شہید مہین[OCR?]". Their heads were cut off, and the body came fighting as far as this spot. Someone said "دیکھو کیا تعجب ہے کہ بغیر سر بدن چلا آتے مین", and on hearing this they fell. These are the graves of "امام جعفر" and "امام صادق", from the time of Sultan Mahmud Ghaznavi (p. 127).
- **Shaikh Jauhar[OCR?] and the Khan**: the saint warned the Khan, who had ordered him out of Lahore. What followed for the Khan is illegible (p. 120).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **Tombs compared**: the author says the tombs of Shah Abul Maali, Daud Bandagi at Shergarh, and Pir Dastgir Abd al-Qadir Jilani are alike: "گویا یہ ہر سہ مقبرہ و مزار باہم مطابق ہین" (p. 124).
- **Graveyard crowding**: the author explains the density of graves by the graveyard's nearness to the city (p. 130).
- **The author writes from his own visits** ("کمترین نے … اس مقام کو دیکھا", p. 121; his family graves, p. 127). For Abul Maali's chronograms he relies on material sent by a descendant (p. 130).

## Citable passages

- "ان اولیآء اللہ لا یموتون بل ینتقلون[OCR?]" — the inscription over Shah Abul Maali's door: "The friends of God do not die but move on." (p. 123)
- "گویا یہ ہر سہ مقبرہ و مزار باہم مطابق ہین" — "As if all three tombs and shrines match one another" (Abul Maali; Daud Bandagi at Shergarh; Pir Dastgir). (p. 124)
- "یہاں سال ہر میں ایک دفعہ ۷[OCR?] ربیع الاول کو جو تاریخ وفات حضرت کی ہی میلہ ہوتا" — "Here once a year, on 7[OCR?] Rabi al-Awwal, the date of the Hazrat's death, a mela is held." (p. 126)
- "اب بھی وہان لوگ مٹی سے گھوڑے بطور نذر چڑھاتی ہین" — of Ghore Shah's grave: "Even now people offer clay horses there as votive gifts." (p. 123)
- "اس مقام کو اگر محشر[OCR?] القبور کہا جاوے تو بجا ہو گا" — "If this place were called the gathering-ground of graves, it would be fitting." (p. 130)

## Doubts

- **Damage inventory**: `[illegible]` runs through every page, and `[OCR?]` sits on most names and numerals. Pp. 120, 122, 124 and 132 are the worst. Every date is quoted as printed, with its flags. The years at pp. 120 and 130 (death) are illegible.
- **Abul Maali's death date: the chunk contradicts itself.** The mela is held on "۷[OCR?] ربیع الاول", called his death date (p. 126). The quoted death date is "سولویں ماہ ربیع الاول", the 16th (p. 130). The ۷ is OCR-flagged. Check against the PDF.
- **Abul Maali's birth**: "سنہ ۹۶۰[OCR?] نہصد[OCR?] و شست[OCR?]" (p. 130). The digits and the words agree as transcribed, but all three are flagged. The source title is damaged: "صاحب [illegible] الاولیا".
- **Abul Maali's father**: in "حضرت شاہ محمد رضا[OCR?] [illegible] کی جو خلف شاہ محمد فاضل[OCR?] صاحب والد حضرت شاہ ابو المعالی" (p. 124), it is not clear whether Muhammad Raza[OCR?] or Muhammad Fazil[OCR?] is the father. Do not use this without checking the PDF.
- **shergarh** is mapped on the person rule (Daud Bandagi = principal figure "Daud Bandagi Kirmani") and on the place name "شیر گڑھ[OCR?]". Both words are OCR-flagged, and the book does not give "Kirmani".
- **shrine-of-mian-mir**: mapped on a contemporaneity statement (p. 125) and a cross-reference (p. 131). At p. 121 "میاں میر[OCR?]" appears as a place beside the Lat Sahib's kothi. That is not mapped as a fact about the shrine.
- **gurdwara-shaheed-ganj-singh-singhnian** is mapped on the name "شہید گنج" only. The book does not say "Singh Singhnian" and does not name women or children. "شہید گنج" is used for three things: the area with Mir Mannu's tomb (p. 121), the Sikh samadh near Sarai Mian Muhammad Sultan (pp. 122–123), and a graveyard mound south of Abul Maali's tomb (p. 126). The chunk does not say these are one site. The Sikh-killer's name is "میر[OCR?] مسعود[OCR?]" at p. 122, but the Shahid Ganj area is tied to "میر منو" at p. 121. The book does not equate the two.
- **samadhi-of-maharaja-ranjit-singh** is matched on the principal figure only. The samadhi is not mentioned. Every word of "مہا راجہ[OCR?] رنجیت[OCR?] سنگہ" is flagged (p. 130). The unnamed "مہاراجہ صاحب" at p. 123 is not attributed to him.
- **Multan bullet**: the attachment is place-level, not site-specific. The material is the conquest of Multan and the fate of its Nawabs, and it is recorded at their Lahore graves.
- **Lahore bullet**: the attachment is place-level. Only Mir Mannu's governorship of Lahore and the order against Sikhs are carried there (p. 121).
- **Not mapped**:
  - "فقیر چراغ الدین" (p. 126) and "سید چراغ شاہ" (p. 132) have nothing to do with shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh as given here.
  - "ایوب شاہ بادشاہ[OCR?]" (p. 125) is not mapped to shrine-of-hazrat-muhammad-ayub-shah-bukhari, since the chunk gives no link.
  - "سید جلال الدین [illegible] کوٹھال[OCR?]" (p. 124) is not mapped to any Jalaluddin row.
  - Sultan Mahmud Ghaznavi (p. 127) does not reach darbar-malik-ahmad-ayaz, because Ayaz is not named.
- **"پنجابی"** at p. 121 labels the language of a saying about Mir Mannu. It is not province material, so there is no province bullet.
- **Who is "Shaikh Jauhar[OCR?]"**: the qita'a at p. 120 names "عبد الجلیل آن قطب العالم". Whether that is Shaikh Jauhar[OCR?] (or the subject of "تذکرہ قطب العالم") cannot be settled from this chunk.
- **"صاحب سفینہ"** (p. 131) is cited without a full title.
- **Folio markers are not cited.** As transcribed they are irregular: p. 122 repeats "114" after p. 121's "115", and p. 131 has none.
