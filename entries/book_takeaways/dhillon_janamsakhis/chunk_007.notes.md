# dhillon_janamsakhis — chunk 007 — pp. 194–227

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **gurudwara-janam-asthan-nankana-sahib**, **gurdwara-babay-de-ber**, **gurdwara-balila-sahib-bal-lila-sahib**, **gurdwara-chakki-sahib**, **gurdwara-choa-sahib**, **gurdwara-darbar-sahib-kartarpur**, **gurdwara-khoohi-bhai-lalo-bhai-lalo-di-khooi**, **gurdwara-malji-sahib**, **gurdwara-panja-sahib**, **gurdwara-patti-sahib**, **gurdwara-pehli-patshahi-jind-pir-sukkur**, **gurdwara-rori-sahib**, **gurdwara-sach-khand-sahib**, **gurdwara-sacha-sauda**, **gurdwara-sahib-saidpur-guru-nanak-dev-ji**, **gurdwara-sri-kiara-sahib**, **gurdwara-sri-tilganji-sahib**, **gurdwara-tambo-sahib** (the book's "Nanak", "the Guru"; person-level):
  - In Dhillon's retelling, Nanak deferred departure at his sister Nanki's plea; after her death and Jai Ram's "there was nothing now to hold Nanak back" (p. 194, folio 214).
  - With "the ever faithful Mardana" he "travelled north to Multan and then to Sukkur", south along the Indus to "Thatta (now in Sindh)", west to "Hinglaj {now in Balochistan)", joined Haj pilgrims and "crossed the Arabian Sea" (p. 194, folio 214).
  - "Nanak was dressed in blue robes, a colour considered sacred by the Muslims"; he carried "a stalf [text-layer?] [staff], a prayer mar [text-layer?] [mat], the holy book Quran" and an ablution pot (pp. 194–195, folios 214–215).
  - In the retelling he preached each evening on the caravan route to Mecca that "true religion meant living a simple, honest life" (p. 195, folio 215).
  - The Mecca story ("The Abode of God"): Nanak tells a maulvi "We are travellers from Hindustan… we are nor [text-layer?] [not] pilgrims because we are not Muslims" (p. 196, folio 216); he sleeps with feet toward the Kaaba, is struck by the qazi, and asks to have his feet pointed "cowards [text-layer?] [towards] a place where God does not reside" (pp. 198–200, folios 218–220); the qazi concedes "God lives everywhere" (p. 201, folio 221).
  - Ch. 15 "Music and Worship": from Mecca to Medina, then north-east "till they reached Baghdad on rhe [text-layer?] [the] banks of the river Tigris" (p. 204, folio not stated); he camped in a tomb on the city's outskirts (p. 205, folio not stated), sang with Mardana's rabaab, and, facing a hostile crowd with stones, sang the azaan, after which "the fundamentalists fell as one on cheir [text-layer?] [their] knees, to pray" (pp. 206–209, folios 227–230).
  - Ch. 16 "The Widow and Her Lost Son": near Baghdad on the Tigris, Nanak (described as "from Hindoscan [text-layer?] [Hindostan]") consoles a widow whose son drowned twelve years earlier: "You have not lost your son; you have only lose [text-layer?] [lost] his body" (pp. 219–222, folios 241–244).
  - Return journey "co Karcarpur [text-layer?] [to Kartarpur]": Baghdad → Iran → Kabul → Peshawar; "passed through Kandahar (Afghanistan) and Hasan Abdal (Pakistan)"; "a south-eastern route co [text-layer?] [to] the Punjab"; then Saidpur (p. 224, folio 246).
  - "Finally Nanak reached Karcarpur [text-layer?] [Kartarpur], which marked the end of his fourth and last udasi" (p. 225, folio 247).
  - Ch. 17: at Kartarpur Nanak "laid aside the pilgrim's clothes… and assumed the garb of an ordinary householder" (p. 227, folio 251).

- **gurdwara-babay-nanki** (the book's "Nanki"): "Nanki came down with a mysterious ailment and breathed her last in 1518 at the age of 6:fcy-four [text-layer?] [sixty-four]" (p. 194, folio 214). Her husband "Jai Ram, not able to sustain the loss, also passed away" three days later (p. 194, folio 214).

- **gurdwara-rori-sahib** (the book's "Mardana"): accompanies Nanak to Mecca, Medina and Baghdad (pp. 194, 196, 204, folios 214, 216, not stated); "Mardana tuned his rabaab and struck up the accompaniment co [text-layer?] [to] his master's singing" at the Baghdad tomb (p. 206, folio 227); visits "Tilla Balnath and Katasraj" with Nanak (p. 224, folio 246); at Saidpur "legend has it that he and Mardana coo [text-layer?] [too] were taken prisoner" (p. 224, folio 246).

- **gurdwara-khoohi-bhai-lalo-bhai-lalo-di-khooi** (the book's "Lalo"): "they came to Saidpur, where one of Nanak's fuse [text-layer?] [first] disciples Lalo resided" (p. 224, folio 246). Person-level; the book does not tie Saidpur to any archive place.

- **katas-raj-temples** (the book's "Katasraj"): "Mardana and he visited the shrines of Tilla Balnath and Katasraj (both now in Pakistan)" on the return to the Punjab (p. 224, folio 246).

- **gurdwara-panja-sahib** (the book's "Hasan Abdal (Pakistan)"): Nanak "passed through… Hasan Abdal (Pakistan)" on his return journey; nothing more is said of the place (p. 224, folio 246).

- **shaktipeeth-shri-hinglaj-mata-mandir** (the book's "Hinglaj {now in Balochistan)"): from Thatta Nanak "went west to Hinglaj {now in Balochistan), where he mer [text-layer?] [met] a group of pilgrims who had embarked on the Haj" (p. 194, folio 214). No shrine or goddess is mentioned.

- **prahladpuri-temple**, **shrine-of-bahauddin-zakariya**, **shrine-of-hafiz-muhammad-jamal-multani**, **shrine-of-hazrat-shah-ali-akbar-shah-ali-akbar-shamsi**, **shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam**, **shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari**, **shrine-of-shah-yusaf-gardez**, **shrine-of-syed-musa-pak** (the book's "Multan"): Nanak and Mardana "fuse [text-layer?] [first] travelled north to Multan" at the start of the journey to Mecca (p. 194, folio 214).

- **gurdwara-dash-mesh-pita**, **gurdwara-pehli-patshahi-jind-pir-sukkur**, **kalka-cave-temple-asthan-of-kalka-devi**, **sadh-belo-sadh-belo-island-temple**, **sant-baba-asudaram-darbar-panno-aqil** (the book's "Sukkur"): from Multan they went "to Sukkur from where he turned southwards along the river Indus" (p. 194, folio 214).

- **dargah-pir-ratan-nath-jee**, **gorakhnath-goraknath-temple**, **gurdwara-bhai-beba-singh**, **gurdwara-bhai-joga-singh**, **kali-bari-mandir**, **panj-tirath**, **rahman-baba-mausoleum-rehman-baba-shrine**, **shrine-of-akhund-darweza-baba**, **shrine-of-mian-umar-baba-chamkani**, **wadpagga-sharif** (the book's "Peshawar"): "He visited Kabul before reaching Peshawar" on the return from Baghdad (p. 224, folio 246).

- **allo-mahar**, **gurdwara-babay-de-ber**, **jagannath-temple-sialkot**, **shahwala-teja-singh-mandir**, **shrine-of-imam-ali-ul-haq** (the book's "Sialkot"): during "the Mughal Emperor Babur's third incursion into India", "Sialkot had offered no resistance and had been spared the usual bloody massacre and plunder" (p. 224, folio 246).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)
- **Daulat Khan**: "at that time making his bid for the throne in Delhi", came one last time to persuade Nanak to give up his travels; "This was to be their last meeting" (p. 194, folio 214).
- **Thatta**: "Thatta (now in Sindh)", reached going south along the Indus from Sukkur (p. 194, folio 214). No archive row.
- **Jeddah, Mecca, the Kaaba, Medina**: the boat "brought them to Jeddah" (p. 195, folio 215); a mosque and maulvi at "a little habitation on the outskirts of Mecca" (pp. 195–196, folios 215–216); "Qazi Rukn-ud-clin [text-layer?] [Rukn-ud-din] (who was a strict follower of Islam)" (p. 198, folio 218); Medina (p. 204, folio not stated).
- **Baghdad**: "founded in A.D. 769 on rhe [text-layer?] [the] site of an older city of rhe [the] same name by Caliph Al-Mansur"; on the "Khurasan route" (p. 204, folio not stated); "focal point of the Islamic Golden Age"; lost pre-eminence with "the shifting of the capital to Samara"; "Halagu Khan, the Mongol chieftain, sacked it in 1258" (p. 205, folio not stated). A tomb on its outskirts "built of undressed scone [text-layer?] [stone]", courtyard with "arched alcoves" (p. 205, folio not stated).
- **Widow's settlement "on the banks op [text-layer?] [of] the Tigris, some distance from Baghdad"** (p. 213, folio not stated); ferry disaster: "Twem:y-three [text-layer?] [Twenty-three] people, including my son, never returned" (p. 221, folio 243).
- **Iran, Kabul, Kandahar (Afghanistan)**: return route (p. 224, folio 246).
- **Tilla Balnath**: "the shrines of Tilla Balnath and Katasraj (both now in Pakistan)" (p. 224, folio 246). Not mapped (see Doubts).
- **Saidpur**: Lalo's home; captured by Babur after resisting — soldiers "killed mercilessly", civilians "sold into slavery or held captive" (p. 224, folio 246). Babur ("Bahar [text-layer?] [Babar]") visited Nanak, freed him and at his request all Saidpur's prisoners, "returned all the property" and "paid compensation" (pp. 224–225, folios 246–247). The scenes moved Nanak to hymns that "find pride of place in the Adi Gramh [text-layer?] [Adi Granth]" (p. 225, folio 247).
- **Kartarpur**: "had by now grown into a full-fledged township"; people "from different social backgrounds… castes and creeds" came to live there (p. 227, folio 251). Not mapped (see Doubts).

## Practices, institutions, economy
- The Mecca-outskirts maulvi: "Members of the congregation take turns co [text-layer?] [to] send me food" (p. 197, folio 217).
- The Baghdad tomb had no attendants but was freshly swept, with "remnants of the Thursday evening prayers - Little oil lamps and a fresh chaddar covering the grave" (p. 206, folio 227).
- After prayers Nanak had the boys "come and receive prasad" (p. 206, folio 227); "Singing is an essential part of our worship" versus "Music does not form a part of our worship" (p. 207, folio 228).
- The proposed punishment was "the traditionally Arab one - stoning unto death" (p. 208, folio 229).
- At Kartarpur Nanak set up a routine showing its people were "by no means members ofa [of a] religious or monastic order"; each went about "his or her ordinary, everyday occupation" (p. 227, folio 251).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)
- Mecca: the maulvi's single meal "was more than enough co [to] feed the three of them" (p. 197, folio 217).
- Baghdad: Nanak's azaan made the hostile crowd drop their stones; "They knew chat [that]… they had experienced divinity" (p. 209, folio 230).
- Widow: a faqir foretold "A holy man will come from the east and he will restore your son to you" (p. 218, folio 240); she dreamed of a radiant holy man she later knew was Nanak (p. 221, folio 243); "Nanak had, indeed, given her son back to her" — by consolation, not resurrection, in the retelling (p. 223, folio not stated).
- Saidpur: "legend has it" Nanak and Mardana were taken prisoner and Babur released them (p. 224, folio 246).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)
- Author's reflection: "The most potenc [potent] force in uniting people and motivating them to violence is a perceived threat co [to] their religion" (p. 208, folio 229).
- Author: "The azaan is considered by many, including non-Muslims, to be the most beautiful music in the world" (p. 209, folio 230).
- Author: "The Prophet Muhammad had banned the singing of songs, which could cause evil passions. The singing of the eulogies of God, like the tlUJ.an [text-layer?] [azaan], however, was food for the soul" (p. 210, folio 231).
- In the retelling Nanak argues that mosques, temples and the Kaaba "become the repositories of the prayers" and "attain a measure of divinity" but "cannot contain and limit the spirit of God" (pp. 200–201, folios 220–221).
- The Baghdad crowd was "reassured to find that there was nothing in what he said… that could be interpreted as a criticism oflslam [of Islam]" (p. 223, folio not stated).

## Doubts
- **Damage inventory (text_layer):** `[source stamp: SikhBookClub.com watermark]` at the foot of nearly every page (garbled as `lsikhBookCLub.com`, `/jikhBookCL~ m`, `SikhBook(LulL:._com`, etc.) — stamp, not content.
