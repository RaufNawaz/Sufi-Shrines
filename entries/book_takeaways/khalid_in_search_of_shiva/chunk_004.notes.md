## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **mazar-of-bulleh-shah** (the book's "Bulleh Shah"; named as a Sufi exemplar and poet, not as a shrine):
  - "Bulleh Shah, one of the most celebrated Sufis from Punjab, worked as the servant of a dancing girl for twelve years", which the author reads as "an act clearly meant to criticize the divisions of caste in society"; "Bulleh Shah was a Syed, and the dancing girls, like the eunuchs, occupy the lowest rungs of society" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
  - Called "the Punjabi mystic and poet of the early eighteenth century, Bulleh Shah"; the motif of the mystic as a despicable dog before God is found in his poetry: "'Dogs are awake at night, dogs are superior to you [believers].'" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
  - According to Iqbal Qaiser: "Bulleh Shah was a Qadri. The Qadri sect has a history of peaceful coexistence with other religions but that changed when Mumtaz Qadri murdered Salman Taseer." (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).

- **amb-temples-amb-sharif**, **bhagnari-mandir**, **chandragup-baba-chandragup**, **katas-raj-temples**, **shahwala-teja-singh-mandir**, **shiv-mandir-chiti-ghati**, **shree-ratneshwar-mahadev-temple-karachi**, **umarkot-amarkot-shiv-mandir** (the book's "Shiva"; deity and cult, no archive site named — person-level):
  - Citing "Jürgen Wasim, in Journey to God": "Shivaism, a cult established around the worship of Shiva, explains partially the esotericism associated with eunuchs"; "Shiva is known as Ardhanarisvara that is half male and half female, with the right side being male and the left side being female"; "This duality is also reflected in Shiva being seen as the creator and the destroyer"; "The image of Shiva which is the union of linga and yoni is the ultimate symbol of the union of masculinity with femininity" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
  - Wasim compares the dance at Sufi festivals with "the tandava—the violent dance of Shiva"; quoted: "The form and significance of the dervish dance may also be influenced in Indo-Pakistan by Shaivaism. One need only recall the position of Shiva as nataraja (Lord of the Dance)…" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated). "Shiva also happens to be the master of lasya, a gentle lyrical dance" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).

  - Malang androgyny: Wasim quoted, "These ideas apparently go back to Shaivite influence" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
  - Hashish: per Wasim, "for Hindus consumption of hashish, which is linked to the cult of Shiva, enjoys a 'quasi-religious veneration'" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated). Malangs' long locks: "Shiva in his avatar as mahayogi with his long braided hair piled on top of his head like a pyramid"; "the holy river Ganges flowing out of his hair" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
  - Dogs: citing Dalrymple, From the Holy Mountain, Shiva in his terrifying form (ugra) "is accompanied by a pack of dogs"; "In Tantrism, Shiva in the incarnation of Bhairava, is depicted either with the face of a dog or has a dog as his vehicle"; dogs at "the temple of Kalbhairav in Varanasi" are garlanded and given food offerings (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).

## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Baba Mast** ("Khwaja Abdul Aziz Mast, also known as Baba Mast"), shrine "in the outskirts of Chunian": a "Muslim saint" whose festival draws "thousands of eunuchs from all over the country"; per Majeed, eldest of four sons of "Syed Akbar Ali Shah", designated his spiritual descendant; studied at "Dar-ul-Ulum" (Deoband) then "Sir Syed Ahmed Khan's Aligarh University"; "Baba Mast died without any progeny on 5 January 1995"; appointed "Sahibzada Mushtaq Hussain as his spiritual descendant" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Syed Akbar Ali Shah**, father of Baba Mast; the festival "is the urs celebration of Syed Akbar Ali Shah" (per Majeed) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Ghulam Mustafa Shah**, Baba Mast's brother, "a religious puritanical, a fundamentalist" (per Majeed); his son, a nephew, "feels threatened" by Mushtaq Hussain (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Mushtaq Hussain**, "the spiritual descendant of Baba Mast and the current living saint"; "the son of Khwaja Abdul Hayee who was a contemporary of Baba Mast and his senior at Dar-ul-Ulum" (per Majeed) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Peer Jahania**, Chunian: per Iqbal Qaiser, "a Muslim saint during the Mughal era", buried at his "baithak" in a mosque complex facing Chunian's wall; "the story of Chunian begins from this mosque"; converted the untouchables of the city (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Chunian**: "once a major army station of the Mughal Empire"; per Iqbal Qaiser, citing "the Encyclopedia of Sikh Literature [p. 475], Chunian is the plural for Chuni which means pearl"; "Till 1972, Chunian district was part of Lahore" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Changa Manga** forest: per Iqbal Qaiser, Forest Department records; British-era forest; "The forest project officially began in 1866. In 1960, the Pakistan government gave it the status of a national park"; name legends (two dacoits, vs. villages "named Changa and Manga" and the railway station); 1857 fighters "Nazam Lohar, Malangi and Har Naam Singh" hid there (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Peer Abbas** ("Peer Abbas Kutiyanali Sarkar", "Peer Abbas the master of dogs"), shrine at "Pattoki on Multan Road, a few kilometres from Lahore": per Jaffar/Jasim Qazmi, "born in 1914, in India", "a graduate from Allahabad University", moved at Partition "to a village in Jhang", itinerant, settled at Pattoki only "in the last ten years of his life"; died "On 6 September 1968", "only fifty-four"; "never married"; "Malamti school of thought" (author) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Baba Jan Muhammad Mast** ("Baba Jan"), shrine on a mound by "the village of Chak 22", reached from "Ada Ghaziabad"; friend of Peer Abbas; the shopkeeper says "There are two shrines of Jan Muhammad Mast… At one of the shrines, the saint is still alive"; per guardian "Syed Bahadur Ali", Peer Abbas "spent thirteen years here in chila" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Graves of Peer Abbas's dogs** "Mohsin and Qamar", at "Midhiyan wali bhani, the village behind us" (per Syed Bahadur Ali) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Musa Sahi Suhag**, "a fifteenth-century Muslim mystic from Ahmedabad", "withdrew into the community of eunuchs to conceal his spiritual abilities" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Dar-ul-Ulum, Deoband**: "established in 1867 by a religious scholar, Hafiz Syed Abid Hussain at Deoband in the United Provinces of India"; per Majeed, Deoband "became puritanical after Ashraf Ali Thanvi, the one who first translated the Quran into Urdu"; per Kamran, puritanical movement of "the eighteenth century, spearheaded by Shah Walliullah" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Badshahi mosque**: per Majeed, its maulvi "refused to offer the namaz-e-jannazah for Salman Taseer" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- **Dharmarajika / Taxila**: see the Margalla hills bullet above (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Bare mentions: "Baba Aban Shah" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated); "Qitmir" the dog of the cave sleepers (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Baba Mast's urs: "ten-day celebrations of urs"; "organized between the dates of 5 and 13 Kante, which usually fall in the month of the October in the Georgian calendar" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated); this year it overlapped "Eid-ul-Azha" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Mushtaq Hussain stays in a tent "for the ten days during the festival"; guests served "chicken alu as opposed to lentils reserved for the populace"; "chatna" ("the masculine version of chutney") and "kawa" ("strong black tea, heavily sweetened but without any milk") are the shrine's specialities; Baba Mast "would prepare it himself" (per Majeed) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Custodianship: per Majeed, the nephews "look after the shrine and the festival"; Mushtaq Hussain "does not practise dum here nor does he take any share in the collections from the festival. All of that goes to the nephews" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- "There are about 2,000 eunuchs here" (Majeed) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated); eunuch groups "from towns and cities across Pakistan"; guardians in "kurta dhoti and turban and holding long and thick colourful bamboos" protect the eunuchs (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated); Kashi: "I have been coming here for the past eighteen years"; Yasir: "We don't come here for business but pilgrimage"; "Here we are known as Babe ki chidiyaan [the birds of the saint]" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated). "Facing the camps of the eunuchs were camps for prostitutes" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated); "a few eunuchs who I interviewed here acknowledged that if they are approached by a client, they do sell sex" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- At the graves: devotees "kissed the part of the grave where the feet of the occupant would be, while others walked around it"; malangs prepare bhang and smoke hashish around fires (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated); "the authorities allow their use at Sufi shrines because of their link to metaphysical experiences" (author) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Baithak of the saint with a stall selling "pictures of the saint and his nephew"; "pictures of the young nephews of the saint cashing in on the legacy of their uncle" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Saima: "Only recently did the Supreme Court order NADRA to issue us national identity cards"; "The maulvis don't say our namaz-e-jinnazah and don't permit us to be buried in a regular graveyard" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Peer Abbas shrine: dogs wear "wreaths around their necks" to distinguish them from strays; devotees bring "sweets, milk and meat for the dogs"; "about thirty dogs of the saint"; dogs no longer allowed inside because "a few devotees … objected" (per Jaffar Qazmi) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated); the author's inference that the objectors are major donors is his own (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated). Nawaz Dogar has looked after the dogs "About thirty years" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated). Gadi nasheen: Peer Abbas's brother, "an old fragile man in his eighties"; Jaffar Qazmi, "forty-year-old", "a school teacher at the government school in Pattoki", "next in line" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated). "This shrine was recently constructed by the department of Auqaf. Its minaret now is the tallest in the city" (Jasim Qazmi) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated); urs posters on city walls, "a huge affair attended by thousands of people" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Baba Jan's shrine: "a simple single-storey structure, with a green dome on top"; "Ancient waan trees" shade devotees' graves, burials presumed paid (author) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Dogs' graves festival: per Niyamat, "Every year we arrange a festival for them … dhol, kabadai, halwa"; "There is also a majlis" (a boy); held "In summer, at the end of Jhet [local month]"; "The land behind the graves belonged to Peer Abbas and he used to work on it himself"; numbers have reduced (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Baba Mast and his eunuchs, expelled by Ghulam Mustafa, "settled at the canal a few kilometres away"; that year "people abandoned the shrine and celebrated the urs at the canal" until Ghulam Mustafa asked him back (per Majeed) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- The canal as boundary: "anyone who crosses the canal comes under the protection of the saint"; a bicycle thief "turned blind" on crossing it and now lives there permanently (told by a eunuch) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Baba Mast's ducks, "Baba ki batakhein": all from one pair; "Every morning at the time of the prayer, all of them gather at the baithak of the saint … It is a miracle" (per Majeed) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Peer Abbas sheltered "a bitch and her puppies" with his shawl in rain; they followed him; dogs named "Wakil [lawyer], Havaldar [constable], Judge, SP [superintendent of police]" (per Jaffar Qazmi) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Curses on those who harmed his dogs: one "suffered from a terrible disease and died"; another must "leave his country and would never see it again" (per Jaffar Qazmi); during urs week "the dogs do not eat at all" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Nawaz Dogar: appointed in a dream by Peer Abbas "to serve his dogs"; his parents had the same dream (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Peer Abbas "predicted his own death a week before he died" (per Jasim Qazmi) (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author links eunuch dance and malang practice (androgyny, hashish, ash, matted hair, dogs) to "Hindu Shaivism", largely via Jürgen Wasim (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Saint's love for outcasts (Baba Mast, Peer Jahania, Bulleh Shah, Musa Sahi Suhag) read as "rebellion against the normative society" and self-debasement (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Peer Abbas's dog-company as Malamti self-humiliation; a lineage traced "From the cynics … to the holy fools of Christianity, and then to holy men like Peer Abbas" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Puritanical/Deobandi Islam eroding shrine practice (the dogs barred, the Lahore drug ban); Iqbal Qaiser counters that shrine culture "was not the antidote for Islamic militancy" and notes exploitation by "peers" (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
- Circumambulation of Baba Jan's grave compared with the Buddhist circling of the Dharmarajika stupa (p. 11, ch. 5, folio not stated).
