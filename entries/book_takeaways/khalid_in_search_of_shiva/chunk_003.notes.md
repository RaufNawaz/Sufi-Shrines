## Other saints, sites and events (not in the archive)

- **Shrine of Baba Lal Hassan / the sacred log** (location not stated in this chapter; reached by car from Nauman's house; ch. 4 later names "shrines similar to the ones at Kamalia and Apal Muri"): "a newly constructed tomb of the saint, a single-storey building waiting for a paint job" stands "in the middle of an empty field"; the log lies on "vacant land spared from the vegetation", on bricks "arbitrarily put together to differentiate the sacred space", covered with "a green cloth"; "A few burned-out lamps were placed under the trunk"; uncovered parts "blackened due to weathering" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated). The log was earlier referred to by "Syed Faheem Abbas"; Altaf called it "nothing special" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
  - Guardian: "The thirty-seven-year-old Imaad Hussain Shah, the current guardian of the shrine, who drew his lineage from that of the saint"; "I am from the ninth generation of the saint" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
  - According to Imaad, "Baba Lal Hassan [the occupant of the grave] came to this place from a village in Tehsil Shourkot, Jang [about seventy-five kilometres from here]" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
  - Worshippers seen: an old man "about seventy years old and wearing a kurta and dhoti" bowed touching the log with his forehead, then prayed standing; a group of women removed their shoes before stepping on the bricks, and "the woman with the burqa bent and kissed the trunk" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated). Old men played cards nearby; child musicians performed for small change (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- **Banyan tree at Apal Muri**: "located in the outskirts of a small village called Apal Muri near my ancestral village of Jalap, district Sargodha", "next to Takht Hazara"; brick platform in the middle; "Numerous graves covered with green cloths were scattered around it, one of which was the grave of the saint" (saint not named); cloth "mostly green" tied on branches "as a supplication"; visitors' names "Engraved with a knife" and mobile numbers; tourists from neighbouring areas "ritualistically offered prayers to the graves" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
  - According to Munawar Begum, who lives next to the tree: the other graves are "all devotees of the saint"; her family refuses to cut branches overhanging her courtyard (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
  - According to a friend of the author's father from Jalap: "This tree is now dying"; locals "have started cutting the branches"; "some representatives from the Geological Department" measured it "over an area of four acres"; "only about three acres are left" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).

- **Tableeghi Jamaat centre, Raiwind**: "the heart of Islamic orthodoxy in Pakistan"; headquarters of the Tableeghi Jamaat in Pakistan; "spread over several acres"; main hall "divided into three floors with a basement where construction work was underway"; separate female section; madrassah behind the halls divided into local and international sections; students "Chinese, Southeast Asians, Afghanis, Central Asians, Africans and even Europeans"; informants Ismael (Australian) and Abraham (Macedonian, "about twenty-five"); Uzbek students "had come via Afghanistan" (p. 10, ch. 4, folio not stated).

- Offerings at tree shrines: green cloth on the log; lamps; cloth tied on banyan branches as supplication; prayers at graves (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).

Nightly zikr in pairs, one speaking on a religious matter, then the other (Mussadiq) (p. 10, ch. 4, folio not stated). ## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- According to Imaad: Baba Lal Hassan's stepbrothers withheld his share; "the saint cursed the land and cast a spell over the animals" until they returned it; he gave it to his stepsisters (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- According to Imaad: women refused the saint water under a kikkar; "“Take your tree with you,” the girls mocked him. “So be it,” he replied"; the tree followed him and "has been here ever since"; "This happened about 300 years ago"; "It hasn’t even weathered with time" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- According to Munawar Begum: "This tree was laid by the saint who is now buried under it. He had a long life, several hundreds of years" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- According to Munawar Begum: "Djinns live in the tree. They are all pious, Muslims. Hindu djinns are evil"; cutting the tree would anger them; they are heard at night "as sheep, sometimes also as humans" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- The pseudo-Alexander talking trees near Jhelum (under the Jhelum bullet) (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author argues the Baba Lal Hassan shrine "must have been an ancient shrine, dedicated to the acacia tree which later became associated with the saint"; "The story must have been created by his followers" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- Evidence offered: Indus seals depicting sacred trees, peepal and acacia "most frequently depicted", swastika associated with acacia; James's *Tree of Life*; Sleeman on Dashara tree worship; Khadiravani Tara, "the Tara of the acacia forest"; Mirza-Sahiban; pre-Islamic Arabian acacia worship and Palgrave's 1862-63 observation of Muslims dancing round an acacia for rain (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- Djinns "are yakshas, ancient Hindu tree spirits"; banyans are "‘bodh’ in Punjabi, a name that they borrow from Buddhism" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- On Apal Muri: "Like other shrines this one also would have converted after Partition. The grave could have been a samadhi or an idol"; "The tree became a Muslim banyan tree" (p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).
- Tableeghi Jamaat set against folk Islam; the author places its origin in reaction to the Arya Samaj Shuddhi movement (p. 10, ch. 4, folio not stated); history recast after "jahalliya" (p. 10, ch. 4, folio not stated).
