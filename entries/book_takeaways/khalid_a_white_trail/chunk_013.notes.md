## Province-level material

- PROVINCE sindh (book's word: "Sindh"; refers to: the book's present, the author's fieldwork period — no year stated):
  - "The majority of the Hindu community is based in Sindh and Southern Punjab, where they have a proportionate representation from the lower and the upper castes"; there they "are a part of the social fabric of the area" (p. 233, folio not stated).
  - The author could not "delve into much detail about the experiences of the community from these regions" (p. 233, folio not stated).

- PROVINCE punjab (book's words: "Southern Punjab", "Central Punjab", "Punjab"; refers to: the book's present, the author's fieldwork period — no year stated):
  - The majority of the Hindu community is based in "Sindh and Southern Punjab" with lower and upper castes represented (p. 233, folio not stated).
  - Hindus experience "ghettoization" in "Central Punjab where I conducted my research"; "My perception is that the conditions under which members of the Hindu community live in Central Punjab are the harshest compared to the other regions" (p. 233, folio not stated).
  - "Almost all the interviews I conducted were of Hindus belonging to the lower castes because there are hardly any high-caste Hindus in this region"; his observations "from Central Punjab" cannot be "construed as being reflective of Hindu communities in areas where there is a considerable population" (pp. 233–234, folio not stated).
  - "The majority of the Christian minority is based in Punjab", so the author's Christian material "can be generalized to be reflective of the larger Christian community in the country" (p. 234, folio not stated).
  - Nankana's Sikh interviewees "remember this internal migration from KPK to Punjab", which gives "a good picture of the different socio-economic conditions in both of these provinces" (p. 235, folio not stated).

- PROVINCE khyber pakhtunkhwa (book's words: "Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa (KPK)", "Khyber Pakthunkhwa"; refers to: the book's present and the Sikhs' recent migration — no year stated):
  - The Nankana Sikhs are "recent migrants from Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa (KPK) where there is a considerable Sikh population"; their migration "from KPK to Punjab" shows "the different socio-economic conditions in both of these provinces" (p. 235, folio not stated).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author frames the book as "about the experience of being a minority in Pakistan", "fraught with danger"; gaining minorities' trust "took some doing" (p. 233, folio not stated).
- Limitations he states: Hindu interviews all from Lahore; lower-caste only; "My accounts are merely a few snapshots" (pp. 233–234, folio not stated).
- "one of the basic purposes of this book is to highlight that the experiences of the different religious minorities in Pakistan which cannot be generalized into one" (sentence as printed) (p. 234, folio not stated).
- His Christian interview pool, "due to its different socio-economic conditions was much more diverse than the Hindu group" (p. 234, folio not stated).
