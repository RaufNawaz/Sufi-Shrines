## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **prahladpuri-temple** (the book's "temple in the memory of Prahlad" / "temple of Prahlad Bhagat" / "the historical Prahlad Temple", Multan):
  - The contents list the opening Hindu chapter as "Holi at Multan" (p. 5, folio not stated).
  - After the Prahlad legend (see Legends), "His devotees built a temple in the memory of Prahlad at the spot where he supposedly killed his father – on a mound outside the city, which later became a popular spiritual site with mystics and saints" (p. 20, folio not stated).
  - "The temple stands even today, a worn down building with several cracks in its walls. It has remained abandoned ever since partition, occasionally becoming victim to the passion of fanatic mobs like the one in 1992" (p. 20, folio not stated).
  - Because the mound was linked with the temple, "the area came to be considered holy", which drew Muslim mystics, among them Bahauddin Zakariya, "whose shrine came to be situated next to the temple" (p. 20, folio not stated).
  - The temple "now lies in obscurity under the shadow of this massive shrine"; "even though in shambles now", it is called "an anomaly next to the shrine, an unacceptable act of treason in 'Muslim' Pakistan" (p. 20, folio not stated).
  - According to Parvati Devi, the temple "comes under the jurisdiction of the Awqaf department (a government institution that looks after all the historical Muslim shrines and mosques of the country)"; she says "It is our demand that the temple be restored and given to the Hindus of the city who are its real owners" (p. 24, folio not stated).
  - Devi recalls that "in 2006 and 2008" the shrine authorities of Bahauddin Zakariya "extended the shrine into the premises of the historical Prahlad Temple by constructing a place for ablutions first and then a Langar Hall" (p. 24, folio not stated).
  - "25 year old Usman Chaudary, President of the RAS (Organisation for Religious and Sexual Minorities) society filed a petition in the High Court" against this; "the Court passed a stay order against any further construction on the premises of the temple"; the RAS Society "has petitioned that the temple be handed back to the Hindu community in the city" (p. 24, folio not stated).

## Practices, institutions, economy

- Holika burning at Multan (about midnight on the eve of Holi, in March): "primarily an all-women's event"; "Only married women take part in this ceremony" (per Devi); "about thirty people"; women circle the pyre of hay and sticks singing devotional songs, pour oil from steel glasses and wind a thread around it; "After seven rounds" a man lights it; the hay leaves "bright pink ash" (pp. 18–19, folio not stated).
- Prasad: "Halwa and berries are offered as prasad to the devotees"; Devi marks foreheads with vermillion — "This will protect them from evil" (pp. 17–19, folio not stated).
- Graffiti in Urdu reading "'Diwali Mubarak'" in the courtyard (p. 18, folio not stated).
- Holi day: dhol-wala on a roof; bhang "mixed in almond-milk" would normally be drunk but boys "resort to drinking alcohol"; non-Muslims may buy alcohol on a government "permit card" at "a lower price", and some "sell it to their Muslim clients at exorbitant prices" (pp. 23, folio not stated).
- Gendered restraint: most women watch; "This is Holi with a tinge of Muslim piety" (p. 25, folio not stated).
- Matka-breaking: a matka tied to a wire between two buildings; a human pyramid of "ten boys" at the base, four lighter boys above, with Durga Mata chants (p. 26, folio not stated; chunk ends mid-scene).
- Custodianship: the Prahlad temple is under the "Awqaf department" (per Devi); High Court stay order on RAS Society petition (p. 24, folio not stated).
- Demographics as the book states: Muslims "about 97%"; of the 3% non-Muslims "a large chunk belongs to the Ahmadiyya community"; "In 1947 ... the population of the non-Muslims was about 30% (without including the Ahmadiyya community)", most of whom "have migrated or converted to Islam" (p. 12, folio not stated).
- Method: the CAP Minority Project "officially began in September 2010"; "over two hundred oral history interviews" in two years; "about fifty narratives" of long structured interviews; short interviews at festivals (pp. 10–11, folio not stated).

## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- "According to folk legend", Holika was the sister of Hiranyakashipu, ruler of Multan, a devotee of Lord Vishnu who grew arrogant and forbade Vishnu's worship; Vishnu "took birth in his house in the shape of his son – Prahlad Bhagat"; Holika's magical fire-proof shawl blew off her onto Prahlad in the fire, so "Holika was burnt while Prahlad was saved"; Prahlad later killed his father (pp. 19–20, folio not stated).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- "Pakistani historiography discards Hindu and Sikh narratives from its discourse and where ever they are mentioned, they are always mentioned negatively" (p. 9, folio not stated).
- The book seeks to go "beyond the narrowly defined agenda of religious persecution"; it is "not a list of tortures", and records youth reviving traditions and "syncretism, where Muslims take part in Hindu and Sikh festivals" (p. 13, folio not stated).
- The title comes from "the white part of the Pakistani flag, which is said to represent the minorities"; the book "aims to freeze the religious minorities of these areas, in this time warp" (pp. 11–12, folio not stated).
- On the Prahlad temple and Zakariya shrine: the history of syncretism "is not unconsciously ignored but deliberately eliminated to ensure the conformity of history to the dominant nationalistic discourse" (p. 20, folio not stated).
- Multan's Hindus are "strangers in their own home"; "they are the true descendants of this land" (p. 21, folio not stated).
- Source caveats stated by the author: names of minority members are changed; "in some instances I have taken the liberty to stray from strictly speaking, non-fiction" (p. 15, folio not stated); some off-record interviews "have been presented in that framework [of the festival] for the purpose of understanding" (p. 12, folio not stated).
