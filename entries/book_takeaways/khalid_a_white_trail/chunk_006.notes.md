## Practices, institutions, economy

- Maryabad langar: cauldrons of haleem cooked "since last night" (p. 98, folio not stated); Sabar Elahi: "I have already given away thirty-five cauldrons of food this year in the name of Mother Mary, which has cost me somewhere around one hundred thousand rupees"; he "has been coming here for the past thirty years"; nihari on another cauldron; "In the morning we gave halwa puri. Tomorrow we will make bhong"; "My work has prospered, since I have started giving free food here" (p. 99, folio not stated). Nasreen volunteers cutting "for the service of the community" (p. 99, folio not stated).
- Rosary: "During the festival of Mother Mary, therefore, a lot of devotees spend extra time on their rosaries. This tradition is not popular with the Protestants." (p. 98, folio not stated).
- Sacred Heart security: two guards at the one open gate stop "those who don't look Christian enough", checking names on identity cards; "Unknown Muslims are not allowed inside the Church as a security precaution" (p. 101, folio not stated); on Good Friday guards check "the National Identity Cards (NIC) of all the visitors. Once again, no Muslim is allowed" (p. 119, folio not stated).
- Two services: big churches have an English and an Urdu service, "a legacy from the colonial era"; the English Palm Sunday service is half empty, sung "without music" (p. 105, folio not stated); the Bishop now comes only "for the service in Urdu when thousands of devotees wil [text-layer?] [will] come" versus "the hundred odd people at the moment" (p. 111, folio not stated); the Urdu service has a band, choirs, bongo drums and piano, chorus "Jai Yesu (Long live Jesus)"; the hall overflows (p. 115, folio not stated).
- Collection: a green basket passed along the benches; "ten and twenty rupee notes" and "A few red hundred rupee notes" (p. 113, folio not stated).
- Communion: the priest raises "a small cracker" and wine; nuns distribute "a cracker dipped in wine" to two lines of devotees, "This act marks the end of the service" (p. 113, folio not stated).
- Palm Sunday rally in front of the Bishop's House: palm branches from a table; slogans "Bolo Khudawan Yesu Masih ki (Say, To the Lord Jesus Christ)" — "Jai (Glory)"; "Badshaon ke Badshah ki (The King of Kings)"; "David ke bête ki (The son of David)" (p. 114, folio not stated). According to Aftab, after "the rise of terrorism, the Government of Pakistan forbade us from taking out that rally"; "Now all that has left of that procession is this short walk within the compounds of the Church" (p. 115, folio not stated).
- Kneeling to the altar is "a unique practice confined to the Catholic sect" (p. 112, folio not stated); a devotee touches the Bishop's back and presses the hand to his face "seeking the blessing of the Bishop through physical contact" (p. 114, folio not stated).
- Missionary education: nationalized "during the socialist stint of Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto in the seventies", including FC College; private schools "sprung up during the eighties"; some "de-nationalized" "during the era of General Pervez Musharraf"; FC College "is now run by Christian missionaries" again (p. 102, folio not stated; p. 103, folio not stated). According to Peeda James: "we have only gotten forty percent of our schools and colleges back" (p. 103, folio not stated).
- According to Sebastian Francis, "till the sixties" the Catholic Church used Latin; after the change "There is now a Bible in Urdu"; "Till that point all the clergy positions here were filled by Europeans"; "Now, four decades later, all the clergy positions are with locals" (p. 116, folio not stated).
- Church property: according to Nayamat, "There is vast property associated with the Church, which was allegedly sold by someone" (p. 120, folio not stated).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author: the nationalization of missionary schools meant the loss "of that multi-religious tolerant society"; the elite schools that followed are "a product of the Islamization that Pakistani society went through during the eighties under General Zia-ul-Haq", and this isolation "condones acts of violence against religious minorities" (p. 103, folio not stated).
- The author: the grand colonial churches were "a symbol of power"; in the post-colonial country they "have become mere architectural remnants of what were once powerful political institutions" (p. 105, folio not stated).
- The author: the Banyan tree "has acquired spiritual significance in all the major religions of South Asia, including Sufi Islam, Hinduism and Buddhism" (p. 101, folio not stated; p. 102, folio not stated).
- The author: "This semi-divinity of the Catholic clergy was the major reason why a group of clergymen protested against Catholicism and founded the Protestant Church" (p. 114, folio not stated).
- The author reads the Good Friday sermon as "not ... a religious speech but a poignant political one": the "blasphemer" Christ "could be seen as a symbol for all those individuals who have suffered as blasphemy accused" (p. 119, folio not stated).
- The author on John Joseph: "His sacrifice, like that of Christ, was meant to be the reason for the salvation of millions of Christians living in Pakistan. However, nothing changed." (p. 118, folio not stated; p. 119, folio not stated).
