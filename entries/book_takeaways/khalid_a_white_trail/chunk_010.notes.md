## Legends and miracles (karamat)

- Nanak's twenty rupees spent feeding Jogis at Mandi Chuharkhana; he hid under the Tamboo tree ("According to the legend") (p. 182, folio not stated).
- According to Kartar, a red flower in the Tamboo compound "grows nowhere else in the world"; scientists' attempts to plant it elsewhere "all have failed. This is a miracle of the Guru." (p. 182, folio not stated; p. 183, folio not stated).
- A cobra shaded the sleeping boy Nanak with its hood (depicted in a painting; commemorated at Mal Ji Sahib) (p. 191, folio not stated).
- Guru Arjan "was lifted alive into the sky by God" ("Legend has it"); his followers "realized that the Guru had performed a miracle" (p. 195, folio not stated).

## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- The author reads the sky at Tamboo Sahib as symbolic: dark blue is the "'monolithic' Muslim culture", the diminishing orange "the multi-religious pluralistic society that Pakistan was at her onset" (p. 181, folio not stated).
- The author says Kartar's Muslim friends avoid joined hands in prayer as "too close to 'Hindu' culture, a culture they have learned to hate from their birth" (p. 181, folio not stated).
- The author: not sharing utensils with Sikhs is "adopted from the Hindu concept of un-touchability"; in post-partition Pakistan it was "extended to high-caste Hindus as well as Christians", then "to all non-Muslims", practised by "an over-whelming majority ... ignorant of the practice's cultural origin" (p. 186, folio not stated; p. 187, folio not stated).
- The author: after the creation of Pakistan the country "began to shun all cultural festivals" linked to its Hindu and Sikh past, as Muslims shaped a religious identity "inspired by the puritanical version of Islam imported from Arabia" (p. 193, folio not stated).
