## Arguments and interpretations (academic books)

- **The bibliography is built on one Pakistani catalogue and one Lahore collection, and says so.**
  Its manuscript base is `A Comprehensive Catalogue of Persian Manuscripts in Pakistan, ed. Ahmad
  Monzavi`, published by `the Iran Pakistan Institute of Persian Studies in Islamabad`, plus `the
  catalog of the Sherani Collection at the University of the Punjab in Lahore` (p. 233, folio not
  stated). **For this project's queue that is the finding: the acquisition path for Chishti
  manuscripts in Pakistan runs through Monzavi and Sherani, and the authors treat both as primary.**
- **The list is a sample and the authors concede the larger holdings lie elsewhere** — `published
  catalogs of libraries in Hyderabad, Calcutta, London, etc.` hold `considerably more` (p. 233,
  folio not stated).
- **A genre distinction asserted by the table of contents rather than argued** — `7· "RETROSPECTIVE"
  MALFUZAT` against `8. "ORIGINAL" MALFUZAT` (pp. 238–239, folios 228–229). All the earliest
  Chishti malfuzat — `Anis al-arwah`, `Ganj al-asrar`, `Asrar al-awliya`, `Dalil al-'arifin`,
  `Fawa'id al-salikin`, `Rahat al-qulub` — fall on the retrospective side; `Fawa'id al-fu'ad` opens
  the original side. **The reason is not given in this chunk: (not stated).**
- **A source-critical correction made inside an entry** — of the Burhanpuri tazkira: `Although
  described as a malfuzat, it is a biography of a descendant of Ganj-i Shakzar` (p. 237, folio 227).
- **A correction of an attribution, made against Munzawi** — `Rida al-Din Husayn Khan. Tazhkira-i
  Nizam al-Din Awrangabadi. Karachi ATU, p. 202, 3 QF I65. Corrected to Mir Diya' al-Din Husayn, Sar
  guzhasht nama-i Nizam al-Din Awrangabadi, by Munzawi, XI, 98I, no. 225.` (p. 237, folio 227). And
  a doubt about an identification: `This may be the similarly titled work on Bijapur by Ibrahim
  Zubayri, as Munzawi supposes.` (p. 235, folio 225).
- **An anonymous work whose ascription the book records as contested** — `Anon. Tadzkirat al-asfiya:
  Biographies of 31 Chishtis written in 951/1544. Munzawi, XI, 849, no. 56 (1 MS), citing Storey, I,
  1265, where the work is ascribed to Najaf'Ali and Safdar 'Ali in 1270/1853-4 with unspecified
  subject (Asafiyya, III, 380).` (p. 234, folio 224) — a three-century discrepancy in ascription,
  left standing.
- **A genealogical controversy about the Chishti chain, recorded as a refutation.**
  `Fakhr al-Din Dihlawi (d. 1199/1725). Fakhr al-Hasan. Monzavi, III, no. 3198 (2 MSS). A refutation
  of Shah Wali Allah's claim that the Chishti lineage from Hasan al-Basri to 'Ali is spurious. Has
  been translated into Arabic by Muhammad 'Ali Khayrabadi as al-Qawl al-mustahsan fi sharh Fakhr
  al-Hasan.` (p. 236, folio 226).
- **Samaʿ as a live defended practice into the eighteenth century** — `Sayf al-maslul 'ala' man
  a'rada 'an sama' al-rasul ... A defense of music by a disciple of Shah B'hika Chishti Lahuri`
