# waris_shah_hir_ranjha — chunk 007 — p. 57

One page, the last of the book: the **close of Waris Shah's own epilogue** in his own voice
(`[col 1 of 2]` through the first two lines of `[col 2 of 2]`), followed by the edition's closing
**"Notes" block, notes 1–8** (`[col 2 of 2]`). The epilogue's opening is in the preceding chunks;
this page begins mid-sentence ("will get Pan-disc [text-layer?] [Paradise?] and martyrs claim their
houris"). Despite its length this is **the most archive-relevant page in the book**: it is the only
place the poet states his residence and his teacher, and notes 5 and 7 are the edition's
identifications of two saints.

**Folio, as the page actually prints it.** The running header reads
"Waris Shah: The Adventures of Hir and Ranjha 56" (p. 57), so the printed folio is **56** and
`folio = PDF − 1` holds on this page. Everything below is cited **(p. 57, folio 56)**.

**Who wrote the notes.** Note 8 is **signed by the translator**: "— C. F. Usborne" (p. 57, folio
56); that one note is his. Notes 3 and 4 close with a bare attribution "(Usborne.)" and note 2 says
"Usborne's note says:" — third-person framing. Notes 1, 5, 6 and 7 carry no attribution at all and
are **the annotator's** (a later, unnamed hand, per the earlier chunks). The distinction matters
because notes 5 and 7 — the two identifications — are **unattributed, i.e. the annotator's, not
Usborne's**. See Doubts.

## Shrines and figures in the archive

- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`** (the book's form throughout this page is **"Waris Shah"**):
  **his residence and his teacher, in his own voice — the single most citable sentence in the
  book for this row.** "Waris Shah lives at Jandiala and is the pupil of the Saint of Kasur5"
  (p. 57, folio 56). The page gives the place as **"Jandiala"** alone; it does **not** print
  "Jandiala Sher Khan", and it attaches **no tomb, grave, shrine, ʿurs or custodian** to Jandiala —
  only the poet's residence, in the present tense. A district is (not stated). See Doubts.
- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`**: **the teacher is named only by title in the verse**, "the Saint of
  Kasur" (p. 57, folio 56); the name comes from the annotator's note 5 and is damaged — see the
  "Other saints" entry and Doubts. **No silsila, order or chain of any kind is named anywhere on
  this page** — (not stated). The archive row's "Chishti" is **not** supported here.
- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`**: **he presented the finished poem to his master for approval.**
  "Having finished the story I presented it to my master for his acceptance." (p. 57, folio 56).
  The page does **not** say whether "my master" is the Saint of Kasur or another person — (not
  stated), and note 6 shows the annotator was alert to exactly this kind of referent ambiguity in
  the next sentence.
- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`**: **the claim of spiritual assistance from Shakarganj.** "With the
  aid of Shakarganj7 I have conferred this benefit on the world." (p. 57, folio 56). This is the
  only relation the page states between the poet and any named saint other than his teacher.
- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`**: **his own account of what the poem is and how to read it.** "I
  have written a poem of much pith even as a string of royal pearls. I have written it at length
  and embellished it with various beautiful things. I have written it as a parable. It is as
  beautiful as a necklace of rubies." (p. 57, folio 56). And the reading key: "The story of true
  lovers is like the scent of a rose in a garden. He who reads it with love in his heart will be
  able to separate the true from the false." (p. 57, folio 56).
- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`**: **the poet's own equation of Hir's love with the love of God —
  stated flatly, in his own voice, not by an editor.** "Waris Shah is anxious to see God's face
  even as Hir longed for her lover. I make my request before the Holy Court of Cod
  [text-layer?] [God], who is the Lord of Mercy." (p. 57, folio 56). This is the mystical reading
  of the romance asserted by the author himself on the book's last page.
- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`**: **his stated hope of fame, and his stated poverty of merit.**
  "Waris Shah, your name will be famous, if God be kind." and "Waris Shah has shed the light of his
  genius over all the faithful in the world." (p. 57, folio 56), set against "Waris Shah, I have no
  capital of good works. 0 Cod! [text-layer?] [O God!] Grant me Thy presence." and "0 Almighty
  [text-layer?] [O Almighty] God, overlook the fault of poor Waris Shah." (p. 57, folio 56).
- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`**: **the takhallus in use.** He names himself **six times** on this
  one page, always in the third person or as self-address: "Waris Shah, I have no capital of good
  works"; "Waris Shah lives at Jandiala"; "Waris Shah, your name will be famous"; "Waris Shah has
  shed the light of his genius"; "poor Waris Shah"; "Waris Shah is anxious to see God's face"
  (p. 57, folio 56).
- **`mausoleum-of-waris-shah`**: **a composition date, but not from the poet.** The annotator's
  note 2 states, inside a parenthesis correcting itself: "It was the year 120 of the Bikramajit era.
  (These two dates do not exactly correspond, but the poem was written in AD. 1766.)" (p. 57, folio
  56) — "AD." punctuated as printed. **Waris Shah's own verse on this page gives no date at all**;
  see Doubts, and note that the gloss belongs to a passage on an earlier page, not to this one.
- **`shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar`** (the book's form on this page is **"Shakarganj"** only —
  never "Fariduddin", never "Baba Farid", never "Ganjshakar" as a separate word): **identified by
  the annotator with a place.** Note 7, unattributed: "Shakarganj is the saint of Pakpattan. See
  notes on Introduction." (p. 57, folio 56). Name and place together are what carry the mapping.
  Invoked in the verse as the source of aid for the poem: "With the aid of Shakarganj7 I have
  conferred this benefit on the world." (p. 57, folio 56). **No tomb, shrine building, ʿurs,
  custodian, date or order is attached to him or to Pakpattan** — all (not stated). See Doubts.

## Practices, institutions, economy

- **Discipleship and authorization: the finished poem submitted to the master.** "Having finished
  the story I presented it to my master for his acceptance." (p. 57, folio 56). Together with "is
  the pupil of the Saint of Kasur" (p. 57, folio 56), this is the page's whole institutional
  content: a pupil–teacher tie and a presentation of the work for approval. **No khanqah, langar,
  offering, endowment, land, revenue or custodian appears anywhere on the page** — all (not
  stated).
- **Copying and reciting the poem as acts that earn blessing — the manuscript economy stated as a
  prayer.** "May he who reads or copies my poor efforts derive pleasure." and "May God pardon him
  who copies these words and give his bounty to those who recite it. May its readers enjoy the
  book." (p. 57, folio 56). Copyists, reciters and readers are named as three distinct classes.
- **The poem was composed on request.** "By the Grace of God I have fulfilled the request of my
  dear friends." (p. 57, folio 56). The friends are unnamed and no patron, payment or occasion is
  given — (not stated).
- **Merit accounted in commercial terms.** "Waris Shah, I have no capital of good works." (p. 57,
  folio 56) — the page's one economic metaphor.
- **The eschatology the epilogue assumes**, named in passing: Paradise and the martyrs' houris,
  "will get Pan-disc [text-layer?] [Paradise?] and martyrs claim their houris" (p. 57, folio 56);
  the Prophet's intercession; and the Day of Judgement, "give all the faithful faith, conscience
  and a sight of Thy presence in the Day of Judgement.5" (p. 57, folio 56).
- **The edition's own editorial policy, in Usborne's signed words — a reliability statement the
  consolidator must carry.** Note 8: "This interesting but somewhat rambling epilogue is translated
  at full length without any attempt at excision or condensation. The rest of the poem has been
  considerably condensed but noting [text-layer?] [nothing] important has been omitted. —
  C. F. Usborne" (p. 57, folio 56). **So this page is the only part of the book Usborne states is
  translated in full**; the narrative is an abridgement by his own admission.
- **A glossing practice worth recording: Usborne twice gives the literal Panjabi idiom behind his
  English.** "Petty themes — literally, ground in a hand mill. (Usborne.)" (note 3) and "Grand
  poem — literally, ground my grain in a bullock-driven mill. (Usborne.)" (note 4) (p. 57, folio
  56). Both are milling metaphors for kinds of poetry; **the Panjabi words themselves are not
  printed** — (not stated).

## Arguments and interpretations

- **The annotator treats the poem as usable historical evidence, and says so.** "Waris Shah's
  description of the conditions of his day is probably not exaggerated." (note 1, p. 57, folio 56)
  — a judgement on the poem's reliability about "a period of great confusion and disturbance"
  during "the decline of the Mughal Empire in India".
- **The annotator (or Usborne) flags a dating inconsistency rather than resolving it.** "(These two
  dates do not exactly correspond, but the poem was written in AD. 1766.)" (note 2, p. 57, folio
  56). The two dates in question are "the year 120 of the Bikramajit era" and AD 1766; the note
  asserts 1766 without showing its working.
- **The annotator intervenes to fix a theological referent.** "This refers to God and not to Waris
  Shah's teacher. It is a verse from the Quran." (note 6, p. 57, folio 56), glossing "He elevates
  whom lie [text-layer?] [he] will and throws down whom He will." **The annotator is guarding
  against reading the elevation as the teacher's doing** — relevant to anyone citing this page for
  the pupil–teacher relation.
- **The poet's own defence of the work, and its allegorical claim.** "I have written it as a
  parable." (p. 57, folio 56) and "He who reads it with love in his heart will be able to separate
  the true from the false." (p. 57, folio 56) — the author's stated warrant for a mystical reading,
  with "Waris Shah is anxious to see God's face even as Hir longed for her lover." (p. 57, folio
  56) as its explicit figure.
- **The poet's own disclaimer of error.** "If I have let fall a word in ignorance may God forgive
  me." (p. 57, folio 56).
- **A judgement on the world in two sentences.** "The world is outwardly fair but inwardly it is
  bad even as the sound of a drum is beautiful from afar." (p. 57, folio 56).
- **Usborne's judgement on the epilogue itself**: "interesting but somewhat rambling" (note 8,
  p. 57, folio 56) — his only aesthetic verdict on this page, and his reason for translating it
  whole.

## Doubts

- **Folio agrees with the supplied rule — no disagreement to report.** The header prints "56" on
  PDF 57 (p. 57, folio 56), exactly as `folio = PDF − 1` predicts. Recorded because the rule failed
  elsewhere in this queue.
- **The teacher's name is the most important damaged string on the page and I have not repaired
  it.** Printed: **"Mamdum Mafia Ghulamn Murtaza of Kasur"** (note 5, p. 57, folio 56). My reading
  of each damaged word, offered as **typographic hypotheses only and asserted of nothing**:
  `Mamdum` — an honorific in which a letter-group appears to have collapsed (an `m` where a
  different cluster stood); `Mafia` — the least recoverable of the three, almost certainly not an
  English word and probably a second honorific or a name element, and **I will not guess it**;
  `Ghulamn` — a trailing `n` that does not belong, reading `Ghulam`, which then gives the
  name-pair `Ghulam Murtaza`. **Only `Ghulamn` → `Ghulam` is safe.** Note 5 itself points to the
  fuller identification — "See Introduction, note 4." — which is **outside this chunk**; a human
  should read that note and check this page's image before any form of this name enters an entry.
- **Kasur is not a bare toponym, but it still takes no id, and the reason is not question (a).** The
  page attaches a saint to Kasur — the poet's teacher — so the interim convention for bare toponyms
  is not what governs it. It takes no id because **the saint attached is not Bulleh Shah and is not
  any archive row's principal figure**. The archive row at Kasur is `mazar-of-bulleh-shah` (Bulleh
  Shah / Abdullah Shah Qadri, d. 1757, Qadiri), and **Bulleh Shah is not named anywhere on this
  page** — mapping Kasur to that row would put a fact about one saint's seat into another saint's
  entry. **If a ruling ever attaches toponyms to rows by location alone, `mazar-of-bulleh-shah` is
  the row Kasur would take, and it should be resisted on this page for the reason just given.**
- **Jandiala: I went with the person, not the place, and here is why.** The page says "Waris Shah
  lives at Jandiala" (p. 57, folio 56) — grammatically a fact about the poet, who is
  `mausoleum-of-waris-shah`'s principal figure, so the fact rides the person under the 18 September
  ruling and the toponym needs no id of its own. It matters that **nothing funerary is attached**:
  no tomb, no grave, no shrine, no ʿurs, no custodian — the page has him *living* there. The
  archive row's location is "Jandiala Sher Khan"; **the page prints only "Jandiala"**, and a human
  should confirm they are the same place before the row's location is cited to this book.
- **What this page does NOT support for `mausoleum-of-waris-shah`.** The row carries d. 1798 and
  silsila Chishti. **Neither is on this page**: no death year, no order, no chain, no birth date,
  no district — all (not stated). The only date on the page is the annotator's "AD. 1766" for the
  poem's composition (note 2), and the only relationships are to the Saint of Kasur (teacher) and
  Shakarganj (aid).
- **`shrine-of-fariduddin-ganjshakar` rests on one annotator's sentence plus one bare name in the
  verse.** The evidence is "Shakarganj is the saint of Pakpattan." (note 7) and "With the aid of
  Shakarganj7" (verse) (p. 57, folio 56). The row's own "Baba Fariduddin Mas'ud Ganjshakar",
  d. 1266 and Chishti are **none of them stated here**, and the page never calls Pakpattan a shrine
  or gives a tomb. Note 7 also defers — "See notes on Introduction." — to a note **outside this
  chunk**, which a human should read before the consolidator relies on this identification. The
  mapping is the annotator's identification, not the poet's.
- **Two notes on this page are numbered 5, and the second one looks wrong.** The verse marker on the
  teacher sentence — "the Saint of Kasur5" — matches note 5 correctly. But the **last words of the
  epilogue** also carry a 5: "in the Day of Judgement.5" (p. 57, folio 56), while the note that
  actually discusses the epilogue as a whole is **note 8** ("This interesting but somewhat rambling
  epilogue…"). **I read the final marker as a damaged 8 but have not changed it**; a human should
  check the page image. Nothing above depends on it.
- **The fused-digit damage class is present on this page**: `Kasur5`, `will.6`, `Shakarganj7`,
  `Judgement.5` (p. 57, folio 56). **A digit fused to a word here is a note number, not part of a
  name, a date or a figure** — in particular "Shakarganj7" is not a numbered form of the name.
- **Inventory of text-layer damage, printed form first, my reading after it.** The PDF has no page
  images and renders as crisp digital type, so **these errors are in the published file itself, not
  introduced by our pipeline**. Four classes, eleven flagged positions, all flagged inline as well:
  - **(i) Vocative "O" printed as the digit zero — six occurrences**, the largest class:
    `0 God` [O God] (twice in col 1, once more near the foot of col 1), `0 Cod!` [O God!],
    `0 Lord` [O Lord], `0 Almighty` [O Almighty] (p. 57, folio 56).
  - **(ii) "God" printed as "Cod" — two occurrences**: `0 Cod!` [O God!] and "the Holy Court of
    `Cod`" [God] (p. 57, folio 56). Note that one position carries **both** class (i) and class
    (ii) damage.
  - **(iii) `lie` for "he" — one occurrence**: "He elevates whom `lie` [he] will" (p. 57, folio
    56). The capitalized "He" later in the same sentence extracts correctly, which is what
    identifies it.
  - **(iv) `noting` for "nothing" — one occurrence**, and it sits in Usborne's signed statement
    about the edition: "`noting` [nothing] important has been omitted" (note 8, p. 57, folio 56).
    **The flag must survive into any entry that cites the condensation claim**, since the sentence

  - Plus `Mamdum Mafia Ghulamn Murtaza` (note 5) and `Pan-disc` — see the separate items.
- **Two further suspected damage instances that the brief did not list and that I have not treated
  as settled.** (1) `Pan-disc` in the page's first, mid-sentence line: "will get `Pan-disc` and
  martyrs claim their houris" (p. 57, folio 56). I read it as a broken **"Paradise"** (probably a
  line-break hyphenation, "Para-dise", with the letterforms collapsing), which the parallel with
  "martyrs claim their houris" supports — but this is **my reading from context, not a form the page
  gives**, and the sentence begins on p. 56, outside this chunk, so a human should read the two
  pages together. (2) `art` for "are": "All excellences and dignity `art` in God alone." (p. 57,
  folio 56). This may be damage or may be Usborne's deliberate archaism — he uses "Thy" and "Thou"
  forms freely on this page ("Thy support", "Thy Love", "Thy grace") — so **I have left it unflagged
  inline and record it here only**.
- **The attribution of notes 3 and 4 is genuinely ambiguous and I have not forced it.** Note 8 is
  **signed** "— C. F. Usborne", so it is his. Note 2 frames him in the third person, "Usborne's note
  says:", so the annotator is quoting him — and this is the note that **quotes Usborne explicitly**.
  Notes 3 and 4 end with a bare "(Usborne.)", which could mean either "Usborne wrote this note" or
  "this gloss is taken from Usborne". **Notes 1, 5, 6 and 7 carry nothing at all and are the
  annotator's.** The consequence for the archive is the one to keep: **the two identifications this
  page supplies — the Saint of Kasur (note 5) and Shakarganj as the saint of Pakpattan (note 7) —
  are unattributed, i.e. the later annotator's, and must not be cited as Usborne's.**
- **Note 2's date is the page's only date, and it glosses a passage that is not on this page.** The
  note attaches to "Southern country", which appears on an earlier page; it gives "the year 120 of
  the Bikramajit era" and "AD. 1766" and **admits in its own parenthesis that the two do not
  correspond**. It also identifies "Lammnan Des" with "the present Montgomery district of the
  Punjab" — a colonial district name, which is one of the things that dates the annotator's hand.
  **The composition date 1766 should be cited as the note's claim, not as Waris Shah's statement.**
- **`Shaktipeeth Shri Hinglaj Mata Mandir` does not appear on this page**, so the archive's empty
  id cell for that row is not exercised here. Noted only so the consolidator knows it was checked.
- **Nothing on this page is a karamat**, so the "Legends and miracles" heading is omitted rather
  than filled. The nearest material is eschatological (Paradise, houris, the Prophet's
  intercession, the Day of Judgement) and is recorded under Practices.
