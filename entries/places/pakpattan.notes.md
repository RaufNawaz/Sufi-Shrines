## Framing

- **Farid al-Din** is named only as the saint who died in Pakpattan, which is what Kugle's note says; his life is not retold (the shrine has its own article). The material calls him only "Farid al-Din" (through the bracketed reading), so the article does not add "Ganjshakar".
- **Shaykh Sabir** is named without description: the material says nothing of who he was. The article says his cave retreat is preserved "near the place where" Farid al-Din died, as Kugle does, and does not place it inside the town.
- **The 2001 events.** Only the book's own words are given: "the mob stampede in Pakpattan Sharif", "the 2001 Pakpattan tragedy", "the Pakpattan tragedy of 2001". No cause, no death toll, no place within the town (not even the shrine), no date but 2001. The article does not state that the stampede and the tragedy are the same event: the stampede line (p. 44) carries no year and the quoted lines do not join them. They are set side by side in one paragraph and the reader is left to connect them. See question 1.
- **"Fourteenth century".** The block's own summary heading calls the itinerary "fourteenth-century", but the quoted prose says only "In the century after Mu'in ad-Din's death" and gives the year 1352. The article keeps to the quoted words and the year, and does not say "fourteenth century".
- **Two names for the town.** Rozehnal's "Pakpattan Sharif" is noted in the Overview ("who calls the town Pakpattan Sharif as well as Pakpattan") and appears in the quotations; elsewhere the article uses Pakpattan.
- **"Once he dates by it"** (The Place in the Books) reads Rozehnal's "around the time of" as a time marker, which is what the phrase is; the article does not say what he was dating, because the block does not quote it.
