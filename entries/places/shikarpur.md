## Overview

Michel Boivin calls Shikarpur "the northern city of Sindh" and describes it as a hub of international commerce and the core of merchant networks, a town in which the baniyas were the dominant group (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 30, folio 17). He sets it with Rohri and Sukkur in a triangle on both sides of the Indus River, which he calls the locale of a rich dialogue between creeds (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 31, folio 18). Jürgen Schaflechner mentions the town in connection with its merchants and moneylenders (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 58, folio not stated), and Boivin names two men born there, Nimano Faqir and Sami, the second a cloth merchant who travelled between Shikarpur and Amritsar (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 60, folio 47; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 94, folio 81).

## A City of Merchants

Boivin gives the fullest picture of the town. "Shikarpur, the northern city of Sindh where the Northern dialect of Sindhi known as Utradi or Siraiki was spoken, was a hub of international commerce at this time," he writes (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 30, folio 17). He continues: "It was the core of merchant networks that allowed them to control commerce between Central Asia and overseas countries." (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 30, folio 17).

Schaflechner, in his study of the temple of Hinglaj, also knows Shikarpur as a town of trade. He mentions it in connection with its merchants and moneylenders (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 58, folio not stated), in his survey of Hindu settlement (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 80, folio not stated), and again where he discusses the demographics of merchants (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 98, folio not stated).

One merchant of the town travelled as far as Punjab: Boivin writes of Sami that he was "born in a well-to-do family in Shikarpur. He was himself a cloth merchant and travelled a great deal between Shikarpur and Amritsar in Punjab." (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 94, folio 81).

## Communities and Creeds

Of the town's merchants Boivin writes: "Here, the baniyas were the dominant group." (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 30, folio 17).

He sees Shikarpur as one corner of a wider meeting ground. In his words, "the triangle of Rohri-Sukkur-Shikarpur, on both sides of the Indus River," was "the locale for an amazingly rich dialogue between different creeds" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 31, folio 18).

Boivin also names Nimano Faqir as "born into a rich Hindu family of Shikarpur" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 60, folio 47).

## The Place in the Books

For Boivin, Shikarpur is the northern city of Sindh, where Utradi or Siraiki was spoken, a centre of commerce between Central Asia and overseas countries, a corner of the Rohri, Sukkur and Shikarpur triangle of dialogue between creeds, and the birthplace of Nimano Faqir and of Sami (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 30, folio 17; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 31, folio 18; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 60, folio 47; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 94, folio 81). For Schaflechner it is a merchant town, named in brief passages on merchants, moneylenders and Hindu settlement (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 58, folio not stated; Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 80, folio not stated; Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 98, folio not stated).

Haroon Khalid names the town only as a family origin. He writes that Humayun "belongs to Thatta on his paternal side and Shikarpur on his maternal side" (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 226, folio 200).
