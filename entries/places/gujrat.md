## Overview

Shemeem Burney Abbas names the Gujrat district twice. It is the home of the potter Tulla, father of Sohni, in a tale she tells as beginning "Once upon a time" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 125, folio not stated), and it is where she places Takht Hazara, the home of Ranjha's father and of the Ranjha clan of Jats, which she describes as "now Gujrat District of Pakistan" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 122, folio not stated).

## The Place in the Books

Abbas sets the opening of the tale of Sohni in the district: "Once upon a time there was a well-to-do potter called Tulla who lived in the Gujrat district of the Panjab. He had a beautiful daughter called Sohni, which means the beautiful one" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 125, folio not stated). The passage is told as a tale, and the district is given as the place where, in the tale, Tulla lived (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 125, folio not stated).

Abbas also ties Ranjha to the district through his father. Ranjha, she writes, is "the youngest of the eight sons of Chaudhry Muazzam, also known as Mauju of the Ranjha clan of Jats of Takht Hazara, now Gujrat District of Pakistan" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 122, folio not stated).

The two passages name the district in two ways. The tale of Sohni speaks of "the Gujrat district of the Panjab" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 125, folio not stated), while the account of Ranjha's family places Takht Hazara in what is "now Gujrat District of Pakistan" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 122, folio not stated).
