## Left out, and why

- Schaflechner: "the index headword `Shikarpur, 48` (p. 360, folio not stated)". An index locator; the 48 is a printed page number that the book's pages cannot be matched to (the book prints no recoverable folio), and the brief forbids turning an index locator into a page. Left out.
- Schaflechner: "**No shrine is attached in any of them.**" A remark about how the material was attached to archive rows, not about the place. Left out. (It is consistent with the article, which places no shrine in the town from Schaflechner.)
- Boivin: "and a further bare occurrence at p. 214 (folio 201)". A bare occurrence with no stated content. Left out.
- Khalid: "(The book's Gurdwara Sachkand at Farooqabad is not this place.)" A disambiguation note; left out of the prose. Nothing from Farooqabad is used.
- The attachment lines ("Attached to 3 row(s): gurdwara-sach-khand-sahib, khatwari-darbar-shikarpur, sant-bhagat-kanwar-ram-temple-chak") and ruling (a) boilerplate. Apparatus. In particular, none of the three sites is named in the article, because no book's material here names any of them; the place page lists them already.

## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Schaflechner, pp. 58, 80, 98 (all folio not stated).** The block gives no quotation, only what each passage is about: "named for its merchants and moneylenders" (p. 58), "for merchant demographics" (p. 98), "in the survey of Hindu settlement" (p. 80). Stated as what they are: Schaflechner "mentions it in connection with its merchants and moneylenders", "in his survey of Hindu settlement", and "where he discusses the demographics of merchants". I wrote "in connection with" for the block's "named for" so that no reader takes it to mean the town is named after its merchants. The article claims nothing more from these pages; in The Place in the Books they are called "brief passages".
- **Khalid, p. 226, folio 200, Humayun.** The block quotes only that Humayun "belongs to Thatta on his paternal side and Shikarpur on his maternal side" and does not say who Humayun is. The article quotes the line and frames Shikarpur as a family origin, without identifying Humayun or giving a pronoun beyond the book's own "his".
- **Boivin, p. 30, folio 17, "at this time".** The block quotes the sentence ending "was a hub of international commerce at this time" but does not say which time Boivin means. The article quotes it verbatim and does not name a period. (Question 1.)
- **Boivin, p. 30, "allowed them to control commerce".** "Them" is unresolved in the quotation as given (presumably the merchants, but the block does not say). Quoted verbatim, not paraphrased, so as not to resolve it.
- **Boivin, p. 60, folio 47, Nimano Faqir, and p. 94, folio 81, Sami.** The block gives only their birth in Shikarpur, and for Sami his trade and travel. The article does not say who either man was (poet, saint or otherwise), since the material does not; their own pages carry that if the archive has them.
- **Signpost sentences without a citation**, each introducing the cited sentence after it and adding no fact: "Boivin gives the fullest picture of the town" (a statement about this material); "Schaflechner, in his study of the temple of Hinglaj, also knows Shikarpur as a town of trade" (the book's title, and a summary of the cited sentence that follows); "One merchant of the town travelled as far as Punjab" (joined by a colon to the cited Sami quotation); "He sees Shikarpur as one corner of a wider meeting ground"; "Haroon Khalid names the town only as a family origin".

## Questions for Rauf

2. **Who are Nimano Faqir and Sami?** Boivin's lines here give only their birth in Shikarpur (and Sami's cloth trade between Shikarpur and Amritsar). If either has an archive page or another sourced identification, should the place article say so (citing that source), or stay with what these lines give?
3. **The three sites attached to Shikarpur** (Gurdwara Sach Khand Sahib, Khatwari Darbar, Sant Bhagat Kanwar Ram temple at Chak) are not named in any book's Shikarpur material, so the article does not name them. The place page lists them. Is that right, or would you like a sentence pointing to them, which would have no book citation?
4. **Schaflechner's index locator "Shikarpur, 48".** Left out as an index locator. If someone can check printed page 48 of the book, it may carry more on the town.
