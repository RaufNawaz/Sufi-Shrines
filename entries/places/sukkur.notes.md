# Sukkur team version: notes for Rauf

Written 31 October 2026 under docs/prompts/PLACE_DESCRIPTION_BRIEF.md by a single drafter. The sources file, entries/places/sources/sukkur.sources.md, is about 18,000 characters, short enough for one writer, so there are no parts and no composer. Every "## From" section was read in full. Six of the seven books are cited; Schimmel's *As Through a Veil* is not (see "Left out").

`check_place_description.py team sukkur`: ok, 10,861 characters, 58 citations to 6 books, 28 paragraphs, exit 0. The first run failed once (C1 on Boivin `pp. 68–69, folios 55–56`, see below) and was fixed in the article.

The material is thin. Only Boivin's *Hindu Sufis of South Asia* says much about the two towns, and Bhandari's chronicle gives one episode (the pursuit of Dara). Every other book names Sukkur or Rohri in one or two sentences. Much of the material is about **Rohri**, not Sukkur: the sources file attaches the Rohri blocks to the Sukkur rows because those rows are described as "near Sukkur/Rohri". I kept all of it, always naming Rohri as Rohri, and did not say where Rohri lies in relation to Sukkur (no book here says so; "across the river" was in a first draft and was removed as general knowledge). See question 1.

Dashes: no em dashes and no en dashes in the prose (checked by script with citations removed). The en dashes left are inside page references that the sources print as ranges: `pp. 97–98, folios 84–85` (Boivin, three times) and `pp. 68–69` (Boivin, once). The source's own en dashes inside quotations were avoided: Boivin's "Contrary to other cities in Northern Sindh – for instance, Sukkur, following the Manzilgah riots – no communal violence ..." is paraphrased up to the quoted "no communal violence had ever been registered"; Fateh Muhammad Sehwani's "(1882–1942)" is written "who lived from 1882 to 1942"; "Rohri–Sukkur–Shikarpur triangle" is written "Sukkur, Rohri and Shikarpur as the corners of one triangle". Hyphens: none of my own. The only hyphen in the prose is "roving-minstrel", inside Abbas's verbatim quotation.

No prose paragraph holds Arabic script. There is no verse paragraph.

## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Dhillon, "to Sukkur from where he turned southwards along the river Indus" (p. 194, folio 214)**, which the source calls a "Bare itinerary name". Stated as what it is, in The Place in the Books: "names the town as a stage on a journey". The source does not say who "he" is (the travellers come "From Multan"), so the article quotes and does not name him.
- **Bhandari's campaign mention (pp. 51, 50)**, which the source marks "basis: campaign mention" and says "Nothing is said of any shrine, temple or holy man at Sukkur". Given in History as the chronicle's episode, and in The Place in the Books with that sentence as the plain statement of what the book does and does not say.
- **Boivin, "Rohri dham" (p. 78, folio 65)**: the source gives only the phrase. The article says only that he "speaks of 'Rohri dham'", without glossing *dham*. A candidate for cutting in the public version.
- **Boivin, "an orphanage in Sukkur" (p. 104, folio 91)**: no context in the source. Given as a bare mention: "He also mentions 'an orphanage in Sukkur'".
- **Boivin, "this rehabilitation" (p. 61, folio 48)**: the source does not say what was being rehabilitated. The article quotes the sentence ("In the same passage he writes that ...") and does not identify it. It may be the Manzilgah, given the index entry "Manzilgah Affair 48", but I did not join the two. See question 2.
- **Boivin, Sadh Belo's origin (pp. 68–69, folios 55–56)**: the source gives only the pointer, not the story. Stated as a pointer: "Boivin, who writes the name Sadh Belo, tells the origin of the island shrine". The shrine's own article carries the story.
- **Boivin, "Kandri ... in the Khaipur [text-layer? Khairpur] district" (p. 107, folio 94)**: written "in the district of Khairpur, which the printed text gives as Khaipur".
- **Boivin, Rohri as "a city of the riots" (p. 61, folio 48)**: the source's own summary, not a quotation; given as "Rohri too he names among the cities of the riots".
- **Abbas, Rohri "is a route" (p. 78)**: the source says "no shrine at Rohri is named, no date, no event", and the article says so after the quotation.
- **Schaflechner, Rohri named once in the survey of Hindu settlement (p. 94)**: given as exactly that, with "attaches no shrine to it".
- **Signpost sentences without a citation** (body only, never in the Overview): "In the twentieth century Michel Boivin's Sukkur is a city of riots"; "Boivin also uses Sukkur as a measure for Sehwan"; "Boivin places shrines at Rohri as well"; "Rohri is the birthplace of the figures Boivin follows most closely there"; "Abbas places a singer on the routes out of Rohri"; "Boivin's book gives the two towns the most room". "Sukkur gives Boivin a woman of his account" heads a cited sentence.

## Page references

- **Boivin `pp. 68–69, folios 55–56`**: the source prints "pp. 68–69" and "(folios 55–56)" on two lines with the folios in parentheses, so the checker cannot match the reference with its folios. The article cites `pp. 68–69` only. The folios are 55–56.
- **Schimmel `p. 427, folio 306 as printed`**: the source reads "(p. 427, folio 306 as printed — rule gives 396)". The article cites "p. 427, folio 306 as printed", dropping the em dash clause. The book's own index ("Rohri, 396, 397") supports 396. See question 3.

## Where the Sources Disagree

Not written: I found no real disagreement.
- Bedil: Schimmel "Bedil of Rohri (d. 1872)" (p. 427) and Boivin "a Sufi born in Rohri" (pp. 97–98) agree. Boivin's Rochaldas, born 1879 (p. 55) and "seven years after [Bedil's] demise" (pp. 97–98), fits Schimmel's 1872, but the article does not do that arithmetic.
- Sadh Bela (Schaflechner) against Sadh Belo (Boivin): a spelling, noted in Holy Places ("who writes the name Sadh Belo").
- Vasan Shah's "original sanctuary" at Rohri (p. 68) and "Vasan Shah's darbar is located in Ulhasnagar, while another one is in Rohri" (p. 209): two shrines, not a contradiction.
- Bhandari's two distances to Sukkur (63 *kos* on this side, about a hundred on the other): one reckoning by two routes.

## Questions for Rauf

1. **Rohri.** Over half the material is about Rohri (Boivin's Rochaldas, Bedil, Vasan Shah, Isarlal, Kandri; Schimmel's two poets; Abbas's Alan Faqir). The sources file puts it on Sukkur because the archive's rows near Sukkur also name Rohri. I kept it, always as Rohri. Should Rohri have a place page of its own, in which case this material would move there and the Sukkur article would shrink to Bhandari, Dhillon, Schaflechner's Sadh Bela and Boivin's Sukkur sentences?
2. **Boivin's "this rehabilitation" (p. 61, folio 48).** The source does not say what was rehabilitated "in the harbour on the Indus River". Should someone read p. 61 so the article can name it (perhaps the Manzilgah)?
3. **Schimmel's folio at p. 427.** The page prints folio 306; the sequence and the book's index give 396. The article cites "folio 306 as printed". Should the public bibliography or citation practice record 396?
4. **Bhandari's faujdar (p. 50).** The name "فوج علی بیگ" is marked uncertain. Should someone read the page to name him?
