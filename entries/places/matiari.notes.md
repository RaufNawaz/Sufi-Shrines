## The framing: an article about Hala, not Matiari

All three books speak only of the town of Hala. No line of the material names Matiari or says that Hala lies in it, so the article never says so: it is written about Hala as the books give it, and no sentence links Hala to Matiari (question 1). Makhdoom Nooh, under whose row the material is filed, is named in no book (Boivin's block says so outright), so neither he nor his shrine appears.

## Framing choices

- **Schaflechner, p. 71.** Given as a quoted account's claim: "In an account of the origins of the Goddess Hinglaj that Schaflechner quotes", then "that account's claims, not Schaflechner's own". The sentence "this is the only place the book names Hala" rests on the block's "Named once". No link is drawn between any shrine at Hala and Hinglaj, as the block forbids.
- **Boivin, p. 91, folio 78.** Given as Boivin's record of a birth, in History. The block's "No shrine, saint, ʿurs or custodian is attached to the town" was first written as a sentence and then removed: it is a statement about the book's silence, cited to a single page, and adds nothing a reader of Hala needs.
- **Abbas, p. 46.** The block's paraphrase "Kolhi and Bhil women go to Shah Abdul Latif's shrine" is given as Abbas's statement, with the quotation verbatim. Kolhi and Bhil are named only as the block names them, with nothing added about either community. The closing sentence of that section ("Abbas's Hala is thus farming country, a fertile belt of large feudal farms that offers these women work") restates p. 46 and carries its citation.
- **Spelling of the saint.** In my own sentences, Shah Abdul Latif throughout. Boivin's form, Shah Abd al-Latif, stands in the block outside the quotation ("He was born in Hala in a Sayyid family" has no name), so it does not appear. Abbas's form is not shown in the block either (the name is in the block's paraphrase, not inside the quotation).
- **Sindh.** "Hala in Sindh" is said only as the Schaflechner account's words; Abbas's "Sind" stays inside the Bhit Shah quotation.
- **Pronouns.** None of the three Source lines states an author's gender; the prose uses surnames and "the book" and no gendered pronoun for any author.
