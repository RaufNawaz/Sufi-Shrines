## Overview

Carl W. Ernst and Bruce B. Lawrence write that in the century after Mu'in ad-Din's death Pakpattan stood with Ajmer and Delhi among the main points on a pilgrimage itinerary, a route that Shaykh Zayn ad-Din Shirazi followed in 1352 (Ernst and Lawrence, *Sufi Martyrs of Love*, p. 98, folio not stated). Robert Rozehnal, who calls the town Pakpattan Sharif as well as Pakpattan, writes of a mob stampede there and of "the 2001 Pakpattan tragedy" (Rozehnal, *Islamic Sufism Unbound*, p. 44, folio 34; Rozehnal, *Islamic Sufism Unbound*, p. 121, folio 112). Scott Kugle names Pakpattan as the place where Farid al-Din died, and writes that Shaykh Sabir's cave retreat is preserved near it (Kugle, *Sufis and Saints' Bodies*, p. 331, folio 310, ch. 5 n. 30).

## History

Ernst and Lawrence set Pakpattan on an early route of pilgrimage: "In the century after Mu'in ad-Din's death, Ajmer became one of the main points on a pilgrimage itinerary, along with Delhi and Pakpattan" (Ernst and Lawrence, *Sufi Martyrs of Love*, p. 98, folio not stated). That itinerary, they write, was "followed in 1352 by Shaykh Zayn ad-Din Shirazi, the main successor to Burhan ad-Din Gharib in Khuldabad" (Ernst and Lawrence, *Sufi Martyrs of Love*, p. 98, folio not stated).

Rozehnal refers to "the mob stampede in Pakpattan Sharif" (Rozehnal, *Islamic Sufism Unbound*, p. 44, folio 34). Elsewhere he speaks of "the 2001 Pakpattan tragedy" (Rozehnal, *Islamic Sufism Unbound*, p. 121, folio 112) and of "the Pakpattan tragedy of 2001" (Rozehnal, *Islamic Sufism Unbound*, p. 212, folio 204).

## Holy Places

In a note Kugle ties two saints to the town. Farid al-Din died, he writes, "in Pakpattan in Pakistan", and "Shaykh Sabir's cave retreat is preserved near the place where" he died (Kugle, *Sufis and Saints' Bodies*, p. 331, folio 310, ch. 5 n. 30).

## The Place in the Books

Each of the three books sees Pakpattan from its own concern. For Ernst and Lawrence it is a station of pilgrimage beside Ajmer and Delhi (Ernst and Lawrence, *Sufi Martyrs of Love*, p. 98, folio not stated). For Rozehnal its name recalls a tragedy of 2001, and once he dates by it, writing "around the time of the mob stampede in Pakpattan Sharif" (Rozehnal, *Islamic Sufism Unbound*, p. 44, folio 34; Rozehnal, *Islamic Sufism Unbound*, p. 212, folio 204). For Kugle it is the place where Farid al-Din died, near which Shaykh Sabir's cave retreat is preserved (Kugle, *Sufis and Saints' Bodies*, p. 331, folio 310, ch. 5 n. 30).
