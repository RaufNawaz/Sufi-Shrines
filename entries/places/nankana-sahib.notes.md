## Weak material and how I treated it

Khalid's scenes of Nanak's youth are framed as "In Khalid's reconstruction" or "in Khalid's telling", following the source's "Author's reconstruction". The Sacha Sauda variant is framed as "the version a storyteller at Sachkand gave Khalid".
- **Informants.** The Source line of *A White Trail* records that informant names may be pseudonyms. Kartar and Rajbir are introduced as "An informant Khalid calls Kartar" and "an informant Khalid calls Rajbir". Surjeet and Udam are introduced "as Khalid names them".

- **"A young Nanak 'used to spend a lot of time ...'" (p. 91, folio 73).** The source does not make clear whether this is Iqbal Qaiser's speech or Khalid's narration. My first draft gave it to Iqbal Qaiser; it now reads "Khalid writes".

- **Rai Bular's illness (p. 172, folio 188).** The source quote holds a damaged word ("condicion"). It is paraphrased as "sick and failing fast", without quotation marks.
- **Bhandari, p. 520.** The place name carries [OCR?]. The article says "the reading of the place name is uncertain in the printed text".
- **The picture of Baba Farid (*In Search of Shiva*, p. 13, ch. 7).** The source says the book does not say which gurdwara is meant, and the article says so. The chapter title "Syncretism in the Mainstream" is taken from the book's Source line, which maps p. 13 to ch. 7 under that title.

## Where the Sources Disagree (as in the article)

1. The date of Nanak's birth: Dhillon gives "On 14 April 1469" (pp. 17–18, folios 19–20). *Walking with Nanak*'s chronology gives "October 20, 1469" (p. 314, folio 288).
2. The place of Nanak's birth: Meherban gives Dera Chahal, and other *janamsakhi* writers and scholars give Rai Bhoi di Talwandi (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 43, folio 25). Dhillon and Bhandari also give Talwandi.
3. Whether Nanak ever left Nankana: Jeeta Singh says he never did (p. 228, folio 202; p. 229, folio not stated). Dhillon and Khalid's narrative have him leave and return (Dhillon p. 56, folio 62; p. 123, folio 137; Khalid p. 54, folio 36).
4. How many gurdwaras: *A White Trail* counts seven, most associated with Nanak (p. 192). *Walking with Nanak* counts six associated with Nanak (p. 90, folio 72). The counts may measure different things, so they are listed without a verdict.
5. How many Sikh households: thirty to forty houses ten years before and sevenfold growth (pp. 174–175); about two hundred houses (p. 175); Kartar's fifty, then two hundred to two hundred and fifty (p. 193); about two hundred households in *Walking with Nanak* (p. 87, folio 69).
6. When and from where the Sikh families came: recent migrants from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (p. 235); after the wars of 1965 and 1971 and the Babri Mosque demolition of 1992 (pp. 174–175); Kartar's family, among the first, at the time of Giani Pratap in the sixties (p. 184); Rajbir from Parachinar during the Khalistan Movement (p. 213); Madan Singh's family since the late seventies (p. 215); Inderjit's family in 1965 (*Walking with Nanak*, p. 306, folio 280).
7. How many come for Guru Nanak's birthday: official estimates of ten to fifteen thousand a year, media reports of three thousand in 2011, and Kartar's "thousands" (pp. 222, 182).

Not listed as a disagreement:
- **Who ruled Talwandi, and whose accounts Nanak's family kept.** Dhillon has Rai Bhoe and his son Rai Bular, with Shiv Ram as accountant to Rai Bhoe, succeeded by his son Kalyan Chand Das Bedi (p. 17, folio 19). Dhillon also has Rai Bhoe alive at the return of 1506 and Rai Bular dying later (p. 123, folio 137; p. 172, folio 188). Khalid has Mehta Kalu keep "the accounts of the ruler of Talwindi, Rai Bular Bhatti" (p. 22, folio 4). This would be a disagreement only if Kalyan Chand were Mehta Kalu, and the sources do not equate them, so the article gives both accounts in one paragraph without joining them. See question 1.
- The New Year dates of 14 March and 13 April (p. 217). This is a calendar difference that Khalid reports and explains, not a disagreement between sources.

## Questions for Rauf

1. **Kalyan Chand and Mehta Kalu.** Dhillon makes Shiv Ram accountant to Rai Bhoe, succeeded by his son Kalyan Chand Das Bedi (p. 17, folio 19), and elsewhere calls Nanak's father Mehta Kalu (p. 124, folio 138). Khalid has Mehta Kalu keep the accounts of Rai Bular (p. 22, folio 4). The material never says that Kalyan Chand is Mehta Kalu, so the article does not join them, and it does not list the accountancy as a disagreement. May the public version treat them as one man? If so, Dhillon (accountant to Rai Bhoe's house) and Khalid (accountant to Rai Bular) become a disagreement to list.
2. **Bhandari's "جد ابادری" (p. 520).** The source glosses it as "his grandfather's house" and marks it "as printed". It may be a misprint for جد مادری, maternal grandfather. The article says only "grandfather". Should the public version stay with that?
3. **The p. 175 page reference.** Keep `p. 175, folio "l92" [text-layer?] [192]` as copied, or shorten it to `p. 175` across the place articles? Punjab has the same question for its `folio printed "Ill"` reference.
4. **Sites with no description here.** The material names Gurdwara Janamsthan and "the shrine of Guru Nanak", which may or may not be Janam Asthan, and it names no other gurdwara at Nankana Sahib, although seven archive rows attach to the town. The article does not identify the shrine of the Baba Farid picture with any row. Should it?
