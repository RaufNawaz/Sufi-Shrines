No Overview (the composer's). Festivals (Lohri, Vaisakhi, Basant) went at the end of Communities, as there is no province heading for festivals.

## Whose unit

The books' "Punjab" is not one unit, and the part says so where the source does:
- Rizvi's "the Panjab" of Ghaznavid, Ghurid, Mongol and Afghan times; "west Panjab" in Babur's negotiations (p. 409); "the southern part of the Panjab" in 1175 (p. 128).
- Eaton's "the Lahore and Multan subas" of Abu al-Fazl's statistics, c. 1595 (p. 225), the only place a source names the Mughal provincial units; and his "western Punjab", "southwestern Punjab", "northwest Punjab".
- Ernst and Lawrence's "the Punjab" as one of "two provinces" of Akbar (pp. 112–113); the part adds the sentence "The book does not state the extent of that Mughal province", which the block states.
- Khalid's present day "Pakistani Punjab", "Central Punjab", "Southern Punjab", "West Punjab" (the last a turban style; he does not define it), and "the division of Punjab between India and Pakistan".

## Left out, and why

- Dhillon p. 224 ("a south-eastern route co [text-layer?] [to] the Punjab"): a bare transit. Given as what it is, one clause without a quotation ("The fourth journey closed ... with a south-eastern route back to the Punjab").
- Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 182 ("because Sikhism is seen as a reaction against the feudal land gt [OCR?] … ot [OCR?] [of] Punjab bv [OCR?] ..."): the noun after "feudal land" is lost, so the claim cannot be stated without inventing it. Left out.
- Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 306 ("covering the floods in Sindh and lower Punjab"): a television news mention on the day of a visit, with no date; says nothing of the province. Left out.
- Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 128, the damaged sentence "rin system that is prevalent in Pakistani Punjab" [OCR?]: left out; the readable sentences on Punjabi around it are used.
- Ernst and Lawrence, the endnote to p. 121 (n. 10, Siddiqi's article "The Resurgence of the Chishti Silsilah in the Punjab during the Eighteenth Century", p. 214, folio 203): bibliographic only. Left out.
- Eaton p. 227 Table 9.1 list of clans: used as a list of names in prose. The doabs Eaton names on pp. 224–225 are marked "not attached" in the source and were not used.
- Eaton p. 225, the citation of Ibbetson and Rose: only Irfan Habib's explanation is used; Ibbetson and Rose are not named.
- The sources file's "Label-only uses" and "Kept with their places" paragraphs under *In Search of Shiva* (lines 400 and 402 of the sources file): not attached to the province; left out by rule (Sahari Mal's shrine, Ram Thamman's Vaisakhi, Jhang as "hotbed", Tibba Haji Deen, the Lahore drug ban).
- Rizvi p. 83, Mahmud's and Bahram Shah's reign dates "(388/998-421/1030)", "(1118-52)": left out to avoid a date written with a hyphen that reads as a dash; recoverable for the composer.
- Kugle p. 280, Imdadullah's flight through the Punjab and Sindh after 1857: the block calls it an itinerary to weigh lightly. Given once, framed as "Scott Kugle's brief mention".

## Weak material and how it was treated

- Kugle p. 280 (itinerary): stated as a brief mention, as above.
- Ernst and Lawrence p. 110: the block says Punjab is one of five regions named together and nothing specific to Punjab is said; the part says exactly that.
- Ernst and Lawrence p. 124: the authors set the political reading aside; the part says so ("they then set this reading aside, since 'political contestation is only part of the narrative'").
- *In Search of Shiva* p. 15 on abandoned festivals: the block notes the abandonment is stated for the country's elite; the part says "among the country's elite".
- *In Search of Shiva* p. 16, the Heer Ranjha and Mirza Sahiban sentences, which an earlier reading flagged as possibly label uses: kept, framed as Khalid speaking in conversation and in his own prose.
- *In Search of Shiva* p. 9, the Apal Muri mobile numbers: kept as Khalid's guess ("he guesses that they were 'probably hoping ...'"), under The Province in the Books.
- *Walking with Nanak* pp. 258, 262: framed as the novelised chapter, Khalid imagining the province.
- *Walking with Nanak* p. 234: kept as "some claims" that Hargobind served Jahangir in the Punjab.
- *A White Trail*: the book's own note says informant names were changed; Daria Boatwala is introduced as "An informant he calls Daria Boatwala".

## Disagreements listed

1. When Ranjit Singh became ruler: *Walking with Nanak* p. 34 ("at the end of the eighteenth century") and p. 87 ("in the nineteenth century"); *A White Trail* p. 207 (crowned 1801).
2. How many Hindus live in the Punjab: *A White Trail* p. 87 ("only a few in Punjab") against pp. 31 and 233.
3. Whether Baba Farid converted the clans: clan claims (Eaton pp. 227, 241) against the silence of the earliest sources and the clans' later arrival (Eaton p. 227).
4. The Sufism of Nanak's Punjab: McLeod as quoted by Rizvi (pp. 419–420) against Rizvi (p. 420).

Not listed as disagreements, for the composer to weigh: Rizvi dates Mu'izzu'd-Din's seizure of Multan and "the southern part of the Panjab" to 1175 (p. 128), Dhillon writes "Shahabuddin Muhammad Ghori captured Punjab in 1192" (p. 136). Both are in the History section side by side; they need not describe the same event, so no verdict and no disagreement item.

Also for the composer: Ernst and Lawrence's block gives Taunsawi "(d. 1850)" and calls Muhammad 'Aqil "(d. 1814)" "his successor" in its framing line; the part avoids naming the relationship and gives only "Muhammad 'Aqil and Gul Muhammad Ahmadpuri".
