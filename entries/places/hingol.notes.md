## Weak material and how I treated it

- **"the entry to the Hingol National Park" (Schaflechner, p. 152, folio not stated).** A bare directional mention: the block gives no context. Stated as what it is: he "names, in passing, 'the entry to the Hingol National Park', without describing it further".

The article says "the shrine being Hinglaj's", which rests on the block's heading pairing the park with Las Bela, the attached row (shaktipeeth-shri-hinglaj-mata-mandir) and the book's subject. Question 3.
- **"a district close to Hinglaj" (p. 79).** The block gives this as Schaflechner's description of Las Bela; the article says so, and does not say that the park lies in Las Bela district, which neither book states.
- **The HSM.** The block never expands the abbreviation or says what kind of body it is. The article calls it "the body he calls the HSM" and gives only its founding date and place and its membership as quoted. Question 1.
- **The Tumbel.** The block does not say who they are. The article quotes "the Tumbel spread the Goddess's fame in Las Bela" and adds nothing. Question 2.
- **"the Goddess".** Kept as Schaflechner's word, in quotation; the article does not name her further.
- **Shah Abdul Latif's pilgrimage (Boivin, p. 92, folio 79).** The book's own hedge "probably" is kept inside the quotation and pointed out in the next sentence. Spelled Shah Abdul Latif as the Sindh article spells him; the block writes Shah Abd al-Latif.
- **Parsram's claim (Boivin, p. 50, folio 37).** Kept as Parsram's claim, reported by Boivin, and set inside the Theosophical narrative the block describes. Who Parsram was is not in the block and is not said.
- **Signpost sentences without a citation**, each introducing the cited sentence after it and adding no fact: "Schaflechner names Las Bela as a place of the Goddess's renown"; "Las Bela is also where the body he calls the HSM began" (restating p. 82, cited in the next sentence); "Boivin knows Hinglaj as a goal of pilgrimage"; "Hinglaj appears in poetry too"; "The site also enters a Theosophical account"; "The claim is Parsram's".

## Disagreements

None listed, and no Where the Sources Disagree section. Considered and rejected:
- **Sindh or Baluchistan.** Parsram's claim puts the universal wisdom "in Sindh, in Hinglaj to be exact", and the same sentence calls Hinglaj "a pilgrimage centre located in the Sindhi-speaking area of Baluchistan" (Boivin, p. 50, folio 37). The sentence reconciles the two itself (Sindh as the Sindhi-speaking country, the place in Baluchistan), so the article quotes it whole rather than setting it out as a disagreement. Question 4.
- **Park and district.** Schaflechner sets the shrine in the Hingol National Park (p. 295) and calls Las Bela "a district close to Hinglaj" (p. 79); Boivin places Hinglaj in "the Sindhi-speaking area of Baluchistan". Different descriptions, not opposed claims.
