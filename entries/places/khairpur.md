## Overview

Michel Boivin knows Khairpur as an emirate in Sindh, one of the princely states to which many Kayasht migrated for work (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 26, folio 13). Its amirs, he writes, were staunch Shia Muslims who built imambaras in the nineteenth century and were nonetheless generous patrons of all the religious buildings (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 31, folio 18). He uses the town as a landmark in Sindh, placing near it the Darazi silsila, the dargah of the Jalalani tariqa and Daraza (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 93, folio 80; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 122, folio 109; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 150, folio 137).

## The Emirate and Its Amirs

Boivin names Khairpur as a princely state when he writes of the Kayasht: "many Kayasht migrated to work in the Princely States, including the Khairpur emirate in Sindh" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 26, folio 13).

Of its rulers he writes that "the amirs of Khairpur were themselves staunch Shia Muslims, and they built beautiful imambaras in the nineteenth century, but it did not prevent them from being generous patrons for all of the religious buildings" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 31, folio 18). In his account, then, the amirs' own Shia faith did not narrow their patronage (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 31, folio 18).

## The Landscape Around the Town

Boivin places two lineages of northern Sindh by their nearness to Khairpur: the Darazi silsila, "located close to Khairpur in Northern Sindh" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 93, folio 80), and "the Jalalani tariqa, whose dargah is located in Northern Sindh close to Khairpur" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 122, folio 109). Daraza, too, he puts "not far from Khairpur, in Sindh" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 150, folio 137).

Elsewhere the town serves him only as a point of reference: Halani lies, in his words, "between Nausharo Firuz and Khairpur" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 68, folio 55). Kandri he places in the district of Khairpur, though the name is uncertainly printed on that page (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 107, folio 94). And he gives "Hingorja near Khairpur" as the birthplace of Bhagwan K. Ajwani (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 169, folio 156).
