## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Schimmel (p. 359, folio 341)**: index headword only, plus the indexed figure "Omar of Omarkot". The block says so and calls him "a romance figure, not a saint". The article states it as what it is, in The Place in the Books: "Schimmel's book does not discuss the place; its index carries Omarkot as a headword and, from it, Omar of Omarkot, a figure of romance and not a saint."
- **Bhandari, p. 284**: the block marks it "basis: transit mention". It is kept because it is the only pre-modern narrative of the place in the material (a ruler, a fortress, a royal birth). The block never names the emperor, so the article says only "the emperor" and does not name him. It is given as the chronicle's account.
- **Bhandari, p. 529**: "It is one of suba Thatta's four sarkars". The block's "It" is ambiguous: it may mean Amarkot, or the stretch from Bhakkar, or the passage's sarkar. The article says only that "The passage belongs to the chronicle's account of the suba of Thatta and its four sarkars", and does not call Amarkot a sarkar (question 2).
- **Boivin, p. 178, folio 165**: "between Mirpur Khas and Umerkot" is a bare locator. It is given as Boivin placing "the same stretch of country again".
- **Boivin, p. 188, folio 175**: the "he" of "The area of Umarkot where he lived" is not named in the block. The article quotes it as printed and says only that it comes "in the pages he gives to Pithoro Pir" (pp. 188 to 191 hold the Pithoro Pir material in the block). - **Boivin, p. 189, folio 176**: "the latter officially took power from the first in 1351" does not name "the latter" or "the first" in the block. It is quoted verbatim, and neither is named.

One is a figure in a myth and the other a title in a history, so they do not contradict each other. Both are in the article separately.
- **The year of Akbar's birth (Bhandari, p. 284)**: 949 carries [OCR?], and no other source gives a date, so there is nothing to set against it.

## Persian prose given in English

All the Bhandari material is Persian. The block glosses most of it; where it does not, the article gives a plain rendering without quotation marks:
- "بہزاران مشقت و تعب در حصار امرکوت نزول اقبال واقع شد" became "came after thousands of hardships and toil to the fortress of Amarkot". This is my rendering (question 1).
- "رانا پرشاد حاکم آنجا" became "Rana Parshad, its ruler", and "مقدم عالی سعادت خود" became "counted the arrival his own good fortune", following the block's "counted the arrival ... and did the duties of service".
- "حمیده بانو بیگم" became Hamida Banu Begum, and "بعد رسیدن در نواحی تہتہ" became "after reaching the neighbourhood of Thatta".
- "از بهکر تا نصیر پور و امرکوت مردم ... و جاریجه و دیگران توطن دارند" became "From Bhakkar to Nasirpur and Amarkot ... live the Sabuda ..., together with the Jarija and others".
