## Overview

Michel Boivin describes Luari Sharif, which his book spells Lowari Sharif, as "a village close to Badin in Southern Sindh" and names it as the home of a group of Naqshbandi scholars (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 22, folio 9). In the annexes to the same book he counts it among the important Naqshbandi centres in Sindh, and writes that the official book devoted to its Naqshbandis was written by a Hindu follower (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 228, folio 215).

## A Naqshbandi Centre

Boivin names the place in writing of "Naqshbandi scholars from Lowari Sharif, a village close to Badin in Southern Sindh" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 22, folio 9). In the same passage he writes that "The main author was Makhdum Muhammad Hashim", whose years he gives as 1692 to 1762, and that he "was qazi in Thatta" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 22, folio 9).

In the annexes Boivin returns to the place. The Naqshbandis, he writes, "have several important centres in Sindh, especially that of" Lowari Sharif, "located in Southern Sindh, near Badin" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 228, folio 215). On that page the name of the place is uncertainly printed (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 228, folio 215).

## Communities

The same annex passage ties the Naqshbandis of the place to a Hindu devotee: Boivin writes that "the official book devoted to the Naqshbandis of Lowari Sharif was written by a Hindu follower" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 228, folio 215).
