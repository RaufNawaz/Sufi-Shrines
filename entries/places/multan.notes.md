## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Index and contents (Bhandari, pp. 20 and 589).** Given as what they are: "The printed edition's index gives Multan a headword with many locators" and "The chronicle's table of contents gives three headings to these events". No fact rests on them. The index locators are not cited as pages.
- **Imprints** (Schimmel, Ernst and Lawrence, Abbas): one paragraph under The Place in the Books says only that these books cite works published in Multan.
- **Transit and directional mentions** (Bhandari's Humayun, Khankhanan, Dara and river passages; Khalid's routes): kept where they say something about Multan's place on roads and rivers, framed as such.
- **Legends:** Alexander's arrow ("legend has it"), Hiranyakashipu ("believed to have ruled"), the Shams-i Tabriz flaying ("according to the Indian tradition"), Hallaj and the Carmathians ("according to the tradition"), Zauqi Shah's miraculous journey ("In a miracle that Zauqi Shah narrated"), the Sakhi Sarwar legend (Chishti), the bowl of milk (Dhillon's retelling), and Lal's initiation ("according to the Suhrawardi tradition"): all framed as the books frame them.
- **Hafiz Muhammad Jamal's resistance to the Sikhs** is kept as Ernst and Lawrence's "is also said to have".
- **Informants** (Khalid): Parvati Devi and Usman are introduced as names Khalid gives ("An informant he calls", "as Khalid calls"), because his author's note (recorded in the Source line) says informant names were changed.
- **Shaikh Musa Gilani, governor of Multan for Dara (Bhandari, p. 66):** kept as the governor only. Not identified with the saint of the Musa Pak shrine; the source says the book does not say they are one man.
- **Malik Izz al-Din Ayaz, governor of Multan (Bhandari, p. 398):** given with his printed name only, not identified with Malik Ayaz of Lahore (the source flags this).
- **Kaiqubad's victim (Bhandari, p. 380):** given with a hedge, "though the wording leaves some doubt over who is meant".
- **Ala al-Mulk's counsel (Bhandari, p. 366):** "the reading of that phrase tentative".
- **Lal's ecstatic scene (Kasmani):** the other Sufi figures are not named; the prose says so.
- **Schimmel's women's shrine:** unnamed, no saint named; the prose says so and does not identify it with any site.
- **The unnamed perfect saint near the fort (Chishti, p. 260):** "in a damaged passage", "lies, it seems, near the fort"; not identified with any site.
- **Uncited framing sentences:** the article uses short signpost sentences at the head of some paragraphs ("Bhandari also tells of men sent to Multan or fleeing to it"). They introduce what the cited sentences that follow say and add no fact.

## Where the Sources Disagree (as in the article)

1. Where Uch lies: Schimmel "in Ucch, northeast of Multan" (MDI p. 385, folio 354) against Rozehnal "seventy miles southwest of Multan" (p. 210, folio 202).
2. Who gave Khizr Khan Multan: Chishti, Timur (p. 816); Bhandari, Timur, with Lahore and Dipalpur (p. 331); Bhandari, Sultan Muhammad Shah bin Firuz Shah (p. 241).
3. Who governed Multan when Ghazi took the throne: Chishti, Ghazi Beg Tughluq himself (p. 814); Bhandari, Bahram (p. 354).
4. Balban's son and the Mongols: Chishti, he drove them out (p. 811); Bhandari, he fell after a short fight (p. 385); Abbas, killed in the Battle of Multan (p. 39). These may be different episodes; listed side by side without a verdict.
5. The last Langah years: the child Sultan Husain seated in 945 against the Arghun conquest dated 932 (Bhandari, p. 294), both uncertainly printed.
6. Barbak Shah against Baba Shah for the same commander (Bhandari, pp. 296 and 295).
7. Aurangzeb's camp, 7 Muharram 1069 (p. 66), against the march, 12 Muharram 1068 (p. 65), both uncertainly printed. (The article says "the emperor"; the source does not name him in these lines.)
8. Whose is the Shams Tabriz shrine at Multan: Schimmel's Indian tradition (ATV p. 175) against Abbas's open identification (p. 193).
9. Ivanow's title: "Shams Tabriz of Multan" in Schimmel's text (p. 285) against "Shums Tabrez of Multan" in her bibliography (p. 326).

Not listed as disagreements (judged compatible): Dhillon's "second half of the seventh century" first contact with Muslim armies beside Schimmel's 711 conquest; Rizvi's 1175 seizure by Muʿizzu'd-Din beside Bhandari's undated first campaign of Shihab al-Din; Boivin's 1397 for Pir Muhammad beside Bhandari's 801 for Timur.

## Questions for Rauf

1. **"The emperor" for Aurangzeb and Humayun.** The source lines for Dara's flight and for the wars with Sher Khan say "the emperor" and never name him. The article keeps "the emperor" rather than naming Aurangzeb or Humayun from general knowledge. Is that the right call, given that the reader may not know who is meant?
2. **Chishti's year 1818 (p. 580).** The Urdu writes the year in words ("سن اٹھارہ سوا اٹھارہ"); the article gives "the year written out in words as eighteen eighteen". The word سوا could also be read differently. Should the article say 1818 plainly?
3. **"The Multani dialect" in Rizvi (pp. 350–351, folios 328–329).** The source declined it as a language, not the place, so it is left out. A city article might want it under Poetry, Music and Language. Include it?
4. **Kaiqubad and Kaikhusrau (Bhandari, p. 380).** The article says Kaiqubad summoned Kaikhusrau "whom the text calls his uncle" with a hedge. The Persian may instead mean that someone else, at Kaikhusrau's instigation, was summoned. Keep the hedge, or drop the sentence?
5. **Signpost sentences without a citation** (for example "Bhandari also tells of men sent to Multan or fleeing to it"). They add no fact, but the brief says every factual sentence is cited. The Lahore team version uses the same kind of sentence. Keep them?
6. **The Langah list's internal dates** (945 against 932) and **the Aurangzeb dates** (1069 against 1068) are listed under Where the Sources Disagree as inconsistencies inside one book. If you would rather such misprints stay in the notes and out of the article, say so.
