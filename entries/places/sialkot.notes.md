## Headings used

Not used: "Shrines, Endowments and the State" (no custodianship, *waqf* or offering is recorded for any Sialkot site; the Mughal governors' repairs to the fort are secular and sit in History); "Festivals and Practice" (the only custom, the townspeople's play in the flooded stream, sits with the stream in The City and Its Country rather than under a heading of its own); "In Verse" (no verse in any script).

- **Werbner and Basu**, "named once, as the place Shâh Daulah left". Used in Holy Places and Legends (the quotation) and in The Place in the Books as a single mention. The book's "Shâh Daulah" is written Shah Daulah; Bhandari's sentence keeps "Shah Daula" as the source glosses it. I do not say the two are the same man, though it seems likely (question 4).
- **Schimmel**, one sentence. Quoted whole in A Seat of Learning; The Place in the Books says it is a single sentence.
- **Bhandari's directional mentions** (Dhonkal 12 kos and Purmandal 15 kos, p. 516; the Chenab and the villages of the *pargana*, p. 512; Gujrat's villages, p. 515; Shah Daula's first master, p. 515), which the source marks "*basis: directional mention*". - **Bhandari, Babur (p. 304)**, "*basis: campaign mention*". Given in History as the chronicle tells it.

## Where the Sources Disagree (as in the article)

1. Who founded Sialkot: Bhandari, Raja Shal (Shalkot) and Raja Salbahan (Salkot) (p. 517); Dhillon, Raja Sela, rebuilt by Raja Satya Vachan (p. 136, folio 151); Khalid, Raja Salivahan (p. 184, folio 158); Chishti names the raja of the Puran legend Raja Salwan[OCR?] (p. 553), as holder of the throne rather than founder.
2. When and how Shihab al-Din took Sialkot: Bhandari's fifth campaign (p. 404) and the year 580 after failing at Lahore (p. 517); Dhillon, Ghori's capture of Punjab in 1192 (p. 136, folio 151). I did not convert 580 to a Common Era year.
3. Sialkot under Babur: Bhandari, an order to lay it waste and move its people to Bahlolpur (p. 304); Dhillon, no resistance and spared on the third incursion (p. 224, folio 246). The article says neither passage names the other's campaign; they may be different campaigns.
4. Man Singh's office: *faujdar* of Jammu and *jagirdar* of Sialkot (Bhandari, p. 517) against *faujdar* of Sialkot (Bhandari, p. 203). Inside one source.

Not listed as a disagreement:
- Maulana Kamal's coming: under Akbar in 971 (p. 516) and in the reign of Husain Khan of Kashmir (p. 187). The two framings do not conflict; I dropped an item I had first drafted.
- Hamza Ghaus (Dhillon) and Hamza Ghous (Khalid): a spelling; each book's spelling is kept in its own sentence.
- The well of Puran: "at a little distance from the city" (Khalid, p. 184) and "still there at Sialkot" (Chishti, p. 554). Compatible.
- Sialkot as former seat of government (p. 517) and as the seat after Lahore's ruin until Mahmud (p. 525): the second explains the first.

## Questions for Rauf

1. **Bhandari's "طغائ[OCR?] پاندوان" (p. 517).** If it reads *taghā'ī*, a maternal uncle, Bhandari agrees with Dhillon that the founder was an uncle of the Pandavas. The article keeps it uncertain. Should someone look at the page?
2. **The year 580 (Bhandari, p. 517)** and **971 (p. 516)** are printed as Hijri years ("هجری"), and the article says "the year 580" and "the year 971 of the Hijra". May the public version give Common Era equivalents, or must it stay as printed?
3. **Dhillon's "him" (p. 159, folio 174).** Presumably Guru Nanak leaving Sialkot. Should someone read the page so the article can name him?
4. **Shah Daulah and Shah Daula.** Werbner and Basu's Shah Daulah who left Sialkot for Gujrat, and Bhandari's Shah Daula whose first master lived in Sialkot, are probably the same saint of Gujrat. The article keeps them in adjacent sentences without equating them. May it say they are one man?
5. **The four en dashes inside page ranges**, as on Peshawar, Nankana Sahib, Karachi and Multan. Kept because the checker matches the exact range.
