## Overview

Michel Boivin counts Sukkur among the cities of northern Sindh (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 71, folio 58), and he sets it with Rohri and Shikarpur as the corners of one triangle (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 31, folio 18). Jürgen Schaflechner names it as the city of the Sadh Bela island *tirtha*, "in the city of Sukkur on an island in the River Indus" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 138, folio not stated).

In Sujan Rai Bhandari's Persian chronicle the town is a stage in the pursuit of Dara: news reached the pursuing armies that Dara had passed Bhakkar and on 25 Muharram had halted at Sukkur (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 51). Saf Shikan Khan, the chronicle adds, appointed an officer to the *faujdari* of Sukkur (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 50). Boivin writes of the riots he calls the 'Sukkur riots', which he dates "between December of 1939 and February of 1940", and whose first victim was Bhagat Kanwar Ram (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 61, folio 48).

Boivin's people of the two towns include Rochaldas, born in Rohri in 1879 (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 55, folio 42), Bedil, "a Sufi born in Rohri" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, pp. 97–98, folios 84–85), and Dadi Dhan, who "was born in Sukkur, Sindh" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 115, folio 102). He places two shrines at Rohri, the original sanctuary of Sain Vasan Shah and the shrine of Isarlal (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 68, folio 55; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 150, folio 137).

Annemarie Schimmel names two mystic poets of Rohri, Bedil, who died in 1872, and Janullah, a Suhrawardi of the second half of the eighteenth century (Schimmel, *Mystical Dimensions of Islam*, p. 427, folio 306 as printed; Schimmel, *Mystical Dimensions of Islam*, p. 428, folio 397). Shemeem Burney Abbas writes that the singer Alan Faqir "is reported to have sung on the buses and trains between Rohri and Sehwan Sharif in his youth" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 78, folio not stated).

## History

Sujan Rai Bhandari's Persian chronicle names Sukkur in its account of the pursuit of Dara. News reached the pursuing armies, he writes, that Dara had passed Bhakkar and on 25 Muharram had halted at Sukkur (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 51). From the stage where the news came, the chronicle reckons the way to Sukkur at 63 *kos* on this side of the water and nearly a hundred *kos* on the far side of the river; the words for the nearer bank are uncertain in the printed text (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 51).

Shaikh Mir, Diler Khan and Qubad Khan then crossed toward Sukkur (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 51). On 5 Safar Shaikh Mir reached a place ten *kos* from Sukkur whose name cannot be read in the printed text; because of the thick forest, the dense thorny trees and the hard stages of the way, many beasts perished and the baggage did not arrive, and on the sixth they reached Sukkur (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 51).

Shaikh Mir stayed one day at Sukkur, and the *zamindars* told him that 25 *kos* from Sukkur a road branches off toward Qandahar (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 50). Saf Shikan Khan appointed an officer, whose name is uncertain in the printed text, to the *faujdari* of Sukkur (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 50).

In the twentieth century Michel Boivin's Sukkur is a city of riots. He writes of the 'Sukkur riots', which he dates "between December of 1939 and February of 1940", and whose first victim was Bhagat Kanwar Ram (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 61, folio 48). In the same passage he writes that "the local Hindus opposed this rehabilitation since it was located in the harbour on the Indus River" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 61, folio 48). Rohri too he names among the cities of the riots (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 61, folio 48).

Boivin also uses Sukkur as a measure for Sehwan. Sukkur, following the Manzilgah riots, is his example of the other cities of northern Sindh, against which he sets Sehwan, where "no communal violence had ever been registered" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 71, folio 58). In the same passage he writes that Fateh Muhammad Sehwani, who lived from 1882 to 1942, a man of letters of Sehwan, "himself a Muslim who was involved in the Manzilgah Affair, advocated the Hindus' claim" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 71, folio 58).

## Holy Places

Schaflechner names Sukkur as the city of the Sadh Bela island *tirtha*, "in the city of Sukkur on an island in the River Indus" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 138, folio not stated). Boivin, who writes the name Sadh Belo, tells the origin of the island shrine (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, pp. 68–69).

Boivin places shrines at Rohri as well. He locates the "original sanctuary" of Sain Vasan Shah there (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 68, folio 55), and writes that "Vasan Shah's darbar is located in Ulhasnagar, while another one is in Rohri, on the bank of the Indus River" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 209, folio 196). He also places the shrine of Isarlal at Rohri (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 150, folio 137), and he speaks of "Rohri dham" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 78, folio 65). Kandri, he writes, "is a small town located in Rohri taluqa", in the district of Khairpur, which the printed text gives as Khaipur (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 107, folio 94).

## Communities

Boivin sets Sukkur, Rohri and Shikarpur as the corners of one triangle (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 31, folio 18), and he writes of the towns that "Still another experience was to be born in Shikarpur or in Rohri, which was another story entirely" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 30, folio 17).

Rohri is the birthplace of the figures Boivin follows most closely there. Rochaldas was "born in 1879 in Rohri, in the north of Sindh, to a family that his hagiographers call modest", and his eldest brother "became chief officer of the municipality of Rohri" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 55, folio 42). Boivin relates that Rochaldas found himself "wandering high in the sky above the port on the Indus at Rohri" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 57, folio 44). Bedil, in Boivin's words, was "a Sufi born in Rohri", and Rochaldas was "born in Rohri, like Bedil, but seven years after his demise" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, pp. 97–98, folios 84–85).

Sukkur gives Boivin a woman of his account: Dadi Dhan "was born in Sukkur, Sindh, and her father was a famous lawyer" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 115, folio 102). He also mentions "an orphanage in Sukkur" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 104, folio 91). Schaflechner names Rohri once, in his survey of Hindu settlement, and attaches no shrine to it (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 94, folio not stated).

## Poets and Singers

Schimmel gives Rohri as the home of two mystic poets, roughly a century apart. Of the first she writes that "Bedil of Rohri (d. 1872) should be mentioned as a prolific, though not very outstanding, writer in five languages", and she does not name the five languages (Schimmel, *Mystical Dimensions of Islam*, p. 427, folio 306 as printed). The second is the Suhrawardi mystic Janullah of Rohri, "one of the outstanding Persian poets of Sind from the second half of the eighteenth century", whom she names among those who composed songs on the martyrdom of Shah Inayat (Schimmel, *Mystical Dimensions of Islam*, p. 428, folio 397).

Abbas places a singer on the routes out of Rohri. Alan Faqir, she writes, "is reported to have sung on the buses and trains between Rohri and Sehwan Sharif in his youth. He also sang at the dargahs (shrines) in the Sind", and he "confirmed his roving-minstrel lineage" in his interview with her (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 78, folio not stated). She names no shrine at Rohri, and gives no date or event there (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 78, folio not stated).

## The Place in the Books

Bhandari's chronicle names Sukkur only in its account of the pursuit of Dara, as a halt, a river crossing, a place on the road to Qandahar and the seat of a *faujdari*, and says nothing there of any shrine, temple or holy man (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 51; Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 50).

Harish Dhillon's retelling of the *janamsakhis* names the town as a stage on a journey: from Multan the travellers went "to Sukkur from where he turned southwards along the river Indus" (Dhillon, *Janamsakhis*, p. 194, folio 214). Schaflechner names it for the island *tirtha* of Sadh Bela (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 138, folio not stated).

Schimmel says nothing of Rohri itself; it is for her the home of two poets (Schimmel, *Mystical Dimensions of Islam*, p. 427, folio 306 as printed; Schimmel, *Mystical Dimensions of Islam*, p. 428, folio 397). For Abbas it is one end of a singer's route to Sehwan Sharif (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 78, folio not stated).

Boivin's book gives the two towns the most room. His Sukkur is a city of northern Sindh, a city of riots and the birthplace of Dadi Dhan (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 71, folio 58; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 61, folio 48; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 115, folio 102). His Rohri is a port on the Indus, the birthplace of Rochaldas and of Bedil, and the seat of shrines of Sain Vasan Shah and of Isarlal (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 57, folio 44; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, pp. 97–98, folios 84–85; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 209, folio 196; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 150, folio 137).
