## Framing

- **The one statement.** The sentence gives the bold line's content, "named once, as the provenance of one manuscript among several", in its own words, and the book's four words "one other Dera Ghazi Khan" as the words in which the place is named. The bold line is the only statement of what those four words mean, so the article says exactly that much and no more.
- **"Named once".** Kept in the team version as the bold line's statement about the book ("is named once in Carl W. Ernst and Bruce B. Lawrence's study"). It is a statement about the book, so the public version drops it as source talk.
- **The manuscript.** Nothing is said of what work it holds, its date, its language, its scribe, where it is kept now, or where the other manuscripts come from: the material gives none of these. "One manuscript among several" is the bold line's phrase, copied.
- **The page reference.** "p. 239, folio 229", copied character for character from the block. I did not infer from the Source line which part of the book p. 239 falls in (the Source line gives p. 234 as folio 224 "in the Persian bibliography", which might suggest a bibliography or list of manuscripts near p. 239, but the material does not say so). Nothing about the passage's place in the book is in the prose.
- **The study.** Called "Carl W. Ernst and Bruce B. Lawrence's study", with no description of its subject. An earlier draft said "study of Chishti Sufism" (from the bibliography's subtitle, as mithankot describes Rozehnal's book); I took it out because, set beside "one manuscript", it invites the reader to take the manuscript for a Chishti text, which the material does not say. Removing it also brings the public version's length within the checker's range honestly (see the public notes).
- **The quotation.** "one other Dera Ghazi Khan" is four words, quoted verbatim in double quotes; under the checker's six word threshold, so Q2 does not test it, but it is copied exactly from the block.
