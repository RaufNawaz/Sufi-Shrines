## Framing choices

- **"Princely state" and "emirate".** Both are Boivin's words on p. 26 ("the Princely States, including the Khairpur emirate in Sindh"). The Overview says "an emirate in Sindh, one of the princely states". Nothing is said of when the emirate existed, who founded it, or when the Kayasht came, since the passage does not say.
- **The amirs (p. 31, folio 18).** Quoted in full in the body, so that "themselves" and "it did not prevent them" stand as Boivin wrote them. The Overview paraphrases it ("were nonetheless generous patrons of all the religious buildings"), keeping the book's "all of the religious buildings" without saying whose buildings or where, since the quotation does not say. The body's second sentence ("the amirs' own Shia faith did not narrow their patronage") restates the "but it did not prevent them" clause and carries the same citation.
- **The imambaras are not named or located**, following the block's own remark ("none of those imambaras is named or located: (not stated)"). I did not turn that remark into a sentence ("he names none of them"), since it is the note taker's, not the book's.
- **"Two lineages of northern Sindh"** for the Darazi silsila and the Jalalani tariqa: both passages place them "in Northern Sindh", and silsila and tariqa are the book's own words. I did not call them Sufi orders, and did not say what either is, who founded it, or where exactly its seat is, since the block does not. Only the Jalalani passage mentions a dargah, and only that one is called a dargah.
- **Daraza and the Darazi silsila** are given in separate sentences. The names suggest a link, but the block does not make it, so I did not join them.

## Weak material and how I treated it

All six locating passages are weak in the brief's sense: each places another place near Khairpur and says nothing more of the town.

- **Darazi silsila (p. 93, folio 80), Jalalani tariqa's dargah (p. 122, folio 109), Daraza (p. 150, folio 137).** Kept, in the first paragraph of The Landscape Around the Town and in the Overview, as what shows the religious landscape around the town, each in Boivin's own words and with no addition.
- **Halani (p. 68, folio 55), Kandri (p. 107, folio 94), Hingorja (p. 169, folio 156).** The block does not say these are religious sites, so they are stated as what they are: "Elsewhere the town serves him only as a point of reference: ...". Halani is quoted as lying "between Nausharo Firuz and Khairpur"; Kandri is placed in the district; Hingorja is given as Bhagwan K. Ajwani's birthplace, without saying who Ajwani is, since the block does not. These three are candidates for cutting in the public version.
