## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Bhandari, the whole block: a spelling only identification.** Stated every time as such: "a pargana whose name it spells as Eminabad is spelt, though nothing but the spelling ties that pargana to this town"; "if its place of that spelling is Eminabad"; in the disagreement, "though only the spelling links the chronicle's place to Eminabad". The Persian name is not romanised in the prose (Aminabad or Amanabad would be my choice, not the book's), so the article never writes a romanised form of امن آباد.
- **Bhandari, p. 177, the halting stage.** A transit mention. Stated as what it is, in The Place in the Books: a halting stage on a royal journey where an impostor was brought before the emperor and punished.
- **Bhandari, pp. 515 and 516, Shah Daula's bridge and the Deg.** Directional mentions. Stated as directional, in The Place in the Books: the bridge lies five *karoh* from the place towards Lahore; the Deg passes the villages of four parganas, this one among them. Shah Daula's bridge is named because it helps the reader see the landscape; nothing more is said of Shah Daula.
- **Khalid, p. 191, folio 165, "After our visit to Eminabad".** A transit; kept as the record of the author's visit, with Iqbal Qaiser, in The Place in the Books.
- **Khalid, p. 15, folio not stated, Babur destroyed "the cities of Eminabad and Lahore".** A bare mention, but a statement about the town's history; kept as Khalid's words in History.
- **Khalid, p. 88, folio 70, Sikh communities.** The block says only that Eminabad "is listed among cities with Sikh communities"; the article says exactly that, keeps the uncertain printed name in plain words, and adds no claim that Sikhs live in Eminabad today beyond Khalid's listing (question 3).
- **Khalid, p. 115, "top of page badly damaged".** The decline of trade sentence is paraphrased (the quotation carries bracketed letters) and the article adds "though the page is badly damaged at that point".
- **Signpost sentences without a citation**, each introducing the cited sentence after it and adding no fact: "In Khalid's account the town's fortunes followed the road"; "Khalid's picture of Saidpur in Nanak's day is his own reconstruction"; "Of the present town Khalid writes as a visitor"; "The town Khalid visited had a different people"; "Sujan Rai Bhandari's chronicle records the setting up of a pargana whose name it spells as Eminabad is spelt, though nothing but the spelling ties that pargana to this town" (its second sentence carries the citation).

## Disagreements

One listed:
- **How the town came by its name.** Khalid: Babur took Saidpur and renamed it Eminabad (p. 115, folio 97), and the renaming was in honour of Eni, who fed Babur (p. 116, folio 98). These two are Khalid's and compatible with each other. Bhandari: a town and pargana of that name were established by royal order by Muhammad Amin, a *karori*, in the reign of an emperor styled lord of the world (p. 176). Listed with the identification caveat on the chronicle's side, and no verdict.

Considered and not listed:
- **Bhandari's two lines on p. 176** ("a few years earlier the pargana was established out of Gujrat" and "Muhammad Amin ... established the town and pargana"). They may be one event told twice, or two; the block's own doubt says only "how this squares with the previous line". Given side by side in History, not set up as a disagreement (question 2).
- **Khalid, p. 15 against p. 115**: Babur destroyed "Eminabad" though he renamed Saidpur Eminabad only when he took it. A use of the later name, not a conflict.

## Questions for Rauf

1. **The chronicle's identification.** Every Bhandari line rests on the spelling امن آباد alone. I kept them, hedged each time, because the naming by Muhammad Amin is the one passage that bears on the town's history and conflicts with Khalid. Keep, or drop the chronicle from this article altogether?
2. **Bhandari p. 176's two lines.** The Doubts the block points to are not in the sources file. Should the two lines be listed as an internal disagreement, or stay side by side in History as now?
3. **Khalid p. 88, Sikh communities.** The listing of Eminabad among cities with Sikh communities rests on a damaged printed name ("Eminabyg"). Keep as now, or drop?
4. **Romanisation.** The article never romanises امن آباد; it uses Eminabad (Khalid's spelling) and "that name". The public and Urdu versions may want a decision on whether the chronicle's name is Aminabad, as Muhammad Amin's name suggests.
