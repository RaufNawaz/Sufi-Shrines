## Overview

Jürgen Schaflechner writes of "the Hingol National Park, in which the shrine is located", the shrine being Hinglaj's (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 295, folio not stated). Michel Boivin calls Hinglaj "a pilgrimage centre located in the Sindhi-speaking area of Baluchistan", in a sentence reporting a claim of Parsram's (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 50, folio 37). Schaflechner writes of the nearby district of Las Bela that there the Tumbel spread the Goddess's fame, and from there came the Lohanas who have chiefly made up the body he calls the HSM (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 71, folio not stated; Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 82, folio not stated). Boivin writes that Shah Abdul Latif probably travelled to Hinglaj on pilgrimage, and that Sachal Sarmast's poetry names it among the sacred Hindu places (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 92, folio 79; Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 106, folio 93).

## The Park and the District

For Schaflechner the park is the setting of the shrine: he speaks of "the Hingol National Park, in which the shrine is located" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 295, folio not stated). He also names, in passing, "the entry to the Hingol National Park", without describing it further (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 152, folio not stated).

In his account the neighbouring district is Las Bela, "a district close to Hinglaj" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 79, folio not stated).

## Communities

Schaflechner names Las Bela as a place of the Goddess's renown. In his words, "the Tumbel spread the Goddess's fame in Las Bela" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 71, folio not stated).

Las Bela is also where the body he calls the HSM began. Schaflechner writes that the HSM was "created on January 5, 1986, in Bela (Las Bela district), Balochistan", and that it "has since its inception consisted mainly of Lohanas from the district of Las Bela" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 82, folio not stated). He also names one family of them, "the Dewanis, a Sindhi Lohana family from Las Bela" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 274, folio not stated).

## Pilgrimage and Poetry

Boivin knows Hinglaj as a goal of pilgrimage. Writing of Shah Abdul Latif and the Nathpanthi jogis, he says: "He probably travelled with them on a number of Hindu pilgrimages, including Dwarka and Hinglaj." (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 92, folio 79). The hedge is his own: he does not say that the journey certainly took place.

Hinglaj appears in poetry too. Of Sachal Sarmast's poetry Boivin writes that "There are also a number of sacred Hindu places, such as Hinglaj, Kasi, or Kashi (Banaras), Sri Lanka and the Ganga" (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 106, folio 93).

The site also enters a Theosophical account. Boivin reports that "Parsram claimed that the universal wisdom is kept in Sindh, in Hinglaj to be exact, which is a pilgrimage centre located in the Sindhi-speaking area of Baluchistan." (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 50, folio 37). The claim is Parsram's. It belongs, in Boivin's telling, to a narrative of a universal and primordial wisdom through which humanity could reach immortality, a wisdom lost many centuries ago except in one remote area, usually located in Tibet by a writer whose printed name, Blavatsly, is possibly a misprint for Blavatsky (Boivin, *Hindu Sufis of South Asia*, p. 50, folio 37).
