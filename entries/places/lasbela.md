## Overview

Jürgen Schaflechner, in his study of the Hinglaj temple, describes Las Bela as "a district close to Hinglaj" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 79, folio not stated). He writes that the Tumbel spread the Goddess's fame there, and that the body he calls the HSM was created at Bela in 1986 and has consisted mainly of Lohanas from the district (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 71, folio not stated; Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 82, folio not stated).

## Las Bela and Hinglaj

In Schaflechner's account Las Bela is "a district close to Hinglaj" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 79, folio not stated).

The shrine itself he sets in a park: he speaks of "the Hingol National Park, in which the shrine is located" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 295, folio not stated). He also names, in passing, "the entry to the Hingol National Park", without describing it further (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 152, folio not stated). He does not say in these lines whether the park lies within the district of Las Bela (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 79, folio not stated; Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 295, folio not stated).

## Communities

Schaflechner names Las Bela as a place of the Goddess's renown. In his words, "the Tumbel spread the Goddess's fame in Las Bela" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 71, folio not stated).

Las Bela is also where the body he calls the HSM began. Schaflechner writes that the HSM was "created on January 5, 1986, in Bela (Las Bela district), Balochistan", and that it "has since its inception consisted mainly of Lohanas from the district of Las Bela" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 82, folio not stated).

He also names one Lohana family of the district, "the Dewanis, a Sindhi Lohana family from Las Bela" (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, p. 274, folio not stated).
