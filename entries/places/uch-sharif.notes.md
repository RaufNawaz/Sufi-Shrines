## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Bhandari's campaign, transit and boundary mentions** (pp. 285, 333, 405, 527, 529). The block marks them "Campaign, transit and boundary mentions only". They are kept, because they are the only pre-modern political history of the town in the material. Each one is given as what it is: a campaign that took Uch, a pursuit as far as Uch, a march by way of Uch, and a provincial boundary. None is made into more. The p. 285 march is hedged with "apparently", as the block hedges it.
- **Khalid's "Named on the caravanserai route" (p. 119, folio 101).** This is a bare mention. It is given as exactly that in The Place in the Books ("named, and no more, on the caravanserai route").
- **Khalid's drive "through Bahawalpur" (p. 276).** This is a transit. It is kept only because the same sentence carries "the cultures of Punjab and Sindh merge" and the Siraiki remark.
- **Rizvi's "Uch as an economic statement in the Conclusion" (p. 421, folio not stated).** The block does not say what the statement is. The article says only that the conclusion "returns to Uch ... where it figures in a statement about the economy". You may prefer to drop it (question 4).
- **Rizvi's founding legend (p. 129, folio 111).** The block's grammar does not make clear who gave the order to mount the camel, whether Safiuʾd-Din Kaziruni or someone acting on his word. The article says the story was "told in connection with Shaikh Safiuʾd-Din Kaziruni" and names neither the giver nor the receiver of the order. Rizvi's own verdict ("Although this story would appear mythical...") is kept beside it. It has no separate page in the block, so it carries the p. 129, folio 111 reference of the passage it judges.
- **Rizvi's flying contest with the yogi (pp. 129–130, folios 111–112).** It is not joined to the camel story. The article says only that he "tells of" it.

- **Chishti, p. 526, the tomb passage.** The block marks it "very garbled" and the confiscation "garbled". ## Where the Sources Disagree (as in the article)

1. Direction from Multan: Schimmel puts it "northeast of Multan" (p. 385, folio 354), and Rozehnal "seventy miles southwest of Multan" (p. 210, folio 202). Rizvi says only "near Multan" (p. 258, folio 238).
2. When the Qadiriyya came: Schimmel gives the late fifteenth century, at Ucch (p. 385, folio 354), and elsewhere the late fourteenth century, in the Subcontinent, with no place named (p. 278, folio 247). The block itself says "The two are kept side by side." Rozehnal gives no date but names the founder of the first Qadiriyya *khanaqah* at Uch (p. 259, folio 252).
3. The rivers: Rozehnal saw the town on a hill "overlooking the confluence of the Sutlej and Chenab rivers" (p. 21, folio 10). Khalid writes that the Sutlej "used to flow from here before it was lost forever" after 1960 (pp. 281–282, folios 255–256).

Not listed as disagreements:
- The two Qadiri founding accounts, Schimmel's unnamed missionaries and Rozehnal's Shaykh Muhammad al-Husseini al-Jilani, do not contradict each other. Rozehnal is put inside bullet 2 as context only.
- Rizvi's legend of a town founded where a camel halted, against Rozehnal's "ancient city" and Bhandari's Uch taken from a people in Shihab al-Din's campaign. None of the three dates the founding, so they cannot be set against each other.
- The spellings Ucch (Schimmel), Utch (Boivin), Uchh (Kasmani), Uch, Uch city and Uch Gilanian (Abbas), Uch Sharif Sayyidan (Chishti) and *qasba* of Uch (Bhandari) are recorded in The Place in the Books as each book's usage.
- The two copies of the Chishti block differ only in letter forms (پہر / پھر, نہین / نہیں, مین / میں). None of these is quoted.

## Questions for Rauf

1. **The Gilani ancestor in Chishti (pp. 525, 526).** On p. 525 the ancestor comes *from* Uch Sharif in Akbar's reign. On p. 526 he comes *to* Multan, marries Fatima and is given the territory of Uch, where his descendants still live. These may be two generations, or two moves, or a contradiction inside the book. The article gives them in sequence without joining or opposing them. Should they be listed as a disagreement?
2. **Baha ad-Din Zakaria "of Uch Sharif" (Rozehnal, p. 57).** The block notes that this is "against the archive, which places him at Multan". No book in Uch's material says Multan, so the article gives Rozehnal's wording and no disagreement. The public version could keep it, or drop it as a likely slip of the author's.
3. **Abd al-Qadir Sani (Chishti, p. 527) and 'Abd al-Qadir Jilani Thani (Rozehnal, p. 210).** Hazrat Muhammad Ghaus and Shah Muhammad Ghaus (Chishti) and Shaykh Muhammad al-Husseini al-Jilani (Rozehnal) look like the same Qadiri family, but the material never equates them, so the article keeps them apart. May the public version join them?
4. **Rizvi's conclusion (p. 421, folio not stated).** The block says only "Uch as an economic statement in the Conclusion". Keep the one vague sentence, or drop it?
5. **My English for Bhandari's boundary passage (p. 529),** which has no gloss in the source: "high mountains of hard rock" for کوههائے خارا بلند and "hills of sand" for کوههائے ریگ. Please confirm or correct.
6. **Spelling.** The article writes Uch Sharif in its own voice, Bulle Shah as Abbas prints it, and Sayyid in its own voice, with Syed only inside Abbas's quotations and paraphrase. Should the public version use the glossary's Bulleh Shah?
