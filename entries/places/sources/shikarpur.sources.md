## From Michel Boivin, Hindu Sufis of South Asia

### Place-level material: Shikarpur

*Attached to 3 row(s): gurdwara-sach-khand-sahib, khatwari-darbar-shikarpur, sant-bhagat-kanwar-ram-temple-chak*

*The book's place-level material on "Shikarpur" (3 rows), from its list of rows attached at place or person level, copied into every existing entry that list names (ruling (a); Rauf, 10 October 2026). It is about the place in general, not about this shrine alone.*

- **gurdwara-sach-khand-sahib**, **khatwari-darbar-shikarpur**, **sant-bhagat-kanwar-ram-temple-chak**
  (the book's "Shikarpur") — **place-level.** "Shikarpur, the northern city of Sindh where the Northern
  dialect of Sindhi known as **Utradi or Siraiki** was spoken, was a hub of international commerce at
  this time. It was the core of merchant networks that allowed them to control commerce between Central
  Asia and overseas countries. Here, the **baniyas** were the dominant group." (p. 30, folio 17); one
  corner of "the triangle of Rohri-Sukkur-Shikarpur, on both sides of the Indus River, [which] was the
  locale for an amazingly rich dialogue between different creeds" (p. 31, folio 18); Nimano Faqir "born
  into a rich Hindu family of Shikarpur" (p. 60, folio 47); Sami "born in a well-to-do family in
  Shikarpur. He was himself a cloth merchant and travelled a great deal between Shikarpur and Amritsar in
  Punjab." (p. 94, folio 81); and a further bare occurrence at p. 214 (folio 201).
