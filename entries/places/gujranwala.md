## Overview

Haroon Khalid writes that Ranjit Singh started from a small fief around Gujranwala, which he places seventy five kilometres from the provincial capital (Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 207, folio not stated). In *Walking with Nanak* he records that Ranjit Singh allotted Baba Sahib Singh Bedi land around Gujranwala and Lahore (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 44, folio 26), and counts Gujranwala among the cities whose Sikh communities have newly become assertive (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 88, folio 70). He writes that the city has replaced Eminabad as an important trading centre (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 115, folio 97), and that the businessmen of Sialkot travel often between Lahore, Gujranwala and Sialkot (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 191, folio 165). Shemeem Burney Abbas names Gujranwala, with Faisalabad and Sahiwal, as the heart of the Punjab (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 24, folio not stated).

## History

Both of Khalid's books tie Gujranwala to Ranjit Singh. In *A White Trail* he writes that Ranjit Singh started "from a small fief around Gujranwala, a city seventy-five kilometres away from the provincial capital" (Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 207, folio not stated). In *Walking with Nanak* he records that Ranjit Singh allotted Baba Sahib Singh Bedi land "around the cities of Gujranwala and Lahore" (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 44, folio 26).

## Communities

*Walking with Nanak* includes Gujranwala, printed there as "Gujranawala", in a list of cities whose Sikh communities have newly become assertive (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 88, folio 70).

## A City of Trade

Khalid writes that Gujranwala "has replaced Eminabad as an important trading centre" (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 115, folio 97). Of the businessmen of Sialkot he writes that they "travel frequently between the three important cities of Lahore", Gujranwala and Sialkot; the printed name of Gujranwala in that sentence is unclear (Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 191, folio 165).

## The Place in the Books

For Khalid, Gujranwala is a city he measures by its distance from the provincial capital, and one of three important cities, with Lahore and Sialkot, between which the businessmen of Sialkot travel (Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 207, folio not stated; Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 191, folio 165).

Abbas names the city once, placing it in her picture of the Punjab: "Gujranwala, Faisalabad, and Sahiwal, which are the heart of the Panjab" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 24, folio not stated).
