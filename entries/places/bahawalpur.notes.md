## Where I drew the line on Uch

The brief asks for Uch only where a book sets it within Bahawalpur. Only Abbas does so, at p. 107 ("Uch Gilanian, the city of saints in Bahawalpur"), and that sentence is used once, in Saints, Syeds and Patrons, and recalled in the Overview and The Place in the Books.

Left out as Uch's own account, not Bahawalpur's:
- Rozehnal: "The town as the author saw it in April 1996" (p. 21, folio 10), "Its location, given twice" (p. 210, folio 202), "Uch as a Suhrawardi and Qadiri foundation" (pp. 57, 259, 210), and the index headword. The block itself says "The book never names Uch in a Bahawalpur context", so none of Rozehnal's Uch material is set in Bahawalpur here. The one exception is the imprint of Shahab's *Khitta-i pak Uch* ("Bahawalpur: Urdu Academy, 1967/1993", p. 259, folio 252), which is about Bahawalpur as a place of publication and is stated as exactly that (question 2).
- Abbas: "Thou art an inhabitant of Uch city" and "Bulle Shah's mentor Inayat Shah, he--he belonged to Uch city" (p. 104), and Muhammad Bakhsh's "We came with Bulle Shah's family from Uch Sharif" (p. 112). Abbas does not set either passage in Bahawalpur; both are in the Uch Sharif article.

## Left out, and why

- Abbas: "it recurs as a bare place-name at p. 188 (folio not stated)". A bare mention.
- Abbas: the block's "No archive row carries 'Bahawalpur' as its town, only as its district" and Rozehnal's "this attachment is purely administrative; weigh it lightly". Remarks about how the material was attached, not about the place. For Bahawalpur itself Rozehnal's Bahawalpur material is direct (the city by name), so it is used in full; the "weigh it lightly" concerned attaching it to Uch rows.
- Khalid, *A White Trail*: "the protocol text says 'Bahawalpur 5'; the Uch sites are not mentioned". Apparatus.
- Rozehnal: "the archive's own placement is Bahawalpur District". This is our placement, not the book's; not stated.

## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 276, folio 250, the drive "through Bahawalpur".** A transit, but the sentence itself is about the place ("The boundary between Sindh and Punjab becomes a blur"), so it is kept as Khalid's observation from the road.
- **Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*, p. 284, folio 258, Ayesha Siddiqa "belongs to Bahawalpur, which is only about seventy kilometres from here".** The block does not say where "here" is, nor who Ayesha Siddiqa is. The article quotes the line in The Place in the Books and does not resolve "here" or describe her.
- **Rozehnal, fieldwork base and interview dates** (p. 26, folio 15; pp. 79, 80, 93, 174, 191, 220, folios 69, 70, 83, 166, 183, 212). Stated as what they are, in The Place in the Books: a base for fieldwork, interviews dated 28, 29 and 30 April and 2 May 2001. The block does not say whom he interviewed, so the article does not.
- **Rozehnal, the Nawab** (pp. 72, 74, 87, 88, folios 62, 64, 77, 78). The block says the Nawab is "named as a patron of Wahid Bakhsh's commission, schooling and Civil Secretariat post". The article says exactly that and adds nothing about who Wahid Bakhsh was or which Nawab, since the block does not say.
- **Rozehnal, Allahabad** (p. 21, folio 10). The quotation in the source carries two em dashes ("Allahabad—sixty miles southwest of the city of Bahawalpur—in southern Punjab"), so it is paraphrased, not quoted.
- **Shahab's *Khitta-i pak Uch*** (p. 259, folio 252). A place of publication: given as "with the imprint Bahawalpur: Urdu Academy, 1967/1993", the slash kept as printed rather than read as two editions.
- **Abbas, p. 107.** As in the Uch Sharif article, the man "whose original name was Abdullah Shah" is not identified; the article quotes only "who had settled in Uch Gilanian, the city of saints in Bahawalpur, where Bulle Shah was born", attached to "a family of Syeds".
- **Abbas, pp. 168–169.** Given as she has it: Bahawalpur, with Multan, the seat of the landowning Syeds who "support the shrines of their ancestors". "Support" is kept; the article does not turn it into custodianship.
- **Signpost sentences without a citation**, each introducing the cited sentence after it and adding no fact: "Khalid finds Bahawalpur on the edge of two provinces"; "The one holy place the books set expressly in Bahawalpur is Uch Gilanian" (a statement about this material, true of all four books); "In Abbas's account the Syeds also sustain shrines"; "A Nawab of Bahawalpur appears as a patron too"; "Khalid's *A White Trail* knows Bahawalpur through its Hindus"; "Boys from Bahawalpur came to the festivals as well"; "Its Hindus went on pilgrimage too"; "Each book sees Bahawalpur from a different road"; "Rozehnal knows the city as a scholar at work".

## Questions for Rauf

1. **The Navratri chant (Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 37).** May the article quote 'Ek do teen char, Mata Teri jai jai kar' as printed (it is romanised, in an English book), or should it stay as "chanting to the Mata"? If quoted, it needs an English rendering from someone who can vouch for it; the source gives none.
2. **Shahab's *Khitta-i pak Uch*, "Bahawalpur: Urdu Academy".** A place of publication. I kept it as the one sign in the material of a learned institution in the city. Keep, or drop as an imprint?
3. **Abbas's Uch passages at pp. 104 and 112** (Uch city; the qawwal family that came with Bulle Shah's family from Uch Sharif to Kasur). Abbas does not set these in Bahawalpur, so they stay in the Uch Sharif article only. Should the Bahawalpur article carry a sentence on them, as Uch is in Bahawalpur District?
4. **Spelling.** The article writes Syeds (Abbas's usage, as the only book that speaks of them here) and Bulle Shah inside Abbas's quotation. The public version may want Sayyid and the glossary's Bulleh Shah.
