## Overview

Shemeem Burney Abbas gives Jhang as the land of the Siyal Jats, the family of Hir, whose father Cucak Siyal she calls "the powerful Jat chief of Jhang Siyal" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, pp. 121–122, folios not stated). She describes Jhang Siyal as "a vast agricultural territory situated to the southwest of Lahore in Pakistan" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, pp. 121–122, folios not stated). Hir's story is, in her words, a myth "several hundred years old", and yet "villagers in the district of Jhang do identify Hir's tomb to this day" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 205, folio not stated).

## Hir and the Siyal Jats

Abbas, who calls the story of Hir a myth (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 205, folio not stated), opens her account of Hir with the place: "Hir was the beautiful and witty daughter of Cucak Siyal, the powerful Jat chief of Jhang Siyal, which is a vast agricultural territory situated to the southwest of Lahore in Pakistan" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, pp. 121–122, folios not stated). In the same account she writes that "Hir belonged to the family of the powerful Siyal Jats of Jhang" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 121, folio not stated).

The glossary of her book gives Siyal as the "Clan name of Hir's family; powerful landowners of the Panjab" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 188, folio not stated). Of the Siyal she also writes: "The Siyal tribe is more than usually considerate toward its women; it is one of the few tribes in the Panjab that allow women to inherit property under customary law" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 121, folio not stated).

## Hir's Tomb

Abbas sets the age of the story beside a tomb that is still pointed out: "Although the myth is several hundred years old, villagers in the district of Jhang do identify Hir's tomb to this day" (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 205, folio not stated). The tomb is given only as the one the villagers identify; the passage says nothing of where in the district it stands, of its building, or of any rite held at it (Abbas, *The Female Voice in Sufi Ritual*, p. 205, folio not stated).
