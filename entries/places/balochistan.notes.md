## The unit

Every block names whose "Balochistan" it is, and the article says so: Bhandari's Baluchistan is a wilayat paired with Multan in a jagir, with chiefs of its own, "not called a suba, extent not stated" (the article says the chronicle does not call it a suba and gives no account of its extent); Sorley's is the hill country seen from Sindh in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries; Schaflechner's, Rozehnal's and Khalid's is the modern province; Eaton's is a desert a traveller crosses. Nothing is mapped onto the modern province's borders.

## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Sorley quoting Dames (p. 255, folio 246).** The block warns: "Dames's 'parched-up land' is not named in the quotation; the subject is the Baluchis' poetry, not a bounded province." The article frames the quotation as Dames, quoted by Sorley, "on the poetry of the Baluchis", and says the poetry "is described in the same pages" as wholly oral, without saying it is the province's poetry.
- **Schimmel, *Pain and Grace* (p. 161, folio 161).** Given as what it is: Schimmel lists the hill slopes of Balochistan among the parts of the country where Sindh's folk tales are located. Not used as a claim about the province's extent.
- **Abro's "real Sindh" (Schaflechner, p. 94).** Given as Abro's claim, quoted by Schaflechner, about an ancient past, never as a fact.
- **Abbas on the Brahui (p. 156).** Kept conditional ("Assuming that ..."); "Sassi is said to have been a ruler" kept as said.
- **Khalid's sermon (p. 7, ch. 1).** Given as Khalid's rendering of the kind of sermon a local maulvi preached, not as anyone's claim about Balochistan.
- **Wahid Bakhsh (Rozehnal, p. 127).** Given as Wahid Bakhsh's words in Rozehnal's translation.
- **Schaflechner on insurgency (p. 169).** Given with "citing Axmann", as the block does.
- **Mekranis near the poet (Sorley, p. 255).** Sorley's "must have been" kept as "reckons that ... there must have been". The poet is left as "the poet", unnamed, as the block leaves him.

## Where the Sources Disagree (as in the article)

1. **What guāṭi is.** Abbas's text: the Balochistan name for healing ceremonies for emotionally disturbed women (p. 66); her glossary: "A ritual for making vows at Sufi shrines in Baluchistan" (p. 182). The block keeps the two side by side; so does the article, and the Overview gives both.
2. **Where Sindh ends.** Khalid: the Makran range is "the boundary line between Sindh and Baluchistan" (*Walking with Nanak*, p. 228, folio 202); Abro, as Schaflechner quotes him: the "real Sindh" runs to "the valleys between Rohri and east Balochistan" (p. 94). A judgement call (question 2).

Considered and not listed (judged compatible): Rizvi's 710 advance through Makran and Baluchistan beside Abbas's Sufis coming "After his conquest" (no year); Boivin's Sindhi speaking part of Baluchistan beside Khalid's boundary; Schimmel's folk tale geography (*Pain and Grace*) beside Khalid's boundary (a geography of tales, not of territory; it was in an early draft of item 2 and taken out).

## Questions for Rauf

1. **"The MMA" (Rozehnal, p. 32, folio 22).** The source never expands the abbreviation, so the article cannot. A reader will not know what it is. Leave it, or should the coordinator take its expansion from Rozehnal's book itself?
2. **"Where Sindh ends."** Khalid's present day boundary and Abro's claim about an ancient "real Sindh" are different kinds of statement. I listed them side by side because they draw the edge differently; if you would rather keep only true contradictions in that section, it should go, leaving one item.
3. **Kalhoro.** The printed text reads "Kalh6ro"; the article reads it as Kalhoro without saying so. Is that acceptable, or should it say the printed name is damaged?
4. **Murids.** Burton's "mountaineer Murids" is quoted without a gloss, since the source gives none. A gloss (disciples) would help a reader; may the article give one?
5. **En dashes in two page references** (above). They follow the exact copy rule and the Multan model; say if the no dash rule should win there instead.
