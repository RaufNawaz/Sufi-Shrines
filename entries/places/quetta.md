## Overview

Haroon Khalid writes that after the Babri Mosque demolition "Hindu communities in Sindh and Quetta were attacked" (Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 22, folio not stated). In *In Search of Shiva* he recalls "the attack on Hazara Shias in Quetta which resulted in the death of eighty-four people", and the nationwide protests that followed it (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 15, ch. 9, folio not stated). Jürgen Schaflechner's study of the Hinglaj temple names Quetta in its survey of Hindu settlement in Pakistan (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, pp. 16, 83, 85, 202, folios not stated).

## Communities

In *A White Trail* Khalid counts Quetta among the places where Hindus were attacked after the demolition of the Babri Mosque: "Hindu communities in Sindh and Quetta were attacked" (Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 22, folio not stated). He states this only in general terms, naming Quetta beside Sindh, and gives no account of what happened in the city (Khalid, *A White Trail*, p. 22, folio not stated).

The Hazara Shias of Quetta appear in *In Search of Shiva*. Khalid writes of "the attack on Hazara Shias in Quetta which resulted in the death of eighty-four people", and adds that "there were nationwide protests with calls for action against the culprits, Lashkare-e-Jhangvi, a Sunni extremist organization" (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 15, ch. 9, folio not stated).

## The Place in the Books

Khalid mentions the attack on the Hazara Shias in passing, to mark the time of a visit he made elsewhere, which fell "two days after the attack on Hazara Shias in Quetta" (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 15, ch. 9, folio not stated).

Schaflechner, whose book is about the temple of Hinglaj Devi, names Quetta in its survey of Hindu settlement in Pakistan, on four pages (Schaflechner, *Hinglaj Devi*, pp. 16, 83, 85, 202, folios not stated).
