The material is thin. Most of it is about the city as the capital, as a place of publication, or as the home or fieldwork base of an author. The books name only two religious sites in or by the city (Barri Imam's shrine at Nurpur Shahan, and Pir Mehr Ali Shah's Golra), each in a single sentence of *Embodying Charisma*, and no temple, gurdwara or church inside it. The article is accordingly short (about 2,450 words) and is not padded.

Dashes: there are no em dashes. The only en dashes are inside two page references that the source prints as ranges, "pp. 100–101, folios not stated" (five times) and "pp. 134–135, folios not stated" (once), both Abbas. They are copied exactly, because the checker matches each reference character for character, as Nankana Sahib, Karachi and Multan do. Hyphens: none of my own. The hyphens left are in names and titles as printed: Al-Huda, *Markaz-i Tahqiqat-i Farsi-i Iran u Pakistan*, *Twenty-First Century* and "e-book" in the bibliography. Hyphenated words inside quotations were avoided by paraphrase: "Islamabad-based Jinnah Institute" became "the Jinnah Institute, which is based in Islamabad"; "a well-known travel writer" became "The travel writer Salman Rashid"; "state-sponsored institution" became "an institution sponsored by the state"; "Persian-studies research centre" (the source's description, not the book's) became "the Persian research centre"; "Inter-/national Congress" became "International Congress".

## Headings used

Overview; The Capital (in place of the brief's "History": the books give the city no history, only its present as the seat of government, so the heading says what the section holds); Holy Places; Communities and Circles; Music, Patronage and the State (the brief's "Shrines, Endowments and the State", renamed because the books record no endowment, custodian or offering in the city, but Abbas records the state as patron of Sufi music there); The Place in the Books; Bibliography.

Not used: History, Festivals and Practice, In Verse and Where the Sources Disagree. The material has no history of the place, no festival, no verse in any script, and no disagreement (see below). The one recorded practice, the discontinued weekly *halqa*, sits under Communities and Circles.

It has no page, so the article does not say that the book mentions Islamabad nowhere else. The two imprints themselves (p. 316, folio 298; p. 330, folio 312) are given in The Place in the Books as what they are: a state publication of the Ministry of Religious Affairs, from the First International Congress on Seerat of 1976.
- Khalid, *A White Trail*: the header "Place-level and thin: no religious-site content" is a remark about the material, with no page. Not stated.
- Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*: the remark that "the federal capital of Pakistan" (ch. 5) and "the capital city of Pakistan" (ch. 11) "are not named as Islamabad and are not mapped here". The ch. 11 phrase is left out, as the source asks. The ch. 5 phrase is used only because the Margalla hills block quotes it, and it is kept inside that quotation.
- Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 7: "The government of Pakistan ... had put up barriers" is paraphrased, not quoted, to avoid an ellipsis.
- Khalid, *Walking with Nanak*: "Nothing about any Islamabad site" is a remark about the material, with no page. Not stated.
- Werbner and Basu: "printed both 'Islamabad' and 'Islâmâbâd' (p. 169, folio 158; pp. 216–217, folios 205–206)". A publisher's imprint. The article says only that the volume "also gives the city as a place of publication", citing p. 169, folio 158. The two spellings and the pp. 216–217 reference are left out as an imprint detail, which also keeps one more en dash out of the prose.
- Rozehnal: "Book 14 is cited repeatedly through Chapter 2's notes (pp. 246–248, folios 239–241)". A locator for repeated citation, adding nothing about the city beyond the p. 244 and p. 263 imprints already given. Left out.
- Rozehnal: "No Islamabad shrine or temple is named anywhere in the book: (not stated)" and "The book's one Islamabad-district figure, Sayyid Mehr Ali Shah of Golra, is treated in his own subsection above and is attached by name, not by place." Neither has a page reference in this file, so neither is in the article. In particular, the article does not say that Rozehnal names no shrine in the city.
- Rozehnal, p. 130: the passage runs "Training—founded by Wahid Bakhsh ... disciples—has published". The em dashes stay out: that part is paraphrased, and only the words after the second dash are quoted.
- Abbas: "Some of them hold influential positions with the federal government in Islamabad" (p. 81, folio not stated). The block does not say who "them" are, and I could not give the sentence a subject without inventing one. Left out; see question 3.
- The bold kind of evidence summaries in the Rozehnal and Abbas blocks ("This is one of the very few institutional closures the book records", "The capital as a patronage market, stated flatly", "Performance as political speech") are summaries, not the book, and are not quoted or asserted. "This is one of the very few institutional closures the book records" has no separate page and is left out.

## Weak material and how I treated it

- **Imprints.** Schimmel (pp. 316, 330), Ernst and Lawrence (p. 234, and the "Islamabad MS" at p. 235), Kugle (p. 340) and Werbner and Basu (p. 169) know the city only as a place of publication or of a manuscript. Each is given in The Place in the Books as exactly that, framed as what the book cites, and no fact about the city rests on it. Ernst and Lawrence's "Islamabad MS" is given as "an Islamabad manuscript"; the article does not say where the manuscript is held. Kugle's "Lok Wirsa Isha'at Ghar" is not joined to Abbas's Lok Virsa, the Institute of Folk Heritage: the spellings differ and the source does not equate them (question 2).
- **Schaflechner, p. 50.** The block flags the reading as thin ("Flagged under Doubts so a human can strike it"). It is kept, because it is the city's institution speaking of a holy place, in one sentence under The Capital, with Schaflechner's own single mention noted in The Place in the Books. Strike it if you think it too thin.
- **The Margalla hills (In Search of Shiva, p. 11, ch. 5).** The block calls it a judgement call: the passage is about Dharmarajika at Taxila, which Khalid places "in the heart of Margalla hills, where the federal capital of Pakistan is", not about any Islamabad site. It is kept as Khalid's own geography, framed as "In the same book Khalid sets the capital among the Margalla hills, and a Buddhist site with it", and the article does not say that Dharmarajika is in Islamabad. The coordinator may drop the paragraph and its Overview sentence together.
- **The bodhi tree (In Search of Shiva, p. 9, ch. 3).** Hearsay from Salman Rashid ("I have heard of"), quoted as his words. My first draft added that Khalid does not say he found the tree; that is an inference from silence and was removed.
- **Informants.** *A White Trail*'s Source line records that informant names may be pseudonyms, so Gupta is "an informant Khalid calls Gupta". The date "2nd March 2011" is given as the source prints it.
- **Abbas, the unnamed performer at p. 34.** Lok Virsa "contacted her through Radio Pakistan in Hyderabad". The block does not name "her". The article says "a performer" (question 1).
- **Abbas, pp. 134–135.** The block gives "she sang Layla-Majnun" under the Open University concert, but does not say it is the same occasion. The article opens the sentence "Abbas writes that she sang", and does not say "that evening".
- **Abbas, p. 101.** "Which government is (not stated)" is carried as "without naming the government", inside the cited sentence.
- **Signpost sentences without a citation** introduce the cited sentences after them and add no fact of their own: "The books know Islamabad as the seat of the state"; "The books set a shrine on the edge of the capital" (also in Holy Places); "The books also place religious organisations and circles in the city"; "The capital is also a place of patronage"; "Public anger and violence also reach the city in the books"; "The books know the city first as the capital"; "The capital is also where two killings that Khalid records took place"; "Institutions of the capital also pass judgement on holy places elsewhere"; "Khalid was told of a sacred tree in the city"; "A movement of religious revival among women has its centre in the city"; "A Sufi association publishes its journal from the city"; "Abbas describes the capital as a place where a singer does business"; "The state's cultural institutions in the city hold archives of the music and have sent its performers abroad"; "Each book sees the city from its own side"; "In the other books the city is a place of publication and learning".
- **Abbreviations and terms.** *murid* and *halqa* appear only inside Rozehnal's quotation and are not glossed, because the source does not gloss them.

## Where the Sources Disagree

None found, so the section is not in the article. Nothing in the ten blocks contradicts anything in another or in the same block. Differences that are not disagreements:
- The capital is "Pakistan's capital city" in *Embodying Charisma* (p. 151, folio 140) and "the federal capital of the country" in *Walking with Nanak* (p. 265, folio 239). Same claim.
- Rozehnal's "fourteen issues to date" of *The Sufi Path* (p. 130, folio 121) and his bibliography's Books 13 and 14 of 1995 and 1996 (p. 263, folio not stated) agree.
- Abbas says Islamabad's population "consists of officials linked with the federal government, and the foreign embassies and their support staff" (pp. 100–101); Khalid says Al-Huda "has spread rapidly across the educated middle class of Islamabad" (p. 16, ch. 10). These describe different things and are not set against each other.
- Spellings: Barri Imam (Werbner and Basu, p. 151) against the archive's Bari Imam; Lok Virsa (Abbas) against Lok Wirsa (Kugle). The article keeps each book's spelling in the sentence that cites it.
