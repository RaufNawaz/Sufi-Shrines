## Overview

Haroon Khalid, in his chapter on sacred trees, recounts a legend from a book said to contain the letters of Alexander the Great, in which two talking trees stood a little distance from Jhelum (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated). In the same chapter Salman Rashid says he has heard of a bodhi tree near Jhelum, and Khalid writes that his own ancestor Ghasita Ram moved "from the Jhelum" in the late nineteenth century (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated). Sujan Rai Bhandari's Persian chronicle, tracing the course of a river, has the Bihat pass below the town of Jhelum and there take the name Jhelum (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 511).

## Sacred Trees

Khalid tells Salman Rashid of "a book which allegedly contains the letters of Alexander the Great", letters which, Khalid says, were written after Alexander's death (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated). In one of them, "an old Indian man visited him in Jhelum after his war with Porus, and told him of two talking trees, one male and the other female at a little distance from Jhelum" (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated). Alexander, the letter claims, "then visited the trees and indeed found them to be talking to each other" (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated). The story is a legend, and the hedge on its source is Khalid's own: the book only 'allegedly' holds the letters.

Salman Rashid, in the same chapter, speaks from hearsay: "I have heard of a bodhi tree in Islamabad and one near Jhelum" (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated).

## The River and the Town

Bhandari's chronicle names the town in a mention of a river's course. The Bihat, it says, passes below the town of Jhelum, at the place where a crossing lies, and there takes the name Jhelum (Bhandari, *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*, p. 511). The name of that crossing is uncertain in the printed text.

Khalid's family story touches the same name. He writes that his ancestor Ghasita Ram "moved to this area from the Jhelum sometime in the late nineteenth century" (Khalid, *In Search of Shiva*, p. 9, ch. 3, folio not stated). "The Jhelum" may mean the river rather than the town; the passage does not say which.
