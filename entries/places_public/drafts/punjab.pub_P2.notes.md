## Consensus choices and their basis

- Taunsawi's dates: both kept, as they agree. Team: "Shaikh Sulayman Taunsawi, who died in 1850" and "Khwaja Sulayman Taunsawi, born in 1184 AH and died in 1267 AH". Public: "born in 1184 AH, who died in 1267 AH, in 1850". No conflict between the two; no choice needed.
- Rasul Shahi houses: team says "in all Punjab it had no house but this one ... with no other house, *takia* or *khanqah*, though he then names one more house near Lahore". Public gives the one consistent reading: hardly any house beyond a single house and one more near Lahore, and four faqirs living.
- Ara'in: text and glossary of the same book differ ("gardeners or small cultivators who worked on their own land" vs "A caste of landowners in the Panjab"). Minor; public gives the text's description only (basis: the fuller statement in the body of the book).
- Siyal inheritance: the annotator of Waris Shah and Abbas "say almost the same thing"; merged into one statement, since they are the same claim, not two accounts.
- How the faiths met: team frames Dhillon ("constant conflict") beside Eaton and the porous boundaries of Schimmel and the annotator. These are interpretations, not rival facts; public gives the conflict view as "In one view" in one sentence, then the rest as content.
- Shahidullah Faridi's will: team: "The will itself names only Bahawalpur; the southern Punjab is Rozehnal's reading of it." Public keeps that distinction ("The will names only Bahawalpur, but it has been read as marking out the southern Punjab ...").
- Ahl-e-Hadith and Tableeghi/Deobandi passages: these are observers' views (Siddiqa, Kamran via Khalid). Kept as content, but the claim that shrine guardians "have become immensely rich feeding off the oppressed classes" is framed as the organisations' view ("who in their eyes have grown immensely rich"), since the team gives it as what drives their targeting.
- Waris Shah's line on Baba Farid and the annotator's objection: the poet is named (his own verse, brief rule 2); the objection is kept as plain content ("It is a poet's praise rather than history, for the Punjab had plenty of troubles even after ...").
- O'Brien quotation: kept as a quotation without the surname ("As a colonial officer wrote in 1911 of the Muslim saints of the western Punjab"), per brief rule 2.
- Janamsakhi journeys: "in the more modern accounts" (third journey) and "In the Puratan Janamsakhi" (fifth) dropped as source attribution; the whole passage is framed as Sikh tradition ("as Sikh tradition tells them"). No conflicting dates are given in the team text.
- Time anchors: Chishti material "in the nineteenth century"; Kanhaiya Lal "in the years before 1884" (team: "A few years before Kanhaiya Lal wrote") and "by the 1880s" (silk drawstrings); Bhandari's "from those days" kept without a date.
- "Rajpurana" in the Dhillon quotation is given as Rajputana, the spelling the team uses elsewhere (Schaflechner).

## Left out, and why

- Muhammad Ismail Muhaddith Lahori: kept, but framed as tradition ("Tradition also holds", "He is said to have been"), because the team notes Lahori takes it from a source his editor distrusts.
- Bibi Pak Daman, 615 AH: the title of the *Tazkira* behind the account dropped (uncertainly printed); the content kept.
- Suhrawardi spread to "the Delhi of Sultan Firuz Shah Tughluq": the Sultan's name dropped (team: "whose name the printed text garbles"); "reaching even Delhi" kept.
- Naushahi order "through Shah Maruf Chishti and Qadiri, or through Sayyid Mubarak Haqqani": both names dropped, since the text does not settle which is meant; that the order flourished in Punjab is kept.
- Guru Nanak's return to Talwandi "in 1506": year dropped (team: "a year imperfectly printed").
- Malik Sohrab Dodai's *jagir* "from the fort of Kot Kardaz to Dhankot": both place names dropped (uncertainly printed); "the land from Sitpur to Dhankot" also given without names, since Dhankot is flagged in the same passage.
- Lakhpat Rai's wedding gift: what the gift was dropped (team: its description "cannot be read with certainty"); that every Hindu and Muslim of Lahore received one is kept.
- Mai Bhagi's town: unnamed (printed name uncertain), as in the team.
- Ayesha Siddiqa and the Ahl-e-Hadith organisations: names dropped (imperfectly or uncertainly printed); Tahir Kamran's name dropped as source attribution.
- Hazrat Husain's death: kept; the team notes some words of the passage cannot be read, but both the grief and the clamour like Karbala are given as readable statements.
- Source names and framing throughout (Lahori, Rizvi, Eaton, Schimmel, Boivin, Ernst and Lawrence, Dhillon, Khalid, Kasmani, Abbas, Schaflechner, Cook, Thakur, Sorley, Rafat, Werbner and Basu, Werth, Lubna Chaudhry, Emily Hodges, Irfan Habib, Naveed Alam, the informant name Daria Boatwala): apparatus, removed.
- Lahori's statement of his own selection principle (he includes a saint whose tomb lies in Punjab soil): given as content about the province, not about the book.
- Eaton's chapter question on Heaven and Earth: turned into a plain sentence ("the shrine helped to draw Heaven and Earth together in the minds of the Muslims of rural Punjab").
- Ernst and Lawrence's remark that they "say nothing there that is specific to the Punjab": apparatus, dropped.
- Eaton's Malabar comparison ("Unlike Punjab or Bengal, Malabar lay beyond the orbit of Mughal imperial influence"): dropped as about another region.
- Boota Singh as "the first Pakistani Punjabi Sikh I had interacted with": the author's personal experience dropped; Boota Singh's own claim kept.
- Rafat's citation of two pages for the Janus questionnaire: apparatus.
