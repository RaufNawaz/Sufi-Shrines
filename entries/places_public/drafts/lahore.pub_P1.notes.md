## Time anchors

- Kanhaiya Lal (1884, firm): "By the 1880s" (Muslim sects, mujawirs at Data Ganj Bakhsh, Anarkali bazaar, wages, Golian Wali sarai, Nau Lakha gatehouse, the Executive Engineer).
- Chishti (1867 unconfirmed): "In the nineteenth century" (twelve gates; Lahore once reaching Mozang and Hashim Pura).
- Bhandari (composed regnal year 40 of Aurangzeb): "By the end of the seventeenth century" (craftsmen and mosques), and Jahangir's buildings "still standing in Aurangzeb's reign" for the team's "which, he writes, still stand".
- Khalid and other modern authors: "Today" (minorities, rare Sikhs, few Parsis); "a tradition still told today" for Luv and Kush.

## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- Founding: "Its founding is most often given to Lo, or Lohu, and in one telling Raja Ramchandar had two sons, Kushu and Lohu, and it was Lohu who settled the city. A tradition still told today holds that Lahore and Kasur are named after Luv and Kush." Basis (b): two of the three clear accounts give Lo / Lohu ("whose founding he attributes to Lo"; "it is Lohu who settled the city of Lahore"). The Luv and Kush naming tradition is kept as a separate, living tradition, not merged with the Lohu account (no identity of Lohu with Luv is stated). Chishti's second telling (p. 661: Lo, an illegible second son settling Kasur, Rao founding Mauza Rawan) is dropped as resting on uncertain readings.
- Humayun's return: team has Bhandari, "Lahore passed without a battle into the hands of the servants of the state", and Chishti, "the governor of Lahore, Tatar Khan, came out against him, and Bairam Khan overcame him off his guard". Kept Tatar Khan and Bairam Khan and the Afghans scattering, dropped "without a battle" so the two are not set against each other or merged into a contradiction. Minor.
- Dara Shikoh's second Lahore episode: Bhandari has him leave Lahore for Multan with the treasure; Chishti has him, "after Aurangzeb's accession", return to Lahore, raise a larger army and cross the Indus. Gave Bhandari's fuller account (Multan) and left Chishti's out as a variant of the same flight. Minor for a visitor.
- Mughal capital: Chishti's "capital in Akbar's reign" sits in an uncertainly printed passage; used instead the translator's clear "Akbar had temporarily shifted his capital to Lahore" ("for a while he moved his capital to Lahore").
- The Mongol siege of 639 AH (Bhandari) and "the destruction of Lahore, by the Mongols in 1241" (Rizvi) are given in sequence, the second as "The Mongols' destruction of Lahore is dated 1241"; no conversion of 639 AH is stated.
- Khusraw Shah and Khusraw Malik: Bhandari's twenty eight years for Khusraw Malik kept; Chishti's "seven years" (numeral uncertain) and Bhandari's damaged "thirty eight years" dropped. Chishti's "the Ghurids took the country from Khusrau by a thousand tricks" kept without naming which Khusraw, since the team version does not settle it.
- Mian Wada's khanqah: the two Lal passages (p. 239, "Mian Wada's madrasa"; pp. 194 to 195, "Khanqah Mian Wadda[OCR?]") told as one event, using the clean spelling; "one hundred dervishes" (figure uncertain) given as "many of the khanqah's dervishes", from p. 239; "about a hundred and fifty of Raja Suchet Singh's companions" kept.
- The twelve gates: Chishti's list kept without the two uncertain names (the "Dakki" gate and its second name; "Sarawala" kept only as "Kashmiri", which the team gives as its other name), so the list reads "among them" eleven gates.
- Three rulers of Lahore: Gujjar Singh (clear on p. 326) and Lehna Singh (clear on pp. 855, 858) named; Sobha Singh dropped (uncertainly printed).
- Shah Alam: "Shah Alam died in the city of Lahore", no year (1712 uncertainly printed), and no identification with Bahadur Shah, which the team version does not make.
