## Consensus choices and their basis

- Shah Abdul Latif's dates: the two sources differ by a year at each end ("Shah Abdul Latif of Bhit (1689 to 1752)", Schimmel, *Mystical Dimensions*; "Shah Abdul Latif, whom Schaflechner dates 1690 to 1751"). No shrine date or majority in this part, so rule 4(d): "Accounts of his dates differ by a year, placing his birth in 1689 or 1690 and his death in 1751 or 1752." The coordinator should make this agree with whatever P1/P2 chose.
- Pir Pagara / Pir Pagaro: a spelling variant ("followers of pir Pagara", Lindholm; "disciples of the Pir Pagaro", Schimmel). Minor; one spelling used, Pir Pagaro, both times. Coordinator to normalise across parts.
- Sehwan in state publications: "hardly mention the saint or the shrine of Sehwan until the late 1960s" and "Only from the 1960s onward do Urdu and Sindhi newspapers offer detailed reportage". Not a disagreement (two media); both kept as given.
- The Sarang closing verse: two renderings of one prayer ("Dear friend and sweet beloved, make the whole world prosperous", Shackle; "My Lord, be kind over dear Sind, / Friend! Sweet darling! You may make the whole world happy!", Schimmel). Kept both, each with a translator credit, because the first is the line the team version calls one of the most often quoted and the second is the only one that names Sindh in words; framed as "in another rendering", not as rival accounts.
- Bhakti in Sindh: "Ajwani held that the Bhakti reached Sindh only in the sixteenth century ... but Boivin finds no evidence in him of the influence of the sants". Given as "By one account ... and there is no evidence that its sants ... shaped the spiritual life of the province". Not side by side: one dating and one assessment.
- Collective village responsibility: "might apply word for word to the Sindh of Mughal and Kalhora days". Kept the hedge: "may well have held in Mughal and Kalhora days too".
- Threat to Hindus: "A possible explanation is that the Hindu population ... is more and more under the threat". Hedged: "are, it seems, more and more under threat".
- Pirs and conversions: a strong claim of one author ("the most active people working for the conversion of young Hindu girls to Islam are well-known Sufi pirs"); framed "It has been argued that".
- Time anchors: Sorley's views anchored at 1940 ("By 1940", "in 1940", "the verdict of 1940", "the Sindh of 1940"); Bhandari at "the end of the seventeenth century"; Shackle's present as "Today". No Chishti material in this part.
- People named because they are part of the story (subjects, not the scholars who cite them): Burton, Crowe, Heddle (May 1836), Lieutenant James (1847), Trumpp, Bartle Frere, Diwan Gidumal, Jethmal Parsram, Ram Panjwani, K. R. Malkani, G. M. Syed, Abdul Qadir Mangi, Hussain Soomro, Ishtiaq Ansari, Badar Abro, Khadim Soomro, Molchand, Wahid Bakhsh, Alan Faqir, Abida Parvin, Taj Mastani. Scholars who only interpret (Boivin, Sorley, Schimmel, Shackle, Abbas, Khalid, Schaflechner, Kasmani, Rozehnal, Verkaaik, Ewing, Jamal Malik, Lindholm, Kothari, Aggarwal, Jotwani/Motwani, Ajwani, Sikander Baloch, Naseer Mirza, Tahir Kamran, Asaf Khan, Longworth Dames, Naveed Alam, MacMurdo, Burnes) are not named; their claims are stated, or framed as "it has been argued", "by one account", "one scholar". Translator credits kept for Shackle and Schimmel.

## Left out, and why

- Bhandari's revenue for the *suba* of Thatta ("ten crore, forty nine lakh and seventy thousand dams, the first two words of the sum uncertain in the printed text"): rests on an uncertain reading. The four *sarkars*, 57 *mahals* and five ports are kept.
- The distance of the yellow stone quarry ("six kos, the figure uncertain in the printed text"): dropped; the quarry kept.
- Fish oil used on the boats ("the word uncertain in the printed text"): dropped.
- The name of the *palla* fish ("its name also uncertain in the printed text"): name dropped; the fish that comes up from the salt sea, is netted and is found nowhere else is kept.
- The Talpur madrasa "at a place north of Sehwan whose printed name cannot be read with certainty": dropped from the list; "six madrasas, among them" the other five kept.
- "There is no contemporary account of the working of its educational system ... the best description being Burton's, gathered orally": source talk about what accounts exist; dropped.
- "In the field of Sindh studies, however, there is no published research related to Sufism and the Hindu Sufis in Sindh": a statement about scholarship, not the province; dropped.
- Khalid's "southern Punjab and Sindh are excluded, where there are many such shrines": a remark on the scope of his book; dropped (the Manora view of the Makran range kept).
- Michael Wood's film *In the Footsteps of Alexander* as the vehicle: the film dropped, its point (the Indus and Baluchistan coast through Persia as a route used for centuries) kept.
- "Jotwani, cited in the book as Motwani": the naming muddle dropped; his quotation kept as "One scholar called".
- Abbas's note that the Marui line readings are "all Alan Faqir's political interpretations": compressed to "and read it politically".
- "which Schaflechner dates", "as Aggarwal (2013) has shown", "his chapter plan calls the shrine": attribution apparatus; the content kept.
- The cross-reference "are told in the history above": process talk; the three facts it points to (Zakariyya and Iltutmish, governors and Makhdum-i Jahaniyan, the ruler who became Sayyid Muhammad Ghaus's disciple) stated plainly.
- Nothing else of substance was cut; every festival, practice, community, name, place, date and sum in the team sections that does not rest on an uncertain reading is carried.
