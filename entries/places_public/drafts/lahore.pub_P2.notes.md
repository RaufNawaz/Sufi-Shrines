## Time anchors

- Chishti (1867 unconfirmed): "in the nineteenth century" only (Mozang becoming a mohalla, the Nigar Khana grave, the Jains, the gravediggers of Miani).
- Bhandari: "In Aurangzeb's time", from the bibliography item (composed in regnal year 40 of Aurangzeb) as the Shah Jamal team bibliography gives it. P2's own bibliography is not in team_P2.md; the coordinator should confirm the item matches.

## Consensus and wording choices (rule 4)

- No true side by side disagreements in these sections. Two near variants handled:
  - Khalid's two counts of Lahore's minorities ("five religious communities ... (Hindus, Christians, Sikhs, Baha'is and Zoroastrians)" and "the four religious minorities ... (Hindu, Sikh, Christian and Baha'i)"): gave the five, which contains the four. Basis: the fuller count; the four drops only the Zoroastrians, who appear later as the Parsis.
  - Hujwiri's titles (Schimmel "still a popular place of pilgrimage", Kasmani "Lahore's most prominent shrine complex", Kugle "Lahore's patron saint"): these agree, so stated together without names.
- Shah Hussayn / Madho Lal Husain: the team version does not equate Kugle's Shah Hussayn with Schimmel's Madho Lal Husain, so they stay in separate sentences, not merged. "It is not clear how the two managed to overcome this conflict with Madho's family and the Brahmins of Lahore" rendered as Shah Hussain and Madho (the "two" of the quotation).
- Kugle's Qalandar quotation has "Qadiri" interpolated between quotation segments ('associated with the great" Qadiri "saints'); the public text says "the great saints of the city" to avoid putting Shah Jamal under a Qadiri label that his own article treats as unsettled.
- "the Hindus ... continue to visit a Hindu temple their ancestors visited": kept as present fact ("still visit").
- Miani: kept the village of learned men, the meaning of *mian*, Hafiz Jan Muhammad, Abu Muhammad Qadiri, the split over the *shariat*, the desertion, the resettlement under a second man appointed by the king (his name, Mir Ali, uncertain in print, so unnamed), the gravediggers' dating to Muqim Sahib of the Hujra, and "When Miani was deserted ... it became this graveyard".
- Basant Gar petition: "petitioned the British authorities" for "the court of Major McGregor, the Assistant Agent to the Resident, the name and title uncertainly printed".

## Left out, and why

- Almost every Hijri date of the building inscriptions ("almost every Hijri date he gives is uncertain in the printed text"): Masti Darwaza 1023, Shah Zia Qadri 1126, Sunehri Masjid 1163, Bukhari Khan 1165, Salih Kambo 1058, Wazir Khan chronograms 1043/1044, Gazruni 706, Imam Ghulam Muhammad 1234 and son 1289, Jan Muhammad Huzuri 764, Hassu Teli 1000, Takiya Sadhuwan 1266, Zabita Khan 1251/1278, Amir Shah 1285, Nura 1239, Nur Muhammad 1297, Moran 1223, Bokan Khan 1255, Imam Shah Wali 1294, Shah Bilawal 1046, Lal Husain 1008 AH and Madho 1057 AH, Hamid Qari 1141, Zakariya Khan mosque 1144, Data dome 1247, Shah Kanhh 1294, Allah Jawai's slab 1247, Sarai Golian Wali 1035, Gilani 1072, Shalamar 1057, Shaikh Muhtaram's abjad workings. Kept only the three "printed without doubt" (Pholiyan Wali 1282 AH, Imam ud Din Khan 1266 AH, Gulabi Bagh 1066) and the Christian era 1868 for Data's mosque. The buildings themselves are still named where a non date fact stands. For Rauf: Lal Husain's and Madho's death years are important but marked uncertain in this team section.
- Samvat dates marked uncertain: Radha Kishan shivala 1847 and thakurdwara 919, Chhajju Bhagat, the Samvat 1900 shivala, Rani Rind Awi 1927; Khushal Singh 1844 and Jawahar Singh 1847 (Christian era, marked uncertain).
- Names marked uncertain, dropped or left unnamed: Fateh Mozang, Mauza Achhra and the date palms (whole Mian Natha story left out, since both the place and the trees are uncertain), Panj Dhera / Panj Dher, Gumhat, Mir Ali, Pir Alima and the five coffins, the Kanjar graveyard, the gravediggers' "Siani", the four rupee gravedigger and fruit from *wilayat*, Bawa Thala Gir (now "a man"), Badal-din Gilani, Shah Kanhh, Allah Jawai (now "a sister of Muhammad Sultan the contractor"), Mahin Lal, Rani Rind Awi, the Hanafis (so the clause "most numerous of the Sunnis" is gone), the two uncertain order names, Qutb Yunan, Khair Shahi, the "Lodhi period" of the Turkish soldier, Major McGregor, the Hindu "sadhu" (now "a Hindu"), Nadh Shahi, Sardha Ram Phaluri ("one preacher"), Bhai Sawaya Singh, Kanhaiya Lal "Kambo", Zeb-un-Nisa as Dai Anga's name, Begum Jan, the Jain group's name, the houses of the people of Delhi.
- Uncertain figures: Bahadur Khan's platform sides, Jahangir's platform length, minaret steps and platform of "two hundred and some feet", the iron of the *kalas* fitting (now "a fitting"), the ninety thousand rupees of the Mian Mir church (kept: about eight hundred seats, forty five thousand from government, rest by subscription), the Bhauri Sarkar wells, the Shalamar bighas that do not add up.
- Passages marked damaged or garbled: the Miani graveyard through the reigns of Shah Jahan, Alamgir, Muhammad Shah and Ranjit Singh ("heavily damaged and the order of events approximate"); the Sikh looting of Mauza Miani and the two hundred houses at a rupee a year; the property of hundreds lost under the Sikhs ("very damaged passage"); the link of the Gilani flag to Sher Singh (the flag itself kept); the nobles at the Gilani ancestor's funeral; the largely illegible gathering of the elders of Lahore and Amritsar.
- Kugle's "Shah Hussayn earned himself disrepute among the Muslim notables of Lahore, who saw him as a dissolute drunk": the team version flags the citation [`l&J`, text-layer?]; left out, the second quotation ("this open passion ruined his reputation throughout Lahore") kept.
- Kanhaiya Lal's own conjecture about an arched opening inside the great well.

Source apparatus and author narration (rule 2, rule 6):
- Chishti's descriptions of his own book (a guide to the saints, the afterword, the survey heading, the table of named sites, the promised plan of Miani's graves, his account of the Parsi graveyard "in his account of Miani Sahib") and his *sulh-e kull* claim for the book; the first paragraph keeps the substance (the city's history as a history of its saints, the mazars on every side as memorials of the elders).
- The translator of Madho Lal Hussain's acknowledgement of Ida Sofie Matzen (about the translator, not the place).
- "Five of the mohallas listed in the book's contents for its second part are named after saints" (a count of a table of contents).
- Rizvi's footnote mention of the Padishahi mosque in a discussion of Goldziher on relics.
- Khalid's own project dates ("began to visit ... in 2008"; the project "officially began in September 2010") and that the Abdali chapter opens with a village shrine (the shrine kept, the chapter framing dropped).
- "Kanhaiya Lal surveys ... without comment", "This possession stands against his irenic picture", "which stands against the same picture" (composer's commentary).
- Jwala Dai's mandir "built nine years ago": relative to an unstated date of writing, so the nine years are left out; she, her husband, her son in law and his monthly gift are kept.
- Boivin: "Muhammad Iqbal was a poet born in Lahore". Kept the PhD from Munich and that he was a poet; left out the birthplace. For Rauf: the team version states it, but it is the one claim in these sections that conflicts with the commonly known record (Sialkot), and the public version should not carry it until checked; V1 may call this an omission, in which case restore it only if Rauf agrees.
- Schimmel's dating of the Konya *maqam* ("a few years before she wrote") reduced to "has been raised".
- An illness "the book names" at Shah Dargahi's well: given as "an illness".
