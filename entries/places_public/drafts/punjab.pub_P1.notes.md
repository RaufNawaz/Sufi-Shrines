# Punjab public English, group P1: notes

Sections: Overview; History: From the Earliest Times to Babur; History: Under the Mughals; History: Decline, Sikh Rule, the British and Partition.
Trial check (`trial.py P1`): no errors outside L1/B1. Raw length 42,157 of 69,773 characters (0.60); against the team prose with citations stripped (54,878) about 0.77.

## Units and time anchors

- Bhandari's units ("Mughal *suba* of Lahore", "*wilayat* of Punjab") anchored "at the end of the seventeenth century" (team: "writing about 1107 AH").
- Lahori's Punjab "from Delhi to Peshawar" anchored "In 1875" (team: "writing in 1875").
- Chishti's Punjab "that the British conquered and governed" anchored "in the nineteenth century" (his 1867 unconfirmed).
- Kanhaiya Lal's material anchored "By the 1880s" / "in the 1880s" (Lahore as capital, the courts, Moran Shahi coins still kept, Hir and Ranjha on everyone's lips).
- Sorley's material given with its own event dates (1758, 1760), no anchor on the author.

## Consensus choices

- Founding of Lahore under Lohar Chand: given as "One tradition holds", with "though accounts of the city's beginnings differ". Basis: team "an account he sets against that of the author of the *Khulasat al-Tawarikh*".
- Anangpal's taking of Lahore: given as "Another tradition holds". Basis: team "Retelling *Tuhfat al-Wasilin*" and "His modern editor calls *Tuhfat al-Wasilin* a fabricated tazkira". The editor's judgement itself is apparatus and left out; the legend framing carries the doubt.
- Lahore fighting the Mongols for two months: "a little known tradition holds". Basis: team "he adds that the account is not well known and perhaps no one will believe it".
- Ahmad Shah Abdali's invasions: given as beginning in 1748, with Bulleh Shah seeing invasions in 1748 and 1752 and Abdali's death in 1773 (1187 AH). Basis: team "who in 1748 began a series of invasions" (Waris Shah annotator), "began his various attacks on Punjab in 1161 AH, 1748" (Lahori's editor), "a second invasion of the Punjab by Ahmad Shah Abdali in 1748 and then a third in 1752" (Rafat). The numbering of 1748 as the "second" (Rafat) against "began" (two others) is minor and dropped silently; neither ordinal is given.
- Ahmad Shah Durrani (Schimmel) and Ahmad Shah of the Abdali Afghans (Lyall via Sorley) kept in separate sentences as each account names him; not merged into one statement.
- Ranjit Singh's forty years: team "He ruled the Punjab for forty years" (Lal) and "stability ... which lasted a good forty years" (Khalid); both kept, they agree.
- Bulleh Shah's death 1758 at 78: Shackle and Rafat agree.
- Guru Nanak's fame by his return to Talwandi: framed "Sikh tradition holds" (Dhillon's retelling of the Janamsakhis).
- Faith relations (Dhillon's "constant conflict" and Eaton's "very slow, probably unconscious conversion"): interpretations, not rival facts; both kept as two sides of how the faiths met, without attribution.
- Beginnings of Punjabi mystical poetry: "may have begun as early as the days of Fariduddin Shakarganj and is traced at least to the fifteenth century". Basis: team "traces in one book to the fifteenth century and in another suggests may have emerged as early as the days of Fariduddin Shakarganj".
- Iltutmish in the November 1221 passage kept, though the team notes "the name is misprinted in the text": the team identifies him outright and only reports a misprint, not an uncertain reading. Flag for V1.
- Guru Hargobind's service with Jahangir kept as "Some claim" (team: Khalid reports "some claims"; the Guru's name "misprinted", identified by the team). Flag for V1.

## Left out, and why

Uncertain readings (rule 5), each dropped from the public text or from its list:
- 29 March 1849 (annexation date, "uncertainly printed"); 1848 (the last disturbance); Guru Nanak's return to Talwandi in 1506 ("imperfectly printed").
- 391 AH (first attack on Lahore); 367 AH (Sabuktigin's accession); 693 (Khalji Mongol march); 820 AH (Jasrath's rising); 600 to 855 AH (Multan under Delhi); 952 (Islam Shah's accession); 962 (Humayun's return); 985 (Yusuf Shah's flight); 1014 and 1015 (Jahangir's accession, Khusrau's capture); 1068 and 1069 (war of succession); 7 Dhu'l Qa'da / 17 Amardad (Aurangzeb's march); 1194 (Jani Khan's death); 1805 (Holkar and Amir Khan, verb also garbled: the whole sentence dropped); 1868 (women's prison); January 1882 (industrial arts exhibition); 1751 (end of Abdali's seizure of the Punjab); 1756 to 1758 (Sikhs rise; the event kept without its date); 1523 (Daulat Khan's invitation).
- Names: the second king beside Porus; Thatta in the Pandava journey; Malik Ayaz on Lal p. 17 (kept from p. 23, which is clean); the Chaghatai Khan's name; the sultan named "Shams al-Din Iltutmish" in the Khalji blow (given as "the sultan"); Bahram, governor of Multan; the place Firuz Shah was besieging; Sikha and Gakkhar and Hazara (the raider kept unnamed, Jasrath kept as "the raider's brother" from the clean Lal p. 29); Jasrath Khokhar's escape from Timur (Bhandari p. 191, name uncertain, whole sentence); the son of the raja of Jammu's name; Karor Kot and Dhankot in Husain Langah's conquests; the town Kamran reached and Salim Khan's presence "in Punjab"; the two *parganas*; Bairam Khan's route; Dipalpur in the Mirzas' march; Raja Basu's place of origin; Sulaiman Shikoh's road; Khanjar Khan of Bhera and Mir Miran / Mir Khan (the *faujdari* kept as "given new holders"); Bhikari Khan and his office; McLeod; the Faqir family quarter's name; the name of the School of Art's principal; Ghazni among the Sikh community's places.
- Figures: five lakh *tankas* (Rai Ram Deo Bhatti's farm); one crore *dams* (Khalilullah Khan's reward); the seventy thousand infantry; Anangpal's length of rule.
- Whole passages: Mirza Shah Husain Arghun's four years and Multan's eighty years out of Delhi's hands (Bhandari p. 294, "a damaged passage"); the weighing festival robes (words uncertain, name split by page break); Eaton's reading of Abu al-Fazl's statistics of about 1595 (opening damaged, name misprinted; kept only that the Punjab was two of Akbar's provinces, Lahore and Multan).

Apparatus and source talk:
- Lahori's modern editor's view that no regular and researched history of Punjab has yet been written (historiography, not the place).
- "Mughal is the book's own word for these armies"; "he does not say where in the Punjab" (several); "Bhandari speaks of these mountains as the hills of Punjab, but nowhere says where they lie"; Boivin "does not say whether by present Punjab he means the Pakistani province"; "Bhandari dates these years by the reign, among them the twenty sixth and twenty eighth regnal years" (the linkage of the years to events is not clear in the team text).
- "Sarkar remarked" (the leather industry kept, without the attribution); Sharib's footnote naming *Anwar al-Arifin*; Lahori's inclusion rule for saints (his tomb in the soil of Punjab), which is the method of a book rather than a fact of the place; the editor's crediting Lahori as eyewitness of tomb destruction (the destruction kept).
- "The modern translators and annotators write of 'the Punjab' or 'the old Punjab'" (naming practice of modern writers).
- "Ahmad Shah Abdali had already completed two raids of Punjab and beyond" (Khalid): minor, covered by the series of invasions.
- The duplicate Moran coin passage (Lal p. 47) merged into one sentence.
