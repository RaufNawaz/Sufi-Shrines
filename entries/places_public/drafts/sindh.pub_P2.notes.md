## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- Early Sufism in Sindh. Team: "Abbas writes that 'The Sufi mystical traditions came to the subcontinent of Pakistan and India through the Sind province'" against "Rizvi, by contrast, finds that ... 'no such developments in Sind are recorded'". Public: the entry through Sindh as what is "held", with one clause "accounts differ on how far Sufism spread in those early centuries". Basis (b)/(d): no source settles the extent; the clause carries the doubt.
- Progress of Sufism. Team: "'That there has been any consistent progress of Sufism in Sind I do not believe' ... handed down from age to age by a handful of learned men" against "Shackle gives a different emphasis: ... a strong Sufi presence was rapidly established across Sindh". Public keeps what both allow (the course cannot be traced in detail; little securely established between the thirteenth and eighteenth centuries; a strong Sufi presence established by charismatic figures) and drops Sorley's personal disbelief in "consistent progress" and the "handful of learned men" thesis as the side by side variant. Not merged into a single chain of claims.
- Hallaj's date. Team: "shortly after 900" and "In 905" (Schimmel, Boivin). Both kept, they agree: "shortly after 900 ... In 905 he travelled widely there".
- Orders. Team gives Boivin's three dominant orders (among the Hindu Sufis of Sindh) and Sorley's Jelali and Jemali division. These are two classifications, not contradictions; both kept, in separate paragraphs, Boivin's framed "among the Hindu Sufis of Sindh" as the team version frames it. The Chishti among Sorley's four Jemali orders and the Chishtiyya "not widespread" are compatible and both kept.
- Population. Team: "Del Hoste calculated in 1832 that the population was 850,000, about a quarter of them Hindus, and Burnes in 1828 put it at about one million". Public: "In 1828 and 1832 the population was put at between 850,000 and about a million, about a quarter of them Hindus." Basis (d): a range, no choice made. The quarter Hindus belongs to the 1832 figure; the sentence does not attach it to the 1828 figure as a separate count.
- Where Pakistan's Hindus live. Team (Khalid): most Hindus "scattered in Sindh, Baluchistan, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa" (1998 census) and "The majority of the Hindu community is based in Sindh and Southern Punjab". Minor variant: public gives the 1998 census ranking (largest minority, Christians second), keeps the "outlying regions of Southern Punjab, inner Sindh and Baluchistan" sentence and the Sindh and Southern Punjab social fabric sentence, and drops the KP/Baluchistan scatter clause. With Schaflechner's "home to the majority of Pakistan's Hindus" this is the commonly told version.
- Caste in Sindh. The team version's Communities section describes a live debate (Thakur's "almost absent", Boivin's "no caste system, or no rigid one", Dalit organisations' opposition). The debate itself is the content, so it is kept as "Caste remains contested in the province" without naming who holds which view.
- Hindu population estimate 6 to 10 million: kept with "the figure varying widely", as the team version qualifies it.

## Left out, and why

- Uncertain readings (rule 5): Sawan, "the word uncertainly printed" (the rainy season gloss dropped with it); "Between 1740 and 1800, the second figure imperfectly printed" (kept only "After 1740"); the Commissioner's 1847 population and area, "1,274,732 on 48,000 square miles, both figures imperfectly printed"; "the Sabuda, the name uncertain in the printed text" (dropped from Bhandari's list, "among other peoples" added so the Jareja are not the only people named); "The Jatois, the name imperfectly printed" (Mazaris kept); "Gandiri, the first name uncertain in the printed text" (dropped from the Kamod fishing castes, "among them" kept); Boivin's sentence that "it is certain whether" the untouchables were long established in the east, which "seems to have lost a negative" (dropped, with its "ultimate extension of the great Thar Desert" phrase, which belongs to that sentence; the census of 1843 to 1872 kept).
- Source apparatus and research process: the HRCP and NGO provenance of the 6 to 10 million and 90 per cent figures; Boivin's April 2016 mapping tour (kept only that Jhulelal shrines are found in Southern Sindh and where Thano Bula Khan lies); "deserves to be studied further in Sindh"; Khalid's "could not go into the community's experience in these regions in detail"; Iqbal Qaiser's book as the identifier of the Clifton temple (kept as "has been identified"); "following Thapan"; the names Fremlen, Hamilton, Burnes, Dr Burnes, Sir Alexander Burnes, Del Hoste, Pottinger, Elliot, Lieutenant-Colonel Scott, von Orlich, Crowe, McMurdo, Thakur, Zahid-Khan, Narsain, Lubna Chaudhry, Naseer Mirza, Qalandar Shah and Irfan Habib, Hsuan Tsang and the Chach-nama as sources (their content kept, stated plainly or as "it was said", "one traveller"). The *Ain-i-Akbari* is named as a celebrated text.
- Opinions in an author's own voice that are not facts about the province and would read as a stereotype on a public page: "As Sindhi Muhammadans are notoriously unhappy away from Sind, their fondness for desertion perhaps made them unsuitable in Bombay"; "The Sindhi is by nature an easy-going being, not given to religious subtlety" (its substance kept as the "easygoing character of Sind orthodoxy"); Schimmel's "their women were noted for beauty and not too strict morals" of the Mohana (their low rank kept); Elliot's "priest-ridden country" epithet in the village sentence (Burnes's "priestridden" kept once, as a traveller's words).
- Sorley's term "debasement of Islam": given as "This popular Islam owed much to the religion of the indigenous Sindhis".
- Rizvi's "said to have embraced Islam through a supernatural feat performed by Makhdum Jahaniyan": given as "through a miracle he performed", the "he" referring to Makhdum Jahaniyan.
- "a poll tax, the *sar shumari*, fell on non-Baluchi and Jat Muslims": read as falling on Muslims who were not Baluchis, the Jats among them. V1 should confirm this reading; the alternative is "on non-Baluchi Muslims and on Jat Muslims", which comes to the same people.
- Spelling: the team version has both "Kutch" (Pithoro Pir) and "Kacch" (Sidis); the public uses "Kutch" for both. "Sind" inside the team's quotations is "Sindh" in the public prose except in the proper name "Upper Sind Frontier". Balochistan and Baluchistan follow the team version's sentence by sentence (Hinglaj "in Balochistan", Ghauth Bakhsh "in Sindh and Balochistan", elsewhere "Baluchistan"); the coordinator may wish to normalise.
