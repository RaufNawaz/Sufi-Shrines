## Consensus choices and their basis

- Beginnings of mystical poetry in Punjabi: given as fifteenth century by reliable tradition, possibly as early as Fariduddin's day. Basis: team, "Reliable tradition traces the use of the native language for mystical poetry both in Sind and in the Punjab back to the fifteenth century" and "popular mystical poetry may have emerged as early as in the days of Fariduddin Shakarganj". The two are compatible (one firm floor, one hedged possibility), so both are kept with their own hedges.
- When folk tales became media of mystical teaching: one clause "Accounts differ"; the Madho Lal Hussein claim kept with its hedge ("seem to begin, or to carry on from unknown beginnings"). Basis: team, Madho Lal Hussein's allusions "seem to inaugurate, or to continue from unknown beginnings" the tradition, against "the development of folk tales as media for mystical instruction ... only in the centuries after the death of the historian Badaʾuni". Rule 4(d) style: no verdict chosen; the later dating is not given side by side.
- Guru Arjan's petition: "a reduction was ordered, told as granted on the petition of Guru Arjan". Basis: team, "Bhandari gives this as the petition of Guru Arjan". The light hedge keeps the attribution where the team puts it without naming the author.
- Persian poetry common ground in Guru Nanak's time: hedged "are held to have known". Basis: team frames it as "in Rizvi's argument".
- Moreland and McLeod: kept as "has been read against" and "it has been argued", since the team frames both as one scholar's reading or objection to another.
- Colonial versus postcolonial state and shrines: placed "In central Punjab". Basis: team, "Kasmani, drawing on Umber bin Ibad's work on central Punjab".
- Shrines and the Sikh years: "as one eyewitness saw them". Basis: team, "The Sikh years, in Lahori's editor's estimate, were hard on the shrines ... the Mufti recorded, as an eyewitness".
- Time anchors: Bhandari "at the end of the seventeenth century" (team "writing about 1107 AH"); Kanhaiya Lal "by the 1880s" / "the history of 1884"; Chishti material "in the nineteenth century" (the qita on Dalip Singh, the plea for the khanqahs); Lahori's book "1875"; Sorley "a study of 1940".

## Left out, and why

Uncertain readings (team marks them uncertainly printed, illegible or damaged):
- The name of the women's spinning melody ("whose printed name the text renders uncertainly as Kapa'iti"): melody kept, name dropped.
- The second musical instrument "popular in the old Punjab" ("the other's name uncertainly printed"): only the *nad* kept.
- The rate of the revenue reduction ("at a rate uncertainly printed"): reduction kept, rate dropped.
- The title of the Sanhla Bagh builder's office ("its title damaged in the printed text"): given only as "an office over the buildings of Punjab".
- The year 1058 of Munshi Muhammad Salih's mosque ("the reading of the year uncertain"): dropped.
- Mr Bowring and Major Macgregor ("both names uncertainly printed"): given as "two Deputy Commissioners"; Montgomery kept.
- The names of the five doabs (Bist Jalandhar, Bari Manjha, Rachna, Chunhat, Sind Sagar): the team says "some of the names uncertainly printed" without saying which, so all five names are dropped and each doab is given by its two rivers (with Sialkot and Gujrat, which the team places in them).
- The "other metals" of the northern hills ("whose names are uncertainly printed"): left as "other metals".
- The far end of the suba's breadth from Bhera, and its southern boundary ("a place whose name is uncertainly printed", "a place whose name is also uncertain"): dropped; east, west and north kept.
- The count of Sufis in the Hadiqat al-Awliya ("244 ..., the numeral uncertainly printed"): dropped.
- "the West was a fearful place where giants resided" (Hassan Abdal chapter, "as the damaged sentence can be read"): dropped; the thousand years of invaders from the West kept.
- The qita on Dalip Singh: the team notes one word of the first hemistich is uncertainly printed but gives both likenings (candle, new flower) as fact; kept as the team states them. V1 may wish to check.

Apparatus and author framing dropped:
- Qureshi's caveat that she does not say whether her Punjab is the Indian or the Pakistani side, and Ernst and Lawrence's "without saying on which side of the border": not carried as caveats, but the public text also never assigns either statement to one side of the border.
- Shackle's writing "Panjab without the article": a remark about usage, dropped.
- Bhandari's list of his sources reframed as a fact: "The sultans of the wilayat of Multan had a history of their own".
- Kanhaiya Lal's citations of earlier works kept as "it leaned on earlier works it names" (Province in Books legitimately names the books).
- Bhandari's bounding of the suba of Ajmer on the west by Dipalpur "without saying whether he means the suba, the sarkar or the city": dropped as minor and resting on an ambiguity.
- Schimmel's "liberating" Muslim armies: the evaluative word dropped (it is her characterisation, not a fact).
- "right-winged" of the PML-N and "rubbishes" (the party "rejects the claims"): register softened, sense kept.
- Khalid's school: given as "one elite private school" rather than "his".
- Ernst and Lawrence's folio flag and every page, folio and endnote reference.
