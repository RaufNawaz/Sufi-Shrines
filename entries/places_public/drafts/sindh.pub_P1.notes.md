## Consensus choices

- Mughal annexation: 1592, "though accounts of the year differ". Basis (b)/(c): team "Sorley dates its annexation by Akbar to 1592" and "'Sind was added to the Moghul Empire by Akbar in 1592', Sorley writes", repeated in his chronological table; the one other firm date is Schimmel's "Sind was, since 1595, part of the Mughal Empire"; Bhandari's "year 1001 of the Hijra, a year uncertain in the printed text" is left out (rule 5). The overview, like the team overview, gives 1592 only.
- Arab conquest: 711 (Schimmel twice, Eaton), with Muhammad bin Qasim's conquest "between 711 and 712" kept as the same event (team: Rizvi "conquered Sindh 'between 711 and 712'"). Chishti's "between the years 705 and 715 ... both numbers uncertain in the printed text" left out.
- Nadir Shah's invasion: "usually dated 1739". Basis (b): team "Persian suzerainty and tribute after the invasion of Nadir Shah in 1739" (Sorley p. 35) and Schimmel's "before he reached Delhi in 1739"; Sorley p. 18 gives "the disastrous advent of Nadir Shah in 1737" (dropped as the minority date, the coming kept without a year); the table's 1740 is in a table "its figures imperfectly printed" and is left out.
- Afghan overlordship: about 1747 (team: "about 1747 the passing of suzerainty to the Pathan kingdom at Kabul"; "then, in 1747, under Afghan dominance"). The table's 1750 left out (imperfect figures).
- Talpur rise: 1781 (Schimmel: "The Talpurs, who thus came to power in 1781"), with civil war from 1778 (Sorley p. 35, firm). The table's "final establishment of Talpur ascendancy in 1803" is given without its year (table figures imperfectly printed).
- Sumra rule: "said to have reigned for five hundred years" (Ain-i-Akbari "a Sumra Rajput line of thirty-six princes reigned for five hundred years"; Bhandari "government stayed in the Sumra family for five hundred years"), and rule in Lower Sindh "until the middle of the fourteenth century" (Rizvi; Sorley's "about 1360"; Boivin's Samma "officially took power from the first in 1351"). Sorley's start "about 1025, as the damaged first figure is read" and the Tarikh-i-Tahiri's "700, the figure imperfectly printed" left out; the confusion over Upper and Lower Sindh kept in one clause ("how far Sumra authority reached over each is confused").
- Conversion of Sindh: Sorley's two passages are compatible (conversion by Central Asian peoples from the twelfth century through the Sumra and Samma reigns; most converted in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries; mass conversion under Jam Nizamuddin about 1461) and are told as one narrative without adding anything; they are not rival accounts. Boivin's point that "before the eighteenth century 'the people who identified themselves as Muslims were few', probably because the name was restricted to the sayyids" is kept as a statement about the name, opened with "Even so".
- Partition migration total: "Estimates of their number differ: the general consensus is about a million, though it may have been closer to 0.8 million, if not fewer." Basis: team "He puts the migrants at closer to 0.8 million, if not fewer, against a general consensus of about a million" (the consensus given as the established position, the variant in one clause because the number matters).
- Arab conquest as a punitive expedition: given as "a myth" (only Rizvi addresses it: "Rizvi calls it 'a myth' that the conquest began as a punitive expedition against 'the ruling Raja Dahir'"); no source in the team version holds the other view.
- Watson's agricultural thesis and Flaskerud's portrait of Ali: each a single scholar's view, kept with "In one view" (team: "He reports Andrew Watson's view"; "Boivin reports Ingvild Flaskerud's view").
- Two Firuz Shah episodes (Boivin's siege of Thatta in 1367 with Jam Juna II; Bhandari's three campaigns against a son of Jam Anzar, after which the country was given back) are kept as two separate sentences in two paragraphs and not merged into one event.

## Left out, and why

- Uncertain readings (rule 5): the Arabic title of the Chach Nameh ("one word ... uncertain in the printed text"); Chishti's unnamed Arab commander's name and Walid's years 705 to 715; the names of the eighth century hadith scholar and poet ("both names imperfectly printed"; their death years go with the names, the point kept); the first rulers "the descendants of Bani Tamim Ansari, the reading uncertain" and their 139 years; the Sumra's standing "among the zamindars for their strength and following" ("in a passage partly illegible"); Sabuktigin's only route into India "by way of Sindh, a word before it being illegible"; the name of the raja who fled to Sindh; Mahmud's year 417; the year of Shihab al-Din's fourth campaign; Jam Anzar's year 735 and reign; the name Jam Bani Thatta (kept as "a son of Jam Anzar"); the derivation of "Jam" from Jamshid (through an unreadable word); the name of the jagir given to Jam Bayazid and Jam Ibrahim; Mirza Shah Beg's capture of Thatta in 927; the Arghun and Tarkhan years 930, 962, 965 and Muhammad Baqi's eighteen years ("These years, and the eighteen, are all uncertain"); Jani Beg's year 1001 AH; Shah Jahan's year 1037; Qubad Khan's name and the "foster kin of Shaikh Mir" passage (so the officer left to hold Thatta in the pursuit of Dara Shikoh is not given); the Turkish word *otaq* in Shah Abdul Latif's verse ("its printed form uncertain"); the tribute figures 21, 14, 11 and 7 lakhs ("several of the printed figures imperfect": kept as "fell from reign to reign ... always in arrears"); every year of Sorley's Kalhora and Talpur table (pp. 201 to 202, "its figures imperfectly printed") except 1701 and 1778, which the firm stage list on p. 35 also gives; Erskine's "about 1757" (given as "only a few years before"); the European record gap "between 1662, as the damaged printed figure is read, and 1758"; the year of the British annexation lost on p. 248; the Hijri year of the Sumra in the Tarikh-i-Tahiri.
- Samma Jams: the list is given as names in order, without reign lengths, because the team version says "with many names and figures uncertain in the printed text" without saying which; Jam Bani Thatta, whose name is flagged separately, is dropped. Flag for V1: if V1 judges the names themselves too uncertain, the list can be cut to the Jams attested elsewhere in the team version (Jam Anzar, Jam Nizamuddin known as Jam Nanda, Jam Salahuddin, Jam Firuz).
- 1872 census total: "a population printed as 2,333,527 million" and the same sentence's opening that "Sindh's population statistics are not available in that census" left out; the four group figures are kept. Flag for V1.
- Source apparatus: who writes "Sind", "Sindh" or "the Sind"; the Usborne note's attribution and its link to Ranjha's blessing ("seems to gloss"); Sorley's glossary entry (kept only as "a Sindhi tribe that was at one time ruler of Sindh"); Major Goldney, Callendar, Spiller, Withington, Hamilton, Erskine, Moreland named as sources (their content kept, Callendar as "described", Erskine as "one observer"); the chronicle table of contents entries (their content, Dara Shikoh's coming from Thatta and the conquest of Thatta with Jani Beg's coming, folded into the narrative); the alternative spellings Bakhar and Tatta; "The political history of the hurr ... belongs with the state, below" (signposting; the hurr are in the overview); the closing "So the books bring the province to the present day".
- Kept with a time anchor: Sorley's present ("as they are outside all the large towns today", "the only classic ... has yet produced") as 1940. Bhandari's own day as "the end of the seventeenth century" / "the late seventeenth century". Schaflechner's "within the past decade" as "in recent years" (his book's date is not in the team sections).
- "Muhajirs" glossed as "the Muslim refugees from India" from the team's own pairing of the Muhajirs with "the arrival of Muslim refugees from India"; "Subedar" glossed "the governor"; "kardar" glossed in its plain dictionary sense.
