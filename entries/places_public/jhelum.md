## Sacred Trees

Jhelum has a place in a legend of Alexander the Great. It comes from a book said to contain his letters, letters that were written after his death. In one of them an old Indian man visited Alexander in Jhelum after his war with Porus, an Indian king, and told him of "two talking trees, one male and the other female at a little distance from Jhelum". Alexander, the story goes, then visited the trees and found them indeed talking to each other.

Another sacred tree is spoken of near Jhelum, though only from hearsay. One man has heard of a bodhi tree near Jhelum, and of another in Islamabad. A bodhi tree is the sacred fig, the kind of tree under which the Buddha is said to have attained enlightenment.

## The River and the Town

The town and its river share a name. In the reign of the emperor Aurangzeb, the river called the Bihat passed below the town of Jhelum, and there it took the name Jhelum. The same name runs through one family's history. An ancestor of the family, Ghasita Ram, moved "from the Jhelum" sometime in the late nineteenth century. Here "the Jhelum" may mean the river rather than the town.
