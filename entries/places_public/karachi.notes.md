## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **The temple at Clifton and Guru Nanak.** Public: "those at Clifton and at Ram Bagh are both believed to have stood in his time, and he may have visited them, though accounts differ on whether the Clifton temple is the temple of the sea god that Nanak is said to have visited." Basis (d): team: "Later Sikh writers have asserted' that the temple of the sea god which Nanak visited was the temple at Clifton, and objects that it stood 'on the mainland'"; and "along with the temple at Clifton, the Ram Bagh temple must have been a historical Hindu temple in Nanak's time, and that Nanak must have visited it as well". No shrine position; no majority; the disagreement is given in one clause without choosing. "must have" softened to "believed" and "may have", less certainty than the team gives, not more.
- **The age of the Ram Bagh temple.** Public: "The temple stood before Partition, though accounts of its exact age differ." Basis: both accounts agree it is pre-Partition (team: "an old man told him the temple was one hundred and fifty years old"; the officer: "it existed before Partition"); the one hundred and fifty years and the remark that it "seemed to be the standard age of all temples here" are dropped, since the remark itself casts doubt on the figure and "here" is not clear.
- **When Zauqi Shah came to Karachi.** Public: "The Chishti master Muhammad Zauqi Shah was present at the first meeting of the Muslim League in Karachi in 1907. Accounts differ on when he made the city his home." Basis: the 1907 meeting is what both books give (team: Ernst and Lawrence "was therefore present at the first meeting of the Muslim League in Karachi in 1907"; "Rozehnal likewise records that he attended"); on the settling, Ernst and Lawrence alone give both "finally moved to Karachi" (before 1907) and "After the birth of Pakistan, Zauqi soon settled in Karachi", so (d): no choice, one clause, neither variant set beside the other.
- **The distance to Hinglaj.** Public: "Hinglaj is about two hundred and fifty kilometres from the coastal city, and the road route to it starts in Karachi." Basis: minor, and the team notes judge the two figures may measure different things (team: Khalid "about two hundred and fifty kilometres from the coastal city of Karachi"; Schaflechner "about 240 kilometers long" from Narayanpur). The figure measured from Karachi is the one a reader of a Karachi article needs; the Narayanpur figure is dropped silently.
- **Spellings, minor.** Manghopir (Abbas) used throughout for Mango Pir (Schimmel) and Mangho Pir (Boivin). Manzur Niyazi for Nyazi/Niyazi. Bahauddin given without a second name, because the team version has him as Bahauddin Qawwal, Bahauddin Khan and (text layer) Bahauddin Qutubuddin, and the public must not merge or choose between these.
