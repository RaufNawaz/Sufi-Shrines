## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **The date of Guru Nanak's birth.** Public: "Guru Nanak was born in Talwandi in 1469. Accounts differ on the day, which is given as 14 April or as 20 October." Basis (d): team: Dhillon "On 14 April 1469"; the chronology in *Walking with Nanak* "October 20, 1469". No shrine position is recorded in the team version, each date has one source, and neither book is devoted to the site, so no choice: the agreed year, and the two days as the range in one clause.
- **The place of his birth.** Public: "One account places his birth elsewhere, but the other writers of the *janamsakhis* and Sikh scholars are unanimous that he was born in Rai Bhoi di Talwandi." Basis (b): team: Meherban "claims that Guru Nanak was born in Dera Chahal and not Rai Bhoi di Talwindi", while other *janamsakhi* writers and Sikh scholars "are unanimous that Nanak was born in Rai Bhoi di Talwindi"; Dhillon also places it at Talwandi. Dera Chahal is not named, so the rejected variant is not set beside the established one.
- **Whether Nanak ever left.** Dropped as a variant. Team: "Jeeta Singh of Manora told Khalid that Nanak had never left Nankana Sahib". Every narrative source has him leave and return; the public keeps Jeeta Singh's November festival and his words "from Punjab, Nankana Sahib", not the claim that Nanak never left.
- **How many gurdwaras.** Public: "Nankana Sahib has six or seven gurdwaras, by different counts, most of them associated with incidents from the life of Guru Nanak". Basis (d): team: "There are seven Gurdwaras in Nankana Sahib, most of which are associated with incidents from the life of Baba Guru Nanak" and "a total of six gurdwaras associated with Guru Nanak at Nankana Sahib". One author, two books, no shrine count; the range without choosing.
- **How many Sikh households.** Public: "about two hundred households" (Overview) and "the community grew almost sevenfold, from only about thirty to forty Sikh houses to about two hundred". Basis (b): about two hundred is given by *A White Trail* ("In about ten years, the population in Nankana Sahib increased to about two hundred houses") and *Walking with Nanak* ("about two hundred households"), and falls inside Kartar's "two hundred to two hundred and fifty"; the thirty to forty and the sevenfold growth come from the same page run of *A White Trail* (pp. 174 to 175) and are consistent with it. Kartar's "fifty" a few years before is the minor variant and is dropped.
- **When and from where the families came.** Public: most are "recent migrants from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa", families came after the wars of 1965 and 1971 and the Babri Mosque demolition of 1992, and "Families arrived at different times", followed by each family the team version names (Kartar's among the first, Rajbir and his cousin from Parachinar, Boota Singh's from the Tribal Areas, Inderjit's in 1965, Madan Singh's since the late seventies, Baljeet's among the latest). These are facts about different families, not rival accounts of one event, so each is kept; no single date is given for "the" migration.
- **Pilgrim numbers at Guru Nanak's birthday.** Public: "Thousands come ... from all over the world. Official estimates put the number at ten to fifteen thousand every year ...; in 2011, when it ran from 8 to 10 November, only three thousand were reported to be attending."
