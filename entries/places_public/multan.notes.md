## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Who gave Khizr Khan Multan.** Public: "By most accounts it was Timur who gave Khizr Khan the government of Lahore, Multan and Dipalpur." Basis (b): two of the three statements name Timur (team: "Chishti writes that Timur made him governor of Multan and Dipalpur"; "Bhandari that Timur granted him the government of Lahore, Multan and Dipalpur"); the third ("Sultan Muhammad Shah bin Sultan Firuz Shah gave four provinces", Bhandari p. 241) is dropped, together with its list of the four governors, one of whose names is uncertainly printed. Lahore, Multan and Dipalpur taken from the clear Bhandari passage, since Chishti's "Dipalpur" is uncertainly printed.
- **Who governed Multan when Ghazi Tughluq took the throne.** Public: "The rise of the Tughluqs is tied to Multan, though accounts differ on who governed the city when Ghazi Tughluq marched on Delhi; he killed Khusrau Malik and took the throne as Ghiyas-ud-Din." Basis (d): one account each (team: "Chishti makes Ghazi Beg Tughluq himself the governor of the suba of Multan"; "Bhandari has Ghazi al-Malik march on Delhi in league with Bahram, governor of Multan"), no shrine position, neither earlier nor devoted to the place; no choice made and no variant set beside the other. "Raised an army in the Punjab" left out (the Punjab uncertainly printed).
- **Balban's son and the Mongols.** Public: the prince "came out of Multan of necessity and after a short fight met a martyr's death, in what is remembered as the Battle of Multan." Basis (b): Bhandari ("met a martyr's death after a short fight") and Abbas ("killed in the Battle of Multan") against Chishti alone ("Balban's son Muhammad Shah drove the invaders out of Multan"), whose sentence also rests on an uncertain reading ("most of Multan, the reading uncertain"); the team notes judge these may be different episodes, so Chishti's is dropped, not merged.
- **Where Uch lies.** Team: Schimmel "in Ucch, northeast of Multan"; Rozehnal "seventy miles southwest of Multan". Minor for a visitor to Multan: the public gives only what every account agrees, "Uch, a town near Multan" (Rizvi: "Uch, near Multan").
- **The last Langah years** (945 against 932) and **Aurangzeb's stay** (1069 against 1068): every one of these years is uncertainly printed, so all are left out (rule 5); the disagreement falls away. The days of the month (seventh and twelfth of Muharram, twenty second of Dhu'l-Qaʿda, twenty fifth of Dhu'l-Hijja) are not marked uncertain and are kept.
- **The commander defeated by Sultan Husain Langah** (Barbak Shah / Baba Shah): both readings uncertain; public says "a commander".
- **Whose is the Shams Tabriz shrine at Multan.** Public: "Tradition in India holds that Shams-i Tabriz, Maulana Rumi's spiritual beloved, was flayed alive by the *mollas* in Multan, where his shrine is still venerated, though there is no settled view whether the Multan shrine is his or that of a local Sufi of the same name." Basis (d): Schimmel records the tradition, Abbas leaves the identification open; no shrine position is given in the team version. The Overview keeps the tradition framed as tradition.
- **Ivanow's title** (Shams Tabriz / Shums Tabrez of Multan): bibliographic apparatus; the reference to Ivanow's study is left out altogether.
- **"The emperor"** (team question 1): kept unnamed, as the team version keeps him; neither Humayun nor Aurangzeb is named from general knowledge.
- **1818** (Chishti p. 580). Team: "in the year written out in words as eighteen eighteen an army under Kharak Singh was sent to conquer Multan". Given as "In 1818".
