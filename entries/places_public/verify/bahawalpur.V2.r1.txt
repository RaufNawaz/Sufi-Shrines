- Glosses dropped from the English are deliberate under the brief: Syeds as "descendants of the Prophet Muhammad", and Nawab as "a title of Muslim rulers and nobles". Neither counts as an omission.
- Every number and date matches: sixty miles southwest, about 165 km (given in words), twelve boys, nine nights, and 28, 29 and 30 April and 2 May 2001. Every name matches: Uch Gilanian, Bulle Shah, Wahid Bakhsh, Civil Secretariat, Bahawalnagar, Navratri, Katas, Rahim Yaar Khan, Ayesha Siddiqa, Mas'ud Hasan Shahab, Khitta-i pak Uch and Urdu Academy.
- Overview: ملتان کی طرح ("like Multan") stands for "with Multan". Paragraph 3 renders the same phrase as ملتان کے ساتھ ساتھ, which is closer. Either is acceptable.
- Saints, Syeds and Patrons: نواب ہی کا ہاتھ and سرائیکی پٹی ہی کا both use an emphatic ہی that the English does not have. This is idiomatic and changes no fact, but a plain نواب کا ہاتھ would be more exact.
- "buses of Hindu pilgrims" became یاتریوں سے بھری بسیں ("buses full of pilgrims"). This is the natural Urdu idiom and is acceptable.
