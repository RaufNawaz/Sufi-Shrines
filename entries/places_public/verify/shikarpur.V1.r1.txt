VERDICT: PASS

ISSUES: none

NOTES:
1. Overview, "the core of merchant networks that reached from Central Asia to overseas countries": the team version says the networks "allowed them to control commerce between Central Asia and overseas countries", which is about commerce between the two, not the networks' geographic reach. It is a fair implication, not a new fact, but a closer wording that still leaves "them" unresolved would be: "the core of merchant networks for commerce between Central Asia and overseas countries".
2. A City of Merchants, "Moneylenders worked there beside its merchants.": the team version only says the town is mentioned "in connection with its merchants and moneylenders". "Worked there beside" goes a little further but is supported in substance. A closer wording would be: "It was a town of merchants and moneylenders."
3. Communities and Creeds, the quotation "the locale for an amazingly rich dialogue between different creeds" stands with no speaker at all, so the reader cannot tell whose words they are, and "amazingly" can read as the narrator's own view. The brief allows a quotation to stay with a speaker but no surname. Optional wording: "it made up a triangle that was, as one historian of the Hindu Sufis put it, "the locale for an amazingly rich dialogue between different creeds"." Or drop the quotation marks and paraphrase, as the Overview already does.
4. Repetition (taste): the Overview's commerce sentence comes back almost word for word in A City of Merchants ("hub of international commerce", "core of merchant networks"), and the baniyas sentence comes back in Communities and Creeds. With a team version this short some overlap can't be avoided, but one of each pair could be shortened. Also "Shikarpur was also the birthplace of Nimano Faqir, who was born into a rich Hindu family of the town" says "born" twice; possible wording: "Nimano Faqir was born there, into a rich Hindu family of the town."
5. Checked and fine: Utradi or Siraiki as the northern dialect of Sindhi; "a city of northern Sindh", which rightly softens Boivin's "the northern city" in the narrator's voice and keeps it as the book's view in Shikarpur in Books; the gloss of baniyas is a definition only; "prosperous" for "well-to-do"; Sami is not called Hindu; "them" and "at this time" are left unresolved and no period is named; no identification is given for Nimano Faqir, Sami or Humayun; none of the three attached sites is named; the "in Books" section has no author names and follows the house style of Sukkur and Bahawalpur, both of which passed; no citations, page references, archive or process language, or time anchors; no em or en dashes and no hyphenated words in the prose; the bibliography is the team version's three items, verbatim and in order. The team version records no disagreement and no [OCR?] or uncertain reading, so rules 4 and 5 are not engaged.
