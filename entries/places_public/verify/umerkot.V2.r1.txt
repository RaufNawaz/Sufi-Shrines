NOTES:
- Overview: "راجپوت قبیلے اس پر آپس میں لڑتے رہے" adds "آپس میں" (among themselves) to "fought over by Rajputs". The History section says the fighting was between two Rajput groups, so this does not change the meaning. It is acceptable, but "راجپوت قبیلے اس پر لڑتے رہے" would follow the English more closely.
- Umerkot in Books: the Latin spellings "Umarkot and Omarkot" cannot be kept, because the brief allows no Latin letters. Rendering them as "لاطینی حروف میں اس قصبے کا نام ایک سے زیادہ طرح لکھا گیا ہے" is a reasonable adaptation, not an omission.
- These dropped glosses are allowed by the brief and are not omissions: the gloss of Rajputs ("clans of a warrior caste"), of pir, of Rajab ("the seventh month of the Islamic year"), of the untouchables ("outside and below the Hindu caste order", since اچھوت is ordinary Urdu) and of suba. Rano and sarkar keep an Urdu gloss or explanation, as they should.
- Everything else matches the English: all names, numbers (1351, the fifth of Rajab, 1907, four, around 600 kilometres) and qualifiers (خیال ہے for "is thought", پہلے for "formerly", تقریباً for "around"). The headings match, and the legends (Mumal, Omar of Omarkot) are still told as legends.
