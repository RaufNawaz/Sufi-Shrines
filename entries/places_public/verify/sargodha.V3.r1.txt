1. [ambiguity / unidiomatic collocation] "ضلع سرگودھا میں ایک مقبرہ واقع ہے جس سے ایک شخص کے آبا و اجداد وابستہ تھے۔" -> The whole article is this one sentence, and to a native reader it is opaque. (a) "ایک شخص" has no referent anywhere in the text; in a reference work an unnamed "ایک شخص" reads as a leftover placeholder, and the reader is left asking "کون شخص؟". (b) "سے ... وابستہ تھے" is a calque of English "associated with": for people and a tomb, وابستہ suggests attendants, devotees or a professional tie, and does not say what the actual relation was (buried there? custodians? descendants of the saint?). (c) "ایک مقبرہ ... ایک شخص" repeats ایک twice in a short sentence. The idiomatic Urdu for a family's connection is "کا تعلق تھا". The missing name and the exact relation cannot be supplied without adding facts, so the writer must put them in from the source. The best wording possible without new facts: -> "ضلع سرگودھا میں ایک مقبرہ ہے جس سے ایک شخص کے آبا و اجداد کا تعلق تھا۔" (Once the source names the person, replace "ایک شخص" with that name, e.g. "جس سے [نام] کے آبا و اجداد کا تعلق تھا۔")
