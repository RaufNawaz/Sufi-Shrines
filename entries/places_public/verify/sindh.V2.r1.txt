NOTES (style only, not counted as issues):
- Overview: "its faqirs, the wandering holy men" is rendered "آوارہ گرد فقیر". In ordinary Urdu, آوارہ گرد leans pejorative ("vagrant"); "گھومتے پھرتے فقیر" or "سیلانی فقیر" would be neutral. Under issue 6, "گھومتے پھرتے" is suggested for the yogis too; if both are used, vary one of them.
- Samma paragraph: the English "Jam Salahuddin, a kinsman of Jam Firuz whose sister was married to Sultan Muzaffar of Gujarat" could grammatically make the sister Firuz's. The Urdu settles it as Salahuddin's sister, which fits his raising armies from Gujarat. I accept it, but the writer should confirm it against the source.
- Mughal unit paragraph: in the English, the expenses belong to the "person of rank". In "جس کے اخراجات عموماً آمدنی سے بڑھ جاتے تھے" they attach to the office (منصب). The meaning is effectively the same.
- Rochaldas paragraph: the Urdu makes "some Hindus had already sent their wives and children to India..." its own sentence, so Rochaldas is no longer clearly its source. The English is ambiguous after the semicolon, so this is acceptable. Joining it to the previous sentence with "؛" would keep the English structure.
- Dropped glosses (دربار، زمیندار، مہاجر، منصب ذات، کاردار handled separately) and dropped italics are deliberate per the brief. The three headings correspond, and there is no verse. Every figure and date I checked matches the English (711, 905, 1228, 1351, 1367, 1461, 887ھ, 1592, 1739, 8 April 1758, 1781, 1843, 1852, 1866, the 1870s census figures, 21 and 6 per cent, 246,000, 500,000, 140,000, 14 January).
