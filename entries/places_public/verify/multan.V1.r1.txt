- Bibliography: identical to the team version's, item for item and in order (checked with diff). There are no en or em dashes. `check_place_description.py public multan --english-only`: ok, 0.58.
- Omissions: I swept the team version section by section and found no important omission. The material left out is either an uncertain reading (the Langah and Aurangzeb years, the tribute, the sums, Ghurak, Rai Sanhra, Sivi, Agnew, Kahn Singh, Abdali's birthplace, the London letter), a hedged reading (Kaiqubad's killing of Kaikhusrau at Rohtak, which the team itself says "leaves some doubt over who is meant"), a dropped variant (Khizr Khan's p. 241 grant, Chishti's Muhammad Shah driving out the Mughals, which also rests on "most of Multan, the reading uncertain"), or apparatus (index, contents, Ivanow's title). I accept each of these choices. Rule 4 handling of Uch, the Shams Tabriz shrine, the Tughluq governorship and Balban's son is sound, and no chains are merged.
- Present tense on dated observations (taste, but it touches precision): "Multan is one of the ancient cities of the Punjab, a city with a fort of brick" puts the late seventeenth century fort in the present, and "of the Punjab" narrows Bhandari's "one of the ancient cities". Possible fix: "Multan is one of the ancient cities, and by the end of the seventeenth century it had a fort of brick; it stands where three rivers merge before the Indus." Likewise, "and Multan has one" (Schimmel's women's shrine, a visit recalled decades ago) could read "and Multan had one".
- Overview: "and it was a commercial hub for national and international trade" follows a sentence anchored to the end of the seventeenth century, so it reads as dated to then. Dhillon's statement is undated. Consider starting a new sentence: "It was long a commercial hub ..." or "Multan was a commercial hub ...".
- "Then came the Ghurids." and "The rise of the Tughluqs" use dynasty names the team version never uses (it has "Ghori" and "Ghazi Tughluq"). The identification is implicit in the team paragraph, but "Then came Ghori." and "The rise of Ghazi Tughluq" would be closer.
- "Multan later became a dependency of the Fatimids of Egypt, until Sultan Mahmud dealt a blow to Ismaʿili power there." The team does not order the Fatimid dependency after the Carmathians, so "later" is an inference. "Until" leans on the team overview's "broke Ismaʿili power", which is acceptable. A safer form: "Multan also became a dependency of the Fatimids of Egypt before Sultan Mahmud dealt a blow to Ismaʿili power there." The same applies to the Overview's "until Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni broke".
- "The Carmathians, or Qaramita": the team uses Schimmel's "Carmathians" and Bhandari's "Qaramita" separately and never equates them. They are the same name in English and Arabic forms, so I accept this as a definitional gloss.
- "Under Balban the city belonged to the heir to the throne." The team version gives Sindh in jagir and Multan to administer. "was in the charge of the heir to the throne" is more exact.
- "its *ʿulamaʾ* ... are remembered for refusing a *fatwa*": "remembered for" adds fame. "once refused a *fatwa*" is the team's sense.
- "Music has long sounded at the tombs and hospices of Multan." This is close to the brief's banned "has a long history", though the material does run from Nizam ad-Din's time to the present. Possible fix: "Music is heard at the tombs and hospices of Multan, then as now."
- "The Suraj temple was once claimed to be the biggest in the subcontinent, before Mahmud destroyed its giant idol." "Before" draws a time link the team does not make. Possible fix: "The Suraj temple was once claimed to be the biggest in the subcontinent; Mahmud damaged it and destroyed its giant idol."
- "others of the Nawabs' family were captured ... and brought to Lahore": the team has "Sarfraz Khan ... and others were captured with their women and children, came to Lahore". "Family" and "brought" are both slight inferences. Possible fix: "others of the Nawabs were captured with their women and children and came to Lahore".
- "the rebellion was given a religious colour against the infidel Ranjit Singh": now that the team's attribution is gone, "the infidel" can read as the site's own voice. Possible fix: "against Ranjit Singh as an infidel".
- "the families arrange the yearly rituals there, the Eid and the Muharram ceremonies": the source's "annual rituals such as" has become an exhaustive list. Possible fix: "yearly rituals there such as the Eid and the Muharram ceremonies".
- "the Puratan account of Nanak's first journey ... places Tulamba ... in Multan": the team has Dhillon locating it "in the Puratan account". Acceptable, but "in the Puratan account ... Tulamba, the modern Makhdumpur, is placed in Multan" keeps the same uncertainty about who does the placing.
- "Families of Muftis, scholars of religious law, and of Gardezis": the team has "the Muftis and Gardezis". "Families of" is a reasonable reading of family names, but the gloss "scholars of religious law" then fits awkwardly. Consider dropping the gloss.
- Informant names (Parvati Devi, Shaila Bibi, Usman): the team introduces them as names Khalid gives ("An informant he calls"), because his author's note says that informants' names were changed. Dropping the framing follows the Lahore public article. I record this for Rauf as a minor point, not an issue.
- Several words the team prose gives as plain (the Langahs' "revolt" p. 324, the Mongol "attacks" p. 297, "to guard Multan" p. 294, Dara "from Multan" p. 52) carry [OCR?] in the team notes' Persian glosses. The team version treats them as clear, so the public article may too. Noted only so the team knows.
- "Multan's history before Islam is told as a long one." "Is told" is mild source narration. "Multan's history before Islam is a long one." would do, since the hedges that follow ("it is said", "legend has it") carry the uncertainty.
