## Overview

Sukkur is a city of northern Sindh. On an island in the River Indus, within the city, lies Sadh Bela, a Hindu *tirtha*, or holy place of pilgrimage. With Rohri and Shikarpur, Sukkur makes one corner of a triangle of towns. Rohri has shrines of its own and was the home of two mystic poets.

Sukkur was a halt in the pursuit of Dara and the seat of a *faujdari*, a district military command. In the twentieth century it was a city of riots, and the first victim of the Sukkur riots was Bhagat Kanwar Ram. Dadi Dhan was born in Sukkur, while Rohri was the birthplace of Rochaldas, in 1879, and of Bedil, a poet and Sufi, or Muslim mystic, who died in 1872. Another poet of Rohri, Janullah, wrote in Persian in the second half of the eighteenth century, and the singer Alan Faqir is reported to have sung on the buses and trains between Rohri and Sehwan Sharif in his youth.

## History

Sukkur has a place in the story of the pursuit of Dara. News reached the armies pursuing him that Dara had passed Bhakkar and, on the 25th of Muharram, the first month of the Islamic year, had halted at Sukkur. From the stage where the news came, the way to Sukkur was reckoned at 63 *kos*, an old measure of distance, and at nearly a hundred *kos* by the far side of the river.

Shaikh Mir, Diler Khan and Qubad Khan then crossed toward Sukkur. On the 5th of Safar, the second month of the Islamic year, Shaikh Mir reached a place ten *kos* from the town. The forest was thick, the thorny trees were dense and the stages of the way were hard, so that many beasts perished and the baggage did not arrive. On the sixth they reached Sukkur.

Shaikh Mir stayed one day at Sukkur. The *zamindars*, the local landholders, told him that 25 *kos* from Sukkur a road branches off toward Qandahar. Saf Shikan Khan appointed an officer to the *faujdari* of Sukkur.

In the twentieth century Sukkur was a city of riots. The Sukkur riots took place between December 1939 and February 1940, and their first victim was Bhagat Kanwar Ram. Rohri too was among the cities of the riots.

Sukkur, after the Manzilgah riots, stands with the other cities of northern Sindh in contrast to Sehwan, where "no communal violence had ever been registered". Fateh Muhammad Sehwani, a man of letters of Sehwan who lived from 1882 to 1942, was "himself a Muslim who was involved in the Manzilgah Affair", and he "advocated the Hindus' claim".

## Holy Places

In Sukkur itself stands Sadh Bela, the Hindu *tirtha* "in the city of Sukkur on an island in the River Indus". The island shrine, whose name is also written Sadh Belo, has its own story of how it began.

Rohri has shrines as well. It holds the original sanctuary of Sain Vasan Shah. His *darbar*, or shrine, is in Ulhasnagar, and another stands in Rohri, on the bank of the Indus River. The shrine of Isarlal is also at Rohri. Kandri is a small town in Rohri *taluqa*, a subdivision of a district, in the district of Khairpur.

## Communities

Sukkur, Rohri and Shikarpur form the corners of one triangle. As one historian put it, "Still another experience was to be born in Shikarpur or in Rohri, which was another story entirely". Rohri is also counted among the places of Hindu settlement.

Rohri was the birthplace of Rochaldas, born there in 1879, in the north of Sindh, to a family that the writers of his life call modest. His eldest brother became chief officer of the municipality of Rohri. It is related that Rochaldas once found himself "wandering high in the sky above the port on the Indus at Rohri". Bedil, a Sufi, was also born in Rohri, and Rochaldas was born there seven years after Bedil's death.

Sukkur was the birthplace of Dadi Dhan, whose father was a famous lawyer. There was also an orphanage in Sukkur.

## Poets and Singers

Rohri was the home of two mystic poets, roughly a century apart. The first is Bedil of Rohri, who died in 1872. One scholar of Islamic mysticism called him "a prolific, though not very outstanding, writer in five languages".

The second is Janullah of Rohri, a Suhrawardi, that is, a follower of the Suhrawardiyya, a Sufi order. He was "one of the outstanding Persian poets of Sind from the second half of the eighteenth century", and he was among those who composed songs on the martyrdom of Shah Inayat.

Rohri also stands at one end of a singer's road. Alan Faqir "is reported to have sung on the buses and trains between Rohri and Sehwan Sharif in his youth". He also sang at the *dargahs*, the Sufi shrines, of Sindh, and he confirmed his "roving-minstrel lineage".

## Sukkur in Books

A Persian history written in Aurangzeb's reign names Sukkur only in the pursuit of Dara, as a halt, a river crossing, a place on the road to Qandahar and the seat of a *faujdari*. It says nothing of any shrine, temple or holy man there.

A modern retelling of the *janamsakhis*, the traditional stories of Guru Nanak's life, names the town as a stage on a journey: from Multan the travellers went "to Sukkur from where he turned southwards along the river Indus". A study of the Hindu temple of Hinglaj Devi names Sukkur for the island *tirtha* of Sadh Bela.

A scholar of Islamic mysticism says nothing of Rohri itself and knows it as the home of two poets. For a study of the female voice in Sufi ritual, it is one end of a singer's route to Sehwan Sharif.

A study of the Hindu Sufis of South Asia gives the two towns the most room. Its Sukkur is a city of northern Sindh, a city of riots and the birthplace of Dadi Dhan. Its Rohri is a port on the Indus, the birthplace of Rochaldas and of Bedil, and the seat of shrines of Sain Vasan Shah and of Isarlal.
