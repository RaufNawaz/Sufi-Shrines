## Overview

Shikarpur is a city of northern Sindh, where the northern dialect of Sindhi known as Utradi or Siraiki was spoken. It was a hub of international commerce and the core of merchant networks for commerce between Central Asia and overseas countries, and among its merchants the *baniyas*, members of the trading castes, were the dominant group. With Rohri and Sukkur, on both sides of the Indus River, it formed a triangle of towns where different creeds met in a rich dialogue.

## A City of Merchants

Shikarpur was a town of trade, of merchants and moneylenders. As a hub of international commerce, it was the core of merchant networks through which commerce between Central Asia and overseas countries was controlled.

One merchant of the town travelled as far as Punjab. Sami was born into a prosperous family in Shikarpur. He was himself a cloth merchant, and he travelled a great deal between Shikarpur and Amritsar in Punjab.

## Communities and Creeds

Among the town's merchants, the *baniyas* were the dominant group. Nimano Faqir was born there, into a rich Hindu family of the town.

The town was one corner of a wider meeting ground. Together with Rohri and Sukkur, on both sides of the Indus River, it made up a triangle that was, as one historian of the Hindu Sufis put it, "the locale for an amazingly rich dialogue between different creeds".

## Shikarpur in Books

To a study of the Hindu Sufis of South Asia, Shikarpur is the northern city of Sindh, where Utradi or Siraiki was spoken, a centre of commerce between Central Asia and overseas countries, a corner of the triangle of dialogue between creeds, and the birthplace of Nimano Faqir and of Sami. To a study of the Hindu temple of Hinglaj Devi, it is a merchant town, named in brief passages on its merchants and moneylenders, on Hindu settlement and on the demographics of merchants.

A third book knows the town only as a family origin: a man called Humayun "belongs to Thatta on his paternal side and Shikarpur on his maternal side".
