## Overview

Hingol National Park is the setting of the shrine of Hinglaj, the Hindu temple of the goddess Hinglaj Devi and a centre of pilgrimage in the part of Balochistan where Sindhi is spoken. Close to Hinglaj lies the district of Las Bela, where the Goddess's fame was spread. Lohanas from Las Bela, members of a Hindu trading caste, have chiefly made up a body known as the HSM. Shah Abdul Latif probably travelled to Hinglaj on pilgrimage, and the poetry of Sachal Sarmast names it among the sacred Hindu places.

## The Park and Las Bela

The shrine of Hinglaj lies within the Hingol National Park. Las Bela is a district close to Hinglaj, and in Las Bela the Tumbel spread the Goddess's fame.

Las Bela is also where the HSM began. The body was founded on 5 January 1986 at Bela, in Las Bela district, Balochistan, and from its beginning it has consisted mainly of Lohanas from the district of Las Bela. The Dewanis, a Sindhi Lohana family, also come from Las Bela.

## Pilgrimage and Poetry

Hinglaj is a goal of pilgrimage. Shah Abdul Latif probably travelled with the Nathpanthi jogis, Hindu ascetics of the Nath order, on a number of Hindu pilgrimages, among them those to Dwarka and to Hinglaj.

Hinglaj appears in poetry too. The poetry of Sachal Sarmast names a number of sacred Hindu places, among them Hinglaj, Sri Lanka, the Ganga, and Kasi or Kashi, the city of Banaras.

Hinglaj also has a place in a Theosophical account, Theosophy being a modern spiritual movement. Parsram "claimed that the universal wisdom is kept in Sindh, in Hinglaj to be exact". His claim belongs to a story of a universal and primordial wisdom through which humanity could reach immortality. That wisdom, the story holds, was lost many centuries ago except in one remote area, which was usually located in Tibet.
