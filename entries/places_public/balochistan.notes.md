## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **What *guāṭi* is.** Public: "In Balochistan such healing ceremonies are called *guāṭi*, though accounts of the word differ." Team: "In her text Abbas calls guāṭi the name in Balochistan for the healing ceremonies to which families bring emotionally disturbed women ... Her glossary defines it as 'A ritual for making vows at Sufi shrines in Baluchistan'." Both are one author's; no shrine position (a), no majority (b). Chosen under (c) in spirit: the definition given in the account of the practice itself (the passage on the *dhammāl*), over the one line glossary gloss; the vow ritual is not set beside it. Flag for V1: if V1 judges this a case for (d), the sentence should say only that the word names a ritual at Sufi shrines in Balochistan and that accounts of it differ.
- **Where Sindh ends.** Team: Khalid calls the Makran range "the boundary line between Sindh and Baluchistan"; "Badar Abro, whom Schaflechner quotes, makes the 'real Sindh' cover 'the valleys between Rohri and east Balochistan' ... Schaflechner gives this as Abro's claim about an ancient past." Treated as two statements of different kinds, as the team notes (question 2) suggest: the present day boundary is given as fact in Land and Routes ("From Manora one can see the Makran mountain range, the boundary line between Sindh and Balochistan"); the "real Sindh" is given only as a claim about an ancient past, unnamed, in Balochistan in Books ("One writer claims an ancient past in which the 'real Sindh' covered ..."). They are not set side by side and not merged.
