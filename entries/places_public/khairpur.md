## Overview

Khairpur was an emirate in Sindh, one of the princely states to which many Kayasht, a caste of scribes, migrated for work. Its rulers, the *amirs*, were staunch Shia Muslims. They built *imambaras*, halls for Shia mourning gatherings, in the nineteenth century, and they were nonetheless generous patrons of all the religious buildings. Two spiritual lineages of northern Sindh, the Darazi and the Jalalani, are found close to the town, and Daraza lies not far from it.

## The Emirate and Its Amirs

Khairpur was one of the princely states. Many Kayasht migrated to work in those states, and the Khairpur emirate in Sindh was among them.

The amirs of Khairpur were themselves staunch Shia Muslims, and in the nineteenth century they built beautiful *imambaras*. Yet their own Shia faith did not narrow their patronage: it did not prevent them from being generous patrons of all the religious buildings.

## The Landscape Around the Town

Close to Khairpur, in northern Sindh, is the Darazi *silsila*, a Sufi spiritual lineage. The *dargah*, or Sufi shrine, of the Jalalani *tariqa*, a Sufi path or order, is also located in northern Sindh close to Khairpur. Daraza, too, lies not far from Khairpur, in Sindh.

Halani lies between Nausharo Firuz and Khairpur, and Hingorja, near Khairpur, was the birthplace of Bhagwan K. Ajwani.
