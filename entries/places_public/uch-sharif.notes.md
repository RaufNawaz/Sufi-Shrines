## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Where Uch lies from Multan.** Public: "it lies seventy miles from Multan, though accounts differ on its direction from that city." Basis (d): team: Schimmel "places Ucch northeast of Multan"; Rozehnal "seventy miles southwest of Multan in Pakistan"; Rizvi "says only that it is near Multan". No shrine position, no majority (one each way, one neutral), no account devoted to the site, so no direction is chosen. The distance is given by one source and contradicted by none, so it stays; the direction is the disputed part. Elsewhere the public says only "near Multan" and "near Bahawalpur" (Boivin: "Utch near Bahawalpur in Punjab").
- **When the Qadiriyya came.** Public: "Its first missionaries settled at Uch in the late fifteenth century, though accounts differ on when the order first reached the Subcontinent". Basis: the only statement about Uch is team "the first missionaries of the Qadiriyya settled in Ucch in the late fifteenth century"; the other date ("the order was introduced in the late fourteenth century" in the Subcontinent, "naming no place") is about the Subcontinent, so the difference is given in one clause about the Subcontinent and the Uch date stands. Rozehnal's founder of the first Qadiri *khanaqah*, Shaykh Muhammad al-Husseini al-Jilani, is kept in a separate sentence and not made one of Schimmel's unnamed missionaries (team notes: "do not contradict each other", never equated).
- **The rivers at Uch.** Public, Overview: "Built on a hill overlooking the confluence of the Sutlej and Chenab rivers"; History: "Accounts differ over the rivers today. The Sutlej is said to have flowed from here until it was lost after the Indus Water Treaty was signed between India and Pakistan in 1960, and in Nanak's day, it is thought, these buildings stood intact with a mighty river flowing beside them." Basis (d): team: Rozehnal "on a hill overlooking the confluence of the Sutlej and Chenab rivers"; Khalid "used to flow from there 'before it was lost forever after the Indus Water Treaty was signed between India and Pakistan in 1960'". The siting on the hill is uncontested and stays as fact; whether the Sutlej still flows there is the disagreement, given in one clause, with the loss after 1960 hedged ("is said to") because it is the disputed account; it is kept because it is how the place changed. Khalid's "Nanak must have seen these buildings intact" is given as "it is thought".
- **Nanak's arrival from Kartarpur.** Public: "in one retelling of the visit Nanak had arrived only a day before, travelling from Kartarpur." Team: "In Khalid's reconstruction of the visit, 'Uch Sharif was one of the most famous Muslim shrines in Southern Punjab and Nanak had arrived here only a day ago, travelling from Kartarpur'". A reconstruction, so framed as a retelling, not as fact.
- **The Gilani ancestor (team question 1).** Public: "The genealogy of Hazrat Pir Muhammad Shah Sahib has the Gilani ancestor coming from Uch Sharif in the reign of Akbar Shah." Team: "Chishti cites a genealogy of Hazrat Pir Muhammad Shah Sahib for the statement that the Gilani ancestor came from Uch Sharif in the reign of Akbar Shah". The p. 526 sequel (to Multan, Fatima, "the territory of Uch") is left out under rule 5, since its link to Uch is "the name uncertain in the printed text" and the descendants' still being there "a phrase whose reading is also uncertain"; so the question of joining or opposing the two passages does not arise in the public text.
- **Baha ad-Din Zakaria "of Uch Sharif" (team question 2).** Public: "Baha ad-Din Zakaria, the Suhrawardi saint, is also named as a saint of Uch Sharif." Team: "Rozehnal calls Baha ad-Din Zakaria 'the well-known Suhrawardi saint of Uch Sharif'". Kept, since no source in the team version contradicts it, but in the softest wording the source allows ("is also named as"), since the team notes record that the archive places him at Multan. Rauf may prefer to drop it.
- **Abd al-Qadir Sani and 'Abd al-Qadir Jilani Thani; Hazrat Muhammad Ghaus and Shah Muhammad Ghaus (team question 3).** Kept apart, as the team keeps them: Thani (d. 1533, the famous Qadiri saint, eldest son of Shaykh Muhammad al-Husseini al-Jilani) in History; the descendants of Abd al-Qadir Sani who hold the *sajjada* in Custodians; the four sons of Hazrat Muhammad Ghaus beside the tomb of Shah Muhammad Ghaus in A City of Saints. None is equated with another.
- **"The Baba" of the lonely mosque.** Public: "another story tells of a Baba who found a lonely mosque in Uch." Team: "Rizvi writes that 'The Baba found a lonely mosque in Uch'"; team notes: "not identified with Baba Farid". Written as "a Baba", in a sentence apart from Baba Farid's fast, so the two are not merged.
- **The man initiated at Uch.** Public: "Local legends suggest that a man who went to Multan and Uch may have been formally initiated into the Suhrawardiyya there." Team: "'he went to Multan and Uch. At Uch it is possible that he was formally initiated into the Suhrawardiyya order as is suggested by local legends'"; the team calls him "one man". Unnamed, hedged.
- **Abdullah Shah and Bulle Shah.** Public: "The family of a man whose original name was Abdullah Shah were Syeds who had settled in Uch Gilanian, the town where Bulle Shah was born." Team: "'his family were Syeds who had settled in Uch Gilanian, the city of saints in Bahawalpur, where Bulle Shah was born'"; the team notes do not identify the man with Bulle Shah, and neither does the public text.
- **The founding legend.** Public: "In a story told of Shaikh Safiuʾd-Din Kaziruni, an order was given to mount a camel ... and there, it is said, the Shaikh founded a town later called Uch." Team: "told in connection with Shaikh Safiuʾd-Din Kaziruni ... 'the Shaikh founded a town later called Uch'"; team notes: the source does not make clear who gave or received the order, so the public, like the team, names neither. Framed as legend. Rizvi's verdict ("mythical", "typical of medieval tales relating to sufis") is kept only in Uch Sharif in Books, as the view of "a history of Sufism in India".
- **Spellings, minor.** Uch Sharif in the narrator's voice; Sayyid in the narrator's voice, Syed only where the team has it from Abbas (Abdullah Shah's family, the landowning Syeds); Bulle Shah as the team prints it (team question 6 on Bulleh Shah is Rauf's); Jalaluddin Surkhposh Bukhari as the team writes it; 'Abd al-Qadir Jilani Thani with the team's apostrophe.

## Uncertain or damaged readings (rule 5), left out

- Bhandari, p. 405, the people from whom Shihab al-Din took Uch ("a people whose name is uncertain in the printed text, read as Hatahata"): the campaign is kept ("took the town and left a deputy there"), the people is not named.
- Bhandari, p. 285, the march from Thatta toward Maldeo's country "apparently by way of Uch", and Uch "a dependency of Multan" ("whose reading is uncertain in the printed text"): both left out, in History and in Custodians.
- Chishti, p. 526, "the territory of Uch, the name uncertain in the printed text" and descendants "settled there still, a phrase whose reading is also uncertain": the whole sequel (Multan, Fatima, the gift of the territory) left out, since without Uch it says nothing of the place.
- Chishti, p. 526, the tomb passage "badly damaged in the printed text" (mosques and buildings kept flourishing, "something confiscated in the time of Alamgir"): left out.
- Chishti, p. 584, the Nawab's title "Khan Bahadur" (uncertain), "a boil" ("the word for the ailment also uncertain in print") and the physician "Ghulam Muhyi-ud-Din, the name uncertain in print": the story is kept with "a Nawab", "an ailment of the cheek that would not heal" and "a skilled physician", none of which rests on the uncertain words. The teller, Hazrat Shah Sardar Sahib, son of Hazrat Pir Muhammad Shah Sahib Gilani, is kept as a family name.
- Chishti, p. 584, the Faqirs "served the Sadat of Uch" is kept: the team gives it as plain fact, following the block's gloss, though the word after it is illegible.
