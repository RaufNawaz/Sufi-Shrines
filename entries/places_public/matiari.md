## Overview

Hala is a town at the heart of a fertile agricultural belt, a country of large feudal farms. It is the birthplace of the saint Shah Abdul Latif, who was born there into a Sayyid family, a family descended from the Prophet Muhammad. Women of the Kolhi and Bhil communities go to Shah Abdul Latif's shrine and can make a living on the farms of Hala. A legend of the Hindu Goddess Hinglaj holds that the town was once called Kohala.

## Legend and History

An older name for the town comes from a legend. An account of the origins of the Goddess Hinglaj tells that "Hala in Sindh was called Kohala in the former times", and that the Goddess was once the queen of the Kohala or Kohana area. Both the old name and the Goddess's reign over the land belong to that legend.

Hala is also the birthplace of a saint. Shah Abdul Latif was born in Hala, into a Sayyid family.

## The Shrine and the Farms

Women of the Kolhi and Bhil communities go to Shah Abdul Latif's shrine, and what draws them above all is the work that Hala offers. The town is the heart of a fertile agricultural belt, where they can make a living working on the large feudal farms, the estates of great landowners. Where the shrine itself stands is not settled: it is placed both in Hala and at Bhit Shah.
