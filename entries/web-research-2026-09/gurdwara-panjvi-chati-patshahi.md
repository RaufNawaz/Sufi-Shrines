# Gurdwara Panjvi Chati Patshahi
gurdwara-panjvi-chati-patshahi / researched 5 September 2026 / batch H · Punjab villages,
Nankana Sahib, Sialkot

## Verified findings

1. The primary published register of Sikh shrines in Pakistan documents this exact gurdwara at
   Nankana Sahib, confirming it commemorates **two separate Gurus in one paired shrine** — the
   fifth, Guru Arjan Dev, and the sixth, Guru Hargobind — rather than a conflation of the two
   into a single figure. This directly answers the batch brief's question: it is one complex
   with two distinct shrines, not a mistaken merger.

   > "GURDWARA 5TH AND 6TH GURU, AT NANKANA SAHIB, DISTRICT SHEIKHUPURA. Both of these
   > historical shrines are situated near Tambo Sahib on the road leading towards Janamasthan
   > Nankana Sahib. The shrine of Sat Gur Arjun Dev Ji is without a dome whereas the sacred
   > shrine of the Patshah of meeri peeri Guru Hargobind has been built with dome and both the
   > shrines have a common boundary wall."

   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (1998), p. 252, full-text scan.
   https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan — accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability: published book (full-text scan on archive.org) — citable.

2. The same source dates and details Guru Hargobind's visit specifically, matching the sheet's
   `event_year: 1613` and note exactly.

   > "The sixth Patshah came to visit Nankana Sahib in the month of Har Samvat 1670 (1613 AD)
   > while returning from Kashmir. The Guru's disciples established this Gur Mela at the site
   > where he had stayed. 13 ghumaon of land is said to be endowed to the Gurdham. The tree under
   > which Guru Dev Ji stayed, has withered away. A piece of that tree has been preserved in a
   > glass case and placed inside the dome of the shrine of the 6th Guru so that the visiting
   > Sangats may see it."

   Citation: same as #1, p. 252. Note: the source gives no comparable date or narrative for
   Guru Arjan Dev's own visit — only Guru Hargobind's 1613 visit is dated and explained here,
   which matches the sheet's Description (which also dates only the Hargobind visit) and its
   `event_note`.

## Conflicts

- **Checked and absent, not a contradiction:** the earlier numbered register of Sikh shrines
  in West Pakistan — a government publication predating Qaiser's book by 36 years — lists six
  gurdwaras at Nankana Sahib (Bal Lila, Janam Asthan, Kiara Sahib, Malji Sahib, Maulvi Patti,
  Tambo Sahib) but does **not** include a "5th and 6th Guru"/Panjvin-Chhevin Patshahi entry
  among them.

  Citation: Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan* (Department of
  Archaeology, Ministry of Education, Government of Pakistan, Karachi, 1962), Contents listing
  and Nankana Sahib section. https://archive.org/details/furg-sikh-shrines-in-west-pakistan-by-khan-mohammad-wal
  — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: official government publication — citable.

  This is reported as a gap, not resolved: it may mean the 1962 survey simply omitted a
  smaller/less-visited shrine within its selective register (its own list of Nankana gurdwaras
  is not exhaustive by its own presentation), or it may bear on how established the site was as
  a distinct, named gurdwara at that date. Either way, it means only one, not two, independent
  published sources were found describing this specific gurdwara.
- No conflict was found between Qaiser's account and the current entry's description of the
  paired domeless/domed shrines, the common boundary wall, or the 1613 Hargobind visit date —
  the current entry's wording closely tracks Qaiser's account (via intermediate web sources, not
  cited directly as such).

## Verdict

PARTIAL — one verified, independent, citable, on-point primary source (Iqbal Qaiser, 1998, p.
252) confirms the gurdwara honours two separate Gurus in a single paired complex and dates Guru
Hargobind's visit exactly as the sheet records. A second published register (Khan, 1962) was
checked but does not include this gurdwara at all, so the ≥2-independent-source bar for STRONG
was not met, though the single source found is a strong, directly-fetched, primary one.
