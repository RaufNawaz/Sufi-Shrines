## Verified findings

1. The account already cited generically in the current entry's bibliography was fetched
   directly and confirms the founding date, location, and grant amounts, but — importantly —
   also **explicitly equates** this temple with the much older Shivala Teja Singh temple, which
   the current entry treats as a separate, unrelated building.

   > "Constructed in 2007 in the Paris Road area of Sialkot, it was also known as Shawala Teja
   > Singh Temple." ... "The temple renovated in early 2017 with a grant of 2,000,000 rupees
   > from Punjab Provisional Tax." ... "The special grant was given through the minority MPA
   > Joseph Hakim Din due to the efforts of Hakem Ratan Lal Bhagat, Member of the District Peace
   > Committee."

   Citation: OB Bureau, "Did You Know There's A Jagannath Temple In Pakistan And Bangladesh
   Too!," *Odishabytes*, published 14 August 2020,
   https://odishabytes.com/theres-a-jagannath-temple-in-pakistan-and-bangladesh-too/amp/ —
   accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: regional online news/culture outlet — borderline
   under the reliability bar (not a major national paper, but a named, dated, byline-carrying
   publication, distinct from a personal blog); already relied on generically by the current
   entry, so treated here as the same tier the entry itself already uses, not upgraded.

2. The Sialkot district government's own website independently confirms official use of the
   name "Jagannath Temple" for a site in Sialkot, though it supplies no descriptive text beyond

   > narrative text accompanies the entry.

   Citation: District Government of Sialkot, Government of the Punjab, official website,

## Conflicts

- **This pass's own source directly conflicts with the current entry's central editorial
  judgment.** The current entry states: "The two are sometimes conflated in secondary
  sources... but the facts recorded for each... do not overlap, and this entry treats them as
  distinct pending stronger evidence otherwise." The Odishabytes account quoted above does not
  merely get redirected or confused by a search engine (as the current entry's Wikipedia
  example shows) — it makes the equation as a positive assertion in its own prose ("it was also
  known as Shawala Teja Singh Temple"), attached to the same 2007-construction/Paris-Road
  narrative the current entry otherwise treats as describing a distinct, newer building. This
  does not resolve which account is correct — a modern, government-funded 2007 temple and a
  reputedly ~1,000-year-old temple sealed at Partition are hard to square as literally the same
  building — but it is a second, independent instance (beyond the Wikipedia redirect already
  noted) of a citable-tier source treating the two names as one site, and should be weighed
  accordingly.
- **Ruled out, a name near-miss worth recording explicitly:** press coverage of an October 2025
  restoration announcement in Sialkot (Punjab Minister for Auqaf) discusses the "Shiwala Teja
  Singh" temple and a separate site called the "Khu of Puran Bhagat" (a well associated with the
  legendary Sindhi/Punjabi figure Puran Bhagat) as the two sites being restored — not the
  Jagannath Temple, and not "Puran Nagar." The similarity between "Puran Bhagat" and "Puran
  Nagar" (the current entry's stated location) is coincidental; these are different places, and
  this source does not mention the Jagannath Temple at all.
  Citation (for the ruling-out only, not otherwise used): *The Nation*, "Restoration of
  900-year-old Shiwala Teja Singh temple in Sialkot to begin soon: Arora," 28 October 2025,
  https://www.nation.com.pk/28-Oct-2025/restoration-900-year-old-shiwala-teja-singh-temple-sialkot-begin-soon-arora
  — fetched 5 September 2026; confirms it does not name the Jagannath Temple or Puran Nagar.
- **Ruled out, a separate near-miss already implied but now independently confirmed:** an
  Express Tribune report on a Hindu temple in Sialkot "reopen[ing]... after 72 years" was found
  in search results to be about the Shivala Teja Singh temple specifically (matching multiple
  independent secondary summaries), not the Jagannath Temple — but the article itself returned
  HTTP 403 on direct fetch and so is not cited as a verified source here, only recorded as
  checked and excluded.

## Verdict

PARTIAL — two sources were fetched directly (Odishabytes and the Sialkot district government's
own gallery page), but only the first carries any substantive, quotable content, and that
content actively conflicts with the current entry's own editorial decision to treat this temple
and Shivala Teja Singh as distinct. The government page independently confirms only that the
name "Jagannath Temple" is used officially, without adding facts. Given how thin the second
source is, this is reported as PARTIAL rather than STRONG, and the identity-conflation question
the current entry already flagged remains open — now with one additional citable source on the
"conflated" side of the ledger.
