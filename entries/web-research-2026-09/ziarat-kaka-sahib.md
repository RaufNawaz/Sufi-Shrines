# Ziarat Kaka Sahib
ziarat-kaka-sahib / researched 5 September 2026 / batch E · Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Lahore

## Verified findings

1. **An academic paper on exactly this saint exists and independently confirms the current
   entry's named teachers, verbatim.** Footnote 18 of a peer-reviewed history journal article
   states: "Abdullah Ansari Sultanpuri and Maulana Abdul Lateef Sultanpuri were among the
   teachers of Kaka Sahib. Both of them were experts the knowledge of the time." The main text
   separately has him sent "by his father to Qazi Abul Aala to acquire literacy and knowledge"
   and learning "Mishkat Sharif from Sheikh Akhuddeen Seljuki." All four names in the current
   entry's teacher list are corroborated independently here.
   Citation: Syed Umar Hayat and Altaf Ullah, "The Life and Times of Hazrat Sheikh Rahamkar Kaka
   Sahib R.A: An Overview," *Pakistan — Annual Research Journal*, Vol. 54 (2018), pp. 128–138
   (National Institute of Historical and Cultural Research, Quaid-i-Azam University, Islamabad),
   https://pscpesh.org.pk/PDFs/PJ/Volume_54/10-%20Life%20and%20times%20of%20Sheikh%20Rahamkar%20Hazrat%20Kaka%20Sahib%20(Dr.%20Syed%20Umar%20Hayat%20&%20Dr.%20Altaf%20Ullah)%2018%20July%202018.pdf
   — fetched and read directly (PDF), accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: academic paper —
   citable.

2. **The same paper independently gives the exact same construction year and builder as the
   current entry.** "After the *wisal* of Kaka Sahib R.A his son Sheikh Abdul Haleem R.A built
   a gorgeous tomb at his *mazar* in 1661 A.D." — citing Muhammad Shafi Sabir, *Shakhsiat-i-Sarhad*
   (Peshawar: University Book Agency, n.d.), p. 32.
   Citation: same as #1, p. 130.

3. **Independent corroboration of the four-Sufi-order lineage claim, in two forms.** As personal
   training: "As per some of the sources he was trained spiritually in the lines of *Qadria*,
   *Naqshbandia*, *Chishtia* and *Suhrawardia*." As ancestral inheritance: "Kaka Sahib's
   forefathers belonged to the traditions of *Qadria*, *Naqshbandia*, *Chishtia* and
   *Suhrawardia*. Hence, Kaka Sahib R.A might have inherited the impact of all those traditions
   of Sufis."
   Citation: same as #1, p. 130.

4. **Independent corroboration of the Uwaisi framing (no living, named pir).** The paper quotes
   Kaka Sahib's own son Sheikh Abdul Haleem's book directly: asked "who is your pir?", "Kaka
   Sahib R.A did not mention any name... 'My Maslak is slavery of Allah…'" and states plainly
   "according to most of the sources, he was an *Awaisi* in Sufi tradition."
   Citation: same as #1, pp. 130–131.

5. **Independent corroboration of the mausoleum's decorative programme**, in near-identical
   language to the current entry: "his white mausoleum has beautiful mosaic work and floral
   pattern all over inside the structure in Mughal architecture style."
   Citation: Sher Alam Shinwari, "Kaka Sahib shrine – a place of spiritual satisfaction for
   many," Dawn, 26 July 2015, https://www.dawn.com/news/1196455 — fetched directly, accessed
   5 September 2026. Reliability: established press — citable.

6. **The shrine is administered independently of any government department, including Auqaf —
   contrary to this pass's research brief, which expected an Auqaf connection.** "A 13-member
   Darbar council, which was formed in 2003," runs the shrine, and it remains "not under the
   control of any government department including 'Auqaf'."
   Citation: same as #5.

7. **Khushal Khan Khattak's discipleship is corroborated by three independent, directly fetched
   sources**, not just one: "Notable disciples included... Legendary Pakhtun warrior and poet
   Khushal Khan Khattak and his father Shahbaz Khan were also disciples" (Dawn, 2015); "His
   pupils... included... Legendary Pakhtun warrior and poet Khushal Khan Khattak and his father
   Shahbaz Khan" (Dawn, 2016); and "Famous national leader and poet Khushal Khan Khattak was
   Kaka Sahib's *mureed* and his (Khushhal Khan Khattak's) family possessed much devotion for
   Kaka Sahib R.A" (academic paper, 2018), which further names "Khushal Khan Khattak wrote in
   his '*Biaz*' that Hazrat Sheikh Rehamkar Kaka Sahib R.A called him one month before his death
   and gave different advices."
   Citations: (a) Sher Alam Shinwari, Dawn, 26 July 2015, https://www.dawn.com/news/1196455;
   (b) "Devotees throng shrine of Kaka Sahib on his 374th Urs," Dawn, 2 May 2016,
   https://www.dawn.com/news/1255748; (c) Hayat and Ullah (2018), p. 131, as in #1. All three
   fetched directly, accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: (a)/(b) established press, (c)
   academic paper — all citable.

8. **A precise Gregorian birth date is given, and it supports 1575 over 1576.** "According to
   Bahadur Shah Zafar, he was born in the night between 30th Shabaan and 1st Ramzaan in 983
   Hijri (4 December 1575)" — citing Syed Bahadur Shah Zafar Kakakhel, *Sheikh Rahamkar Kaka
   Sahib* (Peshawar: Junaid Paper Mart, 2007), p. 65.
   Citation: same as #1, p. 129.

9. **The father, Bahadur Baba, is independently named and given a specific silsila.** "His
   father Sheikh Bahadur Baba R.A was the *Qutub-i-Alam* of the time and his *silsila* (chain of
   Sufism) was *Suhrawardia*."
   Citation: same as #1, p. 129.

10. **Neither the KP Directorate General of Archaeology & Museums' official sites-and-monuments
    listing, nor Wikipedia's "List of cultural heritage sites in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa," includes
    Ziarat Kaka Sahib.** The archaeology directorate's page lists 18 sites (e.g. Takht-i-Bahi,
    Gor Khatri, Bazira) and does not include it; the heritage-sites list runs KPK-1 through
    KPK-90 plus FATA-1–5 and also does not include it (its only Nowshera District entry is
    "Black Rock," KPK-48). This is a negative finding against one of this pass's stated research
    leads — no evidence found of official protected/listed status.
    Citation: Directorate General of Archaeology & Museums, KP, "Sites & Monuments,"
    https://www.kparchaeology.gkp.pk/sites-and-monuments/, fetched 5 September 2026; and
    "List of cultural heritage sites in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa," Wikipedia,
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cultural_heritage_sites_in_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa, fetched
    5 September 2026 (used only to check for the site's presence/absence in the list, not for
    any narrative claim). Reliability: official body page — citable; Wikipedia list —
    consulted directly as a primary negative-check, not cited for content.

11. **Five sons are named, and the article states the fourth built the shrine's most-cited
    primary source.** "Kaka Sahib R.A had five sons, Hazrat Sheikh Syed Ziauddin R.A, Hazrat
    Sheikh Syed Muhammad Gul R.A, Hazrat Sheikh Syed Abdul Khalil R.A, Hazrat Sheikh Syed Abdul
    Haleem R.A and Hazrat Syed Najamuddin R.A." His book "*Manaqib-i-Shaikh Rahamkar* is one of
    the most famous primary sources on the life and times of Kaka Sahib R.A."
    Citation: same as #1, p. 133.

12. **The annual urs has fixed calendar dates in the Islamic year.** "Every year from 12 to 24
    Rajab Urs is being held with the name of *Mela*."
    Citation: same as #1, p. 133.

## Conflicts

- **Birth year: 1575 vs 1576.** The academic paper gives 983 AH = 1575 AD, with a specific
  Gregorian date (4 December 1575), sourced to Bahadur Shah Zafar Kakakhel's book (see finding
  8). Dawn's 2015 feature instead states he was "born on the first of Ramazan in 1576." The
  current entry already hedges between the two ("1575, sometimes given as 1576") — this pass
  did not resolve which is correct, only that both years trace to named, fetched sources rather
  than one being an error of transcription.

- **Death day, within the same academic paper.** The same 2018 article states in one place that
  "Kaka Sahib R.A passed away from this world on Friday 24 Rajab 1063 Hijra (20 June 1653 AD)"
  (citing Sher Afzal Khan Brikoti, p. 263) and in another that "this pious and virtuous
  personality passed away on Friday 21 Rajab 1063 Hijri from this world" — an internal
  inconsistency in the source itself, not resolved here. Separately, Dawn's 2015 piece gives the
  death as "Rajab 24 in 1653," agreeing with the paper's first figure, not its second.

- **Death year in the Hijri calendar: 1063 AH vs 1064 AH.** The academic paper (citing Brikoti)
  gives 1063 AH throughout. A different piece (Chitral Today, see below on its reliability)
  gives "24th Rajab 1064 AH." Not resolved here.

- **Distance from Nowshera city: 12 km vs 9 km.** Dawn (2015) and the Wikipedia stub both give
  "around 12 km south of the Nowshera city." The academic paper instead states "His magnificent
  tomb is situated at Ziarat Kaka Sahib, which is nine kilometres away from Nowshera." Not
  resolved here.

- **When and by whom the tomb was built, within Dawn's own 2015 account.** The same Dawn
  article that supplies finding 5's architectural description also states the tomb was "built
  by Kaka Sahib's direct descendants and followers in early 17th century" — plural
  "descendants," and a period (early 17th century, i.e. before roughly 1633) that precedes Kaka
  Sahib's own death in 1653 by Dawn's own account elsewhere in the same piece. This sits
  awkwardly against the specific, single-builder, single-year claim (Sheikh Abdul Haleem, 1661)
  that both the current entry and the academic paper (finding 2) give. Reported, not resolved.

- **The current entry's claim that Bahadur Baba was "a disciple of the nearby saint Akhund
  Panju Baba" could not be verified, and the academic paper points a different way.** The 2018
  paper discusses Akhund Panju Baba only as Kaka Sahib's *own* contemporary — "According to some
  researchers Kaka Sahib R.A was contemporary of Sheikh Akhud Panju (R.A.) and some say Sheikh
  Akhund Panju R.A was older than Kaka Sahib R.A" — and separately lists him among Sufis "with
  whom Kaka Sahib R.A met," alongside "Hazrat Syed Aadam Benori R.A... Pir Sabaaq R.A and Ikhwan
  Salik R.A." It gives Bahadur Baba's own affiliation as Suhrawardia (finding 9) and never
  mentions a father–Panju Baba discipleship. This does not contradict the current entry outright
  (the two claims are about different relationships — father-to-Panju-Baba vs.
  saint-to-Panju-Baba — and could both be true), but nothing found in this pass corroborates the
  specific claim as stated in the current entry, which should be flagged as unverified rather
  than confirmed.

- **A colonial-era gazetteer check came up empty.** The full text of the *Gazetteer of the
  Peshawar District, 1897–98* was fetched and searched for "Kaka Sahib," "Kastir Gul," "Ziarat,"
  "Kakakhel," and "Rahmkar"/"Rahamkar" — none appear in the retrieved text. This is reported as
  a negative result rather than proof of absence: the fetched text may have been truncated by
  the tool before reaching any later section that discusses religious sites, and the 1883–84 and
  other gazetteer editions were not separately checked in full. Caroe's *The Pathans* (1958) was
  searched for but no accessible full text or specific passage could be located in this pass.

## Verdict

STRONG — a peer-reviewed academic journal article specifically about this saint (Hayat & Ullah,
*Pakistan — Annual Research Journal* Vol. 54, 2018) was located, fetched in full, and
independently corroborates the current entry's exact teacher list, its four-Sufi-order lineage
claim, its Uwaisi framing, and — most notably — its precise construction claim (son Sheikh Abdul
Haleem, 1661), while two Dawn features and a The News report, all fetched directly, independently
corroborate the architecture, the disciple Khushal Khan Khattak, and add a genuinely new fact the
current entry lacks (the shrine's administration by an independent 13-member Darbar council since
2003, not Auqaf). That density of corroboration, plus several precisely quotable conflicts
(birth year, death day, distance from Nowshera), is well past this pass's two-independent-source
bar for STRONG.
