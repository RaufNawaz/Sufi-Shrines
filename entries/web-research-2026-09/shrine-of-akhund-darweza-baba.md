## Conflicts

- **Death year.** The current entry gives "traditionally 1533-1638" and states he "died in the reign of Shah Jahan." Neither source verified in this pass states a death year for Akhund Darweza at all — a direct search of the Khan & Yaqubi (2017) full text, the most detailed account of his life found here, turned up no death year anywhere in it. The "1638 / reign of Shah Jahan" claim is therefore unverified rather than confirmed in this pass, though nothing found contradicts it either.
- **Title of his tazkira.** The current entry gives "Tazkirat al-Abrar wa'l-Ashrar." Search-engine synopses (not independently fetched as primary analytical text — see Unverified leads) render this "Tadhkirat al-Abrar wal-Ashrar," and a Persian-language manuscript scan on archive.org is catalogued as "Tazkira tul-Abraar val-Ashraar." These read as transliteration variants of one title rather than a genuine conflict, but the exact spelling/diacritics could not be pinned down by a fetched, citable secondary source (as distinct from a manuscript's own cover title) in this pass.
- **"Khalifa" framing.** The current entry's claim (via the cross-referenced Alam Faqri tazkira) that Akhund Darweza was Pir Baba's "most renowned khalifa" is corroborated in substance — Khan & Yaqubi independently place him taking "his oath of allegiance" to Pir Baba — but the fetched, quoted text of that paper does not itself use the word "khalifa"/caliph in the passage obtained here, so treat the specific title as reinforced, not verbatim-confirmed.

## Verdict

STRONG — two independent, fetched and verified academic sources: Khan & Yaqubi (2017) for biography and the doctrinal (not political) framing of his opposition to Bayazid Ansari, and Dinakhel (2021) independently confirming his authorship of *Makhzan al-Islam* via real manuscript holdings and a named printed edition. The death year and the exact spelling of his tazkira's title remain unverified rather than confirmed by anything fetched in this pass.
