## Conflicts

- The current entry already flags an open question: this entry's primary sourcing places the
  shrine at Akbarpura, Nowshera District, while a separate tazkira compendium places an "Akhund
  Panju Baba" at Misri Pura, Peshawar Sadar, with a different name-origin story and no mention of
  Bahadur Baba/Kaka Sahib. No source found in this pass resolves this either way.
- A muddying, non-citable data point surfaced during this pass, reported because it bears
  directly on that open question and not because it settles it: Wikipedia's "Talk:Akbarpura"
  discussion page (fetched directly) carries a claim that "he started his teachings and preaching
  from the Misri Pura area of Akbarpura, where today his tomb is present" — i.e., that "Misri
  Pura" may itself be the name of a locality *inside* Akbarpura, not (as the current entry's
  second source has it) a separate place near Peshawar Sadar. This is not usable as a citation:
  it is a talk page, not an article, and — critically — it was itself flagged by other Wikipedia
  editors on the same page as unsourced original research ("I removed unverified content"; a
  need for "verifiable citation") and does not stand in the live article. It is reported here as
  a third possibility (a single shared sub-locality name, rather than either "one saint, one
  place" or "two saints, one honorific") that no reliable source confirms or refutes.
- The Wikipedia "Akhund Panju Baba Mosque" article's own raw wikitext was fetched directly and
  checked: its sentence naming the saint's disciples ("Bahadur Baba, father of Kaka Sahib, Akhund
  Salak Baba, Pir Sabak, Ali Khan Baba of charsadda") carries no `<ref>` citation of its own in
  the underlying markup. It cannot be treated as independent corroboration of the current entry's
  Bahadur Baba/Kaka Sahib lineage claim — only as a second, equally uncited repetition of the same
  tradition.
- Two mutually inconsistent, non-citable founding-legend variants were noticed but not elevated
  to a sourced conflict: a YouTube video titled "Akhun Panju Baba a Sufi of Peshawar who stopped
  King Akbar on his way to Afghanistan" has the saint stopping Akbar, while the Wikipedia mosque
  article's own text has the opposite framing ("local lore... attributes its establishment to a
  miracle spring conjured by the eponymous Sufi saint upon Akbar's visit," i.e. Akbar visiting
  him). Neither is citable; recorded to show the legend is not stable even in uncited retellings.
