# Shrine of Makhdoom Nooh (Hala)
shrine-of-makhdoom-nooh-hala / researched 5 September 2026 / batch C · Sindh Sufi

## Verified findings

1. Silsila and founding of the Sarwari order — corroborates the current entry's account,
   across two independently published pieces by the same author.

   > "He was a Suhrawardi-Awaisi Sufi and the founder of the Sarwari order in Sindh."

   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Makhdoom Mystics of Hala," *The Friday Times*, 25 September
   2020, https://www.thefridaytimes.com/25-Sep-2020/makhdoom-mystics-of-hala — accessed
   5 September 2026. Reliability: established press — citable.

   A second, separately published piece by the same author frames it slightly differently, as a
   sub-order rather than a distinct one:

   > Makhdoom Nuh "was believed to have been the founder of the Sarwari Jamat, a sub-order of
   > the Suhrawardi Silsila of Sufism," which "became dominant in the Suhrawardi silsila in
   > sixteenth- and seventeenth-century Sindh."

   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Sufis of 16th-century Sindh," *The Friday Times*, 17 August
   2018, https://www.thefridaytimes.com/17-Aug-2018/sufis-of-16th-century-sindh — accessed
   5 September 2026. Reliability: established press — citable. (Both pieces share an author;
   noted so this is not mistaken for two fully independent corroborations of the "sub-order"
   framing specifically.)

2. The Persian Quran translation — confirmed.

   > "He was the first religious scholar and Sufi of Sindh who translated the Holy Quran into
   > the Persian language."

   Citation: same as finding #1 (2020 piece).

3. The two relocations of his remains — confirms the current entry's dates (1601, 1777) in
   full, and adds the original village name and a flood cause.

   > Born "in Tori village near Halakandi" in 1505; his remains were moved in 1601 to Mula
   > Sanwani, and again in 1777 to New Hala after "the Indus river inundated Mula Sanwani and
   > other villages."

   Citation: same as finding #1 (2020 piece).

4. A new fact not in the current entry: the tomb at New Hala was built in 1790, by a named
   individual.

   > "His tomb was constructed in 1790 CE by Makhdoom Pir Muhammad Zaman."

   Citation: same as finding #1 (2020 piece).

5. An independent, differently-authored 2009 piece confirms the saint's identity, dates (as a
   range matching the current entry's `figure_born`/`figure_died` exactly), order, and death
   circumstance, and adds a new legend.

   > Makhdoom Lutuf Allah, known as Makhdoom Nuh, lived 1500-1590 and belonged to the
   > "Saharwardia order of Sindh." He "expired in Hala Kandi during the Mughal expedition" in
   > 1590; legend holds that Emperor Akbar's general Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan attended his
   > Fateha gathering before a military campaign and went on to win it.

   Citation: Manzoor H. Kureshi, "Homage to Makhdoom Nuh of Hala," *Dawn*, 24 October 2009,
   https://www.dawn.com/news/498350/homage-to-makhdoom-nuh-of-hala — accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability: established press — citable.

## Conflicts

1. **Age at death.** The current entry's existing corroborating source (Alam Faqri's tazkira)
   states the saint "died at the age of 87." Both new sources found in this pass give a younger
   age: Kalhoro's 2020 piece implies age 84 (born 1505, died 1589), and Kureshi's 2009 *Dawn*
   piece gives the round range 1500-1590 (also implying roughly 84-90, but explicitly not 87).
   Neither new source corroborates the tazkira's specific age.

2. **Exact birth/death years.** Kalhoro (2020) gives 1505-1589; Kureshi/*Dawn* (2009) gives the
   round 1500-1590 (matching the current entry's own `figure_born`/`figure_died` fields
   exactly). The two new sources do not agree with each other to the year, though both sit
   inside the current entry's stated range.

3. **Order framing.** Finding #1 shows the same author describing the Sarwari order in two
   different ways two years apart — "the founder of the Sarwari order" (2020) vs. "founder of
   the Sarwari Jamat, a sub-order of the Suhrawardi Silsila" (2018). The current entry's
   Description follows the "branch of the order" framing. Not a contradiction between sources
   so much as an inconsistency within one author's own account, worth noting rather than
   silently picking the cleaner version.

## Verdict

STRONG — two genuinely independent, directly-fetched, citable sources (Kalhoro/*Friday Times*
2020 and Kureshi/*Dawn* 2009, thirteen years apart, different authors) corroborate the saint's
identity, order, and the Persian Quran translation, and jointly surface a real conflict with the
current entry's own corroborating source over his age at death.
