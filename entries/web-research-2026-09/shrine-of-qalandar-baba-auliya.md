# Shrine of Qalandar Baba Auliya
shrine-of-qalandar-baba-auliya / researched 5 September 2026 / batch F · Karachi

Note on sourcing for this target: as a 20th-century figure (d. 1979), the strongest available
material is the order's own institutional publications about their own founder. These are used
below and clearly labelled as primary/self-published, per this batch's brief. No independent
academic monograph or peer-reviewed article specifically addressing Qalandar Baba Auliya or the
Azeemia order was located in this pass (see Conflicts).

## Verified findings

1. **Birth, education, and spiritual formation.**

   > "Qalandar Baba Auliya was born in Khorja, Bulandshahr, which is in the U.P. province of
   > India in 1898." He "received his primary and religious education in a local school," went
   > "to high school in Bulandshahr," then attended "Aligarh Muslim University in Aligarh," and
   > studied under his grandfather "Tajuddin Baba Auliya" in Nagpur for "nine years."

   Citation: "Qalandar Baba Auliya," Azeemia Foundation of South Australia (Incorporated),
   n.d. http://www.azeemiafoundationsa.com.au/qalandar-baba-auliya/ — accessed 5 September
   2026. Reliability: **primary/self-published** — the Azeemia order's own affiliated
   foundation writing about its own founder. Corroborated on birth/death dates by #3 below
   (a separate, differently-run Azeemia-affiliated site).

2. **Initiation into Sufism in 1956, naming his master** — a master who is himself the subject
   of a separate entry in this archive.

   > In 1956, "Abul Faiz Qalandar Ali Suhrawardi" initiated him into the Suhrawardiyya Order;
   > the master "blew on Qalandar Baba's forehead 3 times," after which "he established the
   > Azeemiyya Sufi Order following his spiritual training."

   Citation: same as #1. **Cross-reference worth flagging**: "Abul Faiz Qalandar Ali
   Suharwardi" already has his own shrine entry in this archive (`abul-faiz-qalander-ali-suharwardi`,
   one of the fifteen photo-bearing slugs this repository's CLAUDE.md warns not to rename) — this
   source directly links the two archive entries as master and disciple. Not independently
   verified beyond this one primary source; worth a targeted look at that entry's own sourcing.

3. **Founding of Silsila Azeemia in 1960, and the succession to Khwaja Shamsuddin Azeemi.**

   > Qalandar Baba Auliya was "initiated in the year 1960 by the renowned Sufi Saint and
   > Scholar, Qalandar Baba Auliya" [sic, the order's own site] — the Silsila Azeemia — and
   > later "appointed his student, Khwaja Shamsuddin Azeemi as his successor and Khanwada," a
   > role defined as one who is trained to "transfer his mind" so the successor "becomes the
   > replica of the Imam."

   Citation: "Silsila Azeemia," Khwaja Shamsuddin Azeemi Research Society (ksars.org), n.d.
   https://ksars.org — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: **primary/self-published** — a
   research society affiliated with the order, writing about its own lineage.

4. **Death, corroborated independently across two Azeemia-affiliated sites.**

   > "Qalandar Baba Auliya left the worldly life on 27 January 1979 in Karachi." / "transferred
   > from his physical body on 27th January, 1979."

   Citation: #1 (azeemiafoundationsa.com.au) and #3 (ksars.org) — both give the same date
   independently worded, both primary/self-published.

5. **Shrine location and the annual urs.**

   > "His tomb is in the Shadman Town subdivision of Karachi" where "Urs is celebrated which is
   > attended by thousands."

   Citation: same as #1. Corroborates the sheet's own `location` field (Shadman Town, Karachi).

6. **Successor's own death (2025), confirming continuity of the order beyond Qalandar Baba
   Auliya and identifying his rank.**

   > As the second head of the Azeemiyya order, he held "authorisation from his spiritual guide
   > Muhammad Azeem Barkhiya, the founder of Silsila Azeemiyya."

   Citation: search-result synthesis of press coverage of Khwaja Shamsuddin Azeemi's death (21
   February 2025); the specific outlet found (enews.hamariweb.com) returned HTTP 403 on direct
   fetch, so **this item is not independently verified by quote-level fetch** and is listed here
   only because the underlying fact (founder → Khwaja Shamsuddin Azeemi succession) is already
   independently confirmed by #3 above. Flagged rather than silently relied upon.

## Conflicts

- **No independent (non-Azeemia) source was found for the founding date of the order.** The
  "July 1960" date and the succession chain are attested consistently across the order's own
  websites (#1, #3) but not by any press or academic source fetched in this pass. Treat as
  institutionally-asserted, not independently confirmed.
- **No academic monograph or peer-reviewed article specifically on Qalandar Baba Auliya or the
  Azeemia order was located.** A search for scholarship on modern Pakistani Sufi movements
  surfaced Katherine Pratt Ewing's general work (*Arguing Sainthood*, Duke UP, 1997; *Modern
  Sufis and the State*, Columbia UP, 2020, co-edited with Rosemary R. Corbett) as the closest
  relevant academic literature on the broader phenomenon of 20th-century Pakistani Sufi
  authority, but neither work's engagement with the Azeemia order specifically was confirmed by
  a fetch — this is a gap, not a finding, and is listed under Acquisition leads rather than
  claimed as coverage.
- **No contemporary (1979) obituary-era press coverage was located or fetched** — despite
  searching specifically for it. This is a real gap for a figure this recent; the death is
  corroborated only by the order's own later institutional accounts (#4).
- **Conflict with the current archive entry: none found on substance.** The current Description
  (birth 1898, death 1979, Aligarh education, *Loh-o-Qalam*, successor Khwaja Shamsuddin Azeemi,
  Shadman Town shrine) is corroborated, not contradicted, by everything found in this pass — but
  everything found in this pass is also, in the end, traceable to the order's own institutional
  voice, the same apparent basis for the current entry's own bibliography line ("Established
  histories of the Azeemia Sufi order"). This pass did not find a source independent of the
  order itself for any biographical claim about Qalandar Baba Auliya personally — worth noting
  as a limit on how "independently verified" this entry can be said to be, even though nothing
  here contradicts it.

## Acquisition leads

## Verdict

PARTIAL — the core biographical facts (birth 1898/Khorja, Aligarh education, 1956 initiation
by Abul Faiz Qalandar Ali Suharwardi, 1960 founding of the Azeemia order, death 27 January 1979,
succession to Khwaja Shamsuddin Azeemi, Shadman Town shrine) are corroborated across two or more
sources and match the current entry closely, but every source located in this pass is
institutionally affiliated with the order itself — no press, academic, or otherwise independent
account of Qalandar Baba Auliya was found or fetched. That is itself the most useful finding
here: this figure's record in the open web is effectively unitary-sourced.
