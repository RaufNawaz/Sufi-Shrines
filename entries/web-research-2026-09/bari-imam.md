# Bari Imam

bari-imam / researched 5 September 2026 / batch G · Lahore and Islamabad

## Verified findings

1. **Birth and death years match the current entry, from established press.**
   Quote: "Born in 1617 in the Jehlum area of Punjab, Bari Imam, whose real name was Syed Shah Abdul Latif" ... "Bari Imam died in 1705" ... "He settled in the Nur Pur Shahan area."
   Citation: Muhammad Umar and Suhail Yusuf, "Syed Shah Abdul Latif: 'Khushki kay Imam'," *Dawn* (Multimedia), 10 July 2014, https://www.dawn.com/news/1118335, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).
   Note: independently corroborates the current entry's `figure_born` (1617) and `figure_died` (1705).

2. **An academic thesis gives the area's pre-saint name, and a different reported birthplace.**
   Quote: "It is a common belief that Bari Imam was born in present day Chakwal. His father, Syed Mehmood Shah, migrated to the Baghan village now known as Aabpara." ... "Bari Imam came to this area in the 1600s. Before his arrival, the area was known as Chorpur but after his teachings of love, peace and harmony spread throughout the region it became Noorpur Shahan (Yasin, 2016)."
   Citation: Asif Javed, *Religious Commodification in Pakistan: A Case Study of Bari Imam, Islamabad* (MPhil thesis, Department of Development Studies, Pakistan Institute of Development Economics, supervised by Dr. Zulfiqar Ali, 2018), pp. 20–21 (locale chapter). PDF: https://file-thesis.pide.org.pk/pdf/mphil-development-studies-2015-asif-javed--religious-commodification-in-pakistan-a-case-study-of-bari-imam-islamabad.pdf, accessed 5 September 2026 (fetched directly and its text extracted for this session; the "(Yasin, 2016)" the thesis itself cites is finding 6 below, independently confirmed).
   Reliability class: academic thesis (institutional/graduate research, Pakistan Institute of Development Economics).
   Note: see Conflicts — "present day Chakwal" is a different specific claim from "the Jehlum area" (finding 1), though Chakwal was administratively part of Jhelum District until 1985.

3. **The saint's 40-day retreat site and the urs's suspension, per the same thesis.**
   Quote: "He also spent 40 days in prayer in a cave in the Margalla Hills that is now known as Loh-e-Dandi." ... "The urs (celebration at the death anniversary of the saint) is celebrated during the month of Jeth (May-June), however due to law and order situation prevailing in the country the celebration of urs was stopped by the government in 2005."
   Citation: Asif Javed, PIDE thesis (2018), as above, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: academic thesis.

4. **Construction under Aurangzeb, renovation, and the 2008 reconstruction naming the presiding official.**
   Quote: "The first building of the shrine of Bari Imam was built by the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb which was later renovated during the British Raj. In 2008, the caretaker Prime Minister of Pakistan, Muhammad Mian Soomro inaugurated the renovation of shrine of the Bari Imam complex which is completed now and reminds the visitor as replica of Masjid-i-Nabvi (Yasin, 2016)."
   Citation: Asif Javed, PIDE thesis (2018), as above, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: academic thesis, itself citing Aamir Yasin's 2016 Dawn report (finding 6).

5. **A separately recorded oral miracle-legend, explicitly framed by the thesis as informant testimony, not history.**
   Quote: "The respondents also shared that the shrine was built approximately 450 years ago by Emperor Aurangzeb. When the emperor visited Bari Sarkar, he was on the bank of stream. Aurangzeb presented a jewel as a gift to him. The Sarkar threw the jewel into stream. The emperor was astonished that he gave a precious jewel to him but he threw it into the water. He asked the reason. Sarkar asked him to close his eyes. When the emperor closed his eyes, he saw the stream ful[l of jewels]..."
   Citation: Asif Javed, PIDE thesis (2018), as above (field interview data, ch. on shrine legends), accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: academic thesis (primary ethnographic fieldwork; presented here as reported oral tradition, not verified history).

6. **The urs was stopped after the bombing — but the two Dawn sources disagree on the month.**
   Quote: "Although the urs at the saint's mausoleum was stopped by the government after a suicide bombing at the site in June 2005, the shrine continues to hum with activity all through the day."
   Citation: Aamir Yasin, "The bustling shrine of Bari Imam," *Dawn*, 15 May 2016, https://www.dawn.com/news/1258461, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn). See Conflicts.

7. **The bombing's date and casualty figures, from a Dawn news report on the subsequent arrests.**
   Quote: "At least 20 people were killed and 100 others wounded when a suicide bomber blew himself at the shrine on May 27, 2005." The report adds that the intended target was Jalil Naqvi, who was to address a Majlis at the shrine that day and "escaped unhurt."
   Citation: "Two involved in Bari Imam suicide attack arrested," *Dawn*, 14 August 2011, https://www.dawn.com/news/651709/two-involved-in-bari-imam-suicide-attack-arrested, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

8. **Administration by the ICT Auqaf Department, and its stated responsibilities.**
   Quote: "the Auqaf department is also responsible for holding of urs and other religious festivals such as Naat, Qirat and Mehfil-e-Shabeena competitions. The shrine of Bari Imam was also renovated by Auqaf department."
   Citation: Asif Javed, PIDE thesis (2018), as above, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: academic thesis.

9. **The shrine today sits within a contested informal economy and a tense relationship with the Capital Development Authority.**
   Quote: "most of them reported to be mentally and physically pressurized by the capital development authority (CDA) which, they fear, may result in the loss of their property."
   Citation: Asif Javed, PIDE thesis (2018), as above, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: academic thesis.

10. **The shrine's precinct hosts marginalized communities as a matter of documented anthropological observation.**
    Quote: "shrines function as a tolerant religious space for the people from marginalized sections of the society. The active participation of drug dealers, malangs, female prostitutes, and male-female transgender sex workers in the religious and economic fields of the shrine complex were also observed during the field work of this research."
    Citation: Asif Javed, PIDE thesis (2018), as above, accessed 5 September 2026.
    Reliability class: academic thesis (direct fieldwork observation).

## Conflicts

- **Month of the bombing.** Aamir Yasin's 2016 Dawn piece states the urs was suspended "after a suicide bombing at the site in June 2005" (finding 6); a separate 2011 Dawn news report gives the attack's own date as "May 27, 2005" (finding 7). Both are established-press Dawn sources; neither cites the other. Reported, not resolved.
- **Birthplace.** The current entry does not specify a birthplace beyond "born 1617." Finding 1 (Dawn 2014) says "the Jehlum area of Punjab"; finding 2 (PIDE thesis, 2018, itself hedged as "a common belief") says "present day Chakwal." Chakwal was part of Jhelum District until it became a separate district in 1985, so the two claims are not necessarily incompatible, but they name different specific places and neither source acknowledges the other's version.
- **Could not verify:** the current entry's claim that Bari Imam "is said to have been a contemporary of the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb, who is remembered as honouring him" is broadly consistent with, but not identical to, what was found here — Aurangzeb is credited by multiple sources with *building the shrine* (findings 1, 4, 5), a stronger and more specific claim than "honouring him," and one the current entry does not itself make. Worth reconciling in a future pass.
- **Could not verify:** the current entry's claim that Bari Imam "settled ... after study and long spiritual wandering" and "took the Sufi path — associated with the Qadiri tradition." The Qadiri affiliation is widely repeated in search-result snippets (including a Wikipedia summary, not citable) but no source fetched and quoted this session states it directly; it is reported here as unconfirmed rather than false.
- **Could not verify, and worth flagging as a real gap:** none of the sources fetched this session gave a specific date for **when** the shrine complex was renovated/rebuilt beyond "2008" (finding 4) and none gave the cost figure ("Rs 641 million") that appeared only in an unfetched search-result snippet — that figure is therefore not included as a verified finding, only noted here as unconfirmed.

## Verdict

**STRONG** — an academic MPhil thesis (fetched and read in full, not merely searched) and three separate established-press Dawn articles were verified, together corroborating the current entry's birth/death years and the 2005-bombing/urs-suspension narrative, while surfacing a genuine press-vs-press conflict on the bombing's month and a body of well-documented anthropological material (marginalized communities, CDA tension, the Chorpur place-name, the Loh-e-Dandi retreat cave) that the current entry does not yet reflect at all.
