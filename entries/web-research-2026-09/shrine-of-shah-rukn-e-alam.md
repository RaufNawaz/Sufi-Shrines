# Shrine of Shah Rukn-e-Alam
shrine-of-shah-rukn-e-alam / researched 5 September 2026 / batch A · Multan

## Verified findings

1. Precise birth and death dates (day of week, Hijri and AD), and the line of succession.

   > "Friday, the 9th of Ramadan-ul-Mubarak 649 Hijri (1251 AD)" [birth]; "Friday, the 7th of
   > Jamadi-ul-Awwal 735 Hijri (1335 AD)" [death]; he was "the son of Sadar-Al-Din Arif" and
   > "the grandson and successor of Sheikh Bahauddin Zakariya," and designated "Sheikh
   > Hameed-ud-Din Al-Hakim (RA)" as his own spiritual successor.

   Citation: "Hazrat Rukn-ud-Din (RA), Multan," Auqaf & Religious Affairs Department, Government
   of Punjab, https://auqaf.punjab.gov.pk/shrine_ruknuddin — accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability: official government body — citable. This matches the current entry's
   `figure_born` (1251) and `figure_died` (1335) and adds the exact day/month, which the current
   entry does not carry.

2. The tomb's construction and hand-over sequence — corroborating and dating the current
   entry's own hedge ("said to have been built originally for the Tughluq sultan Ghiyath-ud-Din
   and then given over to the saint").

   > "built by Ghias-ud-Din Tughlaq during his governorship of Depalpur, between (1320 - 1324
   > AD)" and "given by his son, Muhammad bin Tughluq to the descendants of Shah Rukn-e-Alam for
   > the latter's burial in 1330 AD." He was initially buried in his grandfather's mausoleum and
   > "his coffin was transferred to the present mausoleum" afterward.

   Citation: same as #1. This distinguishes two dates the current entry compresses into one
   `year_built: 1330 (circa)`: the structure itself rose 1320–1324, under a different patron and
   (by this account) for a different original purpose, and was only assigned to Rukn-e-Alam's
   line in 1330. Worth a human read before treating "1330" as the building's date rather than
   the date it became this saint's tomb.

3. 1970s Auqaf Department restoration, and current UNESCO tentative-list status.

   > "In the 1970s, the mausoleum was thoroughly repaired and renovated by the Auqaf Department,"
   > with "new tiles and brickwork done by the Kashigars of Multan." "The tomb is on the
   > tentative list as a UNESCO World Heritage Site."

   Citation: same as #1.

4. The Aga Khan Award for Architecture restoration — specific enough to replace the current
   entry's vague "receiving in modern times awards for its conservation."

   > Client: Government of Punjab. Architect: "Awqaf Department / Muhammad Wali Ullah Khan."
   > "Conservation commenced in late 1971 and was completed in six years" (i.e. by 1977); "the
   > foundations and the lower sections of the brick walls were rebuilt, destroyed tiles were
   > replaced, damaged woodwork repaired and the site landscaped." A craft-training programme
   > taught "33 novices" traditional Multani tilework, wood carving and terra cotta. The jury
   > commended it "for its contribution to reviving some of the great crafts of 600 years ago."
   > Award cycle 1981–1983.

   Citation: "Tomb of Shah Rukn-i-'Alam," Aga Khan Development Network (AKDN), Aga Khan Award
   for Architecture record, https://the.akdn/en/how-we-work/our-agencies/aga-khan-trust-culture/akaa/tomb-shah-rukn-i-alam
   — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: official body (AKDN) — citable.

## Conflicts

None found against the current entry's core claims — this pass corroborates and sharpens the
existing account (dates, Tughluq-tomb-then-given-over narrative, restoration) rather than
contradicting it. The one thing worth flagging to a human, not a conflict but a compression: the
current entry's single `year_built: 1330 (circa)` covers what source #2 above splits into two
distinct dates eleven years apart (built 1320–1324 for another purpose; assigned to the saint's
family in 1330).
