# Shrine of Pir Mangho
shrine-of-pir-mangho / researched 5 September 2026 / batch F · Karachi

## Verified findings

1. **The shrine's dedication and setting.**

   > "the swampy crocodile shrine to Sufi saint Haji Syed Shaikh Sultan — more popularly known
   > as Mangho Pir"

   Citation: AFP, "The crocodile shrine of Mangho Pir and its long-forgotten African past,"
   Dawn, 6 April 2018. https://www.dawn.com/news/1399778 — accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability: established press (wire copy via Dawn) — citable.

2. **Sheedi community history and marginalisation** (same source as #1).

   > "it is generally accepted that Pakistan holds the highest number of Sheedis on the
   > subcontinent, upwards of around 50,000 people"; "the Sheedis rose to lofty positions as
   > generals and leaders during the Mughal Empire" but "found themselves wholly shunned when
   > Pakistan was created in 1947."

   Citation: same as #1.

3. **The crocodile-origin legend and the "oldest crocodile."**

   > "Legend holds that lice on the Sufi saint's head transformed into the reptiles."
   > "The oldest crocodile — known as More Sawab, and believed to be anywhere between 70 and
   > 100 years old"; "Mangho Pir is also home to over 100 lumbering crocodiles."

   Citation: same as #1.

4. **Festival history — a nine-year security-driven hiatus** (same source as #1).

   > "The celebrations this March were the first time the festival has been held in nine
   > years."

   Citation: same as #1.

5. **A different, more elaborated founding legend, naming a specific Chishti connection** —
   explicitly framed by the source itself as one of several myths, not as settled history.

   > "One of the most dominant myths is the story of Sakhi Sultan, a Hindu who converted to
   > Islam and became a devotee of Baba Fariduddin Masood Ganj, a revered mystic of the Chisti
   > order." "According to legend, the lice in the saint's hair became the crocodiles."

   Citation: Sadaf Shahzad, "Sheedis (or Siddis) of Manghopir: A People with African Roots,"
   Youlin Magazine, 10 June 2020.
   https://youlinmagazine.com/article/sheedis-(or-siddis)-of-manghopir-a-people-with-african-roots/MTc2MA==
   — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: named-author cultural-affairs magazine
   (specifically flagged as a venue to check in this batch's brief) — citable, treated as one
   step below wire/daily press.

6. **Crocodile count and behaviour, corroborating #3** (same source as #5).

   > "At present, there are over 100 crocodiles at the shrine, including More Sahab, the oldest
   > crocodile who is about 70-100 years old." "There have been no incidents of the crocodile
   > attacking any devotee."

   Citation: same as #5.

7. **The Sheedi Mela's ritual centrepiece** (same source as #5).

   > "The high point of the festival is when More Sahab accepts a large slab of meat from the
   > shrine's gaddi nasheen (caretaker), signaling a peaceful, prosperous, and wealthy year
   > ahead for the community." Adds that the mela "returned to Manghopir in full swing" in 2017
   > "after a long hiatus" caused by "heavy militancy and terrorism in the area."

   Citation: same as #5.

8. **An earlier press account of the same festival, with a substantially larger crocodile
   count and a different founding story.**

   > "The shrine features 200 or so crocodiles that bask in a pond at the foot of the shrine,"
   > which visitors feed "whole chickens and other choice cuts of meat." According to local
   > tradition, the shrine honours a man "named Mangho who gave up his wayward ways after a
   > rendezvous" with a 12th-century Sufi master. The drumming at the dhammal is "clearly
   > reminiscent of African tribal rhythms."

   Citation: "KARACHI: Manghopir urs a living tribute to Sheedi culture," Dawn, 16 July 2007
   (byline "QAM"). https://www.dawn.com/news/256583/karachi-manghopir-urs-a-living-tribute-to-sheedi-culture
   — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established press (Dawn) — citable.

9. **The sulphur springs and their healing legend.**

   > "Hot water. It sprang from the earth and flowed into two streams. Then came the lepers,
   > thinking the streams had curative powers; bathing in their sulphur-rich water could rid
   > them of their affliction." Names the saint as "Haji Sakhi Sultan – better known as Haji
   > Mangha or Mangho Pir."

   Citation: Maqbool Ahmed and Zehra Nawab, "Harmony springs eternal," Herald (Dawn Group), 5
   September 2016. https://herald.dawn.com/news/1153174/harmony-springs-eternal — accessed 5
   September 2026. Reliability: established press (Herald/Dawn Group) — citable.

10. **Archaeological/scientific claims about the crocodile population's antiquity, and
    behavioural notes.**

    > "The pond is populated by dozens of crocodiles who are regularly fed by visitors to the
    > shrine." "Carbon-dating methods have placed the bones to belong to the Bronze age
    > (3300-1200 BC)." "There has never been a crocodile attack reported here."

    Citation: Nadeem F. Paracha, "From legend to science: The crocodiles of Manghopir," Dawn,

## Conflicts

- **Crocodile count varies by source and year**: "dozens" (Dawn/Paracha, 2016, #10) vs. "over
  100" (Dawn/AFP 2018 and Youlin 2020, #3/#6) vs. "200 or so" (Dawn 2007, #8). These may reflect
  real population change over a decade, or loose reporting — not resolved here.
- **Sheedi population estimate varies widely**: "upwards of around 50,000" (Dawn/AFP 2018, #2)
  vs. "over 50,000-250,000" as of 2018 (Youlin 2020, #5) — a fivefold range from sources dated
  the same year.
- **Year the festival resumed after its security-driven hiatus is inconsistent**: 2017 (Youlin
  2020, #7) vs. "the first time... in nine years" as of the reported March 2018 event (Dawn/AFP
  2018, #4). These may both be true (a smaller 2017 return followed by a fuller 2018 one) or may
  simply disagree — reported, not resolved.
- **The founding/origin legend differs in specificity and content** between sources: Youlin
  (#5) names the saint "Sakhi Sultan," a Hindu convert and disciple of the 13th-century Chishti
  saint Baba Fariduddin Masood Ganj (i.e., Baba Farid); Dawn 2007 (#8) instead describes an
  unnamed "rendezvous" with a "12th-century Sufi master." Both are explicitly presented as
  legend by their own sources, not as documented history.
- **The saint's name is spelled/rendered differently across sources**: "Haji Syed Shaikh
  Sultan" (Dawn/AFP 2018, #1), "Haji Sakhi Sultan" (Herald 2016, #9), "Sakhi Sultan" (Youlin
  2020, #5) — the sheet's own `principal_figure` field ("Haji Syed Sakhi Sultan") most closely
  matches the Herald/Youlin spelling, not the AFP one.
- **Conflict with the current archive entry**: the sheet's `year_built` is given as "773
  (circa)" — the identical value given for Abdullah Shah Ghazi in this same batch (see that
  file). No source found in this pass, including a dedicated search of two colonial gazetteers,
  supports any 8th-century date for Pir Mangho; the sources found describe the saint only as
  medieval, and a claim seen only in unfetched search results places his arrival "from Iraq...
  in the 13th century" (not verified, not cited above). The identity of this value with the
  other shrine's `year_built` is worth a data-QA look independent of this research pass.
- **Checked and ruled out**: neither Henry Cousens's *The Antiquities of Sind* (1929, fetched in
  full) nor the general 1907 *Gazetteer of the Province of Sind* (fetched in full) mention
  Manghopir, its shrine, crocodiles, or springs at all.
- **Checked and ruled out**: the *Gazetteer of the Province of Sind, Vol. I, Karachi District*
  (J.W. Smyth, comp.; archive.org `dli.csl.3395`, fetched in full) names "Pir Mangho" only twice,
  both as a road waypoint ("Karachi to Pir Mangho (10 miles)"), with no "Places of Interest"
  entry devoted to it despite that chapter covering many other district sites.

## Verdict

STRONG — multiple independent, directly-fetched, established-press sources (Dawn/AFP 2018,
Dawn/Paracha 2016, Dawn 2007, Herald 2016, Youlin Magazine 2020) corroborate the core account
(crocodile pool, sulphur springs, Sheedi community, annual mela), though crocodile counts,
population estimates, and the exact founding legend vary substantially across them and are
reported as open conflicts rather than resolved.
