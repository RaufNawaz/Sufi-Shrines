# Shrine of Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht
shrine-of-makhdoom-jahaniyan-jahangasht / researched 5 September 2026 / batch B · Uch Sharif and west-Punjab khanqahs

## Verified findings

1. Official UNESCO tentative-list document names Jahaniyan Jahangasht as a historical figure
   connected to the Uch monument complex — but his own shrine, the subject of this entry, is not
   one of the five structures the nomination itself covers.

   > "The proposed property consists of 5 monuments at the South-West corner of Uch Sharif...
   > The oldest are the fourteenth century AD tomb and mosque of the Central Asian Sufi
   > Jalaluddin Bukhari... These structures were joined by a series of domed tombs; the first is
   > said to have been built for Baha' al-Halim by his pupil, the Suharwardiya Sufi saint
   > Jahaniyan Jahangasht (1307-1383 AD), the second for the latter's great grant daughter, Bibi
   > Jalwindi, in c.1494 and the third for the latter's architect."

   Citation: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Tentative Lists, "Tomb of Bibi Jawindi, Baha'al-Halim
   and Ustead and the Tomb and Mosque of Jalaluddin Bukhari," Ref. 1883, submitted 30/01/2004 by
   the Department of Archaeology and Museums, Government of Pakistan.
   https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1883/ — content retrieved via the r.jina.ai
   text-extraction proxy, accessed 5 September 2026, because direct WebFetch of the live page
   returned HTTP 403 Forbidden and web.archive.org could not be reached at all by this session's
   tools.
   Reliability: official body document — citable, with the above access caveat noted. The
   retrieved text matches, sentence for sentence, the same passage independently quoted by the
   August 2026 pass's `tomb-of-bahaal-halim-uch-sharif.md` (which reached it via a Wayback
   Machine snapshot), corroborating that the proxy returned the genuine document.

   The five monuments named in the nomination's own title and description are: the tomb and
   mosque of Jalaluddin Bukhari (i.e. Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari, this saint's grandfather,
   d. c. 1290 per other sources), the tomb of Baha'al-Halim, the tomb of Bibi Jawindi, and the
   tomb of "the latter's architect" (Ustad Nuriya). Jahaniyan Jahangasht himself appears in the
   document only as the builder of Baha'al-Halim's tomb and the ancestor of Bibi Jawindi — his
   own shrine is not among the five and is not otherwise described in the retrieved text.

2. The same document's lifespan for Jahaniyan Jahangasht, "(1307-1383 AD)," differs by one year
   at each end from the sheet's figure_born 1308 / figure_died 1384. See Conflicts.

## Conflicts

- **Birth/death year, by one year each.** UNESCO's document gives "1307-1383 AD"; the sheet
  gives 1308/1384. Both pairings recur across different secondary sources found in search
  results (not independently verified); this pass cannot adjudicate between them and only
  reports the discrepancy against the one official document verified directly.
- **A specific construction date and patron for this saint's own tomb was found, but only in a
  non-citable source.** pakheritage.org ("Naqoosh-e-Javidan," a heritage/photography site with
  no visible institutional publisher — already flagged as excluded by the reliability bar by the
  August 2026 pass, for a neighbouring Uch tomb) states of this tomb specifically: "Ghazi Khan
  ruler of Dera Ghazi Khan constructed his tomb in 1453 AD," with a detailed physical description
  (a 41 ft square brick tomb, wooden roof, glazed-tile decoration; an adjoining mosque measuring
  43 ft 6 in by 33 ft 10 in, "reconstructed around 100 years ago"). The sheet currently leaves
  `year_built` blank ("unknown"). This claim is specific and checkable, but comes from a
  blog-tier source this pass could not corroborate elsewhere — reported as an unverified lead,
  not a fact, precisely because it is specific enough to be worth checking against a citable
  source (the one candidate found, the Punjab Archaeology Department's monument index, returned
  HTTP 403 Forbidden to this pass — see Unverified leads).
- No conflict found on the saint's basic biography (Suhrawardi order, grandson of Jalaluddin
  Surkh-Posh Bukhari, extensive travels) — nothing found here contradicts the current entry's
  account of this.
