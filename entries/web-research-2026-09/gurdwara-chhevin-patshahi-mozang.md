# Gurdwara Chhevin Patshahi, Mozang
gurdwara-chhevin-patshahi-mozang / researched 5 September 2026 / batch E · Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Lahore

## Verified findings

1. **Location, the 1619 visit, and the Bibi Kaulan connection (Qaiser's version).**
   Quote: "Guru Hargobind Ji came to Lahore in 1619 AD with Diwan Chandu. It was from here that he asked Gur Premi (lover and devotee) Bibi Kaulan to ride his horse. The shrine is on Temple road in Mozang."
   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998) — entry "Chhevin Patshahi, Mozang," listed at p. 266 in the book's table of contents; retrieved via archive.org's full-text search-inside index at scan page 274 (item `HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan`), https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan) — citable.
   This **confirms** the current entry's 1619 date and the Mata Kaulan/Bibi Kaulan connection, and adds a name absent from the current entry: **Diwan Chandu**, said to have accompanied the Guru to Lahore.

2. **The Ranjit Singh-era original structure and its 1926 rebuilding under Sardar Mehar Singh.**
   Quote (verbatim, including an OCR artifact in the original scan): "The initial building of this sacred place was built by Maharaja Ranjit Singh It was shifted to [OCR garbled] building in 1926 AD through the courtesy of late Sardar Mehar Singh."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998 (as above), accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan) — citable.
   This directly **confirms** the current entry's claim of an original Ranjit Singh-era structure, a 1926 rebuild, and Sardar Mehar Singh's patronage — the strongest single confirmation found in this pass for either target entry.

3. **Daily worship and Akhand Path, administrative history 1927–present (as of 1998), and property endowment.**
   Quote: "Congregation was held daily, Shiromni Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee remained in charge of its maintenance from 1927 to 1947 and at present it is in the custody of Waqf Board."
   Second quote, same entry: "Nine Shops inside the building o[f] Gurdwara sahib are endowed to it. An Akhand Patth (complete recitation) is done on the birth anniversary of Gur Dev Ji."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998 (as above), accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan) — citable.
   This **confirms** daily pre-Partition worship and an annual Akhand Path timed to Guru Hargobind's Gurpurb (birth anniversary), and adds a fact absent from the current entry: **nine shops inside the building are endowed to the gurdwara**. See Conflicts for a caveat on tense/timing.

4. **Independent corroboration that Guru Hargobind's Lahore residence at Mozang became a gurdwara — a second, separate published source.**
   Quote: "Guru Har Gobind resided in Lahore for sometime. His residence at Mozang (now on Temple Road, Bhundpura) was later on converted into a gurdwara."
   Citation: Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan*, Memoir of the Department of Archaeology in Pakistan No. 3 (Karachi: Department of Archaeology, Ministry of Education & Information, Government of Pakistan, 1962), Lahore District section, entry 9 ("Guru Har Gobind"), p. 38. Full-text scan: https://archive.org/details/furg-sikh-shrines-in-west-pakistan-by-khan-mohammad-wal, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: official body / published government report (full-text scan) — citable.
   This is an **independent second source**, confirming the Temple Road/Mozang location and the residence-into-gurdwara story, and adding a locality name not found elsewhere: **"Bhundpura."**

5. **A markedly different telling of the Kaulan story, from an established-press source, under a different name for the same gurdwara.**
   Quotes: "Located just 200 yards from the Mozang police station, it attracts Sikh pilgrims all the year round." / "It is claimed that a gent by the name of Qazi Rustam Khan kidnapped, or purchased a Hindu girl and converted her to Islam." / "Maharajah Ranjit Singh built an excellent huge temple at the place where the escape took place, and the tree where the guru tied his horse still exists."
   Citation: Majid Sheikh, "Temple Road and legend of Mata Kaulan," *Dawn*, 18 May 2014, https://www.dawn.com/news/1106983, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn) — citable.
   The article calls the site **"Gurdwara Chatti Badshahi"** rather than "Chhevin Patshahi" — clearly a phonetic variant of the same name ("Chhati/Chhevin Patshahi" = Sixth Sovereignty), not a different gurdwara: same location (Temple Road, 200 yards from Mozang police station), same guru, same Kaulan connection. See Conflicts for how its version of the Kaulan story differs from Qaiser's.

## Conflicts

- **The Kaulan narrative itself, told two incompatible ways**: Qaiser (1998) has Guru Hargobind simply ask "Gur Premi (lover and devotee) Bibi Kaulan to ride his horse" — implying she was already a devotee, travelling with or near the Guru. Majid Sheikh in Dawn (2014) tells a wholly different story: Kaulan is "a Hindu girl" kidnapped or purchased and forcibly converted to Islam by one **"Qazi Rustam Khan,"** tortured, who then escapes to the Guru, who ties his horse to a tree at the site (a tree the column says still exists). The two accounts do not share a single named antagonist or plot beat — Qaiser names "Diwan Chandu" as the Guru's companion into Lahore and gives no captor for Kaulan at all; Dawn names "Qazi Rustam Khan" as her captor and never mentions Diwan Chandu. **Not resolved here** — both are reported as found.
- **Timing of the daily worship and Akhand Path**: the current entry frames both as belonging to "the pre-1947 period," implying they lapsed at Partition. Qaiser's 1998 text describes the same practices in a construction that reads as still-current as of the book's writing ("Congregation was held daily...An Akhand Patth...**is** done on the birth anniversary"), immediately after noting present-day (1998) Waqf Board custody. Whether the daily worship/Akhand Path continued past 1947 and into the 1990s, or whether Qaiser is retrospectively describing pre-Partition custom, is ambiguous in the source and not resolved here.
- **A locality name with no second source**: Khan Waliullah Khan's 1962 entry names the area "Bhundpura" alongside Temple Road; this detail appears in no other source checked in this pass (not Qaiser, not Dawn, not the current entry) and is reported as new information rather than a corroborated fact.
- **The current entry's "protected heritage, not resident worship" framing** for the post-Partition period is not contradicted by anything found here, but nor is it independently confirmed — Qaiser's account stops at "present...it is in the custody of Waqf Board" (1998) and says nothing about whether worship is resident or not.

## Near-misses explicitly ruled out (there are several other "Chhevin Patshahi" gurdwaras)

Per the caution that "Chhevin Patshahi" names many separate Guru Hargobind halts across Punjab, the following were checked by name/location and are **not** this site:

- Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan (1962), entry 118, "Gurdwara Chhewin Padshahi," at **Nankana, Sheikhupura District**, p. 65 — a different district entirely, fetched and confirmed distinct.
- Wikipedia, "Gurdwara Patshahi Chhevin (Hadiara)" — a village called Hadiara — seen in search results only, not fetched; ruled out by place-name alone.
- AllAboutSikhs/SikhiWiki, "Gurdwara Chhevin Patshahi, **Guru Mangat**, Distt Lahore" — a different Lahore-district locality (Guru Mangat, not Mozang) — seen in search results only, not fetched; ruled out by place-name.
- "Gurdwara Chhevin Patshahi, **Hafizabad**" (Pakistan Geotagging blog) — a different district — seen in search snippet only; ruled out by location.
- "Gurdwara Chhevin Patshahi, **Jalandhar**" (incredibleindia.gov.in) — in India, not Pakistan — ruled out by country.
- The current entry's own third Lahore gurdwara of this name, at **Jhalian**, and the **Chowmala/Bhati Gate** gurdwara (this pass's other target) are both separately documented and not conflated here with Mozang.

## Verdict

**STRONG** — three independent verified sources (Qaiser 1998; Khan Waliullah Khan 1962, official Department of Archaeology memoir; Majid Sheikh/Dawn 2014), with two genuine, reported-not-resolved conflicts: two incompatible tellings of the Mata Kaulan story, and an ambiguity over whether daily worship/Akhand Path continued past 1947.
