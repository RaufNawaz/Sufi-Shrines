# Gurdwara Chowmala Sahib
gurdwara-chowmala-sahib / researched 5 September 2026 / batch E · Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Lahore

## Verified findings

1. **Location and origin — Chowmala Mohalla inside Bhati Gate; Guru Hargobind at the house of Bhai Jivan; a second Sikh figure, Bhai Biddi Chand, also associated with the site.**
   Quote: "This sacred shrine is located in Chowmala Mohalla inside Bhati Gate. Guru Hargobind ji stayed in the house of Bhai Jivan, held a number of congregation (Diwan) and blessed the devotees with eternal teachings."
   Second quote, same entry: "Bhai Biddi Chand Ji also stayed here when he came to take back the horses of Guru Ji. Bhai Jivan converted this house into a Gurdwara. Its initial charge was with the mahants."
   Citation: Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (Lahore: Punjabi History Board, 1998) — entry listed as "Chaumala Saheb, Lahore" at p. 264 in the book's own table of contents; the passage itself was retrieved via archive.org's full-text search-inside index at scan/leaf page 272 (item `HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan`), https://archive.org/details/HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan, accessed 5 September 2026. (Direct fetch of the book's `_djvu.txt` stream only surfaced front matter and the table of contents — pages 264/272 were reached instead through the archive.org internal search-inside API, `https://ia600105.us.archive.org/fulltext/inside.php?item_id=HistoricalSikhShrinesInPakistan...&q=...`.)
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan on archive.org) — citable.
   **Note the spelling**: Qaiser's book spells the name **"Bhai Jivan,"** not "Bhai Jiwan" as the current entry has it — likely just a transliteration variant, flagged rather than silently normalised.

2. **The 1915 hall enlargement and the 1921 change of control from a mahant to Sikh Sangat administration.**
   Quote: "In 1915 the hall of Gurdwara Sahib was enlarged. Sikh Sangats took over the control from Mahant Hari Singh in 1921 AD. Initially a local Committee was constituted then it passed onto the control of Shiromni Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee from 1927 to 1947 AD."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998 (as above), accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan) — citable.
   This directly **confirms** the current entry's "hall is recorded as having been enlarged in 1915" — the one fact the current entry already had that this pass could verify against a real source.

3. **Present-day custodian (as of the book's 1998 writing), pilgrim facilities, land endowment, and the Basant Panchami fair.**
   Quote: "The Auqaf Department has the charge of this place now. There were a large number of rooms for pilgrims to stay. A house in Bhati gate and 87 ghumaon of land in Khudpur village of Tehsil Lahore is attached to Gurdwara. A fair used to be held on Basant Panchami."
   Citation: Qaiser 1998 (as above), accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan) — citable.
   This **confirms** the current entry's claim of an annual Basant Panchami fair "once held," and adds several facts absent from the current entry entirely: the 1921 mahant-to-Sangat handover, SGPC administration 1927–1947, current (1998) Auqaf Department custodianship, pilgrim lodging, and the specific Khudpur village land endowment.

## Conflicts

- **Whether the structure survives**: the current entry hedges — "some reports suggest that the original structure...may no longer survive intact." Qaiser's 1998 account, the only citable source found, does not address demolition either way; it describes the site as still held by the Auqaf Department as of 1998, which is compatible with the building still standing at that date but says nothing about its condition since. A non-citable source (see Unverified leads) makes a much more specific and starker claim of total demolition, but it cannot be used to confirm or deny this.
- **The 1620 event date**: the current entry's structured `event_year` field gives 1620 for "Guru Hargobind's visit; Bhai Jiwan's house converted." No source found in this pass — including Qaiser's own entry — gives any year for the visit itself; Qaiser dates only the 1915 enlargement and the 1921/1927 administrative changes. The 1620 figure is **unverified** against every source checked here.
- **No corroborating second source was found for this site at all** — see Verdict.
