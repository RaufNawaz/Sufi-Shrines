# Shrine of Baba Shah Chiragh

shrine-of-baba-shah-chiragh / researched 5 September 2026 / batch G · Lahore and Islamabad

## Verified findings

1. **Ancestry, Qadiri/Gilani lineage, and death year — corroborated from the primary 1892 source.**
   Quote: "The ancestors of Abdul Razzak, alias Shah Chiragh, were residents of Uch, in Bahawalpur, and their pedigree joins the celebrated saint of Gilan, Muhy-ud-din, known as Pir Dastgir. Shah Chiragh flourished in the days of Shah Jahan, and died in 10GS A. H. (1657 A. D.). The present mausoleum was raised to his memory by the orders of the Emperor Aurangzeb." (OCR renders "1068" as "10GS"; the parenthetical Gregorian year, 1657 A.D., is unambiguous and matches the current entry.)
   Citation: Syed Muhammad Latif, *Lahore: Its History, Architectural Remains and Antiquities* (Lahore, 1892), p. 193, entry "The Tomb of Shah Chiragh." Full-text scan: https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.107067/2015.107067.Lahore-Its-History-Architectural-Remains-And-Antiquities_djvu.txt, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan, 1892).

2. **A different, more elaborate legend of the dome's construction, tied to Mauj Darya Bukhari — not the one currently published.**
   Quote: "The saint, according to his will, was buried in the closet in which he was accustomed to say his prayers. The tomb remained without a dome for a long time, and the story is still current that a lion used to come there every Thursday and clean the place, using his tail as broom. At length the saint Mauj Daria, Bokhari, was seen in a dream by the guardian of shrine, and it was intimated that a large dome should be constructed over the remains of Abdul Razzak. This was accordingly done."
   Citation: Latif, *Lahore* (1892), p. 193, as above, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

3. **The adjoining mosque's patron, per Latif, is not the patron the current entry names.**
   Quote: "The masjid to the west of the tomb, now occupied by the Accountant General's Office was built by Nawab Khan Bahadur Khan, viceroy of Lahore, during the reign of Mahomed Shah. It was built with the money obtained by the sale of the ornaments belonging to the Nawab's mother who had made a will to that effect."
   Citation: Latif, *Lahore* (1892), p. 193, as above, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan). See Conflicts — this appears to name a different patron from the current entry's "Zakariya Khan," and the 1716 date is not given by Latif at all.

4. **A fair is (or was, as of 1892) held annually at the tomb.**
   Quote: "A fair is annually held at the tomb of Shah Chiragh."
   Citation: Latif, *Lahore* (1892), p. 193, as above, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan).

5. **Official 1893-94 district gazetteer: the tomb reused as a government office, with specific adaptation dates.**
   Quote: "Other architectural remains of the period are the tomb of Shah Chiragh, used as a Government office..." and, separately, in the account of Lahore's modern public buildings: "the Accountant-General's Office (Chiragh Shah) adapted in 1860 and again improved in front in 1890."
   Citation: G. C. Walker (comp.), *Gazetteer of the Lahore District, 1893-94* (Lahore: Civil and Military Gazette Press), pp. as indexed. Full-text scan: https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.105616/2015.105616.Gazetteer-Of-The-Lahore-District-1893-94_djvu.txt, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: official body / colonial government gazetteer (full-text scan).

## Conflicts

- **The adjoining mosque's patron and date, and a probable conflation with a different Lahore shrine.** The current entry states the mosque was "added later, in 1716, under the patronage of Lahore's Mughal viceroy Zakariya Khan during the reign of Muhammad Shah Rangeela." Latif's 1892 account of Shah Chiragh itself names the builder as "Nawab Khan Bahadur Khan," funded by the sale of his mother's ornaments, with no date given (finding 3, above). Separately, Latif's own book *does* describe a mosque built by "Nawab Zakaria Khan, Governor of Lahore during the reign of Mahomed Shah" — but this passage is in his entry on the **shrine of Madho Lal Hussain** at Baghbanpura (pp. 145–146 of the same 1892 text), not Shah Chiragh, and the mosque's inscription there gives a construction date of "1144 A. H. (1731 A. H.)" [sic, recte 1731/32 A.D.], not 1716. The current entry's specific pairing of "Zakariya Khan" with "1716" was not found anywhere in the sources fetched this session, and its two named ingredients (a Zakariya Khan mosque, and Muhammad Shah's reign) match Madho Lal Hussain's shrine in Latif far more closely than they match Shah Chiragh's. This is reported, not resolved — it may be a genuine, separately-sourced fact about Shah Chiragh from a source not checked here, or it may be a detail carried over from the wrong shrine.
- **The Mauj Darya Bukhari connection is described two different ways.** The current entry's own "Corroborating Sources" section (added in a prior session, citing Alam Faqri's tazkira) states that "Mauj Darya Bukhari's own grave lies inside Shah Chiragh's mausoleum complex." Latif 1892 instead has Mauj Darya Bukhari appear only as a figure in a guardian's dream, instructing that a dome be built over Abdul Razzak's remains (finding 2) — a supernatural-visitation story, with no claim that Mauj Darya Bukhari is himself buried there. Both link the two saints, but by different mechanisms; neither source was checked against the other before now.
- **Could not verify:** the current entry's specific architectural inventory — eight graves total (including father's and grandfather's), cusped-arch niches, panelled cartouches, surviving floral fresco, and a formal listing as a protected monument by the Punjab Directorate General of Archaeology — appears in none of the sources fetched this session. The DG Archaeology / DOAM Punjab page that (per search snippets only) covers this site, https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10194, could not be reached at all from this environment (WebFetch failed silently twice; a direct `curl` also failed to connect) — so the "protected heritage monument" claim is neither confirmed nor contradicted here, only unconfirmable this session.
- **Could not verify:** "Auqaf Department... has administered the shrine since 1973." Not found in Latif 1892 or the 1893-94 Gazetteer (neither could address a post-1892 date); a personal blog's search-result snippet asserts "In 1973 this building was taken over by Aukaf Department" but the blog is not a citable source and was not itself fetched for verification. Reported as unverified in the current entry, not as a finding.

## Verdict

**STRONG** — two independent primary/official sources were fetched directly and quoted verbatim (Latif 1892; the 1893-94 district gazetteer), and between them they corroborate the core ancestry/death-year/Aurangzeb claim while surfacing a substantive, well-evidenced conflict over who built the adjoining mosque and when — with a specific, checkable alternative explanation (conflation with the Madho Lal Hussain shrine's own Zakaria Khan mosque) that the archive should weigh before its next revision of this entry.
