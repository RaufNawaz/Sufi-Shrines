# Ranmal Sharif
ranmal-sharif / researched 5 September 2026 / batch H · Punjab villages, Nankana Sahib, Sialkot

## Verified findings

1. The shrine's location, the saint's identity as founder of the Naushahia branch of the
   Qadiri order, and his death date are independently corroborated by established press.

   > "The shrine of Hazrat Haji Muhammad Naushah Ganj Bakhsh is located at Ranmal Sharif in
   > Phalia tehsil in the district of Mandi Bahauddin," identifying him as "Hazrat Haji Muhammad
   > Naushah Ganj Bakhsh Naushah Qadiri (d. 1654), the founder of Naushahia branch of Qadiri
   > Silsila."

   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "The Cult Of Mai Nawab Sahib," *The Friday Times*, 18
   December 2021, https://thefridaytimes.com/18-Dec-2021/the-cult-of-mai-nawab-sahib — accessed
   5 September 2026. Reliability: established press (author is a published anthropologist
   writing on Sindh/Punjab Sufi shrines) — citable. Confirms the sheet's `figure_died: 1654`
   and the Ranmal Sharif/Phalia/Naushahia identification exactly; the article does not give a
   birth year.

2. The same article documents that the Naushahiyya silsila spread outward from this line
   through named successors into the Pothohar region (Rawalpindi, Jhelum, Attock districts),
   via "Hazrat Syed Mir Kalan Badshah, after receiving robe of initiation from his mentor
   Khawaja Hafiz Qaimuddin Barkandaz... [who] spread the Naushahiyya silsila in the Pothohar
   region" — evidence, independent of the sheet, that the order named for this shrine is a
   real and traceable devotional network, not merely local legend.

   Citation: same as #1.

## Conflicts

- **Checked and absent, not a contradiction:** two separate editions of the Gujrat District
  Gazetteer — the district that historically included Phalia tehsil before Mandi Bahauddin was
  split off — were searched in full text for "Ranmal," "Naushah/Nausha," "Ganj Bakhsh,"
  "Naushahia," and "Qadiri." Neither mentions the saint or the shrine at all:
  - *Gazetteer of the Gujrat District, 1883-84* (Punjab Government; Lahore: Arya Press, 1884),
    https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.35269 — accessed 5 September 2026.
  - C. E. Luard, *Gujrat District Gazetteer* (1893), https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.35649
    — accessed 5 September 2026.
  This is worth recording as a genuine negative result — the two standard colonial gazetteers
  for this saint's home district are silent on him — rather than treated as counter-evidence to
  the shrine's existence, which is independently well attested (see Verified findings).
- No conflict found between the Friday Times source and the current entry's core claims
  (founder identity, order name, death year, location).
