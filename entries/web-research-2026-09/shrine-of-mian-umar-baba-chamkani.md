## Conflicts

- The current entry already flags an unresolved internal date conflict: Alam Faqri's tazkira
  gives the saint's death as Rajab 1119 AH (~1707-08 CE), roughly seven decades before the 1776
  death year used elsewhere in the same entry. This pass's new source (finding #1, Dawn 2018)
  independently repeats the 1671-1776 tradition but does not engage the tazkira's earlier date
  at all — it is a second source for the "1776 side," not an adjudication. The conflict stands
  exactly as the entry already states it.
- New complication, not a resolution: the Peshawar District Gazetteer (finding #9) records an
  "insurrection in 1779 by Mian Umar of Chamkanni" — a political/military event three years after
  the saint's own traditionally reported death. No source found in this pass says whether this is
  the same devotional figure (in which case the 1776 death date would be wrong, or the 1779 event
  misdated, or this is a different man), a namesake relative, or an unrelated historical actor who
  happens to share the clan name "of Chamkanni." This muddies rather than helps adjudicate the
  entry's existing date conflict, and is reported without being resolved, per the task's
  instruction.
- Internal inconsistency inside the Dawn (2018) source itself (finding #6): it dates the paternal
  grandfather's migration to Faridabad to "the early 15th century," which — given the saint's own
  reported 1671 birth — leaves roughly two and a half centuries unaccounted for between a
  grandfather's migration and his grandson's birth. Reported as found in the source; this may be
  the article's own error, and is not resolved here.
- Near-miss checked and ruled out: "Umar Daraz Khallil," a 20th/21st-century Pashto film actor
  (d. 2016), was — per his own Wikipedia page, fetched directly — also "born in Chamkani Village
  in the suburbs of Peshawar." Confirmed to have no connection to the saint: different era
  (career from 1971), different family (father "film and stage actor Khalil Khan"), no religious
  content anywhere in his biography. Checked and ruled out, not used.
- Near-miss checked and ruled out: "Mian Muhammad Umar," a sitting member of the Khyber
  Pakhtunkhwa Provincial Assembly from Nowshera District (in office since 29 February 2024) —
  per his Wikipedia page, fetched directly and confirmed to be an unrelated living politician who
  merely shares the name. Checked and ruled out, not used.
- Wikipedia's "Chamkani, Peshawar" article was fetched directly and checked: it contains no
  mention of Mian Umar Baba, Mian Muhammad Umar Chamkani, or any shrine at all. Checked and ruled
  out as a source (not merely unfetched).

than settling it.
