# Jhollay Lal Mandir
jhollay-lal-mandir / researched 5 September 2026 / batch D · Sindh Hindu-Nanakpanthi-Sikh and Balochistan

## Verified findings

1. **Location, custodianship, and building description.** The Udero Lal Research Project (ULRP)
   — described on its own site as "an international and multidisciplinary project funded by the
   National Center for Scientific Research (CNRS) and hosted by the Centre for South Asian
   Studies at the School for Advanced Study in Social Sciences, Paris (EHESS)" — published a
   dedicated field-documentation post on this exact temple in 2016.

   > "The exact location of this temple is Darya Lal Street, Jodia bazaar, Karachi."

   > "Now, this locality is home to Lasis, Hindus from Lasbela district of Baluchistan and they
   > take care of this temple."

   Citation: Udero Lal Research Project, "Jhulay Lal Mandir, Jodia Bazaar Karachi," 10 July 2016,
   https://uderolalresearchproject.wordpress.com/2016/07/10/jhulay-lal-mandir-jodia-bazaar-karachi/,
   accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: academic field-research project (CNRS-funded, hosted
   at EHESS Paris), published in blog form rather than peer-reviewed — treated as citable given
   its institutional academic backing, but flagged here for the reviewer's own judgment since it
   sits outside this project's usual reliability categories (published book, academic paper,
   established press, official body).

   This single source independently confirms several details the current entry already states:
   the exact street/location, the Lasi-community custodianship, the wooden-then-renovated
   building history, and the rooftop swing (*jhula*) the popular name is drawn from — the post
   also describes "a stone based structure with arched opening... a smaller square building with
   a hall housing various icons and photos of Jhulay Lal along with a Jhula (swing)," matching
   the current entry's architectural description closely.

## Conflicts

None found between sources — but a **structural asymmetry** is worth flagging rather than
resolving. Every other citable source located on Karachi's Jhulelal/Darya Lal tradition — the
Wikipedia "Darya Lal Mandir" article's own reference list (chased directly, not cited here as
Wikipedia itself: Express Tribune 8 Nov 2018, Dawn 6 Jan 2016, Express Tribune 14 Dec 2015, Dawn
16 Sep 2015) and a further Express Tribune piece found independently ("Stuck in Karachi traffic?
It's okay, so is Jhulel Lal," 26 February 2025, fetched directly) — concerns the larger, separate
Darya Lal Sankat Mochan Mandir near the Custom House/Native Jetty, not the Jodia Bazaar temple.
The Express Tribune 2025 piece was fetched and checked specifically for any Jodia Bazaar/Lasi
content and confirmed to contain none — it discusses only the Udero Lal shrine near Tando Adam
Khan and mentions Karachi only in a single metaphorical opening line. So the Jodia Bazaar temple
that is this file's subject rests, in the record assembled here, on one source only, while the
much more heavily covered Karachi Jhulelal site is a different building entirely — exactly the
distinction the current entry itself draws, and worth the reader's attention rather than treating
the general Jhulelal literature as if it applies to this specific temple.
