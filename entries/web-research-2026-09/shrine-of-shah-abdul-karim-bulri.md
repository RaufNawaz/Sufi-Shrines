# Shrine of Shah Abdul Karim Bulri
shrine-of-shah-abdul-karim-bulri / researched 5 September 2026 / batch C · Sindh Sufi

## Verified findings

1. Birth year and the relationship to Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai (Friday Times account).

   > "Hazrat Shah Abdul Karim Bulri was born in a Syed family in Mutalvi town, now called
   > Matiari, in 1538... He died at Bulri village in 1623."

   The same piece calls him "the great-great-grandfather of Shah Abdul Latif Bhitai
   (1689-1752)."

   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Patron Saint of Bulri," *The Friday Times*, 2 October 2020,

   > daughters; surviving sons named as Syed Jalal Shah, Syed Burhan Shah, Syed Lal Muhammad

   Citation: same as #1.

   > Shah Abdul Latif Bhitai "laid the foundation of the tomb and three-domed mosque in 1154

   Citation: same as #1. (The same source also gives a completion year two years later, but the

   https://archive.org/details/Bayan-ul-arifeensindhiMalfuzatShahKareemBulriWaro — accessed

## Conflicts

1. **Birth year**: 1538 (Kalhoro/*Friday Times*, finding #1) vs 1536 (current entry's
   Description, and the Endowment Fund Trust Sindh heritage page fetched in this pass, which
   gives "Shah Abdul Karim Bulri (1536-1623)").

2. **Generational distance to Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai — now a three-way rather than two-way
   split.** The current entry's Description calls him the "great-great-grandfather" (4
   generations) on its primary sourcing, but its own Alam Faqri citation already called him the
   "great-grandfather" (3 generations) — a conflict the entry already flagged. This pass adds
   two more data points on each side: Kalhoro's *Friday Times* piece (finding #1) independently
   says "great-great-grandfather," while the archive.org catalogue record for the saint's own
   *Bayan al-Arifin* (finding #4) independently calls him "great grandfather" — so the split is
   now evidenced 2-to-2 across independent sources, not resolved by this pass.

3. **Date of the *Bayan al-Arifin*'s completion**: Kalhoro's *Friday Times* piece states the
   author "compiled [the saint's sayings and verses] in 1629, six years after his death in
   1623"; the current entry's Description instead says the work was "completed in 1630, seven
   years after the poet's death." One year apart on both figures.

4. **The name of the compiler.** The current entry's Description names the compiler "a
   disciple, Mir Daryai Tharawi." Neither source found in this pass uses that name: Kalhoro's
   *Friday Times* piece names him "Abdul Raza bin Muhammad Wasi," and the archive.org catalogue
   record for the same work names him "Muhammad Raza bin Abdul-Wasay" (a name the two new
   sources broadly agree with each other on, reordered). This is a real discrepancy with the
   entry's existing text, not just a spelling variant — it is a different name in a different
   order, not corroborated by either new source, and is reported here rather than resolved.

## Verdict

STRONG — three independent, directly-fetched, citable sources (Kalhoro/*Friday Times* 2020,
the archive.org catalogue record of the *Bayan al-Arifin*, and *Dawn* 2024) corroborate the
core facts of the saint's life and shrine, while also surfacing two new, precisely-dated
conflicts (birth year, and the compiler's name) that the current entry does not carry.
