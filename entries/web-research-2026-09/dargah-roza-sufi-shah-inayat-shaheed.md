# Dargah / Roza Sufi Shah Inayat Shaheed

## Verified findings

1. Birth details and birthplace — adds a specific village and year, not in the current entry
   (which leaves `figure_born` blank).

   > Shah Inayat was "born in 1655/56 AD in Miranpur, a village opposite Jhok Sharif," on land
   > later occupied by Shah Murad Sugar Mills near Hyderabad. "Shah Inayat belonged to a family
   > of religious scholars" and inherited ancestral lands designated for running a seminary.

   Citation: "The socialist Sufi and the Jhok war," *Dawn*, Sunday Magazine (InpaperMagazine),
   20 July 2014, https://www.dawn.com/news/1120029 — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability:
   established press — citable.

2. The commune, the siege, and the manner of his death — corroborates and sharpens the current
   entry's account.

   > "The troika of a feudal lord, a Maulvi and a Pir" convinced the governor of Thatta to
   > attack the commune; Shah Inayat "busied the commanders of the invading army into a
   > never-ending series of talks while his followers raided the tents of enemy troops at
   > night," until "the enemy understood the strategy, beheaded Shah Inayat, massacred his
   > followers and took the head of their Master to Delhi."

   Citation: same as finding #1.

3. The urs and its distinctive rituals, in a companion piece by a named author — none of this
   is in the current entry.

   > The annual urs falls "on the 16th day of Safar" and, unlike other Sufi shrines, "begins
   > with sama — a melodious session of poetry recital sans the accompaniment of any musical
   > instrument" — with no "smoking hashish or consuming cannabis" or "dancing girls or boys."
   > Sajjada Nasheen Sufi Attaulah Sattari leads sama sessions each morning.

   Citation: Abbas Jalbani, "The Jhok of Sufi warriors," *Dawn*, Sunday Magazine, 20 July 2014,
   https://www.dawn.com/news/1120030 — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established
   press — citable.

4. An independently-authored, differently-sourced account gives a different birthplace, father,
   and lineage — a genuine conflict with finding #1 (see Conflicts §1), and new detail the
   current entry does not have at all.

   > Shah Inayat was "born in Thatta during Emperor Aurangzeb's reign"; his great-grandfather was
   > "Makhdoom Sadho Langah" and his father "Makhdoom Fazalullah, described as 'an unpretentious
   > ascetic.'" He studied "under Shah Abdul Malik in the Deccan" and "Shah Ghulam Muhammad in
   > Delhi," the latter teacher having "came to Thatta along with Shah Inayat."

   Citation: Raza Naeem, "Remembering the Socialist Sufi of the Indus Valley, Shah Inayat
   Shaheed," *The Wire*, 25 October 2018,
   https://m.thewire.in/article/religion/socialist-sufi-indus-valley-sufi-shah-inayat-shaheed —
   accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established independent press (India) — citable, though
   this is the only source in this pass to give the father's/great-grandfather's names, so it is
   reported as single-sourced within this pass.

5. A later urs report gives a specific anniversary count and confirms continuing observance.

   > The Sindh government declared a local holiday in Sujawal district for the saint's "309th
   > urs" in November 2017, featuring "372 traditional circles within the shrine courtyard" and
   > recitation of Sufi kalaam.

   Citation: "Three-day urs of Shah Inayat gets under way in Jhok Sharif," *Dawn*, 7 November
   2017, https://www.dawn.com/news/1368790 — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability:
   established press — citable. (Note, reported without resolving it: a strict Gregorian count
   from a 1718 death to a November 2017 urs would be roughly the 299th anniversary, not the
   309th; the discrepancy may simply reflect that "Nth urs" is conventionally counted by Hijri
   rather than Gregorian years, which run shorter — this pass did not verify which convention
   the reporting used.)

## Conflicts

1. **Birthplace and family.** Finding #1 (*Dawn*, 2014) places his birth in "Miranpur, a village
   opposite Jhok Sharif" and describes his family only as "religious scholars," without naming
   them. Finding #4 (*The Wire*, 2018) instead places his birth in "Thatta" city and names a
   father (Makhdoom Fazalullah) and great-grandfather (Makhdoom Sadho Langah) that appear in no
   other source found in this pass. The two accounts cannot both be describing the same
   birthplace as stated.

2. **Manner of death.** The current entry's Description says he was "taken by treachery and put
   to death." The *Dawn* sources (findings #1-2) instead describe an extended siege/battle in
   which he used peace negotiations as cover for night raids until the besieging force "beheaded"
   him once they "understood the strategy" — a different emphasis (military stratagem outwitted,
   rather than betrayal) though not necessarily incompatible with "treachery" playing some role.
   Reported as a difference in framing, not resolved.

3. **Anniversary arithmetic**, per finding #5 above — flagged, not resolved.

## Verdict

STRONG — at least three independent, directly-fetched, citable sources (two separate *Dawn*
pieces from 2014, a further *Dawn* piece from 2017, and *The Wire* in 2018) corroborate the
core narrative of the commune, the siege, the execution, and the ongoing urs — while also
surfacing a genuine, unresolved conflict over his birthplace and parentage that the current
entry, which names neither, does not currently reflect either way.
