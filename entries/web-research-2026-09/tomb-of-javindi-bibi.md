# Tomb of Javindi Bibi
tomb-of-javindi-bibi / researched 5 September 2026 / batch B · Uch Sharif and west-Punjab khanqahs

## Verified findings

1. Official UNESCO tentative-list document dates the tomb to c.1494 and gives its architectural
   description.

   > "the second for the latter's great grant daughter, Bibi Jalwindi, in c.1494... They all have
   > three tiers, an octagonal base supporting a zone of transition surmounted by a dome and are
   > richly decorated with carved timber, cut and moulded brick, and blue and white faience
   > mosaic tiles. The basement walls taper and are supported by 8 engaged tapering corner
   > towers."

   Citation: UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Tentative Lists, "Tomb of Bibi Jawindi, Baha'al-Halim
   and Ustead and the Tomb and Mosque of Jalaluddin Bukhari," Ref. 1883 (submitted 30/01/2004,
   Dept. of Archaeology and Museums, Government of Pakistan).
   https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1883/ — retrieved via r.jina.ai proxy, accessed
   5 September 2026 (direct access 403; Wayback Machine unreachable by this session's tools).
   Reliability:
   official body document — citable; text cross-checked against the August 2026 pass's
   independent quote of the same passage (reached there via a Wayback Machine snapshot) and the
   two match word for word.

   Note: this document does not name a builder for Bibi Jawindi's tomb in the passage retrieved
   — it identifies her only as Jahaniyan Jahangasht's "great grant[grand]daughter."

2. An academic paper independently gives a named builder, a precise date, and further
   architectural description.

   > "The tomb of Bibi Jawindi was constructed by a prince of Khurasan, Muhammad Dilshad, in AD
   > 1493 and is a placing example of structure comportment the traces of Central Asia."

   Citation: Nusrat Bibi, "The Land of Saints Uch Sharif," International Journal of Scientific
   and Research Publications, Vol. 11, Issue 10 (October 2021), pp. 283-284, ISSN 2250-3153, DOI:
   10.29322/IJSRP.11.10.2021.p11832. https://www.ijsrp.org/research-paper-1021/ijsrp-p11832.pdf
   — content retrieved via the r.jina.ai text-extraction proxy, accessed 5 September 2026,
   because direct WebFetch could not parse the underlying PDF's compressed content stream.
   Reliability:
   academic paper (open-access, DOI-registered) — citable per the bar, though flagged (as the
   August 2026 pass also flagged it, for the neighbouring Ustad Nuriya entry): a Textile Design
   department publication, not a specialist archaeology/history journal, with uneven English.

   The same source also describes an octagonal, three-storey structure; a glazed-tile parapet
   bearing an "Allah" inscription; arched clerestory windows; and "Multani tiles that show
   geometrical and floral patterns."

## Conflicts

- **Date, by about a year, between my two own sources.** UNESCO's document gives "c.1494"; the
  IJSRP paper gives an unhedged "AD 1493." The sheet's current entry already carries "1493
  (circa)" — consistent with the paper, off by about a year from the UNESCO document. Neither of
  my sources contradicts the current entry; they mildly disagree with each other.
- **Builder's name and origin, only single-sourced and inconsistent with unverified tourism
  copy.** The IJSRP paper is the only one of my two verified sources to name a builder at all
  ("Muhammad Dilshad," "a prince of Khurasan"); the UNESCO document, in the passage retrieved,
  names no builder. Widely-repeated tourism copy (seen only in a search-result snippet — a
  Tripadvisor caption — never independently fetched, and Tripadvisor is excluded by the
  reliability bar regardless) calls him "an Iranian prince, Dilshad" rather than a prince of
  Khurasan; historic Khurasan spans present-day north-eastern Iran and parts of Central Asia, so
  this is not a hard contradiction, but the specific regional label differs between what could be
  verified and what circulates unverified. The current entry's own text ("an Iranian prince,
  Dilshad") matches the unverified tourism phrasing rather than the one verified academic
  source's "prince of Khurasan" — flagged, not resolved.
- The current entry's own qa_note already records that the sheet originally gave 1291 and named
  the wrong figure, since corrected to 1493 (circa) with the right figure (Bibi Jawindi). Nothing
  found this pass contradicts the corrected entry; both of my verified sources support a
  construction date in the 1493-94 range for a monument connected to Bibi Jawindi.

## Verdict

STRONG — two independent, directly-fetched, citable sources agree on a construction date in the
1493-94 range (an official UNESCO tentative-list document, and a DOI-registered academic paper),
corroborating the sheet's already-corrected `year_built`. They diverge on, and only one names, a
specific builder and his regional origin, reported as an open point rather than resolved.
