## Overview

In the old city of Multan stands the shrine of Hazrat Shah Yusuf Gardez — one of the earliest Sufi saints of the region and, by tradition, a founding figure in the Islamisation of Multan. Coming from Gardez in what is now Afghanistan, he settled in Multan in the early twelfth century and founded the line of the Gardezi sayyids, long among the leading religious families of the city. His shrine, a low tomb famed for its exquisite blue tilework, is one of the most distinctive and revered monuments of the “City of Saints.”

## The Life of the Saint

Sayyid Yusuf, called Shah Yusuf Gardez, is remembered as having come from Gardez to Multan around the early twelfth century (tradition places his arrival in the years around 1088 and his death in the first half of the 1100s), when Islam was still taking root in the region. A Sayyid of the family of the Prophet, he is honoured as one of the first great saints of Multan, a preacher whose presence helped make the city a centre of the faith even before the coming of the great Suhrawardi masters. He founded the Gardezi sayyid family, which would remain prominent in the religious and civic life of Multan for centuries. He died at Multan and was buried there.

## The Shrine and its Devotion

The shrine of Shah Yusuf Gardez is celebrated for its architecture as much as its sanctity: a low, rectangular tomb sheathed in the brilliant blue-and-white glazed *kashi* tilework of Multan, it is one of the loveliest of the city’s many shrines. Kept by the Gardezi family, it is a continuous place of pilgrimage, where devotees recite the Fatiha and seek the blessing of one of Multan’s founding saints, and its annual urs gathers the faithful in the customary manner. It stands among the oldest sacred monuments of the city.

## Legacy

Shah Yusuf Gardez holds an honoured place as one of the earliest saints of Multan and the founder of the Gardezi sayyids — a pioneer of Sufism in the Indus valley whose coming, tradition holds, helped turn Multan toward the faith it would come to embody as the “City of Saints.” His tile-clad shrine remains a jewel of the old city and a living centre of devotion.
