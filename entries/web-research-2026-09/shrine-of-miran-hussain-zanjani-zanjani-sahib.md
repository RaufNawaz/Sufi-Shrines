# Shrine of Miran Hussain Zanjani (Zanjani Sahib)

shrine-of-miran-hussain-zanjani-zanjani-sahib / researched 5 September 2026 / batch G · Lahore and Islamabad

## Verified findings

1. **Birth, arrival, and death years match the current entry, from an independent press source; father's name is a new detail.**
   Quote: "His father's name was Syed Ali Mahmood and was born in year 958 AD (or 347 AH) in Zanjan, Iran" ... "came to Lahore around 998 AD (or 387 AH)" ... "died in 1042 AD (or 431 AH)."
   Citation: Majid Sheikh, "Harking back: Pioneering Muslims before Data Sahib came to Lahore," *Dawn*, 26 May 2024, https://www.dawn.com/news/1835638, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn, "Harking back" column).
   Note: this independently corroborates the current entry's `figure_born` (958) and `figure_died` (1042) fields exactly.

2. **Two named brothers, buried in two different, named Lahore locations — not "nearby" Chah Miran.**
   Quote: on Yaqub Zanjani, "He lies buried just behind the Lady Aitchison Hospital next to Mayo Hospital in a beautiful shrine." On Musa Hasan Zanjani, "He was buried in Misri Shah next to the police station."
   Citation: Majid Sheikh, *Dawn*, 26 May 2024 (as above), accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn).

3. **A second Dawn column, by the same author, names the saint buried at Chah Miran differently.**
   Quote: "It is claimed that he was honoured to lead the funeral prayers. Zanjani is buried in Chah Miran," referring, in context, to a saint named "Sheikh Khwaja Hasan Zanjani" whose funeral procession Ali Hasan Hajveri (Data Ganj Bakhsh) is said to have encountered on his own arrival in Lahore.
   Citation: Majid Sheikh, "Harking back: The mystery and magic of Lahore's patron saint," *Dawn*, 13 August 2023, https://www.dawn.com/news/1769801, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: established press (Dawn). See Conflicts — the name given here does not match "Miran Hussain Zanjani" (or "Syed Meeran Hussain Zanjani") used in the current entry and in the same author's other, later column.

4. **Official Auqaf & Religious Affairs Department page confirms the shrine and a numbered annual urs, but gives no dates for the saint himself.**
   Quote: "The 1011th Annual Urs Ceremonies of Hazrat Miran Hussain Zanjani (RA) has been started" (page dated 24 November 2019).
   Citation: Auqaf & Religious Affairs Department, Government of the Punjab, "Urs Hazrat Miran Hussain (RA)," https://auqaf.punjab.gov.pk/urs-miran-hussain, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: official body (provincial government department page).
   Note, not asserted as fact: if this urs count is reckoned in Hijri years, "1011th" in a page dated late 2019 (~1441 AH) would place the saint's death year at roughly 430 AH (~1038–39 CE) — in the neighbourhood of, though not identical to, the 431 AH / 1042 CE given by Majid Sheikh (finding 1). This arithmetic was performed here, is sensitive to which year the department began counting, and is not a claim made by the source itself; it is offered only as a rough consistency check, not a verified fact.

5. **Latif's exhaustive 1892 survey of Lahore's monuments and saints has no entry on this saint at all.**
   The full text of Syed Muhammad Latif's *Lahore: Its History, Architectural Remains and Antiquities* (1892) — which does cover Shah Chiragh, Data Ganj Bakhsh, Madho Lal Hussain, and dozens of other Lahore shrines in detail — contains no passage on "Zanjani," "Miran Hussain," "Chah Miran," or "Khui/Khoi Miran" as a saint. Its only use of the name "Zanjani" is a footnote citing "Sheikh Ahmad Zanjani, the author of Tuhfat-ul-Wasilin" for an unrelated claim about Lahore's mythical founding by "Raja Prichat, a Pandu King," adding that "Sheikh Ahmad compiled his book in 435 A.H., or 1043 A.D." — a different individual (a chronicler, not the saint), noted here only because his book's date is close to the saint's reported death year.
   Citation: Syed Muhammad Latif, *Lahore: Its History, Architectural Remains and Antiquities* (1892), full text https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.107067/2015.107067.Lahore-Its-History-Architectural-Remains-And-Antiquities_djvu.txt, searched in full, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: published book (full-text scan) — an absence, not a positive claim, but a source that plausibly should have covered this saint and does not.

6. **The 1893-94 district gazetteer confirms "Khoi Miran" as a real, named quarter of Lahore, independent of any saint-legend.**
   Quote: the 1891 census's list of the city's municipal wards includes "(15). Khoi Miran."
   Citation: G. C. Walker (comp.), *Gazetteer of the Lahore District, 1893-94*, full text https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.105616/2015.105616.Gazetteer-Of-The-Lahore-District-1893-94_djvu.txt, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability class: official body / colonial government gazetteer (full-text scan).

## Conflicts

- **Two names for one grave, from the same author.** Majid Sheikh's 2024 Dawn column calls the saint buried at Chah Miran "Syed Meeran Hussain Zanjani" (matching the current entry); his own 2023 Dawn column, describing what reads as the same foundational Lahore legend (Data Ganj Bakhsh's arrival coinciding with a Zanjani funeral procession), names him "Sheikh Khwaja Hasan Zanjani." Neither column cross-references the other. Reported, not resolved.
- **The brother said to lie "nearby" is, per an independent source, elsewhere in the city.** The current entry states the saint "was buried alongside his brother, Hazrat Musa Hussain Zanjani, whose own shrine lies nearby." Majid Sheikh's 2024 column instead gives three Zanjani brothers with three separately located shrines across Lahore — Meeran Hussain at Chah Miran, Yaqub at Mayo Hospital/Lady Aitchison Hospital, and "Musa Hasan Zanjani" (a different middle name from the current entry's "Musa Hussain") at Misri Shah, "next to the police station" — none of which reads as "alongside" or "nearby" Chah Miran.
- **An un-sourced, drastically later chronology exists on Wikipedia and should not be trusted, but is flagged here as a caution.** The "Zanjani Sahib shrine" Wikipedia article carries, in an uncited supplementary block, the figures "Wafat: 22 Shawwal 740 AH" (≈1339–40 CE) and a migration "725 AH" (≈1325 CE) "from Delhi [to the] Deccan," describing the man as a "khalifa & mureed of Hazrat Shaykh Nizamuddin Auliya" — the Delhi Chishti saint who died in 725 AH/1325 CE. These figures place a "Peer Zanjani" three centuries later than, and in a completely different region from, the Zanjan-born, early-Ghaznavid-era missionary described everywhere else in this file, and the Nizamuddin Auliya connection reads as a plausible conflation with an unrelated Sufi of the same surname region. This is not cited to anything and was not independently verified anywhere; it is reported only as an observed hazard, in the same spirit as this project's own caution against assembling a real man's biography for the wrong saint — not as a finding about this shrine.
- **Could not verify:** the current entry's claim that Zanjani travelled to Lahore "on the instruction of his spiritual master, Sheikh Abu al-Fazal Khatil." This name appears in none of the sources fetched this session (it derives solely from Alam Faqri's tazkira, already in the current entry's own bibliography, which was not independently available to re-check here).
- **Could not verify:** the current entry's architectural description — "a simple raised platform without a dome" — was not addressed in any source fetched this session, positively or negatively.
- **Minor date variance:** the current entry gives "thirty-six years" of preaching; Majid Sheikh's 2024 column (958→998→1042) implies roughly 44 years in Lahore total but does not itself state a "years preached" figure, so this is not a direct contradiction, only an unconfirmed number in the current entry.

## Verdict

**STRONG** — two independent Dawn columns (by the same columnist, four years apart) and an official Punjab Auqaf page were fetched and quoted, corroborating the current entry's birth/death years, but the two columns disagree with each other and with the current entry on the saint's exact name and on where his brothers are buried — a genuine, reportable conflict at the center of the entry rather than at its margins.
