# Dargah of Khwaja Muhammad Zaman (Luari Sharif)
dargah-of-khwaja-muhammad-zaman-luari-sharif / researched 5 September 2026 / batch C · Sindh Sufi

## Verified findings

1. A 36-year closure and 2020 reopening of the shrine, entirely new to the current entry.

   > The shrine "remained closed since bloody clashes between two rival groups of devotees 36
   > years ago" — dating the original closure to roughly 1984 — after "the shrine was sealed by
   > the provincial government when two rival groups fought a battle for the custodianship of
   > Sultanul Aulia." It was reopened when "Auqaf Secretary Munawwar Mahesar...personally opened
   > the main entrance of the shrine at Friday noon." A 2015 reopening attempt had failed when
   > "one person lost his life in a firing incident."

   Citation: Muhammad Hashim Khan Bhurgari, "Luari Sharif shrine unsealed after 36 years of
   closure," *Dawn*, 25 January 2020, https://www.dawn.com/news/1530312 — accessed 5 September
   2026. Reliability: established press — citable.

2. The current entry's "police post…since the 1980s" detail is confirmed and dated more
   precisely, and a further architectural detail (2011 flood damage) is added.

   > "The dargah has been closed for public pilgrimage since 1980s" due to property disputes
   > among the saint's heirs; "a permanent police post has been created out side the main
   > entrance." Separately, "a minaret of the shrine was the recent victim which collapsed
   > during the torrential rains of 2011."

   Citation: "Luari Sharif Shrine, Badin," Endowment Fund Trust (EFT) Sindh heritage
   documentation site, https://heritage.eftsindh.com/districts/badin/luari-sharif-shrine.php —
   accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: heritage-documentation body, not a government or
   academic source — moderate reliability, treated as citable but not authoritative.

3. The father's name and epithet, corroborating the current entry.

   > "The grave of Hazrat Abdul Latif alias Dhair Dhani (father of Khwaja Muhammad Zaman)" lies
   > five kilometres north, on the Sim Nala's right bank.

   Citation: same as finding #2. This corroborates — with an added epithet, "Dhair Dhani" — the
   current entry's claim that the saint's father was "Shaikh Abdul Latif."

4. A death date that **conflicts** with the current entry (see Conflicts §1).

   > A separately-published piece names "Makhdum Muhammad Zaman of Luari (d. 1774 AD)" among the
   > "more famous Naqshbandi Sufis of the eighteenth century."

   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Walhari Naqshbandi Sufis of Sindh," *The Friday Times*,
   26 January 2018, https://www.thefridaytimes.com/26-Jan-2018/walhari-naqshbandi-sufis-of-sindh
   — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established press — citable.

## Conflicts

1. **Death year.** The current entry gives "He died in January 1775." Kalhoro's *Friday Times*
   piece (finding #4) instead gives "d. 1774 AD" for a "Makhdum Muhammad Zaman of Luari" —
   the same shrine and same title ("Naqshbandi Sufi… of the eighteenth century"), one year
   apart. Note the current entry's own qa_note already recorded that Alam Faqri's tazkira "gives
   a year of death, but its digits are illegible in the OCR scan" — so this is now a second,
   independently-sourced candidate year (1774) to weigh against the entry's stated 1775, without
   resolving which is right.

2. **No corroboration found, in either direction, for**: the Siddiqi lineage claim (descent from
   the caliph Abu Bakr); the honorific "Sultan al-Aulia" as bestowed by teacher Khwaja Abul
   Masakin (a search-result summary, not independently fetched and verified, does describe this
   same teacher relationship — see Unverified leads); the 1737 khanqah founding date; and the
   claim of "some eighty-five poems" surviving. None of the sources fetched in this pass
   discussed these specifics one way or the other.

## Verdict

STRONG — three independent, directly-fetched, citable sources (a 2020 *Dawn* report, the EFT
Sindh heritage page, and a 2018 Kalhoro *Friday Times* piece) corroborate the shrine's closure
history, its father's identity, and add substantial new detail (the 36-year closure and 2020
reopening, 2011 flood damage) not previously in the archive — while also surfacing a real,
independently-sourced conflict over the saint's death year (1774 vs. the entry's 1775).
