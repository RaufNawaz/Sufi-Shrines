# Dargah of Pir Muhammad Rashid (Roze Dhani), Pir Jo Goth
dargah-of-pir-muhammad-rashid-roze-dhani-pir-jo-goth / researched 5 September 2026 / batch C · Sindh Sufi

## Verified findings

1. Confirms the saint's identity, his epithet, and his position as the first in the Pir Pagara
   line, on the occasion of the 2012 funeral of Pir Pagara VII (Shah Mardan Shah), which was
   held at the same shrine complex.

   > The Dargah-i-Rashidia graveyard at Pir Jo Goth holds "tombs of seven Pir Pagaras," with the
   > previous six listed starting with "Pir Syed Mohammad Rashid Roza Dhani."

   Citation: "Pir Pagara laid to rest amid wailing by his followers," *Dawn*, 12 January 2012,
   https://www.dawn.com/news/687622 — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established
   press — citable.

2. An independent report of the same event corroborates the burial site's name.

   > Pir Pagara VII "was laid to rest on Thursday at Dhani Shah shrine in Pir Jo Goth amid tears
   > and sobs."

   Citation: "Pir Pagara laid to rest in Pir Jo Goth," *Business Recorder*, 13 January 2012,
   https://www.brecorder.com/news/3946574/pir-pagara-laid-to-rest-in-pir-jo-goth-201201131142179
   — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established press — citable.

3. The scale of pilgrimage/observance at the shrine complex, not previously in the current
   entry (reported for the 2012 funeral specifically, but illustrating the site's continuing
   pull).

   > "Hundreds of thousands of people started arriving at the Dargah soon after the announcement
   > of death," and "an area within a radius of seven kilometres around Pir Jo Goth turned into a
   > parking lot of vehicles."

   Citation: same as finding #1.

## Conflicts

None found between the sources fetched in this pass — the two 2012 press reports agree with
each other and with the current entry's account of the saint as the founder/first of the Pir
Pagara/Pir Jo Goth line.

However, several specific claims in the current entry's Description could **not** be verified
by anything fetched in this pass, and are flagged rather than treated as confirmed:

- The claim that he was a "**Qadiri–Naqshbandi**" saint. One search result (a page at
  maktabah.org that returned a 404 when fetched directly, so it cannot be cited) used the label
  "Qadri Naqshbandi Sindhi" for a figure with matching AH dates (1171-1234 AH) — this is at best
  an unverified lead pointing the same direction, not a confirmation.
- The claim that he founded a distinct "**Rashidi Sufi order**" as such.
- The claim that "in contrast to the festive urs kept at most Sufi tombs, the Rashidi tradition
  is said to observe no public urs here," the "27 Rajab" pilgrimage date, the requirement to
  enter with head covered, and the "Shahi (royal) gate."
- The specific dates 1757-1818 for the saint's life.

None of these were confirmed or contradicted by the sources fetched in this pass — they remain
exactly as uncertain as before this research.

## Verdict

PARTIAL — two independent, directly-fetched, citable press sources (*Dawn* and *Business
Recorder*, both January 2012) firmly establish the saint's identity, his epithet, and his
position as founder of the Pir Pagara/Dargah-i-Rashidia line at Pir Jo Goth, and the scale of
continuing devotion at the site. But none of the current entry's more specific claims — the
Qadiri-Naqshbandi silsila label, the "Rashidi order" name, the no-public-urs claim, the 27 Rajab
date, or the exact 1757-1818 dates — were reachable through any source this pass could actually
fetch and verify; the two most promising leads (a maktabah.org page and an academic PDF) were
both technically inaccessible rather than absent.
