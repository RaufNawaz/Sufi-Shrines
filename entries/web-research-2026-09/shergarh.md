# Shergarh
shergarh / researched 5 September 2026 / batch H · Punjab villages, Nankana Sahib, Sialkot

## Verified findings

1. Saiyad families settled around the shrine of Daud Bandagi at Shergarh from early in the
   16th century — consistent with the sheet's 1513 birth date for the saint, though the
   gazetteer does not itself name Daud Bandagi's birth or death years.

   > "The Saiyads are met with chiefly about the shrines of Daud Bandagi at Shergarh; and of
   > Miran Lal, Bahawal Shah and Shah Mukim at Hujra... They settled in this country early in
   > the 16th century."

   Citation: P. J. Fagan, *Gazetteer of the Montgomery District, 1898-99* (Revised Edition,
   compiled and published under the authority of the Punjab Government; Lahore: "Civil and
   Military Gazette" Press, 1900), p. 34 (Chap. III — The People).
   https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.105623/2015.105623.Gazetteer-Of-The-Montgomery-District_djvu.txt
   — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: published colonial district gazetteer — citable.

2. Sher Shah Suri (r. 1540–45) is credited with building a fort at Shergarh, which the
   gazetteer treats as the origin of the town's name/importance — a fact about the place
   distinct from, and earlier discussed than, the saint's own shrine, and not present in the
   current entry.

   > "Sher Shah spent some time at the commencement of his reign in the Punjab, and is said to
   > have built a fort at the town of Shergarh to protect the Nakka country."

   Citation: same as #1, p. 34 (Chap. II — History).

3. The shrine, its Urs, and present-day devotional practice were directly observed and
   described by a journalist visiting on the occasion of the saint's annual urs.

   > "In front of them was the shrine of the 16th century Sufi, Daud Bandagi Kirmani – a modest
   > building, with a small structure and a white dome on top... Devotees circumambulated around
   > the room and then stopped next to the tombstone of the saint, where they would offer a
   > special prayer. Next to the grave of the saint, on a raised platform was the preserved
   > footmark of the saint."

   Citation: Haroon Khalid, "The naked ascetics: Pakistan's Sufi dervishes, Digambar Jain monks
   and Udasi sadhus," *Dawn*, published 22 December 2017, https://www.dawn.com/news/1378207 —
   accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established press — citable. This article also
   states the shrine is "well maintained by the descendants of the saint," whose *havelis*
   surround it, and that the visit coincided with the annual *urs*.

## Conflicts

- **An unresolved ambiguity internal to the Montgomery Gazetteer itself, not a conflict between
  sources, but flagged per the batch's own warning against near-miss identification.** Earlier
  in the same volume, in a passage about the naming legend of Pakpattan, the text reads: "The
  comparison of a spiritual teacher, who carries his disciples across the river of existence
  into paradise, with a ferry-man, has been made in respect of **Pir Baba**, another celebrated
  holy man of the district, who lived at Shergarh" (p. [unnumbered, preceding p. 34], followed
  by a Punjabi couplet). The gazetteer does not state whether "Pir Baba" here is an epithet for
  Daud Bandagi Kirmani himself or a genuinely separate holy man also associated with Shergarh —
  no other saint's name is otherwise linked to Shergarh in the volume, but the text does not
  equate the two names either. Reported here unresolved, exactly because bare toponym/epithet
  matching at Shergarh is the documented hazard for this batch.
- No conflict found between the two verified sources above and the current entry's core claims
  (name, order, general location, active pilgrimage/urs).

## Verdict

STRONG — two independent, fetched, citable sources: a published colonial district gazetteer
(Fagan, 1900) and established press (Dawn/Haroon Khalid, 2017), corroborating the shrine's
location, the saint's identity, and long-standing devotional practice at Shergarh. Neither
source, however, supplies a construction/completion date, so the sheet's blank `year_built`
remains unverified rather than resolved.
