## Verified findings

1. Birth year and birthplace. An independent academic source gives a specific birthplace that matches neither place-name the current entry offers.

   > "Syed 'Alī Tirmidhī Aliās Pīr Bābā, was born in 1502, in the town of Qunduz (a city in the present day Afghanistan). He originally hailed from the town of Tarmiz situated in Uzbekistan."

   Citation: Shahbaz Khan and Himayatullah Yaqubi, "Syed 'Ali Tirmidhi and Akhund Darwezah: Mughal Agents or Popular Saints," *Al-Idah*, Vol. 35, No. 2 (2017), pp. 29–41, Shaykh Zayed Islamic Centre, University of Peshawar. Title/authors/abstract confirmed directly at https://al-idah.pk/index.php/al-idah/article/view/55 (the journal's own record); page-specific quotations were obtained from a full-text mirror of the same article at https://pdfcoffee.com/syed-al-tirmidh-and-akhnd-darwezah-mughal-agents-or-popular-saints-pdf-free.html, because the journal's own PDF stream and the original hosting link (iri.aiou.edu.pk, via Wikipedia's citation) both returned unreadable/404 results on fetch. Accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: academic paper — citable.

2. Age at death, consistent with the commonly cited 1583.

   > "he died at the age of eighty one years"

   Citation: same as #1, p. 35 (1502 + 81 = 1583). The source states an age, not a death year, so this corroborates rather than independently establishes the current entry's figure_died value.

3. A Mughal family connection is discussed in detail, but a Kunar grant is not mentioned anywhere.

   Citation: same as #1, pp. 33, 37. A direct search of the fetched text confirms the word "Kunar" does not appear anywhere in this article.

4. The paper's own conclusion on the "Mughal agent" question, which supports the current entry's framing of Pir Baba's opposition to Bayazid Ansari as doctrinal.

   Citation: same as #1, p. 39. Reliability: academic paper — citable.

5. A dated, named urs event, with a living successor not named in the current entry.

   > "The two-day Urs celebrations of saint Hazrat Pir Baba concluded here on Sunday" ... "Makhdoom Syed Hussain Shah, the successor to the saint, along with family members, local elders and large numbers of devotees, including women, participated"

   Citation: "Urs celebrations of Pir Baba conclude," *Dawn*, 9 April 2018, https://www.dawn.com/news/1400458, accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established press — citable.

## Conflicts

- **Birthplace.** The current entry gives Tirmiz (or, "some accounts," Fergana). The one academic source fetched in this pass gives neither: it states Syed Ali Tirmizi was *born* in Qunduz, in present-day Afghanistan, and that his family only "hailed from" (i.e. originated in, as an epithet) Tirmiz in Uzbekistan. "Fergana" appears in nothing fetched in this pass. Three place-names now stand across two accounts, unresolved.
- **Urs length.** The current entry states the urs runs "24 to 26 of the Islamic month of Rajab" — three days. Dawn's own 2018 report of the actual event describes it as "the two-day Urs." Dawn's article gives no Islamic-calendar dates at all, so the "24–26 Rajab" figure is not corroborated by anything fetched in this pass (it recurs across several non-fetched, non-citable pages only — see Unverified leads).
- **Kunar land grant from Humayun.** Not found. The most detailed account of Syed Ali Tirmizi's Mughal family ties located in this pass (Khan & Yaqubi 2017) discusses his father's Mughal rank and his own refusal to travel to Delhi, but never mentions Kunar or a land grant, despite being exactly the kind of source that would. This does not disprove the claim, but it is unverified here.
- **Mosque construction "c. 1938–1965."** Nothing found, for or against.
- **December 2008 attack / 2009 closure.** Search-engine synopses (not independently fetched — see Unverified leads) describe a December 2008 attack destroying "Ya Rasool Allah" and "Ya Ali" hoardings, and an April 2009 Taliban seizure of Buner that closed the shrine — consistent with the current entry's account, including its "2009... briefly closed" detail. But the two Express Tribune articles that appear to be the underlying source could not actually be fetched (403 or a CAPTCHA wall on every attempt, including a text-proxy mirror), so this remains an unverified lead, not a confirmed finding, in this pass.

## Verdict

STRONG — two independent, fetched and verified sources: an academic paper (Khan & Yaqubi 2017, *Al-Idah*) supplying new biographical detail and directly addressing the Mughal-agent question, and an established-press report (*Dawn*, 2018) independently confirming the urs and naming a living successor.
