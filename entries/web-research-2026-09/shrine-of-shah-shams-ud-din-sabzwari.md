# Shrine of Shah Shams-ud-Din Sabzwari
shrine-of-shah-shams-ud-din-sabzwari / researched 5 September 2026 / batch A · Multan

## Verified findings

1. Biography, missionary range, and shrine construction (official government account).

   > "Hazrat Shah Shams-ud-Din Sabzwari Multani (RA) ... was a Muslim Sufi missionary." "He was
   > born at 'Sabzwar' in Iran where he spent his childhood and adolescence in pursuit of
   > education." "He arrived in Multan in early 1200 AD... his activities ranged from
   > Badakshan, through Kashmir, and from Punjab, Sindh to Gujrat with Multan as his
   > headquarters." "His shrine was built by his grandson in 1330 AD." "The tomb is square, 30
   > feet (9.1 m) in height surmounted by a hemispherical dome," decorated with "ornamental
   > glazed tiles."

   Citation: "Hazrat Shah Shams Sabzwari (RA), Multan," Auqaf & Religious Affairs Department,
   Government of Punjab, https://auqaf.punjab.gov.pk/shrine_shah_shams_sabzwari — accessed
   5 September 2026. Reliability: official government body — citable.

2. A 2024 peer-reviewed article devoted specifically to resolving this saint's identity, and its
   own dating of his burial.

   > "Shah Shamas Sabzwari, Shah Shamas Multani, Shah Shamas Tabrizi, Shah Shamas Maghribi, Shah
   > Shamas Prinda, Shah Qasim Anwar, Shah Shamas Iraqi etc. these contradictions are resolved
   > in this article." The abstract states he "was buried in Multan in 675AH."

   Citation: Sajid Maqsood and Turab Ul Hassan Sargana, "Shah Shamas Tabrizi Sabzwari, Saint Of
   Multan," *Migration Letters*, vol. 21, no. S14 (2024), pp. 1184–1200,
   https://migrationletters.com/index.php/ml/article/view/11908 — accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability: peer-reviewed academic journal article — citable, though the full text (a PDF)
   could not be parsed this session, so only the abstract is verified; the body of the article,
   which claims to resolve the name-conflation problem, is unread.

## Conflicts

**Major, unresolved conflict on the death date — the most important finding of this pass.**
Three figures are in circulation for when this saint died, and they do not agree even on the
century:

- **The current archive entry**: `figure_died: 1276` (no `year_built` note beyond "Rebuilt
  1779").
- **The 2024 academic article** (finding #2): buried in 675 AH — the search tool's own
  conversion renders this "approximately 1276 CE," which would agree with the current entry.
- **The official Auqaf Punjab government page** (finding #1), quoted directly: "died 757
  AH/1356 AD" — eighty years later, and on the institution that actually administers the shrine.

I could not adjudicate this. A further, unverified lead (see below) suggests the discrepancy may
not be a simple error but a conflation of two distinct saints venerated at related sites in
Multan — one "Shamsuddin Sabzwari," an Ismaili-associated missionary said to have died in 1276,
and a separate "Pir Shams" said to have died in 1356 "based on the plaque at the mausoleum" —
but I was not able to fetch a source making this claim directly (see Unverified leads), so it is
reported as a lead, not a finding. Either way: the current entry's `figure_died: 1276` is
**not** uncontested, and the institution that runs the shrine publishes a different figure.

Secondary, minor point: neither verified source states or denies an Ismaili affiliation for this
saint (the current entry's Description already frames this as "tradition," which neither source
contradicts, but neither confirms it either — I could not fetch a source that verifies this
independently; see Unverified leads for Daftary).
