# Shah Yousuf
shah-yousuf / researched 5 September 2026 / batch H · Punjab villages, Nankana Sahib, Sialkot

## Verified findings

1. A colonial-era district gazetteer directly documents a tomb at a place called "Shah Yusaf,"
   7 miles south of Shahpur town — matching the sheet's location field exactly — and gives a
   specific foundation date that **corroborates**, rather than leaves unverified, the sheet's
   currently-flagged "15th c. date provisional; uncorroborated" note. 900 AH converts to
   approximately 1494–95 CE, a late-15th-century date.

   > "At Shah Yusaf, 7 miles south of Shahpur, there is a small but elegant tomb, ornamented
   > outside by coloured tiles, which was erected in 900 A. H. to the memory of a holy man of
   > that name, whose descendants still reside at the spot and subsist on the offerings of
   > worshippers."

   Citation: *Punjab District Gazetteers, Volume XXX A, Shahpur District, 1917* (compiled and
   published under the authority of the Punjab Government; Lahore: Printed by the
   Superintendent, Government Printing, Punjab, 1918), Chap. I-B (Architectural remains).
   https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.105632/2015.105632.Punjab-District-Gazetteers-Shahpur-District-Vol30_djvu.txt
   — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: published colonial district gazetteer — citable.

2. The same volume separately lists the site among the district's notable shrines, corroborating
   both the name and the distance from Shahpur internally within the same source.

   > "Other shrines with a considerable reputation are the Nawab Sahib, Inayat-Vilayet, and
   > Badshah Sahib at Khushab; Shah Yusaf about 7 miles south of Shahpur; Hafiz Rahmat Ullah,
   > north-east of the Uchhali lake..."

   Citation: same as #1, Chap. I-C (Population — Fairs and pilgrimages).

   Note: both quotes are from the *same* published volume, so they count as one independent
   source, not two, under this pass's evidentiary bar — see Verdict.

3. An earlier edition of the same district's gazetteer was checked and does **not** mention
   Shah Yusaf at all, despite having its own "tombs and shrines" content (e.g. it documents a
   different holy man, "Pir Kava-nath," at Bhera). This is recorded as a negative check, not a
   contradiction — it may reflect the 1883-84 survey's narrower scope rather than the shrine's
   absence at that date.

   Citation: *Gazetteer of the Shahpur District, 1883-84* (Punjab Government; Lahore, 1895),
   https://archive.org/details/in.gov.ignca.30710 — accessed 5 September 2026.

## Conflicts

- **Direct conflict with the current entry's account of the neighbouring town of Shahpur's own
  founding figure.** The current entry states Shahpur "take[s] its own name from a different,
  later Sufi figure — Syed Shams Shah Shirazi, said to have arrived around 1606 and to have
  converted the formerly Hindu-majority settlement then known as Rampur." The 1917/1918 Shahpur
  District Gazetteer instead gives:

  > "Shahpur with the adjoining villages, Nathuwala, Jalalpur and Kotla-Sayadau, was founded by
  > the Sayads who still form the proprietary body. The common ancestor was Shah Shamas whose
  > tomb may still be seen near Shahpur... He is now worshipped as a saint and a large fair is
  > annually held in his honour between 23rd and 25th Chet."

  This account names the founding ancestor only as "Shah Shamas" (no "Shirazi" surname), gives
  no date, and describes no Rampur/Hindu-majority-conversion narrative — it is silent on all
  three specifics the current entry asserts. The gazetteer also separately notes (Chap. I-B, on
  minor sects) an unrelated "Shamsis" community "who number 205, [and] are believers in Shah
  Shams Tabrez, of Multan" — a different, more famous Sufi figure whose similar name is a
  documented conflation risk but is explicitly not the same person as Shahpur's founding
  ancestor in this text. This conflict is reported, not resolved: it is possible the "Shirazi"/
  1606/Rampur account rests on a different, more detailed local-history source not consulted
  here, but the gazetteer's own founding narrative for the town does not support it as given.
- No conflict was found regarding the Shah Yusuf shrine itself between the gazetteer and the
  current entry — the gazetteer's "erected in 900 A.H." finding actively **corroborates** the
  entry's own flagged-as-uncorroborated 15th-century date rather than contradicting it.

## Verdict

PARTIAL — one independent, fetched, citable source (the 1917/1918 Shahpur District Gazetteer)
gives a specific, on-point account of the Shah Yusaf tomb matching the sheet's location exactly
and supplying a 900 AH (c. 1494–95 CE) foundation date that corroborates rather than leaves
unresolved the sheet's own flagged-uncorroborated 15th-century note. This is a single source,
so it does not meet the ≥2-independent-source bar for STRONG, but it is unusually precise for
this batch's village-shrine targets and should be treated as a strong lead for a future patch.
A separate, real conflict was found concerning the neighbouring town of Shahpur's own founding
legend (see Conflicts) — not about this shrine directly, but about a claim in the same entry.
