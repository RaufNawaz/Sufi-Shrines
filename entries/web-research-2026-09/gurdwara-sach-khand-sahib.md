# Gurdwara Sach Khand Sahib
gurdwara-sach-khand-sahib / researched 5 September 2026 / batch D · Sindh Hindu-Nanakpanthi-Sikh and Balochistan

## Verified findings

1. **Official register confirms name, location, and plot size.** Matches the current entry's
   "just over 3,000 square feet" claim closely.

   > "Gurdwara Sach Khand Sahib | Shikarpur | 3078 sqft"

   Citation: Evacuee Trust Property Board (Pakistan), "List of Gurdwaras" [Functional register],
   https://etpb.gov.pk/shrines/gurdwaras/functional/, accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability:
   official body — citable.

2. **Vikash Singh Khalsa's role and the March 2017 Kandhkot meeting, independently confirmed.**

   > "Gurdwara Sachkhand Hazoor Sahib Shikarpur Sindh president Vikash Singh Khalsa (right)
   > presenting a sirapao to PSC patron chief Ramesh Singh"

   The same article reports the meeting "held at Gurdwara Guru Tegh Bahadur Sahib Sikh Sabha
   Kandhkot in Sindh," coordinated by the Pakistan Sikh Council, at which "a decision was made to
   form the committee for the 'support, protection and unity' of the gurdwaras" of Sindh and
   Balochistan.

   Citation: Asia Samachar, "Sindh, Balochistan gurdwaras come together," 31 March 2017,
   https://asiasamachar.com/2017/03/31/sindh-balochistan-gurdwaras-come-together/, accessed
   5 September 2026. Reliability: online Sikh-diaspora news portal (Malaysia-based); carries
   bylined/dated reporting but is **not** on this project's core established-press list
   (Dawn/Tribune/Friday Times/etc.) — flagged explicitly for the reviewer's own judgment rather
   than presented as equivalent to those outlets.

3. **The "recent construction" claim is independently echoed** by a further source, again below
   this project's strict bar but worth recording because it corroborates a specific, checkable
   fact.

   > "The Gurdwara Sahib at Shikarpur like other Gurdwaras in North Sindh came into existence
   > only few years ago."

   Citation: Inderjeet Singh, "Guru Nanak in Shikarpur, Sindh," SikhNet, 21 June 2017,
   https://www.sikhnet.com/news/guru-nanak-shikarpur-sindh, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability: community/devotional news site, no institutional editorial standing — below this
   project's bar; the author appears to be an independent writer/researcher on Sikh history in
   Pakistan (the same byline appears on sikhmissionarysociety.org), which is why the fact is
   recorded here rather than discarded outright, with the caveat stated plainly.

**Checked and ruled out** (contain no mention of "Sach Khand"/"Sachkhand" in Shikarpur, despite
being exactly the kind of source that should cover it):
- Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Gurus in Shikarpur," *The Friday Times*, 12 January 2018 (fetched
  directly) — covers several *other* named historic Sikh/Sevapanthi sites in Shikarpur (the
  Khatwari Darbar founded by Bhai Gurdas; the Singhan Wari Darbar in Nandi Bazaar founded by
  Diyal Singh) but never names Sach Khand Sahib.
- Iqbal Qaiser, *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan* (1998), full text (fetched directly) — no
  mention of "Sach Khand"/"Sachkhand" anywhere, and no Shikarpur gurdwara named at all.
- Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan, *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan* (1962), full text (fetched
  directly) — names only two gurdwaras for the whole of Sukkur District, both at Rohri, none at
  Shikarpur — consistent with the present Shikarpur gurdwara being a later (indeed, per finding
  3, a recent) foundation.
- Naseem Mughal, *Shikarpur Heritage* (Sindh Archives, 2012), full text (fetched directly) — no
  mention of "gurdwara," "Sikh," "Nanakpanthi," or "Udasi" anywhere. It does independently confirm
  the place-name Kalhoro uses: "Homeopathic Hospital was set up by Dr. Manhorlal in the early
  40s near Khatwari Dharmsala" — confirming "Khatwari" as a real Shikarpur locality, though this
  book gives no religious information about it.

## Conflicts

1. **The current entry's own bibliography rests partly on sources below this project's
   reliability bar.** "SikhNet" and "DiscoverSikhism," both cited in the current entry's
   Bibliography, are community/devotional sites with no named author or institutional
   affiliation (confirmed by direct fetch of the DiscoverSikhism page) — neither meets the
   published-book/academic-paper/established-press/official-body bar this pass applies. This
   does not mean their facts are false, but it means the entry's `support_level: Source-documented`
   currently rests in part on sources this batch would not independently certify.

2. **The "Possible Second Site" (Puj Udasian Samadha Ashram) claim is entirely uncitable and
   unconfirmed by every other source checked here.** DiscoverSikhism's page on "Gurdwara Sri Guru
   Nanak Sahib, Shikarpur" (fetched directly) describes a distinct-sounding site — "A wooden
   baradari is built on these samadhs and has been painted red," beside "non-Sikh samadhs of
   udasis" and a banyan tree — and explicitly does **not** equate it with Sach Khand Sahib or
   Sachkhand Hazoor Sahib. Its own location field reads "(precise location unknown)." None of
   the citable sources checked in this pass (ETPB, Asia Samachar, Kalhoro, Qaiser, Waliullah
   Khan, Shikarpur Heritage) mentions this second site, under this or any name, at all — so its
   very existence, and its relationship to Sach Khand Sahib, rests solely on a source that falls
   outside this project's reliability bar. The current entry is right to leave the question
   open; it is worth knowing that no better source was found to close it.
