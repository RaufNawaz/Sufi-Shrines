# Gurdas Ram Mandir
gurdas-ram-mandir / researched 5 September 2026 / batch D · Sindh Hindu-Nanakpanthi-Sikh and Balochistan

## Verified findings

1. **Official register confirms location and functional status.** The Evacuee Trust Property
   Board's own published register of functional mandirs lists this site by the exact name and
   location the current entry uses.

   > "Gurdas Ram Mandir Radhan Town Tehsil Mehar" — location "Mehar, Sindh"

   Citation: Evacuee Trust Property Board (Pakistan), "List of Mandirs" [Functional register],
   https://etpb.gov.pk/shrines/mandirs/functional/, accessed 5 September 2026 — entry 13 of the
   list. Reliability: official body (statutory board of the Government of Pakistan) — citable.

2. **The Shikarpur "Bhai Gurdas" the current entry distinguishes this figure from is real,
   documented, and unconnected to Radhan.** Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro's piece on Shikarpur's Sikh
   heritage confirms Bhai Gurdas as founder of the Khatwari Darbar there, a distinct,
   well-documented figure — supporting (though not itself mentioning Radhan) the current entry's
   claim that the Radhan Gurdas Ram is an unrelated local namesake.

   > "In one of the narrow lanes of Shikarpur is located the famous Khatwari Darbar, which was
   > founded by Bhai Gurdas, the founder of Sevapanth, who was a disciple of Guru Gobind Singh."

   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Gurus in Shikarpur," *The Friday Times*, 12 January 2018,
   https://www.thefridaytimes.com/12-Jan-2018/gurus-in-shikarpur, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability: established press (The Friday Times) — citable. Note: this article never
   mentions Radhan, Mehar, or a "Gurdas Ram" by that exact name — it independently establishes
   only that Shikarpur's Bhai Gurdas is a separate, documented figure, as the current entry
   itself already assumes.

Nothing about the mandir's founding date, architecture, or the identity/dates of its namesake
Gurdas Ram was found in any source checked (Kalhoro's other Friday Times/Youlin pieces on
Sindh's Nanakpanthi saints — "Nanakpanthi Saints of Sindh," "Historic Temples and Darbars of
Chak," "The Sikhs of Sindh" (sikhchic.com, 26 May 2013) — all fetched directly and searched;
none names Gurdas Ram, Radhan, or Mehar. Iqbal Qaiser's *Historical Sikh Shrines in Pakistan*
and Khan Mohammad Waliullah Khan's *Sikh Shrines in West Pakistan* both cover gurdwaras, not
Hindu mandirs, and neither was expected to include this site — checked anyway, no match).

## Conflicts

1. **A near-miss, not a contradiction — two different mandirs share almost the same name.**
   The current entry notes, without resolving it, that "a separate Wikipedia listing of Hindu
   temples in Pakistan describes a 'Bhai Gurdas Ram Mandir' in Dadu District as non-functional, a
   discrepancy that may reflect the same site under changed circumstances." Chasing Wikipedia's
   own citation for that non-functional listing (not citing Wikipedia itself, per this project's
   rules) leads to a second ETPB register — the **non-functional** one — which was fetched
   directly:

   > "Bhai Gurdas Ram Mandir" — district "Dadu," address "Near Shahi Bazar, Shahi Bazar K.N Shah"

   Citation: Evacuee Trust Property Board (Pakistan), "Non Functional" [Non-functional mandirs
   register], https://etpb.gov.pk/shrines/mandirs/non-functional/, accessed 5 September 2026 —
   row 38. Reliability: official body — citable.

   This is a **different town** from Radhan Town in Mehar Taluka, where ETPB's *functional*
   register places the mandir that is this file's subject (finding 1, above). ETPB's own two
   registers therefore most likely describe **two separate mandirs with confusingly similar
   names** in different parts of Dadu District, not one mandir under disputed status. This
   should probably replace the current entry's "noted here without resolution" framing — though
   it is reported here as a conflict, per this project's rule that conflicts are surfaced, not
   silently resolved by the researcher.
