# Sial Sharif
sial-sharif / researched 5 September 2026 / batch B · Uch Sharif and west-Punjab khanqahs

## Verified findings

1. An official district-government page confirms the shrine's order affiliation and its most
   important disciple line.

   > "Sufi shrine of Khwajah Muhammad Shamsuddin Sialvi, popularly known as Pir Sial, who was a
   > waliullah (saint) belonging to the Chishti order."

   The same page calls the khanqah "among the foremost Chishti center[s] of the Punjab," with
   disciples including "20th-century saint Pir Meher Ali Shah of Golra Sharif."

   Citation: Government of Punjab, District Sargodha, "Important Places,"
   https://sargodha.punjab.gov.pk/important_places — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability:
   official body (district government) — citable.

2. A peer-reviewed academic article treats the Sialvi khanqah's later trajectory as a case study
   in growing sectarian exclusion within the Chishti revival, and names Sulaiman Taunsvi as a
   founding figure of that revival.

   > "The khanaqah of Sial Sharif in Sargodha district illustrates this exclusionary trend"

   and, from the same article's footnotes:

   > "With Sulaman Taunsawi, Khawja Muhammad 'Aqil of Kot Mithan and Hafiz Muhammad Jamal of
   > Multan, Chishti pirs came to epitomise the Islamic revivalist streak..."

   Citation: Tahir Kamran and Amir Khan Shahid, "Shari'a, Shi'as and Chishtiya Revivalism:
   Contextualising the Growth of Sectarianism in the Tradition of the Sialvi Saints of the
   Punjab," Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society, Vol. 24, Issue 3 (July 2014), pp. 477-492.
   https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-royal-asiatic-society/article/abs/sharia-shias-and-chishtiya-revivalism-contextualising-the-growth-of-sectarianism-in-the-tradition-of-the-sialvi-saints-of-the-punjab/F15229CFE8D951389D5535C6C9DD4371
   — accessed 5 September 2026 (abstract and footnotes fetched directly; the full article is
   paywalled and was not reached). Reliability: peer-reviewed academic journal — citable.

3. Established press independently confirms the Sialvi-Taunsvi discipleship named in the current
   entry.

   > "Khwaja Shamsuddin, alias Pir Sial, was himself a deputy of Khwaja Shah Muhammad Suleiman
   > Taunsvi"

   Citation: Zulfiqar Ali Kalhoro, "Chishti Mystics in Rawalpindi's Harraka Sharif," The Friday
   Times, 20 March 2025. https://thefridaytimes.com/20-Mar-2025/chishti-mystics-in-rawalpindi-s-harraka-sharif
   — accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established press — citable.

## Conflicts

- **A sectarian complication in the Sialvi line's later history, absent from the current entry.**
  The Kamran & Shahid article situates Sial Sharif's khanqah within a documented shift toward
  "exclusionary" sectarianism in the 19th-20th century Chishti revival, associated (per its
  abstract and the visible footnote material) with a later Sialvi pir, Khwaja Qamar-ud-Din
  Sialvi, and a text attributed to him, Mazhab-i Shi'a, reflecting anti-Shi'a polemic. The
  current entry describes the line's successors only in celebratory terms ("kept by his
  successors, who have continued as its sajjada-nashins and as prominent religious figures")
  with no mention of this. Reported as an omission the current entry might want to weigh, not a
  contradiction of anything it says about Shamsuddin Sialvi's own life (1799-1883) — the
  sectarian turn described concerns later successors.
- No conflict found on the core facts given in the current entry (dates 1799-1883, Chishti
  order, discipleship under the Taunsvi line, training of Meher Ali Shah of Golra) — all
  corroborated rather than contradicted by the sources above.
