# Shah Noorani Shrine (Syed Bilawal Shah Noorani)
shah-noorani-shrine-syed-bilawal-shah-noorani / researched 5 September 2026 / batch D · Sindh Hindu-Nanakpanthi-Sikh and Balochistan

## Verified findings

1. **Location, legend, and pilgrimage practice.**

   > "Lahoot Lamakan, a spiritual destination next to the shrine of Hazrat Bilawal Shah Noorani
   > in the Wadh area of Khuzdar district."

   > "Each year in the month of Sha'aban, thousands of devotees make their way on foot across
   > perilous terrain and seven mountains from Lal Baag in Sehwan to the Shah Bilawal Noorani
   > shrine."

   The same piece describes devotees as "known as Lahootis" who "rest at 14 destinations and
   spend a night at each of them," and notes a related site en route where, by legend, "Hazrat
   Shah Abdul Latif... had stayed here on his way to Lahoot Lamakan. He had dug a well and
   constructed a mosque — which stands to this day."

   Citation: Qurban Ali Khushik, "Khuzdar's Shah Bilawal Noorani shrine: A journey of devotion,"
   *Dawn*, 13 November 2016, https://www.dawn.com/news/1296027, accessed 5 September 2026.
   Reliability: established press (Dawn) — citable.

2. **The November 2016 bombing.**

   > "At least 52 people were killed and 102 were injured on Saturday evening in a bombing at
   > the shrine of Shah Norani in Khuzdar district of Balochistan."

   The article also states the blast struck "the spot where the *dhamaal* (Sufi ritual) was
   being performed, within the premises of the shrine," with roughly 500 devotees gathered, and
   that the shrine "is frequented by a large number of devotees on Friday, and is visited by
   people from across the country. Iranian nationals also frequent the shrine," being
   "approximately 150 kilometres from Karachi."

   Citation: "Tragedy unfolds as terror strikes Shah Noorani shrine," *Dawn*, 13 November 2016,
   https://www.dawn.com/news/1295998, accessed 5 September 2026. Reliability: established press
   (Dawn) — citable.

## Conflicts

1. **Casualty count varies sharply by outlet, and only one figure here is independently
   verified.** Dawn (fetched directly, finding 2) reports 52 killed / 102 injured. Search results
   only (not independently fetched — Express Tribune's domain returned HTTP 403 on every attempt
   in this session, including via a read-through proxy) surfaced a differently headlined Express
   Tribune report, "At least 45 dead, over 100 injured in Khuzdar's Shah Noorani shrine
   explosion," and other outlets' figures of 43 (GlobalSecurity, reproducing a VOA wire) and an
   apparently early, later-revised Tribune headline of "12 dead." The current entry's own text
   avoids a specific number ("killed and injured many worshippers"), which this spread of
   figures suggests was the right call — no single count is safely citable here, and the ones
   above are reported as an unverified spread, not resolved into one.

2. **A possible fifty-year date discrepancy for the saint's arrival in the valley — flagged, not
   resolved, and not independently verified.** Wikipedia's own reference list for its "Syed
   Bilawal Shah Noorani" article (chased directly, per this project's rule against citing
   Wikipedia itself) attributes to a named historian the claim that Noorani "traveled from
   Thatta to an area of present Balochistan in or about 1449 AD, during the realm of Jam
   Nizamuddin II Samo." Separately, a WebSearch summary of Salman Rashid's "Tales from Lahoot
   valley" (*Express Tribune*, 31 August 2012) — **not independently fetched**, the article
   returned HTTP 403 on every attempt including through a proxy — describes the saint leaving
   Thatta "around 1494" under "Jam Nizamuddin Samma," having "probably [been] called 'The
   Lunatic.'" 1449 and 1494 are a transposed pair, which is exactly the kind of thing that
   deserves suspicion rather than either being quietly preferred. This bears directly on the
   current entry's own `year_built: 1450 (century)` / `year_built_note: "15th c.; saint active
   c. 1449"` — which this pass could not independently confirm or overturn, since the one source
   that might resolve it could not be fetched.

## Verdict

STRONG — two independently fetched, established-press (Dawn) sources verify the shrine's
location, its distinctive Sha'aban pilgrimage practice, and the 2016 bombing. But the current
entry's own founding-date claim rests on ground this pass could not independently confirm: the
one directly relevant source (Rashid's Express Tribune piece) was unreachable, and what could be
seen of it in search results suggests a real, unresolved fifty-year discrepancy with the date
Wikipedia's citation trail otherwise supports.
