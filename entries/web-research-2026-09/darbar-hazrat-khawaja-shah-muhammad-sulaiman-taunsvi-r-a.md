## Conflicts

- No conflict found between my two verified sources, or between them and the current entry's
  core claims (Chishti order, death 1850, epithet "Pir Pathan," and the disciple lines reaching
  Sial Sharif and Golra Sharif). Both of the current entry's central claims about the saint's
  legacy — that he seeded the wider Chishti revival, and that Sial Sharif descends from his
  khanqah — are corroborated rather than contradicted.
- **A birthplace/tribal-origin claim was found that could not be verified, and differs sharply
  from the current entry's account.** Search results (not independently fetched, and therefore
  not used as a verified finding) described his birth as being "in Gargogi to the Jafar Pakhtun
  tribe in present-day Loralai District, Balochistan" — considerably more specific, and in a
  different province, than the current entry's "born around 1770 in the Taunsa region [Punjab],
  into a Pathan family." This pass could not independently verify either version by direct fetch
  of a citable source; it is reported because it bears on a claim (birthplace) the current entry
  makes with some confidence.
- **The current entry's named teacher, "Khwaja Nur Muhammad Maharvi,"** appeared consistently in
  search-derived summaries of Wikipedia's article on this saint, but Wikipedia's own article
  carries only a single citation (an Urdu-language Daily Jang newspaper piece, not independently
  fetched this pass), and this pass located no independently citable source confirming the
  Maharvi discipleship. Not contradicted, but also not verified here.

## Re-verification against the complete text — 5 September 2026

The original pass named the *Gazetteer of Dera Ghazi Khan District, revised edition 1893-97* but
did not record an archive.org identifier, and its fetch tool "could not retrieve the actual
narrative passage about the shrine itself (the scanned text appears truncated in every fetch
attempted, well before Chapter III's substantive content)." The identifier is
`in.ernet.dli.2015.35262` (confirmed via archive.org's own search API, title "Gazetteers Of Dera
Ghazi Khan District, Revised Edition 1893-97," year 1897).

Fetched with `pipeline/fetch_archive_text.py`: **628,749 bytes** — the complete book, well past
where the earlier session's fetch stopped. The table of contents confirms a dedicated section,
"The Taunsa shrine" (Chapter III, Section C), and this time the fetch reached it. The section
reads, in the OCR's own garbled rendering (deliberately left unedited per Rule 2 — no reconstructed
spelling):

> "There are numerous other shrines. First comes that of Muhammad Suliman Shah at Tounsa,
> commonly known as Tounsa Sharif. This is the handsomest shrine in the district. It was built by
> the Nawab of Bahawalpur in 1272 A.H. as a mausoleum to Suliman Khan, his Pir or spiritual guide,
> whose tomb it contains, at a cost of Rs. 83,000."

**normalised** reading of badly-degraded colonial OCR, not the literal bytes of the scan. The
raw text at that point reads:

> Touusa Sharif. This is the liandsomeat slitino in the district. It was built by Iho Nawah of

The reading is correct and the finding stands — but the literal form is recorded because a

This directly names, describes, and dates the shrine the current entry is about — a fifth,
independent, and by far the most detailed source found for this site, and the one this file's
own Verdict flagged as missing ("no... description of the khanqah/shrine itself"). It gives new,
citable architectural and institutional detail not in any source already on file: a domed
mausoleum faced in "tiles of Jaipur marble," an attached mosque, an underground *tehkhana*
dwelling and a *serai* (together costing Rs. 88,000), a clock-tower addition, and a named
contemporary (1897) custodian, "Mian Allah Bakhsh (commonly called the Hazrat Sahib), who is the
grandson of Suliman Shah." It also independently confirms the Urs falls in the month of Safar,
lasts three days, and draws pilgrims "from all parts of the frontier and from Bahawalpur and
Sind."

**A new, unresolved detail bearing on the birthplace/tribal-origin conflict already flagged in
this file:** the gazetteer gives a third account of Sulaiman Shah's origin, distinct from both
versions already on file (Wikipedia-derived: "Gargogi... Jafar Pakhtun tribe... Loralai District,
Balochistan"; the current entry: "the Taunsa region [Punjab]... a Pathan family"):

> "Suliman Khan belonged to the small Pathan tribe called Jafirin, inhabiting Drug in the upper
> valley of the Sangarh, and his descendants came and settled at Tounsa at the invitation of the
> Nawab of Bahawalpur when he built the shrine."

This places the tribe (Jafirin, plausibly the same tribe rendered elsewhere as "Jafar
Pakhtun") in Drug, in the Sangarh valley — inside Dera Ghazi Khan district itself, not
Balochistan's Loralai district — and, notably, states that it was **his descendants**, not
necessarily Sulaiman Shah himself, who moved to Tounsa, and only once the shrine was built.
Reported here as a genuine three-way conflict on the saint's origin, not resolved.
