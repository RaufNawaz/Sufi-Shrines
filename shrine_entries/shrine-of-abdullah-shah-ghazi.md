## Overview

On a low hill in Clifton, overlooking the Arabian Sea in the heart of Karachi, stands the shrine of Hazrat Abdullah Shah Ghazi — the patron saint of the city, whose green-domed darbar is among the most visited and most beloved sacred places in Pakistan’s largest metropolis. Tradition places him in the eighth century and holds him to be a descendant of the Prophet through the line of Imam Hasan; for the people of Karachi he is the guardian of the city, believed to shield it from storm and calamity, and his shrine is a ceaseless resort of pilgrims of every kind.

## The Life of the Saint

The historical record of Abdullah Shah Ghazi is thin and wrapped in legend, but the tradition is strong and old. He is remembered as a Sayyid of the family of the Prophet — by the usual account a descendant of Imam Hasan — who came to Sindh in the early Islamic centuries (the eighth century, in the age when Islam was first spreading in the region through Muhammad bin Qasim and after), as a merchant, warrior (_ghazi_), and preacher. He is said to have settled by the sea near what became Karachi, and to have died there, some traditions holding that he was martyred. His grave on the Clifton hill became a place of blessing, and over the centuries grew into the great shrine that now bears his name.

## The Patron Saint of Karachi

Abdullah Shah Ghazi is woven into the very identity of Karachi. It is widely believed that his presence protects the coastal city from cyclones and disaster, and that the sea has never brought ruin upon Karachi because the saint keeps watch over it from his hill. His shrine draws an immense and continuous cross-section of the city — rich and poor, of every sect and background — who come to recite the Fatiha, tie vows, distribute food, and share in the qawwali and the _dhamal_ that fill its evenings, above all on Thursdays and at the annual urs. Like other great shrines, it has been a target of violence — a suicide bombing struck it in 2010 — but the devotion of Karachi has never faltered, and the shrine, since rebuilt and expanded with its landmark green dome, remains thronged.

## Legacy

Abdullah Shah Ghazi is the spiritual guardian of Karachi — a saint of the earliest Islamic centuries whose shrine on the Clifton hill has become the sacred heart of a modern megacity of many millions. In him a coastal metropolis of migrants and strangers finds a common patron and a place of refuge, and his darbar remains one of the most vibrant centres of popular devotion in Pakistan.
