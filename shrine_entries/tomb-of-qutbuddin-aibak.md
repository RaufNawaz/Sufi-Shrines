## Current site description (as of 10 October 2026)

*The row's `Description` cell exactly as it appears in `src/data/shrines-fallback.json` on 10 October 2026, untouched. Not yet checked against sources.*

Tucked amid the crowded lanes of Aibak Road in the Anarkali quarter, this modest garden tomb marks the burial place of Sultan Qutb ud-Din Aibak, founder of the Mamluk, or "Slave," dynasty and the first ruler of the Delhi Sultanate. Born around 1150 in Turkestan, Aibak was sold into slavery as a child and eventually rose, through service to the Ghurid ruler Muhammad Ghori, to command Ghurid territories in northern India following the decisive Second Battle of Tarain in 1192. After Ghori's assassination in 1206, Aibak established independent rule based in Lahore, laying the foundations of Muslim dynastic government in the subcontinent; he is also remembered as the patron who commissioned Delhi's Qutb Minar. He died in Lahore in November 1210 after a fall from his horse while playing chaugan, an early form of polo, and was succeeded eventually by his son-in-law Iltutmish. The tomb's physical history has been anything but continuous: historians dispute whether a substantial mausoleum ever stood over the grave in the medieval period, and for long the site was a simple, informal grave hemmed in by the houses of the Anarkali bazaar. The structure visitors see today is essentially a 1970s reconstruction by Pakistan's Department of Archaeology and Museums, deliberately evoking early Sultanate-period style within a small walled garden. Unlike Lahore's Sufi shrines, the tomb hosts no urs, no pilgrimage tradition and no ongoing devotional practice; it is maintained purely as a protected historical monument, valued chiefly for its symbolic role marking the thirteenth-century founding moment of Muslim dynastic rule in South Asia, centred, briefly, on Lahore itself. Punjab's Directorate General of Archaeology states that the grave was "originally... only one grave without any tomb or structure," that a marble tomb was later "pulled down by Ranjit Singh," and that the department itself "built the present tomb over the grave in 1990." Earlier still, per Dawn's "Harking Back" column, Sultan Iltutmish had ordered "a simple small room, one barely able to enclose the grave" — at first a mud room, later bricked in during Akbar's reign — and Aibak himself had wanted Lahore's dead buried together, designating land south of Mozang village as a graveyard for "at least 150,000 dead," the origin of the present Miani Sahib graveyard. The same column instead dates the present mausoleum's actual construction to 1972, ordered by Prime Minister Zulfiqar Ali Bhutto after the Bangladesh war — who is quoted remarking that Aibak "was a great and kind ruler and deserved the respect of the poor" — with the design led by architect Kamil Khan Mumtaz; Daily Times likewise places the rebuilding "during the 1970s," rather than in 1990 as the Archaeology Department's own page states.

"لاہور مین شالامار باغ" is Shah Jahan's memorial: "چشم روزگار نے کوئی ایسا باغ کم دیکھا ہوگا" (p. 833).
- After Aurangzeb's accession, "دارا شکوہ نے لاہور کو مراجعت کی", raised a larger army there, then crossed the "دریای سندہ" (p. 836). Dara's betrayer, after handing him over in Delhi, "لاہور کیطرف چلا گیا" and was killed on the road (p. 838).
- At the start of Alamgir's reign Nawab Khalil Allah Khan held "دارہ[OCR?] لاہور" and, with a mansab of "شش ہزاری", was appointed "صوبہ دار لاہور" (p. 497).

- In the succession war after Aurangzeb, Muhammad Mu'izz al-Din, governor of Multan, came with a large army and stores "بمقام لاہور" to his father Sultan Mu'azzam Bahadur Shah, and both went on to Akbarabad (p. 845). "شاہ عالم شہر لاہور مین سنہ ۱۷۱۲ء [OCR?] مین فوت ہوا" (p. 846).

- **Shrine / Darbar.** **Not present.** Anarkali, where the archive places the tomb, appears only as a cantonment and bazaar (pp. 34–35, folios 37–38).
