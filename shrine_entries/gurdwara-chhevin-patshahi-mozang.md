## Overview

On Temple Road in the Mozang locality of Lahore stands Gurdwara Chhevin Patshahi, one of several Lahore-area shrines commemorating a visit by Guru Hargobind, the sixth Sikh Guru — hence "Chhevin Patshahi," the "Sixth Sovereignty." The site is bound up in Sikh tradition with the memory of Mata Kaulan (also remembered as Bibi Kaulan), a devoted follower associated with the Guru whose story is preserved in the devotional literature of the Mozang gurdwara tradition.

## History

The event commemorated here is traditionally dated to 1619. An original structure is recorded as having been raised under Maharaja Ranjit Singh in the early nineteenth century, and the building seen today was rebuilt in 1926 under the patronage of Sardar Mehar Singh. In the pre-1947 period the gurdwara sustained daily congregational worship, and it is recorded as having observed an annual Akhand Path, the unbroken reading of the Guru Granth Sahib, around Guru Hargobind's birth anniversary (Gurpurb).

## Present State

Since Partition the gurdwara has been maintained, like most of Lahore's Sikh shrines, as part of Pakistan's protected Sikh heritage rather than as a site of resident congregational worship, though it remains distinguished among the city's several Chhevin Patshahi gurdwaras — including those at Chowmala/Bhati Gate and at Jhalian — each marking a separate halt in Guru Hargobind's travels.
