## Overview

The Tomb of Bibi Jawindi at Uch Sharif is among the most beautiful — and most poignant — monuments of Islamic architecture in Pakistan: a jewel of blue-and-white glazed tilework, half-ruined by the flooding of the river, that has become the very emblem of Uch Sharif’s medieval glory. Bibi Jawindi was a saintly woman of the great Bukhari sayyid family of Uch, a descendant of Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht, and her ornate mausoleum, raised in the late fifteenth century, stands within the famous cluster of tombs in the Bukhari quarter of the town.

## Bibi Jawindi and her Tomb

Bibi Jawindi is remembered as a pious woman of the Jalali Bukhari line — by tradition a great-granddaughter of the celebrated saint Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht — venerated for her holiness. Her tomb was built around 1493, tradition holds, by an Iranian prince, Dilshad, in her honour. It is an octagonal, tiered structure crowned by a dome, its surfaces sheathed in the exquisite cobalt-blue, turquoise, and white _kashi_ tilework for which Uch and Multan are famed, and adorned with corner turrets — a masterpiece of the regional style that flourished around the tombs of the Suhrawardi saints. Over the centuries the changing course of the river undermined the structure, and a great part of it collapsed (by tradition in a flood in the nineteenth century), leaving the ravishing half-tomb that visitors see today — a fragment whose surviving beauty only sharpens the sense of what has been lost.

## The Monuments of Uch Sharif

Bibi Jawindi’s tomb does not stand alone: it is the finest of a group of contiguous mausolea in the Bukhari quarter of Uch — including those associated with Baha al-Halim (a teacher of the line) and the tomb’s own builder — that together form one of the most important surviving ensembles of pre-Mughal funerary architecture in the subcontinent. So exceptional are they that the monuments of Uch Sharif have been placed on UNESCO’s Tentative List of World Heritage sites, and they have been the subject of international conservation efforts. The tombs make Uch Sharif a destination for lovers of architecture and history as much as for pilgrims.

## Devotion and Legacy

As the resting place of a holy woman of the revered Bukhari line, Bibi Jawindi’s tomb is honoured by pilgrims who come to Uch to visit the shrines of the sayyids, and it forms part of the sacred fabric of the town. But its fame today is above all as a monument — a breathtaking, wounded masterpiece that stands for the vanished splendour of medieval Uch and for the artistry of the tile-workers who adorned the shrines of the Punjab’s saints. It is, at once, a place of devotion, a heritage treasure, and an elegy in brick and glaze.
