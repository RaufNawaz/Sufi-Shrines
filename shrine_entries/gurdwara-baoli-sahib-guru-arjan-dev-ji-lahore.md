## Current site description (as of 10 October 2026)

*The row's `Description` cell exactly as it appears in `src/data/shrines-fallback.json` on 10 October 2026, untouched. Not yet checked against sources.*

Gurdwara Baoli Sahib stands in the crowded Dabbi Bazaar at the very heart of the walled city of Lahore, where its precinct once adjoined the Loha, Kesera and Guru bazaars. It commemorates a visit of *Guru Arjan Dev*, the fifth Sikh Guru, to Lahore in 1599. In the tradition preserved by the community, a devotee named *Bhai Chhajju Bhagat*, together with a Pathan merchant, laid a purse of gold *mohars* at the Guru's feet and asked that it be spent for religious purposes; the Guru had a *baoli* — a roofed stepwell — sunk at this spot to provide water for the people of the quarter. The stepwell later suffered in the turbulence of the eighteenth century: it is said to have been filled in and built over during the governorship of Nawab Yahya Khan, at the instigation of Diwan Jaspat Rai. It was rediscovered and restored during the reign of *Maharaja Ranjit Singh*, who in 1834 had a new building raised and a *sarovar* (sacred tank) dug, and the gurdwara passed under the management of the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee in 1927. Today the site is also known as Baoli Bagh, its surroundings laid out as a garden. As a shrine tied to Guru Arjan — the compiler of the *Adi Granth* and the first martyr of the Sikh faith, whose principal Lahore memorial is the Gurdwara Dera Sahib by the Fort — Baoli Sahib is a significant station on the map of Sikh Lahore.

Independent verification substantially corroborates and enriches this account. Iqbal Qaiser's 1998 survey of Sikh shrines gives the same origin story and specifies that the dispute was resolved before the Guru in Samvat 1616, while Majid Sheikh's reporting for *Dawn* fixes the disputed purse at "282 gold mohars" and adds that Maharaja Ranjit Singh's 1834 restoration followed his own recovery from illness after his astrologers advised him to bathe in the baoli's water — corroborated independently by the Department of Archaeology's own 1962 memoir, which further records a mosque as having stood on the site between the Sikh gurus' era and Ranjit Singh's reign, a detail found in neither Qaiser's account nor this entry's own Nawab Yahya Khan narrative, and not reconciled with either here. Sheikh's reporting also adds that it was at Gurdwara Baoli Sahib, not elsewhere in Dabbi Bazaar, that the original Adi Granth manuscript (the Kartarpur *pothi*) was kept in the custody of the Sodhi family, carried in grand procession to the Lahore Fort under Ranjit Singh's patronage, and restored to the Sodhi family after the British annexation of 1849. By 2015 the baoli itself had been reduced to "a mere small opening in an open space," with only a few traditional healers still drawing water from it as the water table fell, and by 2018 the same reporter separately flagged the demolition of the gurdwara structure itself as an unaddressed grievance.
