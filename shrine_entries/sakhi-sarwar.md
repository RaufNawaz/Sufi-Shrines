## Overview

In the town of Sakhi Sarwar (Nigaha), at the foot of the Sulaiman range in the Dera Ghazi Khan district, stands the shrine of Sultan Sakhi Sarwar — a saint of the twelfth century honoured across an extraordinary breadth of communities, and known by the loving title _Lakhdata_, “the giver of hundreds of thousands,” for his boundless generosity. For centuries his cult was one of the most widespread in the western Punjab, drawing not only Muslims but Hindus and Sikhs, and his shrine at Nigaha remains a major place of pilgrimage in the south-west of the province.

## The Life and Cult of the Saint

Sayyid Ahmad Sultan, called Sakhi Sarwar (“the Generous Chief”), lived in the twelfth century; tradition places his origins in the region of Multan/Shahkot, and his life is wrapped in legend more than documented history. He is remembered as a saint of miraculous power and open-handed charity, and after his death his shrine at the place now called Sakhi Sarwar became the centre of a devotional cult of remarkable reach and endurance. His devotees — the _Sultanias_ or _Sarwarias_ — carried his banners in seasonal pilgrimages, and until modern times his following notably crossed the boundaries of religion, with Hindus and Sikhs of the Punjab honouring him alongside Muslims; the Sikh tradition itself preserves references to the _Sakhi Sarwar_ pilgrims. This shared devotion made him one of the great examples of the composite religious culture of the old Punjab.

## The Shrine and its Devotion

The shrine complex at Sakhi Sarwar (Nigaha), set in the hills, comprises the saint’s tomb together with a mosque and associated buildings, and it has drawn pilgrims for centuries. Devotees come to seek blessing, fulfil vows, and honour the _Lakhdata_, and the annual pilgrimage and urs are great occasions of music, offering, and celebration in the regional manner. Like other major shrines, it has been touched by the violence of recent years — a suicide bombing struck a procession of its pilgrims in 2011 — yet the devotion has continued. The darbar remains the defining landmark of its town.

## Legacy

Sultan Sakhi Sarwar is remembered as one of the most widely venerated saints of the old western Punjab — the open-handed _Lakhdata_ whose cult, crossing the lines of Muslim, Hindu, and Sikh, embodied the shared sacred world of the region before the divisions of modern times. His shrine at Nigaha endures as a major centre of pilgrimage and as a monument to that older, composite devotion.
