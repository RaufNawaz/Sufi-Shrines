## Overview

Gurdwara Panjvin and Chhevin Patshahi — the gurdwara "of the Fifth and Sixth Kingships" — stands in Nankana Sahib near Gurdwara Tambu Sahib, on the road leading to the central shrine of Janam Asthan. Unlike most of the town's shrines, which commemorate the boyhood of *Guru Nanak Dev Ji*, this gurdwara honours two of his successors: the fifth Guru, *Arjan Dev*, and the sixth Guru, *Hargobind*, whose visits to the birthplace of the founder are remembered here. It is thus a reminder that Nankana Sahib drew not only the devotion of later pilgrims but the presence of the living Gurus themselves.

## The Visits of the Fifth and Sixth Gurus

The gurdwara marks the tradition that both Guru Arjan Dev and Guru Hargobind came to the town of Guru Nanak's birth. Guru Hargobind — revered as the master of *miri* and *piri*, the union of temporal and spiritual authority — is remembered to have visited Nankana Sahib in the month of *Har* in Samvat 1670, corresponding to 1613 CE, while returning from a journey to Kashmir. In memory of his stay, his followers are said to have established a *Gur Mela*, a fair, at the very spot where he had rested, and the site came in time to honour the fifth Guru as well. Within the enclosure there are accordingly two shrines: that of Guru Arjan Dev, built plainly and without a dome, and that of Guru Hargobind, surmounted by a dome, the two standing together within a common boundary wall.

## The Gurdwara

The paired shrines of the fifth and sixth Gurus form one of the quieter corners of the Nankana Sahib complex, less frequented than the great shrine of Janam Asthan but valued by pilgrims who make the full round of the town's sacred sites. Their situation beside Tambu Sahib places them within the compact eastern cluster of gurdwaras, and their modest architecture — the domeless sanctum of Guru Arjan set beside the domed sanctum of Guru Hargobind — gives visible form to the succession of the Gurus. As with the other shrines of Nankana Sahib, the *Guru Granth Sahib* is installed within, and the site is cared for by the bodies charged with Sikh heritage in Pakistan.

## Legacy

Since 1947 the gurdwara, like its neighbours, has stood without a local congregation, coming to life chiefly when pilgrim *jathas* arrive for the great anniversaries. Its dedication to the fifth and sixth Gurus lends the sacred geography of Nankana Sahib a fuller shape: the town is not only the cradle of Guru Nanak but a place touched by the later Gurus who carried his message forward — Arjan Dev, the compiler of the scripture and the first martyr of the faith, and Hargobind, who first girded the sword of temporal power. In honouring their visits, Gurdwara Panjvin and Chhevin Patshahi joins the beginning of the Sikh story to its unfolding.

- **Suba Lahore described** (c. 041; book's words: "صوبه لاهور" / "این صوبه"; also "پنجاب" in "پنج دریاے پنجاب" and "اهل پنجاب"; period: the author's time, c. 1107 AH, a Mughal suba; the suba as described includes places outside Pakistan — Batala, the Jalandhar doab, the Kangra and Jammu hills, Sirhind):
  - Organised by doabs: "اماکن مشهوره پنج دوابه", then "شش دریاے این صوبه که برین پنج دوابه حایل است" (p. 513).   - "پنج دریاے پنجاب" join the Indus beyond Multan in the Baloch country; from there the whole stream is called Sind and becomes "بحر زخار[OCR?]" (p. 510). The combined Sutlej–Beas ends in the Indus, "که راوی و چناب و بهت نیز درو واصل است" (p. 513).
  - "بالجمله صوبه لاهور ولایتی است خوش آب و هوا": summers very hot, winters cold; melons and grapes like Iran's and Turan's, mangoes like Hindustan's; rice better than Bengal's and sugarcane better than the Deccan's (p. 510).
  - Irrigation: "اکثر مدار اب یاری[OCR?] چاه است"; a wheel of "سیصد و شصت چوب" and more than a hundred small pots, turned by a pair of oxen, each turn lifting "چندین صد من اب"; kharif and rabi and cheap grain depend on rain (p. 510).
  - Gold washed from the sand, especially on "دریاے بیاه و بهت"; the northern hills have mines of silver, copper, "روئین[OCR?]" and "جسد[OCR?]"; the miners profit, "و محصول و بسرکار بادشاهی دهند" (p. 510).
  - Size: "طول این صوبه از اب ستلج تا دریاے سند صد و هشتاد کروه"; "عرض از بهیره تا چوکهندی[OCR?] هشتاد و هفت کروه" (p. 510). Borders: "شرق رویه سهرند و غرب سو ملتان و شمال کشمیر و جنوب بیپاپور[OCR?]" (p. 510).
