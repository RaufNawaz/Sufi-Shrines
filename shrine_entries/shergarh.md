## Overview

At Shergarh, in the Okara district of the Punjab, stands the shrine of Hazrat Daud Bandagi Kirmani (1513–1575) — a revered Qadiri saint of the sixteenth century who did much to spread the Qadiri order in the Punjab. A contemporary of the Mughal emperors Humayun and Akbar and of the great Lahori saints of his age, Daud Bandagi made Shergarh a centre of devotion, and his darbar remains a place of pilgrimage in central Punjab.

## The Life of the Saint

Daud, honoured with the title _Bandagi_ (“servitude,” marking his devotion) and the epithet _Kirmani_ for his family’s origins, was born in 1513. He gave himself to religious learning and the Sufi path, and became a shaykh of the Qadiri order — the line of Shaikh Abd al-Qadir Jilani of Baghdad — at a time when that order was taking firm root in the subcontinent. From his base at Shergarh he taught, guided disciples, and spread the Qadiri way across the Punjab, living through the turbulent early Mughal decades. He was among the notable saints of the generation of Shah Hussain of Lahore, part of the flowering of Punjabi Sufism in the sixteenth century. He died in 1575 and was buried at Shergarh.

## The Shrine and its Devotion

The shrine of Daud Bandagi Kirmani at Shergarh is a centre of pilgrimage kept by his successors, where devotees come to recite the Fatiha and seek the saint’s blessing. It maintains the customary devotions — prayer, qawwali, and the free kitchen — and its annual urs gathers the faithful of the region. The place takes its sacred identity from the darbar of the saint who made it a seat of the Qadiri order.

## Legacy

Daud Bandagi Kirmani is remembered as one of the saints who firmly planted the Qadiri order in the sixteenth-century Punjab — a devout shaykh of the age of Akbar whose shrine at Shergarh has kept his memory for four and a half centuries. He belongs to the great generation of Punjabi Sufism, and his darbar remains a living centre of the devotion he inspired.

### Doubts the notes raise on this row

**I5. `shergarh` / Daud Bandagi Kirmani — the name is placed on a shared name only, and "Bandagi"
never appears.** A footnote's "حضرت داؤد کرمانی" is placed against the row on the shared name alone,
with no place, no date and no silsila, while the book's own contents call the Shergarh figure
"شیخ داؤد **جہنی وال** شیر گڈھی" (p. 47, folio 35, حاشیہ 4; chunk 003). The entry heading then reads
"شیخ داؤد **چونی وال** شیر گڈھی [OCR?]" (p. 48, folio 36); the entry's own account of the father moving
to **qasba Chunian** favours چونی وال, but the heading carries the flag and the contents form does
not, **so neither reading can be preferred on the transcription alone** (chunk 004). The index has
"داؤد کرمانی" without "Bandagi" (p. 317, folio 305; chunk 029) and the place index has
"**شیر گڈھ |**" with **no page number at all** (p. 335, folio 323; chunk 031). Note also that
`darbar-abul-muali-qadri` carries a "Syed Dawood Bandgi Kirmani" affiliation, and that the book's
"شیخ **طاہر** بندگی" is one letter from "داؤد بندگی" in transcription (p. 274, folio 262; chunk 026).
