## Overview

In Karachi lies the shrine of Hazrat Qalandar Baba Auliya — Sayyid Muhammad Azeem Barkhiya (1898–1979) — a modern Sufi master and the founder of the Azeemia Sufi order. Unusual among the saints for the intellectual character of his teaching, Qalandar Baba Auliya sought to present the inner science of Sufism in a form intelligible to the modern mind, and through his order and his writings his influence has spread widely in Pakistan and abroad. His darbar is a contemporary shrine and the centre of a living Sufi movement.

## The Life of the Saint

Muhammad Azeem was born in 1898 at Khurja, in the United Provinces of British India, into a Sayyid family, and is known by the spiritual title _Qalandar Baba Auliya_. After the creation of Pakistan he settled in Karachi. He is remembered as a saint of deep contemplative attainment who devoted himself to expounding the inner realities of the Sufi path — the science of the soul, of thought, and of the unseen — and to guiding disciples. His best-known work, _Loh-o-Qalam_ (“The Tablet and the Pen”), sets out his teaching on metaphysics and the workings of the mind and spirit, and is studied as a foundational text by his followers. He died in 1979 and was buried in Karachi, where his shrine arose.

## The Azeemia Order

Qalandar Baba Auliya’s lasting legacy is the Azeemia Sufi order, which he founded and which was carried on and spread by his foremost disciple, Khwaja Shamsuddin Azeemi. The order is distinctive for its emphasis on the systematic teaching of Sufi inner science — meditation, spiritual healing, and the understanding of consciousness — presented in a modern idiom, and it has established centres in Pakistan and among the diaspora. The shrine in Karachi is the spiritual centre of this living movement, a place of both devotion and study.

## Legacy

Qalandar Baba Auliya represents the continuation of the Sufi tradition into the twentieth century and its meeting with the modern world — a saint who framed the ancient inner science of the mystics for contemporary seekers, and founded an order that carries his teaching still. His shrine in Karachi is at once a place of pilgrimage and the heart of the Azeemia order he brought into being.
