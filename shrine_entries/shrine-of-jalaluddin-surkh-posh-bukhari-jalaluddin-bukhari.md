## Overview

At Uch Sharif, the ancient town near the confluence of the Chenab and the Sutlej in southern Punjab, lies the shrine of Sayyid Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari (1192–1291), the saint who made Uch one of the great centres of Sufism in the subcontinent and the founding father of the celebrated Bukhari sayyids. “Surkh-Posh,” the Red-Robed, came from Bukhara to settle at Uch, where he propagated the Suhrawardi order and founded a spiritual dynasty whose descendants — among them Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht — would shape the religious history of the region for centuries. His shrine anchors the remarkable cluster of tombs that has made Uch Sharif a place of pilgrimage and a treasury of Islamic architecture.

## The Life of the Saint

Sayyid Jalaluddin was born in 1192 at Bukhara, in Central Asia, into a family of Sayyids tracing descent from the Prophet. Migrating southward in the age of the Mongol upheavals, he came by way of Bhakkar in Sind and settled at last at Uch, which he made the base of his mission. He is remembered as “Surkh-Posh” for the red garment he habitually wore, and as a propagator of the Suhrawardi way, connected to the great Multan line of Bahauddin Zakariya. From Uch he preached and guided, and he founded the family of the Bukhari sayyids that would become one of the most influential religious lineages of the Punjab and Sind. He died at Uch in 1291, full of years, and was buried there.

## Uch Sharif and the Bukhari Sayyids

Through Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh and his descendants, Uch Sharif became a spiritual capital of the southern Punjab — rivalling Multan as a seat of the Suhrawardi order and producing a succession of saints of the first rank. Chief among his descendants was his grandson, Sayyid Jalaluddin known as Makhdoom Jahaniyan Jahangasht, one of the most revered Suhrawardi saints of the fourteenth century, and the line continued through generations of sajjada-nashins who carried great religious and worldly authority. The town they made holy is today a dense and evocative landscape of shrines and tombs — among them the exquisite tiled mausolea of Bibi Jawindi and others — and it is counted among the most important heritage sites of Pakistan.

## The Shrine and its Devotion

The shrine of Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari at Uch is a working darbar and the ancestral seat of the Bukhari sayyids, visited throughout the year by pilgrims who come to honour the founder of the line and to seek his blessing. It keeps the round of prayer, qawwali, and the free kitchen, and its annual urs draws devotees from across the region. Standing amid the celebrated monuments of Uch Sharif, the shrine is at once a place of living devotion and a link to the deep medieval history of Sufism in the subcontinent.

## Legacy

Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari holds a foundational place in the spiritual history of the Punjab and Sind: the Bukhara-born saint who rooted the Suhrawardi way at Uch and fathered a dynasty of saints whose influence endured for centuries. Through his descendants — above all Jahaniyan Jahangasht — his line shaped the religion and society of the region, and through the shrines of Uch Sharif his memory is bound to one of the great sacred landscapes of Pakistan.

### Doubts the notes raise on this row

**I7. `shrine-of-jalaluddin-surkh-posh-bukhari-jalaluddin-bukhari` — Multan, not Uch, and no death year.** The chunk has
him migrating from Bukhara to **Multan** in 635 AH / 1227 CE; the row locates him at **Uch Sharif** and
dates him 1291. **The chunk gives him no death year and no "پوش"**; the identification rests on the
name and on his position as grandfather of Makhdum Jahaniyan Jahangasht (p. 349, folio 337; chunk 032).
The place index puts Uch on folios 157, 160, 161, 169 — not on 156 or the Multan folios — so the two
accounts are not joined (chunk 031). A bare index line, "جلال بخاری سید | ۱۴۶" at the first Multan
folio, "could be either, or a third man" and was **not assigned**, since the index has a separate
explicit line for "سرخ بخاری اوچی" (pp. 315, 318; folios 303, 306; chunk 029).
