## Overview

At Fateh Pur Sharif — a small town in the Gandava plain of the Jhal Magsi District of Balochistan — stands the *dargah* of Syed Rakhyal Shah, the Qadiri Sufi poet-saint whom the people of the region remember simply as *Saeen Rakhyal*, "the Master Rakhyal." It is one of the principal shrines of Jhal Magsi, named in the district's own heritage listings alongside the *dargah* of Muhammad Ayub Shah Bukhari at Gandava and the shrines of Pir Lakha and Pir Chhatal Shah near Kotra. The shrine is inseparable from the verse its saint left behind — the multi-volume *Bahar al-'ishq*, "The Springtide of Love" — for Rakhyal Shah is honoured less as a miracle-worker than as a poet of divine longing whose words are still sung across the Baloch, Sindhi and Saraiki country that meets in this corner of the Kachhi lowlands.

## The Life of Saeen Rakhyal Shah

Rakhyal Shah is generally held to have lived from 1842 to 1940, a span of nearly a century across the colonial Indus frontier. Of Baloch origin and associated with the Qadiri tradition — the spreadsheet preserves his title as *Sufi al-Qadiri* — he devoted his life to the theme of *'ishq*, the love of the Divine, and became so beloved that, in the phrase of one modern scholar, "the poet himself grew to be the head of a cult." What most distinguishes him is his reach across languages: he is said to have composed in five tongues — Sindhi, Balochi, Saraiki, Urdu and Persian — a fluency that made his message intelligible to the mixed peoples of the Sindh–Balochistan borderland and that helped his fame outlast him. Around his person gathered a circle of *murids* (disciples) whose devotion would, after his death, give rise to the shrine that now bears his name.

## The Dargah and the Bahar al-'ishq

After Rakhyal Shah died in 1940, his followers raised a *dargah* over his grave at Fateh Pur, and the town — dignified with the epithet *Sharif*, "the noble" — grew into a place of pilgrimage. By local tradition the complex also preserves the memory of other holy men of Fatehpur, among them Cheezal (Chizal) Shah, whose name likewise appears among the venerated figures of the site. An annual *urs* marks the saint's death anniversary, drawing devotees who come to recite and hear his poetry; the *Bahar al-'ishq* remains the living heart of the shrine's devotional life, its couplets treasured as both scripture and song.

## Devotion and its Trials

Like many Sufi shrines of the early twenty-first century, the dargah at Fatehpur has suffered the violence of those who reject the inclusive piety it embodies. It was struck by a suicide bombing in 2005 and again, far more grievously, in October 2017, when an attack during the Thursday-night gathering killed some twenty worshippers as they performed *dhamaal*. That the target was a shrine whose saint preached love in five languages lent the atrocity a bitter irony widely remarked upon in the Pakistani press. Yet the *urs* has continued, and pilgrims have returned — an endurance that is itself part of the shrine's story.

## A Note on Dating

The dataset records a founding date of 1359 for this site. This is difficult to reconcile with the life of its principal saint: Rakhyal Shah belongs to the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and his *dargah* was built only after his death in 1940. The medieval date, if it is not simply an error, may attach to an older foundation or an earlier holy figure at the Fatehpur site rather than to the present shrine. Readers should treat the 1359 date with caution; the securely attested history of the dargah begins in the twentieth century.

## Legacy

Rakhyal Shah endures as one of Balochistan's most cherished mystic poets, and his shrine is among the rare centres of shared Baloch–Sindhi devotional culture in the province. In a region more often noted for its aridity and its hardships, the *dargah* at Fatehpur keeps alive a tradition of poetry, music and hospitality, and the saint's own vision — that love is the truest road to God — continues to answer, in the plainest way, the violence that has periodically sought to silence it.
