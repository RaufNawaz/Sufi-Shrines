## Overview

At Golra Sharif, at the foot of the Margalla Hills on the edge of Islamabad, stands the shrine of Hazrat Pir Sayyid Meher Ali Shah (1859–1937) — one of the most influential Chishti saints and scholars of modern South Asia. A master of both the inward path and the religious sciences, Meher Ali Shah is remembered as a great defender of orthodox Sunni belief, a renowned orator and author, and the founder of a living Sufi centre whose successors remain prominent to this day. His darbar is among the most visited shrines of the Pothohar region and the Islamabad-Rawalpindi area.

## The Life of the Saint

Meher Ali Shah was born in 1859 at Golra, into a Sayyid family of learning and sanctity descended from the Prophet. He received a thorough classical education in the Islamic sciences at the seminaries of the age and became a scholar of the first rank, while in the Sufi path he was initiated in the Chishti order, taking spiritual allegiance in the line of Khwaja Shamsuddin Sialvi of Sial Sharif. Combining deep mystical experience with formidable learning, he became a widely sought guide and teacher. He is especially remembered for his role in the religious controversies of his time — above all his prominent stand against the claims of Mirza Ghulam Ahmad of Qadian, which he opposed in debate and in writing, most famously in his work *Saif-e-Chishtiyai*. He died at Golra in 1937 and was buried there.

## Learning, Devotion, and Poetry

Pir Meher Ali Shah united the roles of *shaykh*, scholar, and poet. He wrote in defence of Sunni doctrine and on the love of the Prophet, and he was himself a composer of devotional verse — his Punjabi and Persian *naat* in praise of the Prophet, cherished by his followers, remain among the loved poetry of the tradition. His teaching held firmly to the union of the Sharia and the Sufi path, insisting that mystical devotion must rest on sound belief and the example of the Prophet. He drew to himself a vast following, and the guidance he gave — sober, learned, and centred on the love of God and His Messenger — shaped the religious life of the region.

## The Shrine and its Living Line

The shrine of Meher Ali Shah at Golra is a large and busy complex, kept by his descendants, who have continued as its sajjada-nashins and as noted religious figures — his successors, among them Pir Ghulam Mohyuddin (Babuji) and later keepers, maintaining the darbar and its teaching mission. The shrine holds the five daily prayers, gatherings of *naat* and qawwali, and the free kitchen, and its annual urs draws great crowds from across the Pothohar and beyond. As a centre of both pilgrimage and learning, Golra Sharif carries the saint’s legacy of devotion joined to scholarship.

## Legacy

Pir Meher Ali Shah stands among the towering figures of modern Sunni Sufism in South Asia — a saint whose combination of mystical stature, scholarship, and public defence of orthodox belief gave him an authority felt far beyond his own region. Through his writings, his poetry in praise of the Prophet, and the living line of his successors at Golra, his influence endures, and his shrine at the edge of the capital remains one of the great centres of Chishti devotion in Pakistan.
