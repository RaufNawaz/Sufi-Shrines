## Overview

At Kot Mithan (Mithankot), in the Rajanpur district on the edge of the Cholistan desert, lies the shrine of Khwaja Ghulam Farid (1845–1901) — the supreme poet of the Seraiki language and one of the last great masters of the classical Sufi _kafi_. A Chishti saint of the Kot Mithan line, Ghulam Farid poured the mysticism of _wahdat al-wujud_ into verses of extraordinary tenderness, sung in the sweet Seraiki of the southern Punjab and steeped in the landscape of the Rohi, the great desert he loved. His shrine is the spiritual centre of the Seraiki belt, and his kafis are sung across it to this day.

## The Life of the Poet-Saint

Ghulam Farid was born in 1845 at Chachran, near Kot Mithan, into a Sayyid Koreja family of the Chishti Sufi tradition; his father and, after him, his elder brother were the spiritual guides (_sajjada-nashins_) of their line, and on his brother’s death Ghulam Farid succeeded to the seat. Learned in Arabic and Persian and in the religious sciences, he was nonetheless a poet and mystic to the core, and he is famous for the years he spent in the wilderness of the Rohi (Cholistan), whose dunes, herders, and camel-caravans fill his poetry with an unmistakable local beauty. He was a contemporary and, in matters of the spirit, a critic of the Nawabs of Bahawalpur — remembered for counselling the ruler in the interest of the people. He died in 1901 at Chachran and was buried at Kot Mithan.

## The Poetry

Khwaja Ghulam Farid’s fame rests on his _Diwan_ of Seraiki _kafis_ — hundreds of short mystical lyrics, cast (like those of Shah Hussain and Bulleh Shah before him) largely in the longing voice of a woman for her absent beloved, in which the Beloved is God and the desert of Rohi the very landscape of the soul. His verse unites the boldest metaphysics of the unity of being with the sweetest folk-idiom of the southern Punjab, and its refrains are known by heart across the Seraiki country. Most famous of all is his declaration that the Beloved is everything, the only reality:

میڈا عشق وی توں، میڈا یار وی توں

[“You are my love, and You are my beloved; You alone are all.”]

His kafis are sung to this day by the folk-singers and qawwals of the Seraiki belt, and he is honoured as the poet who gave that language its highest literary and spiritual expression.

## The Shrine

Khwaja Ghulam Farid’s shrine at Kot Mithan is the chief centre of pilgrimage of the Seraiki region, kept by his descendants and drawing devotees throughout the year for prayer, blessing, and the singing of his verse. The annual urs gathers great crowds for days of kafi-singing, qawwali, and _langar_, and the town of Kot Mithan is known above all as the resting place of the poet. His memory is inseparable from the cultural identity of the Seraiki people.

## Legacy

Khwaja Ghulam Farid is to Seraiki what Bhittai is to Sindhi and Bulleh Shah to Punjabi — the poet who raised his mother-tongue to the summit of mystical expression and made of its desert landscape an image of the soul’s love for God. His kafis remain among the most cherished poetry of the southern Punjab, and his shrine at Kot Mithan endures as the spiritual and cultural heart of the Seraiki belt.

In the closing, the author says his Persian *خزینۃ الاصفیا* was too large, so "شائقین ملک پنجاب کا یہ مطلب حاصل نہیں ہوتا تھا کہ کتنے بزرگ پنجاب کے ملک میں صاحب طریقت گذرے ہیں", a difficulty this short Urdu book removes (p. 280, folio 268).

- (book's word: "پنجاب"; refers to: the scholars of Punjab "شیخ حامد سے لے کر حضرت شیخ بہاؤالدین زکریا ملتانی تک"; editor's footnote (modern)): Maulana Muhammad Aqil Lahori's *تحفۃ المسلمین* records Shaikh Hamid Qari's sayings and also gives "پنجاب کے اکابر علما کے حالات" across that span. The editor says its rare manuscript is in his own library (p. 278, folio 266).
