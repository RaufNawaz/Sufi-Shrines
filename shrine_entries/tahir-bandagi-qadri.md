## Overview

In Lahore's Miani Sahib Graveyard -- by local report the largest graveyard in Asia -- the oldest seat belongs not to the dead in general but to one man in particular: Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri, a scholar and Qur'an-teacher who built a mosque, a madrasa, and a library on this ground while he was still alive, and who was buried beside his own mosque when he died in 1630. The graveyard, according to the shrine's own field survey, grew up around his tomb over the following centuries, as generation after generation asked to be laid to rest near him -- not the other way around.

## The Life of Hazrat Tahir Bandagi Qadri

He was born in Lahore in 1576 CE. His father, Fazlullah, gave him his early education, and he went on to memorize the Qur'an. For advanced learning he became a disciple (*mureed*) of Hazrat Shah Sikandar, who is credited with giving him the name "Tahir Bandagi" -- telling him, as the survey records it, "you are meant for the spiritual reformation of people; simply remain devoted to the service of humanity at all times."

The survey also preserves an episode presented as a real trial in his life: a period in which he became enamored of a Hindu woman and stepped back from his religious work. His well-wishers are said to have appealed to Hazrat Mujaddid Alf Sani on his behalf, after which he returned to his path and formally became Mujaddid Alf Sani's disciple as well -- later teaching Mujaddid Alf Sani's own children. The survey reports this plainly, as part of his story rather than something to smooth over.

In his later years he married twice -- to Mah-e-Khanam and to Ismatullah, the survey noting the latter as the daughter of an Afghan king -- and spent his nights, by the same account, absorbed in remembrance (*zikr*) rather than in the comforts that came with his following. He is described as periodically fasting and practicing self-denial for stretches of fifteen to twenty days, and as never allowing the crowds who gathered around him to produce pride.

He died on 8 Muharram 1040 AH, which the survey gives as 8 August 1630 CE -- the two calendars agreeing exactly -- at the age of 54, consistent with the birth year of 1576 the same survey states.

## The Mosque, Madrasa, and Library

The land that is now Miani Sahib Graveyard was, in Hazrat Tahir Bandagi's lifetime, gifted to him so that he could teach. On it he built a mosque, a madrasa, and a library (*kutub khana*), and taught the Qur'an there himself. The survey records one specific episode from this period: Sikh raiders, believing gold was hidden inside boxes on the premises, broke in and found only books -- and, on discovering this, threw the books back into the locality and left. When Hazrat Tahir Bandagi died, his resting place beside this mosque became his shrine, and rulers and disciples of the period are said to have kept it under careful administrative care from the start.

## Legends and Disputed Accounts

Several traditions are attached to the shrine, and the survey is candid that not all of them are agreed on even by today's visitors.

One account holds that Hazrat Tahir Bandagi, while leading Taraweeh prayers, was granted a vision of the Prophet Muhammad, who told him that whoever looked upon him, learned from him, or was buried near him would enter Paradise -- offered as the reason so many notable figures chose burial around his tomb.

A second, more contested tradition concerns jinn. The survey records that graves near the shrine are popularly said to belong to jinn who watch over the site, and that some visitors report having seen figures in human form vanish before their eyes. But the survey is explicit that this is not universally accepted even among visitors: others say that although the graves exist, they have never personally witnessed any jinn or wild animal there, and regard the jinn stories as ignorance -- pointing out, in their own study of Hazrat Tahir Bandagi's life, that he himself never taught or endorsed such beliefs. Both positions are recorded by the survey as live, current opinions, not settled fact.

## Why the Shrine Matters to Visitors

The survey attributes the shrine's continuing importance to answered prayer across generations: families who saw their petitions answered here return, and bring their children to do the same. Hazrat Tahir Bandagi is described by visitors as a "perfect saint" (*wali-e-kamil*) who was granted visions of the Prophet and of other venerable elders on multiple occasions, and the presence of Jinnat and of other noble burials nearby is offered by devotees as further evidence, in their eyes, of his spiritual standing.

## Devotional Life

A mosque stands beside the shrine where the five daily prayers are held, and visitors come both for prayer and to recite the Fatiha and make personal supplications. The survey notes that some families have visited for generations, following parents and grandparents before them. The main recurring gatherings are Mehfil-e-Naat and langar, held weekly on Thursday nights and Fridays.

Daily attendance is given as 1,200 to 1,600 visitors.

## The Annual Urs

The Urs falls every year on the 7th, 8th, and 9th of Muharram, drawing an estimated 250,000 to 300,000 pilgrims across the three days. The current custodians (*Gaddi Nasheen*) describe Hazrat Tahir Bandagi as someone who did not care for drumming (*dhol*) or ecstatic dance (*dhamal*), and say the Urs is kept accordingly restrained -- centered on Mehfil-e-Naat and langar rather than music or dancing.

## Custodianship

The shrine is overseen today by its Gaddi Nasheen (current custodians), with several people formally employed as staff who, per the survey, consider caring for the shrine year-round to be a privilege rather than a job.

**The book's scope and the survey of Lahore**
- The commissioner "مسٹر ولیم کولڈسٹریم [OCR?]" ordered the author "کہ حالاتِ عمارات و مزارات و مقابر و مساجد نواحِ لاہور مفصل تحریر کروں" — to write a detailed account of the buildings, mazars, tombs and mosques of Lahore's environs. The author calls the task very hard "کیونکہ صدہا سال کے حالات بہم پہونچانے [OCR?] [illegible] مشکل", because the history of hundreds of years had to be gathered (p. 28). Elsewhere he says Lala Amin Chand's work on Lahore's buildings and people was cut short when "مسٹر[OCR?] ولیم[OCR?] گولڈ اسٹریم[OCR?]" left for Shahjahanabad (p. 734).
- The book "مشعرِ احوالِ مقابرِ بزرگانِ اہلِ اسلام وغیرہ عمارات و [illegible] و مراسمِ قدیمہ [illegible] لاہور" (reports on the tombs of Muslim elders, other buildings and the old customs of Lahore), "لیکن فی الاصل تواریخِ [OCR?] اولیا [illegible] ہے", but at root it is a history of saints (p. 29).
- Mufti Ghulam Sarwar had long sought "حالاتِ حضرات نواحیِ لاہور" and could not get them, "کیونکہ حضرات سجادہ نشیناں [illegible] اطلاعِ حال اپنا [illegible]" (p. 31).
- The survey proper opens under the heading "آغاز احوال قبور و مزارات و مقبرہ ہائے بزرگان لاہور اول[OCR?]" (p. 45).
- A forward reference: "مکانات اہل[OCR?] دہلی[OCR?] شہر لاہور میں تحریر یہ ہوگا انشاء اللہ تعالی" (the houses of the people[OCR?] of Delhi[OCR?] in the city of Lahore will be written up, God willing) (p. 292).
- The author cites Lala Amin Chand's *Safarnama* ("مطبوعہ کوہ نور"), its account of the city ("حال شہر لاہور کے بیان میں بہ صفحہ با دن[OCR?]"), and disputes it (p. 733).
- The afterword gives the book's scope as "چارون طرف لاہور کے جسقدر مزار ہین بزرگان سلف کی یادگار ہین اون سب کی حالات"; claims no book had yet been written or printed that covered "آثار [OCR?] قدیمہ لاہور" in full; and says it covers "بزرگان ہنود و اہل اسلام" and that its "صلح کلی" respects every religion (p. 872).

**Foundation legends** (two versions, kept side by side)
- In the Shah Kamal section, introduced "کہتے[OCR?] ہیں": Raja Ram Chand had three sons; "ایک لو[OCR?] نے [illegible] لاہور بنایا" (one, Lo[OCR?], built Lahore); a second, name lost in "[illegible: 4 words]", "قصور[OCR?] آباد کیا"; the third, "مسمی راو[OCR?]", founded Mauza Rawan. **Pre-Mughal rulers and invasions**
- When Sabuktigin first invaded Hind, in "سن [illegible] سو ستہتر [OCR?] ہجری", "لاہور مین راجہ جی پال حاکم تہا" (p. 804). Sabuktigin, having won the Afghans over, "لاہور و ملتان کو اپنے قبضہ مین کر لیا" (p. 804).
- Jaipal, defeated, asked that a trusted man of Sabuktigin's come with him to Lahore to collect the tribute; he came with "نواب خیر اللہ خان [OCR?]". At his court "بطرف چپ چہتری اور بطرف راست برہمن کہتری"; the Chhatris urged him to keep his word, the Brahmins not to pay; Jaipal broke the agreement and imprisoned the envoy. Sabuktigin "لاہور پر چڑہ آیا"; the rajas of Delhi, Ajmer and Kalinjar[OCR?] helped Jaipal, but the Hindus were defeated (p. 804).
- In Sultan Mahmud's time "خاندان توار [OCR?] سی ایک راجہ اوسا [OCR?] لاہور و قنوج کا حاکم تہا" (p. 804).
- Mas'ud, facing the Turkmans (Tughril Beg), was "لاچار لاہور کے طرف روانہ ہوا"; his army looted the treasury at the Indus ("دریا سندہ") and put the blinded Muhammad back on the throne (p. 807).
- In Maudud's time the Hindu rajas besieged Lahore, "جو دار الخلافت اہل اسلام کا تہا", for "سات [OCR?] مہینہ" until the besieged drove them off (p. 807).
- "خسرو شاہ ولد بہرام [illegible] لاہور مین آکر بعالم مجبوری دار السلطنت بنایا"; he ruled "سات [OCR?] برس تک لاہور مین"; his son Khusrau Malik succeeded. The Ghurids took "یہہ ملک" from Khusrau "بہزار مکر و فریب", and "پہر یہان بہی سلطنت غوریان کی ہوئی" (pp. 808–809).
- From "کتاب خلاصۃ التواریخ": after "جہاں [illegible]", Lahore lay deserted ("ویران پڑا رہا") for a long time, until an amir of "سلطان سلوک[OCR?]" settled it again; "کامراں" is named in the damaged continuation (p. 430). Settlement then grew (p. 431).

**Mughal Lahore**
- When Humayun returns to Hindustan, "حاکم لاہور تاتار خان" comes out against him; Bairam Khan overcomes him off guard (p. 823). After Hemu takes Delhi, "اکبر نے لاہور مین کچہہ جمعیت بہم پہونچا کی دہلی پر یورش کی" (p. 824).
- Akbar's second enthronement after Humayun's death: "دوبارہ شہنشاہ حسن تخت نشینی[OCR?] کلاہور میں مسعقد ہوا" (garbled) (p. 648).
- "اکبر بادشاہ[OCR?]" turned to Lahore's settlement from the start of his reign, built a city and a "دولت خانہ", raised large buildings and stayed for a time (p. 431). Lahore is called the capital in Akbar's time: Malik Ali was kotwal in "عہد اکبر بادشاہ" and held "تمام کوتوالی ... دارالسلطنتہ لاہور" (p. 194; surrounding words heavily flagged: "تمر[OCR?] قرویہ[OCR?] حتماً[OCR?] طلوکہ[OCR?] ... مقلم[OCR?]"); also "ملک علی کوتوال لاہور[OCR?]" (p. 193). Akbar is said to have been in Lahore when the Gilani ancestor came: "ان ایام میں اکبر بادشاہ … لاہو یہ رہا[OCR?]" (heavily damaged) (p. 525).
- Khwaja Ayaz (Nur Jahan's father), his wife and the infant travelled with a caravan "اونکی ساتہہ وہ لاہور تک پہنچی"; there he took service and rose to "میر منشی" under Akbar (p. 827).
- Coin legends of "نور الدین جہانگیر ابن اکبر بادشاہ": the transcriber's summary of four illegible lines names mints including "لاہور [OCR?]" and "احمد آباد [OCR?]" (p. 828).
- After Mahabat Khan's victory, "نورجہان لاہور کیطرف بہاگ گئی" (p. 829). Jahangir, ill on his way back from Kashmir, came to Lahore and died there ("جب وہان سے واپس ہوکر لاہور مین آئے تو ... خیمہ زن ملک عدم ہوگئے"); date as printed "تاریخ [illegible] ماہ نومبر سنہ ۱۶۲[illegible]ء [OCR?] مطابق سنہ [illegible]۳۶ [OCR?] ہجری" (p. 830). He was buried "شہر [OCR?] لاہور کے شمال رویہ لب دریای راوی موضع شاہدرہ مین" (p. 830); the tomb is "دریای راوی کی کنارے پر شہر لاہور سے بطرف شمال بفاصلہ چار میل" (p. 855), and from its minarets "تمام شہر لاہور معہ مسجد بادشاہی و وزیر خان وغیرہ عمارات نظر آتی ہین" (p. 860).
- "شاہجہاں بادشاہ لاہور میں ٹھہرے[OCR?]", in the anecdote of Haji Nur (p. 200). Shah Jahan is said to have had "بہت سی عمارات واقع لاہور" built, and Nawab Wazir Khan was appointed "ماظم[OCR?] لاہور" (p. 738); Wazir Khan is "ناظم لاہور" again in "جب نواب صاحب ناظم لاہور رستے[OCR?]" (p. 749). "لاہور مین شالامار باغ" is Shah Jahan's memorial: "چشم روزگار نے کوئی ایسا باغ کم دیکھا ہوگا" (p. 833).
- After Aurangzeb's accession, "دارا شکوہ نے لاہور کو مراجعت کی", raised a larger army there, then crossed the "دریای سندہ" (p. 836). Dara's betrayer, after handing him over in Delhi, "لاہور کیطرف چلا گیا" and was killed on the road (p. 838).
- At the start of Alamgir's reign Nawab Khalil Allah Khan held "دارہ[OCR?] لاہور" and, with a mansab of "شش ہزاری", was appointed "صوبہ دار لاہور" (p. 497).
- The great buildings stood until "عہد عالمگیر"; great amirs built "بڑی بڑی عمارات سنگین"; Abu al-Hasan son of Asaf Khan's buildings were among Lahore's chief ornaments (p. 431).
- In the succession war after Aurangzeb, Muhammad Mu'izz al-Din, governor of Multan, came with a large army and stores "بمقام لاہور" to his father Sultan Mu'azzam Bahadur Shah, and both went on to Akbarabad (p. 845). "شاہ عالم شہر لاہور مین سنہ ۱۷۱۲ء [OCR?] مین فوت ہوا" (p. 846).

**Later Mughal governors (nazims and subadars)**
- Abdus Samad Khan was given the title "سیف الدولہ دلیر جنگ بہادر" and appointed to Lahore by "شاہ عالم" (heavily flagged); he arrived when his son Zakariya Khan was "تیرہ سال کا" (p. 714). Zakariya Khan was appointed "ناظم لاہور" through the Sayyid brothers' support, Abdus Samad Khan moving to "ناظم ملتان"; later he continues as "ناظم لاہور" under Muhammad Shah, "شوق عمارت انکو معہ[OCR?] کمال تہا" (p. 716).
- In the reign of "محمد شاہ بادشاہ" a "حاکم[OCR?] لاہور" is named in connection with "مال گزاری[OCR?]"; the rest is damaged (p. 372).
- Mir Momin Khan became "ناظم[OCR?]" of Lahore in the time of "محمد شاہ[OCR?]" (p. 172); administration of the subjects was good, "رعایا ہی لاہور اوست بند ولسیت[OCR?] بہت عن[OCR?]"; he was not autonomous ("اگرچہ یہ نخود مختار نہ سے تھے"), "نبج[OCR?] باں[OCR?] خان" being "اصلی ناظم لاہور"; most buildings between Mochi Gate and Kashmiri Bazaar are his memorials, "دروازہ موچی و جنوب باروبہ[OCR?] بازار کشمیری اکثر عمارات … یادگار انکی"; and "یہ آخری ناظم لاہور ہوے" — after him no nazim was appointed or came (p. 173). Set beside this, without reconciliation: Nawab Abdullah Khan appointed "ناظم لاہور" in "سنہ ۱۱۶۱[OCR?]" (p. 717); the "حاکم لاہور" of p. 579; "خواجہ عابد صوبہ دار لاہور" (p. 578).
- Mir Mannu "بعہد محمد شاہ یہ نواب لاہور میں حاکم تھا"; the Sikhs had grown in number, and there was a general order to seize and kill them (p. 121). A Panjabi saying about him is illegible: "میر منو ساڈی[OCR?] [illegible] اسی[OCR?]" (p. 121).
- "بعدہ سنہ ۱۱۵۵[OCR?] میں صوبہ ملتان لاہور سکو شامل ہوا" (pp. 717–718).
- Under Nawab Abdullah Khan, "ناظم لاہور" from "سنہ ۱۱۶۱[OCR?]": "لاہور میں قحط پڑا", and he squeezed the people; proverb: "حکومت نواب عبداللہ[OCR?] نہ چکی[OCR?] رہی نہ چلہا[OCR?]" (p. 717).
- "خواجہ عابد صوبہ دار لاہور" attacked Charat Singh's garhi in "سن سترہ سو بیو کے ستہ[OCR?]"; "سکھوں کو فتح نصیب ہوئی" (p. 578).
- A Nawab ("نواب عارض[OCR?]", name damaged) came "کابل سے لاہور میں" (p. 722).
- The "حاکم اعلیٰ لاہور" is named as an office in Rajab Ali Khan's career (p. 141).

**Nadir Shah and Ahmad Shah Abdali**
