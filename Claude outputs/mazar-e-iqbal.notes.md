## Consensus choices

- **Birthplace:** "Accounts of his birthplace differ, but it is generally given as the city of Sialkot". Basis (a): the site's own account (Sialkot, 9 November 1877), resting on the standard biographies of the bibliography; Boivin's note "a poet born in Lahore" is the one dissenting source (and the same book prints the same years, 1877 to 1938). "Generally" follows the Baba Shah Kamal pilot's use with a basis (a) position. The variant is not named.
- **Day of death:** "He died in Lahore in 1938, on 21 April, the date the nation observes, though accounts of the day differ." Basis (a), the date the commemoration follows (the national observance of the death anniversary), with the standard biographies behind it. The tomb's records' variant day (21 September) is not listed. The year, 1938, is common to every source.
- **Dates of the poetry:** the site's dates for the Urdu collections (*Bang-e-Dara* 1924, *Bal-e-Jibril* 1935, *Zarb-e-Kalim* 1936) and the dated list of Lahore printings (*Asrar-e-Khudi* 1915 to *Armaghān-i Ḥijāz* 1938) are given as one chronology, since no title is dated differently by the two. The summary "from 1915 until 1935" is dropped as a minor variant, basis (b): two dated lists against one summary phrase. No span is stated.
- **The dissertation:** *The Development of Metaphysics in Persia*, published in 1908. Basis (a) and (b) for the title (the site and Rizvi); "Iran" in one listing and the place of publication (London or Cambridge) dropped as minor variants. The year 1908 is common to both listings. The Munich doctorate is common to the site and Boivin.
- **The lectures:** "printed at Lahore in 1930 as *Six Lectures on the Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam* and many times since as *The Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam*". Basis (c), the earliest printing given ("1930 and often"); the later editions (1934/1982, a 1944 reprint) are summed up as "many times since" rather than listed. Minor.
- **Iqbal and Sufism:** not treated as a conflict of fact. The site's account (his stand against a decayed mysticism that counselled the annihilation of the self) and the scholarly picture (his attacks on pirism and on certain Sufi poetry, yet his close reliance on classical Sufi thought and his *ghazal* for Nizam ad-Din Awliya') are told as one picture: a critic of the Sufism of his own day who drew on the early masters. Each element is stated as the team version states it; nothing is merged into a claim no source makes.
- **The Perfect Man and Nietzsche:** the site's account is kept (Nietzsche's *Übermensch* fascinated and repelled him; the *mard-e-momin* is a figure of immense will whose power is surrendered to God), without its word "likewise" (the asserted resemblance); and Schimmel's point is given only in its supported negative form, "his idea of the Perfect Man ... is not derived from the Nietzschean superman". What it IS derived from ("these theories") is left unnamed, since the team version does not say which theories. Not side by side, and no chain of influence is invented.
- ***jabr mahmud*:** "a higher predestination", the gloss the team version ties to Iqbal; the other gloss ("agreeable constraint", given without reference to him) dropped as minor.
- **Bausani's article date (1958 or 1959) and the titles of Schimmel's own studies (Beginning or Beginnings; Prophet or Prophet of Islam):** minor bibliographic variants; the articles are mentioned without a date, and the variant titled works are not named.
