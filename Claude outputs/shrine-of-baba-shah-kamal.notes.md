## Consensus choices

- **His order:** "There is no settled view of his order, but he is most often counted a Suhrawardi", and he "is said to have come of a Suhrawardi family". Basis (b): both books that speak of him give Suhrawardi, and it agrees with (a), since the shrine's own title includes Suhrawardi. The local double title Qadiri Suhrawardi is kept as the shrine's honorific and explained. The Qadiri Shah Kamal of Kaithal is placed in the namesakes paragraph as remembered separately, as the team version's index evidence has it.
- **His dates:** life placed between the late sixteenth and the seventeenth century, much the same years as his brother's, with one clause: "Accounts of his dates differ, and no year of his death is settled." Basis (a), the shrine's (site text) window. The single year given elsewhere, 1080 AH, is marked uncertain in the team version and is left out (rule 5). The reign printed in the same clause ("in the era of Shah Jahan") is also left out: it is the variant under rule 4, and the team version says the year and reign are printed together without being reconciled, so it cannot be given as settled.
- **Which brother:** "the elder brother", basis (a). Treated as minor and the variant dropped silently: "second brother" and "the one Shah Jamal and the other Shah Kamal" may mean "other brother" rather than birth order (team notes, decision 2), so the team version does not show a real conflict.
- **His descent:** father Syed Ahmad Shah, with the family's descent traced by the shrine to Jalaluddin Surkh-Posh Bukhari of Uch Sharif, basis (a), plus one clause: "Accounts of the family's ancestry differ in detail, but Shah Kamal is remembered as a Sayyid". All three accounts make him a Sayyid, so that is given as common ground. The variant (Sayyid Husaini of a Suhrawardi family from Baha al-Din Zakariya) is not listed.
- **Distance from Lahore:** three miles south of the city. The other figure (two *kos*) rests on a reading the team version marks uncertain, so rule 5 removes it and only the legible figure remains; treated as minor in any case (rule 4, third bullet).
- **Neighbouring village:** "near the villages of Ichhra and Rawan". Rawan and Ranwan treated as a minor spelling variant; Rawan kept, basis (c), as the name in the earliest account devoted to this tomb and the name the founding legend uses. The modern location (Muslim Town near Ichhra; Karnalpura, Muslim Town, Combo Colony) does not conflict with it and is given as today's.
- **State of the tomb:** told as change over time rather than as a conflict: in the nineteenth century (Chishti) the wall partly fallen and the grave broken in places; by the 1880s the wall fallen but the tomb still standing, in need of repair; today rated "Satisfactory". No choice needed. The word the team version marks uncertain ("sound") is not used.
- **The founding legend:** the version told in the section on this tomb (Raja Ram Chand's three sons, one building Lahore and another founding Rawan), basis (c), as the earliest account devoted to this site, with one clause: "The tale is told in more than one way". The two sons version (Kushu and Lohu) is not listed.
- **Identification (a framing choice, not a listed disagreement):** the description of Shah Jamal's brother (devout, given to *jazb*, of a Suhrawardi family) and the account of the Shah Kamal buried near Ichhra (a Sayyid famed for *karamat*, the tomb's fabric, the builders, the survival) are given as being about this saint and this tomb. The team version holds back on both (the first "ties" no brother to the tomb; the second is "matched on location"), but the shrine itself identifies its saint as Shah Jamal's brother, and the tomb near Ichhra matches the tomb described beside Shah Jamal's *khanqah* door for door (four doors, three closed, the southern open; octagonal platform and dome). V1 should confirm this does not claim more certainty than the team version allows.

- **`## Where the Sources Disagree`:** resolved item by item above.
- **Uncertain readings (rule 5):** the death year 1080 AH and the reign printed with it; the illegible word in the heading of Chishti's section on the tomb; the direction word in "south of mauza Rawan"; "two *kos*"; the platform's height of a *gaz* and a half; the *gondi* trees to the west and south (the *kikar* to the north is kept); the arched opening in each side of the dome (the word for the opening is uncertain); "sound" in the 1880s condition; in the legend, the names Lo, Qasur and Rao and the second son's lost name (the line kept as three sons: one built Lahore, a second settled another town, the third founded Rawan, since its point still stands); the namesakes Shah Kamal Kaithali as Qadiri pir of Shaikh Tahir, the contents page Sayyid Kamal, and the Samvat 1900 year of Sayyid Kamal Shah Chishti (the man and his place outside the Kashmiri Gate are kept).
- **The Ichhra setting passages:** Mian Natha's seclusion at Ichhra and the date palms reciting the *tasbih*; Khalilullah Khan and Jai Singh halting at Ichhra on 11 Dhu al-Hijja; the emperor camping at Ichhra on 24 Muharram and riding to Lahore on the 25th. In this team version the place name is marked uncertain in every one of these passages (and the word for the palms too), so they rest on uncertain readings. The Shah Jamal pilot kept the emperor's camp because its team version did not mark that passage; this one does.
- **Two unidentified Shah Kamals in Chishti:** the mother said to be the daughter of a Hazrat Sayyid Shah Kamal (a damaged passage; whom it concerns cannot be said) and the bare name in the spiritual chain of Badhu Shah (between Hazrat Fuzail Shah and Shah Sikandar; which Shah Kamal is not said). Neither is about this saint as far as the team version can tell.
- **Absences of record stated as absences:** that the 1880s account records no *ʿurs*, fair, offering, keeper, endowment or land; that the brother's notice gives no grave, date or place. Stating these as facts about the tomb would claim more than the team version shows. The one absence kept, because it explains why his order is unsettled and all three accounts agree on it, is "No master of his own and no chain of spiritual descent are remembered for him."
- **The descent variant and "second brother":** see the consensus choices.
- **Kept but reworded:** "In a history that records demolition after demolition in the city" becomes "In a city where building after building was pulled down"; the Kugle sentence on the Qalandars is kept without the author.
