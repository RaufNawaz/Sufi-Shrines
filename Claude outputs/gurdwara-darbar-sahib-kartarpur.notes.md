## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Founding year:** "around 1504 ...; accounts of when he founded it differ". Basis (a), the site text; neither other source gives a year (Dhillon sets the founding between the second and third journeys, Khalid has Nanak settle after twenty four years of travel), so the team version does not show every other source against it. Left out: the dating implied by Dhillon's sequence and his modern scheme (1506 to 1509, 1514 to 1516), and Khalid's twenty four years as a measure of the founding.
- **Meaning of the name:** "the abode of the Creator", basis (a). Minor; Dhillon's "the abode of God" dropped silently.
- **How long he lived there:** "by many accounts he spent some eighteen years there", basis (a). Minor; the seventeen years of Khalid's reconstruction dropped silently.
- **Number and length of the journeys:** "Accounts of how many journeys he made differ, but his *udasis* ... are most often counted as four; they lasted more than twenty years". Count: basis (b) (Dhillon's "more modern accounts" and Dhillon's own view against the Puratan Janamsakhi's five). Length: "more than twenty years" holds both the modern scheme's twenty three and Khalid's about twenty four, and gives no figure of its own. Route: Khalid's compass summary. Left out: the Puratan scheme's five journeys and dates, the modern scheme's four date ranges.
- **The land:** Ajit Randhawa of Pakhoke with a group of farmers (the janamsakhi telling, framed). Left out: Khalid's "a piece of land that was given to him by one of his devotees", a second telling of the same gift.
- **Who came to live there:** the Guru's parents and Mardana's parents (janamsakhi telling) and his wife and children (Khalid), given without dates, as compatible facts. Left out: the timing in each (Dhillon: before the Guru set out again; Khalid: when he settled), so that the two sequences are not joined.
- **The Guru's wife:** Sulakhini, daughter of Mul Chand and Chando Rani of the town of Batala, married on 24 September 1487, "though accounts of the marriage differ". Basis (b) for Batala (Dhillon; Bhandari also has him marry at Batala). Left out: the reconstruction's Sulakhni of Pakhoke near Gurdaspur, and Bhandari's marriage after "travelling most of the world".
- **The younger son:** Lakhmi Das, basis (b) (Dhillon, Bhandari). Left out: Khalid's Lachman Das and Sri Lachman.
- **Which son was passed over:** the site's "set aside his own sons", basis (a). Sri Chand appears only as the founder of the Udasi sect and as not recognised as Guru by the mainstream Sikh community (Boivin, Khalid's A White Trail); neither is a statement of whom the father passed over. Left out: Bhandari's Lakhmi Das as the son passed over, Khalid's "preferred Angad Dev over his son Sri Chand" and "Ignored by his father", and the reconstruction's scene with Sri Chand.
- **Where Lehna was going:** Jwalamukhi, in the janamsakhi telling (Dhillon). Minor; the reconstruction's "temple of Durga" dropped silently. Basis: the only telling outside the author's avowed reconstruction.
- **Numbering of Guru Angad:** the second Guru, basis (a) with (b) (site, Dhillon). Minor; Rizvi's "As the First Guru" dropped silently (see Points for V1).
- **Date of death:** "by tradition on 22 September 1539; accounts of the date of his death differ". Basis (a) (the site's "by tradition", with Dhillon's same day and Rizvi's year). Left out: Bhandari's reign of Salim Shah Afghan and age between seventy and eighty.
- **Place of death:** at Kartarpur, basis (a) with Rizvi. Minor; Dhillon's "under a tree on the bank of the river Ravi, just outside Kartarpur" dropped silently (see Points for V1).
- **The flowers:** the site's telling (the sheet lifted, a heap of flowers, each community takes its share and raises a memorial), framed as the janamsakhi tradition, basis (a). Left out: Dhillon's telling (the flowers laid by both communities found unwithered next day, "He is ours, our peer", his closing lesson), a second telling of the same episode.
- **Whose grave:** the site's *samadhi* and grave side by side, basis (a), with the granthi's "There is a grave of Nanak next to his samadhi", which agrees. Left out: Khalid's suggestion that it might be Bhai Mardana's, and Rizvi's statement that the Guru left no memorial.
- **The present building:** "dates from the early twentieth century and is said to have been built with the patronage of the Maharaja of Patiala; accounts of its building differ". Basis (a). Left out: the flood "several years ago" and the new building "built later" (Iqbal Qaiser), and Inderjit's "We established this gurdwara in 2001". Kept: the renovation of the structure in 2001 and the keepers' first *langar* then (see Points for V1).
- **Spellings:** Sulakhini (Dhillon's), Lehna (the site's, for Bhandari's Lahna), *sevadars*, *rabaab*, *fateha* as the team version spells them. Minor.
