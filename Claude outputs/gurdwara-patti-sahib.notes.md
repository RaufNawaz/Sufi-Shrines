## Consensus choices (rule 4)

- **Where the gurdwara stands:** midway between Janam Asthan and Gurdwara Bal Lila, on the road from the town centre towards the railway station, "though accounts of its exact distance from the Janam Asthan differ". Basis (a), the site's account. Left out: "only a few metres away" and "only a few hundred metres away" from the Janamasthan, and "across the road from Gurdwara Balila" (one item in the team version's disagreement entry; giving it beside "midway" would read as a second placing).
- **The teacher of the acrostic:** the site's "village schoolmaster", unnamed, basis (a), with "Accounts of his teachers and of what he was taught differ". Left out: Pandit Gopal, the teacher named in the tale as told to Jasdeep (the rival name; a single clause, no second name).
- **What he was taught:** the site's Punjabi and accounts, Sanskrit with a Hindu *pandit*, Persian with a Muslim *maulvi* named in some tellings Qutbuddin, basis (a), in the same clause as the teacher. Left out: "Hindi and then Sanskrit" at the site; instruction in Hinduism at seven and Persian two years later, so that neither the ages nor the subjects of another account are joined to the site's.
- **The first poem:** "Tradition holds that this composition is the *Patti Likhi* ... it is told that it was the first poem Nanak wrote, and that he wrote it here." The site's identification of the acrostic with the *Patti Likhi* is framed as tradition; "first poem ... here ... recorded in the Guru Granth Sahib" is the travelogue's own statement (not the Pandit Gopal tale), and agrees with the site. See Points for V1.
- **The Muslim mentor of his youth:** the dervish Sayyad Hussain inside the janamsakhi frame, "accounts of the dervish's name differ". Basis (b) by elimination: the rival "Sayyed Hassan" stands only in the author's avowed reconstruction (the Malji Sahib and Bal Lila rulings). Left out: Sayyed Hassan and the middle path.
- **Who lives at the gurdwara:** the Sikh families from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and the Tribal Areas who found refuge there after 1965 and 1971, Jasdeep, and the rooms taken over by local Sikhs with Kartar's family among them. Basis (a) fails here: both other sources (the two travelogues) say families live there, against the site's picture of a shrine visited chiefly by pilgrims, so basis (b). Kept from the site, as compatible: the departure of the worshipping community at Partition in 1947, and pilgrim *jathas* on the great anniversaries (without "chiefly" or "now"). Left out: "now visited chiefly by pilgrim jathas".
- **Its care and upkeep:** the site's "well kept", "maintained by the authorities charged with the care of Sikh heritage in Pakistan", basis (a), with "though accounts of its care differ". Giani Pratap kept as a named keeper (rule 7) with his decision to stay and look after the buildings, as V1 ruled on the Janam Asthan. Left out: "That was the first time someone had taken the initiative to take care of the gurdwaras in Nankana Sahib after Partition" (Janam Asthan V1 issue 2).
- **Security:** minor for a visitor and two statements against each other; both left out ("no security at this particular shrine" and guards at the entrance to all the town's gurdwaras), together with "All year round most of them remain locked".
- **When and why the Sikh families came:** the wars of 1965 and 1971 for the refuge at Patti Sahib (both travelogues), and for the town as a whole the wars "as well as the demolition of Babri Mosque in 1992" in the travelogue's own sentence. The 1992 cause is an addition in one source, not a contradiction, and was accepted on Gurdwara Malji Sahib. See Points for V1.
- **How many gurdwaras Nankana has:** "several gurdwaras, most of them associated with incidents from his life, though accounts of their number differ". Basis (d), no figure given. Left out: six and seven.
- **Size of the Sikh community:** "most often put at about two hundred households, though accounts of its size differ" (the Malji Sahib wording). Basis (b): about two hundred odd households, and Kartar's "almost two hundred to two hundred and fifty". Left out: Kartar's fifty houses a few years before, and the range.
- **Date of birth:** the year 1469 only, "accounts of the exact day differ". Left out: 14 April and 20 October.
- **The age at which the signs appeared:** "In his childhood", the text's own reading; the editor's variant (the age of ten) dropped as minor.
- **The marriage:** Sulakhini, daughter of Mul Chand and Chando Rani of Batala, 24 September 1487, "though accounts of the marriage differ", as on the Kartarpur, Malji and Bal Lila rows. The chronicle has him marry at Batala after travelling most of the world, which does not fit the date before the journeys. Left out: the chronicle's sequence and his settling in a village of Batala on the Ravi.
- **The journeys:** "Accounts of how many journeys he made differ, but ... lasted more than twenty years". Basis (d): "more than twenty years" holds twenty three and about twenty four and gives no figure of its own. Left out: five in the Puratan Janamsakhi, four in modern accounts, the last in 1523 to 1524.
- **Date of death:** 22 September 1539, "though accounts of his death differ". The only date given; the chronicle gives a reign (Salim Shah Afghan) and an age between seventy and eighty, left out as a rival. See Points for V1.
- **The son passed over:** Lakhmi Das (the spelling of the sons' list); Lakhmidas as one word dropped as a spelling variant. Successor: Lehna, Guru Angad, with "not his son but his most loyal student", one appointment told twice in agreement (accepted on the Nankana rows).
- **The *aarti*:** one translation kept (the verse paragraph "In the salver of the firmament ..."); the inline "The sky the salver ..." dropped as a second rendering of the same lines.
- **Birth by the blessing of Baba Naulakh Hazari:** one telling only, the guide's ("born by the blessing"), with "which Sikhs do not accept"; the booklet's "prayed for his mother" not grafted on (Janam Asthan ruling).
- **Spellings:** Janam Asthan and Bal Lila (the site's) for Janamasthan, Janamathsan and Balila; Pashto for "Pasthu"; Rai Bhoi di Talwandi for Rai-Bhoi-Di-Talwandi and Talwindi. Minor.

## Points for V1

1. **Length.** 18,705 against 40,839 characters of prose (0.46) before the one word edit, inside L1; no exception claimed. Nothing is padded. The team version carries about 190 citations, a 6,500 character disagreement section, the author's reconstruction, and a long survey of the Guru's life and of other writers' views, which rules 2, 4 and 5 remove. Please sweep for an important omission.
2. **The first poem "here".** "it is told that it was the first poem Nanak wrote, and that he wrote it here" rests on the travelogue's own sentence ("here ... Nanak wrote his first poem, which is recorded in the Guru Granth Sahib"), not on the Pandit Gopal tale, and agrees with the site. Fallback: delete "and it is told that it was the first poem Nanak wrote, and that he wrote it here".
3. **Who lives at the gurdwara (basis b over a).** Both other sources set families in its rooms, so I read the team version as showing every other source against the site on this point, and kept the families, Jasdeep and Kartar's rooms, with the site's 1947 departure and the anniversary pilgrims (not "chiefly"). Fallback: add "though accounts of who lives there differ" after "found refuge in Gurdwara Patti Sahib".
4. **Care: site plus Giani Pratap.** The site's upkeep by the authorities with "though accounts of its care differ", and Giani Pratap's decision to stay and care for the buildings in the 1960s, without the "first time" clause (Janam Asthan ruling).
5. **Kartar's family and his father's service** are placed "inside one of the town's gurdwaras", as Kartar names none (Malji Sahib issue 2). His family's rooms at Patti Sahib in 2011 are a separate statement, not joined to Giani Pratap's housing of them. "earlier there had been only a prayer" drops "at the Gurdwara" for the same reason.
6. **1992.** Kept for the town as a whole, in the travelogue's own sentence; the refuge at Patti Sahib keeps only 1965 and 1971. Fallback: delete "as well as the demolition of the Babri Mosque in 1992".
7. **Death date.** 22 September 1539 with "though accounts of his death differ". This is the only date in the team version; the rival gives a reign, which is left out unnamed. Fallback: "Guru Nanak died in 1539, though accounts of his death differ" is not available either (the reign is not a year), so the alternative is "Accounts of the date of his death differ" with no date.
8. **The marriage clause** is added though the team version files no marriage disagreement, because the chronicle's marriage after the travels does not fit 1487 (Kartarpur, Malji and Bal Lila precedent). Fallback: delete the clause.
9. **The alphabet poems of the Muslim poets.** "The alphabet poem was a form that the Muslim poets of the Punjab knew too" is the team version's own conclusion ("a form that Muslim and Sikh poets of the Punjab both knew"); the Golden Alphabets are given as of the Muslim countryside poets only, and not tied to Nanak.
